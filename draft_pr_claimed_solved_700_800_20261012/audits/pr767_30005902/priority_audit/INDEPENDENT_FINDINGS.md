The complete Farinati MathOverflow answer (7 June 2019, answer 12:13; edit 12:30) gives the left-invariant derivation splitting and points to FS. It does not name the exact finite27 algebra or explicitly call itself an answer to the later OWR question.

Crucial independent finding: the institutional UBA copy of the final FS journal article is 7 pages, whereas arXiv math/0207243v1 is 5 pages. The final printed p.2864 contains Examples 2.3 and 2.4 absent from v1. They give degree-one convolution and coordinate-group Lie bracket computations. A connected nonabelian smooth affine group such as the upper-unitriangular 3-by-3 group in characteristic zero yields a nonzero bracket. Its polynomial coordinate Hopf algebra is commutative and noncocommutative and hence not quasitriangular. This is a prior full counterexample class to the original unrestricted yes/no question, even without matching finite27.

Caveat: Ex2.4's displayed tangent-space notation omits the dual; the literal vector-space identification must be read via Der(A,k)=(I/I^2)^*. The explicit Heisenberg computation will avoid dependence on that notation or generic smoothness assumptions. Source bracket orientation is L_f=(id tensor f)Delta; original tensor-induction convention is R_f=(f tensor id)Delta, reversing the scalar commutator in degree one. Nonvanishing persists.
