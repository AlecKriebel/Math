## Claim being tested

On a countable undirected simple graph of uniformly bounded degree, after removing isolated vertices, rates satisfy 0<p_v<=P but need not have a common positive lower bound. Each initial edge weight is a finite positive **integer**, with no uniform spatial bound. For 0<=alpha<1 the claimed conclusion is a unique equilibrium positive on every edge and simultaneous almost-sure coordinatewise convergence N_e(t)/t to it. Neither unrestricted nonnegative uniqueness nor uniform spatial convergence is asserted. The empty edge set is vacuous. ## Independent proof reconstruction

Write c=1-alpha and beta=alpha/c for 0<alpha<1. The positive equilibrium equation is equivalent to x_uv^c=z_u+z_v and z_v T_v(z)=p_v, where T_v(z)=sum_{w~v}(z_v+z_w)^beta. In both directions this is a bijection because S_v(x)=T_v(z) and all denominators are finite and positive. The diagonal term supplies d(v) z_v^(beta+1)<=p_v, hence z_v<=(p_v/d(v))^c<=P^c=:B for *every* positive equilibrium.

For finite F, the displayed potential sums each touching edge once with outside coordinates zero. Its derivative is T_v(z)-p_v/z_v. The exponent beta+1 is greater than one; each incident power controls the corresponding coordinate at infinity. Since every retained vertex has an incident edge, the finite power sum is coercive against the negative logarithms. On bounded sets a coordinate tending to zero forces the potential to +infinity. The strictly convex -p_v log z_v terms give a unique interior minimizer. At that minimizer the same upper bound holds; T_v<=[d(v)(2B)^beta] implies the coordinate-dependent lower bound p_v/[d(v)(2B)^beta]. A diagonal extraction along finite exhaustion retains this bound at each fixed vertex and passes each finite neighbor sum to the limit. No uniform lower rate is needed.

For positive equilibrium uniqueness take M=sup_v|z_v-y_v|. Boundedness makes M finite; choose one global sign attaining this supremum in the approximate sense. At z_v-y_v>M-epsilon, every neighbor difference is at least -M, so each pair-sum for z is at least the corresponding y sum minus epsilon. Uniform continuity of r^beta on [0,2B] gives T_v(z)-T_v(y)>=-D omega(epsilon). Also z_v>M-epsilon, so T_v(z)>=(M-epsilon)^beta. Subtracting z_v T_v(z)=y_v T_v(y) gives a lower bound (M-epsilon)^(beta+1)-BD omega(epsilon)>0 for sufficiently small epsilon, a contradiction. This uses neither attained extrema nor lower bounds on y_v.

The graphical construction queries only neighboring clock histories. A chronological path of m predecessors has at most (D+1)^m local choices and rate-product bounded by P^m. Integrating ordered times supplies T^m/m!. The expected number of such paths tends to zero. Local finite-time clocks are almost surely finite, so the predecessor tree is finitely branching. König's lemma rules out infinite ancestry, and a countable intersection over edge queries and integer horizons constructs the process. Large but finite initial counts are local input values and do not change this ancestry argument.

For fixed edge e={u,v}, N_e(t)<=n_e+C_u(t)+C_v(t), so limsup N_e(t)/t<=p_u+p_v<=2P. The finite initial term vanishes for each coordinate even if the initial array is unbounded. Define q_e=p_u/S_u(N-)+p_v/S_v(N-) and Q_e=integral q_e. The predictable edge intensity is N_e(t-)^alpha q_e(t), bounded by p_u+p_v, because each endpoint's selection probability is at most one.

The integer transform H(n)=sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j^-alpha has exact increment n^-alpha and H(n)=n^c/c+O(1). Thus H(N_e)-H(n_e)=Q_e+M_e and <M_e>=integral N_e^-alpha dQ_e<=Q_e. This bracket is exact: intensity times squared jump is N_e^alpha q_e times N_e^-2alpha. Since N_e>=1 and q_e<=2P, finite-time square integrability follows before any persistence claim.

For the martingale law put A=1+Q_e and L=integral A^-1 dM_e. Then E<L>_infinity<=1, giving L^2 boundedness and almost-sure convergence. Integration by parts yields M_e/A=L-A^-1 integral L dA. On Q_e->infinity, the last weighted average tends to the same limit as L, so M_e/Q_e->0. This is a random-clock argument with no independence assumption and no circular linear-growth input.

The endpoint strong-law upper bound applies eventually to the finitely many edges at u and v. It gives q_e(t)>=k_e,epsilon t^-alpha with k_e,epsilon=[p_u/d(u)+p_v/d(v)]/(2P+epsilon)^alpha>0. Integrating first proves Q_e->infinity and liminf Q_e/t^c>=k_e,epsilon/c. Only then does the martingale law imply N_e^c/(c Q_e)->1. Letting epsilon decrease to zero proves the displayed positive coordinatewise lower growth bound. Conversely H(N_e)=O(t^c), and H=Q_e+o(Q_e)+constant gives Q_e=O(t^c); therefore M_e/t^c->0. This order avoids assuming the desired persistence to prove itself.

The vertex primitives A_v(t)=c t^-c integral_0^t p_v/S_v(N(s)) ds satisfy X_uv(t)^c=A_u(t)+A_v(t)+o(1). Nonnegativity and one incident edge give limsup A_v<=(2P)^c; the same local upper-count bound gives liminf A_v>=p_v/[d(v)(2P)^alpha]>0. In log time a'_v=c[p_v/S_v(X)-a_v]. The finitely many incident-edge lower bounds give a local eventual Lipschitz bound for each coordinate. It need not be uniform in v. Arzelà–Ascoli and countably many coordinates and compact intervals give a complete limiting trajectory b. Its coordinates have the inherited positive lower bounds and common upper bound. Errors tending to zero in physical time are uniformly small on each shifted compact log-time interval. Finite sums and positive limiting denominators justify the integral-equation limit b'_v=c[p_v/T_v(b)-b_v], making it classical. No assertion about a global Banach-space flow is required.

To test rigidity independently, fix any bounded positive complete classical solution b and let M=sup_{v,s}|b_v(s)-z_v|. A single coordinate at a single time lies within any epsilon of one of the two extremes. At an upper extreme d_v=b_v-z_v>=M-epsilon, all pair sums differ from equilibrium by at least -epsilon. Thus T_v(b)>=T_v(z)-D omega(epsilon), while b_v>M/2 yields T_v(b)>=(M/2)^beta. Hence R_v(b)-z_v<=BD omega(epsilon)/(M/2)^beta<M/4, and d'_v<=-cM/2. At a lower extreme d_v<=-M+epsilon, all pair sums differ by at most epsilon. Thus T_v(b)<=T_v(z)+D omega(epsilon) and z_v>M/2. Reciprocal monotonicity gives R_v(b)-z_v>=-z_v D omega(epsilon)/(T_v(z)+D omega(epsilon))>-M/4. It follows that d'_v>=cM/2. The lower-strip bound uses the equilibrium denominator, not a uniformly positive current denominator. These estimates are uniform over vertices and times solely because the strip itself bounds b_v or z_v away from zero, and D,B are global.

For the coordinate and time inside a strict upper strip, its open time-component cannot have finite left endpoint: continuity gives value M-epsilon at such an endpoint, and integration of a strictly negative derivative would prevent entry. Hence the component extends to minus infinity. Backward integration forces d_v to exceed M. The lower strip gives the reversed contradiction. No derivative of an infinite supremum is used. Therefore b=z. Any failure of a_v->z_v has a translated subsequence whose limit differs at zero, contradicting rigidity. The edge representation gives convergence. A countable intersection gives the simultaneous almost-sure conclusion. At alpha=0, positive weights have constant selection probabilities 1/d(v); independent Poisson marking supplies both the explicit equilibrium and the strong law directly.

## Counterexample and boundary probes

- On the unit-rate line, the alternating edge array (2,0,2,0,...) has one positive incident edge per vertex. Positive edges receive total rate two; zero edges receive zero for alpha>0. The all-one array and both matching phases are distinct equilibria. This falsifies unrestricted nonnegative uniqueness, but does not touch positive uniqueness.
- No common lower rate is introduced by the equilibrium extraction, stochastic persistence, compactness, or rigidity steps. The local constants can decay to zero.
- Unbounded initial counts cause no fixed-coordinate divergence after division by t, no infinite local denominator (degree is finite), and no clock explosion at a queried vertex.
- Disconnected countable graphs are treated by the same local proof or a countable intersection over components; no connectivity premise is used.
- Alpha=0 is separately handled on the positive state space, so no convention for 0^0 is required. Critical alpha=1, zero initial weights, unbounded degree, unbounded rates, and uniform convergence are excluded.
- Finite graphs, a single edge, vertices of degree one, and the empty edge set fit the claims as written.
