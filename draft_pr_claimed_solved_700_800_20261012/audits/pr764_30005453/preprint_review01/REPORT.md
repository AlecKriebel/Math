## Analytic challenges and their resolutions

The full derivation is in INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION.md; the following summarizes the adversarial checks rather than relying on computations.

1. **Finite-volume/log-barrier existence and automatic boundedness.** The power/incidence reduction is bijective on positive arrays. Every positive equilibrium gives `z_v T_v(z)=p_v`, forcing `z_v <= (p_v/d(v))^(1-alpha) <= P^(1-alpha)` before any bounded-solution restriction. The finite potential counts each touching edge once. Every retained vertex has an incident edge, so the power terms dominate the logarithms at infinity. The negative logarithmic barriers prevent a minimizing sequence from reaching a coordinate hyperplane on a bounded sublevel set. Their strictly convex contribution removes the possible bipartite edge-power degeneracy. The minimizer's derivative is exactly `T_v-p_v/z_v`, with no missing factor. Common upper and coordinate-dependent strictly positive lower bounds survive diagonal exhaustion; finite neighbor sums pass to the limit. A lower bound uniform over vertices is never introduced.
2. **Approximate absolute-maximum uniqueness.** An orientation with signed supremum equal to the absolute supremum can be fixed. Near that supremum every pair sum loses at most epsilon, so a common continuity modulus on a bounded interval controls all adverse terms. The large selected coordinate protects the local power sum. The resulting strictly positive lower bound contradicts equality of the equilibrium equations. No attained maximum, ratio bound or uniform lower rate is used. Uniform continuity works even when the power is below one.
3. **Causal clock ancestry.** Each event depends only on previous clocks at its vertex and its neighbors, at most `D+1` choices. Strict time ordering yields a factorial denominator, including repeated visits to a clock through Poisson factorial moments. Local finite branching and the absence of arbitrarily long ordered paths rule out infinite ancestry. Countable queries/horizons give consistency even when the graph's total firing rate is infinite. Arbitrarily large remote initial counts do not alter the clock ancestry.
4. **Exact compensator and noncircular martingale law.** For the integer harmonic transform, the jump at count n is exactly `n^(-alpha)`. Multiplying by the edge intensity gives precisely q; multiplying its square gives exactly `n^(-alpha) q`. Finite-horizon square integrability follows from `n>=1` and bounded endpoint clocks. Integrating M against `(1+Q)^(-1)` gives bracket expectation at most one. Martingale convergence, integration by parts and weighted Cesaro convergence give `M/Q -> 0` on `Q -> infinity`, with no independent-clock assumption. The finite incident-neighborhood upper count bound first proves `q >= k t^(-alpha)` and hence `Q -> infinity`. Persistence is a consequence, not an assumption, of this law.
5. **Positive coordinatewise linear growth.** The lower q bound integrates on the correct `t^(1-alpha)` scale. The harmonic asymptotic and martingale law give the printed strictly positive coordinate lower bound. The upper endpoint-clock law then gives `Q=O(t^(1-alpha))` and the required scaled martingale error. The finite initial harmonic term vanishes for each edge, even for a spatially unbounded initial array. All events can be intersected because the coordinates are countable; no common spatial random time follows or is used.
6. **Complete product-topology ODE limits.** Vertex primitives have a common limiting upper bound and individual positive lower bounds. Each vertex has an eventual local Lipschitz bound from its finitely many positive incident growth bounds. A diagonal over vertices and compact time windows yields a complete coordinatewise locally uniform limit. Edge errors are uniformly small on each translated compact log-time interval because its minimum physical time tends to infinity. Finite local sums and positive limiting denominators justify the integral-equation passage. The limit is classical without assuming a supremum-norm compact prelimit process or a global Banach-space flow.
7. **Both extreme strips and backward rigidity.** Near the upper space-time extreme, the current coordinate is at least `M/2`, protecting the current denominator. Near the lower extreme, the equilibrium coordinate is at least `M/2`, protecting the equilibrium denominator. Reciprocal monotonicity gives the lower-strip bound even if the current denominator is tiny. A common modulus supplies strict inward forward drift in each strip, without an attained infinite supremum or differentiation of that supremum. One fixed coordinate lies in a strict strip. Its strip-time component cannot have a finite left endpoint; backward integration on a component extending to minus infinity contradicts the common upper bound. Thus every complete limit is the equilibrium. A separated subsequence at one coordinate would produce a contradictory complete limit, proving actual convergence.

I found no transfer of a central difficulty to an unsupported equivalent statement. The required positivity, completeness and upper bound in the rigidity lemma are all proved for the limiting trajectories to which it is applied.
