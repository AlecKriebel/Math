## Direct independent deductions

The target-only diagonal assignment noticed before candidate exposure is valid:
each of the first four quadratics evaluates to
`(alpha-1+1-alpha)t^2`, and each of the last two to
`(beta-1+1-beta)t^2`. Thus every parameter pair, including the singular pairs
excluded by the stronger theorem, has a graded surjection to `k[t]`. Its kernel
is the ideal generated by `x2-x1,x3-x1,x4-x1`: quotienting the free algebra by
those linear identifications gives the free algebra on one generator. The
quadratic relation ideal has no degree-one component, so this kernel is nonzero
and proper and omits `x1`. The quotient is a domain. This already falsifies the
prime assertion, but the candidate proves the substantially stronger curve
quotient needed to remove target/image-dimensional ambiguity.

For that quotient, work in the free algebra before assuming any commutation.
Set `x1=x3=X`, `x2=x4=Y`. The four alpha relations respectively become

`alpha XX-XX+XX-alpha XX`, `alpha XY-XY+XY-alpha XY`,
`alpha YX-YX+YX-alpha YX`, `alpha YY-YY+YY-alpha YY`.

Both remaining relations become
`Q=beta XX-XY+YX-beta YY`. Successive quotienting is exact: for the original
free algebra `F`, quadratic ideal `J`, and linear ideal `L`,
`F/(J+L) = (F/L)/image(J)`. Since `F/L` is freely generated by `X,Y`, its
relation ideal is exactly `(Q)`. This proves manuscript lines 92–98; simply
checking vanishing in a further image would have been insufficient.

## Twisted section algebra and exact kernel

Let `S=k[s,t]` and `theta(s)=s+beta*t`, `theta(t)=beta*s+t`. Its determinant is
`D=1-beta^2`. The hypothesis makes `theta` an automorphism over any field. The
product `f*g=f theta^m(g)` for `deg f=m` is associative: both bracketings of a
homogeneous triple equal `f theta^m(g) theta^(m+n)(h)`. The scalar 1 is its unit.
For arbitrary nonzero finite sums, the unique top-degree product is
`f_m theta^m(g_n)`, nonzero in the polynomial domain. Thus the entire algebra
is a domain, not merely a ring with nonzero homogeneous products.

The same vector space has degree dimensions `n+1`. Degree one generates it:
if all of `S_n` is obtained, the span of `S_n* S_1` is
`S_n theta^n(S_1)=S_n S_1=S_(n+1)`. This is a span equality, not the assertion
that each polynomial is one product.

Expansion gives the zero polynomial
`beta*s(s+beta*t)-s(beta*s+t)+t(s+beta*t)-beta*t(beta*s+t)`.
Therefore there is a graded surjection `A=k<X,Y>/(Q) -> B`.

For injectivity, the candidate's change `U=X-Y,V=Y` is invertible over every
field; its inverse is `X=U+V,Y=V`. Expansion gives
`Q=beta U^2+(beta-1)UV+(beta+1)VU`. Since `beta+1 != 0`,

`VU=a UV+b U^2`, where `a=(1-beta)/(1+beta)` and
`b=-beta/(1+beta)`.

Replacing `VU` by either output strictly lowers fixed-length lexicographic
order with `V>U`; the common prefix is unchanged and the next letter changes
from `V` to `U`. There are only finitely many words of a fixed length, so
reduction terminates. A word without `VU` has every `U` preceding every `V`,
hence is `U^i V^(n-i)`. These `n+1` words span degree n, even without proving
uniqueness of reduction. The already established surjection to `B_n` has
dimension `n+1`, forcing equality and injectivity in each degree. Direct sums
then give an algebra isomorphism, and the map from `R` has exact kernel `I`.
There is no circular use of Hilbert series, confluence, or domainhood of `R`.

An independent classical consistency check is the Ore description
`k[U][V;tau,delta]`, `tau(U)=aU`, `delta(U)=bU^2`.
Here `a` is nonzero because `beta != 1`. Define
`delta(U^n)=b(1+a+...+a^(n-1))U^(n+1)`, with `delta(1)=0`.
The geometric-sum identity proves the `tau`-derivation law on monomials and
then by linearity. The defining rule is the same `VU=aUV+bU^2`; its unique
leading word `VU` has no self-overlap, and disjoint reductions commute. This
agrees with the ordered basis and domain conclusion, though the candidate's
shorter dimension argument does not need this extra machinery.

## Geometric definition and positive dimension

The nonsingular matrix `[[1,beta],[beta,1]]` induces the printed projective
automorphism of `P^1_k`. Pulling the coordinate forms back along this explicit
point map gives exactly `theta`, with positive rather than inverse pullbacks.
Choose the associated lift on `O(1)` and its tensor powers. The n-fold bundle
`L_n` is `O(n)` and its section space is `S_n`; the compatible section product
is precisely `f theta^m(g)`. Thus this is the standard THCR
`B(P^1_k,O(1),sigma)`. Serre vanishing on `P^1` proves sigma-ampleness, so the
construction meets conventions that require it.

`P^1_k` is projective, integral, and of dimension one over any field, including
finite or nonperfect fields. The homomorphism is unital, graded, surjective,
and has nonzero degree n piece for every n. Its image has GK-dimension two.
It is therefore not an augmentation, finite-dimensional image, point-only
section ring, one-sided point module, or a map to a large target with only
one-dimensional graded growth.

## Ideal and growth assertions

An ideal generated by homogeneous degree-two elements in the nonnegatively
graded free algebra has no degree-one component. The four original degree-one
classes are consequently independent, and `x3-x1` is nonzero in `R`. The
two-sided ideal `I=(x3-x1,x4-x2)` is homogeneous and contained in `R_+`.
The image of `x1` is the nonzero degree-one section `s`, so `x1` is absent
from `I`. Thus `0 != I < R_+`. The domain quotient proves complete primeness,
which implies ordinary two-sided primeness and graded primeness. No assumption
that the original ring is noetherian or itself a domain is needed.

The degree dimensions `n+1` give Hilbert series `(1-z)^(-2)`. Since degree
one generates, the standard generating subspace through degree n has dimension
`sum_(j=0)^n(j+1)=(n+1)(n+2)/2`; logarithmic growth gives GK-dimension two.
This is an all-degree derivation, not an extrapolation from the finite tests.

## Boundary and interpretation attacks

* Algebraic independence over the prime subfield implies `beta^2 != 1`; the
  nonzero polynomial `T^2-1` is nonzero also in characteristic two.
* No division by two, extraction of roots, countability argument, or extension
  of scalars is used. In characteristic two `U=X+Y` remains a valid change,
  and the same determinant, spanning, and domain arguments apply.
* At `beta=1`, `Q=(X+Y)(X-Y)`; at `beta=-1`,
  `Q=-(X-Y)(X+Y)`. Both linear factors remain nonzero because the relation is
  quadratic. In characteristic two these coincide and give a nonzero square
  zero. The exclusions are substantive and accurately described.
* At `beta=0`, `Q=YX-XY`, and the target is the ordinary polynomial plane.
  The hypothesis allows this stronger boundary even though the original
  independent beta is necessarily nonzero.
* Ordinary commutative multiplication leaves `beta(s^2-t^2)` and therefore
  fails generically. The inverse adjugate lift leaves
  `2 beta(s^2-t^2)` before determinant scaling. In characteristic two the
  inverse projective map agrees with the printed one, so that latter residual
  is not a characteristic-two rejection test.
* The journal fractions `c=(theta-1)/(theta+1)`,
  `d=(rho-1)/(rho+1)` are invertible rational changes only when `2 != 0`.
  In characteristic two they collapse to one when defined. Manuscript
  lines 168–176 correctly separates the direct-presentation theorem from
  claims about that geometric family's dimension and noetherianity.
* The historical source leaves “nontrivial” informal, but explicitly imposes
  none of injectivity, common fraction field, birationality, or surface-sized
  image. The theorem disproves both literal assertions under stronger usual
  nontriviality requirements than just nonzero maps. It does not address a
  reformulated conjecture requiring a surface image or preserved quotient ring.
