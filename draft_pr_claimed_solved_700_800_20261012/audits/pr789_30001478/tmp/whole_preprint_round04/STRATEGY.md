## Exact target

Let $k$ be an uncountable algebraically closed field, let $\alpha,\beta$ be algebraically independent over its prime subfield, and let $$R=R(\alpha,\beta)=k\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\rangle/(f_1,\ldots,f_6),$$ where $$\begin{aligned}f_1&=x_1(\alpha x_1-x_3)+x_3(x_1-\alpha x_3),&f_2&=x_1(\alpha x_2-x_4)+x_3(x_2-\alpha x_4),\\f_3&=x_2(\alpha x_1-x_3)+x_4(x_1-\alpha x_3),&f_4&=x_2(\alpha x_2-x_4)+x_4(x_2-\alpha x_4),\\f_5&=x_1(\beta x_1-x_2)+x_4(x_1-\beta x_2),&f_6&=x_1(\beta x_3-x_4)+x_4(x_3-\beta x_4).\end{aligned}$$ Is the augmentation ideal $R_+$ the only nontrivial graded prime ideal of $R$, and does $R$ admit no nontrivial map to a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring?

The task additionally identifies positive-dimensional twisted homogeneous coordinate rings (THCRs) as the intended target class. These words are absent from the quoted printed statement, so interpretation must remain explicit. I initially interpret a graded prime as a proper homogeneous two-sided ideal whose quotient is prime. “Only nontrivial” should exclude 0 and the unit ideal but cannot exclude additional proper nonzero primes by definition. A map should be checked under both arbitrary unital graded homomorphism and surjective graded homomorphism interpretations. Constant/augmentation maps should not be confused with maps with nonzero degree-one or infinite-dimensional image. ## Immediate independently derived falsification

Set every generator `x_i` equal to a degree-one indeterminate `t`. Each displayed relation becomes `(c-1)t^2+(1-c)t^2=0` (with c alpha or beta), in every characteristic. Thus there is a surjective graded map `R -> k[t]`. Its kernel is the homogeneous ideal generated by `x_2-x_1, x_3-x_1, x_4-x_1`. It is proper, nonzero (relations are quadratic, so R_1 has the four independent generators), strictly smaller than R_+, and completely prime. This already falsifies the literal graded-prime assertion under every admissible field and parameters. Composition with `k[t] -> k[u,v]`, `t -> u`, gives a nonzero degree-preserving map with infinite-dimensional image to `B(P^1,O(1),id)=k[u,v]`. This falsifies “no nontrivial map to a positive-dimensional THCR” if arbitrary maps with nonzero positive-degree image count. If surjectivity or dense projective image is intended, this composition alone is insufficient and that stronger interpretation must be tested separately.

A stronger possible quotient follows by setting `x_3=x_1=a` and `x_4=x_2=b`. Relations 1–4 vanish identically; relations 5 and 6 both become

`q = beta*a^2 - a*b + b*a - beta*b^2`.

Hence `R -> A = k<a,b>/(q)` is a surjective graded homomorphism. The relation matrix `[[beta,-1],[1,-beta]]` has determinant `1-beta^2`, nonzero because beta is transcendental. For characteristic not 2, setting `u=a+b`, `v=a-b` rewrites `2q=(beta+1)u*v+(beta-1)v*u`, giving a quantum-plane quotient. In characteristic 2, setting `u=a+b`, `v=a` gives `q=beta*u^2+(beta+1)(u*v+v*u)`, giving an Ore/Jordan-plane presentation. These calculations suggest A is a domain and a THCR of P^1 with a nontrivial automorphism, but the exact automorphism and isomorphism must be derived and checked before promotion. This stronger quotient could address even the surjective interpretation.
