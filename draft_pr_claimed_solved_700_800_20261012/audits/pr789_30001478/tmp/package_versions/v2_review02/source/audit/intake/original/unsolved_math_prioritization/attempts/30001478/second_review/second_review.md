# Second adversarial review: a projective-line quotient

## Verdict and scope

**PASS.** The candidate supplies a complete algebraic counterexample to both literal assertions in problem 30001478 / OWR-4335-003. No mathematical correction to the frozen proof is required. Retain the first audit's separate characteristic-scope addendum: the direct six-relation algebra works in characteristic 2, but the journal's fractional parameter conversion does not.

This is a second independent review, not peer review or a claim of novelty or publication priority. The review does not classify all primes, give a surface-dimensional image, or contradict an obstruction to a common graded quotient ring. The two supplied archives were preserved byte-for-byte; this package is separate.

## 1. Source-first inspection

Before reading the authored proof and first mathematical audit, I freshly retrieved the official OWR report and the actual publisher PDF, extracted their text, and visually inspected the following pages:

- OWR Report 22/2010: printed pp.1361-1362, PDF pages 43-44. The preceding page supplies the uncountable algebraically closed base-field context.
- Rogalski and Sierra, *Some projective surfaces of GK-dimension 4*, Compositio Mathematica 148 (2012), 1195-1237: printed pp.1198, 1201-1202, PDF pages 5, 8-9. The article opening was also read for the base-field context.

The source formulas have four generators and six homogeneous quadratic relations. In the OWR notation their expanded forms are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\alpha x_1x_1-x_1x_3+x_3x_1-\alpha x_3x_3,\\
f_2&=\alpha x_1x_2-x_1x_4+x_3x_2-\alpha x_3x_4,\\
f_3&=\alpha x_2x_1-x_2x_3+x_4x_1-\alpha x_4x_3,\\
f_4&=\alpha x_2x_2-x_2x_4+x_4x_2-\alpha x_4x_4,\\
f_5&=\beta x_1x_1-x_1x_2+x_4x_1-\beta x_4x_2,\\
f_6&=\beta x_1x_3-x_1x_4+x_4x_3-\beta x_4x_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every coefficient, sign, and generator index agrees with the author certificate. In the journal display alpha and beta are denoted c and d. Neither source's conjectural assertions impose an injectivity, birationality, or minimum image-dimension condition. The journal separately discusses an obstruction to equal graded quotient rings before stating the stronger conjectures. A counterexample to those conjectures need not defeat that preceding obstruction.

The retrieved PDFs matched the previously recorded byte counts and hashes. The package contains only public source metadata and this original review, not PDFs or extracted source text. The inspection establishes what the cited texts print. It is not an exhaustive search for later work.

## 2. Independent quotient calculation

Let R be the algebra over a field k with the displayed presentation, with alpha arbitrary and beta satisfying beta squared unequal to 1. Put

\[
I=(x_3-x_1,x_4-x_2).
\]

In the free algebra, impose the two linear equations first and write X=x1=x3, Y=x2=x4. The images of the relations are exactly

\[
(0,0,0,0,Q,Q),\qquad Q=\beta X^2-XY+YX-\beta Y^2.
\]

Consequently R/I is **exactly** k<X,Y>/(Q). This is the universal property of successive quotients of a free algebra; it is stronger than just checking that some homomorphic images annihilate the relations. No value of alpha or beta was specialized.

For U=X-Y and V=Y, substitution gives

\[
Q=\beta U^2+(\beta-1)UV+(\beta+1)VU.
\]

The change of basis has determinant 1 and works in characteristic 2. As beta+1 is nonzero, write

\[
VU=qUV+rU^2,\qquad q=\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta},\quad
r=\frac{-\beta}{1+\beta}.
\]

The rule VU to qUV+rU squared lowers degree-lexicographic order with V greater than U. It has no overlap ambiguity because VU has different first and last letters; disjoint replacements commute. Thus U^i V^j are normal forms. Alternatively, the frozen proof only needs that these words span, then obtains independence from the target below. Either argument proves the claimed degree bounds without extrapolating finite computations.

There is also an Ore-extension interpretation k[U][V;s,delta], where s(U)=qU and delta(U)=rU squared. For n at least 1,

\[
\delta(U^n)=r(1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1})U^{n+1}.
\]

This formula directly satisfies delta(ab)=s(a)delta(b)+delta(a)b on monomials. Since q is nonzero, s is an automorphism. Leading V-degree in a product proves that the extension is a domain. This includes characteristic 2, where q=1 and r=beta/(1+beta).

## 3. Pullback convention and the geometric target

On S=k[s,t], take the graded automorphism T with

\[
T(s)=s+\beta t,\qquad T(t)=\beta s+t.
\]

Its determinant is 1-beta squared. For homogeneous f of degree m define f star g=f T^m(g). Both bracketings of a triple product give f T^m(g) T^(m+n)(h), so this is an associative unital algebra B. Top homogeneous terms prove that B is a domain. Its degree-n vector space is S_n, of dimension n+1. It is generated by s,t because S_n T^n(S_1)=S_(n+1).

Set sigma([s:t])=[s+beta t:beta s+t]. Its pullback on linear forms is T. Choosing this lift identifies the successive tensor products of O(1) with O(n); multiplication of their sections is precisely the star product. Thus B is B(P1,O(1),sigma), with an ample, indeed sigma-ample, line bundle. There is no inverse inserted in this convention.

The image of Q is

\[
\beta s(s+\beta t)-s(\beta s+t)+t(s+\beta t)-\beta t(\beta s+t)=0.
\]

This gives a graded unital surjection k<X,Y>/(Q) to B. The normal-form bound gives dimension at most n+1 in degree n and the surjection gives at least n+1. The map is therefore an isomorphism in every degree. In particular the exact kernel in R is I.

For an adversarial convention check, using the adjugate lift of the inverse matrix instead would produce residual 2 beta(s squared minus t squared) before dividing by the determinant. Using ordinary untwisted multiplication produces beta(s squared minus t squared). These are not the required zero identities. In characteristic 2 the first residual vanishes because the inverse lift is projectively the same transformation; the characteristic-free pullback proof above remains the correct argument.

## 4. Primeness, nontriviality, and possible hidden restrictions

The ideal defining R is homogeneous and generated in degree 2, so R_1 has the four displayed generators as a basis. In particular x3-x1 is nonzero. Since R/I is a domain, I is a completely prime two-sided ideal, which implies both ordinary primeness and graded primeness. It is homogeneous, lies in R+, and omits x1 because its image is the nonzero section s. Thus

\[
0\ne I\subsetneq R_+.
\]

The quotient map is not an augmentation or a finite-dimensional image. Its degree-n image has dimension n+1 and its growth through degree n is (n+1)(n+2)/2; hence its GK-dimension is 2. The target projective variety has positive dimension. These facts exclude the usual trivial-map interpretations without needing to settle their weakest possible meaning.

Even an additional requirement that the target variety be a surface would not rescue the literal map assertion. Let C=B(P1 times P1,O(1,1),sigma times id), and fix a nonzero linear form z on the second factor. There is a graded injection B to C given on degree n by f to f tensor z^n. The multiplication agrees because the second factor has identity twist. The composite R to C therefore still has infinite-dimensional image. This optional observation does **not** claim a surface-dimensional image or a birational map.

## 5. Genericity and the characteristic-2 boundary

Algebraic independence of alpha,beta over the prime subfield implies beta squared unequal to 1. Thus the proof applies to every parameter pair required by the direct OWR presentation; its generality is actually greater. No hidden exceptional locus containing all independent parameters appears.

The journal writes c=(theta-1)/(theta+1) and d=(rho-1)/(rho+1). In characteristic different from 2 these fractional transformations have inverses theta=(1+c)/(1-c), rho=(1+d)/(1-d). They therefore preserve the generated rational function field and algebraic independence. Moreover

\[
1-d^2=\frac{4\rho}{(\rho+1)^2}\ne0.
\]

In characteristic 2, each displayed fraction equals 1 when defined, and the coordinate changes used to diagonalize the journal's geometric parameters also degenerate. The review makes **no** identification of that collapsing journal parameter family with arbitrary independent alpha,beta in characteristic 2. The direct presented algebra, its quotient, and its prime ideal are valid there independently. The existing scope addendum states this accurately and should accompany the result.

The excluded beta=1 and beta=-1 boundaries are substantive: Q(1)=(X+Y)(X-Y) and Q(-1)=-(X-Y)(X+Y). The corresponding nonzero linear factors survive in degree one, so those quotients are not domains. No boundary case is silently included.

## 6. Reproducibility and limitations

The immutable author archive and first audit archive were extracted into new paths with spaces and replayed from an unrelated working directory, in normal and optimized Python modes. All 47 author controls and 16 first-audit controls passed. The first-audit verifier also replayed its independent symbolic work and 36 finite-field/degree cases.

This package adds a fresh standard-library checker which imports no author or prior-auditor code. It reconstructs the source tensors, checks the quotient and Ore identities, distinguishes the pullback, inverse-lift, and untwisted conventions, and verifies the fractional-parameter determinant and characteristic-2 collapse. Separate GF(4) controls use both non-prime-field admissible beta values, verify the characteristic-2 Ore relation, and compute twisted-word span ranks in degrees 0 through 8. These are finite implementation checks, not a universal proof.

The package verifier enforces the exact regular-file inventory and manifest hashes before executing the checker. Its test suite covers normal, optimized, relocated, malformed-inventory, and rehashed semantic-mutation controls. Full corpus bytes and the complete default-serialized review pair were independently rehashed; only hashes, sizes, and match results are retained here. No external state was changed or material published.

### Narrow input-verifier hardening caveat

The author and first-audit verifiers derive their package root by resolving the verifier's own path. On a disposable copy, replacing the verifier itself with a symlink to the genuine original verifier causes it to inspect the original package, not the invoked copy. Both input verifiers consequently return PASS even if the invoked copy also has an extra file. This was reproduced in normal and optimized modes. It does not invalidate their genuine fresh-extraction results, the archive hashes, or the mathematics; it narrows an overbroad interpretation of their nonregular-entry rejection guarantee.

The separate two-line `verifier_path_hardening.patch` preserves the invoked absolute path instead of resolving its symlink. It applies to a separate extraction of the first audit, covering both the outer verifier and its nested author verifier. Rebuild the nested manifest and then the outer manifest after applying it. A released derivative must also update the old package's provenance and README claims about an unchanged author verifier; this delta is not a ready-to-publish replacement audit. In an ephemeral patched extraction, normal and optimized baselines still pass, and both self-symlink controls reject the copied package. Exact results are recorded in `path_hardening_results.json`. Neither original archive was modified. The new review-package verifier includes this hardening from the outset and explicitly tests it.
