                                A classical Wu-manifold answer
                                    to a free-rank question
                                                     Alec Kriebel∗

                                        16 October 2026 (version 1.0)


                                                        Abstract
           The ﬁxed Wu manifold W = SU (3)/SO(3) is a ﬁnite simply connected complex with the rational
       homotopy type of S 5 . Its free toral rank and free 2-rank are zero, whereas its free p-rank is one for every
       odd prime. Thus it answers the existence question printed in Hanke’s 2010 Oberwolfach contribution,
       using the actual-space free-action deﬁnitions there. The proof assembles classical facts: an almost-free
       circle action supplies every odd cyclic action, and a nonzero Stiefel–Whitney number excludes every
       continuous free involution. We give the arguments explicitly, including the arbitrary-action odd-primary
       upper bound. This is an explanatory research note; historical priority remains unresolved and no ﬁrstness
       claim is made.


1      The printed question and the ﬁxed witness
For a ﬁnite connected complex X, let

         rkT (X) = max{r : (S 1 )r acts freely on X},           rkp (X) = max{r : (Z/p)r acts freely on X}.

Actions here are continuous and act on the actual ﬁxed space. In [1, p. 1912], Hanke asks whether there is
such an X with abelian fundamental group acting trivially on its higher homotopy, ﬁnite-dimensional total
rational homotopy, and rkp (X) > rkT (X) for inﬁnitely many primes p. These ranks do not optimize over
rationally or homotopy equivalent replacements, and the torus action must be free rather than almost free.

Theorem 1. Let W = SU (3)/SO(3), with the real-matrix embedding of SO(3). It is a ﬁnite simply connected
complex of rational homotopy type S 5 , and

                        rkT (W ) = rk2 (W ) = 0,        rkp (W ) = 1    for every odd prime p.

Moreover, every cyclic group of odd order acts freely and smoothly on W .
    The same W works before the prime is chosen. This supplies an aﬃrmative answer to the literal existence
question in [1]. Its rational homotopy Euler characteristic is −1, so the asymptotic bound rkp (X) ≤ −χπ (X)
reported there is respected. The circle used below is almost free. Consequently this example does not give
a strict gap against an almost-free or rational toral rank.

   Write x and y for the nonzero classes in H 2 (W ; F2 ) and H 3 (W ; F2 ), respectively. The classical tangent
characteristic-number calculation is

                                w2 (W ) = x,         w3 (W ) = y,        ⟨xy, [W ]⟩ = 1.                     (1)

A modern explicit account is [2, Lemma 4.41, Corollary 4.44, Proposition 4.45], which credits older work of
Barden, Landweber–Stong and Calabi. Here is a direct reconstruction of the part needed.
    The tangent isotropy representation of SO(3) is its conjugation action on i Sym0 (R3 ), by su(3) = so(3) ⊕
i Sym0 (R3 ). A coordinate rotation splits this representation into a ﬁxed line and two real rotation planes
of weights 1 and 2. Its map π1 (SO(3)) → π1 (SO(5)) is therefore nonzero, since 1 + 2 ≡ 1 (mod 2). The
boundary π2 (W ) → π1 (SO(3)) is an isomorphism. Pulling the tangent bundle to a representative generator
sphere gives the nontrivial clutching bundle on S 2 , so its w2 is nonzero. The sphere’s integral Hurewicz class
generates Z/2 and remains nonzero after reduction mod 2. Thus it detects w2 (W ) = x.
    For 0 → Z/2 → Z/4 → Z/2 → 0, universal coeﬃcients identify the degree-two coeﬃcient reduction with

                                       Hom(Z/2, Z/4) −→ Hom(Z/2, Z/2).

Both possible values 0, 2 reduce to zero. The connecting map Sq 1 is consequently nonzero on x, giving
Sq 1 x = y. Orientation and Sq 1 w2 = w1 w2 + w3 give w3 = y. The complementary one-dimensional mod-
2 cohomology groups pair nondegenerately, so ⟨xy, [W ]⟩ = 1. These are tangent classes, not merely the
characteristic classes of the principal SO(3)-bundle.


3     Odd cyclic actions and the obstruction at two
For z ∈ S 1 , set
                              D(z) = diag(z, z, z −2 ) ∈ SU (3),         z · [g] = [D(z)g].
                         −1
If z ﬁxes a coset, then g D(z)g ∈ SO(3). Every determinant-one real orthogonal matrix of odd size has
eigenvalue 1. Thus z = 1 or z −2 = 1, so every stabilizer is contained in {1, −1}. Every ﬁnite subgroup of odd
order therefore acts freely. This includes order 3: its nonidentity scalar matrices have no eigenvalue 1. The
full circle is not free, since D(−1) = diag(−1, −1, 1) ∈ SO(3) ﬁxes the identity coset. This is the (1, 1, −2)
instance of the classical circle-isotropy calculation in [3, Proposition 4.2].
Lemma 1. A closed topological manifold with a nonzero tangent Stiefel–Whitney number admits no free
continuous involution.
Proof. A free involution has disjoint translated coordinate neighborhoods. Its quotient N is a closed topo-
logical manifold and q : M → N an ordinary two-sheeted covering. Local diagonal neighborhoods identify
the topological tangent microbundle of M with q ∗ τN . Naturality of the mod-2 Thom-class and Steenrod-
square deﬁnition of Stiefel–Whitney classes gives wi (M ) = q ∗ wi (N ). With mod-2 fundamental classes,
q∗ [M ] = 2[N ] = 0. Hence, for every partition i1 + · · · + ik = dim M ,

                         ⟨wi1 (M ) · · · wik (M ), [M ]⟩ = ⟨wi1 (N ) · · · wik (N ), q∗ [M ]⟩ = 0,

a contradiction. Smoothness, local linearity and orientability of N are unnecessary.

     Freeness makes W/E a closed topological ﬁve-manifold and identiﬁes the Borel construction with it up
to homotopy. Its cohomology vanishes above degree ﬁve, a contradiction. Higher-rank elementary abelian
actions restrict to rank two and are excluded as well. Together with the cyclic construction this proves
rkp (W ) = 1 for every odd p, completing Theorem 1. The universal conclusions follow from the proofs, not
from sampling primes.


5    Attribution, veriﬁcation and status
Earlier actual free ﬁnite-versus-circle gaps must be distinguished from the extra hypotheses in Hanke’s
question. Mundet i Riera [5, Theorem 1.11, Section 13], already in the ﬁrst 2021 preprint as Theorem 1.14 and
Section 15, constructs regular self-covers of every odd degree of the ﬁxed Cappell–Weinberger–Yan manifolds.
These are circle-cover pullbacks, so every odd prime gives a genuinely free cyclic deck action; the manifolds
admit no continuous circle action. They fail the abelian fundamental-group hypothesis: the mapping-torus
group contains the nontrivial centerless group π1 (V ), hence a nonabelian subgroup [6, Section 2, Step 4,
manuscript p. 8]. Taking the product in their construction retains that subgroup. They are not excluded
merely by an eﬀective-versus-free distinction.
    The Wu manifold, its characteristic number and the circle action used here are classical. This note
assembles them to document the answer to the printed 2010 question. An extensive but bounded priority
audit on 16 October 2026 veriﬁed no complete predecessor meeting all its hypotheses in the inspected
statements. Some older books, theses, journal versions and citation trails remain unread. Historical priority
remains unresolved; no ﬁrstness, new-ingredient, certiﬁed-novelty or current worldwide-open claim is made.
Exact versions, read scopes and gaps are recorded in the companion archive.
