## Actual free actions, rather than merely effective symmetry

[Mundet arXivv1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05599v1),10December2021, Theorem1.14 and §15, proves regular self-covers of every odd degree for each fixed CWY manifold X=T(h)×H. The published version renumbers these as Theorem1.11/§13. The proof identifies the cover with the pullback of the standard degree-d circle cover, with total space T(h^d)×H homeomorphic to X. This explicitly supplies cyclic deck groups. In particular, d=p gives a genuine free Cp action on the same X for every odd prime; it is not just a sequence of effective actions or prime-dependent spaces. The absence of nontrivial circle actions is continuous/topological. Thus rk_T(X)=0 and rk_p(X)>=1 for every oddp, without needing any exact higher-rank classification.

## Rigorous nonabelian-pi1 exclusion

[CWYv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.2321v3), §2Step4 p8, states that G=pi1(V) is centerless and gives pi1(T(h))=G semidirect_h* Z. Here G embeds as the kernel of the mapping-torus projection. It is nontrivial: the same passage asserts noninner outer monodromy of order2, which is impossible for the trivial group. As a second check, Step2 p5 gives injective torus-boundary groups; the amalgamated-product construction preserves these nontrivial subgroups.

A nontrivial centerless group is nonabelian. Therefore pi1(T(h)) is nonabelian, and pi1(T(h)×H) still contains that subgroup. This does not assume H has positive dimension or a nonabelian group. It also does not confuse centerless G with the mapping-torus group, whose center is described as infinite cyclic. Nontrivial monodromy provides another check: an abelian semidirect product could not have the displayed nontrivial conjugation relation. Consequently the examples fail Hanke's abelian-pi1 hypothesis decisively, regardless of their vanishing higher homotopy. No stronger claim about every exact-CW convention is needed for this exclusion.
