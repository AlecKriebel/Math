Let T be nonempty two-sided irreducible sofic over finite A. Fix the specified onto self-code f, its finite window ell and any total completion F. Let L be its factorial regular language, including epsilon. Output a finite word only when a complete local-rule window is present. The bad language B consists of words whose finite output is outside L. It is regular by delayed sliding-window output into an L-automaton. A bad input factor remains bad in every superword because its output remains an output factor; factoriality of L proves the ideal property. Every allowed input word occurs in an actual T-point, proving L disjoint B without a finite sampling assumption.

1. Any SFT extension restricts from a larger alphabet to A without losing T or the extension property. The restricted set remains SFT: add forbidden single symbols and retain the finite forbidden list. For large n, every forbidden word is excluded by the allowed n-block constraint, so T[n] lies in the restricted SFT. Write the given completion and proposed extension on the union of their finite coordinate windows. They agree on all allowed words of that larger interval; for n at least its span they agree on T[n]. This proves quantification over every extension radius, completion and containing alphabet, rather than only a particular fixed rule.

2. F(T[n]) lies in T exactly when B misses the full finite language of T[n]. Both directions use actual occurring input intervals and finite forbidden output intervals. Onto f is used only to upgrade inclusion to equality, since T is already contained in T[n]. The given-map extension question therefore has no hidden assumption that a failed radius search can stop after an empirical cutoff.

3. Irreducibility gives a strongly connected n-block overlap graph. Long n-locally legal words extend in both directions. Short occurring words must belong to L, since they lie in an allowed n-block; conversely words in L occur in T and hence T[n]. Thus the whole finite language is LT, including the short exceptions. The appended-letter overlap graph with every vertex initial/final recognizes this entire language, not just words of length n or more. A path can be extended bi-infinitely. An occurring short word is read after a preceding n-1 block, which avoids the common short-word/vacuous-factor mistake.

5. The saturation construction does not require a mixing or synchronizing hypothesis. Irreducibility concatenates the finite list L_n(T) into one long allowed word U, and it also connects U to U within T. Strong connectivity of the canonical overlap graph connects U, a putative bad occurring word w, and U within T[n]. The resulting W is bad by the two-sided ideal property; the allowed V and W begin and end with U and contain every n-word of T. They have no n-word outside L_n(T). 6. In the PRZ convention a position profile is a split length-k factor when neither end is truncated, with floor(k/2) letters on the left and ceil(k/2) beginning at the position. All length-k factors give such positions. End-truncated profiles are determined by the common k-1 prefixes/suffixes; the long words exclude profiles truncated at both ends. Thus the two entire profile sets agree, without counting multiplicities. This contradicts an LT[k] separator and proves the canonical bound N. This is a genuine general deduction; the finite controls below only test its bookkeeping.
