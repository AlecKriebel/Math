# Independent adversarial audit: the earlier threefold route

Audit date: 2026-10-16 UTC. Target: PR799 / problem30002364, universal forward implication in the exact selected record. This audit did not read the current mathematical, priority, or root reports, and made no Git, PR, sheet, release, or external-person communication changes.

**Finding: the universal forward implication has a verified negative example obtainable from literature predating October2026.** A geometrically simple almost ordinary threefold in the isogeny class `3.2.ac_b_a` has an isogenous representative, after a finite field extension, which is the reduction of a simple CM threefold whose Hodge classes on every power are generated by divisors. The reduction has non-Lefschetz Tate classes on its square. The direct liftability of a particular database representative is unnecessary and is not asserted here.

This is a prior-literature combination and an exact reconstruction, not a claim to have discovered a new mathematical result. The conservative literature cutoff used here is the 2025 paper supplying TableA.2. The authors' label-polynomial code is independently pinned to a commit dated 2024-10-29. No assertion that the whole counterexample was already published in1993 is made. This audit settles the proposed earlier route for the first question; it does not classify all cases of the converse.

## 1. Exact target and success condition

The official Sugiyama contribution defines Tate classes over the algebraic closure as the union of classes fixed over finite extensions, and defines the relevant property on **all powers**. It asks whether that Hodge property implies the Tate property for every reduction. Its positive forward theorem assumes an abelian CM endomorphism field. These distinctions agree with the intake record. [Official OWR32/2013, pp1889–1892, especially1890–1891](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46461?nt=1).

Success therefore requires one CM variety A over the algebraic numbers, a good reduction B, divisor generation of Hodge classes on A^m for every m, and failure of divisor generation of Tate classes on at least one B^m over the algebraic closure. Isogeny and finite extension preserve the latter property, so an isogenous CM-liftable representative suffices.

## 2. Primary evidence and the conflicts it resolves

* TableA.2 of Arango-Pineros–Frengley–Vemulapalli lists `3.2.ac_b_a` as geometrically simple, dimension3, Newton slopes `(0,0,1/2,1/2,1,1)`, and angle rank2. The column “Exceptional: No” concerns this threefold, not all its powers. [arXiv2505.09589v1, PDFp31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09589v1).
* The same authors' code explicitly associates this label to `P(x)=x^6-2x^5+x^4+2x^2-8x+8`. The retrieved main version equals the version in commit `826f8fc85440f2f60edcf03f9ffa008a8af4d262` of 2024-10-29. [Pinned primary code](https://github.com/sarangop1728/Galois-Frob-Polys/blob/826f8fc85440f2f60edcf03f9ffa008a8af4d262/tables/verify-angle-rank.m).
* Ribet's original paper treats arbitrary CM fields and assumes the CM type is simple; it does not assume the field is abelian. Its rank bound is `2+log_2(d) <= rank(E,S) <= d+1`. Example(3.7) explicitly concludes nondegeneracy in dimensions1,2,3. For d=3 the rank is4. [Ribet, Division fields of Abelian varieties with complex multiplication,1980/81, printedpp83–87, PDFpp10–14](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/division_fields.pdf).
* Tate's original exposition states the required same-dimensional lifting result in Theorem2: after finite extension a simple finite-field variety becomes isogenous to a characteristic-zero CM reduction, with a CM field chosen inside its endomorphism algebra and splitting that algebra. Theorem1 and its examples give the endomorphism algebra and dimension; Lemmas3–6 give the CM construction and Frobenius comparison. [Tate, Bourbaki exp352, Nov1968, collected1971, printedpp96–105, especially102, PDFp9](https://www.numdam.org/item/SB_1968-1969__11__95_0.pdf).

The proposed Zarhin1995 chapter body was **not** recovered: the official PDF URL redirects to an access page. Its publisher abstract is compatible with this conclusion but is not used to certify a theorem. Ribet and Tate close the needed bridge without it.

There is a material conflict in a candidate source. DKZv3 Corollary3.7 states that odd almost ordinary varieties can have angle rank g−1. However, Remark3.5 asserts a general equivalence of lift nondegeneracy and reduction maximal angle rank. The second statement cannot hold generally, by the explicit construction below. Its proposed passage from the Shimura–Taniyama slope formula to equality of ranks loses information: slopes average CM-type data over a decomposition group. Such averaging need not preserve the dimension of its Galois span. We use neither that claimed equality nor the unverified numerical label in Corollary3.7 as a premise. [DKZ, arXiv2112.02455v3, printed/PDFpp6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02455v3).

## 3. Exact arithmetic, geometric simplicity, and extensions

All computations below are exact PARI/GP2.17.4 operations and are independently rerunnable with `arithmetic.gp`. No floating-point recognition is used.

Set

`P(x)=x^6-2x^5+x^4+2x^2-8x+8`, `R(t)=t^3-2t^2-5t+8`.

The identity `P(x)=x^3 R(x+2/x)` holds. Sturm's theorem puts all three roots of R in `(-14/5,14/5)`, strictly inside `(-2sqrt(2),2sqrt(2))`. Consequently P is a2-Weil polynomial. It is irreducible. Honda–Tate existence gives a simple variety X over F2 with this Frobenius; because q=p, Tate's endomorphism formula gives `End^0_F2(X)=Q(pi)` and dimension3.

A splitting field is defined by the irreducible polynomial

`L(y)=y^12-14y^10+109y^8-356y^6+452y^4-352y^2+1024`.

The checker verifies six exact roots of P in this field and that its roots of unity are exactly `{1,-1}`. The resultant `Res(P(x),P(-x))=33554432` excludes opposite roots. Hence distinct conjugates of pi have no root-of-unity ratio. For every n>=1 their n-th powers remain distinct, so `Q(pi^n)=Q(pi)` has degree6. The Frobenius characteristic polynomial after every finite extension stays irreducible; therefore X is geometrically simple. This also rules out a collapse of the CM field during extension.

There are six primes of L above2, each with ramification index2 and residue degree1. With a checkable ordering of the roots and primes, their valuations are

```
M = [2 1 1 0 0 2
     2 2 0 0 1 1
     1 0 2 1 0 2
     0 0 2 2 1 1
     1 2 0 1 2 0
     0 1 1 2 2 0].
```

The normalized slopes are the entries of any column divided by2: `(0,0,1/2,1/2,1,1)`. The rational rank of M is3. The kernel of the valuations on the group generated by the roots is torsion: a product with zero finite valuations is a unit, its norm forces total exponent zero, all its archimedean absolute values are then1, and Kronecker's theorem makes it a root of unity. Thus that multiplicative group has rank3, and angle rank is3−1=2. Replacing all roots by their n-th powers multiplies valuations by n and does not change the rank.

## 4. An explicit exotic Tate class on the square

Over `F=Q(u)`, `u^2=-8`, there is the exact factorization

```
P(x)=(x^3-x^2-u*x/2+u)(x^3-x^2+u*x/2-u).
```

Let `a,b,c` be the roots of either cubic. Their product is `+u` or `-u`, so `(abc)^2=-8`. No two of these roots are complex conjugate: conjugate roots have product2, and substitution in the cubics, or the exact checker, excludes such a pair.

Over an algebraic closure of Q_l, choose the corresponding one-dimensional eigenspaces in each of the two copies of H^1(X). Wedge one eigenvector for a,b,c in the first copy and one for a,b,c in the second. This is a nonzero degree6 class on X^2. Frobenius acts by `(abc)^2=-8`; after the codimension3 twist its eigenvalue is `-8/2^3=-1`. It is therefore a Tate class after the quadratic extension F4/F2, exactly as allowed in the official question.

In degree2, an eigenpair is Tate precisely when its Frobenius product divided by2 is a root of unity. Equivalently, one root divided by the complex conjugate of the other is a root of unity. The no-root-ratio result above shows this happens precisely for complex-conjugate pairs. Every product of degree2 Tate classes is therefore balanced: each root and its conjugate occur equally often. The displayed degree6 wedge uses a,b,c twice and their conjugates zero times, so is not in that algebra.

This argument takes place after extending coefficient fields only to diagonalize Frobenius. The invariant space and the divisor-generated subspace are defined over Q_l; a nonzero quotient after scalar extension implies a nonzero quotient over Q_l. Thus a rational l-adic non-Lefschetz Tate class exists, without requiring the chosen individual eigenline to descend.

The exact enumeration supplies a useful control. The Tate and divisor dimensions on X itself, by codimension, are both `(1,3,3,1)`. On X^2 they are respectively `(1,12,51,90,51,12,1)` and `(1,12,51,88,51,12,1)`. Hence there are two extra dimensions in codimension3 on the square even though X has no exceptional class. The checker also swaps a selected root with its conjugate and verifies the resulting unbalanced candidate ceases to be Tate.

## 5. Same-dimensional CM reduction and all powers on the Hodge side

Let `K=Q(pi)=End^0_F2(X)`, a CM field of degree6. Apply Tate Theorem2 with the permitted choice `L=K`: a field splits itself as a central algebra. We obtain a CM abelian threefold A over a number field whose reduction B, after a finite residue-field extension, is isogenous to X. By the exact extension argument above, B is geometrically simple.

A is simple over the algebraic numbers. Indeed a nontrivial rational idempotent in its geometric endomorphism algebra, after finite extension, would specialize to a nontrivial idempotent of B. Specialization is injective: it is compatible with the faithful action on an l-adic Tate module and smooth proper base change. A division endomorphism algebra for the geometrically simple B has no such idempotent. The CM action has degree6, so `End^0(A)=K`.

Ribet's dimension3 bound now makes the rank of the CM type4. To spell out the all-powers bridge: the Mumford–Tate torus has cocharacter space spanned by the Galois translates of the CM-type cocharacter; its dimension is exactly that rank. It is contained in

`T={z in Res_(K/Q) G_m : z*conjugate(z) in G_m}`,

a torus of dimension4. Equality of dimensions of connected tori gives `MT(A)=T`. The Hodge group is the norm-one subtorus, of dimension3. Over a splitting field it acts on H^1 with independent characters `+e_i,-e_i`, `1<=i<=3`.

On `H^*(A^m)=exterior^*(H^1(A) direct-sum ... direct-sum H^1(A))`, an invariant exterior monomial is therefore exactly a balanced one. Every balanced monomial can be partitioned into pairs of opposite characters, and is a product of invariant degree2 monomials. Hodge classes are the Hodge-group invariants. Surjectivity of multiplication from degree2 invariants after coefficient extension descends to Q. By the Lefschetz(1,1) theorem these degree2 Hodge classes are divisor classes. This proves divisor generation on **every** power A^m, not just A.

Isogenies induce cohomology-ring isomorphisms preserving Frobenius and degree2 Tate classes. Finite extension also leaves the union of all Tate classes unchanged. Thus B^2 retains the non-Lefschetz Tate class constructed for X^2. This completes the negative example to the universal implication.

There is no conflict with Sugiyama's abelian-field hypothesis: the polynomial has Galois group of order12, while K has degree6 and is not normal, hence is not an abelian extension of Q.

## 6. Validation scope and remaining limits

The mathematical existence and Hodge-side conclusions use the explicitly cited original theorems. The computation checks their finite-field input, including all-extension simplicity and the actual exceptional wedge, rather than treating a numerical angle rank as a certificate. The all-powers Hodge statement is proved by torus invariants; finite enumeration is only a control.

The literal assertion that every member of this isogeny class admits an exact CM lift is not required and has not been verified here. The original Zarhin1995 body remains unread. Neither limitation leaves a gap in the counterexample above, because Tate's theorem supplies an actual CM reduction B in the required isogeny class.
