## 1. Exact target and success criterion

Sugiyama, OWR 32/2013, printed pp. 1889-1892, defines property (*) on p. 1890 to mean that all Hodge classes on every power are generated by degree-one Hodge classes; over an algebraic closure of a finite field, all Tate classes on every power must be generated by degree-one Tate classes. Tate classes are the union of classes fixed after finite field extension, with the usual Tate twist. Question (1) asks whether the characteristic-zero property implies the characteristic-p property at every reduction prime. Question (2) asks for classes permitting a converse. The supplied intake statement matches this interpretation.

To falsify question (1), it suffices to exhibit one CM abelian variety B over Qbar with good reduction A0 such that:

1. Every rational Hodge class on B^n is in the algebra generated by Hodge classes of degree 2, for every n >= 1.
2. Already A0 has a nonzero potential Tate class outside the algebra generated by all potential degree-2 Tate classes.

The word "potential" implements exactly Sugiyama's finite-extension convention. A failure on A0 itself is a failure of the all-powers assertion. No higher-codimension Tate conjecture or equality of numerical and cohomological equivalence is needed.

## 3. Field existence and the actual defect in Proposition 3.2

Write Q0 for the paper's imaginary quadratic field, to distinguish it from the rational field Q. Take p=3, Q0=Q(i), and g=6. Then 3 is inert in Q0. A totally real sextic R in which 3 is unramified and inert has completion the unramified extension of Q_3 of degree 6. It contains the unique unramified quadratic extension Q0 tensor Q_3. Thus all local requirements in Theorem 3.1 hold.

The paper chooses an unramified extension of **Q0_p** of relative degree g/2. That is absolute degree g over **Q_p**. This is correct in both v1 TeX and the final rendered text.

The claim that a g-cycle and an arbitrary transposition generate S_g is false. For example, on six letters, `(0 1 2 3 4 5)` and `(0 3)` generate a group of order 24, not 720. The exact group closure is reproduced by `check_claim.py`.

The standard repair is to impose, at another auxiliary prime, squarefree factorization type `(g-1,1)`. Irreducibility at p makes the Galois group transitive. A `(g-1)`-cycle fixes one point and is transitive on all others; therefore that point stabilizer is transitive on the other points, and transitivity makes the whole group 2-transitive. In a 2-transitive group, conjugates of one transposition include every transposition. The group is therefore S_g. These local factorization conditions and a real-root condition can be imposed by coefficientwise weak approximation. This is an elementary repair of a supporting existence lemma, not a transfer of the target difficulty to another conjecture.

For an entirely explicit control, `check_results.json` gives a monic integral polynomial P of degree 6, in constant-first coefficient order:

```
[719999999999999999999999999999999999995,
 -1763999999999999999999999999999841,
 1624000000000000000000000059,
 -735000000000000000024,
 175000000000144,
 -21000164,

```

Its reductions have squarefree factorization types `(6)` at 3, `(5,1)` at 11, and `(2,1,1,1,1)` at 13. Specifically the irreducible factors of degrees 6, 5, 2 are respectively

```
x^6+x^5+x^4+1 (mod 3),
x^5+2*x^4+1 (mod 11),
x^2+3*x+1 (mod 13).
```

The remaining factors are `x` at 11 and `x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)` at 13. Exact integer evaluations show opposite signs at the ends of each interval `(j*10^6-250000, j*10^6+250000)`, j=1,...,6. The intervals are disjoint, so all six roots are real and distinct. Irreducibility mod 3 proves degree 6 and inert unramified reduction there. The splitting types give S_6 by the preceding argument. Set R=Q[x]/P.

The Galois closure Rtilde is totally real, so it is linearly disjoint from Q0. Hence L=Q0 R is a CM field of degree 12, with Galois closure Ltilde and group C2 x S_6. The C2 factor acts by complex conjugation. This checks every field-existence requirement needed for one counterexample. The paper's stronger infinitude assertion is not needed or certified here.

## 4. Global CM abelian variety and good reduction

There are two places v and vbar of L above 3, interchanged by conjugation, each of local degree g=6. Complex conjugate pairs of embeddings have one member over each place. Choose a CM type Phi with exactly one embedding over v and the other g-1 embeddings over vbar. This is possible simply by making one choice in each conjugate pair.

Tate's original Bourbaki Lemma 4 constructs an abelian scheme of any prescribed CM type over the integers of a number field after finite extension. Its proof includes complex CM construction, number-field definition, and potential good reduction. Thus the present CM abelian variety B is over Qbar and has good reduction at the indicated place. This meets the global hypothesis of Sugiyama's question; a merely local object is not being substituted for it. The CM field acts with rank one on H^1.

Agugliaro's Proposition 5.4 (v1 Proposition 5.3), via the Shimura-Taniyama formula, supplies a Frobenius pi in L with normalized slopes

```
v(pi)=1/g,  vbar(pi)=1-1/g,  v(q)=vbar(q)=1.
```

The special fiber A0 and its geometric base change will be denoted by the same letter when no ambiguity arises.

## 5. Primitive Frobenius, every finite extension, and simplicity

For every positive integer m, Q(pi^m)=L. Here is the group-theoretic detail behind Lemma 5.5 (v1 Lemma 5.4).

The stabilizer of L in Gal(Ltilde/Q)=C2 x S_g is H0={1} x S_(g-1). Let H stabilize pi^m. It contains H0. The unequal v and vbar valuations imply pi^m is neither in Q0 nor in R. Thus H contains neither `{1} x S_g` nor the central conjugation c. Project H to S_g. Since S_(g-1) is maximal in S_g, the image is S_(g-1) or S_g. In the first case, absence of c implies H=H0. In the second, absence of c makes H the graph of a homomorphism S_g -> C2 which is trivial on S_(g-1). Since that subgroup contains a transposition and all transpositions are conjugate, the homomorphism is trivial. Then H={1} x S_g, a contradiction. Thus H=H0 and Q(pi^m)=L.

Consequently the 2g conjugates of pi^m remain distinct for every finite residue-field extension. The characteristic polynomial on H^1 has degree 2g and is the irreducible polynomial of pi^m. A proper abelian subvariety defined over a finite extension would give a proper rational characteristic-polynomial factor. Thus A0 is geometrically simple. Moreover every geometric endomorphism is defined over some finite field extension and commutes with pi^m. Its action on H^1 belongs to a centralizer of dimension 2g. Since L already acts faithfully and has that dimension, End^0(A0)=L. Specialization injects End^0(B) into this field and preserves L, so End^0(B)=L and B is simple as well. Simplicity is stronger than the target needs, but is checked rather than assumed.

## 6. Determinant relation and global CM signature

Fix one embedding of Q0. Its extensions to L form a set `{sigma_1,...,sigma_g}`; their conjugates form the other block. Put pi_i=sigma_i(pi). The C2 x S_g action permutes the first block by S_g and exchanges both blocks by conjugation.

The norm beta=Norm_(L/Q0)(pi)=product_i pi_i is a Weil number of absolute value q^(g/2) at both complex embeddings of Q0. Its only possible finite prime divisors lie over 3. Since 3 is inert in Q0, conjugation fixes that prime; beta*bar(beta)=q^g therefore gives valuation g/2 relative to v(q)=1. Thus beta/q^(g/2) is an algebraic unit all of whose complex absolute values are 1, and is a root of unity. For Q0=Q(i), its order divides 4. Passing to a finite field extension makes

```
product_i pi_i = q^(g/2).
```

This is precisely Agugliaro's Proposition 5.8. No numerical equivalence is used.

Suppose a of the g pi_i have slope 1/g; the others have slope 1-1/g. The determinant valuation gives

```
a/g+(g-a)*(1-1/g)=g/2,
```

so a=g/2, since g>2. Of all 2g embeddings, Phi chooses exactly one low-slope member. Each of the other pairs therefore contributes its high-slope member. In the chosen Q0 block the number of members of Phi is g/2+1 or g/2-1, according to which block contains that unique selected low-slope member. This gives the determinant types `(g/2+1,g/2-1)` and its conjugate, matching v1 Proposition 5.11 and final Proposition 5.12.

Let s_i=1 if sigma_i is in Phi and s_i=-1 otherwise. Then

```
sum_i s_i = +2 or -2,
```

and s is nonconstant for g=6. This is a **global** CM sign vector indexed by the fixed Q0 block, not a local slope vector. Confusing those vectors would invalidate the corollary. The preceding count is the bridge between them. Only valuations on algebraic number fields are needed; the paper's phrase describing a valuation on all of C as discrete is immaterial to this argument.

## 7. The elementary all-powers Hodge corollary

First, the S_g orbit of s spans Q^g. Since s is nonconstant, for some i,j its entries differ. Then `s-(ij)s` is a nonzero multiple of `e_i-e_j`. Applying permutations gives the full trace-zero hyperplane. The sum of all orbit translates is a nonzero constant vector because sum(s)=+/-2. Together these span Q^g. This proof does not require an unproved representation-theoretic criterion.

Here is a direct cohomological proof that this span is enough on **every** power. Put V=H^1(B,Q). As an L-module, V has rank one. Over a splitting field F for L, choose eigenvectors x_i,y_i for sigma_i,bar(sigma_i), using the idempotents of L tensor F. The vectors can be chosen over F inside V tensor F. For B^n, repeat these vectors in each of the n copies of V. Abelian-variety cohomology is their exterior algebra.

For an exterior monomial M, let d_i be the number of x_i factors minus the number of y_i factors, counting the different copies. Its Hodge bidegree difference is `sum_i d_i*s_i`. A rational Hodge class alpha has only monomials of bidegree difference zero. If a monomial has a nonzero coefficient in alpha after scalar extension to F, every Galois conjugate monomial also has a nonzero coefficient: alpha is rational, and Galois acts on the idempotent eigenbasis by permuting the L embeddings. Therefore d is orthogonal to every S_g translate of s. The span result forces d=0.

Thus every monomial occurring in a rational Hodge class is pair-balanced: for each i, it contains equally many x_i and y_i factors. Pair its x_i and y_i occurrences, allowing different copies. Up to an exterior sign it is a product of degree-2 pair-balanced monomials `x_(i,a) wedge y_(i,b)`.

The span D_F of these degree-2 monomials is Galois stable and descends to a rational subspace D_Q of H^2(B^n,Q). Every vector of D_Q has type (1,1). Its algebra spans all pair-balanced monomials after extension to F. Scalar extension is faithful, so the same algebra-generation statement holds over Q. By the Lefschetz (1,1) theorem these rational degree-2 Hodge classes are divisor classes. Hence all Hodge classes on B^n are Lefschetz for every n>=1.

Equivalently the Hodge group is the full norm-one CM torus, but that terminology is not needed for the proof. The original article does not explicitly supply this all-powers conclusion; this section spells out the elementary corollary rather than attributing its wording to Agugliaro.

## 8. A cohomological non-Lefschetz potential Tate class

Over an ell-adic splitting field, write x_i,y_i for the Frobenius eigenvectors corresponding to pi_i,bar(pi_i), with ell != 3. The two determinant lines

```
w_plus=x_1 wedge ... wedge x_g,
w_minus=y_1 wedge ... wedge y_g
```

span a two-dimensional subspace W of H^g(A0,Q_ell): the union of the two blocks is stable under the local Galois action on coefficients, so their sum descends to Q_ell. After the finite extension killing beta/q^(g/2), Frobenius acts as q^(g/2) on both lines. Thus W consists of genuine finite-extension Tate classes, and is nonzero.

There are no additional potential degree-2 Tate lines except `x_i wedge y_i`. Indeed, if a product of two Frobenius eigenvalues is q times a root of unity, some positive power gives `pi_i^m = bar(pi_j)^m`. Section 5 says all conjugates of pi^m are distinct, forcing the two embeddings to be complex conjugates. Thus the algebra generated by **all** degree-2 Tate classes, including those appearing over every finite extension, has only pair-balanced monomials. Neither determinant line is pair-balanced. Their sum W intersects this algebra trivially.

This gives the required non-Lefschetz Tate class already on A0, in codimension 3 when g=6. This is the cohomological content of v1 Lemmas 5.14-5.15 and final Lemma 5.14, verified without appealing to a numerical motive interpretation or an unknown higher Tate conjecture. Whether these particular classes are algebraic is irrelevant to the counterexample to (*).

Combining sections 4, 7, and 8 proves the full negative answer to the universal forward question. It does not claim that arbitrary CM reductions behave this way, and does not address the restricted abelian-CM-field positive results of Sugiyama. Here L has nonabelian Galois closure, as required to avoid that special case.

## 9. Adversarial checks and limits

The following possible failure modes were checked explicitly:

- **All powers versus one variety:** section 7 treats arbitrary n. Failure of the special-fiber conclusion already occurs at n=1.
- **Nonzero determinant Hodge difference is insufficient alone:** a constant sign vector has nonzero sum but orbit rank 1; a nonconstant balanced vector has orbit rank g-1. Both nonconstancy and nonzero sum are used. Exact negative controls verify ranks 1 and 5 at g=6.
- **Small dimension:** at g=2 a sign vector of sum 2 is constant, and the orbit argument fails. The proof uses g=6. Controls also check g=4,6,8 but do not establish a dimension-minimal claim.
- **Finite-extension divisors:** primitive pi^m for every m excludes hidden root-of-unity degree-2 relations. This check is essential under Sugiyama's convention.
- **CM and good reduction over Qbar:** Tate's original Lemma 4 closes the global-model requirement.
- **Existence proof defect:** actual group shortcut is rejected by an order-24 counterexample and repaired by an exact sextic witness. The apparent relative-degree problem is rejected as a false alarm after visual/TeX inspection.
- **Cohomology versus numerical classes:** the determinant obstruction is proved in ell-adic exterior cohomology. The standard conjecture, numerical equivalence, and algebraicity of the exotic class are unnecessary.
- **Circularity:** the Hodge orbit lemma and pairing proof are independent elementary consequences of the source's field/type data. No target conclusion is used in selecting or certifying those data.
- **Dates and novelty:** the source proves more than an abstract-level overlap but does not expressly answer the named OWR question. The conclusion is an elementary predecessor corollary, not a claim that this audit found the first such answer in all literature.

`check_claim.py` uses exact rational row reduction, exact finite permutation-group closure, exact finite-field irreducibility tests, exact CRT coefficients, and exact integer sign evaluations. It checks ranks for g=4,6,8; every local single-low choice in those dimensions; 15,625 bounded integer weight vectors at g=6; the false and repaired group-generation examples; and the explicit field witness. These computations support and control the written proofs; they do not replace the proofs of arbitrary n or of the classical CM construction.

No unsupported central mathematical gap remains for this predecessor corollary. Remaining limits are historiographic: no exhaustive literature search, no earliest-public-announcement certification, no converse classification, no dimension minimality, and no certification of the paper's full infinitude or standard-conjecture claims.
