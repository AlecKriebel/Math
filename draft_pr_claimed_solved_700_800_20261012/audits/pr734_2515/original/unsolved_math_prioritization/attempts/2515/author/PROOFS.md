# A negative answer to Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.6

## Status and scope

This note gives an explicit infinite counterexample for the prime 2. It is an authored mathematical argument, not an editor-accepted solution or a novelty claim. Independent review is required before publication. The elementary finite construction and the wreath-product obstruction should not be represented as new classification results.

The controlling source is the October 2026 update of the 21st Kourovka Notebook, printed page 177, problem 21.6, attributed to A. O. Asar. In mathematical reformulation, the question is whether, whenever a finitary permutation group G is transitive and S is a transitive Sylow p-subgroup of G, there is a transitive subgroup H <= S whose individual cycle supports are all H-blocks and which is totally imprimitive. Here a nonempty subset B is a block when each translate is either B or disjoint from B. The question uses Sylow subgroups of G, not necessarily Sylow subgroups of the full finitary symmetric group.

We construct a countably infinite permutation domain Omega and a transitive, totally imprimitive, locally finite 2-group G <= FSym(Omega) such that **no transitive subgroup of G has the cycle-support block property**. Taking S = G then meets the Sylow hypothesis. Thus even the weaker requirement obtained by omitting total imprimitivity of H fails.

The infinite construction matters: the conclusion is not based on interpreting the source as a finite-domain question.

## 1. An explicit finite permutation group

Let V = F_2^2, with basis e_1, e_2, and let E be the four-dimensional vector space of functions f: V -> F_2. Write

- tau_t f(v) = f(v+t),
- sigma(f) = sum_{v in V} f(v),
- E_0 = ker(sigma), and
- D_i = 1 + tau_{e_i}.

All additions in this section are in characteristic 2. On the eight-point set D = V x F_2, let (f,t) act by

    (v,epsilon) |-> (v+t, epsilon+f(v+t)).

The product, with the right-hand permutation applied first, is

    (f,t)(g,u) = (f + tau_t g, t+u).

This follows by applying the two maps to (v,epsilon). It makes E semidirect V a faithful permutation group of order 64. If t = t_1 e_1 + t_2 e_2, define

    P = { (f,t) : sigma(f) = t_1 }.

Since translations preserve sigma, the map (f,t) |-> sigma(f)+t_1 is a surjective homomorphism to F_2. Consequently P is a group of order 32. Its projection pi to V is onto, with kernel E_0 of order 8.

### Lemma 1. P acts transitively on D.

For any t in V there are functions f of parity sigma(f)=t_1, so pi(P)=V. Thus P is transitive on the four two-point fibres. In any chosen fibre v, an even function supported on v and a different point of V flips its two points. Hence the action is transitive on all eight points. QED.

### Lemma 2. Every transitive subgroup Q of P equals P.

If Q is transitive on D, its image on the four fibres is all of V. Choose

    q_1=(f,e_1) in Q,  q_2=(g,e_2) in Q.

The defining equation for P gives sigma(f)=1 and sigma(g)=0. Squaring q_1 gives (D_1 f,0). Put h=D_1 f. The function h is constant on each of the two e_1-pairs in V. Its two values have sum sigma(f)=1. Thus h is the indicator of exactly one e_1-pair. Conjugation by q_2 on the abelian base E is tau_{e_2}, independently of its base component g. Therefore Q contains both h and tau_{e_2}h. These are the indicators of the two disjoint e_1-pairs and span

    L = ker D_1,

which has dimension 2 and is a subspace of E_0.

The commutator q_1 q_2 q_1^{-1} q_2^{-1} belongs to Q and has base function

    c = D_2 f + D_1 g.

One obtains this formula directly from the product law and (f,t)^{-1}=(tau_t f,t). Both summands have even parity, so c is in E_0. But

    D_1 c = D_1 D_2 f = 1_V.

Indeed, D_1^2=0, and for every v the value of D_1 D_2 f at v is the sum of f on all four points of V, namely 1. Hence c is not in L. The three-dimensional space E_0 is therefore spanned by h, tau_{e_2}h and c, all of which belong to Q. We conclude E_0 <= Q. Together with pi(Q)=V and ker(pi)=E_0, this implies Q=P. QED.

This is a direct proof of minimal transitivity. No classification of finite groups, assertion about regular subgroups, or computational exhaustion is needed.

### Lemma 3. P does not have the cycle-support block property.

Let b=(0,e_2), and let z=(delta_{e_1}+delta_{e_2},0), where delta_v denotes the point-indicator function at v. Both are in P. A cycle of b has support

    C = { (0,0), (e_2,0) }.

The element z fixes (0,0) and maps (e_2,0) to (e_2,1). Thus

    z(C) = { (0,0), (e_2,1) }.

Its intersection with C has one point, so C is not a P-block. QED.

For a concrete labelling, identify v=0,e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2 with 0,1,2,3 and label (v,epsilon) by 2v+epsilon+1. Then

    a = (1 3 2 4)(5 7)(6 8),
    b = (1 5)(2 6)(3 7)(4 8),
    z = (3 4)(5 6),
    C = {1,5},   z(C) = {1,6}.

The group generated by a and b is P: it is contained in P and is transitive, since its fibre projection contains e_1,e_2 and a^2 flips both points of a fibre; alternatively apply the base-function calculation in Lemma 2 directly to a,b. A fully direct check is that a^2 gives one e_1-pair indicator, its b-conjugate gives the other, and [a,b] supplies the third independent vector of E_0; together with the two projected generators these generate P.

Lemmas 2 and 3 show that P contains no transitive subgroup with the desired cycle-support property.

## 2. The block-obstruction transfer lemma

### Lemma 4. Let G act on Omega, let B be a G-block, and suppose the induced action G_{B}^B is permutation-isomorphic to P. Then G has no transitive subgroup with the cycle-support block property.

Here G_B denotes the setwise stabilizer and G_B^B its image on B. Suppose H <= G is transitive. For x,y in B, transitivity supplies h in H with h(x)=y. Since h(B) meets B and B is a G-block, h(B)=B. Thus H_B is transitive on B, and its induced image is a transitive subgroup of P. Lemma 2 implies H_B^B=P.

Choose h_b,h_z in H_B whose restrictions to B are b,z from Lemma 3 under the fixed identification B=D. Because h_b stabilizes B setwise, its two-point orbit C inside B is an entire cycle of h_b on Omega; it cannot leave B between its two points. The restriction of h_z sends C to a distinct set meeting it. Hence C is not an H-block. This contradicts the cycle-support block property for H. QED.

The lifts h_b and h_z can act arbitrarily outside B. Nothing in the proof assumes that either is supported on B, that H contains the base copy of P, or that restriction is injective. Surjectivity of the induced block action is exactly what is used. This addresses the universal quantifier over all transitive subgroups H.

## 3. The infinite finitary counterexample

For n >= 0 put

    Omega_n = D x {0,1,...,2^n-1},
    Omega = D x N_0.

Start with G_0=P on Omega_0. Inductively, identify Omega_{n+1} with two consecutive copies of Omega_n and set

    G_{n+1} = (G_n x G_n) semidirect <s_n>,

where s_n has order 2 and exchanges the two copies coordinate by coordinate. Thus G_{n+1} is the imprimitive wreath product G_n wr C_2. Embed G_n into the first factor, so it acts on Omega_n and fixes the other copy. Extend every element by the identity on Omega minus its finite domain, and define

    G = union_{n>=0} G_n <= Sym(Omega).

### Lemma 5. G is a transitive, locally finite 2-group of finitary permutations.

The embeddings above make the G_n an increasing chain of actual permutation groups. By induction each G_n is finite of 2-power order: |G_0|=32 and |G_{n+1}|=2|G_n|^2. Every finite subset of G is contained in some G_n, so it generates a finite 2-group. In particular every element has 2-power order. Every element fixes Omega outside some Omega_n, a finite set, so G is finitary.

The group G_0 is transitive by Lemma 1. If G_n is transitive on Omega_n, the two base factors of G_{n+1} are transitive on their respective copies and s_n interchanges the copies. Thus G_{n+1} is transitive. Any two points of Omega lie in some Omega_n, so G is transitive on Omega. QED.

### Lemma 6. Each Omega_n is a G-block, and G is totally imprimitive.

At each finite stage m>=n, G_m preserves the partition into consecutive blocks of size |Omega_n|=8*2^n. This is proved by induction on m: each of the two copies preserves its internal partition, while the top swap exchanges corresponding partition members. Consequently an element of G_m maps Omega_n to itself or to a disjoint member of that partition. For m<n, the element stabilizes Omega_n. This proves the block assertion for the union G.

The blocks Omega_n form a strictly increasing chain of finite proper blocks covering Omega. In fact every proper block of a transitive finitary group on an infinite domain is finite: if an infinite proper block A existed, transitivity would give g with g(A) not equal to A, hence g(A) disjoint from A; then g would move every point of A, contrary to finite support. Any proper block containing a fixed point is therefore finite and is contained in a sufficiently large Omega_n (choose the chain based at that point, or translate the displayed chain). Increasing n once more strictly enlarges it. There is no maximal proper block, as required by total imprimitivity. QED.

### Lemma 7. For B=Omega_0, the induced action G_B^B is exactly P.

The equality holds at stage zero. At any later finite stage, the recursion permutes the bottom eight-point fibres D x {i}, and every map between two fibres is obtained by applying an element of P to the D-coordinate. This invariant holds initially, is preserved by taking two independent copies, and is preserved by swapping copies coordinate by coordinate. Therefore, whenever an element stabilizes B, its restriction is an element of P. Conversely the embedded copy G_0=P belongs to G_B and realizes every element of P. QED.

### Theorem. Problem 21.6 has a negative answer.

Take p=2 and the group G in Section 3, with S=G. By Lemma 5, G is a transitive subgroup of FSym(Omega) and is a 2-group. The whole group S=G is a Sylow 2-subgroup of G: it is itself a maximal 2-subgroup, since no larger subgroup of G exists. It is transitive. Lemma 6 also verifies total imprimitivity of this ambient example. By Lemmas 7 and 4, no transitive subgroup H <= S has the cycle-support block property. In particular no such H satisfies the cyclic-block property required in the question. QED.

## 4. Verification boundaries and controls

The proof is finite algebra plus an explicitly defined increasing union. It does not infer an infinite theorem from bounded computation. The accompanying standard-library verifier independently constructs P from its parity equation, exhausts its subgroup lattice, checks that P alone is transitive, and checks the crossing-cycle witness. It also checks all 64 possible pairs of lifts of e_1,e_2, and the complete first wreath extension of order 2048. The latter is a sanity check on the recursive setup, not a substitute for Lemmas 5-7.

Useful controls:

1. Dropping the parity twist produces the full group E semidirect V. It contains a regular subgroup C_2^3, which does have the cycle-support block property. Thus the obstruction is not a generic property of every transitive 2-group on eight points.
2. Replacing z by the identity does not witness a block failure.
3. Checking only selected generators is insufficient: on four points the generators (1 2) and (1 3 2 4) each have block cycle supports relative to their generated dihedral group, but their product has a cycle support that fails the block test.
4. Regular finite actions have the cycle-support block property: in a left regular action the cycle through x under g is <g>x, a translate of the subgroup x^{-1}<g>x. Infinite regular actions cannot be nontrivial subgroups of a finitary symmetric group, because every nonidentity regular permutation is fixed-point-free. This is why a naive infinite regular-subgroup construction is inapplicable.

No scholarly theorem beyond elementary definitions is required for the negative answer. The literature discussion is contextual and is not a dependency of any lemma above.
