## Independent deduction

1. The destination-indexed action is faithful. Applying (g,u) first and (f,t) second sends final coordinate w=v+u+t to epsilon+f(w)+g(w+t); hence the proposed semidirect product law is correct. Translation preserves function parity, so the equation sigma(f)=t1 is a homomorphism kernel of order32. Its projection is all V and the even base can flip a selected fibre, giving transitivity.
2. Any transitive subgroup Q must project onto V, since its fibre action is a subgroup of the regular translation group. Choose lifts of e1,e2. The square of the first lift yields exactly one e1-pair indicator h. Conjugation by the second lift yields the complementary indicator. They span kerD1, a two-dimensional even-function subspace.
3. Direct multiplication gives commutator c=D2f+D1g. Since D1 squared is zero and D1D2f is the constant sum of f, D1c=1V. Thus c lies in the even base but outside that two-dimensional space. All three even directions lie in Q, so Q=P. This deduction is independent of subgroup counts, the classification of finite2-groups, or existence of regular subgroups.
4. b=(0,e2) and the even base z=delta_e1+delta_e2 belong to P. The full b-cycle through (0,0) is C={(0,0),(e2,0)}. The z-translate fixes one point and changes the second fibre coordinate. It intersects C once without equality and certifies a block failure. Testing whole element support would be a different property; the proof uses one actual cycle.
5. If B is a G-block and its full stabilizer image equals this P, any transitive H<=G has transitive H_B image on B. The block axiom forces a transporter between two points of B to stabilize B. Minimal transitivity makes that image P, so lifts of both b and z exist in H_B. A b-lift never leaves B from C, and its orbit closes after two steps; arbitrary additional cycles elsewhere do not alter C. A z-lift gives the crossing translate in H itself. The argument covers H with no embedded copy of P and with nonfaithful restriction. This is the essential universal-H implication.
6. The first-base-factor embeddings in the wreath tower agree as actual permutations on the old finite domain and fix new points. The union is a group; a finite generating set lies in one finite stage. The stage orders are2^(6*2^n-1), proving finite2-groups at all levels. Every individual union element has finite support, and every pair of points lies in a common transitive finite stage. No completion or infinite-support shift is included.
7. At each level, the aligned dyadic partition into copies of each earlier Omega_n is preserved, including top coordinatewise swaps and fixed external points. Thus each Omega_n is a block for the entire union. These finite blocks cover every finite point set, proving the actual printed definition of total imprimitivity directly. The author's further no-maximal-proper-block discussion is not required to match the target definition. Every transitive subgroup also inherits these block chains.
8. The stronger bottom-fibre invariant is that every finite-stage element permutes eight-point fibres and acts from one fibre to another by an element of P on the D-coordinate. It holds initially and is preserved by independent copies and identity-coordinate swaps. Every bottom-block stabilizer therefore restricts into P; the initial base copy realizes all P. The image is exactly P, not merely a supergroup or quotient.
9. The whole locally finite2-group is itself a maximal2-subgroup of G and therefore a Sylow2-subgroup of G under the relevant definition. Apply the unrestricted transfer. There is no transitive H with even the cycle-support block condition, hence no subgroup with the requested cyclic-block property. No unproved group-theory theorem beyond elementary facts is needed.
