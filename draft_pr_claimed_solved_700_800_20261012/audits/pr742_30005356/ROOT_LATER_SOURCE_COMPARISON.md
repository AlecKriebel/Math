# Root comparison of later vector-space classification

Status: source comparison completed; overall priority gate remains pending.

Root independently opened the primary record and PDF of Leon Chini, Model theory of generic vector space endomorphisms III: Reducts, arXiv:2607.17014v1 (submitted 19 July 2026), and read the introduction, vector-space setting, Theorem4.10 and its following Question4.11/context, plus the discussion of ACFH and torsion on p31. The primary version record of the first paper, arXiv:2502.13667v3 (revised15July2026), was also read; its substantive comparison here is left to the independent priority auditor rather than claimed from its abstract.

The later theorem classifies endomorphisms definable in the vector-space-plus-operator language. The following question concerns the extra definable endomorphisms furnished by a richer base language. Its reference to d'Elbee does not prove that the earlier integral polynomial question is answered. The source expressly treats transfer to ACFH as a proposed extension involving division by torsion.

The decisive distinction for the present theorem is checkable without evaluating all of that paper: K* is not a vector space over a field with its given group operations. In characteristic p it has nontrivial ell-th roots of unity for every prime ell unequal to p, hence nonzero ell-torsion, while its p-th power map is bijective. This contradicts the torsion pattern of the additive group of any field-vector space. Dividing by torsion changes the group and the available scalar operators. Therefore the vector-space classification cannot be imported as a classification of the original multiplicative group with field definability.

The broad principle that extra base-language maps may defeat a classification in a smaller language is prior context that must be credited if used. The candidate's parameter-free inverse Frobenius is itself a classical pure-field map. Any publishable note must focus its contribution on the specific negative answer to the printed integral-polynomial question, give due credit for classical Frobenius and d'Elbee's faithfulness, and avoid claiming a new Frobenius theorem or a full endomorphism classification. This root comparison finds no complete earlier proof of the specific counterexample in the inspected passages; it does not certify first historical priority.
