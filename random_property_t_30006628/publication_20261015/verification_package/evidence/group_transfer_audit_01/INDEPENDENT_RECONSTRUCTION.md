# Reconstruction before inherited audit verdicts

The following reasoning was written after reading the candidate proof but before opening any prior audit, second-review verdict, checker, or acceptance file. It is verification of a proposed completed proof.

1. In the free product, a reduced syllable sequence of nonidentity factor elements uniquely represents an element. Its inverse reverses the sequence and inverts each syllable. Different reduced lengths are distinct. A fixed point of inversion must have odd length: for even length, the central two syllables would be mutual inverses, violating reducedness. For odd length 2s+1 it is determined by s outer syllables and one order-two middle syllable, so its count is at most |W_s| times the fixed finite number of self-inverse letters.

2. Consequently the inverse-free block alphabet is an honest set of inverse pairs of distinct free-product elements. Choosing one formal generator per pair and using the other as its formal inverse yields a legitimate finite triangular presentation. A formal block cancellation at a cyclic corner would force the two underlying blocks to be inverses; then the meeting syllables lie in the same factor, impossible in a cyclically reduced sampled syllable relator. This remains true for mixed block lengths, since inverse words have the same syllable length. Duplicate occurrences and relators merely give a multigraph link.

3. Let H0 be generated by the inverse-free reduced words of length L. For arbitrary a,b from the original inverse-closed generating alphabet, choose a factor C different from both factor(a) and factor(b). Choose a common reduced suffix v of length L-1 beginning in C. If L is even the two length-L words av and b^{-1}v cannot be inverse fixed. If L is odd and at least five, choose v also ending in C: C,D,E,C is the length-four factor pattern and appending D,C adds two syllables while preserving reducedness. Then av and b^{-1}v have distinct first/last factors, hence are not inverse fixed. Their product av*(b^{-1}v)^{-1}=ab, even if a,b lie in the same factor and the product reduces. No group presentation/decidability assumption is involved.

4. The subgroup K generated by all products ab is normal: conjugating a generator by c gives (ca)(bc^{-1}), with both factors among the generators of K; inverse-closedness gives the reverse inclusion. Since B generates the original free product, its quotient by K is generated by a single element of order at most two: ab=1 for every a,b and a=a^{-1}. Thus K has index at most two and H0 contains K. A quotient image has index at most two, and adjoining the length-L+1 blocks cannot increase it. The triangular group maps onto that image because its retained relations were sampled relators.

5. Property (T) passes to quotient groups and from a subgroup of finite index to the ambient group, even when the ambient factors have no finite presentation. A factor map from any factor to an infinite amenable group is not a counterexample: random relations can kill its abelianization. Parity may leave a quotient C2; this is compatible with property (T) and is exactly why the proof only promises index at most two.

Preliminary assessment: no group-transfer obstruction found. Remaining checks: explicit finite torsion/parity witnesses, exact external triangular criterion and property (T) transfer hypotheses, and possible interactions with the remaining spectral/probability argument.
