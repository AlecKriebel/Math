## Exact claim and the source question

I first read the frozen `README.md`, `SOURCE_GATE.md`, and `PROOF.md`, then inspected Hayman–Lingham printed p143 / PDF p144 visually. Problem6.72 asks globally whether the arc omitted by a support point of normalized schlicht S has monotone polar argument, and whether its radius–tangent angle is monotone. The adjacent update records what was reported to the compilers; it is not a current-open certificate. [Primary problem collection](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07200).

The candidate is every global maximizer over S of

`Re[a2 + i 10^(-24)(a4 − 3a3)]`.

Its claimed result is nonmonotonicity on an actual omitted arc of polar argument, signed outward radial angle, and the ordinary nonnegative radial angle. Its distinguishing proof obligations are uniform control of every maximizer, a tip-radius bound, and identification of the tested regular trajectory with the actual omitted slit. A counterexample to tangent-direction monotonicity alone is insufficient.

Success for this priority family would be either (a) a complete primary earlier construction with an actual support realization and the requisite polar/radial sign reversals, or (b) a defensible source-based comparison of the relevant earlier constructions and their exact gaps. Abstracts, search snippets, catalogue status, and differences in functional notation are not sufficient.

Brown1985 deserves explicit method credit. The candidate's argument changes the available trajectory sign structure by using the fourth coefficient, then certifies a reversal with a natural-coordinate expansion and bounds. It does not originate the strategy of studying imaginary parts of Schiffer trajectory expressions. Likewise, support realization by maximizing a continuous functional, compactness of S, area-theorem controls, simple-pole uniqueness, and square-root natural coordinates should be described as established tools.

There is a precise limitation to functional-scope comparisons. As linear maps on H(D), a nonzero fourth-coefficient term cannot equal an `αa2+βa3` functional plus a constant, as evaluation on `z^4` shows. But different functionals can share a support point. The stronger exclusion here is Brown's positive polar theorem, conditional on its standard hypotheses, against the candidate's independently verified polar reversal. A mere difference in formula would not establish novelty.

In the candidate coordinates `w=−v^(−2)`, its differential is `4[1+iε(A−Bv²+v⁴)]dv²`, with `A≈−2`, `B≈3`. The proof's first-order angle is `β(t)=ε[t²−(2/5)t⁴]+o(ε)` on the identified actual arc. Brown's reciprocal differential for coefficient degree≤3 lacks that quartic term. This is a constructive comparison of mechanisms, not a claim that all possible degree-four arguments have been exhaustively classified.
