2026-10-15 UTC. Completion estimate: 12% of this final-publication audit.

I directly read the complete Szemberg OWR contribution pp1124–1126 from the cached primary source, plus the 2007v1 final challenge and the Primer2010v2 Q6.1.6 context. The exact target is a lower bound at an arbitrary point of a smooth minimal complex projective surface, for an ample integral line bundle, with denominator `2+|K²|^(1/4)`. The catalogue incorrectly encloses the 2 in the root. It is enough to find one singular curve with smaller quotient; neither an exact Seshadri constant nor a very-general-point failure is needed.

Independent mechanism and potential gaps: start with an integral plane degree-d pencil, with transverse simple base locus and a member with a high ordinary singular point away from it. Blow up all d² base points: write H for a line, E_i for exceptional sections, F=dH-sum E_i. Then F²=0, K_X=-3H+sum E_i. Choose a distinguished E and L0=E+2F. It has L0²=3 and L0.F=1. If all fibres are integral, every vertical curve has positive L0-degree; every horizontal curve other than E has positive F-degree and nonnegative E-degree, while L0.E=1. Nakai proves ampleness. This argument would fail on a reducible fibre not meeting E, so all-fibre integrality is essential.

Take a double cover of the base P1 branched in six smooth fibres, avoiding the special singular fibre; equivalently the smooth double cover of X branched in 6F, defined using O_X(3F). The base cover has genus 2. A local model z²=t at a smooth fibre implies the total space remains smooth. Need connectedness globally: geometrically integral generic fibre plus connected degree-two base extension prevents splitting. The canonical line bundle is q*(K_X+3F), with K_X+3F=(3d-3)H-2 sum E_i. For a strict transform of degree e with multiplicities m_i, a pencil member distinct from it gives sum m_i <= de by Bezout; hence (K_X+3F).C >= (d-3)e. On E_i the degree is 2, and its square is 5d²-18d+9>0 for d>=4. Thus it is ample and so is K_S; minimality and general type follow, with K_S²=10d²-36d+18. [This tentative arithmetic will be compared with the claimed construction, rather than assumed: it follows from six branch fibres.] More generally with four branch fibres K_X+2F has square 3d²-12d+9, K_S²=6d²-24d+18, but the base cover has genus 1. Both may work. On an unbranched special fibre each of its two lifts maps isomorphically to the original integral fibre, retaining singularity multiplicity d-1 and line-bundle degree one. Therefore epsilon <=1/(d-1). With four branch fibres, d=7 gives K²=144 and 1/6<1/(2+144^(1/4)); for all d>=7 the needed polynomial is (d-3)^4-(6d²-24d+18)>0. With six branch fibres, d=7 yields K²=256, giving equality and requiring d>=8 for strictness. Exact branch count is crucial.

The construction is promising, but the exact nonempty pencil parameter locus, connectedness of the chosen base change, and finite threshold have not yet been verified. A citation saying 'd sufficiently large' cannot establish d=7. This checkpoint does not certify novelty or priority. No prior audit verdict or paper proof was read to form this analysis.
