## Mathematical claim and adversarial reconstruction

The actual source is Szemberg's final question in arXiv:0711.0584v1 p7, repeated as OWR21/2009 Conjecture2 printed1124 and Primer0810.0728v2 Q6.1.6 p17. It asks for an arbitrary point and an ample integral line bundle on a smooth minimal complex projective surface, without a Picard-rank restriction. The correct denominator is `2+|K²|^(1/4)`. The catalogue's `fourthroot(2+|K²|)` transcription is different. The paper attacks the former, and supplies more than a transcription-only counterexample.

The strongest checked theorem is: for every integer d>=7, there exist smooth integral complex projective S, ample integral L and a point x, with K_S ample, K_S²=6(d−1)(d−3), L²=6, and an integral curve C' through x of L-degree1 and multiplicity d−1. Thus epsilon(S,L;x)<=1/(d−1)<1/(2+(K_S²)^(1/4)). The proof covers all d, not just a finite computation.

1. **Explicit curve.** For `f=Z(X^(d−1)−Y^(d−1))+X^d`, the coefficients as a linear polynomial in Z are coprime over C[X,Y]. Primitivity and Gauss's lemma prove global irreducibility. In the Z=1 chart the initial form is square-free of degree d−1. The partial derivatives exclude every other projective singularity. An ordinary multiple point can have several local branches while the projective curve remains integral; those assertions are not conflated.
2. **All-integral pencil at finite degree.** Each factor-split multiplication image is closed, with dimension at most N_a+N_(d−a), and deficit `a(d−a)>=d−1`. Their finite union includes repeated-factor/nonreduced forms. Its join to [f] has dimension at most N_d−d+2<N_d for d>=3. A second generator outside that proper closed locus makes every member integral. Smoothness, avoidance of p, and transversality to C0's smooth locus are compatible nonempty opens in the same irreducible parameter space: the complete degree-d system separates the relevant jets. Bezout then supplies d² distinct transverse simple base points away from p. This is a complex existence proof; neither a random sample nor Bauer's asymptotic degree sentence supplies d=7.
3. **One blowup per base point.** The two local generators are parameters u,v; the graph of their ratio is the blowup of (u,v). Every nonzero linear combination has multiplicity one there, and the exceptional curve is a section. Therefore fibres are exactly the integral reduced strict transforms, without hidden vertical exceptional components or infinitely near base points.
4. **A ample on every curve.** F is nef and A=2F+E1 has A²=3, A.E1=1, A.F=1. Every other horizontal integral curve has positive F-degree and nonnegative E1-intersection. A vertical integral curve is an entire reduced integral fibre and has A-degree1. Nakai applies to all curves. A reducible fibre component missed by E1 would invalidate this step; its exclusion was proved, not assumed.
5. **Actual global smooth integral projective cover.** Pick four distinct smooth fibre values away from C. The quartic double cover of P1 is connected of genus one. Its base change is finite flat of rank two. Off the branch fibres it is etale, including above singular fibres. At branch fibres the local equation t=v² in smooth coordinates makes the total space regular. The proper map to connected B has connected fibres, so the total space is connected; regular components cannot meet, giving integrality. The fibre product is projective. No resolution or contraction changes the asserted invariants.
6. **Canonical line bundle and every-curve ampleness.** This particular base cover uses O_P1(2), hence its pulled-back cover algebra uses O_Y(2F); its ramification line bundle is pi*O_Y(2F). Hurwitz gives `K_S=pi*(K_Y+2F)` as line bundles, with no torsion ambiguity or discrepancy at unbranched singular fibres. Put N=(d−3)H+F. Its degree is1 on each exceptional curve, positive on every other curve because H-degree>0 and F-degree>=0, and N²=3(d−1)(d−3)>0 for d>=4. Nakai and finite pullback give ample K_S and L. Adjunction excludes rational(−1)-curves; S itself is minimal of general type. Old exceptional sections lift to elliptic(−2)-curves with canonical degree2.
7. **One lifted fibre, not its total pullback.** At C's unbranched value the two reduced C-points of B give `pi^−1(C)=C' disjoint union C''`, each isomorphic to C as a scheme. Thus mult_x(C')=d−1 and projection gives L.C'=1. The total pullback has degree2; using it as one degree-two curve would be incorrect. These are actual admissible pointed curves in the Seshadri infimum.
8. **Strict comparison for every d.** Let d=u+7 with u>=0. The cubic difference `(d−3)^3−6(d−1)` is `u³+12u²+42u+28`, strictly positive. Equivalently the fourth-power margin is positive. At7, K²=144<256=4^4. The four-branch witnesses at4,5,6 have margins−17,−32,−9; that failure of this witness gives no lower bound or exact epsilon on those surfaces. Six branches instead give K²=256 at7 and equality, a useful negative control.

There is no fixed-surface vanishing, exact epsilon, very-general-point or rank-one counterexample claim. Canonical/adjoint bounds have different polarizations.
