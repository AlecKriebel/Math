# PR 585: independent citation-lineage and priority audit

This audit located no explicit earlier resolution of the precise minimal-surface question in the primary text inspected. Historical priority remains incomplete. The evidence supports preparing an accurately attributed counterexample note without asserting firstness, worldwide priority clearance, or that the question remained open immediately before this work.

This is a bounded literature audit. It is not a new mathematical proof search, a peer review by a human, or an independent certification of every theorem in the cited literature. The candidate's geometric correctness is evaluated separately by the root and mathematical review families.

## Independence and exact target

The source-first checkpoint was saved at 2026-10-15T06:32:07.025793Z before reading the candidate proof or its previous priority search. I first read all eight pages of Szemberg's arXiv:0711.0584v1 and all 37 pages of the Primer arXiv:0810.0728v2, and the mathematical text of the OWR contribution. I visually checked all three displayed target formulas. I subsequently read the OWR reference continuation and the candidate's PROOF.md and SOURCES.md. No other new review-family verdict was used to determine this priority conclusion.

The earliest formulation inspected is Szemberg's final question, preprint page 7, posted 5 November 2007, published in Journal of Algebra 319 (2008), 3112-3119, DOI [10.1016/j.jalgebra.2007.10.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2007.10.036). It asks for a minimal polarized surface and point violating the proposed bound. Szemberg restates the universal lower bound in Conjecture 2 of OWR 21/2009, printed page 1124, DOI [10.4171/OWR/2009/21](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2009/21). The Primer, Question 6.1.6, preprint page 17, repeats it for all minimal surfaces. The additive 2 is outside the fourth root:

    epsilon(S,L;x) >= 1 / (2 + |K_S^2|^(1/4)).

The setting is smooth complex projective surfaces, arbitrary Picard rank, ample integral line bundles, and arbitrary points. The catalogue's alternative placement of the additive 2 does not faithfully transcribe these sources. The candidate addresses the primary formula: its d=7 case has ample K, K^2=144, an ample integral L, and an integral curve of L-degree 1 with multiplicity 6. Thus its upper bound 1/6 is strictly less than 1/(2+144^(1/4)). An exact Seshadri value is not required.

The candidate's minimality upgrade is a connected double base change of the pencil branched at four smooth fibres away from the bad fibre. Its canonical formula and finite pullback of ampleness are standard cover theory. The note should credit Miranda and Bauer for the pencil, explicitly mention the close Bauer-Szemberg Proposition 2.1, and make no novelty assertion for branched covers, double base change, or their combination. Derivability from classical components is not, by itself, documentary evidence that the exact question had already been explicitly resolved.

## A misleading modern collision checked directly

The abstract of Garge-Pramanik's [arXiv:2202.08074v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08074v1), published as Bulletin des Sciences Mathematiques 182 (2023), 103209, could be mistaken for a stronger prior result because it announces vanishing global Seshadri constants. I read the introduction, conventions and complete Section 5, pp.9-11, rather than using the abstract as clearance. Corollary 5.2 requires closed points of unbounded residue-field degree. Over C every closed point has degree 1, so this hypothesis fails. That corollary is not a resolution of either the present complex minimal-surface bound or the fixed complex-surface positivity question. Incidental broad wording following that corollary is not used as a theorem with omitted hypotheses.

## Recommended publication framing

An accurate title can say that the note gives counterexamples to the stated minimal-surface bound. The text can decisively establish the displayed theorem while saying that its ingredients are classical and historical priority has not been fully cleared. Avoid a first solution claim, an assertion of novelty for the mechanism, or a claim that no previous counterexample exists. A suitable sentence is: "We record an explicit family of counterexamples to the proposed bound; the construction uses classical pencil and cover methods, and historical priority has not been fully cleared."

The theorem varies S with d. It does not settle whether one fixed complex surface has a positive infimum over all ample integral line bundles and all points. It also does not contradict very-general-point bounds, the Picard-rank-one theorem, canonical or adjoint lower bounds, or Nagata-Biran-Szemberg conjectures. It establishes only the upper Seshadri bound supplied by the exhibited curve. These distinctions should remain consistent in the abstract, introduction, conclusion, metadata, repository acceptance guide and spreadsheet notes.
