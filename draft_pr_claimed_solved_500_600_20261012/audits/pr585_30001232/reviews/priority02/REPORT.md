## Exact target and comparison criteria

I read the complete Szemberg contribution in [OWR 21/2009](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46224?nt=1), printed pages 1124–1126, and visually inspected Conjecture 2 on page 1124. For a smooth minimal complex projective surface S, every ample integral line bundle L and every point x, the proposed bound is

    epsilon(S,L;x) >= 1 / (2 + |K_S^2|^(1/4)).

There is no Picard-number-one restriction. The additive 2 lies outside the fourth root. The earlier final question on page 7 of [Szemberg, arXiv:0711.0584v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.0584v1) asks for a strict counterexample; [Primer, arXiv:0810.0728v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.0728v2), Question 6.1.6 on page 17, records the universal bound. The separate fixed-surface infimum and bounds at very general points must not be presented as the same question.

The candidate uses varying surfaces with ample K, L^2=6, K^2=6(d−1)(d−3), and a special pointed integral curve with L-degree 1 and multiplicity d−1. At d=7 this gives K^2=144 and epsilon<=1/6. An earlier construction would settle this target only if its minimality and numerical estimates actually imply a strict violation. An assertion that constants become arbitrarily small, without the canonical-square growth rate or minimality, is insufficient.

## Established construction lineage

**Miranda's pencil polarization is classical.** I obtained the author-hosted scan of [Ein–Lazarsfeld, *Seshadri constants on smooth surfaces*, Astérisque 218 (1993), 177–186](https://math.stonybrook.edu/robert.lazarsfeld/Reprints/Ein.Laz.Seshadri.Consts.Smooth.Sfs.pdf). Visual inspection of the complete Example 3.1 on printed page 182 shows the same mechanism: blow up the base points of an irreducible plane pencil, retain a singular fibre of large multiplicity, use an exceptional section, and polarize by aF+E for a>=2. The fibre has polarization degree 1. This is a direct early attribution to Miranda and should be cited alongside Bauer.

I also read the complete statement and proof of Proposition 5.12, preprint pages 29–30, in [Lazarsfeld, arXiv:alg-geom/9408011v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9408011v1). It records that same construction with Miranda's credit. The archive submission is 1994; the author's publication list identifies the later Park City volume as 1997. The 1993 conference date should not be silently used as the publication date of that volume.

In [Bauer, arXiv:math/9903072v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9903072v1), I read pages 1–14, including all of Proposition 3.3, its proof, the supporting Lemma 3.5 and adjacent remarks. This extends the mechanism to suitable blow-ups of arbitrary Picard-number-one surfaces. It still produces nonminimal surfaces with exceptional sections. The special case r=1, a=2 is exactly the candidate's A=2F+E on the intermediate surface. The degree relation d=m+1 is used asymptotically in Proposition 3.3(b); it should not be cited as the sole justification of the finite degree d=7. The candidate's independent pencil argument supplies that finite-degree step.

The candidate's additional operation is a double base change over four smooth fibres, with the singular fibre unbranched. Standard finite-cover, Hurwitz and intersection formulas then enter. What matters for preserving the small quotient is the explicit splitting into two curves mapping isomorphically to the original singular fibre; a general assertion about pullback of Seshadri constants would not replace this argument. No inspected source records this same four-fibre operation together with the precise minimal-surface counterexample. That absence is only a bounded search result. Neither the operation nor the ingredients should be described as new on this evidence.

## Close earlier constructions that do not establish this resolution

I read the full nine-page [Bauer–Szemberg, arXiv:0801.3245v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.3245v1), published as *Seshadri constants on surfaces of general type*, Manuscripta Math. 126 (2008), 167–175, DOI [10.1007/s00229-008-0170-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00229-008-0170-2). It studies the canonical polarization. Example 1.3 on page 6 is a genuinely relevant earlier branched-double-cover construction: a conic bundle gives a genus-two fibration with a nodal curve of canonical degree 1 and canonical quotient 1/2, while K^2 can grow. It does not give the arbitrary-L high-multiplicity quotient tending to zero. A quotient of 1/2 cannot contradict the target, whose proposed lower bound is at most 1/2. The existence of earlier general-type double-cover examples must nevertheless temper any construction novelty language.

Ein–Lazarsfeld Example 3.7, printed pages 184–185, is another relevant distinction. Kollár's double cover of E×E gives line bundles with unbounded indices of very ampleness. This does not mean their Seshadri constants approach zero. The relevant [Murayama dissertation, arXiv:1905.03839v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03839v1), printed pages 19–22, explicitly notes the lower bound 1/2 for the Kollár pullbacks, then reproduces the Miranda example and Bauer's blow-up generalization. I read those complete passages and scanned the entire extracted dissertation for relevant names and construction terms; I did not read all its technical chapters. Its displayed pencil dimension estimate is not used here as a proof premise.

I read all twelve pages of [Roy, arXiv:1905.10197v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10197v1). The first part classifies certain multipoint constants of K, and the second studies rationality and selected polarizations on C×C. No same minimalizing base change or explicit violation of the target appears there. I also read the full eight-page [Hanumanthu lecture notes dated 4 February 2017](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~mathsem/wsc2017/lectures-on-seshadri-constants.pdf); Theorem 2.11 and Lemma 2.12 again establish Miranda's nonminimal construction.

A neighboring paper, [Bauer–Di Rocco–Szemberg, arXiv:math/9806153v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9806153v1), studies cyclic covers and higher order embeddings. I read its introduction, initial main theorem and all final examples, and searched the full extracted text. Those passages concern jet and very ampleness, including abelian and del Pezzo examples, without the present target. I did not read the middle technical proofs completely. Its PDF displays a 2019 compilation date although the versioned archive records 1998; the archive date is the historical evidence.

Selected complete surface slides 7 and 8 in [Demailly's 31 July 2020 memorial lecture](https://mathweb.tifr.res.in/conferences/lecture-series/seshadri-lectures/demailly.pdf) separate varying-surface Miranda examples, fixed-surface positivity and the Picard-one theorem. These slides are context, not a certificate of the target's status through 2026.
