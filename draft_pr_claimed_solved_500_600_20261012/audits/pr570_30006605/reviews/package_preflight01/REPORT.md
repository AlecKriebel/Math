## Independent mathematical reconstruction

Let D=n−1. Connectivity and unit edges put every distance in the integer interval [0,D]. Nonnegative weights make the bounded indices satisfying k w(u)≤R an initial interval. Its largest index b_R(u) is well-defined for R≥0 and equals D when w(u)=0. For each pair x,u,

    w(u)d(x,u)≤R  iff  d(x,u)≤b_R(u)
                       iff  d(x,u)+(D−b_R(u))≤D.

Taking the conjunction proves equality of complete feasible sets with the additive eccentricity cutoff D. This identity works on any finite connected unit-edge graph; medianity is used exactly once, to invoke the fast additive algorithm. No graph alteration or geometric ball assertion enters it. Binary search on [0,D] constructs the integer labels in O(n log n) comparisons, with no unbounded floor or real division. Negative thresholds are infeasible. Singleton graph/support, zeros, equality thresholds and tied centers all behave correctly.

Every radius, hence its minimum, is one attained product k w(u). The conceptual n rows each have n entries, repetitions included. Initially B=D max w(u) is feasible and any vertex witnesses it. Retain the invariant: B is feasible, and B equals the optimum or an active entry equals the optimum. Feasible pivots save their value before deleting every entry at least that value; infeasible pivots delete entries at most that value. This preserves the invariant with exact equality and zero rows. An empty active set forces optimality; the saved witness is central, and the final threshold call returns precisely every center.

A middle of a length-L inclusive interval has at least L/2 entries on each weak side. A weighted median of these middles, weighted by L, places at least M/2 row mass on either weak side. The relevant deletion therefore removes at least M/4 entries. Starting at M=n² gives O(log n) calls. Sorting, interval searches and profile-threshold construction use O(n log n) operations per iteration, including bounded integer multiples with indices at most n−1. Combined with O(n log⁴ n) additive calls and the final complete-output call, this yields O(n log⁵(2n)). Median sparsity absorbs adjacency input. Optimum storage as (k,u) is exact. Rational comparisons use cross-products of O(B+log n) bits and require input-dependent bit cost, as the manuscript says.

## Complete imported proof and appendix repair

I independently read the additive interface and all relevant preliminaries/algorithm/proof in arXiv2410.10235v1 §§2–4, printed pp2–24. Its separate distance-oracle section is not needed. The exact relevant proof checks are:

1. Balanced halfspaces are nonempty gated median induced subgraphs. For x in one side, the maximum from the other side is its distance to that side's gate plus the computed additive eccentricity at the gate. Taking the maximum with its own-side value is exact.
2. With no balanced cut and f≥2 there are no egalitarian halfspaces. The majority rule for median graphs yields the unique common majority vertex v0.
3. Choosing v0 in a farthest-value tie gives the dominance branch: if a(v0)≥d(v0,u)+a(u) for every u, triangle inequality bounds every x,u contribution by d(x,v0)+a(v0), attained at u=v0.
4. The wide ladder is a nonempty set of pairwise orthogonal classes incident at v0 when its selected farthest vertex differs from v0; its cardinality ell is at most cube dimension, hence log_2 s.
5. The disjoint-ladder formula has a direct independent proof: if m is the median of (v0,x,u_max) and m differs from v0, the first edge of any v0–m geodesic belongs to a class in both ladders. Disjoint ladders force m=v0, so d(x,u_max)=d(x,v0)+d(v0,u_max). Triangle inequality and the farthest choice prove the corresponding global eccentricity exactly. No balance constant is used.
6. Successive large sets are intersections of majority halfspaces and retain v0. A slice intersects the next minority with earlier majorities, so it is gated. Incident orthogonal classes at v0 supply a cube and a vertex with exactly the next minority coordinate, showing every slice is nonempty. Every actual recursive graph is therefore connected, gated and median. The source's unnecessarily broader arbitrary-isometric-subgraph wording is not needed.
7. For a slice within its current large set, gate fibers partition its exterior. Boundary weights encode the maximum distance-plus-arrival contribution in each nonempty outside fiber; nonboundary values retain original weights. A boundary fiber value can be smaller than its original arrival value. Keeping both original-weight and fiber-weight calls, exactly as the source does, is essential.
8. For an inside-slice x, the maximum of these two slice eccentricity outputs equals its eccentricity in the current large set: gatedness factors every outside contribution through its gate. Later-slice outputs merge their already computed remainder value with distance-to-gate plus the original-weight slice eccentricity. The final large-set outputs use item5. This is exact for zero weights and ties.
9. The source's nonrecursive work is O(s log²(2s)): cited structural preprocessing, at most log_2 s ladder-size operations, and BFS/scans for each slice. Reweighting does not require a new hidden oracle.
