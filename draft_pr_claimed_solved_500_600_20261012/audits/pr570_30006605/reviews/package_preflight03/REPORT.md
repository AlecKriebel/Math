## Universal bridge and search

For D=n−1 every distance is an integer in [0,D]. For R>=0, b_R(u)=max{k in [0,D]: k*w(u)<=R} is defined even for zero weights; then a_R(u)=D−b_R(u) is a nonnegative integer at most D. Integrality and nonnegativity give, pointwise,

    w(u)*d(x,u)<=R  iff  d(x,u)<=b_R(u)
                           iff  d(x,u)+a_R(u)<=D.

Conjunction over all u proves equality of complete feasible sets. This bridge holds on every finite connected unit-edge graph; medianity enters only the additive oracle. The graph and zero-weight vertices are retained. Equality thresholds are exact. Bounded binary search supplies the labels with O(n log(2n)) original-weight comparisons and requires neither real division nor an unbounded floor.

Every attained radius, hence the minimum, belongs to the n conceptual rows (0,w(u),...,D*w(u)). The incumbent D*max(w) is feasible with any vertex as witness. The invariant is that the incumbent is feasible, and either equals the optimum or some active row entry is optimum. A feasible pivot is saved before deleting the weak upper side; an infeasible pivot deletes the weak lower side. Thus equality, tied rows and zero rows preserve the invariant. An empty active set forces incumbent optimality. Its saved witness is a center; the final complete feasible-set query returns all centers.

A lower-middle row entry has at least half its inclusive length on each weak side. Weighting middle entries by active row lengths makes either branch delete at least one quarter of the total active multiplicity. Starting at n^2 therefore gives O(log(2n)) calls. Sorting and bounded row deletions contribute O(n log^2(2n)) total comparison overhead. With O(n log^4(2n)) additive calls, including preprocessing and the final output call, the total is O(n log^5(2n)). Median sparsity m<=n log_2(n) absorbs adjacency input. The optimum can be stored as (k,u). Rational comparisons use cross-products on O(B+log n)-bit operands; bit and input-reading costs depend on the encoding.
