## Mathematical mechanism and its classical scope

For any family of maps `q_r:G→Q_r`, a persistent `F₂` would embed into every `Q_r`. Consequently one quotient with no `F₂` suffices to obstruct it. A solvable or virtually solvable quotient has no such subgroup. In the virtually solvable case, intersect a putative `F₂` with a solvable subgroup of finite index; Schreier's formula makes that intersection a finite-rank free group of rank at least two, while a subgroup of a solvable group is solvable, a contradiction.

The candidate needs only the metabelian special case. For a free basis `a,b` of any proposed `H`, put `c=[a,b]`, `d=aca⁻¹`, and `w=[c,d]`. Both images of `c,d` lie in the abelian derived subgroup of a metabelian quotient, so `q(w)=1`. In free letters with uppercase denoting inverses, `w` reduces to `xyXYxxyXYXyxxYXX`, a nonempty reduced word. Thus `w≠1` in `H`. Equivalently, `H''` is nontrivial and maps to the identity. This is an elementary group-law argument, not a new general group-theoretic principle.

The figure-eight zero filling is a torus bundle, so its group is an extension `Z²→Q→Z` and hence metabelian. Its infinitude is consistent with the no-finite-surgery hypothesis. The classical complete filling classification and the hyperbolic triangle bases for the remaining exceptional fillings verify that the figure-eight meets that hypothesis at every rational slope, not just at zero. Those ingredients were re-read at the specific locations in the comparison table. The actual rank-two conclusion is then a deduction: for every `H≅F₂`, `H∩N₀≠{1}`. It does not follow that every hyperbolic knot fails, and it does not supply a positive hyperbolic example.

No additional numerical controls were needed for this priority task. Finite searches or model evaluations cannot prove historical priority; the algebraic implication above is a proof, whereas the historical search is bounded evidence.
