## Exact target and success criterion

OWR 8/2020, DOI 10.4171/OWR/2020/8, printed p.493: for an arbitrary hyperbolic knot K with no finite nonmeridional surgery, does there exist H ≤ G(K), H isomorphic to F2, with H ∩ (union over rational r of normal closure of r) = {1}? Each nonidentity h must survive every rational filling. The meridional filling infinity is excluded. Rational zero is included. A product-map injection (intersection of all kernels trivial) is strictly weaker and does not suffice.

2026 arXiv:2604.01697v1, Definition 1.5 calls a hyperbolic knot torsion-free when it has neither finite nor reducing surgery. This is a standing domain for its main minimizer/abundance theorems, not a condition excluding infinite solvable fillings. §7.1 defines a persistent subgroup by persistence of every nonidentity element, then says no hyperbolic examples are known and asks Q7.1 for a hyperbolic K. The displayed question omits the no-finite-surgery qualification; the preceding cyclic-subgroup result supplies a necessary restriction. Context includes an existential search for any hyperbolic positive example, which a negative example does not settle.

## What would count as an exact priority collision

An earlier dated primary source must explicitly state the full target has a negative answer, identify the eligible figure-eight knot as lacking a rank-two persistent free subgroup, or state a general no-F2 obstruction from a solvable/metabelian filling and instantiate an eligible hyperbolic knot (or otherwise expressly resolve the literal universal target). An explicit general obstruction plus a visibly supplied eligible instance is a particularly strong near-collision even without the old question number.

Classical knowledge that figure-eight zero surgery is a torus bundle, together with the elementary fact that a free nonabelian group cannot embed in a solvable group, establishes derivability. Derivability alone is not evidence of an explicit earlier resolution. Results for cyclic persistent subgroups, existence of persistent elements in arbitrary subgroups, no persistence in finite surgery, and injectivity of a product of fillings do not answer the rank-two/all-fillings question. Existence of a persistent free subgroup in some satellite groups also does not answer the hyperbolic question.
