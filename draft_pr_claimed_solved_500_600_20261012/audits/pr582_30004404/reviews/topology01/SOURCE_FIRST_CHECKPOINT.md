## Exact 2020 target

Primary: Kimihiko Motegi, joint work with Tetsuya Ito and Masakazu Teragaito, "Dehn fillings on knot groups," in Oberwolfach Report 8/2020, printed pp. 491-493, final question p. 493; DOI 10.4171/OWR/2020/8. Full original is https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46844 . Read complete pp. 491-493; visually checked final p. 493 (PDF page 29).

Let K range over hyperbolic knots in S^3 with no finite nonmeridional surgery. For the preferred meridian-longitude framing, define N_r = normal closure of mu^p lambda^q when r=p/q is rational in lowest terms. The requested universal statement is:

    for every such K, there is H <= pi_1(E(K)) with H isomorphic to F_2
    and H intersect (union over r in Q of N_r) = {1}.

Equivalently every rational filling quotient must restrict injectively to that single H. This is a union, not an intersection of kernels. Slopes include 0=0/1 and exclude infinity=1/0. The preceding source text states the finite-surgery obstruction; thus the no-finite-surgery hypothesis means no rational surgery with finite fundamental group, not literally no meridional filling (which would be impossible for any knot in S^3). A counterexample requires one admissible K for which every H isomorphic to F_2 has a nonidentity element killed by at least one rational filling.

## Exact later wording and hypothesis reconciliation

Primary: Ito, Motegi, Teragaito, arXiv:2604.01697v1, 2 April 2026, full PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01697v1 and HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01697v1 . Read pp. 1-4 and 16-18, with pp. 16-17 visually checked, including full Section 7.1 and its footnote.

The definition in Section 1.1 uses S(g) subset Q, and Section 1.2 calls a nonidentity g persistent precisely when S(g) is empty. Thus zero is included and infinity excluded. Section 7.1 defines a persistent subgroup by requiring all its nonidentity elements to be persistent. It observes that a nontrivial cyclic persistent subgroup exists exactly when no finite surgery exists, citing its reference [18]. The authors then discuss positive satellite examples and the absence, to their knowledge, of positive hyperbolic examples. Question 7.1 (p. 17) fixes a hyperbolic K and asks for a persistent rank-two free subgroup. It includes no printed no-finite-surgery hypothesis and no exclusion of any rational slope. The torsion-free-hyperbolic hypothesis introduced in Definition 1.5 (p. 3) is explicitly imposed on intervening theorems, but is not printed in Question 7.1; it cannot be silently added there.

Consequently the fixed-arbitrary-K reading is universal and a counterexample answers it negatively, including the restricted no-finite-surgery 2020 reading. However, its preceding motivation also raises the weaker existential problem of whether any hyperbolic knot supplies a positive example. One negative example cannot settle that weaker problem. This contextual tension must be reported, not resolved by changing the definition of persistence or dropping zero surgery. The 2026 paper itself states (proof of Lemma 2.4, p. 7) that the figure-eight knot has no finite surgery; this is a corroborating assertion, not the complete classical source proof audited here.

Theorem 4.7 supplies a hyperbolic metric for all figure-eight Dehn fillings except the unoriented symmetry classes represented by (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(4,1). For rational slopes this leaves exactly 0,+/-1,+/-2,+/-3,+/-4. The text explicitly identifies the sign symmetry from an orientation-reversing homeomorphism. The orbifold Euler characteristics are -1/42,-1/20,-1/12, so each base triangle group is infinite and a quotient of the surgery group. This supplies the missing distinction between Seifert and finite.

## Independent deductions available from those imports

For every rational r outside the nine-element list, the closed filled manifold has a hyperbolic metric, hence infinite fundamental group: a closed hyperbolic manifold is complete and its universal cover is noncompact H^3, which cannot be the finite-sheeted cover of a compact manifold. At 0,+/-4 an incompressible two-sided torus injects Z^2, again excluding finiteness. At +/-1,+/-2,+/-3 the surgery group surjects onto the relevant hyperbolic triangle orbifold group, excluding finiteness. This partitions every rational slope, not merely a bounded sample.

For any torus bundle over S^1, its group has an exact sequence Z^2 -> Q -> Z. Therefore Q' lies in an abelian normal subgroup and Q''=1. For any H isomorphic to F_2, H'' is nontrivial (indeed nonabelian), and every homomorphism H -> Q kills H''. Thus the zero filling alone would obstruct the requested persistent F_2, once the candidate's use of this mechanism and all its framing choices are inspected. No injectivity of the entire fiber subgroup in the original knot group is needed for this argument; only a genuine embedded H isomorphic to F_2 and a homomorphism to the metabelian quotient are needed.
