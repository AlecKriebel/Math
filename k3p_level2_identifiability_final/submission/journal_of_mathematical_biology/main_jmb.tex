\begin{abstract}
We classify robust stochastic ambiguity for binary standard semi-directed
strongly tree-child level-2 phylogenetic networks under the Kimura
three-parameter model. On the principal positive Fourier domain, one network
has a regular full-dimensional analytic containment germ in another if and
only if their labelled topologies agree after independent redirection of
ordinary three-cycle factors. The same criterion characterizes a shared
full-dimensional germ and remains exact under strict continuous-time edge
parameters. Consequently, topology is generically identifiable and, outside
the same proper exceptional set, exactly reconstructible modulo this explicit
triangle ambiguity.

The proof combines positive bridge factorization, physical marginal
submersions, a noncircular bridge-split transfer theorem, and finite exact
classification. Unlike the corresponding reduced-symmetry models, the three
ordinary triangle orientations have normalized rank 14 and share an
irreducible quartic hypersurface in the 15-dimensional three-leaf Fourier
chart. The bridge argument does not assume a universal pointwise noncut rank
criterion; it uses generic noncut detection and 204 exact one-active
obstructions under containment. The remaining local census has fourteen
four-port relation orbits, all separated by polynomial or directed-rank
certificates. Exact restoration and coherent probes globalize the result.

The strong tree-child hypothesis is sharp: for every number of leaves at
least three, a weakly but not strongly tree-child pair shares a strict
continuous-time full-dimensional germ of dimension \(6n-3\). Exact arithmetic,
independent replay implementations, rigorous interval bounds, and fail-closed
mutation tests accompany the computer-assisted steps.
\end{abstract}

\section{Biological interpretation and accessibility}

Phylogenetic networks model histories in which lineages can reticulate as
well as split. In biological terms, the theorem asks an infinite-data
question: if every site-pattern probability were known exactly under K3P,
could a different level-2 reticulation topology explain an open family of the
same observations? Within the strongly tree-child class, the answer is no
except for redirecting an ordinary three-cycle, an ambiguity that preserves
the relevant local observational family.

This identifies a sharp structural boundary, not a claim about practical
sample size. Strong tree-childness prevents the local compensations that
otherwise hide topology; immediately outside that class, the certified weak
examples share a robust full-dimensional family. K3P matters because its
three substitution sectors vary independently, providing greater biological
flexibility than JC or K2P while changing the local triangle from an open
ambient family to a quartic hypersurface.

The result is an information theorem rather than an inference pipeline. It
does not estimate parameters, quantify finite-sequence error, cover model
misspecification, or guarantee numerical conditioning. Those questions are
necessary before applying the exact reconstruction statement to empirical
sequence data.
