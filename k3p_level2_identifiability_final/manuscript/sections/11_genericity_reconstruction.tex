\section{Genericity and reconstruction}\label{sec:genericity}

Let

\[
 \V_N=\overline{\Phi_N(\C^{m_N})}^{\,Z}
\]

be the complex Zariski closure of the polynomial K3P map.  It is irreducible
as the closure of the image of an irreducible affine space.  Let \(r\) be the
generic complex Jacobian rank.  Every Jacobian minor has real-polynomial
coefficients and some \(r\)-minor is nonzero.  Such a polynomial cannot vanish
throughout the Euclidean-open physical domain, so \(d_N\ge r\); the reverse
inequality is immediate.  The generic-rank theorem therefore gives
\(\dim\V_N=r=d_N\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:generic}]
For fixed leaf count \(n\), only finitely many labelled networks occur in the
class.  Indeed, choosing tree or leaf children from the root and from each
reticulation produces \(r+1\) disjoint terminal tree paths, so \(r\le n-1\).
If \(t\) is the number of nonroot tree vertices, degree counting gives
\(t=n+r-2\), and the rooted presentation has

\[
 1+t+r+n=2n+2r-1\le4n-3
\]

vertices.

For a competitor \(N'\not\trianglerel N\), put
\[
 C_{N,N'}=\M_{3,+}(N)\cap\M_{3,+}(N')
\]
and form the physical target incidence correspondence
\[
 Z_{N'}=\{(q,\theta'):
 q=\Phi_{N'}(\theta'),\ q\in\M_{3,+}(N),\
 \theta'\in\Theta_{3,+}(N')\}.
\]
These sets and their projections are semialgebraic by Tarski--Seidenberg
\cite[Theorem~2.2.1]{BochnakCosteRoy1998}.  Define the total source rank-drop
locus
\[
 R_N=\{\theta\in\Theta_{3,+}(N):
 \rank D\Phi_N(\theta)<d_N\}.
\]
Choose a finite Nash stratification
\(R_N=\bigsqcup_\alpha S_\alpha\) on which every restricted map
\(\Phi_N|_{S_\alpha}\) has constant rank.  Since
\(T_\theta S_\alpha\subseteq T_\theta\Theta_{3,+}(N)\),
\[
 \rank D(\Phi_N|_{S_\alpha})(\theta)
 \le \rank D\Phi_N(\theta)\le d_N-1.
\]
The constant-rank theorem and the fact that semialgebraic maps do not
increase dimension give
\(\dim\Phi_N(R_N)\le d_N-1\)
\cite[Theorem~2.8.8]{BochnakCosteRoy1998}.  Thus a
\(d_N\)-dimensional physical intersection cannot be confined to the image of
rank-deficient source parameters.

Stratify the regular source image, the target parameter space, and
\(Z_{N'}\) compatibly with all relevant rank loci, refining so that every
restricted projection has constant rank.  If \(C_{N,N'}\) had dimension
\(d_N\), then \cref{lem:finite-cover} would place a nonempty relatively open
germ of a regular \(d_N\)-dimensional source-image stratum in that
intersection.  Some incidence stratum would project onto a smaller such germ
with rank \(d_N\).  The analytic constant-rank theorem supplies a physical
real-analytic right inverse
\[
 s:q\longmapsto\theta'(q),\qquad
 \Phi_{N'}(s(q))=q,
\]
after shrinking the output germ.  Choose a regular source point above it and
shrink a source-parameter neighborhood \(U\) so that \(\Phi_N(U)\) lies in
the section domain.  Then
\[
 \sigma=s\circ\Phi_N:U\longrightarrow\Theta_{3,+}(N')
\]
is physical and real analytic and satisfies
\(\Phi_N=\Phi_{N'}\circ\sigma\) on \(U\).  This is exactly
\(N\preceqplus N'\), contradicting \cref{thm:main}.  Hence every competing
physical intersection has semialgebraic dimension at most \(d_N-1\).

By \cite[Proposition~2.8.2]{BochnakCosteRoy1998}, a semialgebraic set and its
real Zariski closure have the same dimension; finite unions take the maximum
of the member dimensions
\cite[Proposition~2.8.5(i)]{BochnakCosteRoy1998}.  For completeness, if
\(A=\R[x]/I_\R(C)\) is the coordinate ring of such a real closure, then
complexification gives the finite faithfully flat integral extension
\(A\to A\otimes_\R\C\), which preserves Krull dimension.  Therefore the
complex Zariski closures of both \(C_{N,N'}\) and \(\Phi_N(R_N)\) inside
\(\V_N\) have dimension at most \(d_N-1\) and are proper.

Define \(E_N\subset\V_N\) to be the following finite union (there are only
finitely many binary network topologies on the fixed labelled leaf set):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item the complex Zariski closure in \(\V_N\) of \(\Phi_N(R_N)\);
  \item the complex Zariski closures in \(\V_N\) of \(C_{N,N'}\), over all
        competitors \(N'\not\trianglerel N\);
  \item the singular locus of \(\V_N\); and
  \item the finitely many reconstruction-test hypersurfaces
        \(\V_N\cap Z(P)\) used by R1--R8 for which
        \(P\circ\Phi_N\) is certified nonidentically zero on the intended
        source stratum.
\end{enumerate}
Each member is proper.  A finite union of proper closed sets cannot fill the
irreducible variety \(\V_N\), so \(E_N\) is proper.  Outside it, every
realizing topology is triangle-equivalent to \(N\).
\end{proof}

The generic cut step in this proof uses \cref{prop:generic-noncut}, not a
universal pointwise converse.  Since there are finitely many candidate splits
for fixed \(N\), all noncut minors are simultaneously nonzero outside a
proper algebraic subset.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:reconstruction}]
Given an exact Fourier tensor outside \(E_N\), apply the following finite
procedure.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*.}]
  \item Fourier-transform the exact pattern tensor.
  \item Test the finitely many split flattenings.  By
        \cref{prop:true-cut,prop:generic-noncut}, recover the compatible
        bridge-split system and build the labelled abstract bridge-incidence
        tree.
  \item Use the six-circuit sum of squares to distinguish ordinary
        degree-three components from ordinary sunlets.  This test precedes
        bridge normalization because every circuit is a semi-invariant under
        the positive incidence gauges: both monomials acquire the same
        nonzero factor, so its zero/nonzero value is already defined on the
        projective local tensor orbit.
  \item Factor positive bridge blocks in the \(C,G,T\) sectors and impose
        \eqref{eq:bridge-normalizer}; recover each projective local tensor
        orbit and normalized effective bridge spectrum.
  \item Enumerate the finitely many rigid-support candidates compatible with
        the recovered tree and apply the exact pointwise, quartic, and rank
        tests.
  \item Follow only the exact fixed-full restoration records and their stored
        transports.
  \item Use the one-port segment deck and two-port order deck to recover every
        labelled subdivision word.  No selected tensor uses more than nine
        physical ports.
  \item Assemble coherent mixed-graph candidates and group them into ordinary
        triangle classes.  For each finite class \(\mathcal C\), decide the
        existential semialgebraic statement

        \[
          q\in\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal C}\M_{3,+}(H).
        \]

        Exactly one class is feasible outside \(E_N\); return its canonical
        representative.
\end{enumerate}

Every loop is finite, and real-closed-field quantifier elimination terminates
under the exact-input convention.  The output is a topology class, not a
numerically stable estimator and not a recovery of individual edge parameters
inside the bridge fibre.
\end{proof}
