\section{Genericity and reconstruction}\label{sec:genericity}

Let

\[
 \V_N=\overline{\Phi_N(\C^{m_N})}^{\,Z}
\]

be the complex Zariski closure of the polynomial K3P map.  It is irreducible
as the closure of the image of an irreducible affine space.  Its dimension is
the generic complex Jacobian rank and also \(d_N\), because a nonzero real
Jacobian minor cannot vanish throughout the Euclidean-open physical domain.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:generic}]
For fixed leaf count \(n\), only finitely many labelled networks occur in the
class.  Indeed, choosing tree or leaf children from the root and from each
reticulation produces \(r+1\) disjoint terminal tree paths, so \(r\le n-1\).
If \(t\) is the number of nonroot tree vertices, degree counting gives
\(t=n+r-2\), and the rooted presentation has

\[
 1+t+r+n=2n+2r-1\le4n-3
\]

vertices.

For a competitor \(N'\not\trianglerel N\), the physical intersection
\(\M_{3,+}(N)\cap\M_{3,+}(N')\) is semialgebraic by Tarski--Seidenberg
\cite{BochnakCosteRoy1998}.  If it had dimension \(d_N\), a finite Nash
stratification compatible with the source and target rank loci would contain
a \(d_N\)-dimensional regular source-image stratum and an incidence stratum
projecting with rank \(d_N\).  The analytic constant-rank theorem would give
a physical target section over a source-relative open regular germ, hence
\(N\preceqplus N'\), contradicting \cref{thm:main}.  Every inequivalent
intersection therefore has dimension at most \(d_N-1\).

Take the finite union of the complex Zariski closures of these intersections,
the singular locus of \(\V_N\), the closure of the source rank-drop image,
and the certified reconstruction-test zero loci that are nonidentically zero
on the intended source stratum.  Semialgebraic dimension agrees with the
dimension of its real Zariski closure, and extension from \(\R\) to \(\C\)
preserves dimension.  Every member is proper; a finite union of proper closed
sets cannot fill irreducible \(\V_N\).  Call the union \(E_N\).  Outside it,
every realizing topology is triangle-equivalent to \(N\).
\end{proof}

The generic cut step in this proof uses \cref{prop:generic-noncut}, not a
universal pointwise converse.  Since there are finitely many candidate splits
for fixed \(N\), all noncut minors are simultaneously nonzero outside a
proper algebraic subset.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:reconstruction}]
Given an exact Fourier tensor outside \(E_N\), apply the following finite
procedure.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*.}]
  \item Fourier-transform the exact pattern tensor.
  \item Test the finitely many split flattenings.  By
        \cref{prop:true-cut,prop:generic-noncut}, recover the compatible
        bridge-split system and build the labelled reduced tree.
  \item Use the six-circuit sum of squares to distinguish ordinary
        degree-three components from ordinary sunlets.
  \item Factor positive bridge blocks in the \(C,G,T\) sectors and impose
        \eqref{eq:bridge-normalizer}; recover each projective local tensor
        orbit and normalized effective bridge spectrum.
  \item Enumerate the finitely many rigid-support candidates compatible with
        the recovered tree and apply the exact pointwise, quartic, and rank
        tests.
  \item Follow only the exact fixed-full restoration records and their stored
        transports.
  \item Use the one-port segment deck and two-port order deck to recover every
        labelled subdivision word.  No selected tensor uses more than nine
        physical ports.
  \item Assemble coherent mixed-graph candidates and group them into ordinary
        triangle classes.  For each finite class \(\mathcal C\), decide the
        existential semialgebraic statement

        \[
          q\in\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal C}\M_{3,+}(H).
        \]

        Exactly one class is feasible outside \(E_N\); return its canonical
        representative.
\end{enumerate}

Every loop is finite, and real-closed-field quantifier elimination terminates
under the exact-input convention.  The output is a topology class, not a
numerically stable estimator and not a recovery of individual edge parameters
inside the bridge fibre.
\end{proof}
