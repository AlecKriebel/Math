# Minimality and complexity analysis

This note gives only lower bounds supported by first-principles proofs.  It
makes no claim that the present ten-complex support or its ten reversible
pairs are globally minimal.

Throughout, inert complexes and reactions of the form \(y\to y\) are deleted.
Write

- \(n\) for the number of species;
- \(s=\dim S\) for the stoichiometric dimension;
- \(m\) for the number of complexes;
- \(\ell\) for the number of linkage classes; and
- \(\delta=m-\ell-s\) for the deficiency.

The target property is a positive-dimensional semialgebraic equilibrium
subset in one positive compatibility class together with
\(\gcd(F_1,\ldots,F_n)=1\).

## 1. Two elementary algebraic lemmas

### Lemma 1: codimension one forces a common factor

Let \(R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), let
\(I=(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\ne(0)\), and suppose the real common-zero set contains a
semialgebraic subset of dimension at least \(n-1\).  Then the \(f_i\) have a
nonconstant common factor.

Indeed, a semialgebraic cell of dimension \(d\) has a coordinate projection
containing an open subset of \(\mathbb R^d\); no algebraic set of dimension
less than \(d\) can contain such a cell.  Thus the Zariski closure of a real
semialgebraic set cannot have smaller dimension than that set.  Hence some
prime over \(I\) has height at most one.
Height zero would force \(I=(0)\), contrary to the hypothesis.  A height-one
prime in the UFD \(R\) is generated by an irreducible polynomial \(g\), and
\(I\subseteq(g)\), so \(g\mid f_i\) for every \(i\).

Equivalently, if the nonzero coordinates of a rational polynomial field are
coprime, its common-zero set has dimension at most \(n-2\).  The statement is
unchanged if some coordinate is zero.  If all coordinates are zero, the
target already fails.

### Lemma 2: stoichiometric rank one forces a common scalar

If \(s=0\), every reaction vector and hence the entire field is zero.  If
\(s=1\), choose a nonzero rational vector \(v\) spanning \(S\).  Since
every reaction vector is proportional to \(v\), the mass-action field has the
form

\[
F(x)=v f(x)
\]

for one polynomial \(f\in\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\).  A positive equilibrium
forces either \(f=0\) or \(f\) to be nonconstant.  Thus a nonzero field with a
positive equilibrium has a nonconstant common factor.  Consequently every
target construction, with any number of species and without any
reversibility assumption, must have

\[
s\ge2.
\]

## 2. Three species are unconditionally minimal

### Theorem 1

No one- or two-species polynomial mass-action system can have the target
property.  Hence the present three-species construction is species-minimal.

### Proof

For one species, \(s\le1\), so Lemma 2 applies (with the zero field treated
separately).

For two species, Lemma 2 excludes \(s\le1\).  If \(s=2\), a positive
compatibility class is open in \(\mathbb R_{>0}^2\).  A positive-dimensional
equilibrium continuum therefore has dimension at least one, which is
\(n-1\).  Lemma 1 gives a nonconstant common factor unless the field is zero.
This contradicts the target in every case.  No weak-reversibility or linkage
hypothesis was used.  \(\square\)

## 3. One linkage class forces full rank in three species

### Theorem 2

Let a three-species mass-action network have one linkage class.  If one
compatibility class contains a positive-dimensional semialgebraic equilibrium
set and the field is nonzero and coprime, then

\[
s=3.
\]

This statement needs only connectedness of the underlying complex graph, not
weak reversibility.

### Proof

The zero field excludes \(s=0\), and Lemma 2 excludes \(s=1\), so it remains
to exclude \(s=2\).  Choose a
primitive nonzero integer vector \(w\) spanning \(S^\perp\).  Such a vector
exists because reaction vectors are integral.  For every reaction
\(y\to y'\),

\[
w\mathbin\cdot y'=w\mathbin\cdot y.
\]

Connectivity propagates this equality through the unique linkage class, so
there is one integer \(d\) with \(w\cdot y=d\) for every complex.  Define, for
\(\lambda>0\),

\[
T_\lambda(x)_i=\lambda^{w_i}x_i.
\]

Every source monomial has the same weight, and hence

\[
F(T_\lambda x)=\lambda^dF(x).
\]

The positive equilibrium set is therefore invariant under this one-parameter
action.

Let \(M\) be a positive-dimensional smooth semialgebraic stratum of the given
equilibrium continuum.  Every tangent vector to \(M\) lies in
\(S=\ker(w\cdot -)\).  The infinitesimal action direction at \(p\in M\) is

\[
u(p)=(w_1p_1,w_2p_2,w_3p_3),
\]

and

\[
w\cdot u(p)=\sum_iw_i^2p_i>0.
\]

Thus the action direction is transverse to the compatibility plane.  The map
\((p,\lambda)\mapsto T_\lambda p\) has differential rank at least two along
\(M\times\{1\}\).  Its equilibrium image consequently contains a
two-dimensional semialgebraic subset.  Lemma 1, now with \(n=3\), forces a
common factor unless \(F=0\).  Both alternatives contradict the hypothesis.
Therefore \(s=3\).  \(\square\)

The one-linkage assumption is essential to this proof: with several linkage
classes the constants \(w\cdot y\) may differ between classes, so the sum of
their vector fields need not be weighted homogeneous.  No claim is made here
that a three-species, multiple-linkage, rank-two example is impossible.

## 4. Deficiency zero cannot support the target

The following proof also supplies the cleanest complex-count bound.

### Lemma 3: the linkage Laplacian

For a weakly reversible linkage class with \(q\) complexes and positive rate
constants, its kinetic Laplacian \(A_\kappa\) has rank \(q-1\), image equal to
the zero-coordinate-sum subspace, and a one-dimensional kernel generated by a
strictly positive vector.

One direct proof expands a principal \((q-1)\)-minor.  After cancellation, it
is the sum of the positive weights of directed spanning trees oriented toward
the deleted vertex.  Strong connectivity supplies such a tree for every
root, so every principal cofactor is positive.  The column-sum relation gives
rank at most \(q-1\), the cofactor gives the reverse inequality, and the
corresponding adjugate column gives a strictly positive kernel vector.  This
is a finite determinant identity, not a generic-rate argument.

### Theorem 3

Every weakly reversible mass-action network with \(\delta=0\) has at most one
positive equilibrium in each stoichiometric compatibility class.  Therefore
every weakly reversible network satisfying the target has

\[
\delta\ge1.
\]

### Proof

Let \(Y:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n\) send the standard basis vector indexed by
a complex to that complex.  Let

\[
H=\{u\in\mathbb R^m:
  \text{the coordinates of }u\text{ sum to zero in each linkage class}\}.
\]

Then \(\dim H=m-\ell\), \(Y(H)=S\), and
\(\delta=\dim\ker(Y|_H)\).  If \(\delta=0\), the restriction \(Y|_H\) is
injective.

The mass-action field is

\[
F(x)=YA_\kappa\Psi(x),
\qquad \Psi(x)=(x^{y_1},\ldots,x^{y_m}).
\]

Lemma 3 gives \(A_\kappa\Psi(x)\in H\).  At an equilibrium, injectivity of
\(Y|_H\) implies

\[
A_\kappa\Psi(x)=0.
\]

Thus every positive equilibrium is complex-balanced.  If \(x,x'\) are two
such equilibria, Lemma 3 says that on each linkage class the two monomial
vectors are proportional.  Hence

\[
(y-y')\cdot(\log x-\log x')=0
\]

for every pair of complexes in the same linkage class.  These differences
span \(S\), so \(\log x-\log x'\in S^\perp\).  If the two points are also in
one compatibility class, then \(x-x'\in S\), and therefore

\[
0=(x-x')\cdot(\log x-\log x')
  =\sum_i(x_i-x_i')(\log x_i-\log x_i').
\]

Every summand is nonnegative and is zero exactly when \(x_i=x_i'\), because
the logarithm is strictly increasing.  Thus \(x=x'\).  A positive-dimensional
equilibrium subset in one class is impossible.  \(\square\)

## 5. Exact complex and reaction lower bounds

### Theorem 4

Every three-species weakly reversible network satisfying the target has at
least five complexes.  If it has one linkage class, it also has full
stoichiometric rank.

### Proof

Theorem 1 and Lemma 2 give \(s\ge2\), while Theorem 3 gives
\(\delta=m-\ell-s\ge1\).  Consequently \(m\ge4\).  If \(m=4\), the same
inequality forces

\[
\ell=1,\qquad s=2,\qquad\delta=1.
\]

But Theorem 2 excludes a one-linkage rank-two coprime continuum.  Hence
\(m\ne4\), and \(m\ge5\).  The final assertion is exactly Theorem 2.
\(\square\)

Several immediate lower bounds follow:

- every weakly reversible realization has at least five directed reactions,
  because each of its at least five active complexes has an outgoing edge;
- every reversible realization has at least three reversible pairs (six
  directed reactions), because a connected graph on each linkage class needs
  at least \(m-\ell\ge s+1\ge3\) edges;
- a one-linkage reversible realization has at least four reversible pairs
  (eight directed reactions), because \(m\ge5\) and a connected graph on
  \(m\) vertices has at least \(m-1\) edges;
- in three species the maximum total complex degree is at least two, because
  only \(0,X,Y,Z\) have total degree at most one, while at least five distinct
  complexes are required.

For a one-linkage three-species target, the proved parameter bounds are
therefore

\[
n\ge3,\quad s=3,\quad m\ge5,\quad\delta=m-4\ge1,
\quad p\ge4,
\]

where \(p\) is the number of reversible pairs.

## 6. What is and is not minimal in the construction

The present construction has the following complexity profile:

| quantity | proved necessary | construction |
|---|---:|---:|
| species | 3 | 3 |
| stoichiometric rank, one linkage | 3 | 3 |
| complexes | at least 5 | 10 |
| deficiency | at least 1 | 6 |
| reversible pairs, one linkage | at least 4 | 10 |
| directed reactions, reversible one linkage | at least 8 | 20 |
| maximum complex degree | at least 2 | 3 |

Thus species count and, under one linkage, stoichiometric rank are genuinely
minimal.  The following questions remain open on the basis of the present
proofs:

1. whether five, six, seven, eight, or nine complexes can support the target;
2. whether deficiency one can support it;
3. whether four through nine reversible pairs can support a one-linkage
   example; and
4. whether maximum complex degree two is possible.

For its fixed ten vertices, the construction uses ten reversible pairs,
whereas connectivity alone requires nine.  Its undirected graph therefore has
cycle rank one.  This near-tree observation is not a global reaction-count
minimality statement because a different construction may use fewer
complexes.

Earlier searches restricted to a particular cubic conic architecture do not
close any of these gaps.  They did not cover arbitrary complexes, conics,
linkage structures, rates, or dynamically equivalent realizations; moreover,
the final positivity stage used a numerical threshold.  Those searches are
discovery evidence only and cannot justify a theorem that ten complexes or
ten reversible pairs are minimal.

## 7. Computational-claim boundary

No bounded-support computational impossibility claim is used in this note.
All lower bounds are consequences of the proofs above.  The companion script
`verify_complexity_arithmetic.py` checks only the finite integer-arithmetic
reductions used in the count corollaries; it is a replayable sanity check, not
a replacement for the algebraic proofs and not a network enumeration.
