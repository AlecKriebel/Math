## Verdict and exact dependency

The required input is **family 029, Theorem 1.2**, not merely the family 003 Dirichlet theorem. I checked the needed analytic proof in family 029 §§2, 2a, 3–6 and Appendix 10, including the arbitrary-field mechanisms and the principal/nonprincipal contour comparison. **I found no substantive mathematical gap in that inspected proof.** This verdict is a mathematical audit using the cited standard results, not a claim of formal verification or human refereeing. Lack of a Lean formalization of family 029 is not itself an identified mathematical gap.

Family 142 §10 (`10-analytic.tex`, theorem `analytic:hecke`) explicitly imports this statement:

> For every cyclotomic number field F containing μ12 and every finite-order Hecke character η of F, L_F(s,η) has no zero in Re(s)>1−10^-6; the principal pole at 1 is allowed. There is no conductor or height restriction, and the width is independent of the field.

Family 029 `01-introduction.tex` lines 37–63 states precisely this scope. Its proof fixes F and η before choosing data; estimate constants and onsets may depend on them. The final Mellin convergence width does not. Family 142 applies this with K=Q(μ12q), M=K(p^(1/q)). Since μq⊂K and p is unramified in K, X^q−p is Eisenstein at each prime above p, and M/K is cyclic of degree q. Abelian zeta factorization then expresses ζ_M as finite-order Hecke L-functions over K. Thus **all relevant assumptions of the imported theorem are satisfied**. The theorem is stronger than a Dirichlet zero-free theorem, even though ζ_K itself is a product of Dirichlet L-functions.
