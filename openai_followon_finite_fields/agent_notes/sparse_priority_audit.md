# Independent priority audit: proposed sparse finite-field extension

Audited at 2026-10-07 05:26 UTC (2026-10-06 Pacific). This is a priority and mechanism audit, not proof certification and not permission to publish. It does not amend the reviewed v3 record or its publication decision. No external individual was contacted; no Git, Zenodo, or tracker mutation was performed.

## Exact candidate and conclusion

The proposed extension takes a nonzero univariate sparse polynomial over an explicit field K = F_p[T]/(h), with t nonzero coefficient/exponent pairs and binary exponents bounded by N. It seeks the distinct monic irreducible factors in dense form, their binary multiplicities, and the leading coefficient, in bit time polynomial in t, log(N+1), m, log p, and D, where D is the sum of the distinct factors' degrees. Its factor-discovery promise is that every non-X factor multiplicity is prime to p. The separate known-root multiplicity primitive permits arbitrary positive multiplicities, including enormous p-divisible ones.

**Disposition:** positive prior-art evidence identifies every proposed central mathematical mechanism. I cannot certify a genuinely new mechanism or support advertising the construction as a new solution. I have not located an explicitly printed theorem identical in every parameter to the complete combined candidate, and do not claim literal theorem duplication. That absence is not novelty evidence. The most supportable description is an attributed algorithmic consequence of established Padé reconstruction, logarithmic-derivative radical extraction, Mattarei's residue splitting, and the newly available dense factorization theorem. Any publication alleging a distinct new complexity theorem would need a concrete non-immediate advance beyond this combination, with independent proof and priority audits.

## Positive mechanism overlap

### Padé and low-radical dependence

Erich Kaltofen, *Greatest Common Divisors of Polynomials Given by Straight-Line Programs*, JACM 35(1) (1988), 231–264, §8, Algorithm Rational Numerator and Denominator and Theorem 8.1, reconstructs a reduced rational function with numerator/denominator degree bounds d,e from d+e+1 Taylor coefficients. The general multivariate algorithm uses random choices; its univariate linear-system/Padé specialization at a known regular point is exact and deterministic. Removing the X-valuation makes f'/f regular at zero. Sparse binary exponentiation computes its truncation with work polynomial in the truncation degree and input encoding length. This is established rational reconstruction, not an invented step. Primary author PDF: https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/88/Ka88_jacm.pdf . Independent detailed read: [subaudit](sparse_logder_priority_subaudit.md).

Pranjal Dutta, Nitin Saxena, Amit Sinhababu, *Discovering the Roots: Uniform Closure Results for Algebraic Classes*, arXiv:1710.03214 (first public version 9 October 2017), STOC 2018; full JACM version §6.3, Theorem 28, explicitly uses rad_p, deleting factors whose multiplicities are divisible by p. Their logarithmic derivative depends on the degree of this visible radical rather than the full weighted input degree. The theorem gives small circuits over the algebraic closure; §6.2 identifies base-field transfer as a remaining issue. Thus it is not an already-stated uniform deterministic univariate sparse complete-factorization algorithm. It is nevertheless direct positive precedent for the candidate's low-radical logarithmic-derivative mechanism and promise. Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03214 ; exact author-PDF details and reviewed statements are in the independent subaudit.

### Known-root multiplicity: the key recursion is public

Sandro Mattarei, *Root multiplicities and number of nonzero coefficients of a polynomial*, arXiv:math/0512239v2, 20 January 2006 (v1 publicly submitted 12 December 2005, 12:33:19 UTC); J. Algebra Appl. 6(3) (2007), 469–475. Theorem 2 states that weight is at least the product of one plus the base-p digits of a nonzero root's multiplicity. Its §2 proof splits exponents by residue modulo p: child multiplicities are at least the quotient digit, some attain it, and active child leading values determine the remainder digit. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/math/0512239 ; public-version history: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0512239 . I independently read this exact proof.

The proposed trie is a constructive specialization of that proof, rather than a materially different mechanism. At root 1, write f(X)=sum_r X^r F_r(X^p). If child orders are q_r and leading coefficients a_r, put q=min(q_r); the multiplicity equals pq+j, where j is the order at 1 of sum_{q_r=q} a_r X^r. For a active residues, the first a binomial moments find j<a by Vandermonde invertibility; a<=p, so required factorials are invertible. Propagate the first nonzero coefficient recursively. There are at most t(1+floor(log_p N)) occupied nodes. This is my constructive complexity inference from the published structural proof, **not a claim that Mattarei printed this algorithm or its bit bound**. Normalizing an arbitrary known nonzero root alpha by substituting alpha X preserves support and requires binary exponentiation only. No enumeration of all p residues is needed.

This positive overlap defeats a claim to have invented the crucial sparse multiplicity recursion. Explicit bookkeeping and implementation may remain useful, but they alone do not demonstrate the new mathematical contribution demanded by the project protocol.

## Exact comparisons and boundaries

Joachim von zur Gathen, *Factoring sparse multivariate polynomials*, FOCS 1983, pp. 172–179 (later JCSS 1985): introduction explicitly uses **unary exponents**. Theorem 5.B, p.176, assumes no irreducible-factor multiplicity is divisible by the characteristic; its randomized complexity is polynomial in the numeric total degree d and maximal input/factor sparsity M. Theorem 5.E handles finite fields and p-divisible multiplicities, again with numeric degree dependence. Hence the candidate's promise predates this project, but that theorem does not supply the proposed log N/dense-radical-output bound. Primary author scan: https://vonzurgathen.online/old/files/gat83e.pdf . I read pp.172,176–178 using OCR of rendered pages; scan formulas should not be inferred from OCR alone.

Aminadav Chuyoon and Amir Shpilka, *On Factorization of Sparse Polynomials of Bounded Individual Degree*, ECCC TR26-036, first publication 8 March 2026 12:21 (ECCC timezone unspecified). Lemma 4.13, pp.24–25, computes primitive-divisor multiplicities over arbitrary characteristic with time poly(n,(d^2)!,s^(d^3),ell), by interpolating bounded-degree images and ordinary division. Lemma 4.19, p.28, assumes characteristic zero or >2d and uses poly(n,d!,s^d,ell). Theorem 1.9 and Corollary 8.6 retain bounded numeric individual degree. These are exact related multiplicity algorithms; none of these inspected statements replaces d by log N or uses only D as the output-degree parameter. Primary: https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/036/ and its download PDF. I read the actual lemmas and proofs, not only the abstract.

Alexander Demin and Joris van der Hoeven, *Factoring sparse polynomials fast*, arXiv:2312.17380, first public 28 December 2023; final author version 23 February 2025; J. Complexity 88 (2025), 101934. The actual §§1.1,1.4,2.1–2.3 explain the numeric total-degree dependence, randomized sparse evaluation/interpolation model, and separate univariate factorization cost. This does not establish the candidate's binary-exponent complexity, uniform finite-field scope, or complete multiplicity primitive. Primary final author text: https://www.texmacs.org/joris/sparsefact/sparsefact.html . No equivalence claim is inferred from their broad title.

Ruichen Qiu, Yichuan Cao, Qiao-Long Huang, Ruyong Feng, Xiao-Shan Gao, *Output-sensitive Sparse Polynomial GCD over Finite Fields is NP-hard*, arXiv:2606.12144v1, submitted 10 June 2026 14:37:49 UTC. I read Theorems 3–4 and the input model. Their sparse-output GCD obstruction uses input/gcd term counts and log degrees, not the dense degree of the **entire radical of the input**. A promise that the entire radical has degree D can have enormous D on their hard instances even when the gcd is constant or linear. Thus the paper cannot be cited as automatically contradicting this candidate. It also supplies no positive priority certificate. Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12144v1 . The HTML has an internal manuscript date of August 24, 2026; public priority here is the arXiv submission record, not that internal date.

## Related source that must not be imported as a theorem

Graham H. Norton, *A Note on the Games-Chan Algorithm*, arXiv:2209.00148v2 (14 September 2022), §2, Corollary 2.3, gives a dense q-folding recursion for multiplicity at 1. Its logarithmic count of recursion levels is not a sparse-input bit-complexity theorem. More seriously, the displayed arbitrary-q formula fails on the following exact case:

Over F3, f=1+X+X^2=(X-1)^2, N=3, N'=1. The three blocks are 1,X,X^2, and their fold is zero. Corollary 2.3 returns min(0+2,0+1)=1, while the true multiplicity is 2. This counterexample is exact polynomial arithmetic, not numerical evidence. The text also uses an unqualified minimal-polynomial-of-a-sum equality which is false in general because leading components can cancel. We do not rely on this paper to prove either the candidate or a priority equivalence. Primary exact v2 PDF view: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00148v2 . The binary Games–Chan algorithm and reliable generalizations should be cited separately if needed; this audit has not independently rebuilt all their proofs.

## Separate follow-up: all multiplicities through sparse/dense Cartier recursion

Added 2026-10-07 05:36 UTC. The parent subsequently proposed a stronger candidate with **no multiplicity-prime-to-p promise**. Its exact target remains deterministic bit time polynomial in t, log(N+1), m, log p and the unweighted dense radical degree D. Its representation is a polynomial F=U/V, with sparse U of nonincreasing term count and dense V dividing U. If A_U records the nonzero base-p multiplicity residues of U and U=A_U H_U^p, the zero-residue coefficient section represents H_U as a sparse numerator divided by a dense auxiliary denominator. The corresponding dense denominator root gives the next polynomial quotient. The proposed proof claims that the auxiliary denominator degree grows by at most D per recursion level, and that at most 1+floor(log_p N) levels suffice. This paragraph records the assertion under review, **not a proof verdict**.

This stronger candidate must be distinguished from the inherited tame mechanism above. The individual section-root identity is itself inherited. Let sigma raise coefficients to pth powers, without changing exponents, and let S_0 select coefficients whose exponents are divisible by p and divide those exponents by p. From U=A_U sigma(H_U)(X^p), the classical identity S_0(g(X)h(X^p))=S_0(g)h gives

    H_U = sigma^(-1)(S_0(U)) / sigma^(-1)(S_0(A_U)).

A_U(0)!=0 guarantees that the auxiliary denominator is nonzero. This direct specialization of the product identity should not be advertised as a new Cartier identity or new polynomial-root identity. Mattarei's Theorem 2 gives the separate bound e mod p <= t-1 for a known nonzero root of a t-term U, and therefore prevents an implicit polynomial dependence on p when constructing the visible residue factor A_U.

Eric Rowland and Reem Yassawi, *Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions*, arXiv:1310.8635 (first public submission 31 October 2013, 18:47:07 UTC; inspected v2 dated 23 April 2014), J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 27 (2015), 245–288, DOI 10.5802/jtnb.901. Definition 1.7 and Proposition 1.9 supply coefficient sections and the product pull-out identity; the proof of Theorem 2.1 maintains bounded-degree numerators with a fixed rational denominator. Its algorithm forms powers of a dense denominator and addresses automatic coefficient sequences. It does not state a sparse-numerator complete factorization recursion whose auxiliary denominator is bounded by original radical degree times recursion depth. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/1310.8635 ; public-version record: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8635 ; journal source: https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.901.pdf . Alin Bostan, Gilles Christol and Philippe Dumas, *Fast Computation of the Nth Term of an Algebraic Series over a Finite Prime Field*, ISSAC 2016; arXiv:1602.00545v2 (18 May 2016; first submission 1 February 2016, 14:42:39 UTC). Theorem 9 has genuine quasi-linear preprocessing dependence on p, alongside logarithmic dependence on the coefficient index. The subaudit separately checks Giesbrecht–Roche's lacunary perfect-power work and Huang–Cao–Qiu–Gao arXiv:2607.02364. The former's finite-field sparse Newton algorithm requires characteristic greater than input degree and has a conjectural sparsity bound. The latter applies direct inverse Frobenius to an already represented sparse perfect power and retains numeric total-degree and scalar-root parameters. Neither inspected algorithm supplies the proposed sparse/dense quotient closure. See the subaudit for the exact statements and source locations.

**Current priority disposition for the stronger route:** the primary sources inspected supply the identities and several individual subroutines, but do not establish the asserted combined representation invariant, its radical-degree growth bound, signed multiplicity bookkeeping, or resulting complete all-characteristic bit bound. Those assertions form a concrete potential contribution requiring rigorous proof, broader comparison and fresh complete-package audits. This audit supplies no assertion of being first and no automatic novelty clearance. In particular, no prior algorithm has been declared absent merely because a keyword search failed. The earlier tame-route disposition remains unchanged; it must not be silently promoted into a novelty claim for the stronger route or vice versa.
