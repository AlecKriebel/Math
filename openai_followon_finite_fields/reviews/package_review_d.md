## Mathematical falsification and strongest verified claim

I read the entire canonical manuscript and derivation, the dependency ledger, all included proof-review notes, both independent sparse priority audits and their subaudits, the main production implementations and tests, the independent reduction implementations, generated fixtures, certificates, source manifests, intended metadata, licenses and publication README. The exact sparse claim is complete factorization of every nonzero univariate sparse polynomial over supplied `K=F_p[T]/(h)`, with binary exponents and arbitrary binary multiplicities, dense distinct irreducible outputs of total degree `D`, and polynomial bit cost in `t,b,D,m,L`. It rejects zero, handles constants and monomials separately, and does not promise a bound in sparse output term count, binary construction degree alone, a small GCD degree, or arbitrary-circuit size. The reduction to a correct complete dense oracle is self-contained; its unconditional complexity corollary additionally uses the cited upstream theorem.

I tried to break the following proof points rather than infer validity from agreement among earlier reviewers:

1. **Visible denominator and Padé certification.** In `U'/U`, factors whose multiplicities vanish modulo `p` disappear, but every other factor contributes a nonzero simple pole, so the reduced denominator is exactly the visible radical. With `U(0) != 0`, normalization `Q(0)=1` is legitimate. Once the trial bound reaches the true denominator degree, any Padé solution with the specified numerator degree satisfies the cross-product degree argument. Exact sparse identity `U'Q=UA` excludes accidental matching of only the first `2B` coefficients. Doubling costs depend on radical/denominator degree rather than numeric `deg U`.

2. **Known-root valuation and binary exponents.** The quotient `E=K[Z]/(g)` has dimension `delta` over `K` and `m delta` over `F_p`; `g != X` makes its formal root invertible. Grouping occupied base-`p` residues retains the full integer exponents. At an occupied digit node, the binomial matrix through order `s-1` is invertible for `s` distinct residues, with `s <= p` and `j! != 0` for `j < p`. An active child's first contribution at `pv+j` precedes every inactive child's possible contribution. This justifies the minimum-valuation trie and the bound `e mod p <= t-1`, without enumerating `p` residues or a multiplicity. Reducing exponents by a root's multiplicative order would be wrong and is not used.

3. **Rational rather than polynomial descent.** The invariant `F=U/V` is polynomial, hence `V|U`. The algorithm need not sparsely divide by the visible residue product. It applies the Cartier pull-out identity to both numerator and denominator, takes inverse coefficient Frobenius on both zero sections, and retains the unit. For every factor, `floor(e_U/p) >= floor(e_V/p)`, so the actual new quotient remains polynomial. Its radical is contained in the old radical even when the representation introduces auxiliary denominator factors.

4. **Denominator growth and cancellations.** With `v=deg V` and actual quotient radical degree `D_F`, `D_U <= D_F+v`, and the full, degree-unbounded-in-`p` residue argument gives `v_new <= ((p-1)(D_F+v)+v)/p <= v+D_F`. Therefore `v_k <= kD`. Artifact factors and signed carries do not invalidate this inequality: it refers to the actual quotient, not an unproved claim that every represented factor belongs to the original input. Dense residue construction instead uses `deg A_U <= min(p-1,t-1)D_U`, preventing hidden numeric-`p` output work. The signed factor ledger telescopes to original multiplicities; negative residues and artifact cancellation are necessary and tested.

5. **Termination, units and certificates.** Zero-section numerator support never increases and maximal exponent is divided by `p` each level; at most `b` nonconstant levels occur. A constant numerator forces a constant denominator under `V|U`. The stripped `X` valuation and original leading coefficient are restored. An output certificate checks distinct monic irreducibility, exact original sparse valuations and weighted degree, which proves the product without expanding binary-large powers. It does not accept a merely plausible signed ledger as a certificate.

6. **Dimensions, calls and bit cost.** I checked at most `2b` dense calls, degrees bounded by `(b+1)D`, Padé elimination dimensions, known-factor quotient dimensions, the residue degree bound, inverse Frobenius exponent bit length and signed-weight bit length. Direct prime-Frobenius descent gives at most `2b(b+1)D` prime calls of degree at most `(b+1)D`. Canonical finite-field coordinates preclude coefficient-height growth. The loose `O(M^24)` nonoracle allowance covers the displayed schoolbook/elimination/powering loops; no numeric-length loop in `p` or `N` is needed. The final unused weight is covered by the stated bit bound because the number of actual levels also shrinks as `log_p N`.

I independently constructed 15 additional examples over `F_8`, `F_9`, `F_25`, `F_16` and `F_7` with nonprime-field coefficients, a scalar, an `X^13` shift, a linear factor and an independently Frobenius-irreducible quadratic. Their multiplicities include `2p+1+p^k` with `k=3,17,91`. Sparse terms were assembled independently using multiplication and Frobenius exponent shifts; exact factors, units, multiplicities and denominator bounds were checked. All passed, including exponent encodings up to 256 bits and 92 levels. I also tested `p=2^127-1`, `11(X-2)^(3+p^7)(X-5)^2`, with 12 terms, 889-bit degree and `D=2`, using an explicit two-root test oracle rather than enumerating the field; all eight levels passed.
