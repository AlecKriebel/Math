## Mathematical falsification of the sparse claim

The precise domain is a nonzero sparse polynomial over the explicit field `K=F_p[T]/h`, with promised prime `p`, monic irreducible positive-degree `h`, canonical nonzero terms and full nonnegative binary exponents. Duplicate exponents are combined before the theorem's term count is applied. `D` pays for **every** distinct densely returned factor. Zero is excluded; constants and monomials terminate with their unit and possible factor `X`. Prescribed construction degree and optional extraction degree `r` are numerical dense degrees, never silently replaced by their bit lengths.

I read the complete canonical manuscript, detailed sparse derivation, dependency ledger, both sparse prototypes, all packaged checking scripts, and the elementary arithmetic interfaces. The new sparse adversary independently derived the stages before comparing code. We attacked the following possible failures:

- **Logarithmic poles and Padé:** separability of irreducibles over a finite field makes the reduced denominator of `U'/U` precisely the product of factors whose numerator multiplicity is nonzero modulo `p`. Other factors cannot cancel that local pole. The augmented system has `B` rows and `B+1` columns. Once `B` covers the true denominator degree, cross multiplication has degree below `2B` and vanishing order at least `2B`, so every affine solution represents the true rational function. The exact sparse identity rejects misleading finite Taylor prefixes. Derivative zero correctly returns denominator one and continues descent.
- **Known-factor valuation:** the quotient `K[Z]/g` is an explicit field of dimension `deg(g)` and its formal root is nonzero after removing `X`. Distinct exponents remain distinct in each residue bucket. The active binomial matrix is a Vandermonde divided by invertible factorials with indices strictly below `p`. The initial order is `pv+j`, with `j` below the active bucket count. The stack implementation avoids a recursion-depth restriction and never replaces full exponents by residues modulo a multiplicative-group order. It supplies exact multiplicities and the residue bound `e_U(g) mod p <= t-1` without a characteristic-sized scan.
- **Rational Cartier descent:** `U=A_U H_U^p` and `V=A_V H_V^p` retain scalar units. The zero section gives `U_new=W H_U`, with inverse coefficient Frobenius applied to both sections. Taking `V_new=W H_V` preserves `V_new | U_new`; nonzero constant terms prevent a zero section denominator. The floor-multiplicity difference is nonnegative and its radical lies in the old polynomial radical. It is incorrect to floor-divide the quotient multiplicity itself, and this proof does not do so.
- **Degree and termination:** for `v=deg(V)` and `D_F=deg(rad(F))`, `D_U<=D_F+v` and `deg(V_new)<=v+(1-1/p)D_F<=v+D`. Thus level `k` has denominator degree at most `kD` and visible denominator degree at most `(k+1)D`. The sparse numerator shrinks by `p` and retains at most the original `t` terms. Termination uses that numerator, not an unproved decrease of the quotient degree. There are at most `b=max(1,ceil(log2(N+1)))` stages, at most `2b` dense calls, each degree at most `(b+1)D`. The direct prime-field reduction consequently fits `2b(b+1)D` calls of that maximum degree.
- **Signed ledger and certification:** negative residues and artificial section factors are retained until telescoping cancellation. The final ledger gives the original nonnegative multiplicities; the original leading coefficient supplies the monic-factor unit. Irreducibility, distinctness, original-input valuations and weighted-degree equality certify completeness without expanding huge powers. Dense expansion helpers are expressly unsuitable for huge sparse multiplicities.
- **Cost:** adaptive matrices, residue products, quotient fields, coefficient lengths, sparse exact-identity products, integer exponents and signed weights all stay polynomial in `t,b,D,m,L`. The residue product is bounded by `(t-1)(b+1)D`; inverse Frobenius has exponent bit length `O(mL)`. Schoolbook arithmetic supports the deliberately loose `O(M^24)` overhead in the manuscript, with `M=1+t+b+D+m+L`. I checked polynomial dimensions and exponent accounting, not an instruction-level optimized implementation or a practical constant.
