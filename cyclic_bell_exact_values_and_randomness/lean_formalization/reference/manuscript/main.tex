\begin{abstract}
Perito, D'Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Ac\'in, and Augusiak introduced two
cyclic Bell families designed for efficient certification of maximal global
randomness.  For the first reduced operator they proved the general bound
\(d\sqrt2\), supplied an attaining strategy of value
\(2\csc(\pi/(2d))\), and conjectured that this sharper value is exact.  We
prove that formula in every dimension; the polar-decomposition argument also
applies to commuting-operator realizations.  Its scalar equality phases yield
two complementary structural results.  Every attained finite-dimensional
tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family has, on the
support of Alice's reduced state, all \(d\) equality roots with equal
multiplicity, so \(d\) divides the supported dimension.  At the same time,
conditional permutations
of those phases generate a large orbit of exact maximizers invisible to all
first-harmonic correlators in the score.  For every \(d\geq4\), a final-two
swap maximizes both augmented cyclic families while producing a nonuniform
designated joint-output distribution.  Thus maximal value of the displayed
scalar functional alone does not certify the proposed uniform
\(2\log_2d\) bits or uniquely determine the canonical behavior.  This does
not challenge certification conditioned on the complete canonical behavior,
nor determine the worst-case guessing probability.  An exact \(d=4\)
certificate over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})\) accompanies the all-dimensional
proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Bell inequalities can certify randomness without a model of the devices, but
the conditioning data matter.  A complete behavior
\(p(a,b\mid x,y)\) contains far more information than one scalar Bell value.
If a Bell maximum exposes a unique behavior, symmetries of the score may force
uniform outputs and, with an operator-level argument, privacy against a
purifying adversary.  If the maximizing face contains several behaviors, the
same symmetry argument need not apply to each of them
\cite{DharaEtAl2013,NietoSillerasEtAl2014,BancalEtAl2014}.

Perito, D'Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Ac\'in, and Augusiak recently introduced two
families built around cyclic Fourier correlators
\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}.  They defined the families, found explicit
maximally-entangled strategies, and proved the general first-family bound
\(d\sqrt2\).  Their strategy has value \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))\); they
conjectured equality and confirmed it numerically through \(d=6\) with the
NPA hierarchy~\cite{NPA2007}.  They also proved the exact value of the second
reduced family by a sum-of-squares identity.  Their symmetry analysis motivated
maximal-global-randomness statements; for selected dimensions they evaluated
Eve's guessing probability numerically after fixing the complete canonical
behavior.  These are logically different inputs to a randomness problem.

The surrounding literature includes high-dimensional and modular Bell
inequalities \cite{BuhrmanMassar2005,SalavrakosEtAl2017,KaniewskiEtAl2019},
self-testing from few measurements \cite{SarkarEtAl2021,KlepEtAl2026}, and
analytic or algorithmic SOS constructions
\cite{BarizienEtAl2024,CocciaEtAl2026}.  Two contemporaneous works of the
originating collaboration pursue all-dimensional global randomness and
three-outcome noise robustness by different constructions
\cite{FarkasMironowiczAugusiak2026,DavinoEtAl2026}.  We use these works to
set the attribution boundary; the family-specific polar bound and the
phase-permuted maximizers below are not imported from them.
To our knowledge, no prior work establishes either the first-family upper
bound in this normalization or the nonuniform exact maximizers constructed
here; the accompanying priority audit records the qualified search basis for
that statement.

This paper unifies the exact-value and randomness questions through the
equality phases of the first operator.  The polar proof reduces its upper
bound to a scalar roots-of-unity extremum.  At equality the relative unitary
may carry the full set of maximizing phases in different cyclic orders.
Those orders leave the Bell-visible first harmonics unchanged, but they alter
higher Fourier data and can change a complete output table.  The same Bob
observables also annihilate the sum-of-squares factors of
Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}, Eqs.~(22)--(23), for the second family.

Our main conclusions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item The finite-dimensional tensor-product, approximate, and
commuting-operator values of the first reduced cyclic operator are all
\(2\csc(\pi/(2d))\).
\item Every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the
first augmented family has all equality roots with equal multiplicity on
Alice's support; in particular, \(d\) divides that supported dimension.
\item A polar-linear permutation theorem gives explicit sufficient conditions
under which phase permutations remain admissible maximizers and preserve the
entire first-harmonic correlator matrix.
\item For each \(d\geq4\), one such maximizer has uniform local marginals but
a nonuniform designated joint table.  With trivial Eve this already gives a
guessing probability strictly larger than \(1/d^2\).
\item The same construction maximizes the second augmented cyclic family and
has the same target table.
\item The result separates scalar Bell-value certification from certification
conditioned on a fixed full behavior.  It is not a claim about the exact
worst-case guessing probability, the complete maximizing face, or the
randomness of the canonical realization.
\end{enumerate}

The exact value and support rigidity are proved in
Sections~\ref{sec:exact}--\ref{sec:rigidity}; the explicit equality orbit is
developed in Section~\ref{sec:phase};
the two biased families are treated in Sections~\ref{sec:biased} and
\ref{sec:second}; and the randomness interpretation is isolated in
Section~\ref{sec:randomness}.  Section~\ref{sec:settings} collects the
binary benchmark and sharply scoped low-setting design conditions.

\section{Bell operators, behaviors, and randomness notions}
\label{sec:framework}

Fix \(d\geq2\), write \(\omega=\e^{2\pi i/d}\), and identify outcomes with
\(\bbZ_d\).  A projective \(d\)-outcome measurement
\(\{M_a\}_{a\in\bbZ_d}\) is encoded by the order-\(d\) unitary
\[
 A=\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}\omega^aM_a,
 \qquad
 M_a=\frac1d\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-ak}A^k.
\]
Zero spectral projections are allowed in the general definition.  All
explicit strategies below have the complete simple spectrum of \(d\)-th
roots, so their outcomes are rank-one projectors.

For a matrix \(T\) written in the displayed computational basis,
\(\overline T\) denotes entrywise complex conjugation, while \(T^T\) and
\(T^\dagger\) denote its transpose and adjoint.  The overbars in the
separately defined names \(\overline{\cI}_d\) and \(\overline{\cF}_d\)
indicate augmentation, not complex conjugation.

Let \(\mathcal Q_q\) denote the set of finite-dimensional tensor-product
correlations, let \(\mathcal Q_{qa}=\overline{\mathcal Q_q}\), and let
\(\mathcal Q_{qc}\) denote the commuting-operator correlation set, in which
Alice's and Bob's PVMs act on one Hilbert space and commute across parties.
Thus
\[
 \mathcal Q_q\subseteq\mathcal Q_{qa}\subseteq\mathcal Q_{qc}.
\]
For a Bell functional \(\mathcal B\), write
\[
 \beta_\mu(\mathcal B)
 =\sup_{p\in\mathcal Q_\mu}\mathcal B(p),
 \qquad \mu\in\{q,qa,qc\}.
\]
The operator inequalities below hold in the commuting model; a matching
finite-dimensional strategy then identifies all three values.
The value theorem extends to the commuting-operator model because it is an
operator inequality, whereas the support-rigidity and privacy results are
stated only for finite-dimensional tensor-product strategies because their
proofs use reduced-state supports, Schmidt decompositions, and purifying-Eve
conditional states; no commuting-operator analogue is claimed here.

For a behavior \(p(a,b\mid x,y)\), a scalar Bell maximum fixes only one
supporting hyperplane of the quantum correlation set.  We distinguish:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the value of a Bell functional;
\item its full maximizing face;
\item a selected canonical strategy and its canonical behavior;
\item the complete observed behavior, including higher Fourier correlators;
\item uniform observed target probabilities; and
\item private randomness against a purifying adversary.
\end{itemize}

For a purification \(\lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE}\) and target settings
\((x_*,y_*)\), let
\[
 \sigma_E^{ab}
 =\Tr_{AB}\!\left[(M_a^{x_*}\otimes N_b^{y_*}\otimes\Id)
 \lvert\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi\rvert
 (M_a^{x_*}\otimes N_b^{y_*}\otimes\Id)\right].
\]
Eve's guessing probability is
\[
 G(AB\mid x_*,y_*,E)
 =\max_{\{Q_{ab}\}}\sum_{a,b}\Tr(Q_{ab}\sigma_E^{ab}).
\]
For model-dependent optimizations below, \(\mathfrak S_q\) denotes finite-
dimensional tripartite tensor-product strategies, including Eve's
\(d^2\)-outcome guessing POVM; \(\mathfrak S_{qa}\) is the closure of the
resulting finite extended correlations; and \(\mathfrak S_{qc}\) consists of
a vector state and three party algebras on one Hilbert space that commute
pairwise across parties, with Eve's guessing POVM in her algebra.  This fixes
the adversarial extension rather than leaving \(q\), \(qa\), or \(qc\)
implicit in a guessing quantity.

Maximal private global randomness means
\(\sigma_E^{ab}=\rho_E/d^2\) for every \(a,b\), and implies
\(G=1/d^2\).  Uniform probabilities are necessary but not sufficient for
this operator-valued condition.  Conversely, one explicit nonuniform target
table, even with one-dimensional Eve, is enough to rule out maximal private
randomness for that realization.

\begin{remark}[Normalization convention]
\label{rem:normalization}
Version 3 of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026} has a localized
normalization discrepancy: the displayed reduced value in its Eq.~(17) and
its \(d=3\) augmented value agree with the operator definition, while the
formula printed beside its first augmented randomness conjecture contains an
additional factor \(d\) in the denominator.  We take the displayed Hermitian
operator as authoritative.  Thus the reduced and augmented values in our
convention are respectively
\[
 M_d=2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right),
 \qquad M_d+1.
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions} records the corresponding adjoint conventions.
Nothing in our argument uses the typographical discrepancy.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact value of the first cyclic operator}
\label{sec:exact}

For commuting Alice and Bob unitaries define
\begin{equation}
 \cI_d
 =\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}
 \Reop\!\left[(A_0+\omega^yA_1)B_y\right].
 \label{eq:Id}
\end{equation}
In the tensor model the product in \eqref{eq:Id} means a tensor product.  The
order-\(d\) relations are part of the Bell scenario, but will not be needed
for the upper bound.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp commuting-operator value]
\label{thm:exact}
Let \(A_0,A_1,B_0,\ldots,B_{d-1}\) be unitaries on an arbitrary complex
Hilbert space and suppose \([A_x,B_y]=0\) for every \(x,y\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \cI_d\leq M_d\Id,
 \qquad M_d=2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right).
 \label{eq:main-bound}
\end{equation}
After imposing the order-\(d\) measurement relations,
\[
 \boxed{\beta_q(\cI_d)=\beta_{qa}(\cI_d)=\beta_{qc}(\cI_d)=M_d}
 \qquad(d\geq2).
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof has two independent ingredients.  The first is an operator identity
that remains valid when a polar factor has a kernel.

\begin{lemma}[Polar positive-factor identity]
\label{lem:polar}
Let \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal B(\cH)\) be commuting unital
\(*\)-algebras, let \(C\in\mathcal A\), let
\(C=V\absop C\) be its canonical polar decomposition, and let
\(B\in\mathcal B\) be unitary.  Put
\[
 P_{C,B}=\absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}-V\absop C^{1/2}B.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\absop C+\absop{C^\dagger}}2-\Reop(CB)
 =\frac12P_{C,B}^\dagger P_{C,B}.
 \label{eq:polar-factor}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\mathcal M=\mathcal A''\), the von Neumann algebra generated by
\(\mathcal A\).  Because \(C\in\mathcal A\), continuous functional
calculus places \(\absop C\), its square root, and
\((\absop C+\varepsilon\Id)^{-1}\) in \(\mathcal M\) for every
\(\varepsilon>0\).  Strong closure then places its support projection there,
since
\[
 \supp\absop C
 =\operatorname*{s\!-\!lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \absop C(\absop C+\varepsilon\Id)^{-1}.
\]
The canonical polar partial isometry belongs to the same algebra; explicitly,
\[
 V=\operatorname*{s\!-\!lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 C(\absop C+\varepsilon\Id)^{-1}.
\]
Indeed, the right-hand side is
\(V\absop C(\absop C+\varepsilon\Id)^{-1}\), which converges strongly to
\(V\supp\absop C=V\).  Elementwise commutation of the two original
algebras gives \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A'\); since
\(\mathcal M=(\mathcal A')'\), every element of \(\mathcal M\), in
particular \(V\) and the functional-calculus factors above, commutes with
\(B\).  This is the only closure step needed in the commuting-operator
model.

The initial projection \(V^\dagger V\) is the support projection of
\(\absop C\), while polar functional calculus gives
\[
 \absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}V=V\absop C^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}V\absop C^{1/2}=C.
\]
Expanding yields
\[
 P_{C,B}^\dagger P_{C,B}
 =\absop{C^\dagger}+\absop C-CB-B^\dagger C^\dagger.
\]
No inverse and no unitary extension of \(V\) has been used.
\end{proof}

The second ingredient is a scalar equality calculation; its elementary
parity-split proof is included in Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}.

\begin{lemma}[Roots-of-unity extremum]
\label{lem:scalar}
For \(z\in\bbT\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\absop{1+\omega^yz}\leq M_d.
 \label{eq:scalar-bound}
\end{equation}
Equality holds exactly when
\begin{equation}
 z^d=(-1)^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:scalar-equality}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}]
Set
\[
 L_y=A_0+\omega^yA_1,
 \qquad U=A_0^\dagger A_1,
 \qquad M_y=\Id+\omega^yU.
\]
Then \(L_y=A_0M_y\).  The operator \(M_y\) is normal because
\[
 M_y^\dagger M_y=M_yM_y^\dagger
 =2\Id+\omega^yU+\omega^{-y}U^\dagger.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \absop{L_y}=\absop{M_y},
 \qquad
 \absop{L_y^\dagger}=A_0\absop{M_y}A_0^\dagger.
\]
Put
\[
 F_d(U)=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\absop{\Id+\omega^yU}.
\]
Continuous functional calculus for the unitary \(U\), applied to
Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}, gives \(0\leq F_d(U)\leq M_d\Id\).  Summing
Lemma~\ref{lem:polar} now gives
\[
 \cI_d
 \leq\frac12\bigl(F_d(U)+A_0F_d(U)A_0^\dagger\bigr)
 \leq M_d\Id.
\]
All functional-calculus operators used here lie in the von Neumann algebra
generated by Alice's observables and therefore commute with Bob's algebra by
the argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}.  Thus no tensor-factor decomposition,
finite-dimensional trace, or cyclicity assumption enters the upper bound.
The finite order-\(d\) strategy rechecked in
Appendix~\ref{app:attainment} attains \(M_d\).  The inclusions among
\(q,qa,qc\) then prove all three equalities.
\end{proof}

For later equality arguments it is useful to retain the complete gap.  If
\(L_y=V_y\absop{L_y}\), define \(P_y=P_{L_y,B_y}\) and
\(G=(M_d\Id-F_d(U))^{1/2}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 M_d\Id-\cI_d
 =\frac12\sum_yP_y^\dagger P_y
 +\frac12G^\dagger G
 +\frac12(GA_0^\dagger)^\dagger(GA_0^\dagger).
}
\label{eq:global-certificate}
\end{equation}
This is an operator identity, not a finite-level NPA certificate.
At the operator level, scalar functional calculus also gives
\[
 F_d(U)=M_d\Id
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U^d=(-1)^{d-1}\Id.
\]
For an individual maximizing vector \(\lvert\psi\rangle\), however, the
exact conclusion from \eqref{eq:global-certificate} is only
\[
 P_y\lvert\psi\rangle=0\ \ (0\leq y<d),\qquad
 G\lvert\psi\rangle=0,\qquad
 GA_0^\dagger\lvert\psi\rangle=0.
\]
These vector-level relations do not yield a global relation for \(U\), a
description outside the state support, or a classification of all maximizing
realizations.  In finite dimension, however, the stabilizer supplied by the
augmented term upgrades them to the support-level spectral restriction in
Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}.

\begin{corollary}[First augmented family]
\label{cor:first-augmented}
For
\[
 \overline{\cI}_d=\cI_d+\Reop(A_0B_d)
\]
with a further order-\(d\) Bob observable,
\[
 \beta_q(\overline{\cI}_d)
 =\beta_{qa}(\overline{\cI}_d)
 =\beta_{qc}(\overline{\cI}_d)=M_d+1.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} and
\(\Reop(A_0B_d)\leq\Id\).  The finite strategy in
Appendix~\ref{app:attainment}, with the aligned added observable, attains
equality.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-dimensional support-level equality rigidity}
\label{sec:rigidity}

The equality certificate yields additional information at the precise scope
below once the first cyclic operator is augmented by its aligned term.
The following result concerns every attained finite-dimensional tensor-
product exact maximizer of the first augmented family.  It does not extend
silently to \(qa\) or \(qc\).

\begin{theorem}[Equal supported multiplicities]
\label{thm:support-rigidity}
Let a finite-dimensional tensor-product strategy with order-\(d\)
observables and state \(\rho_{AB}\) attain
\[
 \langle\overline{\cI}_d\rangle=M_d+1.
\]
For a purification \(\lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE}\), put
\[
 \rho_A=\Tr_{BE}\lvert\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi\rvert,
 \qquad K=\supp\rho_A,
 \qquad U=A_0^\dagger A_1.
\]
Then \(K\) reduces the relative unitary \(U\), and every root of
\(z^d=(-1)^{d-1}\) occurs in \(\spec(U|_K)\) with the same multiplicity.
Consequently,
\[
 d\mid\dim K.
\]
No conclusion is asserted on \(K^\perp\), for approximate maximizers, or
for general commuting-operator realizations.
\end{theorem}

The proof, given in Appendix~\ref{app:rigidity-proof}, starts from the
individual vanishing of every positive residual in
\eqref{eq:global-certificate} and of the augmented stabilizer.  A Schmidt-
support cancellation lemma moves those vector relations to \(K\).  Kernel-
safe polar cancellation then makes each polar partial isometry unitary on
\(K\), adjacent half-angle phases produce one reflection for each equality
root, and a rank inequality forces all root multiplicities to agree.

Equal supported multiplicities are necessary but do not imply a Weyl
representation, uniqueness, or self-testing.  In particular, every explicit
permutation maximizer below has \(K=\bbC^d\) and is compatible with the
theorem.

\section{Equality phases and permutation blindness}
\label{sec:phase}

The scalar equality set in \eqref{eq:scalar-equality} is a set, not an
ordering.  A Bell score built only from first powers can therefore fail to
record how those phases are arranged along a weighted cycle.  The following
general statement isolates the exact sufficient conditions.  It does not
claim to classify every maximizer of every polar-linear functional.

Let \(R\) be a finite coefficient index set, let
\(g_r(z)=\alpha_r+\beta_rz\), and put
\[
 f(z)=\sum_{r\in R}\absop{g_r(z)},
 \qquad M=\max_{z\in\bbT}f(z).
\]
For order-\(d\) observables define
\begin{equation}
 \cJ=\sum_{r\in R}\Reop\!\left[(\alpha_rA_0+\beta_rA_1)B_r\right]
 +\Reop(A_0B_*).
 \label{eq:polar-linear-J}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional phase-permutation theorem]
\label{thm:permutation}
Suppose there are labeled phases \(z_j,s_{rj}\in\bbT\),
\(0\leq j<d\), such that
\[
 f(z_j)=M,\qquad \prod_jz_j=1,
\]
and, for every \(r\),
\[
 s_{rj}\absop{g_r(z_j)}=g_r(z_j),
 \qquad \prod_js_{rj}=1.
\]
At a zero of \(g_r\), \(s_{rj}\) may be any unitary extension phase.
For every permutation \(\kappa\) of the labels, set on \(\bbC^d\)
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A_0&=X,&
 A_1&=X\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),\\
 B_r&=\overline{X\operatorname{diag}(s_{r,\kappa_j})},&
 B_*&=X,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:permutation-strategy}
\end{equation}
where \(X\lvert j\rangle=\lvert j+1\bmod d\rangle\), and use
\(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_j\lvert j,j\rangle\).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.7em]
\item every displayed observable is unitary, has \(d\)-th power \(\Id\),
and has the full simple \(d\)-th-root spectrum;
\item the strategy attains the operator upper bound \(M+1\) for
\eqref{eq:polar-linear-J}; and
\item all local first moments and every complex first-harmonic correlator
\(\langle A_xB_r\rangle\), \(\langle A_xB_*\rangle\) are independent of
\(\kappa\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With \(U=A_0^\dagger A_1\) and
\(L_r=A_0g_r(U)=V_r\absop{L_r}\), Lemma~\ref{lem:polar} and functional
calculus give
\[
 (M+1)\Id-\cJ
 =\frac12\sum_rP_r^\dagger P_r
 +\frac12R_*^\dagger R_*
 +\frac12\bigl(H+A_0HA_0^\dagger\bigr),
\]
where
\[
 P_r=\absop{L_r^\dagger}^{1/2}-V_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2}B_r,
 \quad R_*=\Id-A_0B_*,
 \quad H=M\Id-f(U)\geq0.
\]
Thus \(M+1\) is an operator upper bound.

For arbitrary nonzero weights \(t_j\),
\begin{equation}
 \left(X\operatorname{diag}(t_j)\right)^d
 =\left(\prod_jt_j\right)\Id,
 \label{eq:weighted-cycle}
\end{equation}
and its characteristic polynomial is
\(t^d-\prod_jt_j\).  The product hypotheses therefore prove (i).  Moreover,
for each \(r\), put
\[
 W_r=X\operatorname{diag}(s_{r,\kappa_j}),
 \qquad
 E_r=\supp\absop{L_r}.
\]
Then
\[
 L_r=W_r\absop{L_r},
 \qquad
 V_r=W_rE_r,
\]
where \(V_r\) is the canonical polar partial isometry appearing in \(P_r\).
Consequently
\[
 V_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2}
 =W_rE_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2}
 =W_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2};
\]
the phases extending \(W_r\) on \(\ker L_r\) do not enter the positive
residual.  Since \(B_r=\overline{W_r}\), one has \(B_r^T=W_r^\dagger\), and
therefore
\[
 V_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2}B_r^T
 =W_r\absop{L_r}^{1/2}W_r^\dagger
 =\absop{L_r^\dagger}^{1/2}.
\]
The vectorization identity
\[
 (R\otimes S)\lvert\Phi_d\rangle
 =(RS^T\otimes\Id)\lvert\Phi_d\rangle
\]
now gives \(P_r\lvert\Phi_d\rangle=0\), including when \(g_r(U)\) has a
kernel.  Moreover, \(f(U)=M\Id\), because every \(z_{\kappa_j}\) is a
maximizing phase, and
\[
 (A_0\otimes B_*)\lvert\Phi_d\rangle
 =(X\otimes X)\lvert\Phi_d\rangle
 =\lvert\Phi_d\rangle.
\]
Thus every factor in the displayed gap annihilates
\(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\), proving (ii).  Finally, the maximally-entangled
trace identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle A_0B_r\rangle&=\frac1d\sum_j\overline{s_{rj}},&
 \langle A_1B_r\rangle&=\frac1d\sum_jz_j\overline{s_{rj}},\\
 \langle A_0B_*\rangle&=1,&
 \langle A_1B_*\rangle&=\frac1d\sum_jz_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are symmetric sums of the labeled data.  Every local observable is a
weighted shift and has zero trace.  This proves (iii).
\end{proof}

The theorem explains both its power and its limit.  It gives a concrete
permutation orbit within a maximizing face, but it says nothing about
maximizers whose relative spectrum is different, has different
multiplicities, acts reducibly, or uses a kernel outside the stated phase
data.  Claims about the complete maximizing face require separate rigidity
analysis.

\section{Exact nonuniform maximizers of the first family}
\label{sec:biased}

Put
\begin{equation}
 \delta_d=\begin{cases}0,&d\text{ odd},\\1,&d\text{ even},\end{cases}
 \qquad
 z_k=\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi i(2k+\delta_d)}d\right).
 \label{eq:equality-roots}
\end{equation}
These are precisely the roots in \eqref{eq:scalar-equality}.  For
\(0\leq y,k<d\), define
\[
 s_{y,k}=\frac{1+\omega^yz_k}{\absop{1+\omega^yz_k}}.
\]
The denominator never vanishes.  The polynomial
\(t^d-(-1)^{d-1}\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \prod_kz_k=1,
 \qquad
 \prod_k(1+\omega^yz_k)=2,
 \qquad
 \prod_ks_{y,k}=1.
 \label{eq:cyclic-products}
\end{equation}
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:permutation} applies with
\(g_y(z)=1+\omega^yz\).

For every permutation \(\kappa\), the explicit first-family strategy is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A_0&=X,& A_1&=X Z_\kappa,&
 Z_\kappa&=\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),\\
 V_y&=X\operatorname{diag}(s_{y,\kappa_j}),&
 B_y&=\overline{V_y},& B_d&=X.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:first-strategy}
\end{equation}
Each observable defines a rank-one projective \(d\)-outcome measurement and
\[
 \langle\overline{\cI}_d\rangle=M_d+1.
\]
The Bell-visible correlators are independent of the ordering.  Explicitly,
with \(\alpha_d=\csc(\pi/(2d))\),
\begin{equation}
 \langle A_0B_y\rangle=\frac{\alpha_d}{d},
 \qquad
 \langle A_1B_y\rangle=\frac{\alpha_d\omega^{-y}}d,
 \qquad
 \langle A_0B_d\rangle=1,
 \qquad
 \langle A_1B_d\rangle=0.
 \label{eq:visible-correlators}
\end{equation}
The cyclic ordering \(\kappa_j=j\) reproduces the canonical first-harmonic
behavior.  Other orderings can change higher powers and full probabilities.

To calculate the designated pair \((A_1,B_d)\), set
\begin{equation}
 q_0=1,
 \qquad q_{j+1}=z_{\kappa_j}q_j,
 \qquad
 \widehat q_m=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}q_j\omega^{mj}.
 \label{eq:q-sequence}
\end{equation}
The product condition makes the sequence cyclic, so extend it by
\(q_{j+d}=q_j\), and put \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(q_j)\).  The recurrence
gives \(A_1=QXQ^\dagger\).  Define
\[
 \lvert u_c\rangle
 =\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-cj}\lvert j\rangle,
 \qquad
 \lvert v_a\rangle=Q\lvert u_a\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
 X\lvert u_c\rangle=\omega^c\lvert u_c\rangle,
 \qquad
 A_1\lvert v_a\rangle=\omega^a\lvert v_a\rangle,
\]
so these are the outcome eigenvectors under the encoding
\(A=\sum_a\omega^aM_a\).  The transpose of the outcome-\(b\) projector of
\(B_d=X\) is the projector onto \(\overline{u_b}=u_{-b}\).  Hence the
maximally-entangled trace identity gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 p_\kappa(a,b\mid1,d)
 &=\frac1d\absop{\langle u_{-b}\mid v_a\rangle}^2\\
 &=\frac1{d^3}
   \absop{\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}q_j\omega^{-(a+b)j}}^2
 =\frac{\absop{\widehat q_{-(a+b)}}^2}{d^3}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:target-table}
\end{equation}
Parseval gives \(\sum_m\absop{\widehat q_m}^2=d^2\).  Consequently both
local marginals are exactly \(1/d\), while the joint table is uniform if and
only if every squared Fourier magnitude equals \(d\).

The canonical ordering is Fourier-flat in every dimension.  Indeed, for
\(\kappa_j=j\), the recurrence gives
\[
 q_j=\eta^{j(j-1+\delta_d)},
 \qquad \eta=\e^{\pi i/d},
\]
and the parity choice in \(\delta_d\) makes this formula cyclic.  Reading
indices cyclically, its autocorrelations are
\[
 R_t:=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}q_{j+t}\overline{q_j}
 =\eta^{t(t-1+\delta_d)}
   \sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{tj}
 =\begin{cases}d,&t=0,\\0,&1\leq t<d.\end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \absop{\widehat q_m}^2
 =\sum_{t=0}^{d-1}R_t\omega^{mt}=d
\]
for every \(m\), and the canonical target table is exactly \(1/d^2\).

\begin{theorem}[Biased exact maximizers]
\label{thm:biased}
For every \(d\geq4\), choose the final-two swap
\begin{equation}
 \kappa=(0,1,\ldots,d-3,d-1,d-2).
 \label{eq:final-swap}
\end{equation}
The strategy \eqref{eq:first-strategy} attains \(M_d+1\), has uniform local
marginals, and has a nonuniform target table.  With one-dimensional Eve,
\begin{equation}
 G(AB\mid1,d,E)
 \geq\frac1{d^2}
 +\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)}
 >\frac1{d^2}.
 \label{eq:guessing-gap}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The second cyclic autocorrelation of \eqref{eq:q-sequence} is
\[
 R_2=\sum_jq_{j+2}\overline{q_j}
 =\sum_jz_{\kappa_j}z_{\kappa_{j+1}}.
\]
It vanishes for the cyclic ordering.  The swap \eqref{eq:final-swap} gives
\begin{equation}
 R_2=(z_{d-1}-z_{d-2})(z_{d-3}-z_0),
 \qquad
 \absop{R_2}=4\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)>0.
 \label{eq:R2}
\end{equation}
Hence the Fourier magnitudes are not all \(d\).

For the quantitative estimate put
\(x_m=\absop{\widehat q_m}^2-d\) and
\(\Delta=\max_mx_m\).  Fourier inversion at lag two gives
\[
 R_2=\frac1d\sum_mx_m\omega^{-2m},
\]
where the constant term vanishes for \(d\geq4\).  Since the nonzero
mean-zero sequence \((x_m)\) has at most \(d-1\) positive entries,
\[
 d\absop{R_2}\leq\sum_m\absop{x_m}
 =2\sum_{x_m>0}x_m\leq2(d-1)\Delta.
\]
With trivial Eve, always guessing a most likely pair gives
\(G=(d+\Delta)/d^3\).  Substituting \eqref{eq:R2} proves
\eqref{eq:guessing-gap}.
\end{proof}

For \(d=2,3\), every ordering in this particular permutation family is
Fourier-flat.  Indeed, for any permutation \(\kappa\),
\[
 R_1=\sum_jq_{j+1}\overline{q_j}
 =\sum_jz_{\kappa_j}=\sum_kz_k=0,
 \qquad R_{d-t}=\overline{R_t}.
\]
For \(d=2\), \(R_1\) is the only nonzero-lag autocorrelation; for \(d=3\),
the only such autocorrelations are \(R_1\) and
\(R_2=\overline{R_1}\).  Thus all nonzero-lag autocorrelations vanish and
\(\absop{\widehat q_m}^2=d\) for every \(m\).  This leaves Bell-value
rigidity in those dimensions unresolved.

\section{Extension to the second augmented cyclic family}
\label{sec:second}

The second reduced family of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026} has a different
Alice measurement set but compresses the same first-family Bob observables.
Put \(\eta=\e^{\pi i/d}\), so \(\omega=\eta^2\), and define
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_\ell=
 \frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}\eta^{\ell(\ell-1)}}
 {d\sin(\pi(\ell-\tfrac12)/d)},
 \qquad 0\leq\ell<d,
 \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
where \((-1)^{-1}=-1\) at \(\ell=0\).  For Bob's observables in
\eqref{eq:first-strategy}, let
\[
 \widehat B_\ell=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\ell y}B_y
\]
and define the Hermitian functional
\begin{equation}
 \cF_d=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}
 \Reop\!\left(\overline{\lambda_\ell}A_\ell\widehat B_\ell\right),
 \qquad
 \overline{\cF}_d=\cF_d+\Reop(A_0B_d).
 \label{eq:second-functional}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient normalization]
\label{lem:lambda-normalization}
The coefficients in \eqref{eq:lambda} satisfy
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\absop{\lambda_\ell}^2=1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The logarithmic derivative of the sine product gives, away from its poles,
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\cot\!\left(x+\frac{k\pi}{d}\right)
 =d\cot(dx).
\]
Differentiating yields the general identity
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\csc^2\!\left(x+\frac{k\pi}{d}\right)
 =d^2\csc^2(dx).
 \label{eq:csc-square-sum}
\end{equation}
At \(x=-\pi/(2d)\), the right-hand side is \(d^2\).  Since the numerator
of every \(\lambda_\ell\) has modulus one,
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\absop{\lambda_\ell}^2
 =\frac1{d^2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}
 \csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(\ell-\tfrac12)}d\right)=1.
\]
\end{proof}

The SOS of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}, Eqs.~(22)--(23), with prefactor
\(1/(2d)\), is short and load-bearing.
Fourier orthogonality and unitarity of the \(B_y\)'s give
\[
 \sum_\ell \widehat B_\ell^\dagger\widehat B_\ell=d^2\Id,
 \qquad \sum_\ell\absop{\lambda_\ell}^2=1.
\]
Thus, for
\(P_\ell=d\lambda_\ell\Id-A_\ell\widehat B_\ell\),
\begin{equation}
 d\Id-\cF_d=\frac1{2d}\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}P_\ell^\dagger P_\ell.
 \label{eq:second-sos}
\end{equation}
The identity requires only cross-party commutation, so the source value also
has the immediate commuting-operator reading
\[
 \beta_q(\cF_d)=\beta_{qa}(\cF_d)=\beta_{qc}(\cF_d)=d,
 \qquad
 \beta_q(\overline{\cF}_d)=\beta_{qa}(\overline{\cF}_d)
 =\beta_{qc}(\overline{\cF}_d)=d+1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Permutation maximizers of the second family]
\label{thm:second}
For every permutation \(\kappa\), define
\begin{equation}
 r_\ell=\eta^{-\ell(\ell-1+\delta_d)},
 \qquad
 D_\ell=r_\ell X\operatorname{diag}
 (\omega^{-\ell\kappa_j}),
 \qquad
 A_\ell=\overline{D_\ell}.
 \label{eq:second-A}
\end{equation}
Together with the \(B_y\)'s in \eqref{eq:first-strategy}, these are
order-\(d\) observables and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat B_\ell=d\lambda_\ell D_\ell.
 \label{eq:Fourier-compression}
\end{equation}
They annihilate every factor in \eqref{eq:second-sos}.  With \(B_d=X\), the
augmented value is \(d+1\).  For the swap \eqref{eq:final-swap}, the target
pair \((A_1,B_d)\) has exactly the table \eqref{eq:target-table} and the gap
\eqref{eq:guessing-gap} for every \(d\geq4\).  The complete matrix of complex
first-harmonic correlators is independent of \(\kappa\).  Under the
alternative source-appendix convention that adjoints all Bob observables,
the target table is the same up to the harmless outcome inversion
\(b\mapsto-b\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z_t=\eta^{2t+\delta_d}\), write
\[
 s_{0,t}=\varepsilon_t\eta^{t+\delta_d/2},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_t=\begin{cases}
 +1,&2t+\delta_d<d,\\
 -1,&2t+\delta_d>d.
 \end{cases}
\]
There is no equality case, and
\(s_{y,t}=s_{0,t+y\bmod d}\).  The diagonal weight of
\(\widehat B_\ell\) at a
label \(t\) is therefore \(\omega^{-\ell t}S_\ell\), with
\[
 S_\ell=\sum_t\omega^{\ell t}\overline{s_{0,t}}.
\]
Let \(n=(d+1-\delta_d)/2\) and \(u_\ell=\eta^{2\ell-1}\).  Since
\(u_\ell^d=-1\), two geometric sums give
\[
 S_\ell
 =\eta^{-\delta_d/2}
 \left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}u_\ell^t-\sum_{t=n}^{d-1}u_\ell^t\right)
 =d\lambda_\ell r_\ell.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:Fourier-compression}, including its phase.

By \eqref{eq:weighted-cycle} and
\(\sum_j\kappa_j=d(d-1)/2\),
\[
 D_\ell^d
 =(-1)^{\ell(\ell-1+\delta_d)+\ell(d-1)}\Id=\Id,
\]
because \(\ell(\ell+d-2+\delta_d)\) is even.  Equation
\eqref{eq:Fourier-compression} gives
\[
 P_\ell\lvert\Phi_d\rangle
 =d\lambda_\ell
 (\Id-\overline{D_\ell}\otimes D_\ell)\lvert\Phi_d\rangle=0.
\]
Also \(A_0=X\), so \(X\otimes X\) fixes \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\).
Finally
\[
 A_1=X\operatorname{diag}(\eta^{2\kappa_j+\delta_d})
 =X\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),
\]
which identifies the target table with \eqref{eq:target-table}.  For
\(0\leq\ell,y<d\), the trace identity and \(B_y^T=V_y^\dagger\) give
\begin{align*}
 \langle A_\ell B_y\rangle
 &=\frac{\overline{r_\ell}}d
   \sum_j\omega^{\ell\kappa_j}
   \overline{s_{y,\kappa_j}}\\
 &=\frac{\overline{r_\ell}\omega^{-\ell y}}d
   \sum_t\omega^{\ell t}\overline{s_{0,t}}\\
 &=\lambda_\ell\omega^{-\ell y},
\end{align*}
where the last equality uses \(S_\ell=d\lambda_\ell r_\ell\).  Likewise,
\[
 \langle A_\ell B_d\rangle
 =\frac{\overline{r_\ell}}d
  \sum_j\omega^{\ell\kappa_j}
 =\delta_{\ell0}.
\]
All local first moments vanish because the displayed Alice and Bob
observables are weighted shifts.  These formulas give the complete complex
first-harmonic correlator matrix and show directly that it is independent of
\(\kappa\).
\end{proof}

The theorem uses the SOS of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}, Eqs.~(22)--(23), to
prove global optimality; merely annihilating a candidate collection of
operators would not have sufficed.
What is common to both families is the root ordering, the Bob cycle, and the
target table.  The second family additionally requires the exact Fourier
phases in \eqref{eq:second-A}.  No statement is made here about a complete
classification of its maximizing face.

\section{Randomness interpretation}
\label{sec:randomness}

For \(\mu\in\{q,qa,qc\}\) and either augmented family, define the model-
indexed value-conditioned guessing quantity
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mathrm{val}}^\mu(d;\mathcal B)
 =\sup_{\substack{\mathsf S\in\mathfrak S_\mu:\\
 \langle\mathcal B_d\rangle_{\mathsf S}=\beta_\mu(\mathcal B_d)}}
 G_{\mathsf S}(AB\mid1,d,E),
 \qquad
 \mathcal B\in\{\overline{\cI},\overline{\cF}\}.
 \label{eq:gval-model}
\end{equation}
The finite-dimensional construction embeds into both larger models, so
Theorems~\ref{thm:biased} and \ref{thm:second} imply, for every such \(\mu\),
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mathrm{val}}^\mu(d;\mathcal B)
 \geq\frac1{d^2}
 +\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)}
 >\frac1{d^2}
 \qquad(d\geq4).
 \label{eq:value-conditioned}
\end{equation}
This is only a lower bound on the supremal adversarial guessing probability
over the maximizing face.  It comes from one explicit strategy with trivial
Eve; the final-two swap is not claimed to be worst possible.

The scope can be stated in one sentence:
\begin{quote}
\emph{The maximum scalar Bell value alone does not identify the canonical
behavior or certify maximal global randomness across the entire maximizing
face.}
\end{quote}

This conclusion has a precise relationship to the originating conjecture.
Conjecture~2 of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026} states that maximal violation of
the first augmented functional certifies \(2\log_2d\) random bits for
\((x,y)=(1,d)\).  Its printed maximum contains the isolated normalization
discrepancy described in Remark~\ref{rem:normalization}; for the displayed
operator the actual maximum is \(M_d+1\).  In that normalization, the
constructed strategies prove that the scalar-value implication
\[
 \langle\overline{\cI}_d\rangle=M_d+1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 G(AB\mid1,d,E)=\frac1{d^2}
\]
is false for every \(d\geq4\).  This is a counterexample to the displayed
Bell-value claim in those dimensions, not a determination of the exact
worst-case guessing probability and not a statement about protocols that
condition on additional observed data.

\begin{corollary}[Behavior-level nonuniqueness]
\label{cor:behavior-nonunique}
For either augmented family and every \(d\geq4\), the maximal scalar value
does not determine a unique behavior, even modulo local output relabelings.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The canonical phase order has the uniform target table \(1/d^2\), whereas
the final-two swap has the nonuniform table of
Theorems~\ref{thm:biased} and \ref{thm:second}.  A local output relabeling at
either target input only permutes the entries of that table and therefore
cannot turn a uniform table into a nonuniform one.  Thus the two exact
maximizers represent inequivalent behaviors under those relabelings.
\end{proof}

This behavior-level failure of uniqueness is the only self-testing-related
consequence used here.  We do not claim a classification under every
strategy-level equivalence involving local isometries, ancillary systems, or
transposition.

At \(d=4\), Appendix~\ref{app:d4} gives
\(\max_{a,b}p(a,b\mid1,4)=3/32\).  For the displayed trivial-Eve
realization its min-entropy is exactly
\begin{equation}
 -\log_2(3/32)=5-\log_2 3=3.415037\ldots<4.
 \label{eq:d4-entropy}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if
\(H_{\mathrm{val}}^\mu(d;\mathcal B)
:=-\log_2G_{\mathrm{val}}^\mu(d;\mathcal B)\), then the scalar maximum
certifies at most \(5-\log_2 3\) bits in every model above.  The
number is an upper bound on value-only worst-case conditional min-entropy,
not its exact optimized value.

It is equally important to state what does not follow.  The result does not
dispute a calculation that fixes the complete canonical behavior, and it does
not show that the canonical strategy lacks maximal global randomness.  In
fact, the numerical program described in Appendix B.1 of
Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026} reconstructs the full distribution of the
canonical strategy before optimizing Eve's guessing probability.  The
root-swapped strategy has different higher Fourier correlators and therefore
does not satisfy those full-behavior constraints.

A full-behavior SDP may thus return \(G=1/d^2\) for the canonical table while
\eqref{eq:value-conditioned} holds for the scalar maximum.  Passing from the
first statement to the second would require uniqueness of the maximizing
behavior, self-testing strong enough to control the target PVMs, or other
statistics that exclude the phase-permuted realization.  The source's
\(d=3\) self-testing theorem for the second family is outside our
\(d\geq4\) construction and is not challenged.

\begin{corollary}[No value-only endpoint robustness]
For either augmented family and every \(d\geq4\), there is no valid bound for
all strategies of the form
\[
 G(AB\mid1,d,E)\leq\frac1{d^2}+f_d(\varepsilon),
 \qquad f_d(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\varepsilon\) is only the Bell deficit from the maximum.
More precisely, the asserted bound is required to hold for every strategy
whose deficit is at most \(\varepsilon\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The biased exact maximizer has deficit zero, hence is feasible for every
\(\varepsilon\geq0\), and
has the fixed positive gap in \eqref{eq:value-conditioned}.  Bounds that use
additional observed correlators or the complete behavior are not excluded.
\end{proof}

\section{Benchmarks and design conditions for low-setting private randomness}
\label{sec:settings}

The permutation mechanism is a design warning, not a universal lower bound
on settings.  This section separates one exact resource baseline, a complete
binary benchmark, and a positive sufficient composition criterion from the
still-open higher-dimensional problem.

\begin{proposition}[One-input certification baseline]
\label{prop:one-input}
If either party has only one input, every finite-output nonsignalling behavior
is local and admits a compatible finite-dimensional pure projective
realization in which Eve guesses both outputs perfectly at every input pair.
Therefore neither a scalar score nor even the complete behavior in such a
scenario can device-independently force positive private global randomness
against all compatible realizations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose Alice has one input.  Nonsignalling makes
\(p(a)=\sum_bp(a,b\mid y)\) independent of \(y\).  For \(p(a)>0\), let
\(r_y(b\mid a)=p(a,b\mid y)/p(a)\), and choose an arbitrary conditional
distribution when \(p(a)=0\).  The hidden variable
\[
 \lambda=(a,b_1,\ldots,b_{m_B}),
 \qquad
 \mu(\lambda)=p(a)\prod_yr_y(b_y\mid a)
\]
reproduces the whole behavior by outputting the stored entries.  The pure
state
\[
 \sum_\lambda\sqrt{\mu(\lambda)}
 \lvert\lambda\rangle_A\lvert\lambda\rangle_B
 \lvert\lambda\rangle_E
\]
with diagonal grouping PVMs realizes it.  Eve reads \(\lambda\) and guesses
perfectly.  The other case is symmetric.
\end{proof}

This is a standard locality fact, and it concerns the total test-input
alphabets.  It does not say that a trusted one-input realization is
intrinsically random or nonrandom, nor does it prevent a larger Bell test
from having one designated generation input on each wing.

\begin{theorem}[Binary two-input benchmark]
\label{thm:binary-benchmark}
For binary Hermitian unitaries with cross-party commutation, set
\[
 \mathcal W_2=A_0B_0-2A_0B_1+2A_1B_0+2A_1B_1.
\]
Its finite-dimensional tensor-product, approximate, and commuting-operator
values are all \(3\sqrt3\).  Moreover, every attaining finite-dimensional
tensor-product strategy, with arbitrary purifying Eve, satisfies at the
target pair \((x,y)=(0,0)\)
\[
 \sigma_E^{ab}=\rho_E/4\qquad(a,b\in\bbZ_2).
\]
Hence \(G(AB\mid0,0,E)=1/4\) and
\(H_{\min}(AB\mid E)=2\) bits.
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:binary} gives a self-contained two-square SOS and
operator-valued Fourier proof.  The result is established prior art: after
the output flip \(B_1\mapsto-B_1\), this is the \(\delta=\pi/6\) member of
the Wooltorton--Brown--Colbeck family~\cite{WooltortonEtAl2022}.  We include
the proof as a normalization and privacy benchmark, not as a novelty claim.

\begin{corollary}[Binary setting minimality]
\label{cor:binary-minimality}
In the finite-dimensional projective device-independent model considered
here, two binary inputs per party suffice to certify two private global
output bits.  One input on either wing cannot force any private global
randomness against all compatible realizations.  Thus \((2,2)\) is
componentwise minimal for this binary certification task.
\end{corollary}

For general \(d\), operator-valued Fourier coefficients make the privacy
condition explicit.  For target PVMs \(R=\{R_a\}\) and \(Q=\{Q_c\}\), put
\[
 \widehat\sigma_E(k,\ell)
 =\sum_{a,c}\omega^{ka+\ell c}\sigma_E^{a,c},
 \qquad
 \sigma_E^{a,c}=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{k,\ell}
 \omega^{-ka-\ell c}\widehat\sigma_E(k,\ell).
\]
Thus privacy is equivalent to
\(\widehat\sigma_E(0,0)=\rho_E\) and
\(\widehat\sigma_E(k,\ell)=0\) for every nonzero Fourier index.  Observed
uniformity tests only the scalar trace of these conditions.

\begin{lemma}[Private-MUB composition; sufficient criterion]
\label{lem:private-mub}
Let \(\lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE}\) be a finite-dimensional purification.
Alice has two \(d\)-outcome PVMs \(P=\{P_b\}_{b\in\bbZ_d}\) and
\(R=\{R_a\}_{a\in\bbZ_d}\), and Bob has a \(d\)-outcome PVM
\(Q=\{Q_c\}_{c\in\bbZ_d}\).  Let \(\pi\) be a permutation of \(\bbZ_d\)
and define
\[
 \tau_E^b=\Tr_{AB}\!\left[(P_b\otimes\Id)
 \lvert\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi\rvert(P_b\otimes\Id)\right].
\]
Suppose, for every \(a,b\), that
\begin{align*}
 \tau_E^b&=\rho_E/d,\\
 (\Id\otimes Q_{\pi(b)}\otimes\Id)\lvert\Psi\rangle
 &=(P_b\otimes\Id)\lvert\Psi\rangle,\\
 (P_bR_aP_b\otimes\Id)\lvert\Psi\rangle
 &=d^{-1}(P_b\otimes\Id)\lvert\Psi\rangle.
\end{align*}
Then the target pair \((R,Q)\) obeys
\[
 \sigma_E^{a,\pi(b)}=\rho_E/d^2
 \qquad\text{for every }a,b.
\]
The hypotheses are sufficient; no necessity or low-setting existence claim
is made.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(a,b\) and a Hermitian test operator \(T_E\).  Suppressing identity
factors, projectivity, cross-party commutation, and the matching hypothesis
give
\begin{align*}
 \Tr(T_E\sigma_E^{a,\pi(b)})
 &=\langle\Psi|R_aQ_{\pi(b)}T_EQ_{\pi(b)}R_a|\Psi\rangle\\
 &=\langle\Psi|P_bR_aP_bT_E|\Psi\rangle\\
 &=\frac1d\langle\Psi|P_bT_E|\Psi\rangle
 =\frac1{d^2}\Tr(T_E\rho_E).
\end{align*}
Equality against every Hermitian \(T_E\) proves the operator identity.
\end{proof}

The lemma identifies what a successful composition would have to certify:
a private reference PVM, perfect matching to Bob, and a state-supported MUB
sandwich.  It does not itself construct a Bell functional enforcing those
hypotheses.

\section{Discussion and open problems}

The exact value theorem and the biased maximizers expose a structural
distinction between optimality and rigidity.  The first operator controls a
sum of moduli of first-order linear functions of the relative unitary.  Its
score is sharp enough to determine the allowable scalar phases but not their
cyclic order.  That missing order is invisible to the Bell score and visible
to the target output distribution.

Several questions are now sharply posed.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Determine whether the support-level rigidity conditions, admissible
phase permutations, direct sums, and irrelevant ancillary systems generate
all attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizers of the first
augmented family.  First classify the minimal-support case \(\dim K=d\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:support-rigidity} then forces the equality-root spectrum to
be simple; determine whether every such maximizer is equivalent, under the
explicitly allowed symmetries, to a member of the permutation orbit of
Theorem~\ref{thm:biased}.  Then separately characterize the \(qa\) and \(qc\)
maximizing faces.
\item Determine the exact worst-case guessing probability at the Bell maximum;
the bound \eqref{eq:value-conditioned} need not be tight.
\item Resolve the \(d=2,3\) maximizing faces for the first augmented family.
The permutation orbit studied here is uniform in those dimensions.
\item Classify which phase permutations are related by local isometries and
output relabelings, and which produce genuinely different full behaviors.
\item Find a low-setting modification whose Bell score controls the missing
higher Fourier data, and determine whether it is rigid or robustly
self-testing.
\item Develop robust bounds conditioned on a minimal set of extra correlators,
rather than either one scalar score or the entire behavior.
\end{enumerate}
The positive private-MUB criterion and the negative permutation-blindness
mechanism point in the same direction: a successful low-setting score must
control operator-valued adversarial information, not merely a scalar maximum
or an unordered first-harmonic spectrum.
These problems are natural opportunities for collaboration with the
originating authors and the broader Bell-inequality community.

\section{Proof of the scalar extremum}
\label{app:scalar}

Write \(z=\e^{2is}\), \(h=\pi/d\), and \(a=h/2\).  Half of the sum in
\eqref{eq:scalar-bound} is
\[
 G_d(s)=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\absop{\cos(s+yh)},
\]
which has period \(h\).

If \(d=2m+1\), take \(-a\leq s\leq a\) and reindex by
\(k=-m,\ldots,m\).  Every angle lies in \([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\), so
\[
 G_d(s)=\sum_{k=-m}^m\cos(s+kh)=\csc(a)\cos s.
\]
The maximum occurs exactly at \(s\equiv0\pmod h\), equivalently \(z^d=1\).

If \(d=2m\), take \(0\leq s\leq h\).  The first \(m\) cosines are
nonnegative and the last \(m\) are nonpositive.  Two finite sums give
\[
 G_d(s)=\sum_{y=0}^{m-1}\cos(s+yh)
 -\sum_{y=m}^{2m-1}\cos(s+yh)
 =\csc(a)\cos(s-a).
\]
The maximum occurs exactly at \(s\equiv a\pmod h\), equivalently \(z^d=-1\).
Multiplying by two proves Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}, including the equality set.

\section{Proof of support-level rigidity}
\label{app:rigidity-proof}

We first isolate the finite-dimensional rank step.

\begin{lemma}[Reflection-product rank bound]
\label{lem:reflection-rank}
Let \(T\) be unitary on an \(n\)-dimensional space and let \(E\) be an
orthogonal projection of rank \(r\).  If
\[
 T^d=\Id,
 \qquad \bigl(T(\Id-2E)\bigr)^d=-\Id,
\]
then \(n\leq dr\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(R=\Id-2E\).  Factoring the ordered product gives
\[
 (TR)^d=T^dR_{d-1}\cdots R_0,
 \qquad R_j=T^{-j}RT^j.
\]
Thus \(-\Id=R_{d-1}\cdots R_0\).  Each
\(\Id-R_j=2T^{-j}ET^j\) has rank \(r\), while repeated use of
\(\Id-AB=(\Id-A)+A(\Id-B)\) gives
\[
 \rankop(\Id-R_{d-1}\cdots R_0)
 \leq\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\rankop(\Id-R_j)=dr.
\]
The left side is \(\rankop(2\Id)=n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:support-rigidity}]
Equality in the first reduced bound and in
\(\Reop(A_0B_d)\leq\Id\) is forced separately.  Every positive term in
the augmented gap therefore vanishes on \(\lvert\Psi\rangle\):
\begin{equation}
 P_y\lvert\Psi\rangle=0,
 \qquad
 (M_d\Id-F_d(U))\lvert\Psi\rangle=0,
 \qquad
 (A_0B_d)\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert\Psi\rangle.
 \label{eq:rigidity-residuals}
\end{equation}
Here and below identity factors on the other parties are suppressed.  The
last relation follows from
\(
 \Id-\Reop W=\tfrac12(\Id-W)^\dagger(\Id-W)
\)
for unitary \(W\).

For every Alice operator \(R\), a Schmidt decomposition across
\(A:(BE)\) gives the support-cancellation equivalence
\begin{equation}
 (R\otimes\Id_{BE})\lvert\Psi\rangle=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R|_K=0.
 \label{eq:support-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the squared norm is
\(\Tr(\rho_AR^\dagger R)\).  This formulation includes nonfaithful
\(\rho_A\) and makes no assertion on \(K^\perp\).
The middle relation in \eqref{eq:rigidity-residuals} therefore gives
\[
 K\subseteq L:=\operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_d}(U),
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_d=\{z\in\bbT:z^d=(-1)^{d-1}\}.
\]
The augmented stabilizer, after tracing out \(BE\), gives
\(\rho_A=A_0\rho_AA_0^\dagger\), hence \(A_0K=K\).

Write \(L_y=V_y\absop{L_y}\) and define the possible kernel projection
\[
 E_y^{\mathrm{bad}}=\mathbf1_{\{-\omega^{-y}\}}(U),
 \qquad
 F_y=V_yV_y^\dagger
 =A_0(\Id-E_y^{\mathrm{bad}})A_0^\dagger.
\]
The bad phase is not in \(\mathcal Z_d\), because its \(d\)-th power is
\((-1)^d\).  Thus \(E_y^{\mathrm{bad}}K=0\), and \(A_0K=K\) gives
\(F_y|_K=\Id_K\).  Polar functional calculus factors the first residual as
\[
 P_y=\absop{L_y^\dagger}^{1/2}(\Id-V_yB_y).
\]
The vector
\(q_y=(\Id-V_yB_y)\lvert\Psi\rangle\) lies in
\(\operatorname{ran}F_y\) because \(F_y\) fixes the state's Alice support
and \(F_yV_y=V_y\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:rigidity-residuals} also puts
\(q_y\) in \(\ker\absop{L_y^\dagger}^{1/2}=\ker F_y\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 (V_yB_y)\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert\Psi\rangle.
 \label{eq:polar-stabilizer}
\end{equation}
This cancellation uses neither an inverse nor a globally unitary extension
of \(V_y\).

The space \(K\) is contained in the initial support of \(V_y\), so \(V_y\)
is isometric there.  Tracing \eqref{eq:polar-stabilizer} over \(BE\) yields
\(\rho_A=V_y\rho_AV_y^\dagger\), and hence \(V_yK=K\).  Thus
\(S_y=A_0^\dagger V_y\) is unitary on \(K\).  On \(L\), polar functional
calculus gives
\[
 S_y=s_y(U),
 \qquad
 s_y(z)=\frac{1+\omega^yz}{\absop{1+\omega^yz}},
 \qquad s_y(z)^2=\omega^yz.
\]
It follows that \(U|_K=\omega^{-y}(S_y|_K)^2\), so \(U(K)\subseteq K\).
Since \(U\) is unitary and \(K\) is finite-dimensional, this inclusion
implies \(U(K)=K\); hence \(K\) reduces \(U\).
Let \(E_k\) be the spectral projection of \(U|_K\) corresponding to the
root \(z_k\) in \eqref{eq:equality-roots}.

Put \(\eta=\e^{\pi i/d}\), read \(y+1\) modulo \(d\), and let
\(r_*=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\).  Direct division of the adjacent half-angle
phases gives
\[
 \overline{s_y(z_k)}s_{y+1}(z_k)
 =\begin{cases}
 -\eta,&[k+y]_d=r_*,\\
 \eta,&[k+y]_d\neq r_*.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently, for \(k(y)=[r_*-y]_d\),
\begin{equation}
 S_y^\dagger S_{y+1}=\eta(\Id-2E_{k(y)}),
 \qquad
 V_{y+1}=\eta V_y(\Id-2E_{k(y)})
 \quad\text{on }K.
 \label{eq:adjacent-reflection}
\end{equation}
The map \(y\mapsto k(y)\) is a permutation of the root labels.

Since \(V_y\) is Alice-side and \(B_y\) is Bob-side, the two operators
commute.  Thus \eqref{eq:polar-stabilizer}, together with \(B_y^d=\Id\),
gives
\[
 V_y^d\lvert\Psi\rangle
 =(V_yB_y)^d\lvert\Psi\rangle
 =\lvert\Psi\rangle.
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:support-cancellation} to \(V_y^d-\Id\) gives
\(V_y^d|_K=\Id_K\).  Since \(\eta^d=-1\),
\eqref{eq:adjacent-reflection} also gives
\[
 \bigl(V_y(\Id-2E_{k(y)})\bigr)^d=-\Id_K.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:reflection-rank}, with \(n=\dim K\), yields
\(n\leq d\rankop E_k\) for every \(k\).  The \(E_k\)'s are mutually
orthogonal and sum to \(\Id_K\), so their ranks sum to \(n\).  Every one of
the \(d\) inequalities must be an equality:
\(\rankop E_k=n/d\) for all \(k\).  This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Rechecking the canonical attaining strategy}
\label{app:attainment}

This appendix verifies the lower strategy supplied in
Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026} in a polar form.  On \(\bbC^d\), let
\[
 Z\lvert j\rangle=\omega^j\lvert j\rangle,
 \qquad X\lvert j\rangle=\lvert j+1\bmod d\rangle,
 \qquad A_0=Z,\quad A_1=X.
\]
Set
\[
 W_y=\omega^yZ^\dagger X,
 \qquad H_y=\absop{\Id+W_y},
 \qquad
 Q_y=H_y^{-1}(\Id+W_y^\dagger)Z^\dagger,
 \qquad B_y=Q_y^T.
\]
The weighted-shift action gives
\[
 W_y^d=(-1)^{d-1}\Id,
 \qquad
 \spec(W_y)=\{\lambda:\lambda^d=(-1)^{d-1}\}.
\]
The spectrum is simple, and \(-1\) is absent; hence \(H_y\) is invertible.
For \(L_y=Z+\omega^yX=Z(\Id+W_y)\),
\[
 L_y=\underbrace{Z(\Id+W_y)H_y^{-1}}_{V_y}H_y
\]
is the polar decomposition, and \(Q_y=V_y^\dagger\) is unitary.

It remains to check the measurement order.  On the displayed spectrum put
\(p(z)=(1+z)/\absop{1+z}\).  Since
\(Z^\dagger W_yZ=\omega^{-1}W_y\),
\[
 Q_y^d=\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}p(\omega^{-r}W_y)^\dagger.
\]
For each eigenvalue \(\lambda\), the corresponding scalar equals
\[
 \overline{\frac{\prod_r(1+\omega^{-r}\lambda)}
 {\prod_r\absop{1+\omega^{-r}\lambda}}}
 =\overline{\frac{1-(-\lambda)^d}{\absop{1-(-\lambda)^d}}}=1.
\]
Thus \(B_y^d=\Id\).  The weighted-cycle characteristic polynomial shows
that each \(d\)-th root occurs.

Finally,
\[
 \langle\Phi_d\vert L_y\otimes B_y\vert\Phi_d\rangle
 =\frac1d\Tr(L_yV_y^\dagger)=\frac1d\Tr(H_y).
\]
The spectrum of \(W_y\) is the scalar equality set, so summing over \(y\)
gives \(\langle\cI_d\rangle=M_d\).  Taking the extra Bob observable
\(B_d=Z^\dagger\) gives \(\langle Z\otimes Z^\dagger\rangle=1\), proving
Corollary~\ref{cor:first-augmented}.

To identify this polar form with the source observables rather than only
compare their values, write the source expansion as
\[
 B_y^{\mathrm{src}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\lambda^{\mathrm{src}}_{y,k}
 X^{k+1}Z^k,
 \qquad
 \lambda^{\mathrm{src}}_{y,k}=
 \frac{(-1)^k\omega^{k(k+1)/2}\omega^{-y(k+1)}}
 {d\sin(\pi(k+\tfrac12)/d)}.
\]
Its discrete Fourier transform is
\[
 \sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\omega^{my}\lambda^{\mathrm{src}}_{y,k}
 =d\lambda^{\mathrm{src}}_{0,k}\,
 \delta_{k,m-1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ d)}.
\]
At \(m=0,1\) this gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 \sum_yB_y^{\mathrm{src}}=\alpha_dZ^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \sum_y\omega^yB_y^{\mathrm{src}}=\alpha_dX,
 \qquad
 \alpha_d=\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right).
 \label{eq:source-fourier}
\end{equation}
Thus the source strategy saturates the sum of the termwise polar bounds.
Every \(L_y\) is invertible here, so equality in each positive polar deficit
uniquely forces \((B_y^{\mathrm{src}})^T=V_y^\dagger\).  Hence
\(B_y^{\mathrm{src}}=Q_y^T=\overline{V_y}\): the transpose and complex
conjugation are fixed by
\(\langle\Phi_d|R\otimes S|\Phi_d\rangle=d^{-1}\Tr(RS^T)\).

For \(d=3\), the coefficient formula reduces to
\[
 B_y=\frac13\left(2Z^2+2\omega^{2y}X
 -\omega^{y+1}X^2Z\right),
\]
the explicit source observable, with \(\alpha_3=2\).  This appendix claims
no new attaining strategy.

\section{Binary two-input benchmark}
\label{app:binary}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-benchmark} directly, while crediting its
prior-art origin in the main text.  Cross-party identity factors are
suppressed.  The exact operator identity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 3\sqrt3\Id-\mathcal W_2
 ={}&\frac1{2\sqrt3}
 \bigl(\sqrt3A_0-B_0+2B_1\bigr)^2\\
 &+\frac1{\sqrt3}
 \bigl(\sqrt3A_1-B_0-B_1\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:binary-sos}
\end{equation}
Its expansion uses only \(A_x^2=B_y^2=\Id\) and cross-party commutation.
The two same-party Bob anticommutator terms cancel, so
\(\mathcal W_2\leq3\sqrt3\Id\) already in the commuting model.

If a finite-dimensional purified strategy attains equality, both squares
annihilate \(\lvert\Psi\rangle\):
\begin{equation}
 (B_0-2B_1)\lvert\Psi\rangle=\sqrt3A_0\lvert\Psi\rangle,
 \qquad
 (B_0+B_1)\lvert\Psi\rangle=\sqrt3A_1\lvert\Psi\rangle.
 \label{eq:binary-equality}
\end{equation}
Define the Hermitian operators
\[
 X_A=\frac{A_0+2A_1}{\sqrt3},
 \qquad
 Z_B=\frac{B_0-2B_1}{\sqrt3}.
\]
Solving \eqref{eq:binary-equality} gives, explicitly as on-state relations,
\[
 B_0\lvert\Psi\rangle=X_A\lvert\Psi\rangle,
 \qquad
 Z_B\lvert\Psi\rangle=A_0\lvert\Psi\rangle.
\]
Cross-party commutation and unitarity imply
\(X_A^2\lvert\Psi\rangle=Z_B^2\lvert\Psi\rangle
=\lvert\Psi\rangle\).  The exact identities
\[
 \{A_0,X_A\}=\sqrt3(X_A^2-\Id),
 \qquad
 \{Z_B,B_0\}=\sqrt3(Z_B^2-\Id)
\]
therefore yield
\begin{equation}
 \{A_0,X_A\}\lvert\Psi\rangle=0,
 \qquad
 \{Z_B,B_0\}\lvert\Psi\rangle=0.
 \label{eq:binary-anticommutation}
\end{equation}
No global anticommutation relation is asserted.

Put \(S_A=X_AB_0\) and \(S_B=A_0Z_B\).  These Hermitian operators stabilize
the state.  For every Hermitian Eve test \(T_E\), cross-party commutation and
\eqref{eq:binary-anticommutation} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle A_0T_E\rangle
 &=\langle S_AA_0T_E\rangle
 =\langle X_AA_0B_0T_E\rangle
 =-\langle A_0T_ES_A\rangle
 =-\langle A_0T_E\rangle,\\
 \langle B_0T_E\rangle
 &=\langle S_BB_0T_E\rangle
 =-\langle B_0T_ES_B\rangle
 =-\langle B_0T_E\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the commuting factor \(B_0T_E\), respectively \(A_0T_E\), may be
moved through the relevant on-state anticommutator; no global
anticommutation is used.  Finally,
\[
 \langle A_0B_0T_E\rangle=\langle A_0X_AT_E\rangle.
\]
The left side is real, while the complex conjugate of the right side is its
negative by \eqref{eq:binary-anticommutation}; hence the joint moment also
vanishes.  Thus all three nontrivial binary operator-valued Fourier
coefficients are zero:
\[
 \langle A_0T_E\rangle=
 \langle B_0T_E\rangle=
 \langle A_0B_0T_E\rangle=0.
\]
For
\(M_a^0=(\Id+(-1)^aA_0)/2\) and
\(N_b^0=(\Id+(-1)^bB_0)/2\), Fourier inversion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Tr(T_E\sigma_E^{ab})=\frac14\bigl(&\Tr(T_E\rho_E)
 +(-1)^a\langle A_0T_E\rangle+(-1)^b\langle B_0T_E\rangle\\
 &+(-1)^{a+b}\langle A_0B_0T_E\rangle\bigr)
 =\frac14\Tr(T_E\rho_E).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equality for every Hermitian \(T_E\) proves
\(\sigma_E^{ab}=\rho_E/4\).

For attainment, on \(\lvert\Phi_2\rangle\) take
\[
 A_0=Z,
 \quad A_1=-\tfrac12Z+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}X,
 \quad B_0=X,
 \quad B_1=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}Z+\tfrac12X.
\]
All four observables square to \(\Id\), and the maximally entangled trace
identity gives \(\langle\mathcal W_2\rangle=3\sqrt3\).  This finite strategy
and \eqref{eq:binary-sos} establish the \(q=qa=qc\) value; the preceding
operator-valued calculation establishes the stated finite-dimensional
privacy conclusion.

\section{Scoped low-setting observations}
\label{app:settings}

The standard two-input maximally-entangled-qudit self-test
\cite{SarkarEtAl2021} uses Fourier-phase bases.  Up to input swaps,
conjugation, transposition, and outcome relabeling, its four ideal tables have
the form
\begin{equation}
 p(a,b\mid x,y)
 =\frac{\sin^2(\pi\delta_{xy})}
 {d^3\sin^2(\pi(a-b+\delta_{xy})/d)},
 \qquad
 (\delta_{xy})=
 \begin{pmatrix}1/4&3/4\\-1/4&1/4\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:standard-tables}
\end{equation}
Every table has
\[
 p_{\max}=\frac1{2d^3\sin^2(\pi/(4d))}>\frac1{d^2},
\]
so no ideal input pair is uniform.  Its ideal joint min-entropy is
\[
 \log_2\!\left(2d^3\sin^2\frac{\pi}{4d}\right)
 =\log_2d+\log_2\frac{\pi^2}{8}+o(1).
\]
Perfectly anchoring a third Bob setting
to either Alice basis leaves a cross table with
\[
 p_{\max}^{\mathrm{cross}}
 =\frac1{d^3\sin^2(\pi/(2d))}>\frac1{d^2}
 \qquad(d\geq3).
\]
The exception \(d=2\) is precisely the mutually-unbiased qubit case.

There is a broader but still scoped obstruction.  Let \(F\) be the Fourier
matrix, \(\rho=\e^{\pi i/d}\), and
\(R=\operatorname{diag}(1,\rho,\ldots,\rho^{d-1})\).  The real Hermitian
operator system generated by the two ideal Alice PVMs is
\[
 \mathcal S=\{F\operatorname{diag}(x)F^\dagger
 +RF\operatorname{diag}(y)F^\dagger R^\dagger:x,y\in\mathbb R^d\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Computational-MUB exposure obstruction]
\label{prop:mub}
If \(K\in\mathcal S\) has a computational vector \(e_r\) as an eigenvector
with eigenvalue \(\kappa\), then either \(K=\kappa\Id\), or \(\kappa\) is
neither the largest nor the smallest eigenvalue of \(K\).  Consequently, no
nontrivial separately bounded term
\[
 \mathcal L=\sum_bK_b\otimes Q_b,
 \qquad K_b\in\mathcal S,
\]
can expose the computational PVM on \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\) through a
coefficientwise spectral upper bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(K=C+RC'R^\dagger\), with \(C,C'\) Hermitian circulants.  Index rows
and columns by \(0,\ldots,d-1\), and write
\[
 C_{jk}=c_{j-k},
 \qquad
 C'_{jk}=c'_{j-k},
\]
where the coefficient subscripts are understood modulo \(d\), while the
exponent in
\[
 (RC'R^\dagger)_{jk}=\rho^{j-k}c'_{j-k}
\]
uses the ordinary integer difference \(j-k\).  The diagonal equation gives
\(c_0+c'_0=\kappa\).  The off-diagonal components of
\(Ke_r=\kappa e_r\), first with \(j=r+t\) and then with \(j=r-t\), are
\begin{align*}
 c_t+\rho^t c'_t&=0
 &&(1\leq t\leq d-1-r),\\
 c_{d-t}+\rho^{-t}c'_{d-t}&=0
 &&(1\leq t\leq r).
\end{align*}
Put \(s=\min(r,d-1-r)\).  Since \(C\) and \(C'\) are Hermitian,
\[
 c_{d-t}=\overline{c_t},
 \qquad
 c'_{d-t}=\overline{c'_t}
 \qquad(1\leq t\leq d-1).
\]
Conjugating the second indexed family and combining it with the first
therefore gives
\[
 c_t+\rho^t c'_t=0
 \qquad(1\leq t\leq\max(r,d-1-r)=d-1-s).
\]
For \(s+1\leq t\leq d-1\), the index \(u=d-t\) satisfies
\(1\leq u\leq d-1-s\).  Applying the preceding equation at \(u\) and
taking its Hermitian conjugate gives
\[
 c_t+\rho^{t-d}c'_t=0.
\]
Because \(\rho^d=-1\), this is
\[
 c_t-\rho^t c'_t=0
 \qquad(s+1\leq t\leq d-1).
\]
On the common index range
\[
 s+1\leq t\leq d-1-s
\]
(which may be empty), adding and subtracting the last two equations shows
directly that
\[
 c_t=c'_t=0.
\]
Thus only the off-diagonal cyclic modes
\(t\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\cup\{d-s,\ldots,d-1\}\) can survive, with both
sets empty when \(s=0\).

For \(1\leq t\leq s\), the relation
\(c_t+\rho^t c'_t=0\), and its Hermitian conjugate, cancel the entries
having ordinary differences \(t\) and \(-t\).  The only surviving
occurrences are therefore the wraparound differences \(t-d\) and \(d-t\).
Set
\[
 a_t:=c_t+\rho^{t-d}c'_t
     =c_t-\rho^t c'_t
     =2c_t
 \qquad(1\leq t\leq s).
\]
After grouping the first \(s\), middle \(d-2s\), and last \(s\)
coordinates, the upper-right corner has entries
\[
 T_{pq}:=(K-\kappa\Id)_{p,d-s+q}
 =\begin{cases}
 a_{s+p-q},&0\leq p\leq q\leq s-1,\\
 0,&0\leq q<p\leq s-1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Hence \(T\) is an \(s\)-by-\(s\) upper-triangular Toeplitz matrix, and
Hermiticity supplies the lower-left corner:
\[
 K-\kappa\Id=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0&T\\0&0&0\\T^\dagger&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
On the first and last coordinate blocks this is
\(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\T^\dagger&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\).
If \(s=0\), the preceding vanishing already gives \(K=\kappa\Id\).  If
\(s>0\) and \(T=0\), then \(T_{0,s-t}=a_t=0\) for every
\(1\leq t\leq s\).  Hence \(c_t=0\) by \(a_t=2c_t\), and \(c'_t=0\) by
\(c_t+\rho^tc'_t=0\).  Hermiticity eliminates the modes
\(d-s,\ldots,d-1\), while the middle modes already vanish; together with
\(c_0+c'_0=\kappa\), this gives \(K=\kappa\Id\).  Thus a nonscalar \(K\)
forces \(T\neq0\).
An SVD of \(T\) shows that this block matrix has nonzero eigenvalues
\(\pm\sigma_j(T)\).  Hence a nonscalar \(K\) has spectrum on both sides of
\(\kappa\).

To saturate a coefficientwise upper bound on the maximally entangled state
with computational \(Q_b\), every computational outcome has nonzero weight
and the corresponding \(e_b\) must be a maximal eigenvector of \(K_b\).
The first part makes that impossible unless all relevant \(K_b\) are scalar,
in which case the term constrains no measurement.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:mub} rules out only this separately bounded local-
spectral route.  It is not a no-go theorem for \((2,3,d,d)\), and it is not
used in any cyclic-family conclusion.

\section{Source conventions and claim boundary}
\label{app:conventions}

Our first-family definition \eqref{eq:Id} follows the displayed main-text
operator definition in Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}.  A nearby appendix writes
one Bob adjoint differently while its subsequent calculation follows the
main convention.  Replacing every Bob observable by its adjoint transports
between the two notations, but mixing them within one formula does not.  We
therefore state every adjoint explicitly and test the Fourier orientation.

For the second family, \eqref{eq:second-functional} matches the Hermitian
content of the source definition and the SOS of
Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}, Eqs.~(22)--(23), with prefactor \(1/(2d)\).
The source text adjacent to the canonical construction briefly omits Alice's
entrywise conjugation, while its theorem includes it.
Our choice \(A_\ell=\overline{D_\ell}\) is the one that annihilates the
displayed SOS on \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\).

If instead every Bob observable in the source appendix is written with an
adjoint, set \(B'_y=B_y^\dagger\) and \(B'_d=B_d^\dagger\).  This is Bob's
outcome inversion: it sends \(p(a,b)\) to \(p(a,-b)\) while preserving the
maximum, nonuniformity, and guessing probability.  It cannot be mixed
termwise with the no-adjoint convention used in
\eqref{eq:second-functional}.

The source's numerical guessing-probability table explicitly fixes the full
canonical distribution.  Our scalar-value conclusion should therefore not
be read as a criticism of that calculation.  The literal maximal-violation
conjecture and the fixed-behavior numerical experiment have different logical
hypotheses; the present construction separates them.
