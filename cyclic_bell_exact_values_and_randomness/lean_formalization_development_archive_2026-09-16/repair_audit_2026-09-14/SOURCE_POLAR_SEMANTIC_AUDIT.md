## Square roots, kernels, and boundary cases

The source modulus is obtained by finite continuous functional calculus of the actual relative unitary with scalar function `|1+z|`. Arbitrary scalar functions are allowed only on the finite matrix spectrum, whose discreteness is proved; there is no unsupported use of discontinuous functional calculus on a general Hilbert space. Positivity is proved by an explicit Gram factorization, then `CFC.sqrt_unique` identifies the modulus with `sqrt(L†L)`. The claimed inverse is constructed by reciprocal functional calculus after proving the denominator nonzero on the spectrum.

The nonzero proof is valid for every d≥2, including d=2 and composite/even dimensions: if an equality root were −1, its dth-power equation would say `(-1)^d = (-1)^(d−1)`, contradicting the nonzero sign. The coefficient sine arguments lie strictly between 0 and pi, including the first and final coefficients. `NeZero d` supplies the ambient nonzero dimension and the main conclusions explicitly assume d≥2; d=0 is not hidden by division conventions. There is no inappropriate assumption that every arbitrary paper pencil is invertible: invertibility is established specifically for the source attaining pencil.

For general `PolarCanonical`, the hypotheses are `C = V sqrt(C†C)`, `(V†V) sqrt(C†C) = sqrt(C†C)`, unitarity of B, and `CB=BC`. The target positive-factor identity is not an assumption. Initial isometry is transferred to the half-modulus by the C*-identity `XX†=0 ⇒ X=0`, without an inverse, closed range, or a unitary extension of V. Both left modulus identities use positivity and uniqueness of the actual CFC square root. Commutation of C† with the unitary B is derived from CB=BC, then square-root commutation is derived by unitary conjugation. Commutation of V with B is not needed by this proof. If C=0, the premises permit arbitrary V, but every multiplied modulus is zero and the conclusion remains correct. This is a stronger sufficient-conditions proof of the identity, not an illicit assertion of a unitary canonical factor at a kernel.
