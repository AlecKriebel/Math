                The Sperner property of standard graded
           Artinian complete intersections in characteristic zero
                                                    Alec Kriebel
                                          ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                 October 6, 2026


                                                       Abstract
         We record the Sperner property for every standard graded Artinian complete intersection
     over an arbitrary characteristic-zero ﬁeld: the maximum minimal number of generators of an
     ideal, including nonhomogeneous ideals, equals the largest coeﬃcient of its complete-intersection
     Hilbert series. The result is an immediate consequence of OpenAI’s Artinian Eisenbud–Green–
     Harris theorem and the previously published implication of Harima, Wachi, and Watanabe. We
     give an explicit account of that implication, eliminate degree-one generators, justify the passage
     to all ideals by the associated graded ﬁltration, and verify the ﬁeld scope. No new proof of EGH
     or new Lefschetz assertion is claimed.


1    Statement and attribution
Let k be a ﬁeld and let A be a standard graded Artinian k-algebra with A0 = k. Its unique
maximal ideal is m = A>0 , and Nakayama’s lemma gives µA (I) = dimk (I/mI) for every ideal I.
The Dilworth number is maxI◁A µA (I). The algebra has the Sperner property when this equals
maxj dimk Aj . The ideals in this deﬁnition include 0, A, and nonhomogeneous ideals.
Theorem 1. Let k be any characteristic-zero ﬁeld, let n ≥ 0, and let

                          A = k[x1 , . . . , xn ]/(f1 , . . . , fn ),         di = deg fi ≥ 1,

where the variables have degree one and the fi form a homogeneous regular sequence. Then
                                                                        ∏
                                                                        n
                    max µA (I) = max dimk Aj = max[tj ]                     (1 + t + · · · + tdi −1 ).    (1)
                    I◁A                j≥0                   j≥0
                                                                        i=1

If p is the last degree attaining the maximum, equality is attained by mp . For n = 0, or when all
di = 1, the algebra is k, the product is 1, and the maximum is 1, attained by A.
    Harima–Wachi–Watanabe [1, Theorem 11] proved that EGH for a given graded complete in-
tersection implies its Sperner property. Their Deﬁnition 2 already ranges over all ideals; their
Proposition 8 invokes Watanabe’s graded-ideal reduction [2, Lemma 2.4]. OpenAI’s characteristic-
zero EGH theorem [3, Corollary 1.3] supplies the previously conditional input. Its companion [4,
Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] proves stronger Betti inequalities, which are not needed here. This
note records the resulting consequence and checks its scope; neither the upstream breakthrough
nor the known implication and reductions are claimed as new work of this author. The upstream
manuscripts are dated September 23, 2026; the public collection announcement is dated October 6,
2026 [5]. No claim of ﬁrst priority is made.

2    The exact EGH input and scope reductions
The only EGH assertion needed is the following Hilbert-function statement. For S = k[z1 , . . . , zq ]
and a full homogeneous regular sequence g1 , . . . , gq of degrees ai ≥ 2, every homogeneous ideal
Q ⊇ (g1 , . . . , gq ) has a homogeneous counterpart Q′ ⊇ (z1a1 , . . . , zq q ) such that
                                                                           a


                               dimk (S/Q)j = dimk (S/Q′ )j                   for every j ≥ 0.                (2)
We may sort the degrees, since every permutation of a homogeneous system of parameters in the
Cohen–Macaulay ring S is regular; relabeling the pure-power variables then restores the original
indexing. The counterpart may have extra generators in degrees beyond those of Q; no assertion
about its generator count is required. OpenAI [3, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] gives this over
C using commuting linear forms with division-algebra coeﬃcients, an ordered basis, and monomial
extraction. We invoke that external theorem explicitly, rather than deriving it in this note.
    For completeness, its full-length ﬁeld descent needs no embedding of an arbitrary k into C.
For a ﬁxed Q, choose a ﬁnitely generated coeﬃcient ﬁeld k0 ⊆ k over Q containing coeﬃcients of
generators of Q and of the gi . Include the gi in the chosen generators over k0 . Regularity descends
to k0 by faithful ﬂatness. Embed k0 into C and apply the complex theorem after base change. The
resulting counterpart can be chosen monomial. Use the same monomial exponents to deﬁne it over
k0 and then over k. Dimensions of graded pieces are preserved by both ﬁeld extensions from k0 ,
proving (2) over k. Only full-length EGH is used; no general-length Artinian reduction is needed.
    If the original sequence has r linear members, these are linearly independent: dependence would
give fewer than n generators for an ideal of height n, contradicting the height theorem. A graded
linear coordinate change eliminates them and gives
                                      A ≃ k[z1 , . . . , zn−r ]/(g1 , . . . , gn−r ).
The remaining forms retain their degrees, all at least two. Their Artinian quotient makes them
a homogeneous system of parameters in a polynomial ring; Cohen–Macaulayness makes them a
regular sequence. The graded isomorphism preserves all ideals, the numbers µA (I), and all Hilbert
dimensions. Each eliminated factor in (1) is 1. If r = n, the case A = k is immediate.
   Successive regular-sequence exact sequences give

Put c = i (di − 1). Artinian complete-intersection duality gives Ac ≃ k and perfect multiplication
pairings Aj × Ac−j → Ac ; equivalently the Koszul resolution gives the graded canonical module
A(c). The Hilbert function is symmetric and unimodal. An elementary explanation of unimodality
is the symmetric chain decomposition used next.


3    Matching and the homogeneous bound
We reproduce the mechanism of [1, Propositions 7–8 and Theorem 11]. Let
                                          B = k[z1 , . . . , zq ]/(z1a1 , . . . , zqaq ).
Its monomial basis is the ranked product of chains 0 ≤ ei ≤ ai − 1. Such a product admits a
symmetric saturated chain decomposition. One direct induction starts with the rectangle 0 ≤ i ≤ a,
0 ≤ j ≤ b, where a ≤ b, and uses the chains
               (r, 0), (r, 1), . . . , (r, b − r), (r + 1, b − r), . . . , (a, b − r),          0 ≤ r ≤ a.

                                                                2
They partition the rectangle and their endpoint ranks sum to a + b. Applying this construction to
each old chain times a new factor preserves symmetry about half the total rank. Rank sizes are
therefore unimodal. Whenever hj ≤ hj+1 , each chain meeting rank j extends to rank j + 1: below
the middle this follows from symmetry; at or above the middle no new chain starts, so an ending
would force a strict decrease. Chain successors inject any rank-j subset into its upper shadow.
   Consequently, for every vector subspace W ⊆ Bj ,

                             hj ≤ hj+1    =⇒     dimk W ≤ dimk B1 W.                             (4)

Indeed, echelonize a basis of W by a multiplicative monomial order. Its leading monomials form a
set of size dim W . Each surviving variable multiple of a leading monomial is the leading monomial
of the corresponding product. Its upper shadow thus lies in the leading monomial set of B1 W , and
the chain injection proves the inequality.
    For V ⊆ Aj , apply (2) to the preimage of the ideal AV in the polynomial ring, obtaining an
ideal J of B with HA/AV = HB/J . The algebras A, B have the same Hilbert function, and standard
grading gives
                             dim Jj = dim V,      dim Jj+1 = dim A1 V.
For V = 0 the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, if hj ≤ hj+1 , (4) and the ideal property give

                       dim V = dim Jj ≤ dim B1 Jj ≤ dim Jj+1 = dim A1 V.                         (5)

This is the matching property of A. In particular, Hilbert matching has not been used to assert
equality of ideal-generator counts.
    Let p be the last maximal Hilbert degree. Matching holds in every degree j < p, and symmetry
                                                                                        ⊕
gives c − p ≤ p. If a homogeneous ideal I has initial degree α < p, its truncation I ′ = j≥α+1 Ij is
an ideal and
                               µ(I ′ ) − µ(I) = dim A1 Iα − dim Iα ≥ 0.                          (6)
This follows by counting the degree pieces of I/mI. Iterating, including the zero-ideal case, yields

                                         µ(I) ≤ µ(I ∩ mp ).                                      (7)

   Now assume I ⊆ mp , and set J = AnnA (mI). Gorenstein duality gives dim Ann(U ) =
dim A − dim U for every ideal U . To see the equality with the orthogonal complement, compose
multiplication with projection onto Ac . A vector orthogonal to the ideal U is orthogonal to every
multiple of its elements; nondegeneracy then shows that it annihilates them. Since mJ ⊆ Ann(I),

                            µ(J) ≥ dim Ann(mI) − dim Ann(I) = µ(I).                              (8)

The graded perfect pairings also give Ann(mu ) = mc−u+1 for 0 ≤ u ≤ c + 1: one inclusion follows by
degree, and any nonzero lower-degree element pairs nontrivially with an element of mu . Therefore

                                  J ⊇ Ann(mp+1 ) = mc−p ⊇ mp .

Applying (7) to J gives µ(I) ≤ µ(J) ≤ µ(mp ) = hp . Applying it ﬁrst to any homogeneous I proves
the homogeneous bound.

                   µ(I) = dim J − dim grF (mI) ≤ dim J − dim mJ = µ(J) ≤ hp .

gives
                   (IAK )/(mK IAK ) ≃ (I/mI) ⊗k K,       µAK (IAK ) = µA (I),
and dimK (AK )j = hj . An upper bound over K descends to every ideal over k; the equality witness
already exists over k. No descent of arbitrary ideals from K is asserted.


5    Examples and limitations
The following Hilbert functions are coeﬃcient lists in increasing degree. They illustrate (1), rather
than serve as evidence for EGH.
                          degrees        Hilbert function                 maximum
                        ∅ or (1, 1, 1)   1                                   1
                          (1, 2, 4)      1, 2, 2, 2, 1                       2
                          (2, 2, 2)      1, 3, 3, 1                          3
                          (2, 3, 4)      1, 3, 5, 6, 5, 3, 1                 6
                          (3, 3, 3)      1, 3, 6, 7, 6, 3, 1                 7
                       eight quadrics    1, 8, 28, 56, 70, 56, 28, 8, 1      70
                                 (  n    )
For n quadrics the maximum is ⌊n/2⌋     . For instance, in the eight-quadric case every ideal has at

most 70 generators and m has exactly 70. The accompanying integer program reproduces the
examples by convolution and independent enumeration of bounded exponent vectors.
    The theorem concerns characteristic zero and standard graded Artinian complete intersections.
It supplies no weak or strong Lefschetz property, no assertion for nongraded complete intersections,
and no unrestricted positive-characteristic assertion. The downstream conditional argument and
monomial matching work over any ﬁeld; the restriction comes from the general EGH input used
here.
