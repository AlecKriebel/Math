                                               Abstract
        Vértesi and Bene exhibited a 3 × 2 Bell scenario in which qubit positive-operator-valued
     measurements (POVMs) outperform qubit projection-valued measurements (PVMs). We prove
     the matching setting lower bound. For arbitrary finite, input-dependent output alphabets, the
     shared-randomness convex hulls of bipartite qubit-POVM and qubit-PVM behaviors are equal
     when both parties have two inputs. Equivalently, no two-input linear Bell functional separates
     the two convexified sets. We also give a rational 3×2 functional, an exact qubit-POVM strategy,
     and a global analytic qubit-PVM upper bound with a strict exact gap. Hence 3 × 2 is the
     minimum input architecture, up to exchanging the parties. The equality proof combines a one-
     binary-party Lorentz-cone simulation, an extremal binary–ternary reduction, a physically exact
     Lorentz incidence manifold, finite-POVM duality, and a rank trichotomy based on the incidence
     second variation. The result concerns convexified behavior sets; it does not assert equality of
     the raw nonconvex strategy images or same-state simulation.

1   Introduction
A positive-operator-valued measurement (POVM) is more general than a projection-valued measure-
ment (PVM) on a fixed Hilbert space. Naimark dilation represents a POVM projectively only after
adding degrees of freedom, so it does not preserve a fixed local dimension. Bell experiments there-
fore offer an operational question: how many measurement inputs are needed before generalized
measurements on local qubits generate correlations unavailable to projective qubit measurements?


                                                   1
This belongs to the broader study of Bell nonlocality and dimension-constrained quantum behavior
[1, 2].
    Gisin asked whether some Bell inequality and quantum state can require nonprojective mea-
surements for optimal violation, noting that binary outcomes cannot furnish such an example [3].
Vértesi and Bene answered that existence question for two qubits using three inputs on Alice and
two on Bob [4]; subsequent work refined that family [5], and related device-independent construc-
tions certify nonprojective qubit measurements with larger input architectures [6]. These results
do not settle the two-input behavior-set question.
    Two recent results approach nearby boundaries from different directions. Cerf and Ollivier de-
velop a second-order perturbative method for (n, 2, d) Bell scenarios near deterministic correlations
and propose, without resolving, a route to distinguish raw fixed-dimensional POVM and PVM
correlation sets [7]. Their proved qubit result concerns local dichotomic geometry and neither es-
tablishes nor implies the global, convexified arbitrary-output equality proved here. Panahi et al.,
using an intermediate reduction attributed to Masanes together with Schmidt compression, show
that when one party has two binary settings, every bipartite convexly extremal quantum correlation
admits a two-qubit realization even if the other party has arbitrary settings and outcomes [8]. That
is a Hilbert-space dimension theorem, not a projectivization of the arbitrary-output measurements.
The one-binary-party theorem below instead supplies a common PVM mixture of the complete
fixed-qubit behavior.
    To our knowledge, equality of the shared-randomness-convexified fixed-qubit POVM and PVM
behavior sets for all bipartite two-input architectures with finite, input-dependent output alphabets,
and the resulting 3 × 2 minimum-input classification, were not previously known. It also differs
from operator-level projective simulability of individual POVMs [9, 10]: a Bell behavior may admit
a projective realization using a different state even when the original local POVM is not itself a
mixture of PVMs.
    Strong reductions are known when every measurement is dichotomic [11, 12]; genuine ternary
effects are precisely what those arguments do not address. They also make exhaustive PVM con-
ventions essential. A published ternary-measurement claim was later corrected after a qubit PVM
with a zero effect was found to attain the same value [13, 14]. We therefore retain zero projectors,
deterministic inputs, every support pattern, and stochastic postprocessing throughout.

Contributions
We work throughout with local Hilbert-space dimension at most two and with shared-randomness
convexification. Our results are:

1. An explicit rational 3 × 2 Bell functional has an exact algebraic qubit-POVM strategy above a
   global analytic upper bound for all qubit-PVM strategies (theorem 3.1).
2. If one party has binary outcomes on both of its two inputs, every qubit POVM behavior is a
   common convex mixture of complete qubit-PVM behaviors (theorem 4.2).
3. Any remaining strict two-input separator reduces to a pure entangled strategy with one binary
   PVM and one ternary rank-one POVM per party (theorem 4.5).
4. The residual architecture cannot strictly exceed the PVM support function (theorem 9.3). The
   proof treats every smooth and singular rank of an exact Lorentz incidence model.
5. Consequently 3 × 2 is the minimum input architecture, up to exchanging the parties (corol-
   lary 9.4).

    The equality is not an operator identity between POVMs and PVMs, nor does it assert simula-
tion on the same state. It is an equality of convex behavior sets. The distinction is central.

Proof roadmap
The exact separation combines a CHSH locking term with a three-state discrimination task. For the
equality theorem, convex separation first turns any counterexample into a global Bell maximizer.
A Lorentz-cone circuit argument handles every case in which one party is binary. A common-span
filtering perturbation and extremal-POVM dimension bound reduce all other counterexamples to
one binary and one ternary measurement per party.
     In the residual architecture, the five local effects form a unique binary-versus-ternary circuit.
Lorentz coordinates describe the complete strategy by a 4 × 4 probability block P and a four-
parameter metric g, subject to five quadratic null equations. Finite POVM duality makes every
determinant multiplier strictly positive at a hypothetical strict separator. The constrained second
variation then has ambient inertia (4, 12). If the metric differential has rank at least two, dimension
alone forces an uphill direction. Rank one contradicts positivity through an exact projective-fiber
theorem, while rank zero has an explicit deterministic decomposition.

4     Two-input reductions
4.1   One binary party
Lemma 4.1 (Circuits of a three-dimensional pointed cone). Let K be a pointed convex cone of real
dimension at most three. Consider a support-minimal equality between positive sums of nonzero
extreme-ray vectors of K, after canceling any ray that occurs on both sides. Positive coeﬀicients
may be absorbed into the ray vectors. Then either the equality is a one-versus-one cancellation on
the same ray, or K has dimension three and the relation has the form

                                          r0 + r1 = s 0 + s 1

with four distinct rays. In particular, every nontrivial circuit is two-versus-two.




                                                     8
Proof. Move the two sums to opposite sides and attach the corresponding positive and negative
coeﬀicients. Support minimality makes this a minimally supported linear dependence. Indeed, if
a proper subset admitted any nonzero dependence, add a suﬀiciently small signed multiple of it to
the original coeﬀicient vector and vary the multiple until one coeﬀicient first vanishes; all original
signs remain weakly unchanged, yielding a smaller positive relation after cancellation. A minimally
dependent set in dimension d has at most d + 1 vectors, so at most four rays occur.
    Pointedness excludes a dependence with all signs equal. If one side contained a single extreme
ray and the other side contained at least two different rays, division by its positive coeﬀicient would
express that extreme ray as a nontrivial positive sum of other cone rays, a contradiction. Thus a
nontrivial relation contains at least two rays on each side and at most four total, forcing d = 3
and the displayed two-versus-two form. A pointed cone of dimension one or two has at most two
extreme rays, so only one-versus-one, same-ray cancellations remain there. A zero vector is not an
extreme ray and is simply omitted.

Corollary 9.4 (Minimum input architecture). Up to exchanging the parties, 3 × 2 is the minimum
input architecture for a strict fixed-qubit POVM-over-PVM Bell separation.

Proof. With one input on either party every nonsignaling behavior is local, hence a deterministic
PVM behavior maximizes every Bell functional. Two inputs on both parties are excluded by
theorem 9.3. Theorem 3.1 supplies an exact 3 × 2 separation.


10 Operational consequences and limitations
For every Bell functional with two inputs per party, optimizing over fixed-qubit PVMs already
provides the fixed-qubit POVM bound. Thus a value above that bound excludes all local-qubit
realizations, not merely projective ones.
    Likewise, no qubit behavior with two inputs per party can certify that a nonprojective local
measurement was indispensable: after shared-randomness convexification, a shared-randomness
mixture of PVM realizations always exists. This does not identify the state or measurements in a
particular laboratory realization, and it does not assert raw-set equality without shared randomness.
    Equivalently, the theorem rules out every two-input linear Bell-functional separation for bipar-
tite qubits after shared-randomness convexification. It does not prove equality of the raw nonconvex
behavior sets.
    We do not claim the exact global POVM or PVM optimum of (1). We also do not infer
a private-randomness entropy bound: such a statement requires an adversarial extension of the
convex decomposition, not only equality of observed behavior sets.


11 Discussion
The threshold result is special to local dimension two. Natural open questions include minimum-
input laws in dimension three and higher, multipartite scenarios, equality without convexification,
constructive simulation algorithms for generic two-input behaviors, instruments and sequential
measurements, and experimentally robust exact 3 × 2 witnesses. Determining the exact two-qubit
POVM and PVM optima of (1) is also open but unnecessary for the separation.
    The proof illustrates a distinction between operator-level and behavioral generality. Ternary
qubit POVMs are genuine generalized measurements, yet two inputs do not provide enough Bell
structure to make them indispensable after the state and shared-randomness decomposition may

change. A third input can lock the state and steering geometry while reserving another measurement
for a discrimination advantage.

C Degenerate cases in the one-binary-party theorem
If B0 , B1 are nondegenerate and have linearly independent Bloch axes, ΦB maps the positive cone
onto a three-dimensional Lorentz cone. An extreme ray of the image lies on the boundary ellipse.
Its minimum-norm Bloch lift in the plane of the two axes has norm one; a perpendicular component
would exceed the Bloch ball. Thus the positive lift in spanR {I, B0 , B1 } is unique and rank one.
    If the Bloch axes are parallel or antiparallel, the image is the cone over an interval and has two
extreme rays. Every positive relation reduces to one-versus-one cancellations or a two-versus-two
relation with repeated zero-padded outcomes. The same purification construction applies.
    If one observable is scalar and the other nondegenerate, the image is again two-dimensional; the
scalar input is deterministic and does not add a coordinate. If both are scalar, Bob is deterministic
on both inputs and the behavior is local. A rank-deficient circuit sum Ω forces all lifted rays to be
collinear and is realized by a product purification. Zero effects receive zero projectors. These cases
also show that circuit weights remain nonnegative and sum to one at the boundary.
