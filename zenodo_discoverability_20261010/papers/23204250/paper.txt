 Exact semideﬁnite complexity of the symmetric traveling-salesman
                            polytope
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                              October 6, 2026


                                                 Abstract
          The exponential real PSD-rank theorem for shifted perfect-matching matrices in the OpenAI
      Math Release implies that the symmetric traveling-salesman polytope on N cities has exact real
      semideﬁnite extension complexity 2Θ(N ) , measured by PSD matrix order. We give explicit data
      for Yannakakis’s 3n-city face projection, a 2n-city contraction variant, a padding projection
      covering every suﬃciently large city count, and a subset-state ﬂow upper bound. The exponential
      lower-bound method and its central estimate belong to the cited matching theorem; this note
      supplies an explicit consequence and veriﬁcation package, not a new lower-bound method. The
      exponential input has been independently examined in mathematical audits; the accompanying
      Lean statements cover only superpolynomial bounds. No conventional human peer review has
      been completed.


1    Scope, convention and attribution
                               N
For N ≥ 3, let PTSP (N ) ⊆ R( 2 ) be the convex hull of incidence vectors of undirected Hamiltonian
cycles in KN , counting each unordered edge once. For even n ≥ 2, let PPM (n) denote the analogous
convex hull of perfect matchings in Kn . For a nonempty polytope P , deﬁne xcpsd,R (P ) to be the
least positive integer r for which
                                           P = π(L ∩ Sr+ ),                                      (1)
where L is an aﬃne subspace of the real symmetric r-by-r matrices and π is aﬃne. The size is
r, the matrix order. Aﬃne equations are unrestricted. Multiple PSD blocks are counted by their
total order, through a block-diagonal embedding. Counting only the largest block gives a diﬀerent
notion: scalar nonnegativity blocks alone would then have size one.
    This convention also covers an LMI with unrestricted auxiliary real variables, of order r, when
its exact image is a bounded nonempty polytope. Write the aﬃne matrix map as T (z) and the
aﬃne output as U (z). Any direction in the kernel of the linear part of T preserves feasibility for
every real multiple. Boundedness forces the linear part of U to vanish on that kernel. Thus U
factors aﬃnely through T on its aﬃne image, and extends to an aﬃne map on symmetric matrices,
yielding (1).

Theorem 1. There exist constants c > 0 and N0 such that for every N ≥ N0 ,

                    2cN ≤ xcpsd,R (PTSP (N )) ≤ 2(N − 1) + (N − 1)(N − 2)2N −3 .

Consequently xcpsd,R (PTSP (N )) = 2Θ(N ) .

3    Faces, images and the explicit reductions
Lemma 4. If F is a nonempty face of P and T is aﬃne, then xcpsd,R (T (F )) ≤ xcpsd,R (F ) ≤
xcpsd,R (P ).

Proof. Given (1), express F as {x ∈ P : h(x) = b} for a supporting aﬃne functional. Add the
aﬃne equality h(π(X)) = b to L. The image of the resulting PSD section is exactly F . Compose
π with T for the ﬁrst inequality. No PSD row or column is added. Several supporting equalities
work in the same way; their nonnegative slacks can be summed to expose their intersection. This
argument requires neither strict feasibility nor boundedness of the lifted section.

4    Padding and the exponent for all city counts
Proposition 5. For N ≥ m ≥ 3, PTSP (m) is a linear image of a face of PTSP (N ), and therefore
xcpsd,R (PTSP (N )) ≥ xcpsd,R (PTSP (m)).

Proof. The case N = m is the identity. Otherwise write the old cities as {v} ∪ W and new cities
as z1 , . . . , zt , t = N − m. Force the path edges H = {vz1 , z1 z2 , . . . , zt−1 zt } by equality in the valid

5    The exact upper bound and ﬁnite checks
Fix city 1, put Q = {2, . . . , N } and q = N − 1 ≥ 2. Create a directed acyclic graph with source s,
sink t, and states (S, i) for nonempty S ⊆ Q, i ∈ S. Its arcs are
                    s → ({i}, i),   (S, i) → (S ∪ {j}, j) (j ∈ Q \ S),   (Q, i) → t.
Label these arcs by edges {1, i}, {i, j}, {i, 1}, respectively. There are 2 + q2q−1 vertices and
                                       mN = 2q + q(q − 1)2q−2
arcs, counting the middle arcs by ordered i ̸= j and the 2q−2 choices of S containing i but excluding
j. A source-to-sink path lists a permutation of Q; its labels form a tour, and every tour has two
such orientations. Nonnegative unit ﬂows are precisely convex combinations of these paths: follow
positive arcs, use conservation to reach t, subtract the path’s minimum positive ﬂow, and repeat.
Each step removes an arc from positive support. A residual circulation is impossible in an acyclic
graph, and the extracted coeﬃcients sum to one. The edge-label sum projection therefore gives
exactly PTSP (N ). Embed the arc variables as the diagonal of X ∈ Sm      N
                                                                        + , imposing zero oﬀ-diagonal
entries and the ﬂow conservation equations. PSD is equivalent to nonnegativity of the diagonal
variables, proving the upper bound in Theorem 1. This is the standard subset-state dynamic-
programming formulation, often associated with Held–Karp [7]; the explicit count and projection
above do not depend on a complexity-theoretic assumption.
    The accompanying exact graph checks enumerate matching pairs for n = 2, 4, 6, 8 and verify
both gadgets, tour connectedness, every matching’s preimage, and the small ambient faces. Padding
checks enumerate old tours and path-face tours, including m = 3. Independent checks verify
subset-state paths and the upper-bound arc count. The number of connected ordered matching
pairs is 1, 6, 120, 5040, respectively. Negative controls reject disconnected subtours and the missing
diagonal-one constraints. These ﬁnite checks catch implementation and construction errors; they
do not prove an asymptotic lower bound.
