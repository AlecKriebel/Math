                                                     Abstract
        Let θ be an antimorphic involution of a free monoid. If a ﬁnite word w has alternating θ-periods p, q
     and |w| ≥ p + q − gcd(p, q), then gcd(p, q) is an alternating θ-period of w. This proves the unnumbered
     conjecture stated after Theorem 23 in Nowotka’s contribution, joint with Bischoﬀ, to Mini-Workshop:
     Combinatorics on Words (2010). The proof applies the classical Fine–Wilf theorem to the common
     reﬂected extension θ(w)w and recovers the alternating phase at its center. The length formula cannot
     be decreased by one uniformly.


1    The statement
Let A be an arbitrary alphabet, and let A∗ be its free monoid, with empty word ε. An antimorphic involution
is a map θ : A∗ → A∗ such that θ(xy) = θ(y)θ(x) and θ2 = id. A positive integer p is an alternating θ-period
of a ﬁnite word w if there is a word u ∈ Ap such that w is a preﬁx of (uθ(u))ω . The periods need not be
minimal. This deﬁnition prescribes alternating blocks; it diﬀers from a preﬁx of an arbitrary concatenation
of u and θ(u).
Theorem 1. Suppose that w has alternating θ-periods p and q, and put d = gcd(p, q). If L = |w| ≥ p + q − d,
then, for the length-d preﬁx v of w, the word w is a preﬁx of (vθ(v))ω .
   This is the exact alternating conclusion in the unnumbered conjecture following Theorem 23 on printed
page 2220 of [2]; it is distinct from numbered Conjecture 27 there. Theorem 23 gives the suﬃcient bound
p + q. Bischoﬀ’s thesis [3, Lemma 3.9 and Satz 3.11] also develops reﬂection and doubled ordinary periods
and proves that longer bound. We use those classical symmetries with a common extension of the entire
ﬁnite preﬁx. Fixed letters of θ are allowed; in particular, ordinary reversal is included.


2    The common extension
For a word of length n, an ordinary period h > 0 means that letters in positions i and i + h agree whenever
both positions occur. We use the classical Fine–Wilf theorem [1]: a word with ordinary periods h, k and
length at least h + k − gcd(h, k) has ordinary period gcd(h, k).

Lemma 1. There is an involutive permutation σ : A → A such that

                                      θ(a0 · · · an−1 ) = σ(an−1 ) · · · σ(a0 ).

Proof. The identity θ(ε) = θ(ε)2 forces θ(ε) = ε. Since θ is bijective, nonempty words have nonempty images.
If the image of a letter were xy with both factors nonempty, applying θ would factor that letter as the two
nonempty words θ(y)θ(x), a contradiction. Thus letters map to letters. Involutivity and the antimorphism
rule give the assertion.

Lemma 2. If L = |w| ≥ p and p is an alternating θ-period of w, then θ(w)w has ordinary period 2p.

Proposition 1. The suﬃcient formula p + q − gcd(p, q) cannot be replaced uniformly by p + q − gcd(p, q) − 1.
Proof. Take distinct letters a, b, let θ be reversal, and set w = abb, p = 2, q = 3. The word abb is a preﬁx of
(ab ba)ω and of (abb bba)ω , so both alternating periods occur. Its length is 3 = 2 + 3 − 1 − 1, but alternating
period 1 would force the constant word aaa · · · . It does not occur.
    This example establishes only the stated uniform obstruction. It does not assert a sharp minimum length
for each pair (p, q), alphabet, or involution. Theorem 1 also covers p = q and divisibility of one period by
the other.


3     Related results and veriﬁcation
The ordinary Fine–Wilf threshold and reﬂection/doubled-period mechanisms are prior work [1, 2, 3]. Theo-
rems 25 and 26 of Czeizler–Kari–Seki [4] concern a θ-palindromic base word and common θ-primitive roots.
Their Theorem 28 doubles a base block to uθ(u); Corollary 32 treats more general block powers at the longer
bound 2p + q − d. Kari–Seki [5] improve that general suﬃcient bound to 2p + q − d − ⌊d/2⌋ for p > q ≥ 2d.
These hypotheses and conclusions diﬀer from the alternating-preﬁx assertion proved here.
    Simpson’s published Theorem 3.4 [6] concerns palindromic periodicities with half-periods h1 , h2 and
reﬂection oﬀsets r1 , r2 , and requires length at least 2h1 + 2h2 − D, where D = gcd(2(r2 − r1 ), 2h1 , 2h2 ); its
conclusion is an ordinary period D. At the common alternating phase, h1 = p, h2 = q and D = 2d, so that
condition is 2p + 2q − 2d, twice the length threshold in Theorem 1. Thus this close prior result does not
directly give the short bound on w.
