                 Rational Hodge classes on mixed products
                               of K3 moduli spaces
                                                  Alec Kriebel∗

                                                October 6, 2026



                                                     Abstract
              Combining OpenAI’s rational Hodge theorem for arbitrary mixed products of projective
          K3 surfaces with Bülles’ Chow-motive decomposition, we record rational Hodge in every codi-
          mension for ﬁnite mixed products of the smooth projective K3 moduli spaces covered by that
          decomposition. The factors may have diﬀerent K3 bases and include Hilbert schemes, twisted
          stable-sheaf moduli, and eligible moduli of Bridgeland-stable objects. We give the explicit pull-
          push argument, retaining smoothness, projectivity, and stability hypotheses. The contribution
          is an assembled corollary of the cited results; it supplies neither a new proof of the base K3
          theorem nor a new motivic reduction.

     An empty factor gives an empty product. A smooth projective zero-dimensional factor over C
is a ﬁnite disjoint union of points; remove it on each component and apply the preceding argument.
The empty product is a point. Codimensions outside [0, dim X] have zero target. For n ≥ 1,
S [n] is the smooth projective moduli of rank-one ideal sheaves with Mukai vector (1, 0, 1 − n) and
is in the untwisted sheaf case. For n = 0 it is a point. This proves the stated Hilbert-scheme
consequence.

Remark 3 (Non-ﬁne and twisted moduli). The split maps used here are Bülles’ algebraic Chow
correspondences. We do not construct them by presuming that an honest universal family exists. The
cited construction uses quasi-universal/twisted data and the normalized diagonal identity underlying
Markman’s result [4, Section 3]. Raw duplication of a universal family does not preserve its Ext
diagonal formula: multiplicities must be normalized. The present argument needs only the actual
rational motive splitting, which already covers the Brauer classes in Theorem 1.


3    Scope and veriﬁcation
The transfer explicitly uses Hodge for mixed products KJ ; separate self-power theorems alone do
not supply this premise. Conversely, S [1] = S shows that the unrestricted mixed-moduli conclusion
includes the mixed-K3 base theorem. Thus, given Bülles’ established reduction, these two universal
assertions are equivalent. This explains exactly where the principal mathematical input lies.
    No claim is made about arbitrary deformations of K3[n] type, singular moduli or semistable com-
pactiﬁcations, moduli on arbitrary abelian surfaces, the integral or generalized Hodge conjecture,
or ﬁnite-dimensionality of Chow motives.
    The veriﬁcation materials distinguish the explicit correspondence argument from the unformal-
ized geometric input in [7]. The upstream Kuga–Satake Lean module checks rational Cliﬀord/tensor
identities; it does not formalize algebraic cycles, the universal Kuga–Satake theorem, or the the-
orem of this note. Reproducible small linear-algebra and sign checks are supporting evidence for
parts of the upstream audit, not certiﬁcates for its full geometry.
