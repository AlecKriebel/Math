    Bilinear ergodic Hilbert variation for commuting transformations
             as a consequence of annular triangular variation
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                             October 6, 2026


                                                 Abstract
          We give an explicit restriction and transference consequence of OpenAI’s October 5, 2026
      full annular variation theorem for the triangular Hilbert transform. For arbitrary commuting
      invertible probability-preserving transformations S, T and complex f, g ∈ L3 , the symmetric
      bilinear Hilbert sums have bounded pointwise r-variation in L3/2 for every r > 2, and converge
      almost everywhere and in L3/2 . The restriction uses boxes of ﬁxed positive area and a uniformly
      summable kernel error. The analytic breakthrough is the cited continuous theorem; the present
      note supplies the discrete restriction and full measure-theoretic transfer.

3    Finite-window transference and convergence
Proof of Theorem 1. Choose measurable representatives of f, g taking ﬁnite values outside their null
sets. Remove all preimages of those null sets under ﬁnite words in S, S −1 , T, T −1 . The resulting
invariant full-measure set carries every value in the ﬁnite sums. A change of representatives aﬀects
only such a countable union of null sets. The same convention treats transformations commuting
modulo null sets: remove all such word preimages of the failure set, after which all required identities
hold on the invariant complement.
    Fix an integer M ≥ 1. Let WM (x) be the maximum in (1) over lists with endpoints in {0, . . . , M }.
There are ﬁnitely many such lists, so WM is measurable. For L ≥ 1 let QL = {−L, . . . , L}2 . At
each x form ﬁnite arrays
                                       Ax (i, j) = f (S i T j x)1QL+M (i, j),
                                       Bx (i, j) = g(S i T j x)1QL+M (i, j).

For (i, j) ∈ QL and |n| ≤ M , every required entry is in QL+M . Commutation gives exactly

                           hN (Ax , Bx )(i, j) = HN (f, g)(S i T j x)        (0 ≤ N ≤ M ).

    Fix, for example, r = 3. The variation is ﬁnite almost everywhere. Any scalar sequence
with ﬁnite r-variation is Cauchy: otherwise some ε > 0 admits arbitrarily late pairs of indices
whose values diﬀer by at least ε. Recursively choose J strictly ordered disjoint pairs. A partition
containing their endpoints has variation at least εJ 1/r , a contradiction. Completeness of C gives a
ﬁnite limit H. It is measurable as the limit along integers, setting it to zero on the exceptional set.
    Since H0 = 0, H∗ := supN |HN | ≤ V3 (HN ) belongs to Lp . Also |H| ≤ H∗ . The measurable tail
supN ≥m |HN − H| tends to zero pointwise and is bounded by 2H∗ . Dominated convergence applied
to its pth power proves (3), hence norm convergence. The maximal bound follows from (2); the
limit bound follows from domination. Norm limits of the bilinear identities for HN show that H is
complex bilinear on L3 × L3 .

4    Scope and veriﬁcation disclosure
The truncations are symmetric and the kernel is odd. This proof gives no one-sided commuting
Cesàro convergence, no noncommuting theorem, no r = 2 estimate, and no extension of the input
exponents beyond L3 . No numerical evidence substitutes for the analytic estimate. Supplemental
exact kernel checks test only Section 2 bookkeeping.
   The continuous theorem is attributed to OpenAI using its supplied manuscript citation. The
dependency ledger records the version and scope of independent automated proof audits. Such
audits are evidence, not formal certiﬁcation. No theorem in this note is claimed to have been
formalized. AI tools were used extensively in research, drafting and veriﬁcation. This preprint has
not undergone conventional human peer review or refereeing at publication.
