                                 A note on Γ-supercyclicity of
                               dissipative composition operators
                                                    Alec Kriebel∗

                                    September 23, 2026 (version 1.0.1)


                                                      Abstract
              We record an aﬃrmative answer to the question on arbitrary-Γ supercyclicity posed in Ober-
          wolfach Report 19/2024. In the report’s separable Lp setting, a dissipative composition operator
          of bounded distortion is Γ-supercyclic if and only if its associated bilateral weighted shift is
          Γ-supercyclic, for every subset Γ ⊆ C. The observation follows from existing results of Abbar
          and of Abbar–Kuznetsova, together with a sequence-space representation of the composition
          operator. We give a short direct proof to make the application explicit. Neither the scalar
          criterion nor the ampliﬁcation mechanism is claimed as new.


1        The question and the statement
For a bounded operator T on a complex Banach space and a set Γ ⊆ C, call T Γ-supercyclic if some
vector x has dense orbit
                                   {λT n x : n ≥ 0, λ ∈ Γ}.
This terminology follows Charpentier, Ernst, and Menet [3]. In her contribution to Oberwolfach
Report 19/2024, D’Aniello, reporting joint work with Maiuriello, asks whether the equivalence
of this property for a dissipative composition operator of bounded distortion and its associated
shift holds for arbitrary Γ [6, p. 1083]. The cases of ordinary hypercyclicity and real or complex
supercyclicity are part of the preceding work [4, 5].

Proposition 3 (Ampliﬁcation). The operators S and SE are Γ-supercyclic for exactly the same
subsets Γ ⊆ C.
Proof. The forward implication is Lemma 2. For the reverse, choose e ∈ E and ℓ ∈ E ∗ with ℓ(e) = 1.
The bounded surjection Q : YE → Y , (Qu)j = ℓ(uj ), satisﬁes QSE = SQ and sends a dense Γ-orbit
to a dense Γ-orbit. If Γ∗ = ∅, neither operator has such an orbit, so this case also satisﬁes the
equivalence.

    Separability cannot be dropped from this application merely because the measure is σ-ﬁnite.
Every projective orbit is contained in the separable closed span of a countable orbit. For example,
on X = Z × Ω, let Ω = {0, 1}I , with I uncountable, carry the product of fair Bernoulli probability
measures, give layer j mass 2−|j| times that measure, and set f (j, t) = (j +1, t). Bounded distortion
holds with K = 1 and Tf is bounded and invertible. Its Lp space is nonseparable, while the scalar
shift is hypercyclic by (3). This is a scope clariﬁcation, not an additional novelty claim.

For completeness, the following deduction identiﬁes the relation to Abbar–Kuznetsova [2, Theorem
B] without using the proof of Lemma 2. On the discrete group Z, take the translation parameters
