                        A sharp dyadic threshold in a
                 characteristic-two random-cone construction
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           October 6, 2026


                                                Abstract
         OpenAI’s October 4 construction uses a projective plane of order q = 128 and seven extra
     letters to obtain a ﬁnitely presented torsion-free group with a ﬁnite two-dimensional classifying
     complex and a characteristic-two group algebra that is not directly ﬁnite. We determine the least
     dyadic order q ≥ 4 at which its unchanged squared turn weights have exponential word-mass
     decay: it is q = 32. A three-class reduction and exact rational certiﬁcates prove contraction at
     32 and exponential growth at 4, 8, 16. Checking the remaining parameter-dependent estimates
     yields the same construction on a 532-edge rose. This is a threshold for this speciﬁed model,
     rather than an optimum among all group-algebra examples. Classical HNN embedding also
     gives a two-generator realization. We spell out the inherited scalar idempotent and nonzero
     absorbed cyclic projective module, which remain valid over every characteristic-two ﬁeld. The
     construction is existential; no numerical matching or group-ring multiplication certiﬁcate is
     claimed.


1    Scope and inherited input
For a torsion-free group G and ﬁeld K, the scalar idempotent conjecture asks whether e2 = e in
K[G] forces e ∈ {0, 1} [2, Problem 1(c)]. The positive-characteristic version includes characteristic
two. The pivotal input here is the exact October 4 manuscript [1, Theorem 1.1], at repository
commit adc7f124: it constructs a ﬁnitely presented torsion-free group with a ﬁnite 2-dimensional
K(G, 1) and scalar elements

                             ab = 1,       ac = 0,       c 6= 0    in F2 [G].                            (1)

The breakthrough and construction belong to OpenAI. Its probabilistic and geometric framework
also draws on its September 23 zero-divisor manuscript [7]. Our independent source audits found
no substantive gap in the arguments used; their precise scope is recorded in the supplement. The
earlier family-197 Lean example has torsion and does not formalize this October 4 theorem. No
full formal veriﬁcation or conventional human refereeing is claimed.
     Our contribution is the exact dyadic threshold for the unchanged seven-extra model in Section 2,
together with the parameter check in Section 3. The defect-idempotent and module consequences
were already publicly recorded in the author’s triage [6]; they are included for interpretation, not
oﬀered as a new discovery. The two-generator specialization in Section 4 uses classical machinery.
We make no ﬁrst-priority claim and no characteristic-zero, odd-characteristic or reduced group
C ∗ -algebra projection claim.

3    Carrying the construction to order 32
Proposition 2. The October 4 random-cone construction remains valid at q = 32 with the same
seven-extra types. It gives a ﬁnitely presented torsion-free group G generated by at most 532 ele-
ments, a ﬁnite 2-dimensional K(G, 1), and the scalar triple (1).
Proof. Here v = 1057, p = 33/1057 and |T | = 1064. For suﬃciently large m ≡ 1 (mod 4), take m
vertices in every line class on side A and m − 1 on side B, with n = vm. Each ordinary outgoing set
is its 33-point line. Give the distinguished A vertex all seven extras; for each complement of a Fano
line assign that four-element extra set at (33m − 1)/4 vertices of A and 33(m − 1)/4 of B, evenly
among line classes. All other extra sets are empty. Fix xB ∈ B before choosing the matchings.
The line-capacity coeﬃcient is 7 · 33/(4 · 1057) = 33/604 < 1, so disjoint assignments exist for all
suﬃciently large m. Each extra is in four complements and each extra pair in two, while ordinary
lines have odd intersection size. Thus the source’s exact inverse-slot balances and parity identities
persist. Degrees are now 33, 37, 40, with minimum 33 and maximum d∗ = 40.

4    Classical two-generator realization
The number 532 describes this random-cone recipe, not the smallest possible group generating
set. We record explicitly why classical HNN embedding [8, 9] already yields two generators while
preserving the stronger ﬁnite classifying-space condition.

Proposition 3. Every group G with a ﬁnite 2-dimensional K(G, 1) embeds in a group H gen-
erated by two elements with a ﬁnite 2-dimensional K(H, 1). If G has an aspherical presentation
on g1 , . . . , gs with r relators, H has an aspherical presentation on two generators with r relators.
Torsion-freeness is preserved.

Proof. Set B = G ∗ F (x, y), and in B deﬁne

                  u0 = y,    v0 = x,      ui = x−i yxi ,   vi = y −i xgi y i   (1 ≤ i ≤ s).

The ui freely generate: they are a ﬁnite subset of the standard free basis of the kernel of F (x, y) → Z,
x 7→ 1, y 7→ 0. Projection killing G sends the vi to the corresponding free-basis subset with x, y
interchanged, and proves they freely generate too. The HNN extension with relations t−1 ui t = vi
therefore embeds B. Its zeroth relation gives y = txt−1 ; the others give gi = x−1 y i t−1 x−i yxi ty −i .
Substituting for y expresses gi by the word

                                 Wi = x−1 txi t−2 x−i txt−1 xi t2 x−i t−1 .

Thus H = hx, ti, and its presentation is hx, t | Rj (W1 , . . . , Ws ), 1 ≤ j ≤ ri.
    Let X be the ﬁnite classifying complex for G, with its maximal 1-skeleton tree collapsed. The
vertex space X ∨ R2 is a ﬁnite K(B, 1). Attach the mapping cylinder of a rose with s + 1 petals
via the two injective maps representing ui and vi . This gives a ﬁnite complex of dimension at
most two for H. Its universal cover is a tree of contractible vertex and edge spaces; successively
adjoining ﬁnite subtrees gives contractible homotopy pushouts. Sphere maps have compact image
contained in a ﬁnite subcomplex, and hence a ﬁnite subtree, so the cover is contractible. This

proves asphericity. Finite-order elements of an HNN extension are conjugate into its base; the base
free product is torsion-free when G is, proving the assertion.
    To see that the ﬁnal two-generator presentation itself is aspherical, ﬁrst consider the subcomplex
having only the s+1 HNN deﬁning cells. Each gi occurs once in its own such cell and in no other such
cell, so those generator-cell pairs can be collapsed there; then collapse y with the zeroth deﬁning
cell. The resulting retraction to the (x, t) rose sends gi to Wi . Homotopy invariance of attaching
cells carries the original r relators to Rj (W ), proving homotopy equivalence with the displayed
presentation complex. This uses these speciﬁc eliminations, not arbitrary Tietze moves.

    An embedding G ,→ H induces an injective map K[G] → K[H] because distinct group elements
remain distinct basis elements. The scalar triple is preserved. No support is shortened or computed
by this transport. A torsion-free group generated by at most one element is trivial or inﬁnite cyclic;
its group algebra over a ﬁeld is a domain. Consequently two is the least possible number of
generators for such an idempotent example, but the embedding mechanism is classical.


5    Ring and right-module proof
The reduction to a scalar idempotent is classical [3, Proposition kaplansky_relations]; the split-
ting is the regular right-module interpretation of direct ﬁniteness [4, p. 9]. We include the maps to
ﬁx handedness.

Proposition 4. Let R be a nonzero unital ring and suppose (1) holds in R. Then e = 1 − ba is
an idempotent other than 0 and 1, and P = eR has all the right-module and K0 properties in the
consequence theorem below. No characteristic assumption is needed for this proposition.

Proof. Put f = ba. Then f 2 = b(ab)a = f , so e2 = (1 − f )2 = 1 − 2f + f 2 = 1 − f = e. Also
ec = c − b(ac) = c 6= 0, whence e 6= 0. If e = 1, then ba = 0, and a = a(ba) = 0 contradicts ab = 1
in a nonzero ring. Thus e 6= 1.
    The complementary idempotents f and e satisfy f e = ef = 0 and f + e = 1. Therefore
R = f R + eR. If x = f r = es, then x = f x = f es = 0, giving the direct sum

                                          RR = baR ⊕ eR.                                           (4)

Since baR ⊆ bR and b = bab, the reverse inclusion holds as well: baR = bR. Left multiplication by
b is a right R-linear isomorphism RR → bR, with inverse left multiplication by a. Indeed abr = r
for every r, and ba(br) = br for every br ∈ bR.
    Consequently (4) identiﬁes RR with RR ⊕ P . For a fully explicit pair of inverse maps, use

                    Φ(r) = (ar, er),      Ψ(u, p) = bu + p   (r, u ∈ R, p ∈ eR).

The identities ae = eb = 0, ep = p, and ab = 1 give ΨΦ(r) = bar + er = r and ΦΨ(u, p) = (u, p).
They also show ker(r 7→ ar) = eR; in particular c ∈ P .
   The module P is a direct summand of the free right module RR , hence projective. It is cyclic,
generated by e, and nonzero because it contains e = e · 1 6= 0. By deﬁnition, K0 (R) is the group
completion of the direct-sum monoid of ﬁnitely generated projective right modules [5, Chapter II,
Section 2]. Hence the isomorphism implies [R] = [R] + [P ] and therefore [P ] = 0 in that abelian
group. It does not imply P = 0. Rather, R ⊕ 0 = ∼ R ⊕ P with 0 6=∼ P exhibits failure of projective
cancellation.
