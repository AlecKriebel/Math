                Reduced C*-algebras of the Thompson family:
                an attributed consequence of nonamenability
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                            October 6, 2026


                                                 Abstract
         Combining OpenAI’s announced nonamenability theorem for the standard Thompson group
     F with the published equivalences of Le Boudec and Matte Bon gives simplicity of the reduced
     group C*-algebras of F , T , Aut(F ) and the abstract commensurator Comm(F ). Their canonical
     traces are their unique tracial states by the discrete-group trace theorem of Breuillard, Kalantar,
     Kennedy and Ozawa. We record the exact hypotheses, countability and attribution, and the
     corresponding consequence for countable circle-homeomorphism overgroups of the standard ac-
     tion. The argument is an application of established reductions, not a new simplicity criterion or
     an independent proof of nonamenability. Unique trace for T was already known unconditionally.
     All conclusions involving the new input have exactly the same dependency on its validity.


1    Scope, deﬁnitions and inherited inputs
All groups carry the discrete topology. Thompson’s group F consists of the increasing piecewise-
linear homeomorphisms of [0, 1] with ﬁnitely many pieces, dyadic breakpoints and slopes in 2Z .
It ﬁxes both endpoints. Identifying them realizes the standard subgroup F ≤ Homeo(S1 ), where
S1 = R/Z. Thompson’s group T consists of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms with
ﬁnitely many dyadic linear pieces, slopes in 2Z and preservation of the dyadic points. Thus the
speciﬁed F is a subgroup of T .
    Here Aut(F ) is the full abstract automorphism group, including orientation-reversing automor-
phisms in its line realization. The abstract commensurator Comm(F ) is formed from isomorphisms
between ﬁnite-index subgroups of F , with two identiﬁed if their restrictions agree on some common
ﬁnite-index subgroup. Composition after restriction gives its group law. These are the full groups
in [2, Corollary 4.4], not just their orientation-preserving subgroups. Faithful line realizations are
described by Brin and by Burillo–Cleary–Röver [5, 6].
    For a discrete group G, let λg δh = δgh on ℓ2 (G). The reduced algebra Cr∗ (G) is the norm closure
of the linear span of the λg . Its canonical tracial state is

Theorem 1 (Established simplicity reductions, Le Boudec–Matte Bon). The following three state-
ments are equivalent:
                   F is nonamenable,          Cr∗ (F ) is simple,      Cr∗ (T ) is simple.
The following three statements are also equivalent:
              F is nonamenable,     Cr∗ (Aut(F )) is simple,        Cr∗ (Comm(F )) is simple.
If F is nonamenable, every countable G ≤ Homeo(S1 ) containing the speciﬁed standard subgroup
F is C*-simple.
    These are exactly [2, Corollaries 4.2 and 4.4, Theorem 4.1]. The group identiﬁcations in Corol-
lary 4.4 concern the full abstract groups deﬁned above. Their proof uses the corresponding line
action and Theorem 4.3. No assertion about an arbitrary abstract copy of F in an unrelated action
is used.
Theorem 2 (Discrete-group trace characterization, BKKO). For a discrete group G, its reduced
algebra has a unique tracial state if and only if its amenable radical Ra (G) is trivial. Every C*-
simple discrete group has trivial amenable radical.
   Here Ra (G) is the largest amenable normal subgroup. The characterization and the C*-
simplicity consequence are [3, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 4.3]. More precisely, every tracial state
on Cr∗ (G) vanishes on λg for g ∈
                                / Ra (G) [3, Theorem 4.1]. If the radical is trivial, density of the
group algebra forces agreement with τG . This is a theorem about reduced group algebras; simplicity
and unique trace are distinct properties of general C*-algebras.


2    The consequence and its proof
Corollary 3. Using the nonamenability theorem of [1], each of
                     Cr∗ (F ),    Cr∗ (T ),      Cr∗ (Aut(F )),       Cr∗ (Comm(F ))
is simple and has exactly one tracial state, its canonical trace.
Proof. The input theorem concerns exactly the standard dyadic PL group F deﬁned above. Apply
both equivalences in Theorem 1 to obtain the four simplicity statements. Apply Theorem 2 sepa-
rately to each discrete group to obtain a trivial amenable radical and therefore the unique canonical
trace.
    For completeness, all four groups are countable, as required by the simplicity source. The
ﬁnite dyadic descriptions make F and T countable, and F is generated by two elements [4]. An
automorphism of F is determined by the images of these generators, so Aut(F ) is countable. A
ﬁnitely generated group has ﬁnitely many subgroups of each ﬁxed ﬁnite index: each is a point
stabilizer for a permutation action on a ﬁnite set, and there are only ﬁnitely many possible images
of a ﬁnite generating set in the relevant symmetric group. Each ﬁnite-index subgroup is ﬁnitely
generated by Schreier’s lemma. For each ordered pair of such subgroups, there are at most countably
many homomorphisms, since ﬁnitely many generator images in the countable group determine one.
The union of all their isomorphisms is countable, hence its quotient Comm(F ) is countable.

Corollary 4. Using the same input, every countable subgroup G ≤ Homeo(S1 ) containing the
standard circle action of F has simple Cr∗ (G) and unique canonical trace.
Proof. Use Theorem 1 and then Theorem 2. The circle action is faithful, and the stated containment
and countability are exactly the hypotheses in [2, Theorem 4.1].
   The action-level formulation is inherited as well. For its mechanism, every nonempty open
arc contains a smaller dyadic interval supporting an aﬃne copy of F . Thus its rigid stabilizer
(the pointwise stabilizer of the complement) is nonamenable. The rigid-stabilizer criterion [2,
Theorem 3.7] applies. This explanation requires the speciﬁed local action; an arbitrary abstract
embedding of F is not a substitute for the containment hypothesis.


3    Prior status and veriﬁcation boundaries
The implications used in Corollary 3 were printed long before the new nonamenability input.
Le Boudec–Matte Bon credit the direction from C*-simplicity of T to nonamenability of F to
Haagerup–Olesen [7, Theorem 5.5]. The new input belongs entirely to OpenAI. This note only
collects its consequences under the existing reductions. We make no claim of a ﬁrst observation, a
new analytic mechanism, or an independent solution of the amenability problem.
     The unique-trace assertion for T was already unconditional: T is simple and nonamenable, hence
has trivial amenable radical. Haagerup–Olesen explicitly record this and credit Dudko–Medynets
[7, discussion after Theorem 5.5]. The equivalence between nonamenability of F and its reduced
unique-trace property is also already explicit [7, Remark 5.6]. Our fresh priority audit found no
indexed earlier note combining the newly inspected input with this exact four-group package, but
failed searches cannot prove absence or public priority. The supplement gives the dated queries
and inspected corpus scope.
     Only reduced algebras are asserted. For any nontrivial group the full algebra C ∗ (G) has the
augmentation character and the distinct canonical regular trace pulled back from Cr∗ (G); they
diﬀer on every nonidentity group element. Its augmentation kernel is proper and nonzero, so the
full algebra is not simple. C*-simplicity also does not mean abstract group simplicity: F has proper
normal subgroups, including its derived subgroup [4]. C*-simplicity of V and nV was already known
[2, Theorems 1.5 and 4.5] and is not a new consequence claimed here.
