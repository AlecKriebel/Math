                                                 Abstract
          The ﬁnite-energy multimode entropy photon-number inequality proved in the OpenAI mathe-
      matics release supplies the sharp entropy premise in the previously conditional pure-loss bosonic
      dynamic-capacity converses. We give its precise classical, quantum and entanglement conse-
      quence under an average photon constraint, including arbitrary entanglement across uses. For
      public, private and secret-key resources, an operational correction is necessary: the printed cat-
      alytic secrecy condition of Wilde–Hsieh protects consumed key jointly with generated secrets
      and excludes the one-time pad. A zero-energy converse disproves the displayed region under
      that literal condition. We establish the formula with an explicit generated-secret secrecy crite-
      rion, corrected converse accounting, and direct key-assisted coding. The formulas and original
      conditional reductions are inherited; this is a consequence and operational audit, not an inde-
      pendent proof of EPnI. We rely on the cited unrefereed EPnI proof. Its analytical arguments
      and formal-source semantics were audited, but its Lean build was not reproduced.

    For achievability ﬁrst ﬁx 0 < N ′ < N . Truncate Gaussian labels to compact sets and ap-

Next project each conditional input onto a one-mode number cutoﬀ and normalize. Its conditional

els. States, energies and entropies converge. Puriﬁcations compress to ﬁnite reference dimension;
projected coherent states remain pure. For suﬃciently ﬁne approximation the mean cost µ < N .
    On a ﬁxed cutoﬀ the cost observable has norm at most K per use. Random quantum father
codes have expected input trace-close to the selected conditional product state [7, Propositions 2–3];
the direct private construction has the same property by random sampling. Classical typical types
change per-use cost by at most |X|δK, and expected-input trace error ϵn changes it by at most Kϵn .
Thus expected code cost is at most n(µ + |X|δK + Kϵn ) < nN for small δ and large n. Markov
gives a ﬁxed positive probability of cost at most nN . The probability that error or secrecy exceeds
the square root of its vanishing expectation tends to zero. Their intersection therefore contains a
feasible good code. This selects the original codes without postselecting quantum data or changing
rates. Take coding limits, approximation limits and then N ′ ↑ N . At N = 0 the corrected model
attains the conversion cones with vacuum channel inputs.
    Both displayed unions are closed: for convergent rate triples, take a subsequence of their pa-
rameters in compact [0, 1] and pass the inequalities by continuity. They are convex as well. Use
s ∈ [0, g(ηN )] instead of λ. All right sides are concave in s by Lemma 2, because g(g −1 (s)/η) is
concave and fκ (s) is convex. Their feasible sets in rate-plus-s space are convex; projection gives
the unions. This handles time sharing and all required closures.


7    Boundaries and veriﬁcation limits
At N = 0 every right side is zero, giving the quantum and corrected private conversion cones.
Proposition 4 is the diﬀerent literal-model conclusion. At λ = 0 the conditional entropy terms
vanish; at λ = 1 they are the full thermal values. At η = 1/2, B, E have equal entropies, so
Q + E ≤ 0 and P + S ≤ 0. At η = 1 the environment entropy is zero, giving the energy-constrained
noiseless formulas. No argument divides by N , 1 − η or a zero entropy.
    The unchanged literal private target is refuted; the positive private theorem has the explicit
repaired convention. There is no claim for superlinear catalysts, stronger energy constraints, se-
mantic security, thermal-channel quantum capacity, ampliﬁers or two-way assistance. Ordinary
single-resource pure-loss capacities and ordinary classical two-receiver broadcast capacity are not
new. The cited conﬁdential-broadcast η < 1/2 formula fails the η = 0 common-message check when
Bob must decode; no conﬁdential-broadcast extension is included.
