                           Metric rigidity at the boundary
                             of cubic compactiﬁcations
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           October 6, 2026


                                               Abstract
         We deduce a sharp Cartier-index criterion for complex-structure rigidity on singular Kähler–
     Einstein Fano varieties. If −KX = rL with L ample Cartier and r > dim(X)/2 + 1, then
     the tangent sheaf is stable and every parallel orthogonal complex structure on the regular lo-
     cus equals the given one or its negative. The proof combines the decomposition theorem of
     Druel–Guenancia–Păun with a Hilbert-polynomial index bound and local positive-Ricci curva-
     ture. Applied to cubic n-fold limits, n ≥ 5, it identiﬁes the ordinary metric Gromov–Hausdorﬀ
     compactiﬁcation with cubic GIT modulo conjugation, including the singular boundary. We
     also record the complex-preserving comparison as an attributed consequence of the unrestricted
     ordinary-double-point gap, Li–Liu’s global Reeb minimum, and Spotti–Sun’s established trans-
     fer. The complex algebraic comparison is already publicly stated in stronger form by Kong–
     Shen–Zhao–Zhao; no ﬁrst-solution claim is made.

Theorem 1 (Sharp Cartier-index rigidity). Let X be an irreducible normal projective klt Fano of
dimension m ≥ 1 carrying a weak KE current with bounded potentials, smooth on Xreg . Suppose an
                                                                                              [−1]
ample Cartier line bundle L and a positive integer r satisfy the actual line-bundle identity ωX ≃
L⊗r and r > m/2 + 1. Then TX , and the tangent sheaf of every ﬁnite quasi-étale cover, is slope
stable for the anticanonical polarization. No such cover is a product of two positive-dimensional
normal projective varieties. Every parallel orthogonal complex structure on (Xreg , g) is J or −J.
The strict numerical threshold is sharp in this class.

Proof of Theorem 1. Druel–Guenancia–Păun [12, Theorem A] supply a ﬁnite quasi-étale cover f :
Y → X and an isometric algebraic product of positive-dimensional weak KE klt Fanos Yi with stable
tangent sheaves. Their theorem treats the incomplete regular locus; we do not apply complete de
Rham splitting there. Put M = f ∗ L. Quasi-étaleness gives ωY ≃ M ⊗r .

    For each factor choose a regular point b of the complementary product. The restriction Mi =
M |Yi ×{b} is ample Cartier. On Yi,reg × {b} the smooth product canonical formula identiﬁes ωY with
ωYi tensored with the ﬁxed one-dimensional space det(Tb∗ B). Normal reﬂexive extension therefore
gives ωYi ≃ Mi⊗r globally. This also works for the factors of any algebraic product cover: quasi-
        [−1]

étale pullback is klt, and adjoining smooth coordinates tests klt on a factor.
    Let Z be such a factor, of dimension d, with −KZ = rA and A ample Cartier. For j = 1, . . . , r−1,
klt Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing applies to −jA, since −jA−KZ = (r−j)A is ample [18, Theorem
2.22]. Negative ampleness gives H 0 (Z, −jA) = 0 as well. Hence the nonzero degree-d polynomial
PZ (t) = χ(Z, A⊗t ) has the r − 1 distinct roots −1, . . . , −(r − 1), so d ≥ r − 1. Euler characteristic
is polynomial at every integer, including negative integers [15, Lemma 33.45.1]. Two factors would
imply m ≥ 2(r − 1), contradicting the strict index bound. The DGP cover therefore has one factor
and TY is stable.
    Stability descends without a Q-factoriality assumption. If a proper saturated F ⊂ TX had slope
at least µ(TX ), pull it back on the ﬁnite étale big open set and extend reﬂexively and saturate to
G ⊂ TY . Both complements have codimension at least two. Choose a general complete-intersection
curve of large anticanonical degree in that big open set. On its étale inverse image, ordinary bundle
degrees multiply by deg f , giving

               µf ∗ (−KX ) (G) = deg(f )µ−KX (F),       µ−KY (TY ) = deg(f )µ−KX (TX ).

This contradicts stability upstairs. The same hypotheses hold on every ﬁnite quasi-étale cover,
proving its tangent stability as well.
    Let I be another parallel orthogonal complex structure on Xreg . The real two-form η(u, v) =
g(Iu, v) is parallel. Its (2, 0) part relative to J is parallel. On a KE manifold with Ric(g) = λg,
λ > 0, contraction of Chern curvature on Λp T ∗(1,0) is −pλ Id, up to the common curvature sign
convention. A parallel section has zero curvature action, so this contraction annihilates it. Thus no
nonzero parallel (2, 0) part exists. This is a local identity, requiring no completeness or integration.
    Consequently η is of type (1, 1), equivalently −JIJ = I, so IJ = JI. The parallel endomorphism
I of TX1,0reg is holomorphic. The reﬂexive sheaf Hom(TX , TX ) extends it across the codimension-two
complement; GAGA makes it algebraic [19, Theorem 2]. A stable sheaf is simple: the kernel/image
slope inequalities exclude a nonzero endomorphism of intermediate generic rank. Characteristic
polynomial coeﬃcients extend to global regular functions on normal projective X, hence are con-
stant. Subtracting a complex eigenvalue then gives an endomorphism of deﬁcient generic rank,
which must be zero. Thus I = c Id. The equation I 2 = −1 gives c = ±i, or I = ±J as a real
endomorphism.
    For sharpness take X = Pr−1 × Pr−1 , r ≥ 2, and L = O(1, 1). Then m = 2r − 2 and −KX = rL.
The product of equally normalized Fubini–Study metrics is KE. Conjugation on only one factor
is an isometry whose pullback complex structure is (−J1 , J2 ), neither J nor −J. This falsiﬁes
including equality in the uniform criterion; it is not a cubic counterexample.

Here is an explicit positivity check for the relevant CM polarization. Put S = P(H 0 (Pn+1 , O(3))),
let h and s denote the hyperplane classes on Pn+1 and S, and put r = n − 1. The universal cubic
has class 3h + s, and its relative anticanonical class is rh − s. In the standard Fano normalization,

                        c1 (λCM ) = −π∗ (rh − s)n+1 (3h + s)
                                                     
                                 = rn 3(n + 1) − r s = 2(n + 2)(n − 1)n s.

The expression is computed on the product; the universal-cubic factor implements pushforward
from the hypersurface. Any positive conventional rescaling preserves the sign. The coeﬃcient is
strictly positive, so the CM polarization agrees in sign with the standard GIT polarization.
    Spotti–Sun §5.2 applies their moduli continuity method from §4.2. The initial Fermat cubic is
KE; the smooth cubic moduli locus is connected. KE existence is open there, and the cubic-limit
and CM facts make it closed. The map from the complex GH compactiﬁcation to GIT is continuous;
uniqueness of the KE metric on a ﬁxed Fano variety gives injectivity. Its compact image contains
the dense smooth GIT locus, giving surjectivity. Compactness and the Hausdorﬀ analytic GIT
topology give a homeomorphism. This is precisely the established transfer [3, Theorem 1.3(2)],
rather than a new continuity theorem.

6    Isometries on the singular cubic boundary
Proof of Theorem 2. The complex comparison is the preceding corollary with [1]. Its KE boundary
varieties are normal klt cubic hypersurfaces, and adjunction gives −KX = (n − 1)HX with HX
ample Cartier. Since n − 1 > n/2 + 1 for n ≥ 5, Theorem 1 applies.
    Donaldson–Sun [13, Proposition 2.14] identify the metric singular set and the analytic singular
set in this GH-limit setting. A metric isometry F : X → Z therefore maps Xreg onto Zreg . Myers–
Steenrod makes it a smooth Riemannian isometry locally there; this local assertion requires no
completeness of the regular locus. Thus I = F ∗ JZ is parallel and orthogonal. Theorem 1 gives
I = JX or −JX with a single global sign. Hence F is holomorphic on the regular locus, or
holomorphic to Z.
    To extend it, choose an analytic chart about F (x) and a neighborhood of x whose continuous
image lies in that chart. Each target coordinate composed with F is holomorphic on the regular
locus and locally bounded. Normal Riemann extension makes these functions holomorphic on the
whole neighborhood. They equal the continuous original coordinates by density and satisfy the
target equations. Repeat for F −1 . This gives a global holomorphic isomorphism, algebraic by
GAGA [19, Proposition 15], with target Z or Z. The metric and analytic topologies agree in the
GH algebraization; the local extension principle also appears in [13, Proposition 2.4].
    Grothendieck’s Picard Lefschetz theorem [14, Exposé XII, Corollaire 3.7] applies to global
projective complete intersections of dimension at least three, including singular ones. It gives
Pic(X) = Z[HX ]. Canonical preservation implies (n − 1)(F ∗ HZ − HX ) = 0, taking Z in the an-
tiholomorphic case. The Picard group is torsion free, so F ∗ HZ = HX . The complete hyperplane
system is the cubic embedding, since the hypersurface sequence gives h0 (X, HX ) = n + 2. Accord-
ingly these isomorphisms give precisely the corresponding projective cubic GIT points and their
conjugates.
    The forgetful map from the complex GH compactiﬁcation to ordinary metric GH space is
continuous. Any metric limit of smooth KE cubics has a polarized subsequential lift by polarized
                                         GH,metric
compactness, so its image is exactly Mn            . The preceding isometry classiﬁcation makes its
ﬁbers σ-orbits. Conversely complex conjugation changes both J and the real Kähler two-form by
sign, preserving the Riemannian metric. The induced map from Gn (C)/⟨σ⟩ is therefore a continuous
bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorﬀ space, hence a homeomorphism.

   No eﬀective GH-distance estimate or modulus of continuity is asserted. The sharpness example
in Theorem 1 concerns general Fanos; it does not establish a failure of cubic metric rigidity in
dimension four.

7    Prior results and the additional deduction
The lower-dimensional comparison is prior work: cubic surfaces by Odaka–Spotti–Sun, cubic three-
folds by Liu–Xu, and cubic fourfolds by Liu [8, 9, 10]. Cubic curves are not included in a positive-KE
Fano headline. Smooth KE existence should not be advertised as newly discovered here.
    The primary author page [11] currently links a September 30, 2026 manuscript, publicly down-
loaded on October 6, 2026 (Paciﬁc time). Its Theorem 1.1 states the arbitrary-dimensional cubic
comparison, including stacks and good moduli spaces; its proof uses reﬁned local inequalities and
tangent-sheaf semistability. The actual earliest web posting is unveriﬁed, and a manuscript date
alone does not establish public priority. Its present public availability is veriﬁed. This audit does
not independently certify every lemma of that separate paper.
    The complex cubic comparison is therefore prior public work. Our gap-based account instan-
tiates the known Spotti–Sun reduction; the unrestricted gap proof belongs to OpenAI, and global
Reeb minimization to Li–Liu.
    Theorem 1 and the singular metric-ﬁber argument are the additional deductions recorded here.
Smooth cubic tangent stability already follows from Peternell–Wiśniewski [16, Corollary 1.5 and
Theorem 2.3]. Smooth positive-KE rigidity up to factorwise conjugation is explained by Spotti
[17, Theorem 1.1.1]. Odaka–Spotti–Sun expressly give the ordinary metric quotient by conjugation
for their singular del Pezzo-surface compactiﬁcations [8, §§1–2]. The present argument checks the
singular higher-dimensional boundary by the Cartier-index exclusion of product covers, reﬂexive
endomorphisms, and the GH regular-locus theorem. It uses DGP’s splitting, not a newly invented
decomposition principle. The threshold’s product counterexample is elementary.
    Primary-source comparisons distinguish these exact scopes; they do not certify ﬁrst public dis-
closure of every possible equivalent corollary. We make no ﬁrst-priority claim and do not advertise
a new solution of the complex cubic K/GIT problem. The contribution is a modest, explicitly
attributed singular metric-rigidity criterion and its boundary application.
