         Even Minkowski uniqueness as a consequence of
                   logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                  Abstract
         We record consequences of OpenAI’s recently released origin-symmetric logarithmic Brunn–
     Minkowski inequality. For every ambient dimension n ≥ 2 and 0 ≤ p < 1, every smooth
     strictly positive even density on S n−1 has exactly one smooth positive even support-function
     solution of h1−p det(∇2 h + hI) = f , with positive curvature-radius matrix. For 0 < p < 1,
     identical Lp surface-area measures determine arbitrary full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex
     bodies. We give the nonsmooth ﬁrst-variation and strictness argument explicitly and a local
     version of the smooth endpoint transfer of He–Liu. Existence and regularity are established
     inputs. These are consequences of the new inequality and known reductions, not a new proof
     of logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski or a claim of ﬁrst public announcement. Equal-volume boxes
     exclude arbitrary-measure uniqueness at p = 0.

Theorem 1. Let n ≥ 2.
    1. For every 0 ≤ p < 1 and every strictly positive even f ∈ C ∞ (S n−1 ), there exists exactly one
       positive even h ∈ C ∞ (S n−1 ) satisfying

                                h1−p det Q(h) = f,            Q(h) = ∇2 h + hI > 0.                (2)

      Here the Hessian is spherical and the determinant is on the (n−1)-dimensional tangent space.
      Equivalently, h is the support function of a smooth origin-symmetric body with strictly positive
      Gauss curvature.

    2. If 0 < p < 1 and K, L are arbitrary origin-symmetric convex bodies with SK,p = SL,p , then
       K = L.

4    Existence, regularity and the endpoint boundary
For existence we apply the symmetry-preserving theorem of Bianchi, Böröczky, Colesanti and
Yang [7, Theorem 1.7 and Proposition 7.3]: for −n < p < 1 and a density bounded above and
below by positive constants, there is a convex-body solution of the Lp measure equation, preserving
invariance under a closed subgroup of O(n). Apply this with G = {I, −I} and density f . The
resulting full-dimensional symmetric body has 0 ∈ int K, hence hK > 0.
     Since 0 ∈ int K, Bianchi–Böröczky–Colesanti [9, Theorem 1.1(i)] gives a C 1 boundary contain-
ing no segment: its exceptional set is empty because the normal cone at the origin is {0}. A C 1
boundary implies strict convexity of the homogeneous support function on aﬃne hyperplanes avoid-
ing the origin, supplying the support-chart hypothesis of Chou–Wang [8, Proposition 1.2]. That
proposition gives h ∈ C 2,α and higher regularity for smooth data; see also [9, Theorem 1.1(iv)].
The measure equation is nonsingular because min h > 0. For n = 2, the distributional equation
h′′ + h = f hp−1 gives the same conclusion directly by bootstrapping. Convexity gives Q(h) ≥ 0; the
positive determinant in (2) makes every eigenvalue strictly positive. Interior elliptic bootstrapping
yields C ∞ . This proves existence in Theorem 1(1).

     The endpoint restriction is necessary. For the box B(a) = ni=1 [−ai , ai ], ai > 0, the atoms
of SB(a) at ±ei have mass V (B(a))/(2ai ). Since hB(a) (±ei ) = ai , every atom of SB(a),0 has mass
V (B(a))/2. Thus B(1, . . . , 1) and B(s, s−1 , 1, . . . , 1), s > 0, s ̸= 1, are distinct symmetric equal-
volume bodies with identical L0 measure. The corresponding cone-volume atoms have mass V /(2n).
Smooth positive data in Theorem 1(1) exclude this singular example.
