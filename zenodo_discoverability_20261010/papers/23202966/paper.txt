                      Binary consequences of relation-liveness
                              automata lower bounds
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                            October 6, 2026


                                                 Abstract
          We give explicit ﬁxed-binary-alphabet consequences of OpenAI’s two relation-liveness lower
      bounds. Encoding each relation on h points by its h2 adjacency bits gives a language recognized
      by a one-way nondeterministic automaton with Nh = (3h3 − h)/2 + 2 states. Every equivalent
      two-way deterministic automaton, and every two-way nondeterministic automaton for its full
                                                   1/3
      binary complement, requires 2Ω(h) = 2Ω(Nh ) states. We specify all transition rules, including
      an exact sh2 -state pullback for an s-state binary target. The binary language rejects incomplete
      blocks; a fooling set proves that its one-way nondeterministic state complexity is Θ(h3 ). The
      exponential obstructions are inherited from the two upstream theorems; binary adjacency coding
      is established machinery.


1    Scope, provenance, and conventions
Sakoda and Sipser introduced the state cost of deterministic simulation of two-way nondeterministic
automata [1]. Here we take as inputs the two explicit relation-language lower bounds in [2, 3]. Both
sources state their growing-alphabet scope. Our contribution is an explicit transfer to one binary
alphabet with full-word semantics, exact state counts, and a matching cubic order for the chosen
source encoding. We do not give an independent proof of the upstream exponential obstructions.
    Adjacency-bit encoding already appears in Kapoutsis’s discussion of binary one-way liveness [4,
Section 5] and [5, Section 8], there for deterministic targets with few reversals. That discussion does
not specify the treatment of incomplete binary blocks. Our cubic source-size claim concerns exactly
the total language deﬁned below. Other encodings can have diﬀerent source costs. A public binary-
construction record [9] also uses standard coding and target pullback, with a quadratic inherited
lower bound. Neither antecedent supplies the unrestricted exponential inputs used here. We make
no claim to have invented binary coding or to have independently resolved the base conjecture.
    For comparison, Adeogun–Kapoutsis [6, Theorem 1] proved a quadratic unrestricted determin-
istic lower bound for relation liveness. Their later method limitation [7, Theorem 2.1] bounds a
particular chain method, rather than every possible proof. Guillon–Prigioniero–Taheri [8, Section 1]
describe read-only 2NFA complementation as open; their polynomial complementation theorem
uses 1-limited automata, which may rewrite cells. Our results concern read-only machines. The
upstream manuscript dates are September 25, 2026; the public repository release was veriﬁed on
October 6, 2026. These dates should not be conﬂated with priority claims.
    Our marked model has one initial state and a set of accepting states. The head starts on the
left endmarker of ◁x▷; the two endmarkers are distinct, including on empty input. A transition
depends on the state and scanned symbol, chooses a new state and a move in {−1, 0, 1}, and moves


                                                     1
the head accordingly. Outward moves at endmarkers are disabled. Partial transitions are allowed.
Acceptance is reachability of an accepting state by a ﬁnite run, including zero transitions. Missing
transitions and inﬁnite nonaccepting runs do not accept. A 2DFA has at most one transition per
row; a 2NFA may have several. All states are counted. Our one-way source moves right on every
body bit; its only stay is the accepting move at the right marker. We also give an ordinary epsilon-
free 1NFA, whose acceptance is tested after consuming the whole word. The distinction matters
for empty input. Requiring a positive ﬁnite accepting run is also covered below.


2     Language and exact source compiler
Fix h ≥ 2, let H = {0, . . . , h − 1}, and let Σh be all relations on H. Products are in path order:
(p, r) ∈ RS exactly when some q has (p, q) ∈ R and (q, r) ∈ S. Write

                     Lh = {R1 · · · Rk ∈ Σ∗h : R1 · · · Rk 6= ∅},      R1 · · · R0 = IH .

Set b = h2 . The bit at oﬀset t = ph + q of enc(R) is 1 exactly when (p, q) ∈ R. Extend enc by
concatenation, with enc(ε) = ε, and deﬁne on every binary word

                                        Kh = enc(Lh ) ⊆ {0, 1}∗ .

Every length-b binary block codes exactly one relation. Thus Kh rejects all words whose lengths
are not divisible by b, and otherwise tests nonemptiness of the decoded product. It contains ε.
Lemma 1. Kh has a marked one-way NFA with exactly Nh = (3h3 − h)/2 + 2 states, and an
ordinary epsilon-free 1NFA with Nh − 1 states.

          U (p, t) (0 ≤ p < h, 0 ≤ t < (p + 1)h),          V (q, t)   (0 ≤ q < h, q + 1 ≤ t < b).

no rules. For a body bit c, give U (p, t) the following rules:
    • If t < (p + 1)h − 1, it may skip the bit and move right to U (p, t + 1).

    • If c = 1 and bt/hc = p, put q = t mod h. It may move right to V (q, t + 1) when t < b − 1,
      and to U (q, 0) when t = b − 1.
From V (q, t) either body bit moves right to V (q, t + 1) for t < b − 1, and to U (q, 0) for t = b − 1.
All rules not listed are absent. Every listed destination is in the displayed state ranges: a chosen
edge at oﬀset ph + q gives t + 1 ≥ q + 1. There are no left moves.
    A surviving branch through a full block must choose exactly one 1 in the row of its preceding
vertex. It then arrives at the next block as U (q, 0), precisely when the block relation contains that
edge. Skipping the entire row is impossible because the last row oﬀset has no skip rule. Induction
on complete blocks identiﬁes surviving boundary vertices with endpoints of paths through the
decoded relations. Only complete boundaries can accept at the right marker. This proves rejection
of partial blocks as well as correctness on valid words. On empty input, the initial right move and
the accepting stay give acceptance because H is nonempty. All accepting runs have at least two
transitions, so positive-run acceptance gives the same source language.

Proposition 2. Every ordinary 1NFA recognizing Kh has at least h3 states. The same bound holds
for a marked right-only source in our ﬁnite-run model. Consequently the one-way state complexity
of this strict-block language is Θ(h3 ).
Proof. Let Pp = {(p, p)} and z = enc(IH ). For p ∈ H and 0 ≤ t < b, put
                         xp,t = enc(Pp ) pref t (z),       yp,t = suﬀ b−t (z) enc(Pp ).
Here the preﬁx and suﬃx lengths add to b, including t = 0. For each matching pair, xp,t yp,t encodes
Pp IH Pp = Pp , so belongs to Kh . A crossed pair with t 6= u has length 3b + t − u, which is not
divisible by b, since 0 < |t − u| < b. When t = u but p 6= r, the crossed word encodes Pp IH Pr = ∅.
Thus all hb = h3 diagonal pairs accept and all oﬀ-diagonal crossed pairs reject.
    Choose an accepting run for each diagonal pair and its state at the cut between x and y. If
two cuts had the same state, splicing the preﬁx of one run to the suﬃx of the other would accept
a crossed word. The cuts must therefore occupy distinct states. This is the standard fooling-set
argument, here with its explicit witnesses. For a marked source, an accepting run cannot accept
before visiting the right marker: otherwise the same ﬁnite preﬁx of its run accepts an extension by
one bit, whose length is malformed. Such a run therefore has a cut after the preﬁx before reading
the suﬃx, and the same splicing argument applies. Stay moves, if allowed in a right-only source,
can be assigned to either side of the cut and do not aﬀect the argument.


3    Exact pullback for all two-way targets
Lemma 3. An s-state binary 2NFA T has an explicit sb-state 2NFA T enc over Σh satisfying
                               T enc accepts w       ⇐⇒      T accepts enc(w).
Determinism is preserved. This equivalence holds for both zero-step and positive-run acceptance,
with partial transitions and nonaccepting inﬁnite runs allowed.
Proof. If Q is the state set of T , use Q × {0, . . . , b − 1}. The initial state is (q0 , 0); (q, t) accepts
exactly when q accepts. At a relation letter R, inspect its bit at oﬀset t. For every transition

as in T . No transition is supplied for a missing row.
     On a relation word of length k, map a conﬁguration at real body cell j ∈ {1, . . . , k} with state
(q, t) to the binary conﬁguration with state q at position (j − 1)b + t + 1. At either real marker,
map all phases to the corresponding binary marker with state q. The rules above show that each
transition maps to exactly one transition of T , and that every transition of T from the image
conﬁguration lifts from each corresponding pullback conﬁguration. Marker phases need not be
unique, because marker rows ignore them. On the empty word, moves between its adjacent markers
remain correct under this mapping. Starting from the prescribed initial states, induction on run
length proves ﬁnite-run equivalence, with the same length. Acceptance and time-zero acceptance
are preserved; hence positive-run acceptance is preserved too. Transition-by-transition lifting also
preserves inﬁnite runs. An inﬁnite run supplies no new ﬁnite accepting witness on either side. The
construction has exactly sb states and at most one rule per row if T does.

    For a complementing target using positive-run acceptance, add a new nonaccepting initial state
with the old initial state’s transition row, whose destinations remain old states. Under zero-step
acceptance this s+1-state machine accepts exactly the old positive-run language: its ﬁrst transition
is the required positive ﬁrst step. Hence the complementation statement remains stretched expo-
nential, with the explicit variant 2b(h−2)/127c ≤ 2((s + 1)h2 + 1). The deterministic formula already
covers both conventions. If a convention requires total transition rows, an additional nonaccepting
sink with legal stay loops supplies missing rules; adding a sink changes the source bound by one
and does not weaken a lower bound for the larger partial model. Conventions requiring acceptance
at the right endmarker or halting before accepting form subclasses of the target model; the same
lower bounds apply.
    The exponent here is proportional to the cube root of the binary source size. We do not assert
2Ω(n) for n source states, or a uniform complexity separation such as L 6= NL. The transition
tables of relation-alphabet pullbacks can be large; this is a state-count argument, not a small-table
uniform algorithm claim.
