                                            Abstract
     The classical four-state group-based DNA models form three symmetry strata on G = Z2 ×Z2 :
Jukes–Cantor (JC), Kimura 2-parameter (K2P), and Kimura 3-parameter (K3P) tie the three
nonzero Fourier characters in respectively one, two, and three blocks. Using exact version-bound
results from three companion classiﬁcations, we prove one theorem for binary standard semi-
directed strongly tree-child networks of level at most two. On the principal positive-eigenvalue
domain, and separately in strict continuous time, a source-relative regular full-dimensional con-
tainment from N to N 0 on the same ﬁnite labelled leaf set exists exactly when the labelled
networks diﬀer only in their reticulate-vertex choices at matched ordinary-triangle sites. The
same condition characterizes a common regular germ full-dimensional in both images. Thus the
directed containment relation restricts to an equivalence relation on this class, with no proper
one-sided generic containment, and a generic exact distribution under any one known ﬁxed
model recovers the same combinatorial quotient among labelled networks in this class on the
same leaf set.
     The synthesis also yields uniform local formulas. If r ∈ {1, 2, 3} is the number of nonzero
character-tying blocks, then retained bridge charts have one incidence scale per retained
component–bridge incidence and tying block, serial marginal maps multiply r sector coordi-
nates independently, and adjoining the same cherry raises local image dimension by 2r. The
normalized conservation-supported three-leaf ambient dimension is ar = r(r + 7)/2. A direct
S3 -isotypic calculation for the standard six-edge ordinary-triangle parameterization gives

     Strong tree-childness is a sharp uniform class-level boundary: for every stratum, there ex-

1    Introduction
Identiﬁability is the information-theoretic prerequisite for reconstructing a reticulate evolutionary
history from sequence data. A phylogenetic network combines ordinary Markov substitution on
edges with inheritance choices at reticulations, so its leaf distribution is a structured mixture over
displayed trees. Two nonisomorphic networks may therefore have diﬀerent polynomial closures,
share isolated stochastic points, overlap on full-dimensional real regions, or even exhibit directed
containment of one regular model germ in another. These relations have diﬀerent inferential mean-
ings and cannot be replaced by a single ideal-comparison test.
For level-2 networks, the public frontier developed along two complementary lines. Algebraic and
semialgebraic work established identiﬁability for selected level-1 and level-2 classes, exposed excep-
tional triangle geometry, and developed invariant and Jacobian methods [11, 13, 12, 5, 10, 6, 2, 21].
Combinatorial work determined when quartets or quarnets recover semi-directed network structure
and showed that four-leaf restrictions suﬃce at level two but not for all level-3 networks [14, 16].
The principal preceding level-2 substitution-model theorem proves generic JC identiﬁability for
triangle-free strongly tree-child networks [7]; recent level-1 work obtains full topology identiﬁability
modulo triangle redirection for JC, K2P, and K3P [4].
Four companion papers now establish a sharper three-model, strongly tree-child regular-germ pic-
ture at level at most two. The JC paper removes triangle-freeness, classiﬁes directed regular
full-dimensional containment, reconstructs the exact observable quotient, and proves a triangle-
free sharpness family [19]. The K2P paper establishes the analogous classiﬁcation on the principal
positive Fourier domain and in strict continuous time, with a complete two-sector bridge ﬁbre and
a weak-class 4n − 3 ambiguity family [18]. The K3P paper closes the three-sector case, identiﬁes
the common triangle hypersurface H14 , and proves a weak-class 6n − 3 sharpness family [20]. A
separate collision paper constructs exact strict K2P and K3P tree–theta coincidences outside weak
tree-childness and analyzes their local parameter ﬁbres [17].
The purpose of the present paper is not to reproduce those four proofs. It is to expose the common
theorem, identify the structural reason the same topology quotient survives from JC through K3P,
and derive consequences not visible from any one companion. Model-speciﬁc graph atlases, local
separators, interval certiﬁcates, contextual triangle sections, and witness parameters remain in
the companion papers. Here they enter as load-bearing inputs to a new symmetry-stratiﬁed proof-
dependency schema. The one missing root-containing-factor convention check in the deposited K3P
interface is proved explicitly here. The sitewise proof crosswalk also distinguishes the companions’
potentially narrower whole-factor wording from the full-graph ordinary-triangle predicate actually
carried by their local, restoration, probe, and contextual arguments. In contrast, the ordinary-
triangle rank progression is proved directly here from one S3 -equivariant tangent map, and the ﬁrst
cross-model containment regimes are closed rather than left as an undiﬀerentiated open problem.

2     Networks, symmetry strata, and observational relations
2.1   The ﬁxed semi-directed convention

A rooted binary phylogenetic network on a ﬁnite leaf set is a ﬁnite directed acyclic graph without
parallel arcs: the root has bidegree (0, 2), labelled leaves have bidegree (1, 0), tree vertices have
bidegree (1, 2), and reticulations have bidegree (2, 1). Every vertex is reachable from the root, and
the root is the lowest stable ancestor of the leaves.
To form the standard semi-directed presentation, mark every arc entering a reticulation, undirect
every other arc, delete the binary root, and merge its two incident arcs into one edge while retaining
at each endpoint the arrowhead, if any, present before deletion. We admit the presentation only
when this one-step suppression creates no loop or parallel edge and loses no reticulation arrowhead.
No later exhaustive cleanup is allowed. The result is a simple labelled mixed graph, called a
standard semi-directed network; an admissible rooting is a rooted binary network whose one-step
suppression is exactly that mixed graph. Isomorphisms preserve labels, ordinary edges, retained
arrowheads, and vertex roles [22, 15].
Thus the allowed mixed edges are ordinary unoriented edges and edges carrying one retained ar-
rowhead at a reticulation endpoint. A two-arrowhead edge is not admitted: it would arise from a
root with two reticulation children and is incompatible with the ﬁxed simple standard presentation.
Restriction to a leaf subset is a diﬀerent operation and may prune dead ends or suppress bivalent
vertices only after the displayed-parent choices have been made.
A bridge is an edge whose deletion disconnects the underlying graph, and a blob is a maximal non-
trivial biconnected block. The level is the maximum number of reticulations in a blob; throughout,
“level 2” means level at most two. Removing all bridges decomposes a network into complete ported
factors; contracting every nontrivial factor while retaining intervening branching components gives
the labelled reduced tree of blobs.

Deﬁnition 2.1 (Weak and strong tree-childness). A standard semi-directed network is weakly tree-
child if at least one admissible rooting is tree-child. It is strongly tree-child if it has an admissible

rooting and every admissible rooting is tree-child. Equivalently in the binary ﬁxed convention, every
tail of a retained reticulation edge is incident with two ordinary edges, so no admissible rooting
creates an omnian.

every nontrivial complete factor induced by STC2 has a cycle core or one of four directed theta
cores, together with mandatory child ports and ordered subdivision words [19, 18, 20]. We use that
theorem as a common combinatorial input rather than reproducing its ﬁnite atlas.

2.3    The regular-germ topology quotient

An ordinary triangle site in a full standard mixed graph is a simple three-cycle with one external
incidence at each of its three vertices and with exactly one cycle vertex reticulate. The two cycle
edges incident with that vertex carry the retained arrowheads. The external arms may continue
through an unchanged surrounding context and may, in particular, reconnect inside a theta factor.
A valid local ordinary-triangle redirection changes only which vertex of such a site is reticulate
and the two corresponding cycle arrowheads. It ﬁxes the underlying labelled graph and all three
external incidences pointwise, preserves every edge and arrowhead outside the site, and is allowed
only when both the source and the result belong to STC2 on the same labelled leaf set.

Lemma 2.7 (Triangle sites are blobwise unique). Every blob of a network in STC2 contains at
most one ordinary triangle site, and distinct ordinary triangle sites are vertex-disjoint.

Proof. By the common cycle–theta grammar, a nontrivial blob has a cycle core or a theta core,
with subdivisions and boundary attachments. A cycle core has at most one three-cycle. In a theta
core, let the three internally disjoint pole paths have positive integral lengths `1 , `2 , `3 . Each simple
cycle is the union of two pole paths. If two distinct cycles were triangles, two pairwise sums would
equal three. Up to order, the path lengths would be either (1, 1, 2) or (1, 2, 2). The ﬁrst case has
two parallel pole edges and is excluded by the simple standard convention.
The second case is the simple graph K4 − e. Its cyclomatic number is two, so a binary level-2
orientation has two reticulations and therefore four incoming reticulation edges. No reticulation
can tail one of those edges, because its unique child would then be a reticulation and tree-childness
would fail. Only the two remaining tree vertices can be tails. Each can tail at most one reticulation
edge: otherwise it is incident with fewer than the two ordinary edges required by the strong no-
omnian criterion in deﬁnition 2.1. Thus at most two incoming reticulation edges can be supplied,
not four, a contradiction.
Finally, every cycle lies in one nontrivial blob. Two distinct blobs cannot share a vertex lying on a
cycle in each: each blob would use two diﬀerent incidences there, forcing underlying degree at least
four. Binary degree therefore makes sites in distinct blobs vertex-disjoint.

For a full network N ∈ STC2 , form the marked mixed graph E4 (N ) as follows. Retain the entire
underlying labelled graph, all external incidences, and every retained arrowhead outside an ordinary
triangle site. At each such site, tag its three cycle edges as one triangle block and erase only the two
internal arrowheads, the choice of its reticulate vertex, and the induced site-local child designation.
Marked graphs are considered up to leaf-label-preserving isomorphism that preserves the tags and
all un-erased data. By lemma 2.7, this operation is unambiguous within every cycle or theta factor;
in particular it does not discard the surrounding theta context.

4.1    What the schema records

The proof-dependency schema explains why the three increasingly rich edge models have the same
observable topology quotient once their separate companion interfaces are inserted. It does not
replace the human proofs or ﬁnite certiﬁcates establishing those interfaces, and it does not imply
that every group-based model satisﬁes analogous hypotheses. Nor does it cover signed-eigenvalue
components, nonreversible models, cross-model containments, or higher network level. It remains a
useful blueprint: extending the result to a new model would require separately deﬁning and proving
concrete counterparts of all four blocks.

6.2    A model-uniform structural algorithm

Given an exact normalized Fourier tensor q, accessed through the exact-real oracle interface speciﬁed
below, on a ﬁxed labelled leaf set X, and a known model M ∈ {JC, K2P, K3P} together with the

networks in STC2 on X. Steps 1–6 below are conservative pruning and organization steps. A
candidate is discarded only when an exact test proves it incompatible. Whenever a rank, sign,
denominator, localization, or restoration test is degenerate or inconclusive, the procedure retains
or branches over every compatible alternative; in particular, all local pruning may be skipped
without aﬀecting correctness. The ﬁnite complete-tensor feasibility decisions in Step 7 are the
load-bearing ﬁnal tests.

6.3   Computational interpretation

For a ﬁxed taxon set, the procedure performs ﬁnite candidate enumeration and makes ﬁnitely many
calls in a relative exact-real computation model. It may invoke exact real-ﬁeld operations and sign
evaluation in the coordinates of q, together with decision oracles for ﬁrst-order formulas over real
closed ﬁelds in which q is admitted as an exact parameter tuple. Thus q need not have algebraic
coordinates. The theorem asserts neither a ﬁnite Turing encoding for an arbitrary real input nor a
bit-complexity or numerical-stability bound. The companion packages replay the bounded model-
speciﬁc tests used to construct and reject candidates. The ﬁnal complete-tensor feasibility query is
one exact real-semialgebraic oracle; no bundled production implementation of that general query
is claimed. The procedure is not a production ﬁnite-sample estimator.
The local tests use bounded restrictions as speciﬁed in the companion papers, but Step 7 uses
the complete tensor. Consequently this is not a bounded ﬁnite-marginal suﬃciency theorem (in
particular, it is not a nine-leaf or ten-leaf suﬃciency claim).
The distinction matters operationally. A practical certiﬁer should not force a topology when an
empirical conﬁdence region meets two model images, approaches a triangle or tree–theta ambiguity
stratum, or falls outside the assumed model. The ﬁnite-sample program in problem 8.9 therefore
requires quantitative separation margins and fail-closed outputs in addition to the exact structural
theorem.


6.4   What the quotient does not recover

The structural theorem does not claim recovery of edge spectra, inheritance probabilities, or a
chosen local incidence normalization. The r scales per retained component–bridge incidence are
redundancies of the expanded contraction chart, not by themselves ﬁbres of the original unsplit
parameterization; any genuine internal parameter ﬁbre must be established separately. The theorem
recovers the labelled ordinary-triangle equivalence class Q2 (N ) and makes no stronger numerical-
parameter claim.

8.5   Beyond the classical hierarchy

The transfer/interface architecture suggests a modular route to broader models: establish physical
bridge calculus, localization, local rigidity, and contextual triangle gluing. General equivariant,
reversible, and general Markov models may fail these conditions in diﬀerent ways. At the graph
level, stacked reticulations already create exact observational equivalences for broad transition
models [24], so any universal theorem must recover a canonical observable reduction rather than
an unrestricted network.
A separate biological branch replaces displayed-tree substitution mixtures by the network multi-
species coalescent. Positive arbitrary-level results exist for restricted galled tree-child classes [1],
but the observable summaries and identiﬁability mechanisms are diﬀerent. The long-term objective
is a common canonical network quotient recoverable from site-pattern distributions, gene-tree dis-
tributions, or concordance factors under explicitly stated assumptions, not an unqualiﬁed transfer
of the present Fourier theorem.

8.6   Recommended order

The most eﬃcient sequence is:
 (1) solve the remaining upward, diﬀerent-quotient cross-model reﬁnement directions on STC2 ,
     beginning with four-leaf cases;
 (2) classify strong-class exceptional intersections and build the ﬁnite-sample certiﬁer in parallel;
 (3) extract the minimal localization radius needed at level three;
 (4) only then generalize the transfer axioms to broader substitution or coalescent models.
This order converts the completed same-model theory into model-robust and empirically usable
conclusions before reopening a much larger topology atlas.


9     Scope, proof provenance, and publication architecture
9.1   Exact mathematical scope

The positive theorem concerns binary standard semi-directed strongly tree-child networks of level
at most two under the ﬁxed one-step reticulation-preserving convention, with labelled leaves and

                                                  36
the classical four-state stationary group-based substitution models. The observation is the com-
plete leaf site-pattern tensor from an edge-heterogeneous displayed-tree mixture with uniform root
and independent reticulation choices. The theorem is proved on the principal positive-eigenvalue
domains and separately in strict continuous time. For each known ﬁxed model it classiﬁes source-
relative regular full-dimensional physical containment and common full-dimensional regular germs.
The generic reconstruction theorem compares labelled networks in this same STC2 class on the
same leaf set; it makes no uniqueness claim against networks outside that candidate universe.
The theorem does not assert:
  • equality of complete stochastic images for triangle-related networks;
  • stochastic boundaries, identity, zero, or singular edge transitions, inheritance weights 0 or 1,
    or mixed/signed spectral components;
  • pointwise uniqueness at every strict parameter;
  • complete numerical-parameter identiﬁability;
  • a complete cross-model topology classiﬁcation beyond the directionality theorem, quotient-
    preserving reﬁnement theorem, and three-leaf classiﬁcation of propositions 8.1 and 8.2 and the-
    orem 8.6;
  • K3P coverage on signed-eigenvalue stochastic components;
  • ﬁnite-sample consistency, conﬁdence bounds, or numerical stability;
  • bounded ﬁnite-marginal suﬃciency (the reconstruction procedure ends with a complete-tensor
    feasibility test);
  • recovery when the substitution model is unknown, or a shared-Q, molecular-clock, or global-
    timing theorem;
  • the same result for GTR, general Markov, coalescent, or mixture models;
  • a classiﬁcation of all weakly tree-child or outer level-2 networks;
  • generic reconstruction against competitors outside STC2 ; or
  • any theorem at level three or above.
Accordingly, the precise completion claim is: a complete same-model regular full-dimensional
topology-containment classiﬁcation for the stated strongly tree-child class under JC, K2P, and
K3P on the stated physical domains, together with a sharp uniform class-level boundary. It is not
a classiﬁcation of complete stochastic images or of unknown-model recovery.

9.4    Reproducibility of the new synthesis claims

The separate full reproducibility archive contains four deterministic exact replays; the journal-
facing TeX package does not. The symmetry script enumerates the diagonal actions of the canonical
blockwise stabilizers on all sixteen conservation-supported three-leaf Fourier coordinates and veriﬁes
the orbit counts 5, 10, 16, ambient dimensions 4, 9, 15, and the symmetry-saturation warning for the
order-three subgroup. The triangle script diﬀerentiates the literal K3P sunlet map over Q, veriﬁes
S3 -equivariance, computes the decompositions 71 ⊕ 6V and 41 ⊕ 5V ⊕ sgn, certiﬁes the selected
maximal-minor determinants −1/2 and −1/4, and checks that the nonzero H14 diﬀerential spans
the dual sign conormal and annihilates the triangle tangent image. The cross-model script expands
all six circuit factorizations and mechanically audits the analytic theorem’s count consequences for
all 144 ordered three-leaf pairs; it is not an independent proof of the global analytic directionality
or suﬃciency propositions. The embedded-site script checks the four-leaf theta witness used to
distinguish the sitewise marked relation from a whole-factor reading. It is graph- semantic regression
only and does not establish a collision or common germ.
