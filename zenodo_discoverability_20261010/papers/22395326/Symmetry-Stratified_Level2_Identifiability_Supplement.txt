1     Purpose and dependency boundary
This supplement records the detailed physical-domain calculations, the expanded ported-blob proof-
interface obligations, the proof-dependency crosswalk, and the exact ﬁnite replays underlying the
synthesis paper: symmetry saturation, the representation-theoretic triangle tangent theorem, and
the bounded cross-model classiﬁcation. It does not reproduce the model-speciﬁc topology atlases,
contextual triangle-section constructions, interval boxes, or strict collision parameters. Those re-
main in the four companion papers and their replay packages [3, 2, 4, 1].


2     Fourier domains and strict physical operations
2.1   Inverse Fourier rows

For a K3P edge with spectrum (1, c, g, t), the ﬁrst transition row is
                    1
                      (1 + c + g + t, 1 + c − g − t, 1 − c + g − t, 1 − c − g + t).
                    4
Hence strict transition positivity on the positive-eigenvalue branch is

            0 < c, g, t < 1,   1 + c − g − t > 0,   1 − c + g − t > 0,   1 − c − g + t > 0.

K2P is obtained by setting c = t = s, giving
                               1
                                 (1 + 2s + g, 1 − g, 1 − 2s + g, 1 − g),
                               4
so its principal positive domain is 0 < s, g < 1 with g > 2s − 1. JC is the isotropic specialization
c = g = t = x ∈ (0, 1).

2.2   Strict continuous time

For a symmetric group-based generator on Z2 × Z2 , write the three nontrivial substitution-rate
classes as α, β, γ > 0 and edge time as τ > 0. With a ﬁxed character convention,

                        c = e−2(β+γ)τ ,     g = e−2(α+γ)τ ,     t = e−2(α+β)τ .

                                                    1
Solving for the rates shows that strict positivity of all three rate classes is equivalent to

                                    c > gt,     g > ct,      t > cg.

Under K2P this reduces to g > s2 ; under JC every 0 < x < 1 has a strict continuous-time realization.
No shared generator, clock, or common rate ratio is imposed across edges.


2.3   Composition and strict subdivision

Serial composition multiplies nontrivial Fourier eigenvalues coordinatewise. The following proposi-
tion supplies the physical divisibility used by root movement, marginal sections, and bridge gluing.

Proposition 2.1 (Strict local subdivision). Every edge spectrum in a principal positive domain,
and separately every edge spectrum in a strict continuous-time domain, can be written locally and
analytically as the coordinatewise product of two strict spectra in the same domain.

Proof. For JC, choose ρ ∈ (x, 1) and write x = ρ(x/ρ). For K2P, choose an isotropic factor (ρ, ρ)
suﬃciently close to (1, 1) that (s/ρ, g/ρ) remains strict; in continuous time require additionally
g/ρ > (s/ρ)2 , possible for ρ close enough to one. For K3P, choose (ρ, ρ, ρ) with

                            ρ > max{c, g, t, g + t − c, c + t − g, c + g − t}

in the positive domain and
                                   ρ > max{c, g, t, gt/c, ct/g, cg/t}
in strict continuous time. Then the isotropic factor and the divided spectrum both satisfy the
relevant strict inequalities. Shrinking the target neighborhood makes the choice analytic.


3     Expanded ported-blob proof-interface obligations
The main paper groups the concrete model-speciﬁc interfaces into four blocks. The following
expanded list is an audit crosswalk to the exact companion statements and the explicitly identiﬁed
synthesis completion, not a separately axiomatized arbitrary-model theorem. In particular, A8
contains the simultaneous global-germ/right-inverse interface used for suﬃciency.

A1. Physical divisibility and root movement.
     Every strict edge parameter admits a local analytic factorization into two strict edge pa-
     rameters. Moving the root among admissible rootings of the same standard semi-directed
     mixed graph preserves the physical image up to analytic reparameterization. When a root-
     containing factor must be given a real incoming boundary, admissibility of that boundary
     rooting is a separate graph obligation.

A2. Bridge-tree transfer.
     If N ⪯reg,fd N ′ with N, N ′ ∈ STC2 on the same ﬁnite labelled leaf set, then the labelled
     bridge splits and reduced bridge-incidence trees agree, including the distinction between
     ordinary trivalent components and nontrivial factors.




                                                   2
A3. Local physical bridge gauge.
     At every retained regular strong-class component-incidence chart used by the proof, the
     germ of a ﬁxed-output contraction has exactly one positive scale per retained component–
     bridge incidence and tying sector, and no additional bridge gauge. Its physical ﬁbre is the
     incidence orbit intersected with the complete local physical- realization locus in contraction-
     coordinate space, including component realizability. Internal component-parameter ﬁbres
     are quotiented out; the contraction ﬁbre has a local product chart and no bridge-tree holon-
     omy.

A4. Physical marginal localization.
     Every selected graph-derived marginal is a physical submersion onto its eﬀective child do-
     main with local analytic sections. A ﬁnite semialgebraic-cover argument ﬁxes one target
     incoming/completion type on a source-open stratum, and remote factors cannot compensate
     for a local separator.

A5. Primitive graph grammar.
     Every root-containing factor admits an admissible tree-child presentation at a real labelled
     leaf-bearing incoming boundary under the ﬁxed one-step convention. In such a presen-
     tation, every complete nontrivial factor induced by STC2 has a cycle core or one of four
     directed theta cores and is uniquely speciﬁed by mandatory child ports and labelled ordered
     subdivision words.

A6. Local rigidity and restoration.
     A regular full-dimensional projective containment between complete ported factors implies
     labelled mixed-graph isomorphism or a full-factor transport that forgets only the role and
     two incoming heads at one licensed ordinary- triangle site. Every oﬀ-site arrowhead, role,
     incidence, and ordered port word is restored coherently. The site may lie inside a theta; the
     complete factor need not itself be a three-cycle.

A7. Triangle suﬃciency in context.
     The three ordinary-triangle orientations share a regular germ full-dimensional relative to
      their local images, with analytic physical right inverses into every orientation parameter
      space. The germ and its right inverses survive any unchanged labelled multilinear context,
      including reconnection of two triangle terminals inside a theta factor.

A8. Simultaneous physical gluing.
      Corresponding isomorphic or triangle-related factor germs along a common bridge tree admit
      compatible physical bridge choices whose contraction is a common regular global germ full-
      dimensional in both complete image germs, with analytic physical right inverses into both
      global parameter spaces.

The grouping used in the main paper is T1=(A1–A3), T2=(A4–A5), T3=A6, and T4=(A7–A8).

7    Bounded cross-model replay
The main paper proves that no regular full-dimensional containment can point from a strictly richer
classical model to a poorer one. Conversely, whenever the target model reﬁnes the source, every
triangle-equivalent strong-class pair admits a cross-model containment by composing the source-
model section with the tying-reﬁnement embedding. It also classiﬁes all ordered pairs among the
three-leaf tree T and the three labelled ordinary-sunlet orientations S1 , S2 , S3 under JC, K2P, and
K3P. Containment holds exactly when the target model is at least as rich as the source and the
tree/triangle observable quotients agree.
The script research/verify_cross_model_reconnaissance.py independently expands the six
K3P circuit factorizations from the literal sunlet map. It then applies the proved directionality,
separator, and constructive-embedding cases as an explicit classiﬁcation table over the 122 = 144
ordered model/topology pairs. The enumeration audits the theorem’s count consequences; it is not
