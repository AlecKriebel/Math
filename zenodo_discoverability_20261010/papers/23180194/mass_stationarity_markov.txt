                    Measure-only preserving Markov transports
                          characterize mass-stationarity
                                                 Alec Kriebel∗

                           6 October 2026        — Unrefereed research note


                                                      Abstract
            Let G be a locally compact second countable Hausdorff Abelian group, acting jointly mea-
        surably on an arbitrary measurable space. Let Q be sigma-finite and let ξ be a covariant
        locally finite Radon random measure, nonzero Q-almost everywhere. We prove that full am-
        bient Q-invariance under every invariant, ξ-preserving, ξ-measurable Markov transport kernel
        implies the joint Mecke identity, and hence mass-stationarity. Together with known transport
        invariance this answers Last–Thorisson’s Problem 7.3 in the stated setting. The argument uses
        oriented local symmetric transports away from periods of the measure and normalized period-
        subgroup transports on the remaining set. It requires neither a standard Borel ambient space
        nor a sigma-finite law of ξ, and imposes no finite-intensity assumption.

    The credited inputs are the classical Mecke/Palm equivalence, in the ambient formulation [2,
(2.7)], the equivalence of Palm measures and mass-stationarity [2, Theorem 6.3], and transport
invariance [2, Theorem 4.1]. The contribution of the proof below is deriving (5) from the Markov
hypothesis with measure-only kernel dependence. The bounded weighted-kernel characterization
was already proved in [2, Theorem 7.2]; a weighted kernel may have row mass different from one
and cannot be substituted in our hypothesis. Theorem 1 answers precisely [2, Problem 7.3]. It also
yields the canonical law-of-ξ specialization of the general all-Markov converse discussed in [5, pp.
2692, 2694].
    Important partial predecessors are [3, Propositions 3.6 and 3.8]: for discrete measures, measure-
only Bernoulli tests recover Mecke reversal away from measure-periodic shifts, and give mass-
stationarity in the aperiodic case (under the stated origin-in-support condition). The more general
discrete theorem there allows dependence on an auxiliary state and assumes that state space is
Borel; [3, Remark 3.11] expressly records the periodicity qualification. A positive-density result on
Rd appears in [4, Theorem 6], formulated for the joint pair ((X, Z), ξ) with ξ(dt) = Zt dt, local
integrability on every line and infinite integral on every half-line. Its density-field hypotheses are
not silently identified with a version of Z observable from ξ alone. We cite the retained arXiv
version specified in the bibliography; its complete final journal body has not been checked here.

For µ ∈ M, put
                                      Hµ = {t ∈ G : θt µ = µ}.

by the measurable diagonal of M. Since G is Abelian, Hθs µ = Hµ for every s. For a relatively
compact Borel set B ⊂ G define

                           wB (µ) = µ(Hµ ∩ B),

Integration of the Borel graph against the measure kernel shows that wB and κB are measurable;
local finiteness gives wB < ∞. The last line is a measurable probability kernel and directly gives

of Haar measures.

Completion of Theorem 1. Equations (12) and (16) give c = R∗ c, which is exactly (5). The estab-
lished ambient Mecke characterization [2, (2.7)], credited there to Mecke [1], supplies a stationary
sigma-finite measure P on (Ω, F) with Q = Pξ . By [2, Theorem 6.3], Q is mass-stationary in
the sense of (4). Conversely mass-stationarity gives such a P , and [2, Theorem 4.1] gives (3) for
every invariant ξ-preserving transport kernel, in particular the measure-only Markov kernels of the
theorem.

    The proof permits infinite Q(Ω), infinite ξ(G), and a non-sigma-finite law of ξ. The nonzero
assumption is essential to the source convention: at the zero measure an origin shift can leave
a canonical state fixed regardless of the sampling interpretation. Non-Abelian groups and non-
locally-finite measures lie outside the stated theorem.
    The general Markov question is distinct from Last’s narrower question about kernels obtained
by applying deterministic allocations to the inserted Cox process [5, p. 2673]; see also the sepa-
rate averaged-Cox question [3, Remark 4.8]. Theorem 1 does not establish that smaller-test-class
converse and does not claim to resolve every question in the Oberwolfach report.
    Two bounded literature reviews found no exact prior resolution in the primary bodies and
repository records they checked. This is not an exhaustive literature certificate or a claim of
worldwide firstness. In particular the complete final 2015 journal body and an identified 2016
lecture were not reviewed. Finite computational controls from the development check examples
of preservation, gates, and periods; they are supplementary finite controls, not a proof of the
general result. The proof offered here is the measure-theoretic argument above. This version is an
extensively AI-assisted, unrefereed research note, without human peer review or formal machine
verification.
