               A counterexample to Takao’s self-duality question
                                                Alec Kriebel
                                           Independent researcher
                                           ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                     Abstract
           Takao asked whether a ﬁnite ﬂat commutative group scheme over a Witt ring, killed by p and having
      quasi-supersingular special ﬁber, must be Cartier self-dual in ranks p2n with n ≥ 3. For every p > 3
      and n ≥ 3, we give a counterexample over W (Fp ). A six-dimensional Dieudonné module has an explicit
      ﬁltration by supersingular elliptic p-torsion modules, an explicit ﬁnite Honda complement, and an image-
      intersection invariant distinguishing it from its dual. Both the special ﬁber and its ﬁnite ﬂat Witt-ring
      lift are non-self-dual. The construction is an application of classical Dieudonné and ﬁnite Honda theory.


1    The question and result
Let p > 3 be prime. A ﬁnite commutative group scheme H over a perfect ﬁeld of characteristic p, killed by
p, is quasi-supersingular if it has a subgroup ﬁltration whose successive quotients are Ei [p] for supersingular
elliptic curves Ei over that ﬁeld. This is Takao’s Deﬁnition 1(3) [5]. His Proposition 2 states that, for a ﬁnite
ﬂat commutative W (k)-group scheme G killed by p, of rank p2n , a quasi-supersingular special ﬁber forces
both the special ﬁber and G to be self-dual when n ≤ 2. The unnumbered question immediately following
that proposition asks whether the assertion remains true for n ≥ 3 [5, p. 2479].
Theorem 1. For every prime p > 3 and integer n ≥ 3, put k = Fp and W = W (k). There is a ﬁnite ﬂat
commutative group scheme Gn /W , killed by p and of rank p2n , whose special ﬁber Hn is quasi-supersingular,
such that
                                        Hn ̸≃ HnD ,     Gn ̸≃ GnD .
Here D denotes Cartier duality.
     Thus the answer to the stated higher-rank question is negative, with the ﬁrst possible counterexample
at n = 3, using Takao’s aﬃrmative result for n ≤ 2. The hypothesis concerns the special ﬁber: we do not
require its ﬁltration to lift to W .
     The module used below belongs to the classical cyclic-word framework. In the convention of Pries–Ulmer
it is M (vvf vf f ) [4, §§3.2.1,4.1]; in the forward-arrow order of our contravariant presentation its string is
f f vf vv. The module construction and its duality classiﬁcation are prior mathematics. Oort’s cyclic integral
construction also supplies a balanced supersingular completion [3, §§2.5–2.6]. We give direct calculations
of the ﬁltration, duality obstruction, and ﬁnite Honda complement to answer the speciﬁc question without
relying on the cyclic-word classiﬁcation. No ﬁrst-discovery or ﬁrst-priority claim is made. The supporting
package records the bounded literature audit and its source-access and version limits.

We use contravariant Dieudonné theory over k as in [2, §2]. Write σ for Frobenius on k and also for its lift
to W . On a module killed by p, F is σ-semilinear, V is σ −1 -semilinear, and F V = V F = 0. Let M have

2.1     Quasi-supersingularity
Let I denote the two-dimensional module on x, y with
                                          F x = V x = y,          F y = V y = 0.
Over algebraically closed k, I is the module of the p-torsion of a supersingular elliptic curve. This is the
rank-two case of [2, Lemma 4.9]: the module is deformable and im F = im V .

                          0 = M0 ⊂ M1 = spank (u, v) ⊂ M2 = spank (u, v, w, z) ⊂ M3 = M
is an F, V -stable ﬂag. The three quotient modules are I, using, respectively, the pairs (u, v), (w, z), and
(a, b). Exact contravariance gives subgroups Hi ⊂ H with modules M/M3−i , for 0 ≤ i ≤ 3. Their successive
quotients correspond in reverse order to the three module factors. Each is supersingular elliptic p-torsion;
hence H is quasi-supersingular.

2.2     Failure of Cartier self-duality
For a ﬁnite Dieudonné module T over perfect k, deﬁne
                                             δ(T ) = dimk (im FT2 ∩ im VT2 ).
The images are k-subspaces because the ﬁeld is perfect. An isomorphism commuting with F, V carries both
images to the corresponding images, so δ is an isomorphism invariant. For M ,
                                   im F 2 = ke2 ,         im V 2 = ke4 ,     δ(M ) = 0.

Since our basis matrices have coeﬃcients in Fp , their dual matrices are V t and F t with the appropriate

                                    im(F D )2 = im(V D )2 = ke∗0 ,         δ(M D ) = 1.
Hence M ̸≃ M D , and compatibility of Dieudonné theory with Cartier duality gives H ̸≃ H D . The transpose
description here uses the prime-ﬁeld coeﬃcients; the displayed semilinear dual formulas are the general
convention.

3    An actual ﬁnite ﬂat lift and all higher ranks
View M as a W -module through W → k and put

                                         L = spank (e0 , e3 , e5 ) ⊂ M.

We verify the ﬁnite Honda hypotheses explicitly. The W -length of M is six and pM = 0. The semilinear
operators satisfy F V = V F = p on M . The restriction of V to L sends its displayed basis to e5 , e2 , e4 and
is injective, since k is perfect. Finally, L complements im F = spank (e1 , e2 , e4 ), so the natural map

                                             L/pL −→ M/ im F

is an isomorphism. These are exactly the ﬁnite Honda conditions of Fontaine–Laﬀaille [1, §9.4], in the
p-torsion formulation of Hoshi [2, Remark 3.5.1]. The exactness identities above also verify Hoshi’s deforma-
bility condition.
    Since p ̸= 2, Hoshi’s anti-equivalence [2, Proposition 3.11(2)] produces a ﬁnite ﬂat commutative W -group
scheme G, killed by p, from this ﬁnite Honda system. Its special ﬁber has module M , by [2, Proposi-
tion 3.11(5)], and is therefore H. Its rank is p6 : ﬁnite ﬂat rank is constant over the local base and equals
the special-ﬁber rank. Cartier duality commutes with base change, so an isomorphism G ≃ G D would imply
H ≃ H D . Thus G also fails self-duality. This is a ﬁnite Honda realization over W ; an integral Dieudonné
lattice for a group over k would not, on its own, establish this mixed-characteristic assertion.
    For n ≥ 3, equip I with the ﬁnite Honda subspace kx and set

                               Mn = M ⊕ I ⊕(n−3) ,         Ln = L ⊕ (kx)⊕(n−3) .

The same Honda conditions hold on each block. They give Gn /W of rank p2n . Its special ﬁber is H ×E[p]n−3
and has a quasi-supersingular ﬁltration by extending the ﬁltration already constructed with the product
elliptic factors. Since F 2 = V 2 = 0 on I and on its dual,

                                        δ(Mn ) = 0,        δ(MnD ) = 1.

Therefore Hn and, by base change, Gn are not Cartier self-dual. This proves Theorem 1.

Scope and veriﬁcation. These examples over Fp disprove the universal assertion for perfect residue
ﬁelds; no descent to every perfect ﬁeld is asserted. The result concerns general ﬁnite group schemes and
does not produce a principally polarized Jacobian or decide the surrounding Coleman conjecture. The proof
is symbolic for every allowed p, n; the companion standard-library veriﬁcation programs check the basis,
operators, invariant, Honda conditions, direct sums, and nontrivial Frobenius twists over ﬁnite ﬁelds. Those
computations corroborate the proof and do not replace the cited classiﬁcation theorems or certify historical
priority.
