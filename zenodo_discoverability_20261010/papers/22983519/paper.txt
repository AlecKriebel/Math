                        Radial orthogonality does not imply
                             dual 1-conformal ﬂatness

                                                 Abstract
         We give a negative answer to Kurose’s Question 3(e) in the 1998 collection Open Problems
     in Aﬃne Diﬀerential Geometry and Related Topics. For the product S 2 (1) × R with its product
     metric and Levi–Civita connection, every radial orthogonal distribution in that question is
     integrable, but the dual statistical structure is nowhere locally 1-conformally ﬂat. Integrability
     follows from the ﬁrst variation of energy; nonﬂatness follows from two curvature evaluations. A
     conformal metric and projective connection change also give an example with nowhere zero cubic
     tensor. The same obstruction works in every dimension at least three. The proof is independent
     of computation. Historical priority is unresolved; no claim of a ﬁrst resolution is made.


1    The question and the counterexample
A statistical manifold (M, ∇, h) consists here of a positive-deﬁnite metric h and a torsion-free
connection ∇ such that ∇h is totally symmetric. The dual connection ∇∗ is determined by
                                Xh(Y, Z) = h(∇X Y, Z) + h(Y, ∇∗X Z).
Fix p ∈ M and a ∇-convex neighborhood U of p. For q ∈ U \ {p}, let γq : [0, 1] → U be the aﬃnely
parametrized ∇-geodesic from p to q. Set
                                 Dqp = {w ∈ Tq M : hq (γ̇q (1), w) = 0}.                                  (1)
Only the velocity line matters, so a regular reparametrization does not change this distribution.
    Question 3(e) of [1, p. 126] asks whether integrability of every distribution (1) implies that
(M, ∇∗ , h) is 1-conformally ﬂat. This is the printed question indexed as AMR-059-0011 (6000011).
It excludes the center p and does not exclude Levi–Civita connections; item 3(a) explicitly treats
those as statistical structures.

Theorem 1. Let M = S 2 (1)×R, h = gS 2 (1) +dt2 , and ∇ = ∇h . Every distribution (1) is integrable,
for every center and every convex neighborhood on which it is deﬁned. Nevertheless (M, ∇∗ , h) is
not 1-conformally ﬂat on any nonempty open subset. The same conclusions hold for S 2 (1) × Rn−2
in every dimension n ≥ 3.

3    A non-self-dual example
The failure is not conﬁned to zero cubic tensor. Let h0 = gS 2 (1) + dt2 , ∇0 = ∇h0 , and deﬁne

                        h1 = et h0 ,      ∇1X Y = ∇0X Y + dt(X)Y + dt(Y )X.                               (4)

This is the standard statistical (−1)-conformal change printed in [3, p. 178, equation (2.1)], with
conformal factor t. It combines a projective change of connection with a conformal metric change.
We use it only to remove the possible restriction to self-dual examples; the deformation itself is
prior work.

Proposition 1. The structure (M, ∇1 , h1 ) has nowhere zero cubic tensor and integrable radial
distributions for every center. Its dual is nowhere locally 1-conformally ﬂat.

Proof. The connection is torsion-free, and direct diﬀerentiation gives

               (∇1X h1 )(Y, Z) = −dt(X)h1 (Y, Z) − dt(Y )h1 (X, Z) − dt(Z)h1 (X, Y ).                     (5)

This is symmetric and has value −3et on (∂t , ∂t , ∂t ). The connections ∇1 and ∇0 have the same
unparametrized geodesics:
                                   ∇1γ̇ γ̇ = ∇0γ̇ γ̇ + 2dt(γ̇)γ̇.
The additional acceleration is tangent to the curve and is removed by a regular reparametriza-
tion. More explicitly, if u is a ∇0 -aﬃne parameter then a ∇1 -aﬃne parameter s satisﬁes ds/du =
Ce2t(γ(u)) with C > 0. This works on each compact geodesic segment. It identiﬁes the unique
connecting paths in the corresponding convex neighborhoods. Since h1 and h0 have the same or-
thogonality relation, their radial distributions agree under this identiﬁcation, so Lemma 1 applies.

Veriﬁcation, attribution, and scope
The obstruction is elementary and the ingredients are classical. This note supplies an explicit
negative answer to the printed Question 3(e), with a short direct veriﬁcation. We do not claim that
the Gauss lemma, the curvature criterion, or the construction in (4) is new.
    Historical priority remains unresolved. The documented, bounded literature search found
no earlier explicit answer, but relevant sources remain uninspected: Kurose’s 1999 aﬃne-realization
paper, Binder–Simon’s 2000 problem list, and the full content of Kurose’s 2016 talk on Gauss-type
lemmas and Hessian structures, among other citation leads. An earlier answer or implicit resolution
in those sources cannot be excluded. Accordingly, this preprint makes no ﬁrst-resolution claim and
does not assert that the question remained open at publication. The live database annotations were
subsequently accessed; their machine-generated report is not evidence establishing priority. The
accompanying dated audits record search coverage and remaining gaps separately from the proof.
    The accompanying standard-library Python checker solves the complete pointwise linear system
(3) in exact rational arithmetic. A separately implemented SymPy checker derives the Christoﬀel
symbols, curvature, transformation law, and cubic and dual tensors from h0 = dθ2 +sin2 θ dϕ2 +dt2 .
These checks supplement the proof; they do not replace its quantiﬁed ﬁrst-variation argument or
constitute a proof-assistant formalization. No dimension-two claim or global foliation across cut
loci is needed.
