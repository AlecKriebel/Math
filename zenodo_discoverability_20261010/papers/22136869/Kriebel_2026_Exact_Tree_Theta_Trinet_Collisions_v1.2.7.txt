                                                 Abstract
         We construct exact distributions shared by a phylogenetic tree and a binary semi-directed
     strict level-two theta network under the Kimura two-parameter (K2P) and three-parameter
     (K3P) substitution models. The compact three-leaf K2P construction lies in the strict stochastic
     interior and is an exact counterexample to the formal K2P lemma and the K2P part of the corre-
     sponding global corollary removed between Versions 2 and 3 of Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, and
     Martin. Because K2P is nested in K3P, this collision alone answers both high-level Kimura tree–
     trinet disjointness questions posed in Version 3 negatively. We additionally give an exact K3P
     theta realization whose network parameter lies outside every globally character-relabelled K2P
     specialization, while openly identifying its shared distribution as globally character-relabelled
     K2P. Full-rank Jacobians show that the ﬁxed theta images are Zariski dense in the eﬀective
     K2P and K3P Fourier spaces. Locally, the collision loci have dimensions 17 and 23 and project
     submersively onto the tree models, with 11- and 14-dimensional ﬁxed-output network ﬁbers.
     Consequently, a relatively open dense set of nearby K3P tree distributions outside every glob-
     ally character-relabelled K2P submodel also has theta realizations. These conclusions persist in
     edgewise strictly continuous-time chambers, where generators and rate ratios may vary by edge
     and no clock or global timing is imposed. Finally, a common-subtree argument replaces any
     chosen internal vertex of any labelled unrooted binary tree by one theta blob while preserving
     the full leaf-pattern distribution; hence exact strict-interior collisions occur for every number of
     leaves. For the compact K2P and quartic K3P witnesses, exact veriﬁers separately reconstruct
     the retained-graph Fourier coordinates and perform ordinary-state Markov pruning in every
     pattern.

Keywords: phylogenetic networks; identiﬁability; Kimura substitution models; group-based mod-
els; algebraic statistics; edgewise continuous-time embeddability.

1    Introduction
Identiﬁability asks whether an exact leaf-pattern distribution determines the underlying phyloge-
netic topology. Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, and Martin establish, on their biologically reasonable
restricted parameter space, full identiﬁability of level-one semi-directed networks, modulo the place-
ment of reticulation vertices within triangles, under JC, K2P, and K3P; on the same parameter
space and under JC, they also prove arbitrary-level tree–network distinguishability except when
the network is a tree possibly augmented with 2-blobs [3]. Version 2 of their paper additionally
claimed arbitrary-level K2P tree–trinet distinguishability and a corresponding global tree–network

                                                      1
result [2]. Version 3 removes the formal arbitrary-level K2P lemma and the K2P part of the cor-
responding global corollary, explains that the proposed induction cannot preserve the leaf ordering
required by its K2P invariant, and poses high-level K2P and K3P trinet distinguishability as open
questions [3].
    Earlier algebraic work established generic identiﬁability for JC large-cycle networks and for
triangle-free level-one networks with a ﬁxed number of reticulations under JC, K2P, and K3P [9, 8].
Ardiyansyah studied algebraic distinguishability for simple and semisimple nice strict level-two
networks under JC, K2P, and K3P, obtaining restricted variety-noncontainment results for four
and more leaves [1]. In that framework no two- or three-leaf simple strict level-two network is
nice (Lemma 5.1), so those results neither address nor rule out the present non-nice theta trinet’s
pointwise stochastic-interior intersection with the tree model. Recent results determine dimen-
sions for broad classes of level-one group-based networks and analyze the three-leaf 3-sunlet, the
minimal level-one network [7, 4]. At level two, generic identiﬁability under JC is known for bi-
nary triangle-free strongly tree-child networks [5]. The theta topology studied here is instead a
strict level-two trinet and admits no tree-child rooting. Our question is full pointwise tree–network
distinguishability on that topology, rather than generic identiﬁability within a tree-child class.
    These questions matter for inference from molecular sequence data because an exact model
collision cannot be resolved by increasing sequence length alone: even the population site-pattern
distribution is compatible with both topologies. Three-taxon marginals are also natural local
summaries of larger networks. Consequently, determining which blob structures are detectable from
trinets sets a limit on reconstruction procedures that rely only on those three-taxon distributions
under the assumed substitution model [9].
    The logical starting point is the nesting K2P ⊂ K3P. The strict-interior K2P collision in Sec-
tion 3 therefore resolves both newly explicit high-level Kimura tree–trinet questions negatively. A
separate sign point and a displayed-child audit give exact witnesses to the leaf-order obstruction
now noted in Version 3. We retain a second, quartic K3P construction because it proves some-
thing diﬀerent and stronger at the parameter level: the same tree distribution has an exact theta
realization containing an edge with three distinct nontrivial eigenvalues, outside every globally
character-relabelled K2P parameter specialization. Its comparison tree distribution itself belongs
to a globally character-relabelled K2P submodel, a distinction made explicit below.
    The collisions are not isolated numerical curiosities. Under K2P, the ﬁxed theta map has
full rank in the nine-dimensional aﬃne K2P Fourier space, while the tree model has dimension
six. Under K3P the corresponding dimensions are ﬁfteen and nine, and the theta map again has
full ambient rank. The resulting collision loci are locally smooth of dimensions 17 and 23 and
ﬁber over nearby tree distributions with ﬁxed-output dimensions 11 and 14. In K3P this local
surjectivity supplies nearby shared distributions outside all three globally character-relabelled K2P
strata. The full-rank minors also make both complexiﬁed theta maps dominant, so no additional
polynomial equality vanishes on an entire ﬁxed-theta image in its eﬀective Fourier ambient space.
These statements imply neither universal distinguishability nor generic indistinguishability: tree
equivalence is nongeneric on the theta topology but occurs along substantial exact families.
    The three-leaf equality is also a reusable one-vertex move. Attaching the same three conditional
Markov subtrees to the tree and theta terminals preserves equality. Thus, on every labelled unrooted
binary tree topology and at every chosen internal vertex, one can replace that vertex by a single
theta blob and obtain an exact collision, including edgewise strictly continuous-time and observably
genuine K3P versions. This does not assert that several theta replacements compose independently.
    The results concern the removed arbitrary-level K2P statements and the open high-level K2P
and K3P trinet questions. They do not test the JC trinet inequality, the level-one K2P sunlet
calculation, the level-one full-identiﬁability theorem, the combinatorial restriction lemma, K3P

2.1    Edgewise continuous-time cones
Throughout, “edgewise strictly continuous-time” means that each edge matrix separately has the
form exp(Qe te ), with te > 0, for a symmetric K2P or K3P generator Qe whose relevant substitution-
rate classes are all positive. The generators, rate ratios, and edge times may vary from edge to
edge. We impose no common rate matrix, molecular clock, compatible node times, or other global
temporal constraint.
    For K3P, symmetric continuous-time rates give the following expressions on each edge, where
for readability λC , λG , λT denote the edge-speciﬁc products λC,e te , λG,e te , λT,e te :

                          aC = e−2(λG +λT ) ,        aG = e−2(λC +λT ) ,        aT = e−2(λC +λG ) .

All three rates are positive exactly when

                                    aC > aG aT ,         aG > aC aT ,           aT > aC aG .                  (4)

11     Scope, implications, and open questions
The level-one K2P 3-sunlet calculation in Lemma 4.1 of [3] is not contradicted by these examples.
The new conclusion is narrower: universal three-leaf tree–trinet distinguishability fails on the strict



                                                  17
level-two theta topology. The theta trinet admits no tree-child rooting. Indeed, both ﬁxed reticu-
lation edges incident with p are directed from p to r2 , r3 , and the same holds at q. In every binary
compatible rooting, p and q therefore already have their two outgoing edges, forcing the remaining
core edges to be directed u → p and u → q. Thus both p and q have only reticulation children.
Consequently, the examples do not contradict results restricted to tree-child or strongly tree-child
networks, including the generic level-two JC result of [5].
    The dominance result should not be confused with generic tree equivalence. Let Y be the
corresponding tree variety in the eﬀective aﬃne Fourier space. It is proper, of codimension three
under K2P and six under K3P, so its preimage under the dominant theta map is a proper Zariski-
closed subset. Thus generic physical theta parameters are not tree-equivalent. At the same time,
the local collision loci have dimensions 17 and 23, and every nearby tree distribution has an 11-
or 14-dimensional local family of network realizations. Full distinguishability fails robustly even
though tree equivalence remains nongeneric in theta parameter space.
    For K3P, the exact quartic point separates parameter symmetry from observable symmetry:
a globally character-relabelled K2P tree distribution has a theta realization using genuinely K3P
edge parameters. The local submersion then moves the observable distribution itself outside all
globally character-relabelled K2P strata. Under both Kimura models, a nontrivial three-way blob
is therefore not universally detectable from exact site-pattern distributions. Theorem 19 gives exact
colliding histories for every n ≥ 3. For each constructed pair, even exact knowledge of its full 4n -
pattern distribution cannot distinguish the tree from the network; increasing the number of taxa
alone therefore does not preclude such collisions.
    No JC collision or analogous proof failure is established here. The work also does not decide
K3P distinguishability for a genuine four-attachment blob, restricted multi-blob topologies, generic
rather than pointwise topology identiﬁability, a vector of several leaf-order invariants, or an equiv-
alence relation generated by independently composable theta moves.


12     Future classiﬁcation problems
  1. Classify the complete K2P and K3P tree–theta intersections and positive chambers.

  2. Determine whether the two mechanisms belong to one symmetry-breaking family.

  3. Determine whether the single-theta replacement composes independently across several blobs.

  4. Decide K3P distinguishability for genuine four-attachment blobs and corrected higher-level
     K2P/K3P identiﬁability under additional hypotheses.

  5. Determine whether JC genuinely excludes theta/tree collisions or requires another topology.

  6. Characterize common-generator, clock, global-time, or edge-sharing restrictions that restore

For the compact K2P and quartic K3P collision witnesses, the suite independently compares
ordinary-state pruning with Fourier inversion in every pattern. It diﬀerentiates the explicitly deﬁned

checks against its manifests. A ﬁeld-coverage inventory distinguishes recomputed checks, informa-
