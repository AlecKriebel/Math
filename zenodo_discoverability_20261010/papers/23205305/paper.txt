    Rational-point undecidability for smooth projective varieties
                     over the rational numbers
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                   ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                          October 6, 2026


                                               Abstract
          Combining OpenAI’s negative solution of rational Hilbert’s tenth problem with Poo-
      nen’s established eﬀective transfer, we obtain undecidability of rational-point existence
      for arbitrary smooth projective geometrically integral varieties over Q. We specify a
      homogeneous-equation input promise and reconstruct the terminating contradiction al-
      gorithm. There is also no computable uniform bound on the height of one rational point
      in terms of the size of the presented equations. The arithmetic input is used with two
      explicit corrections documented here and in the accompanying veriﬁcation supplement.
      This is a geometric consequence of that input; neither the geometric reduction nor the
      base undecidability theorem is claimed as a new independent solution.


1     Inputs and precise scope
Let H10(Q) denote the decision problem with input an integer n ≥ 0 and a polynomial
f ∈ Z[x1 , . . . , xn ], asking whether f (a) = 0 for some a ∈ Qn . The number of variables is
part of the input. Constants, including f = 0, are allowed.
    A projective presentation e consists of an integer N ≥ 0 and a ﬁnite list of homogeneous
integer polynomials F1 , . . . , Fr in x0 , . . . , xN . It speciﬁes
                                         (                                 )
                            Xe = Proj Q[x0 , . . . , xN ]/(F1 , . . . , Fr ) .
Deﬁne SPGI(Q) to ask whether Xe (Q) ̸= ∅, under the promise that Xe is smooth and
geometrically integral. Projectivity is supplied by the presentation. Geometric integrality
includes nonemptiness of the geometric scheme. There is no bound on N , dimension, de-
grees, the number of equations, or coeﬃcient heights. The oracle need not terminate on a
presentation violating its promise.
    The geometric construction and the decision transfer below are due to Poonen [1]. His
Remark 1.2(c) credits a prior Q-case route to R. Robinson, through Smoryński, Logical
Number Theory I, §II.7. We do not claim a new reduction. Family 004 [2] asserts the
arithmetic undecidability input that removes the hypothesis from this established implication.
Section 4 speciﬁes the exact input used, its two proof corrections, and the scope of its
source audit. The main contribution of this note is an explicit consequence with valid oracle
queries and presentation conventions, together with the documented corrections. We make
no assertion of ﬁrst priority for the consequence.


2     Poonen’s eﬀective transfer and the promise
We use the following established input from Poonen’s Theorem 1.3, specialized to Q: given
a projective Q-variety X and an open U ⊆ X, one can algorithmically construct a regular

                                                    1
projective variety Y with a morphism π : Y → X whose image on rational points is exactly
U (Q). The output need not be connected or geometrically integral.
    The eﬀectivity clause matters. In Poonen’s §9, each permissible parameter c gives an
output with πc (Yc (Q)) ⊆ U (Q). An eﬀective cardinality bound on the exceptional parameters
allows a ﬁnite list containing a parameter for which equality holds. Taking the disjoint
union of the outputs gives equality without identifying that good parameter. The boundary
exclusion comes from his Châtelet-surface construction. Resolution preserves the lifts on the
smooth ﬁbers used in the argument. These are parts of the cited eﬀective theorem, not a
claim that naive projective closure preserves rational points.

Lemma 1. If a connected smooth projective Q-scheme C has a rational point, then it is
geometrically integral.

Proof. A regular Noetherian scheme has disjoint irreducible components: at an intersection
its local ring would have more than one minimal prime, whereas a regular local ring is
a domain. Its ﬁnitely many components are therefore open and closed. Connectedness
implies that C is integral. The ﬁnite algebra L = H 0 (C, OC ) is consequently a ﬁeld over
Q. A rational point induces a Q-algebra map L → Q, forcing L = Q. Global functions
commute with extension of the base ﬁeld here. Over Q, each smooth connected projective
component has only constant functions and is integral. Thus the number of these components
is dimQ L = 1. Smoothness also gives geometric reducedness, proving geometric integrality.
This is the Q-projective instance of Poonen’s Lemma 10.1.

    The component decomposition and geometric-integrality tests used below are eﬀective
algebraic operations, as in Poonen’s proof of Theorem 1.1(i). For clarity, for a smooth projec-
tive Y , eﬀective coherent cohomology computes the ﬁnite étale algebra H 0 (Y, OY ). Its ﬁeld
factors give the connected components, and a connected component is geometrically inte-
gral precisely when its global-functions ﬁeld has Q-dimension one. Projective constructions
provide embeddings, and elimination gives homogeneous-equation presentations. Regularity
and smoothness agree for ﬁnite-type schemes over the perfect ﬁeld Q. These conventions
ensure that no invalid input is passed to the oracle.

Theorem 2. An algorithm deciding SPGI(Q) on every valid input would decide H10(Q).
Conversely, decidability of H10(Q) would decide SPGI(Q).

Proof. For the forward implication let D be the promised decision procedure. On input f ,
handle constant polynomials directly. Otherwise form the reduced aﬃne zero scheme U of
f and its projective closure X, recording U as an open in X. If U is empty, return NO.
Apply Poonen’s eﬀective theorem to obtain (Y, π). Compute the connected components
C1 , . . . , Cs of Y and their projective presentations. Test geometric integrality and discard
each component that fails. By Lemma 1, discarded components have no rational points.
Every retained component is smooth, projective and geometrically integral. Query D on
each retained presentation, and return YES if and only if some query answers YES.
     Every preprocessing step terminates. The list of valid queries is ﬁnite, so D’s promise
ensures termination of the whole algorithm. Correctness follows from

            f (Qn ) ∋ 0 ⇐⇒ U (Q) ̸= ∅ ⇐⇒ Y (Q) ̸= ∅ ⇐⇒ ∃i : Ci (Q) ̸= ∅.

The last condition is exactly the disjunction of the retained oracle answers. If there are no
retained components it is false.
    Conversely, on a projective presentation take the N + 1 standard aﬃne charts, setting
xj = 1 on chart j. Their equations have a common rational zero precisely when the sum of
their squares does, since Q is ordered. Call an H10(Q) decision procedure on these ﬁnitely

many polynomials and take the disjunction. Every rational projective point lies in one of
these charts, proving the reverse implication.

    The forward construction yields a ﬁnite nonadaptive disjunctive oracle reduction, and
in particular a Turing reduction. It does not establish a single-output many-one reduction
into the promised geometrically integral class. No quantitative or dimension-preservation
assertion is used.

Corollary 3. No algorithm decides rational-point existence for every valid presentation in
the class SPGI(Q).

Proof. OpenAI’s Theorem 1.1 [2], with the proof corrections recorded in Section 4, gives un-
decidability of H10(Q) with arbitrary input number of variables. Theorem 2 would contradict
it if the stated algorithm existed.


3    An explicit height consequence
Specify a size parameter as follows. For a ≥ 0, let b(a) be its binary representation, with
b(0) = 0, and let ℓ(a) be its length. Use the self-delimiting integer code c(a) = 1ℓ(a) 0b(a).
Encode N , r, then the number of monomials in each polynomial. For every monomial encode
a sign bit, c of the nonzero coeﬃcient magnitude, and c of each of its N + 1 exponents.
Require increasing lexicographic exponent vectors and a common total degree within each
polynomial. The zero polynomial has zero monomials. These conventions specify a uniquely
parsable ﬁnite string e; let s(e) be its bit length. The size refers to this presentation and
embedding, not to the isomorphism class of the abstract variety. The general connection
between bounding the least height of a rational point and rational Hilbert’s tenth problem
is prior work; see Skorobogatov–Sofos [5], Section 1.2.
    For P ∈ PN (Q) choose its primitive integer coordinates (z0 , . . . , zN ) with the ﬁrst nonzero
coordinate positive, and set H(P ) = maxj |zj |.

Corollary 4. There is no total computable function B : N → N such that for every valid e
with Xe (Q) ̸= ∅ there exists P ∈ Xe (Q) satisfying H(P ) ≤ B(s(e)).

Proof. Compute M = B(s(e)). Enumerate all nonzero primitive integer vectors in
[−M, M ]N +1 with ﬁrst nonzero coordinate positive, and evaluate the Fi exactly. Return
YES if any vector satisﬁes every equation, and NO otherwise. The enumeration is ﬁnite. The
hypothesized bound puts a point in the enumeration whenever one exists. This would decide
SPGI(Q), contradicting Corollary 3. For M = 0 the enumeration is empty, consistently with
H(P ) ≥ 1.

    The proof also rules out a presentation-dependent computable bound that terminates
on every valid input. It concerns the height of one point when points exist. It makes no
assertion about a ﬁxed-dimensional family, ﬁnite rational-point sets, or all points on one
variety.


4    Arithmetic input and proof corrections
The external arithmetic theorem used is precisely Theorem 1.1 of [2]: no algorithm decides
the existence of a rational zero of an arbitrary integer polynomial, with the number of vari-
ables part of the input. Its proof is not a geometric argument. A hypothetical rational
solvability algorithm decides each ﬁnite existential polynomial test in an eﬀectively enumer-
ated ring theory. Disequalities are replaced by inverse variables, and ﬁnite systems by sums

of squares. If all tests succeed, compactness gives a term-generated ring in a nonstandard
rational ﬁeld, together with an injective additive elliptic index taking 1 to 1. Pole parity
and a ﬁve-point height estimate bound the distinguished indices by ordinary integers, so
the ring’s distinguished roots are ordinary integers. An elementary diagram used to justify
compactness is not queried by the algorithm. Dovetailing an integer-zero search with the
ﬁnite-test search would decide integral Hilbert’s tenth problem, yielding the contradiction.
The source-body reconstruction and exact dependency checks are included in the veriﬁcation
supplement; ﬁnite computation alone is not this proof.
    The pole-parity application uses only the non-CM, full rational two-torsion branch of the
new pointwise converse [3]. For the curves

with l restricted to the parameters selected by family 004’s descent, that construction gives
dimF2 Sel2 (El /Q) = 3 and full rational two-torsion. Thus, with r = rank El (Q),

                                 r + dimF2 Sha(El /Q)[2] = 1.

The extra Selmer class is realized by a rational point using the converse’s ﬁniteness conclusion
for Sha(El /Q)[2∞ ]: the alternating perfect Cassels–Tate pairing forces even dimension of
Sha[2], yielding r = 1 and Sha[2] = 0. Low Selmer corank alone does not supply that
ﬁniteness. Indeed, the full 2∞ -Selmer corank is at most one, since the divisible corank of
Sha[2∞ ] is at most dimF2 Sha[2]. This is the exact hypothesis of the converse. Each El
is ﬁxed before its internal auxiliary twists are chosen; constants may depend on El . The
internal odd twist discriminant must not be confused with l ≡ 3 (mod 4), whose quadratic
ﬁeld has even discriminant. No CM branch or arbitrary rational-two-torsion case is needed.
    Two technical substitutions are made in the proofs we use:

   1. For the non-CM ﬁve-point height argument of [2], use coeﬃcient primes (5, 2). Pub-
      lished Pan modularity at 5 [4] applies without a residual-image restriction; irreducibil-
      ity, oddness, potential semistability and distinct Hodge–Tate weights are veriﬁed from
      the surface’s Tate module. Prime-independent traces identify the same weight-two
      form at 2, which supplies its conductor at 5. The modular factor and isogeny-height
      deductions then give the required power bound. The standalone height supplement
      details every step and explicitly states its retained geometric inputs. Its ﬁve branch
      values come from the ﬁxed discriminant-210 quotient, not arbitrary ﬁve points.

   2. In the non-CM cyclotomic determinant of [3], reconcile inverse Frobenius with the
      direct Euler convention by the universal group-ring correction below. It is integral
      before character evaluation, has central value one, and introduces no factor of two per
      auxiliary prime. The real-place Tate error, smoothing denominators and ﬁxed-base
      clearing are still bounded independently of the number of auxiliary primes.

The supplied component reconstructions and integration audit check their joint use: actual
Selmer-cone maps and homotopies, outer specializations, weighted theta traces, the nonvan-
ishing estimates, and bounded central clearing all refer to the same ﬁnite arithmetic construc-
tions. Their precise scopes and primary-source hypotheses are recorded in the supplement.
No applicable complete Lean formalization of this deduction is claimed.

5    Boundaries, attribution and veriﬁcation
Naive compactiﬁcation cannot replace the eﬀective transfer: Spec Q[x, y]/(x2 + 1) has no
rational point, whereas its closure V (X 2 +Z 2 ) ⊆ P2 contains [0 : 1 : 0]. Resolution alone need
not preserve point existence in the other direction: V (x2 + y 2 ) has a rational singular point
but its normalization is A1Q(i) , which has no rational point. A morphism from a resolution
cannot create a rational point when its target has none. The issue is losing singular points
or retaining boundary points introduced by compactiﬁcation.
    No conclusion for curves, surfaces, ﬁxed dimension, Fano varieties, general type, or a
ﬁnite-point promise follows automatically. This note asserts neither a Turing degree nor
quantitative bounds beyond the stated oracle implication. The height corollary is an elemen-
tary computability consequence and adds no independent arithmetic input.
    The geometric implication was already established by Poonen, with a prior rational-ﬁeld
route credited in his Remark 1.2(c). The arithmetic breakthrough is attributed to OpenAI’s
manuscript-speciﬁc citation. The consequence and its elementary height corollary are not
advertised as independent solutions of rational Hilbert’s tenth problem or as a new geometric
reduction. The accompanying priority audit records public source dates separately from
manuscript dates and search limitations.
