We use the p = 5 specialization of Pan’s Theorem 1.0.4 [2] (author version, printed p. 5). A
continuous irreducible two-dimensional Qp representation of GQ , ramiﬁed at only ﬁnitely many
places, odd, potentially semistable at p, and having distinct Hodge–Tate weights, arises from a
cuspidal eigenform up to twist. There is no residual-image assumption for p ≥ 5. The extra
exclusion in Pan’s statement concerns only p = 3, namely local residual semisimpliﬁcation of the
form η ⊕ ηω.
     Each hypothesis holds for r5 : Faltings’ theorems [3] give semisimplicity and EndGL′ (V5 (A)) =
B ⊗ Q5 . Using (4), the commutant of r5 |GL′ after scalar extension is the scalars; semisimplicity
therefore implies absolute irreducibility, also for r5 . At 5, A acquires semistable reduction over
a further ﬁnite local extension of L′ . Its Tate module is then semistable, as are its constituents.
Hence r5 is potentially semistable. The Hodge–Tate comparison and ﬁnite-extension descent of
the de Rham property [4] give cyclotomic exponents 0, 1 for r5 , each once (cyclotomic Hodge–Tate
weight −1).
     For complex conjugation j, a scalar r5 (j) would make R5 (j) scalar. Betti comparison identiﬁes
µj̄ ◦ j with conjugation followed by a holomorphic quasi-isogeny. It exchanges the two nonzero
Hodge subspaces, contradicting scalar action. Since j 2 = 1, r5 (j) has eigenvalues 1, −1 and is
odd. Outside T ∪ {5}, inertia ﬁxes L, good reduction kills its Tate action, and β is trivial; thus
ramiﬁcation is ﬁnite.
