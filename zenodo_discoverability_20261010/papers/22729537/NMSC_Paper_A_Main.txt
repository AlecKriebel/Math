                                             Abstract
       We determine the exact population-level information in multilocus sequence alignments gen-
   erated by a temporal species network under the independent-lineage Network Multispecies Coa-
   lescent (NMSC), without treating estimated gene trees or concordance factors as observed data.
   With known nonrecombining locus boundaries, contemporaneous labelled sampling, and one
   shared irreducible stationary reversible ﬁnite-state continuous-time Markov chain, the indexed
   family of length-L locus laws is equivalent to the stationary distribution, the trace-normalized
   substitution generator, and the mutation-scaled metric-genealogy law. The ﬁrst step is a com-
   pact moment theorem for the random conditional site-pattern tensor. A determinant coordinate
   then recovers each realized additive gene-tree metric, while a continuum in the ancestral coa-
   lescence tail identiﬁes the shared generator, including JC, K2P, K3P, and repeated-eigenvalue
   GTR strata.
       We compile every ﬁnite temporal NMSC presentation into a ﬁnite typed continuous-time
   response system on partition–occupancy states. Its canonical reachable–observable residual be-
   havior, denoted CCR, is coordinate independent, removes analytically invisible event times, and
   is unique up to typed linear isomorphism. Equality of CCR is equivalent to equality of normal-
   ized metric-genealogy laws. The minimal time-indexed realization has an intrinsic positive cone,
   but its ﬁnite analytic encoding does not supply a stochastic occupancy basis or an NMSC graph.
   Literal graph recovery is impossible on the strict physical domain: arbitrary sample-unary tem-
   poral funnels have identical response in every context. Species trees are nevertheless recovered
   pointwise with all exposed population rates. For one-reticulation presentations we give an ex-
   act ﬁnite port-response quotient and a graph theorem on the incidence-separated subclass; the
   reduced level-1 result additionally assumes scalar-separator faithfulness, and no unconditional
   level-1 or level-2 graph theorem is claimed.
       The ﬁnite-locus hierarchy has two complementary conclusions. On every ﬁxed ﬁnite union of
   compact semianalytic ﬁnite-presentation charts, one ﬁnite locus length determines the complete
   indexed family. The proof uses the Frisch–Siu Noetherian ring of analytic germs along a com-
   pact set and remains valid at coalescent–substitution resonances. No determining length can
   depend only on the number of sampled taxa, reticulations, alphabet size, and sampling design
   if demographic epoch count is unbounded. For every L we construct two strict two-taxon, zero-
   reticulation, known-JC demographic histories with identical laws through length L and diﬀerent
   length-(L + 1) laws. A Vandermonde Jacobian and the implicit-function theorem make the
   collision exact. We ﬁnally separate Borel inversion, ﬁnite proposed-model equivalence testing,
   moment approximation, and statistical consistency.

1     Introduction
Reticulate species histories are latent twice over in sequence data. The species network ﬁrst gen-
erates a random metric gene genealogy through lineage transport, coalescence, and reticulation
routing; the genealogy then generates an alignment through a substitution process. The observable
object is therefore the direct alignment law after both layers have been integrated out. Replacing
this law by estimated gene trees changes the statistical experiment and cannot establish direct-
sequence identiﬁability.
    Previous direct-sequence results establish generic unrooted species-tree recovery under broad
reversible substitution models [1, 8], and logDet distances recover substantial level-1 network topol-
ogy under an NMSC–GTR model [2]. At the gene-tree-summary layer, quartet distributions recover
the tree of blobs and portions of level-1 or galled tree-child structure [3–5]. Full-likelihood intro-
gression models also possess exact label-switching ambiguities, notably bidirectional introgression
[16]. None of these facts by itself classiﬁes the complete family of within-locus sequence laws.
    Earlier work distinguishes displayed-tree sets from NMSC distributions and shows that distin-
guishability depends on sampling. Some networks remain indistinguishable even when gene-tree
branch lengths are included [17]. Our sample-unary-funnel theorem proves contextual replacement
for the complete metric-genealogy law under the sampling convention stated here. Metric-genealogy
ambiguity itself is not new.
    The present paper separates four questions that are often conﬂated:
 1. What mixing measure is determined by all within-locus laws?
 2. Can one invert a conditional sequence tensor to a realized metric genealogy and a shared
    substitution generator?
 3. What ﬁnite object classiﬁes the NMSC metric-genealogy behavior when literal network realiza-
    tion is nonunique?
 4. Does one ﬁnite locus length suﬃce, and what complexity must its bound depend on?
    The answer to the ﬁrst two questions is a universal sequence–genealogy equivalence. The answer
to the third is a ﬁnite typed linear behavior, CCR. The fourth has a positive compact ﬁnite-
presentation theorem and a matching negative theorem showing that unbounded demographic
epoch count cannot be omitted from any universal complexity bound.

1.1   Contributions and deliberate limits
The load-bearing positive theorem is pointwise, not merely generic. It covers any ﬁnite labelled
temporal NMSC presentation in the strict domain and any irreducible stationary reversible gener-
ator. Repeated substitution eigenvalues are handled by matrix-semigroup arguments rather than
spectral-coordinate division. The response theorem allows rectangular boundary maps and chrono-
logically constrained timed words. The ﬁnite-order theorem is pointwise across resonances because
the sequence map is treated by matrix-exponential analyticity, not by rational expressions that
divide by rate diﬀerences.
    Two limits are equally important. First, CCR is a canonical behavior rather than a canoni-
cal phylogenetic graph. Second, compact moment determinacy produces a mathematical inverse
from the complete indexed hierarchy; it is not a ﬁnite-query algorithm. We prove graph recov-
ery completely for sampled-complete species trees. The companion studies the strongly tree-child
level-at-most-two graph inverse; none of its results is used here. Unknown stationary nonreversible
generator recovery remains outside the theorem.

2     Model and observation objects
2.1   Temporal species-network presentations
Let X be a ﬁnite labelled taxon set, let S be a ﬁnite set of labelled sampled lineages, and ﬁx
a known surjection s : S → X. A presentation on X has exactly the taxa in X as its labelled
terminal populations; thus every graph leaf is sampled, possibly by several lineages. This sampled-
complete convention is part of the literal graph object. Present time is 0. A ﬁnite temporal NMSC
presentation consists of a ﬁnite rooted directed acyclic population graph with a time coordinate
increasing backward in time, a ﬁnite event list

                                   0 = t0 < t1 < · · · < tJ < ∞,

a ﬁnite set of extant population arcs in each open epoch (tj , tj+1 ), a positive ﬁnite pairwise co-
alescence rate on every such arc, and an ancestral root population extending from tJ to inﬁnity
with positive ﬁnite rate. Tree boundaries transport lineages deterministically. At a binary reticu-
lation, each lineage independently selects one of two parents with probability γ ∈ (0, 1). All ﬁnite
epoch durations are positive. Additional degree-two vertices are allowed only when they record a
demographic rate change; their number is part of presentation complexity.
    This formulation encompasses binary species networks, reticulation stacks, parallel temporal
arcs, and ﬁnite piecewise-constant demography. Parameter-free degree-two subdivisions are presen-
tations of the same process. A population subnetwork through which at most one sampled ancestral
lineage can ever pass is not silently deleted; its exact invisibility is proved in section 11.

2.2   Genealogy law
Backward in time, current gene lineages move deterministically at tree boundaries, route indepen-
dently at reticulations, and coalesce pairwise on a common population arc according to Kingman’s
rate convention [12]. Loci are independent. Every site in one nonrecombining locus shares the same
realized rooted metric genealogy G. Let

graph interpretation, are convention speciﬁc.

Proof. The sequence–genealogy bridge uses the same common positive-rate ancestral population

17     Discussion
The positive scientiﬁc object is not an estimated gene-tree distribution. The complete indexed
alignment law ﬁrst recovers a compact mixing measure of conditional phylogenetic tensors. Deter-
minants and a shared semigroup then recover the normalized metric-genealogy law. Only after this
direct bridge is established do we ask what species history generated that law.

                                                 34
    The universal inverse problem does not have a graph-valued answer. CCR is the ﬁnite canonical
observable behavior. This is stronger than merely naming equality of distributions: it provides ﬁnite
typed state spaces, analytic ﬂows, marks, boundaries, an intrinsic breakpoint set, minimality, and a
ﬁnite proposed-model equality criterion. It is weaker than a canonical positive network realization,
and the distinction is unavoidable.
    The ﬁnite-locus result is also genuinely two-sided. A ﬁxed compact ﬁnite presentation is ﬁnitely
separated because its analytic coordinate ideal along the compact parameter-pair set is Noetherian.
Yet a bound that ignores demographic complexity is impossible even for two taxa and known JC.
The collision theorem exposes why: ﬁnite locus length samples only ﬁnitely many Laplace moments,
while each additional demographic epoch supplies another independent moment direction.
    Further graph interpretation can compare two positive raw presentations through their common
response, without requiring a positive realization theorem for every abstract CCR. The companion
manuscript investigates this problem for the strongly tree-child level-at-most-two class. A separate
substitution extension is recovery of an unknown stationary nonreversible generator from higher-
order conditional tensors.
