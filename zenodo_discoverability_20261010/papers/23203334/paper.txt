                                                    Abstract
        We extend the signed-impulse proof of the one-species three-dimensional relativistic
     Vlasov–Maxwell theorem in OpenAI’s family 362 manuscript to a ﬁxed ﬁnite collection of
     particle species. Mass normalization gives a common velocity law and constant species-
     dependent accelerations. The entire source–receiver cancellation has coeﬃcient ea eb /ma ;
     the source acceleration cancels before its sign is discarded. A positive mass-weighted
     energy controls a disjoint union of species label spaces, and the angular occupation
     and selected receiver coeﬃcient use one simultaneous momentum bootstrap. We prove
     the resulting global classical theorem for nonnegative smooth compact phase data and
     compatible Cb∞ ∩ L2 Maxwell ﬁelds, without smallness, symmetry or neutrality. The
     universal geometric identity is inherited; the new estimates keep all source and receiver
     coeﬃcients explicit and close the shared-ﬁeld bootstrap.

If a receiver has ea = 0, its momentum is constant and all pair factors vanish. A source with
eb = 0 has free transport and no Maxwell contribution; its positive energy may be retained
harmlessly. Identical mass/charge species can be combined by adding number densities. For
a single species with m = e = 1 every displayed formula reduces to [1]. For two unit masses
and charges +1, −1, same-sign pair coeﬃcients are +1 and cross-sign coeﬃcients −1; source
accelerations are signed before exact cancellation. An extreme ﬁxed mass ratio only enlarges
m−1b , |λb | and |cab | and the normalized initial norms. There is no uniform estimate as a mass
approaches zero. Neutrality is used nowhere in the positive energy, kernels, occupation or
localization proof.

vacuum regularity needed by this proof. Arbitrary smooth ﬁnite energy ﬁelds without those
derivative bounds are not covered. Compact particle phase support is a hypothesis of the
analytic chain. The note does not infer control of support from energy alone, or a scalar
