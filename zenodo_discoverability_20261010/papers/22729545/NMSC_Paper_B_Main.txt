                                                 Abstract
         We classify the observable rooted graph and parameters of ﬁnite strict temporally separated
     binary strongly tree-child species networks of level at most two under the independent-lineage
     Network Multispecies Coalescent (NMSC), with a ﬁxed known surjective taxon sampling map.
     Equality of complete normalized rooted metric-genealogy laws is equivalent, pointwise on the
     full positive domain, to an explicit quotient generated by eight move types.
         The proof recovers current pair co-occupancy from the analytic germ of a ﬁnite positive
     Kingman mixture. Signed jumps reveal every tree event and every pair-exposed reticulation.
     Descendant clusters, chronology, tree-childness and level-two shortcut completion then recon-
     struct the retained graph, including arbitrary decorations. Deterministic witnesses and actual
     three-lineage merger densities recover oriented inheritance weights and the entire ﬁnite demo-
     graphic rate words on exposed populations. Scalar propagation through ancestral reticulations
     links both incoming arms, including equal rates and inheritance one-half. The indexed collection
     of all genealogy marginals on at most three sampled lineages suﬃces for the complete quotient.
         A reticulation with one descendant sampled lineage retains its two typed ﬁrst-exposure out-
     lets and route weights, while its internal clock and singly occupied demographic words are
     unobservable. Sister-transfer direction is invisible exactly when both sides are sample unary;
     the corresponding reversal transfers the whole exposed donor rate word. Together with presen-
     tation suppression, equal-rate composition, unary-corridor replacement, parent exchange and
     labelled isomorphism, these responses exhaust the quotient. Exact hidden-clock and rank-one
     nonbridge examples explain essential obstructions. A separate graph argument derives the ﬁve
     cores and twelve minimum strong repairs.
         The companion study transfers the classiﬁcation to all indexed ordered locus laws with known
     boundaries, jointly with the stationary distribution and trace-normalized shared irreducible sta-
     tionary reversible substitution generator. The reconstruction uses observable current-grouping
     scalars throughout.

2.1   Taxa and sampled lineages
Let X be a ﬁnite labelled taxon set. Let S be a ﬁnite labelled set of sampled gene lineages and

                                             s : S −→ X

a known surjection. For x ∈ X, write mx = |s−1 (x)| ≥ 1. The species network has one terminal

2.2   Raw population networks
A raw presentation N is a ﬁnite rooted binary directed acyclic graph. The root has indegree zero
and outdegree two; a tree vertex has indegree one and outdegree two; a reticulation has indegree
two and outdegree one; and terminal population leaves are indexed by X. Reticulation parents
are distinct in the raw biological presentation. Parallel parent arcs and directed two-cycles are
excluded there. Parallel topological segments may appear only after unported degree-two vertices
are suppressed in a generator core; every full strong repair separates the corresponding raw parents.
Vertices carry temporal coordinates increasing backward in time, and every population interval has
positive duration.
    Every population interval has a positive ﬁnite Kingman pair rate, constant between successive
demographic or biological vertices. Each population therefore has a ﬁnite piecewise-constant rate
word. A demographic rate-change vertex is permitted on an otherwise unchanged population
lineage. Every reticulation has inheritance probability γ ∈ (0, 1), and each extant lineage block
routes independently. Distinct internal biological events (tree joins and reticulations, including the
root join) have distinct absolute ages in the principal theorem. All terminal samples occur at time
zero. Q2 concerns only presentational factorizations of one ﬁxed event operator; it does not admit
simultaneous distinct biological events.
    Above the root, all surviving blocks enter one ancestral population that continues indeﬁnitely
backward in time. It has a ﬁnite demographic rate word followed by a constant positive pair
rate. Thus all sampled blocks ultimately coalesce almost surely, and the metric-genealogy law is a
probability law on complete rooted genealogies. The ﬁnite-duration requirement applies to ﬁnite
intervals between presentation boundaries; the ﬁnal ancestral epoch is unbounded.
    The adjective strongly tree-child is used in the same precise sense as in the companion level-2
topology trilogy. Form the standard semi-directed reduction of the rooted presentation by suppress-
ing the root in the usual way, forgetting the directions of ordinary tree edges, and retaining every
reticulation arrowhead. The presentation is strongly tree-child when this mixed topology is tree-
child under every admissible rooting. In the present binary standard setting this is equivalent to
the local condition that every tail of a retained reticulation arrow has two incident ordinary edges.
In particular the supplied temporal rooting is tree-child: every internal tree vertex has a tree-vertex
or terminal-population child, and every reticulation has a tree-vertex or terminal-population child.
This is strictly stronger than merely requiring the supplied rooting to be tree-child and is the con-
dition for which the ﬁve-core/twelve-repair theorem below is valid. The level is at most two: each
undirected blob contains at most two reticulations.

deterministic normalization R. It applies:
 1. composition of presentational factors of one ﬁxed boundary operator (Q2), a vacuous step on

 2. suppression of parameter-free degree-two vertices;
 3. merger of adjacent equal-rate demographic epochs on an unchanged population lineage;

chain passes through an unstated larger simultaneous-event model class. Any extension to such a
class would require a separate theorem.

3    Observed cells, sampling consistency, and current co-occupancy
An observed marked cell speciﬁes an ordered list of labelled mergers, open intervals for their times,
and no-other-selected-merger conditions. It never speciﬁes population occupancy or a reticulation
route. Every such cell has a ﬁnite sum of nonnegative hidden-history densities. Strict positive
rates, durations, and routing weights imply that its density is positive exactly when at least one
compatible hidden history exists.

Lemma 3.1 (Positive support). Every feasible ﬁnite no-merger routing history has positive proba-
bility, and every feasible marked merger history has positive density on its open time simplex.

Proof. A hidden-history density is a product of positive routing weights, merger rates, and expo-
nential no-merger factors. Infeasible histories contribute zero. The ﬁnite sum cannot cancel.

Theorem 3.2 (NMSC sampling consistency). Restricting a metric genealogy to any selected subset
T ⊆ S and suppressing degree-two genealogy vertices gives the NMSC law on the same population
network started with just those labels.

Proof. The Kingman pair-clock construction restricts consistently to blocks meeting T . A merger
with a deleted block changes neither the retained partition nor its population. At a reticulation
the retained blocks retain independent Bernoulli routes with the same parameter; summing deleted
routes gives one. Deterministic transport commutes with restriction. Apply these facts along the
ﬁnite event list and the ﬁnal ancestral epoch.

Corollary 3.3 (Subset routing identity). If m blocks reach a reticulation, restriction to r selected
blocks gives the 2r assignment weights γ |A| (1 − γ)r−|A| , independently of m.

4    The retained graph from signed pair jumps
For a vertex v, let C(v) ⊆ S be its complete sampled descendant set. Suppress parameter-free
and demographic markers for the present graph argument. Temporarily contract each reticulation
with |C(v)| = 1 into its terminal leaf. The resulting retained graph N ∗ has ordinary tree vertices,
exposed reticulations, and taxon leaves; a contracted reticulate leaf has indegree two. Its hidden
reticulation time is absent. We ﬁrst reconstruct this graph globally and then recover its numerical
parameters.
    The distinct-absolute-age assumption in section 2 applies here: two biological events cannot
contribute opposite jumps at the same time. Possible simultaneous-event cancellation is outside
this theorem. Equality of positive rates at diﬀerent populations remains fully allowed.

Proof. A tree boundary combines its two child populations. Its newly co-occupied pair mass is

Lemma 4.2 (Tree-child clusters give the reachability order). The signed supports recover C(v)
for every retained internal vertex. Together with the known taxon leaves and retained times, these
clusters determine the entire ancestry poset of N ∗ .

Proof. The graph fact that incomparable tree-child vertices have nonnested clusters is established
in Hellmuth et al. [1, Lemma 33 and Corollary 13]. We include its short witness proof and then
apply the observed jump supports. From every vertex u, tree-childness supplies a path to a taxon
leaf all of whose vertices strictly after u have indegree one. Call a sampled label there a tree-only
witness. A vertex incomparable with u cannot reach that label: a path to it must enter this indegree-
one suﬃx through u, or start on the suﬃx, and either alternative makes the vertices comparable.
Incomparable vertices therefore have nonnested descendant clusters.
    Every tree vertex has at least two descendant taxa. Its ordinary-child tree-only witness cannot
be reached through its other child: tracing the indegree-one suﬃx backward would force that path
to return through the parent and create a directed cycle. Each child has a descendant label, so the
two child sets have union of size at least two. Every label in their union occurs as an endpoint of
the cross support Jv , including labels that belong to both child sets. Thus C(v) is the union of the
endpoints of Jv , for tree vertices as well as for exposed-reticulation cliques.
    A reticulation with just one descendant sampled label cannot have a tree child, since that child
has at least two descendant taxa. Its child is an ordinary terminal with one sample. These and
only these reticulations have been contracted in N ∗ ; every other internal vertex is observed.
    Add each taxon leaf with its known cluster s−1 (x) and time zero. Proper cluster inclusion
forces ancestry, because incomparable vertices have nonnested clusters, and ancestry in the opposite
direction would contradict strict inclusion. Equal-cluster vertices are comparable, with the older
observed time above the younger; a contracted singleton leaf is below every retained ancestor. These
rules recover all comparabilities.

12     Scope, novelty, and nonclaims
The graph inverse uses no Fourier coordinate, group-based invariant, or site-pattern rank. It makes
no appeal to triangle varieties from direct substitution models. It depends only on the normalized
genealogy law and is therefore uniform within the companion study’s stated reversible substitution
class. The cycle/theta core list and its twelve minimum repairs are independent graph results.
Decorated reconstruction uses the global signed-jump cluster poset for every ﬁnite expansion.
    The full-likelihood literature documents bidirectional-introgression label switching and the abil-
ity of multiple sampling to help identify sister introgression direction [3, 4]. Simultaneous-BDI
switches are not imported into this temporally separated quotient. The result here is the pointwise
graph-and-parameter classiﬁcation, including the exact exposure criterion, typed unary-fan ﬁber,
whole demographic words, and the stated ﬁnite labelled-marginal bound.
    No claim is made for level three, unknown nonreversible generators, unknown locus bound-
aries, within-locus recombination, arbitrary rate mixtures, practical ﬁnite-sample reconstruction,
or empirical adequacy.
