                                                   Abstract
           This supplement supplies detailed probability and graph arguments for the pointwise raw
       strong-tree-child level-at-most-two NMSC quotient. The proof uses sampling consistency, scalar
       co-occupancy extracted from frozen local genealogy germs, signed biological-event jumps, chrono-
       logical descendant clusters, and explicit two- and three-lineage numerical formulas. It also pre-
       serves the exact hidden-clock and rank-one counterexamples, derives the ﬁve-core/twelve-repair
       catalog, and distinguishes universal proofs from bounded exact veriﬁcation. All times and rates
       are normalized as in the main paper. Rates are positive and constant between demographic
       vertices, ﬁnite durations are positive, inheritance weights are interior, and distinct internal bi-
       ological events have distinct absolute ages. The known taxon sampling map is surjective. No
       population occupancy or historical route tag is observed.


1     Sampled-lineage convention and genealogy cells
Let X be the terminal taxon set, S the sampled-lineage set, and s : S → X the known sampling
map. At time zero there are mx = |s−1 (x)| labelled blocks in the terminal population x. A metric
genealogy has leaves S.
    The rooted presentations in this supplement satisfy the strong semi-directed condition used by
the ﬁve-core theorem: after standard semi-directed reduction, every tail of a retained reticulation
arrow has two incident ordinary edges. This is stronger than requiring only the supplied rooting
to be tree-child.
    As in the main paper, surviving blocks enter a single ancestral population above the root, with
a ﬁnite rate word followed by a constant positive pair rate on the ﬁnal unbounded epoch. Complete
coalescence therefore occurs almost surely.
    For T ⊆ S, a marked T -cell is speciﬁed by:
    1. an ordered list of mergers of blocks of the partition of T ;
    2. an open simplex of allowed merger times lying inside temporal chambers;
    3. the condition that no other merger among the retained T -blocks occurs between listed events.
No route or population occupancy is included in the observed cell.

2    Sampling consistency in a structured coalescent
The proof of the multilineage theorem uses a pathwise restriction construction rather than an
informal appeal to exchangeability.
Lemma 2.1 (Kingman restriction on one population). Run a Kingman coalescent with pair rate
λ(t) on a ﬁnite set of labelled blocks and restrict its partition to a subset T of initial labels. After
deleting mergers involving only deleted labels and suppressing repeated restricted partitions, the
restricted process is a Kingman coalescent on T with the same pair rate.
Proof. Use independent exponential pair clocks on each constant-rate interval. For any two current
restricted blocks, the clock for their current full ancestral blocks has rate λ. Clocks involving
a deleted-only ancestral block do not change the restricted partition. The memoryless property
restarts the same clock system after each event. Piecewise rates follow by concatenation.

Theorem 2.2 (NMSC sampling consistency). Restricting an NMSC metric genealogy on S to
T ⊆ S gives exactly the NMSC law obtained from the same temporal network with only the initial
blocks in T .
Proof. Apply lemma 2.1 on each population epoch. At a tree boundary, deterministic transport
plainly commutes with restriction. At a reticulation, retain the independent Bernoulli route of
each selected block and sum over routes of deleted blocks; their total probability is one. Induction
through the ﬁnite temporal event list proves equality of the restricted partition-valued process,
including its merger times and labels.

4    Signed biological jumps and the graph
At a tree vertex of age h with child sample clusters A, B,

                  ∆qij (h) = Pr(Tij > h, (i ∈ eA , j ∈ eB ) or (i ∈ eB , j ∈ eA )) ≥ 0.

Its strict-positive support is the cross-pair graph A × B, with repeated labels excluded. At a
reticulation v with child population e, age h and inheritance γ,

                          ∆qij (h) = −2γ(1 − γ) Pr(Tij > h, i, j ∈ e) ≤ 0.

The strict-negative support is the full pair clique of the descendant sample cluster C(v). The incom-
ing reticulation arcs are new at h, so no exterior lineage is already in either of them immediately
above the boundary. No merger has an atom at a ﬁxed biological time. Demographic changes do
not move a lineage and hence do not jump q.
    Every permitted ﬁnite routing choice has positive probability, and ﬁnite no-merger survival is
positive. Thus the displayed support equalities hold pointwise, including equal rates and inheritance
one-half, even when A, B overlap. Temporal separation prevents cancellation of distinct biological
events.

Lemma 4.1 (Tree-only witnesses). Every vertex of a rooted tree-child network has a descendant
taxon reached by a path whose vertices after the start have indegree one. Incomparable vertices have
mutually noncontained descendant clusters. Every tree vertex has at least two descendant taxa.

Proof. Repeatedly choose an ordinary child. The ﬁnite acyclic graph eventually reaches a leaf. An
alternative path to that leaf must enter the indegree-one suﬃx through its start, or start on the
suﬃx. If incomparable vertices shared one of these witness leaves, a root-to-leaf path would contain
both and make them comparable. For a tree vertex, its ordinary-child witness cannot be reached
through the other child: entering the same suﬃx would give a directed cycle. A descendant of the
other child is therefore a diﬀerent taxon.

    Consequently every tree vertex is visible and its cluster is the union of the endpoints of its
positive jump graph. Every reticulation with at least two sampled descendants is visible with its
full cluster. A reticulation with only one descendant sampled label can have neither a tree child
(which has two descendant taxa) nor a reticulation child. After degree-two suppression its child is
a singly sampled taxon leaf. Contract this reticulation into that leaf, retaining the two incoming
parent incidences. Call the graph so obtained H; multiply sampled taxon leaves remain single
graph vertices with their known sample clusters.
    For retained internal vertices, ancestry is exactly cluster containment together with decreasing
forward age: u precedes v if and only if C(v) ⊆ C(u) and τ (u) > τ (v). Containment forces
comparability by lemma 4.1; equal clusters are ordered by their distinct observed times. Leaf
incidences follow from the known taxon sample clusters. Thus the signed event book determines
the complete reachability poset and its transitive reduction.

Lemma 4.2 (Reticulation clusters at level two). Distinct reticulations have distinct clusters. In-
comparable reticulations in a level-at-most-two network have disjoint clusters.

Proof. For comparable reticulations r preceding r′ , a tree-only witness below r cannot be entered
from r′ without revisiting r; it belongs to C(r) \ C(r′ ). Incomparability precludes equal clusters by
lemma 4.1.

    If incomparable reticulations had a common descendant, choose root paths with last common
vertex b and descendant paths with ﬁrst common vertex c. Preﬁx interiors are disjoint, as are
suﬃx interiors. An intersection between one preﬁx and the opposite suﬃx would make the two
reticulations comparable. The four paths therefore form a simple undirected cycle through the two
reticulations and c. The two distinct incoming arcs at c make it a third reticulation on that same
cycle. A cycle lies in one blob, contradicting level at most two. This argument uses no observed
blob assignment and permits arbitrary nested decorations.

Lemma 4.3 (Unique shortcut completion). The reachability poset and signed jump supports de-
termine H, except for the topological reversal of a pendant bi-unary sister triangle.

Proof. An arc into an indegree-one vertex is a cover: any alternate path must use the same last
arc and return to its source. Reticulations have one outgoing cover. A tree vertex retains its
ordinary-child cover and loses at most one outgoing shortcut in the transitive reduction. A missing
arc targets a retained reticulation or a contracted singleton leaf. The other parent is lower, and its
incoming arc is a cover.
    A deﬁcient tail v has one ordinary cover child w, with C = C(v) = C(w), and a shortcut head
with nonempty proper cluster D ⊂ C. The positive jump graph at v is the complete graph on C
with the clique on C \ D removed. If |C \ D| ≥ 2, its universal vertices identify D. If the graph
is complete, then D = C \ {a}. For |C| ≥ 3, two distinct candidate heads would both be retained
reticulations in the same observed poset, with overlapping equal-size clusters. This contradicts
lemma 4.2. The head is unique.
    For |C| = 2 the two samples belong to diﬀerent singly sampled taxa. All tree vertices with that
cluster form a chain. Every nonbottom same-cluster tree vertex has a singleton-reticulation shortcut
child; tree-childness therefore forces its sole cover child to be ordinary. An exposed reticulation
may occur above the whole chain, but cannot interrupt it. The bottom tree vertex has the two
terminal heads after unary-reticulation contraction. Two upper shortcut tails cannot target one
head without giving indegree three, or target diﬀerent heads without giving the bottom tree vertex
two reticulation children. Thus at most one upper deﬁcient tail exists. In that ambiguous case
exactly one terminal head is reticulate, with parents the upper and bottom tree vertices; the other
terminal is ordinary. Neither admits an additional outside parent. The subgraph is a pendant sister
triangle, possibly the whole graph when its top is the root. The two head choices give exactly its
bi-unary Q6 reversal. If there is no upper deﬁcient tail, only absence of shortcut ambiguity follows:
a terminal can still have an outside parent, whose incidence is already a cover. This distinction is
essential.

   These arguments recover all decorated attachment positions directly as vertices, clusters and
times. They neither infer an arbitrary larger graph from deleted-port catalogs nor glue unrelated
choices from separate marginal carriers.

6    The quotient soundness and exact obstructions
Lemma 6.1 (Contextual unary-fan response). On the theorem class a nontrivial unexposed retic-
ulation has one singly sampled terminal descendant. Its maximal pre-exposure fan consists of the
unary path into that reticulation and its two incoming arms, stopped at their two parent tree vertices.
Its complete contextual response retains exactly the two typed parent outlets with weights (γ, 1 − γ);
the internal age and all singly occupied rate words are absent.

Proof. The singleton-descendant assertion follows from lemma 4.1 and tree-childness. Both parent
tree vertices have ordinary-side sampled witnesses, so every route ﬁrst becomes capable of co-
occupancy at its parent vertex. Until then there is one sampled block and hence no Kingman
merger or survival loss. Summing its routes gives precisely the stated two weighted exits. Cutting
a complete marked genealogy at those exits proves equality in every ﬁxed surrounding context.
Replacing one valid strict realization by another with the same response is Q8; a one-outlet unary
corridor gives Q4 by the same argument with total weight one. No observable operator selecting
the past route is needed.

Proof. Forget ordinary directions in one blob while retaining its distinguished incoming boundary.
The resulting connected multigraph is biconnected and has minimum degree two after suppression.

subdivisions.

8    Small marginals and consistency across them
The graph event book uses the collection of all pair laws on the ﬁxed labelled set S. Its clusters
are subsets of this same set, and tree ages are absolute normalized times. Thus all pairs determine
one common chronological poset before any numeric witness is selected. Each witness set used for
ordinary rates, inheritance or arm words has size at most three and is selected from that recovered
graph. All such laws belong to the supplied small-marginal collection by sampling consistency.
Numerical formulas are uniquely determined on their positive domains. They therefore attach to
one graph and cannot select incompatible carriers independently in diﬀerent triples.
    Once the complete graph-and-parameter quotient has been recovered, its entire full-sample
law is given by ordinary ﬁnite-state NMSC forward propagation and the Q4/Q8 response kernels.
This proves that the collection of all marginals on at most three sampled lineages determines the
complete Q1–Q8 quotient and full law. Every larger indexed family also suﬃces, including the
eight-lineage family. No minimality is claimed. The ﬁnite proof does not reconstruct each triple
separately and then presume they glue. No locus-length or ﬁnite-data stability bound follows from
analytic continuation of an exact metric-genealogy law.
