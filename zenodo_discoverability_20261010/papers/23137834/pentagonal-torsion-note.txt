1    The pencil and the answer
The original question concerns the normalization of a pencil formed by the ﬁve side lines of a regular
pentagon and the square of its circumcircle equation [1, Question 17, p. 51]. The source already
observes the ﬁve-point inﬁnity subgroup. We compute the other twenty points and the extension
data as well. We ﬁx coordinates, scaling and origin explicitly; changing the scaling changes the
numerical pencil parameter. We make no ﬁrst-priority claim. The universal modular and division-
polynomial ingredients below are classical. Verdure gives the universal full-torsion Kummer criterion,
including specialization [3, Theorem 5, pp. 84–88]. Morton also gives explicit coordinates for all
twenty nonmarked points on the Tate family [4, Theorem 2.1]; its residual polynomial is exactly the
one below after a sign convention change. Our computation concerns the explicit bridge from this
pentagonal plane pencil to that classical family and the resulting complete geometric and arithmetic
answer.

4    The full division ﬁeld and specialization
We specify the modular input; the inﬁnity line or a semisimpliﬁcation alone would not determine this
ﬁeld. Choose ζ with 1 + ζ + ζ −1 = ϕ. Over a ﬁeld containing ζ, the Tate curve with marked P0 has
ﬁve choices of Q0 satisfying e5 (P0 , Q0 ) = ζ, namely Q0 + jP0 . These form the ﬁnite etale degree-ﬁve
full-level cover of the marked Tate parameter line away from its cusps. An elliptic automorphism
ﬁxing a point of order at least four is trivial [2, Lemma 1.1]; thus the cover is ﬁne. Fisher’s full-level

It is real, while K(ζ)/K is imaginary quadratic; hence [L : K] = 4. If v ∈ L and v 5 = a ∈ K ∗ , then
