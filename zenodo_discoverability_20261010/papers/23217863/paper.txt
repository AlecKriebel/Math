      Ordinary Banach–Mazur rigidity of von Neumann algebras
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                            October 7, 2026


                                                Abstract
          Using OpenAI’s all-algebra bounded Hochschild vanishing theorem, we derive local ordinary
      complex Banach–Mazur rigidity for every von Neumann algebra and its canonical predual. The
      threshold depends on the algebra; the conclusion is Jordan ∗-isomorphism. Roydor announced
      the general cohomological implication in 2020, while his published Theorem 1.2 assumes separa-
      ble predual. We give an explicit extension of his geometric argument using deformation stability
      on two ﬁxed source algebras and reconstruction of odd ﬁnite type-I summands from full abelian
      projections. This avoids separability, moving-corner perturbation constants, and enumeration
      of inﬁnite cardinal degrees. The cohomological input and geometric machinery are attributed
      external results; no new solution of the underlying cohomology problem or universal numerical
      threshold is claimed.


1    Statement, inputs and attribution
All Banach spaces and linear maps are complex. Deﬁne

                                      dBM (X, Y ) = inf ∥T ∥ ∥T −1 ∥,
                                                      T

where T ranges over bounded bijective complex-linear maps with bounded inverse; the inﬁmum is
inﬁnity when there is no such map. The literal formula gives dBM (0, 0) = 0 and dBM (0, X) = ∞
for X ̸= 0. For a von Neumann algebra M , write M∗ for its canonical predual, with M = (M∗ )∗
isometrically. A Jordan ∗-isomorphism is a bijective complex-linear map preserving involution and
x ◦ y = (xy + yx)/2.

Theorem 1. For every complex von Neumann algebra M there is εM > 0 such that, for every
complex von Neumann algebra N , either condition

                          dBM (M, N ) < 1 + εM ,          dBM (M∗ , N∗ ) < 1 + εM

implies that M and N are Jordan ∗-isomorphic. With this threshold, Jordan ∗-isomorphism,
algebra-space linear isometry, predual linear isometry, and the two displayed strict distance condi-
tions are equivalent.

    The essential new input is the following attributed theorem.

Theorem 2 (OpenAI, family 295). For every complex von Neumann algebra V and every k ≥ 2,
every bounded complex k-linear Hochschild cocycle V k → V has a bounded complex (k − 1)-linear
primitive. Thus Hbk (V, V ) = 0.

3    Rigidity on a ﬁxed algebra whose unit halves
Say that a von Neumann algebra halves if its unit is the sum of two orthogonal equivalent pro-
jections. Equivalently, its ﬁnite type-I homogeneous summands have even degrees only; properly
inﬁnite summands and type-II summands also halve. This is the projection-halving criterion used
in Roydor’s proof of Theorem 3.2, with the standard structure theory [7, 8]. All inﬁnite cardinal
dimensions are included.

Lemma 4. Let V be a ﬁxed von Neumann algebra which halves and satisﬁes Hb2 (V, V ) =
Hb3 (V, V ) = 0. There is ηV > 0 such that every von Neumann algebra W with dBM (V, W ) < 1 + ηV
is Jordan ∗-isomorphic to V .

Lemma 6. Let O be a ﬁxed von Neumann algebra consisting of ﬁnite homogeneous type-In central
summands with odd n ≥ 3. Choose a projection e which has rank n − 1 on each such summand.
Suppose E = eOe satisﬁes Hb2 (E, E) = Hb3 (E, E) = 0. Then O is locally Jordan rigid for ordinary
Banach–Mazur distance among von Neumann algebras, without a separability assumption.

Proof. Both e and 1 − e have full central carrier; (1 − e)O(1 − e) is abelian, and the ﬁxed algebra
E halves. These assertions follow by writing each ﬁnite homogeneous summand as its arbitrary
abelian center tensored with Mn , choosing the diagonal rank-n − 1 projection, and taking the
bounded central direct product.

    Let T : O → D be a unital self-adjoint near isomorphism as in Lemma 4. Round T (e) to a
projection q using Roydor’s Proposition 2.13. Then 1 − q is equally close to T (1 − e). Lemma 5
gives cD (q) = cD (1 − q) = 1. Roydor’s Lemma 3.1 gives near isomorphisms from E to qDq, and
from (1 − e)O(1 − e) to (1 − q)D(1 − q), with both distortions tending to one. His Proposition 2.13
applied to the latter compression makes (1 − q)D(1 − q) abelian. Its full carrier implies that D
is type I. Lemma 4, applied to the single ﬁxed algebra E, gives an exact Jordan ∗-isomorphism
J : E → qDq.
    For full projection corners, compression identiﬁes their centers with the center of the original
algebra. The center restriction of J therefore induces a ∗-isomorphism β : Z(O) → Z(D). It
preserves arbitrary existing joins: a Jordan ∗-isomorphism and its inverse are positive order iso-
morphisms, hence normal. Let zn be the source homogeneous degree-n central projection and put
wn = β(zn ). The corner wn qDq is homogeneous of degree n − 1. This follows from the usual cen-
tral homomorphism/anti-homomorphism decomposition of a von Neumann Jordan ∗-isomorphism;
either part preserves ﬁnite homogeneous degree.
    Consequently wn q is the sum of n − 1 orthogonal equivalent abelian projections, each with
central carrier wn in D. The complementary projection wn (1 − q) is abelian and also has carrier
wn . Abelian projections with the same central carrier are equivalent [7, 8]. Thus wn is the sum
of n orthogonal equivalent abelian projections, and wn D is homogeneous of degree n. Its center
is identiﬁed by β with that of zn O, so ﬁnite homogeneous matrix classiﬁcation gives zn O =  ∼ wn D
as von Neumann algebras. Taking their bounded central direct product gives O =      ∼ D. The centers
can have arbitrary density. No measurable ﬁber decomposition or inﬁnite-cardinal cancellation was
used.
    All constants were chosen for the ﬁxed E, and the compression bounds depend only on the initial
norm excess. Hence one threshold works for all D, all q and all ﬁnite odd degrees simultaneously.


5    Completion of the algebra and predual conclusions
Proof of Theorem 1. For M ̸= 0, use its three ﬁxed central summands

                                         M = A ⊕ P ⊕ O,

where A is type I1 , O contains the ﬁnite odd type-In summands with n ≥ 3, and P contains
everything else: ﬁnite even and all inﬁnite type-I summands, and all type-II and type-III summands.
Then P halves, and the projection e in Lemma 6 chooses the second ﬁxed source algebra E = eOe.
Theorem 2 applies to each of P, E, since both are von Neumann algebras. This is why the proof
uses universal vanishing; we do not infer the noncentral corner’s cohomology merely by applying a
central restriction to M .
    For suﬃciently small ordinary distance, normalize the initial Banach isomorphism using Roy-
dor’s Corollary 2.4. Its Proposition 2.13 sends the three central projections to an orthogonal
partition of 1N , and Lemma 3.1 gives near isomorphisms from the ﬁxed three source summands
to the corresponding target summands, with uniform distortion bounds converging to one. The
commutative pieces are ∗-isomorphic by Proposition 2.13; Lemmas 4 and 6 handle P and O. Their
ﬁnite direct sum is a Jordan ∗-isomorphism M → N . Omit zero summands. This includes arbitrary
nonseparable centers and arbitrary inﬁnite type-I cardinal dimensions.
    Choose the deformation thresholds once for the at most two nonzero ﬁxed algebras P, E. There
are only ﬁnitely many subsequent center, compression, carrier and halving inequalities; all their
norm error bounds tend to zero as the original distortion tends to one. Choosing a small positive
εM to satisfy them proves the stated ∀M ∃εM ∀N quantiﬁers. No universal constant is inferred.

An inﬁmum-attaining map is unnecessary: a strict distance bound supplies some isomorphism with
distortion strictly below that bound.
    For the predual implication, if S : M∗ → N∗ is a bounded complex-linear isomorphism, its
Banach adjoint S ∗ : N → M has inverse (S −1 )∗ and exactly the same two norms. Taking inﬁma
gives
                                   dBM (M, N ) ≤ dBM (M∗ , N∗ ).
Thus the same algebra threshold suﬃces. Exact Jordan ∗-isomorphisms are normal order isomor-
phisms and induce isometric canonical-predual maps. Conversely, predual isometries adjoint to
algebra-space isometries, and Kadison’s isometry theorem gives Jordan ∗-isomorphism; see also [9,
Theorem 1.1]. This proves all equivalences. For M = 0, either strict distance inequality forces
N = 0, so any positive threshold works.


6    Prior scope and veriﬁcation limits
Ricard–Roydor’s unconditional theorem concerns completely bounded Banach–Mazur distance [4,
Theorem A]; it cannot be used from small ordinary distance, since dBM ≤ dcb has the wrong
direction for that inference. Its predual observation and conditional two-matrix-level version are
also inherited [4, Remarks 3.4 and 3.7]. Roydor’s special ordinary-stable classes include those
whose type-II1 summand has property Γ, a Cartan MASA or McDuﬀ absorption [1, Corollary 1.3].
Neither those cases nor exact predual duality are new contributions.
    A fresh primary-source priority audit on October 7, 2026 checked the literature, equivalent
formulations, companion manuscripts and current upstream version. No explicit duplicate full
arbitrary-algebra consequence and proof was identiﬁed in that bounded search. This does not
establish ﬁrstness. The general conditional formulation was already announced by Roydor; the
present note gives a ﬁxed-source proof extension and records a consequence of OpenAI’s newly
public vanishing theorem. Opposite algebras explain the Jordan conclusion: ordinary Banach
norm geometry need not determine the orientation of the associative product.
    Independent mathematical source audits found no substantive gap in the pinned cohomology
manuscript, and separate adversarial checks examined the ﬁxed-source deformation, carrier and
reconstruction arguments. Finite computations in the supplement are checks of proof mechanisms,
not proofs of the inﬁnite-dimensional input. Static authentication of the actual upstream Lean
sources is distinguished from kernel veriﬁcation: no fresh compilation/import or axiom probe was
reproduced, and this Banach–Mazur consequence is not formalized here. Complete-package reviews
and publication veriﬁcation are recorded separately in the accompanying research records.
