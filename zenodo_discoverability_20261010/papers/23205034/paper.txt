           Output-sensitive sparse factorization over ﬁnite ﬁelds
                         from dense factorization
                                               Alec Kriebel

                                                 Abstract
         We give a deterministic reduction for complete factorization of a sparse univariate polyno-
      mial over an explicitly represented ﬁnite ﬁeld. Its cost is polynomial in the term count, binary
      exponent length, ﬁeld encoding, and total degree of the distinct densely returned factors, in-
      cluding arbitrary binary multiplicities. The reduction maintains a sparse numerator and a dense
      denominator during characteristic-p descent. The numerator degree shrinks by p, while the de-
      nominator degree grows by at most the original radical degree per step. Classical logarithmic
      derivatives, Padé reconstruction, residue multiplicity arguments and Cartier sections supply the
      individual tools. We also detail the established dense factorization and irreducible construction
      reductions of Berlekamp and Shoup. Substitution of OpenAI’s prime-ﬁeld theorem gives uniform
      deterministic polynomial bit complexity, without GRH, randomness or an integer-factorization
      oracle. That input requires the companion general cyclotomic Hecke zero-free theorem; this
      paper neither reproves its breakthrough nor claims formal veriﬁcation.


1    Problem, provenance and exact model
Represent p in binary, put L = dlog2 pe, and supply h ∈ Fp [T ] monic of degree m ≥ 1. Primality
of p and irreducibility of h are promises, or may be checked deterministically. The ﬁeld K =
Fp [T ]/(h) has power basis 1, t, . . . , tm−1 ; every coordinate is a canonical residue with L bits. A
nonzero polynomial f ∈ K[X] of degree n is a dense list. Complete factorization returns its leading
coeﬃcient c and distinct monic irreducibles gi with positive binary multiplicities ei , such that
        ∏
f = c i giei . Constants have an empty list. Zero has no such ﬁnite unique factorization and is
rejected. The ﬁeld and coeﬃcient input uses O((n + 2)mL) bits; output coeﬃcients including the
leading coeﬃcient use O((n + 1)mL) and multiplicities O(n log(n + 1)) bits.
     For construction, a positive degree d may be supplied in binary, but the requested dense output
has (d+1)m coeﬃcient slots. Polynomial in d is polynomial in this output size, and is not polynomial
in log d. No practical eﬃciency is inferred from a ﬁxed polynomial exponent. A dense-input
convention cannot be inferred from a ﬁeld cardinality alone; the supplied deﬁning polynomial is
part of the input.
     Berlekamp’s reduction from general ﬁnite ﬁelds to split prime-ﬁeld polynomials is already public
[9]. Shoup’s construction reduction is public in Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 of [10]. The particular degree-
md lift below also occurs in Algorithm 2 and Theorem 4.1 of Rai [11]. The newly supplied input
is OpenAI’s prime-ﬁeld theorem [12]. We attribute that breakthrough to its source. Consolidating
its implications does not independently solve the base deterministic factorization problem or invent
these reductions.

representation with the degree invariant below.


2     Complete sparse factorization
Let DF denote complete dense factorization over the represented K. This section is a reduction
theorem independent of how that oracle is implemented.
Theorem 1 (Sparse reduction). For every nonzero sparse input in the above model, complete
factorization reduces deterministically to at most 2b calls to DF, each of degree at most (b + 1)D,
with polynomial nonoracle bit cost in t, b, D, m, L. No condition on the multiplicities is required.
With the direct prime-Frobenius reduction below, this uses at most 2b(b+1)D prime-ﬁeld calls, each
degree at most (b + 1)D. Constants and monomials are handled without these calls.

2.4   Pseudocode and bit accounting
The preceding procedures give the following complete deterministic algorithm:

  1. Normalize the sparse list, reject zero, strip X a , set V = 1, the integer weight w = 1, and an
     empty signed factor ledger.

  2. While the numerator is nonconstant, recover its visible denominator by (2), factor it densely,
     and ﬁnd exact multiplicities. Factor V densely. Construct AU , AV , HV as above.

  3. Add w(eU mod p − eV mod p) for each occurring factor. Replace the numerator and denom-
     inator by
                          U ← σ −1 C0 (U ),     V ← σ −1 C0 (AU )HV ,
      and replace w by pw. Continue step 2.

  4. Combine equal factors, delete zero entries, restore (X, a) and the original leading coeﬃcient.
     Check original-input valuations and the weighted degree if an independent certiﬁcate is de-
     sired.

     At level k < b, the two dense calls have degrees at most (k+1)D and kD. Their total degree over
all levels is O(b2 D). Padé matrices have dimensions at most 2 max(1, (b+1)D) by that number plus
one; a ﬁrst known-factor quotient has dimension δ ≤ (b + 1)D over K. Each coordinate always has
L bits. The largest residue product degree is at most (t − 1)(b + 1)D. Multiplicities have O(b) bits;
signed weights, including the ﬁnal unused weight, have O(b + L + log(b + 1)) bits; coeﬃcient inverse
Frobenius uses exponent pm−1 with O(mL) bits. Schoolbook polynomial arithmetic, elimination,

3     Explicit reduction for complete factorization
Let P (u, L) be a nondecreasing bit-cost bound for a deterministic complete factorization algorithm
PF over Fp on degree at most u.

Theorem 2 (Established reduction with explicit oracle accounting). There is a uniform deter-
ministic reduction for complete factorization in the above representation with polynomial nonoracle
overhead in n, m, L. The trace version uses at most mn calls to PF, each of degree at most n, with
sum of queried degrees at most mn2 . An alternative uses at most n calls and sum of degrees at most
n2 . Neither version enumerates the ﬁeld or uses an integer-factorization or primitive-root oracle.

4    Prescribed-degree construction
Shoup [10, Theorem 3.1] gives a deterministic reduction from constructing an irreducible degree-N
polynomial over Fp to PF, with polynomial overhead in N, L. Theorem 4.1 gives the analogous
arbitrary-base implication. For clarity an alternative known lift is as follows. Construct H ∈
Fp [X] irreducible of degree N = md, factor it over K by the preceding theorem, and take the
lexicographically ﬁrst factor. The p-Frobenius orbit of a root has size N . pm -Frobenius acts by
adding m modulo N , so every orbit has size N/ gcd(N, m) = d. Every factor therefore has degree
d and multiplicity one. This proves the lift without ﬁnding an isomorphism or primitive root. It is
the same lift as Rai’s Algorithm 2 [11]. For d = 1 return X; for m = 1 direct prime construction
suﬃces. The extension is the explicit quotient K[X]/(g) for the output g.
    The supplementary construction audit makes Shoup’s oracle use explicit: trial division factors
the numeric output degree N ; each prime-power construction has prime-ﬁeld algebra dimension
at most N 2 ; oracle polynomial degrees are at most N 2 , and there are O(N L log(N + 1)) calls.
The coeﬃcient slots always have L bits. The bound includes p-power Artin–Schreier towers, odd
prime-power binomials and the special 2-power cases. It never factors p − 1. Trial division of N
is permitted by dense output size and is not an integer-factorization oracle on arbitrary binary
inputs. The 2-power exception cannot be omitted: over F3 ,

                             X 4 + 1 = (X 2 + X + 2)(X 2 + 2X + 2),

although −1 is nonsquare. The supplied construction uses the quadratic extension in that case, fol-
lowing the established binomial criterion. Factoring H over K has input size O(dm2 L), polynomial
in the original ﬁeld and dense degree-d output sizes. Thus the whole construction is bit-polynomial
whenever P is polynomial.

6    Reproduction, limitations and disclosure
The supplement contains exact reduction pseudocode, source/dependency ledgers, proof-node au-
dits and standard-library Python implementations. The bundled prime oracle enumerates Fp only
in bounded small examples; its O(p) cost is not polynomial in log p and cannot substantiate prime-
ﬁeld derandomization. Independent trial-division comparison checks 377 small monic inputs; a
second implementation cross-checks 346 cases. Examples cover characteristic two, nontrivial in-
verse coeﬃcient Frobenius, repeated factors, m = 1, constants and rejected zero/oracle errors. The
manuscript makes no assertion of practical eﬃciency or ﬁrst priority. The sparse implementation
additionally compares 960 small monic inputs with dense factorization and checks huge binary expo-
nents, signed cancellations and nontrivial extension coeﬃcients. Separate independent prototypes
check the multiplicity and full-descent reductions. These are ﬁnite checks, not universal complexity
tests. The supplemental priority audit separates the inherited individual mechanisms from the
sparse/dense degree invariant and compares exact primary statements. The earlier dense-only re-
search record was withheld from Zenodo as inherited; the present theorem adds the all-multiplicity
sparse output bound. No claim about general arithmetic circuits, sparse-only output size or poly-
nomial cost in binary prescribed construction degree is made.
