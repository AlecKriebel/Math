# Research log

Date: 3 October 2026. Problem 20001851 / AIM-GEOMETRY-0189.

The recovered target is connectivity of the strict embedding space of arbitrary
open equilateral chains in R3. The prior supplied scalar certificate is partial
background and does not resolve that question. Source retrieval and the genuine
prior-attempt check established a fresh five-attempt budget.

## Substantive attempt ledger

1. **Axis-search reduction.** Proved the exact interval-order theorem and its
   `n`-inequality formulation for `n>=4`; derived the at-most-four-vector
   convex-dependence obstruction. Did not establish universal feasibility.
2. **Constructive ordered-core straightening.** Proved a self-contained path by
   spherical-cap terminal rotations and positive-axis edge interpolation.
   The required initial ordering was not derived for arbitrary unit chains.
3. **Six-bar universal-certificate test.** Constructed exact rational five- and
   six-bar counterexamples to universal scalar certification, while proving
   both unlocked by simple projections. Established robust certificate failure
   in three dimensions. Did not produce a mechanically locked chain.
4. **Exact algebraic counterexample route.** Proved a quantitative clearance
   neighborhood, rational-density reduction for hypothetical locked chains,
   and correct finite-count semialgebraic formulation. No component computation
   or finite upper bound on a first locked bar count was obtained.
5. **Greedy endpoint induction.** Constructed an eight-unit-bar spherical
   barrier and proved its first joint cannot straighten with the tail fixed.
   This falsifies the proposed restricted induction; simultaneous tail motion
   remains unrestricted in the actual problem.

Each attempt has its full mathematical argument in the corresponding
`ATTEMPT_*.md` file. Retrieval, verification runs, review, backup, and packaging
are not counted as further proof attempts.

## Final boundary

The five-attempt mathematical outcome is **unsolved, 5/5**. The results above
are sufficient-class methods, explicit limitations, and reductions. No step
equates certificate failure, fixed-tail trapping, or numerical planner failure
with locking in the full configuration space.

The precise unresolved task is either to construct a strict unit chain with no
path to a straight configuration, or to prove every such chain has such a path
for every finite number of bars. Future work should account for coordinated
joint motion beyond a common scalar axis and beyond fixed-tail endpoint moves.
