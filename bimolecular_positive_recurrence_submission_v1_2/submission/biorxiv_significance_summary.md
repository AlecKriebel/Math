# Systems Biology significance summary

Biochemical species can occur at low copy number, where random reaction events
produce substantial molecule-count fluctuations. Stochastic mass-action
models represent these dynamics as continuous-time Markov chains. Long-run
steady-state analysis requires a stationary probability law rather than
permanent escape toward ever-larger populations.

This paper proves such stability for a structural class of models: finite
weakly reversible networks with one linkage class and no complex containing
more than two molecules. Weak reversibility itself ensures that the population
set reachable from any initial state is a closed communicating class. For
every choice of positive reaction rates, each nonabsorbing class is positive
recurrent and therefore has a unique stationary molecule-count law; an
absorbing singleton has its point-mass law. This supports stationary state
probabilities and long-run averages of bounded observables without tuning rate
parameters.

The result is structural, not universal across biology. It gives no explicit
stationary formula, moment or tail bound, mixing rate, or guarantee of bounded
sample paths. Finite molecule-count means and variances require additional
integrability. No empirical dataset, organism-specific mechanism, or clinical
claim is involved. The contribution is a rigorous steady-state foundation for
stochastic biochemical network models satisfying the stated graph and
molecularity conditions.
