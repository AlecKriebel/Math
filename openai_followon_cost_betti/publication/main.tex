\begin{abstract}
We give a direct calculation of all $L^2$-Betti numbers of the rank-100 amalgam in OpenAI's fixed-price construction. An elementary regular-tree argument makes its cellular boundary injective, proves its displayed presentation complex aspherical, and gives $\beta_n^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0$ for every $n\geq0$, independently of either cost bound. Combining this calculation with OpenAI's positive Bernoulli cost estimate gives an explicit free ergodic probability-measure-preserving orbit relation with cost at least $1+(100\cdot2^{61})^{-1}$ and vanishing $L^2$-Betti numbers. The group infimum cost remains one. The construction and positive cost estimate are due to OpenAI; the cost--Betti comparison and action invariance are due to Gaboriau. This is an attributed consequence and verification note, with a separate direct invariant proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, conventions, and attribution}

For a countable probability-measure-preserving (p.m.p.) equivalence relation $\RR$ on a standard atomless probability space, the cost is the infimum, over generating graphings, of the sum of the domain measures of their partial isomorphisms. Gaboriau proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 \Cost(\RR)-1\geq\betti1(\RR)-\betti0(\RR)
\end{equation}
and asked whether equality always holds \cite[Corollary~3.23 and p.~129]{gab02}; see also his FAQ \cite[p.~1]{faq}. The zeroth term is essential. Infinite classes have $\betti0=0$; a free action of a finite group of order $q$ instead has $\betti0=1/q$, $\betti1=0$, and cost $1-1/q$.

For clarity, we write
\[
 \Cost_*(\Gamma)=\inf\{\Cost(\RR_\alpha):\alpha
       \text{ is an essentially free p.m.p. action of }\Gamma\}
\]
for group infimum cost \cite[Definition~I.5]{gab00}. The action-specific equality, the relation-level equality, and equality for this infimum are distinct assertions; Gaboriau's notes state them separately \cite[Question~2.65]{gabnotes}.

The group and both cost estimates used below are OpenAI's \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI:A-group-without-fixed-price-October-5-2026}. Its manuscript is dated October~5, 2026; the public repository version checked here is the October~6 release at commit \texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}. The general implication from a failure of fixed price to a cost--Betti counterexample was already explicit in Popa--Shlyakhtenko--Vaes \cite[Remark~6.6]{psv}, whose first arXiv version appeared in 2018. The cost-limit deduction for this particular group was already recorded conditionally in our public preliminary triage \cite[\S7 and accompanying audit]{triage}. This note expands that observation with an independent cellular calculation of all $L^2$-Betti numbers and a focused validation record for the upstream cost input. No independent fixed-price discovery or priority for the classical implication is asserted. The bounded priority audit found no earlier instance of the particular direct calculation below; that search does not certify novelty.

\section{Explicit group and actions}

Let
\[
 A=F(a,b_1,\ldots,b_{99}),\quad
 w=ab_1ab_2\cdots ab_{99}a,\quad
 J=\langle b_1,\ldots,b_{99},w\rangle\leq A,
\]
and put $B=J\times\langle t\rangle$, where $\langle t\rangle\cong\ZZ$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group}
 \Gamma=A*_J B
 =\langle a,b_1,\ldots,b_{99},t\mid
          [t,b_i]=1\ (1\leq i\leq99),\ [t,w]=1\rangle.
\end{equation}
We use right actions. On $X_0=[0,1]^\Gamma$ with product Lebesgue probability, the Bernoulli action is $(xg)(h)=x(gh)$. Let $X$ be its invariant conull set of points with trivial stabilizer, with restricted probability $\mu$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:actions}
The group $\Gamma$ is countably infinite and generated by the displayed $101$ elements. There is a surjection $\chi:\Gamma\to\ZZ$ with $\chi(a)=1$ and $\chi(b_i)=\chi(t)=0$. For every positive integer $M$, the action on
\[
 Y_M=X\times\ZZ/M\ZZ,\qquad
 (x,r)g=(xg,r+\chi(g)\bmod M),
\]
with product probability and uniform height coordinate, is free, ergodic, and p.m.p., as is the action on $X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Amalgam normal form embeds $A$ and $B$ in $\Gamma$. In particular $\Gamma$ is infinite and $t$ has infinite order. The displayed generators generate both factors. The generators of $J$ are free: every nonempty reduced word in the $b_i$ and $w$ expands to a nonempty reduced word in $A$, since $w$ starts and ends with $a$, $w^{-1}$ with $a^{-1}$, and opposite adjacent $w$-symbols are excluded by reducedness. This also gives the presentation in \eqref{eq:group}.

The exponent sum of $a$ on $A$ agrees on $J$ with the map $jt^n\mapsto\chi_A(j)$ on $B$, so it extends to $\chi$. Notice $\chi(w)=100$. Coordinate permutations preserve product measure. For $g\neq1$, $xg=x$ implies $x(g)=x(1)$, a null event for independent continuous coordinates. Countability gives the invariant conull free set $X$. The formula for $Y_M$ satisfies the action law and preserves product measure; freeness follows from its first coordinate.

The transformation $x\mapsto xt$ is mixing. Indeed, two cylinder events become independent under $t^n$ for all sufficiently large $n$: their finite coordinate supports become disjoint because $t$ has infinite order. Approximation in measure by cylinders proves mixing for measurable events. A $\Gamma$-invariant subset of $X$ is therefore null or conull. For a $\Gamma$-invariant measurable subset of $Y_M$, each height section is $t$-invariant and hence null or conull. Translation by $a$ cycles the heights and equates their measures. Thus the subset is null or conull. The countable and finite products used here are standard atomless probability spaces.
\end{proof}

\section{Direct cellular computation}

Put $u_0=1$, $u_i=u_{i-1}ab_i$ for $1\leq i\leq99$, and $S=\sum_{i=0}^{99}u_i$. Recall the free-basis substitution used in \cite[\S5.2]{OAI:A-group-without-fixed-price-October-5-2026}: $a,u_1,\ldots,u_{99}$ are a free basis of $A$, with inverse substitutions $b_i=a^{-1}u_{i-1}^{-1}u_i$. The substitutions in both directions recover the generators by induction. Thus $F=\langle u_1,\ldots,u_{99}\rangle$ is free of rank $99$ and embeds in $\Gamma$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:operator}
Right convolution by $S$ and by $t-1$ has trivial kernel on $\ell^2(\Gamma)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Form a bipartite graph with two copies $F_L,F_R$ of $F$ and edges $g_L$--$(gu_i)_R$, $0\leq i\leq99$. It is connected and $100$-regular. A nonbacktracking cycle would give $u_{i_1}u_{j_1}^{-1}\cdots u_{i_k}u_{j_k}^{-1}=1$ with consecutive edge labels distinct. Delete the factors $u_0=1$. No two zeros are adjacent; deletion joins factors of the same sign, and every remaining opposite-sign adjacent pair has distinct indices. A nonidentity factor remains, yielding a nonempty freely reduced word. Hence the graph is the infinite $100$-regular tree.

Adjacency on an infinite $d$-regular tree, $d\geq2$, has no $\ell^2$ kernel at zero. Root the tree at $o$ and put $E_n=\sum_{\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=n}|f(v)|^2$. If adjacency kills $f$, at each vertex of depth $n\geq1$ the sum on its $d-1$ children is minus its parent's value. Cauchy--Schwarz and summation over depth $n\geq2$ give $E_{n+1}\geq E_{n-1}$, since every parent is counted $d-1$ times. The odd and positive even layer sums are nonnegative, nondecreasing, and summable, hence zero. The equation at a neighbor of $o$ then gives $f(o)=0$.

For $(Tf)(g)=\sum_i f(gu_i)$, adjacency is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\T^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so both $T$ and $T^*$ are injective. Right convolution by $S$ on coefficient functions is $T^*$: $(fS)(g)=\sum_i f(gu_i^{-1})$. Decomposition over left cosets $gF$ transfers injectivity to $\ell^2(\Gamma)$. A vector killed by right multiplication by $t-1$ is constant on every infinite right-$t$ orbit and thus zero by square summability. The operators are bounded finite sums of translations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:direct}
The presentation complex in \eqref{eq:group} is a finite $K(\Gamma,1)$ of dimension two. For every $n\geq0$, $\betti n(\Gamma)=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $r_i=tb_it^{-1}b_i^{-1}$ and $r_w=twt^{-1}w^{-1}$. Write coefficients on the left of chosen lifted cells. The completed cellular boundaries are left-$\Gamma$-equivariant and use right multiplication in coordinates:
\[
 \ell^2(\Gamma)^{100}\xrightarrow{d_2}\ell^2(\Gamma)^{101}
 \xrightarrow{d_1}\ell^2(\Gamma),\qquad d_1(f)=\sum_x f_x(x-1).
\]
The lifted attaching path contributes its prefix before each positive letter and minus its prefix after each negative letter, equivalently the classical Fox derivative \cite{fox}. Put $P_i=u_{i-1}a$. Then $D_aw=S$ and $D_{b_i}w=P_i$. Evaluating in $\Gamma$ gives, for $q=(q_1,\ldots,q_{99},q_w)$,
\begin{align*}
 (d_2q)_a&=q_w(t-1)S,\\
 (d_2q)_{b_i}&=q_i(t-1)+q_w(t-1)P_i,\\
 (d_2q)_t&=\sum_{i=1}^{99}q_i(1-b_i)+q_w(1-w).
\end{align*}
The factor order is fixed; none is commuted past $S$ or $P_i$. The identity $S(a-1)+\sum_iP_i(b_i-1)=w-1$ also checks $d_1d_2=0$. If $d_2q=0$, its $a$-coordinate and Lemma~\ref{lem:operator} give $q_w(t-1)=0$, then $q_w=0$. The $b_i$-coordinates give $q_i=0$ for every $i$. Thus $d_2$ is injective.

This restricts to injectivity on finitely supported integral cellular chains. The universal cover is simply connected and two-dimensional, so its first homology and homology above degree two vanish; injectivity kills its second homology. A simply connected acyclic CW complex is contractible by Hurewicz and Whitehead. The presentation complex is therefore a $K(\Gamma,1)$.

The orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{im}d_1$ consists of vectors invariant under all generators, hence constant on the infinite group and zero in $\ell^2$. Its image is dense. Von Neumann dimension additivity for finite Hilbert modules \cite[Properties~1.2(1),(3), p.~106]{gab02}, or \cite[Theorem~1.11 and \S2.3]{lueck}, gives
\[
 \dim_\Gamma\ker d_1=101-1=100,\qquad
 \dim_\Gamma\overline{\operatorname{im}d_2}=100.
\]
The quotient has dimension zero, so $\betti1=0$. Dense image gives $\betti0=0$, injectivity gives $\betti2=0$, and there are no higher cells. The Euler characteristic $1-101+100=0$ is consistent with the result; by itself it would only give $\betti1=\betti2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cost input and exact parameter}

For
\[
 K=e^{96}\frac{96^{99}}{95^{95}},\qquad
 0<\alpha<\frac1{200},\qquad K\alpha^3<\frac12,
\]
OpenAI's theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input}
 \Cost(\RR_X)\geq1+\frac{\alpha}{100},\qquad
 \Cost(\RR_{Y_M})\leq1+\frac{99}{M}\quad(M>100).
\end{equation}
Here $\RR_Z$ denotes the orbit relation of $\Gamma$ on $Z$. The upper estimate has a short direct proof, retained to make the route to vanishing checkable independently of the positive cost input.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:upper}
The second bound in \eqref{eq:input} holds for every integer $M>100$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $0<p<1$. The Borel set
\[
 C_p=\bigl(\{x\in X:x(1)<p\}\cup
 \{x\in X:x(t^n)\geq p\text{ for all }n\in\ZZ\}\bigr)
       \times\ZZ/M\ZZ
\]
has measure $p$ and meets every $t$-orbit. The second set is null by independence of the distinct $t^n$ coordinates and corrects the exceptional orbits. Keep the full $t$-map and the $100$ generators $b_i,w$ on $C_p$. These generate the $B$-relation: for any such $j$ and any $z$, choose $n$ with $zt^n\in C_p$, and use $z\to zt^n\to zt^nj\to zj$, since $j$ commutes with $t$. Their cost is $1+100p$.

Add the $a$-map from heights $0,\ldots,98$, costing $99/M$. Put $u_0=1$, $u_i=u_{i-1}ab_i$, so $w=u_{99}a$ and $\chi(u_i)=i$. If every $a$-edge from heights $k,\ldots,k+98$ is available, take $z$ at height $k+99$ and $y=zu_{99}^{-1}$ at height $k$. The path $y\to yu_1\to\cdots\to yu_{99}=z$ uses those $a$-edges and the supplied $b_i$-edges. The supplied $w$-edge also joins $y$ to $yw=za$. It provides the next $a$-edge. Starting with the initial $99$ heights and taking $k=0,\ldots,M-100$ provides all remaining source heights through $M-1$, including the final edge to height zero. The graphing therefore generates the $\Gamma$-relation. Its cost is $1+100p+99/M$ for every $p>0$; taking the infimum proves the bound.
\end{proof}

We fix $\alpha=2^{-61}$. Its admissibility is certified without floating-point arithmetic:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 K=e^{96}96^4(1+1/95)^{95}<e^{97}96^4<3^{97}96^4,
 \qquad 2\cdot3^{97}96^4<2^{183}.
\end{equation}
The last inequality is an exact integer comparison, reproduced in the supplement; $e<3$ and $(1+1/95)^{95}<e$ give the preceding bounds. Also $2^{61}>200$. Consequently $K\alpha^3<1/2$ and $0<\alpha<1/200$. The positive gap supplied by the first bound in \eqref{eq:input} is
\[
 \eta=\frac1{100\cdot2^{61}}.
\]

\section{The invariant calculation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the group \eqref{eq:group} and its free Bernoulli orbit relation $\RR_X$,
\[
 \betti n(\Gamma)=\betti n(\RR_X)=0\quad(n\geq0),
 \qquad \Cost(\RR_X)\geq1+\frac1{100\cdot2^{61}}>1.
\]
More generally every essentially free p.m.p. action of $\Gamma$ has every $L^2$-Betti number zero. Its group infimum cost satisfies $\Cost_*(\Gamma)=1$. In particular $\RR_X$ has strict inequality in \eqref{eq:comparison}, while $\Cost_*(\Gamma)=1+\betti1(\Gamma)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:direct} and Gaboriau's action-invariance theorem \cite[Corollary~3.16, p.~126]{gab02} give $\betti n(\RR_Z)=\betti n(\Gamma)=0$ for every $n\geq0$ and every essentially free p.m.p. action $Z$. This theorem does not impose ergodicity. Restricting to an invariant conull free set does not change the relation or its measured invariants. Every orbit is infinite, since $\Gamma$ is infinite. Thus $\betti0(\RR_Z)=0$ by \cite[Properties~3.15(1), p.~126]{gab02}.

As a second proof of first-Betti vanishing, already recorded in the preliminary triage, apply \eqref{eq:comparison} to every $Y_M$ and use Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}. The same group invariant satisfies
\[
 0\leq\betti1(\Gamma)=\betti1(\RR_{Y_M})
 \leq\Cost(\RR_{Y_M})-1\leq\frac{99}{M}\quad(M>100).
\]
For every $\epsilon>0$, choose an integer $M>\max\{100,99/\epsilon\}$. Hence $\betti1(\Gamma)=0$. Action invariance gives the asserted vanishing for $X$ and every other free action, and gives $\betti0(\Gamma)=0$. The first bound in \eqref{eq:input}, with \eqref{eq:certificate}, yields the asserted positive cost gap.

For every free action, \eqref{eq:comparison} now gives cost at least one. The sequence $Y_M$ gives infimum at most $1+99/M$ for every $M>100$. Thus $\Cost_*(\Gamma)=1$. This infimum assertion does not assert that some action attains cost one.
\end{proof}

\section{Dependency and verification scope}

The positive cost bound remains the substantial input from OpenAI. Its proof proceeds through relative graphing compression and deployment, transfer to ranks of finite permutation presentations, and expansion with a coefficient-sensitive planar shortening argument. The supplementary verification record identifies the exact statements, source hashes, independent proof audits, and boundary checks for those inputs. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses that input explicitly; it does not independently establish failure of fixed price. The direct all-degree vanishing proof in Proposition~\ref{prop:direct} uses neither cost bound. The group infimum-cost calculation additionally uses Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}. Both remain valid independently of the Bernoulli lower-bound argument.
