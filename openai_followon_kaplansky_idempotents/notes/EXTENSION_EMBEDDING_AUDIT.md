## Claim and input

The pinned October 4 input gives a finitely presented torsion-free group G
with a finite two-dimensional K(G,1), and a,b,c in F_2[G] satisfying ab=1,
ac=0, and c nonzero. The already disclosed consequence gives the scalar
idempotent e=1-ba and a nonzero cyclic projective eF_2[G] with zero K_0 class
absorbed by the regular right module.

A stronger displayed group-theoretic conclusion is available:

> There is a group H generated by two elements, admitting a finite
> two-dimensional K(H,1), into which G embeds. Thus H is torsion-free and
> the images of a,b,c,e give all the same characteristic-two scalar
> idempotent and projective-cancellation conclusions in K[H], for every
> characteristic-two field K.

The algebraic witness is transported, not numerically found. No numerical
matching or new smaller-support idempotent is obtained by this route.

## Explicit finite HNN construction

Write a finite presentation G=<g_1,...,g_n | R_1,...,R_r> and let

    B = G * F(x,y).
    u_0 = y,                   v_0 = x,
    u_i = x^(-i) y x^i,        v_i = y^(-i) x g_i y^i  (1 <= i <= n).

Let U=<u_0,...,u_n> and V=<v_0,...,v_n> inside B. Both displayed lists are
free bases:

* In F(x,y), the kernel of the exponent-sum map to Z taking x to 1 and y
  to 0 has free basis {x^(-j) y x^j : j in Z}. The u_i are a finite subset.
* The quotient q:B->F(x,y) killing G takes the v_i to x and y^(-i)xy^i.
  These lie in the analogous free basis for the kernel of the y
  exponent-sum map. If a nonempty reduced word in formal symbols v_i
  were trivial in B, its image would be a nonempty reduced word in that
  free basis and would be nontrivial. Therefore the v_i freely generate.

Consequently u_i -> v_i extends to an isomorphism U->V. The ordinary HNN
extension

    H = < B,t | t^(-1) u_i t = v_i, 0 <= i <= n >

contains B, and hence G, injectively by the HNN embedding theorem. It has
a finite presentation because there are only n+1 HNN relators.

The relation for i=0 gives y=t x t^(-1). For i>=1, the relation gives

    g_i = x^(-1) y^i t^(-1) x^(-i) y x^i t y^(-i).

Every g_i and y therefore belongs to <x,t>, proving H=<x,t>. On substituting
y=t x t^(-1), put

    W_i(x,t) = x^(-1) t x^i t^(-2) x^(-i) t x t^(-1)
               x^i t^2 x^(-i) t^(-1).

Tietze elimination of y and g_1,...,g_n yields the exact presentation

    H = < x,t | R_j(W_1,...,W_n), 1 <= j <= r >.

Thus the number r of relators is preserved, as in the classical theorem.
For i>=1 the displayed W_i is freely reduced and has length 4i+10, so
this particular encoding sends each source generator to a word of length
at most 4n+10. With the source's 8260-generator recipe, this is at most
33050. This is a bound on the written encoding, not a claimed metric
distortion bound or an explicit numerical example.
An independent free-reduction computation checked the substitution identity
and length count for i=1,2,3,7,8260; the general identity is the displayed
word calculation, not an extrapolation from those checks.

The source group and witness need no solvable-word-problem hypothesis for
this theorem. Redundant or trivial g_i are allowed: q(v_i) still proves
freeness. For n=0, the construction simply yields the free group on x,t.

## Finite two-dimensional classifying complex

Let X be the given finite two-dimensional K(G,1), with a chosen basepoint,
and set Y=X wedge R_2, where R_2 is the two-petal rose labelled x,y.
The free product of G and F_2 has classifying complex Y. Its universal
cover is a tree of contractible covers of X and trees, and is contractible.

Let E be a rose with n+1 petals. Represent the words u_i and v_i by based
cellular loops in Y; the resulting two maps f_-,f_+:E->Y are injective on
fundamental groups by the free-basis proof. Form the graph of spaces

    Z = Y union (E times [0,1]),

identifying the two ends by f_- and f_+. The interval at the vertex of E
gives the stable letter t. Van Kampen gives precisely the presentation
of H above. Z is finite and has dimension at most two: Y has dimension
two, E has dimension one, and its cylinder has dimension two.

For completeness, the asphericity assertion needs the injectivity of the
two edge maps. The universal cover of Z projects onto the Bass–Serre tree
of this HNN extension; vertex spaces are copies of the contractible
universal cover of Y and edge spaces are copies of the contractible
universal cover of E times an interval. Each finite subtree gives a
contractible homotopy pushout, by successively attaching contractible
vertex and edge spaces along contractible edge ends. Every finite sphere
map into this CW complex is contained in a finite subcomplex, and its
projection is contained in a finite subtree. It is therefore null-homotopic.
The universal cover is connected and simply connected and has no higher
homotopy groups, hence is contractible by Whitehead's theorem. This proves
Z is K(H,1). One may use mapping-cylinder models for the attachments;
the maps of roses need not be immersions or subcomplex inclusions.

Torsion-freeness also follows directly: a finite-order element of an HNN
extension is conjugate into its base group (a finite subgroup fixes a
vertex of the Bass–Serre tree). B=G*F_2 is torsion-free by the analogous
free-product normal form, so H is torsion-free. The finite-dimensional
classifying complex is consistent with, rather than a substitute for,
this normal-form check.

### Asphericity of the final two-generator r-relator complex

There is a stronger cell-level statement if the chosen source presentation
is aspherical. Any finite connected two-dimensional K(G,1) supplies such a
presentation: collapse a maximal tree in its one-skeleton to obtain one
vertex, n one-cells g_i, and r two-cells with attaching words R_j. The
collapse is a homotopy equivalence and keeps all two-cells. Take this X
for the graph-of-spaces construction above.

Then Z has one vertex, n+3 one-cells g_1,...,g_n,x,y,t, and r+n+1 two-cells:
the r original cells and the n+1 HNN cells. Let S be the subcomplex formed
by all its one-cells and only the HNN cells, omitting the original R_j
cells. Each g_i occurs exactly once in its own HNN relator and occurs in
no other HNN relator. Accordingly, in S each pair consisting of the g_i
edge and its defining two-cell can be collapsed, retaining x,y,t. After
all n such collapses, y appears exactly once in the remaining cell
t^(-1)ytx^(-1), so that defining edge–cell pair can also be collapsed.
This gives a deformation retraction

    S -> rose(x,t),     y -> t x t^(-1),    g_i -> W_i(x,t).

This is the standard collapse of an added generator and its defining
relator. The other old cells prevent these pairs from being literal free
faces in Z itself; that is why the subcomplex S and the attaching-map
argument are required. Each original attaching map R_j(g_1,...,g_n) in S
is homotopic, via the above retraction, to R_j(W_1,...,W_n) in the retained
rose. Cell attachments along homotopic maps preserve homotopy type, and
attaching finitely many cells is a homotopy pushout along their boundary
cofibrations. Thus replacing S by its rose deformation retract and
carrying all r attaching maps along it proves

    Z  homotopy equivalent to  K(<x,t | R_j(W_1,...,W_n)>).

The final standard presentation complex is therefore itself an aspherical
finite two-dimensional K(H,1), with exactly two one-cells and r two-cells.
Its Euler characteristic is r-1. This agrees with the graph-of-spaces
count:

    chi(H) = chi(G*F_2) - chi(F_(n+1))
           = chi(G)-2+n = (1-n+r)-2+n = r-1.

This is a claim about generator-plus-defining-relator eliminations and
their induced attaching-map homotopies, not about all Tietze moves.
Adding a redundant relator can wedge on a two-sphere and destroy
asphericity even while preserving the group, so mere group presentation
equivalence would not prove this cell-level claim. If an arbitrary finite
presentation of G is used instead of an aspherical source complex, the
two-generator r-relator group presentation is still correct, but its
standard presentation complex need not be aspherical. The separate
graph-of-spaces construction still supplies H with a finite
two-dimensional classifying complex when G has one.

### Small and infinite cases

No finiteness of the underlying group G is needed; n and r are finite,
while the source group can be infinite. No enumeration of every element
of G and no infinitely generated HNN edge group is used. This distinction
is essential: the countable-group version of the classical theorem may
have infinitely many edge generators and cells, and would not by itself
give a finite K(H,1).

For n=1, the free bases are {y,x^(-1)yx} and
{x,y^(-1)xg_1y}; the projection proof still applies, including g_1=1.
The same elimination and topological argument give two generators and
r relators. If G is infinite cyclic with its standard n=1,r=0
classifying circle, H is the rank-two free group; G embeds by the word
W_1 of length 14. If G is trivial and one uses the n=1,r=1 contractible
complex <g_1 | g_1>, the construction instead yields the aspherical
presentation <x,t | W_1>. These checks are compatible with the general
formula and do not rely on nontriviality of any g_i. The n=0 trivial
input yields the rank-two free group as already noted. Of course none
of these small inputs has the required proper one-sided inverse; they
are checks of the embedding theorem, not alternative counterexamples.

## Ring and module transport

The inclusion j:G->H induces an injective unital map K[G]->K[H] for any
field K: distinct source group elements remain distinct basis elements.
The transported a,b,c satisfy ab=1, ac=0, c nonzero and consequently
the transported e=1-ba is a scalar idempotent different from zero and one.
Coefficient extension F_2->K is also injective for every characteristic-two
field. No characteristic-zero or odd-characteristic conclusion follows.

Writing S=K[H] and P=eS, the same right-module maps

    S -> S direct-sum P,  s -> (as,es),
    S direct-sum P -> S,  (u,p) -> bu+p

are inverse. P is cyclic, nonzero and projective, S is isomorphic to
S direct-sum P, and [P]=0 in K_0(S). This does not assert P=0 or
K_0(S)=0. The inclusion preserves the cardinality of any already reduced
finite support, but does not shorten it or compute it. All scalar and
module checks remain the inherited classical algebra.

H actually requires two generators: B contains the nonabelian free factor
F(x,y), so H is nonabelian and cannot be generated by one element.

## Novelty and exact remaining gap

The theorem above is mathematically valid and sharpens the displayed
source/core group's generating-set bound from 8260 to two, while retaining
finite presentation and a finite two-dimensional classifying complex.
The embedding, its formulas, relator preservation, torsion control and
tree-of-spaces dimension argument are classical. The characteristic-two
witness is simply included in the new group ring. This does not produce
a new idempotent mechanism, a new matching, a new support certificate or
an independent direct-finiteness counterexample.
