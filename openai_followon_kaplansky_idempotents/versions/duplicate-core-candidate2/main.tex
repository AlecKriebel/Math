\begin{abstract}
The October 4 manuscript of OpenAI constructs a finitely presented
torsion-free group $G$, with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex,
and $a,b,c\in\Ftwo[G]$ such that $ab=1$, $ac=0$, and $c\ne0$.
Classical ring algebra then makes $e=1-ba$ a nontrivial scalar idempotent.
For $R=\Ftwo[G]$, the right ideal $P=eR$ is nonzero, cyclic and projective,
with $R\cong R\oplus P$ and $[P]=0$ in $K_0(R)$.
The same example works over every characteristic-two field.
We give the complete consequence proof, an existential presentation recipe,
and the scope of the source audit. The result and short proof were already
disclosed in the author's public research triage. This record adds
verification and exposition, claims no new mathematical discovery,
and is withheld from a duplicate novelty preprint deposit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact statement and attribution}
For a field $K$ and a torsion-free group $G$, the scalar form of
Kaplansky's idempotent conjecture asks whether every $e\in K[G]$ with
$e^2=e$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$; see \cite[Problem 1(c)]{Oinert}.
Its positive-characteristic form quantifies over all fields of positive
characteristic. The example below contradicts its characteristic-two
specialization, even for finitely presented groups with a finite
two-dimensional classifying complex.

The pivotal input is precisely \cite[Theorem 1.1]{OpenAI}, at repository
commit \href{https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}{\texttt{adc7f124}} (full hash in the source manifest):
there exist such a group $G$ and $a,b,c\in\Ftwo[G]$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input}
ab=1,\qquad ac=0,\qquad c\ne0.
\end{equation}
The group construction and its new failure of direct finiteness belong to
that source. The reduction from failure of direct finiteness to a
nontrivial idempotent is classical and explicitly appears in
\cite[Proposition \texttt{kaplansky\_relations}]{Gardam}.
The module calculation below is the corresponding standard splitting;
compare the interpretation of direct finiteness of the regular right
module in \cite[p.~9]{Ara}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:consequence}
There exists a finitely presented torsion-free group $G$ admitting a finite
two-dimensional classifying complex and a scalar idempotent
$e\in R=\Ftwo[G]$ with $e\ne0,1$. The right $R$-module $P=eR$ is nonzero,
cyclic and projective, and
\[
R_R\cong R_R\oplus P,\qquad [P]=0\quad\hbox{in }K_0(R).
\]
For every characteristic-two field $K$, the image of the same $e$ in
$K[G]$ remains an idempotent other than $0$ and $1$.
\end{theorem}

The algebraic proof is self-contained in the next section. The existence
of the group is supplied by the cited input, whose actual probabilistic
and topological arguments were separately audited as recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:input-audit}. Neither a scalar idempotent in odd
characteristic or characteristic zero, nor a projection in a reduced
group $C^*$-algebra, is asserted.

\section{Ring and right-module proof}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ring}
Let $R$ be a nonzero unital ring and suppose \eqref{eq:input} holds in $R$.
Then $e=1-ba$ is an idempotent other than $0$ and $1$, and $P=eR$ has all
the right-module and $K_0$ properties in Theorem~\ref{thm:consequence}.
No characteristic assumption is needed for this proposition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $f=ba$. Then $f^2=b(ab)a=f$, so
$e^2=(1-f)^2=1-2f+f^2=1-f=e$.
Also $ec=c-b(ac)=c\ne0$, whence $e\ne0$.
If $e=1$, then $ba=0$, and $a=a(ba)=0$ contradicts $ab=1$ in a
nonzero ring. Thus $e\ne1$.

The complementary idempotents $f$ and $e$ satisfy $fe=ef=0$ and
$f+e=1$. Therefore $R=fR+eR$. If $x=fr=es$, then
$x=fx=fes=0$, giving the direct sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
R_R=baR\oplus eR.
\end{equation}
Since $baR\subseteq bR$ and $b=bab$, the reverse inclusion holds as
well: $baR=bR$. Left multiplication by $b$ is a right $R$-linear
isomorphism $R_R\to bR$, with inverse left multiplication by $a$.
Indeed $abr=r$ for every $r$, and $ba(br)=br$ for every $br\in bR$.

Consequently \eqref{eq:split} identifies $R_R$ with $R_R\oplus P$.
For a fully explicit pair of inverse maps, use
\[
\Phi(r)=(ar,er),\qquad \Psi(u,p)=bu+p
\quad (r,u\in R,\ p\in eR).
\]
The identities $ae=eb=0$, $ep=p$, and $ab=1$ give
$\Psi\Phi(r)=bar+er=r$ and $\Phi\Psi(u,p)=(u,p)$.
They also show $\ker(r\mapsto ar)=eR$; in particular $c\in P$.

The module $P$ is a direct summand of the free right module $R_R$, hence
projective. It is cyclic, generated by $e$, and nonzero because it
contains $e=e\cdot1\ne0$. By definition, $K_0(R)$ is the group completion
of the direct-sum monoid of finitely generated projective right modules
\cite[Chapter II, Section 2]{Weibel}. Hence the isomorphism implies
$[R]=[R]+[P]$ and therefore $[P]=0$ in that abelian group.
It does not imply $P=0$. Rather, $R\oplus0\cong R\oplus P$ with
$0\not\cong P$ exhibits failure of projective cancellation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:consequence}]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:ring} to the group and triple of
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{OpenAI}. If $K$ is any characteristic-two field, the
unique unital embedding $\Ftwo\hookrightarrow K$ induces
\[
\Ftwo[G]\longrightarrow K[G],\qquad
\sum_g\lambda_g g\longmapsto\sum_g\lambda_g g.
\]
This map is multiplicative and injective because the elements of $G$
form a basis over each coefficient field. It preserves $e^2=e$ and
cannot send $e$ or $e-1$ to zero. Thus the same finite-support scalar
idempotent remains nontrivial over every such field. Applying the ring
proposition there also gives $eK[G]$ with the corresponding absorption
and zero $K_0$ class.
\end{proof}

Vanishing of this one class does not assert $K_0(R)=0$. In fact the
scalar inclusion $\Ftwo\to R$ and augmentation $R\to\Ftwo$ split off
$K_0(\Ftwo)=\mathbb Z$ from $K_0(R)$. The characteristic-two field
embedding supplies no coefficient map to a field of odd characteristic
or characteristic zero.

\section{Existence construction and explicitness}\label{sec:input-audit}
The source uses the projective plane over $\mathbb F_{128}$, with
$v=16513$ points and lines, and a signed alphabet of $v+7=16520$ letters.
The seven extras are paired with seven ordinary letters; the remaining
ordinary letters are paired amongst themselves. Thus its rose has 8260
generators. For sufficiently large $m\equiv1\pmod4$, take $m$ vertices
per line class on side $A$ and $m-1$ on side $B$.
Each ordinary outgoing-label set is the corresponding 129-point line.
One distinguished $A$ vertex has all seven extras. The other extra sets
are empty or complements of Fano-plane lines. Each of those seven
four-element complements occurs at $(129m-1)/4$ vertices of $A$ and
$129(m-1)/4$ vertices of $B$, distributed evenly amongst line classes.
This balances inverse-label slots and imposes the exact intersection
parities needed by the source's Proposition 2.1.

Independent random bijections pair inverse slots on each side. Sections
3--5 of \cite{OpenAI} prove positive probability of an outcome with
logarithmic girth, bounded component diameter, and no reduced spherical
arrangement. In Section 6 the cone-picture surgeries give both
asphericity and protection of the designated roots. Attaching cones on
all graph components to the rose yields the finite two-dimensional
classifying complex and its torsion-free fundamental group.

For clarity, a finite presentation is available \emph{after an outcome
has been selected}. In each component $\Lambda$ choose a spanning tree
and root $q_\Lambda$, and let $w_x$ label the tree path from $q_\Lambda$
to $x$. For each non-tree edge with chosen orientation $x\to y$ and
letter $t$, impose the relator $w_x t w_y^{-1}$. These relators normally
generate the labels of all graph loops, exactly as required by the cone
attachment. Choose the designated roots in their respective components
$A',B'$. Then the source supplies
\[
a=\sum_{x\in A'}w_x,\qquad b=\sum_{x\in A'}w_x^{-1},\qquad
c=\sum_{y\in B'}w_y^{-1}
\]
in the presented group algebra. A simultaneous-label step makes each
product contribution constant on a component of the product graph.
The handshake lemma gives cancellation in characteristic two: all
degrees are odd on $A'\times B'$, while the single even-degree exception
$(x_A,x_A)$ on $A'\times A'$ leaves contribution $1$.
Root protection ensures that only $y=x_B$ contributes to the identity
coefficient of $c$, so that coefficient equals $1$.

The parameter $m$ and the matching outcome are existential in this
source. No numerical graph, relator list, explicit reduced support of
$e$, or multiplication certificate is supplied here. The tree recipe
and the source's fully specified probability model establish existence;
they are not a claim that a finite search certificate was obtained.

Our dependency audit checked the repeated-edge issue by reconstructing
the multiplicity-stage first moments and the nearly aligned block grid.
Different stages need not be independent: a realization must survive
each separate first-moment bound. The small unpaired fraction is fixed
before the path-number, length, and comparison bounds. Planar extraction
then applies one fixed triple of bounds to arrangements of every size.
The topology audit checked all four same-edge band surgeries, including
preservation of graph endpoints in rooted disk pictures and essentiality
after splitting a sphere. These checks are recorded with exact source
hashes and limitations in the accompanying research records.

The family-197 Lean documentation names earlier torsion-containing
examples. Its actual finitely presented declaration includes an element
of odd prime order. We did not use that formalization to verify the
October 4 torsion-free theorem and make no claim that this consequence
note is formally verified. An isolated rebuild of the pinned source
compiled its 31-page manuscript; compilation is a document check,
not proof verification. Automated audits are also not human peer review.
