## 1. Ordered basis and monomial extraction: checked directly

The ordered-basis criterion is `02-reduction.tex:14–98`.
Let `N = Delta[x]`, and let A be the commutative graded algebra generated by
the proposed forms. It is legitimate to regard N as a right A-module: the
forms in A commute with each other, though their coefficients need not commute
with Delta. Since `k[x]` is central and N is finite free over it, the submodule
A is finite over `k[x]`. N is finite over A as well.

Setting all `t_i` to zero in the triangular identities forces `f_1=0`, then
`f_2=0`, and so on. The full regular sequence has no projective common zero,
and finiteness over `k[x]` makes `A/(t)` zero-dimensional. The central variables
`x_1,...,x_n` give depth n on N at the graded maximal ideal. Thus `(t)` is a
system of parameters for a finite Cohen–Macaulay module, and is N-regular.
The resulting Hilbert-series identity gives
`Hilb(N/(sum Nt_i)) = dim_k Delta`, concentrated in degree zero. Right
multiplication then generates N from Delta by all ordered t-monomials. In
degree d there are exactly `binom(n+d-1,n-1)` such monomials, equal to
`dim_Delta N_d`, so the spanning collection is a left Delta-basis.

The extraction proposition is `02-reduction.tex:104–154`. I rechecked the
noncommutative bookkeeping:

* The extension of I is two-sided because I consists of central polynomials.
* In a left-Delta expansion, right multiplication by `t_j` moves every exponent
  by the same unit vector without moving a coefficient across a form.
* Consequently the set E of least exponents is upward closed.
* Division-algebra row elimination counts E in degree d as
  `dim_Delta (Delta I)_d`; no commutative coefficient field is needed.
* With reverse-coordinate lex comparison, every term `C_id t_d` for d>i has
  a positive coordinate greater than i, so the least exponent in the displayed
  triangular relation is `a_i e_i`.
* Therefore one monomial ideal, rather than separate degreewise choices,
  contains every `x_i^(a_i)` and has the same entire Hilbert function.

No Betti theorem, flat degeneration, or assertion that an initial ideal has
prescribed pure powers is concealed in this argument.

## 2. Low relations and the simple-factor safeguard: checked locally

The base-point lemma (`03-low-relations.tex:73–92`) uses full regularity and
the retained earlier rows. Its order of substitution is correct: at a putative
base point the forms `t_d`, d>i, vanish; the old rows in increasing d first
give `f_(i+1)=0` and then all later f's. Positive-degree generators have degree
at most b, so base-point freeness of the degree-b part follows from emptiness
of the projective common zero set.

The simple-factor lemma (`:114–212`) has a nontrivial but locally consistent
mechanism. The apolar equation `q(partial)w=0` is justified by annihilating all
degree-b multiples of a linear/quadratic q. For a repeated irreducible factor
`w=p^r h`, its lowest p-adic term forces `q(grad p)=0` on p=0. A nonconstant
Gauss map gives a curve in the characteristic set of degree at most
`deg(p)(deg(p)-1)`; a constant map makes p linear. When every factor is repeated
and linear, logarithmic differentiation gives

`q(grad w / w) = sum m_nu q(v_nu)/p_nu^2 = 0`.

Thus the ambient gradient map lies in the characteristic set. If nonconstant,
restricting it to a varying projective line gives a curve of degree at most
b-1. If constant, w is a pure b-th power and the corresponding annihilator is
evaluation at a projective point, contradicting base-point freeness. These
arguments depend essentially on characteristic zero and on the no-small-curve
hypothesis; they do not assume all members of W are squarefree.

The initial-data proof (`:277–395`) was checked for its main possible
transcendence mistake. The retained coefficient blocks really are jointly
independent over the bounded field of definition of the projected curve:
each rejected r-coordinate block loses at least one unit of transcendence
degree, so at most d_* can be rejected. On the fixed curve, independent generic
quadric sections have nonempty reduced disjoint zero divisors. The valuation
one at a separate zero of each retained quadric supplies independent
squareclasses even after adjoining all constant parameters to Omega. The degree
bound `2^(m-d_*) > D` then contradicts the hypothetical bounded-degree cover.
The proof does not try to infer division from complex specialization.

Finiteness preservation (`:404–426`) also has the necessary O(1) equality:
all forms have degree one, the finite homogeneous extension eliminates any
projective point with all old coordinates zero, and restricted curve degrees
multiply by the finite mapping degree. This is stronger than an arbitrary
finite map of projective varieties, which would not by itself preserve the
ambient degree bound.

## 3. Parameter-space topology: investigated against a primary source

`04-parameters.tex:17–72` uses a homogeneous global join, with an explicit
open cover. On the first open, `1-f(x)` lies in a half-plane avoiding zero,
so a continuous root exists; the x-factor contracts radially. On the overlap,
both f and 1-f admit roots, producing the product of the two nonzero levels
and a strip. The resulting homotopy pushout is the join. A multiplicity-one
factor gives transitive cyclic-cover monodromy, hence a connected initial
level; M joins of connected spaces have no reduced integral homology through
2M-2. Nonzero homogeneous levels are smooth by Euler's identity.

For the parameter theorem (`:74–332`), I checked the numerical ranges and the
compact-support transfer mechanism:

* The defective Jacobian incidence has dimension at most `N_*-M+s-1`.
  Since every component has dimension at least `N_*-s` and M>2s-1, none is
  contained in that incidence; this gives the expected complete intersection.
* The open incidence over the complement of Y is an affine bundle with fiber
  the hyperplane of functionals normalized to evaluate to one.
* The map to `O_(P(W))(1)^x` has smooth connected fibers given by the homogeneous
  levels. Its top proper-support direct image has the relative complex
  orientation; all nearby lower rows through the join-vanishing range vanish.
* The real base dimension 2s and the gap 2M-1 rule out spectral-sequence
  contamination in the stated range. The base retracts to `S^(2s-1)`.
* The closed/open compact-support sequence gives the homology transfer index
  `2s+j-1`. Removing the singular cone costs nothing in the required degrees
  because `kappa <= 2(M-2s+1)-2`. The bound `M>=2h+4s+10` implies this and
  `2s+kappa-1 <= 2M-2` for `kappa=M+2h`.

One likely vulnerability was the quasi-projective Lefschetz citation in
`:287–325`. I obtained Hamm–Lê's original 1985 paper from the journal archive,
not a secondary explanation:
[Hamm–Lê, *Lefschetz theorems on quasi-projective varieties*](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/bsmf.2023/).

required general-hyperplane cell attachment bound. In the present application

surjections down to a smooth surface section use the cited theorem in its

## 4. Division theorem and index comparison: limited reconstruction

The principal arithmetic objective is correctly stated in
`06-descent.tex:100–105`: for a central simple algebra of degree d, proving
that every module's reduced rank is divisible by d excludes nonzero proper
right ideals and proves division. Ordinary divisibility by d of vector-space
dimensions alone would be insufficient, but the paper explicitly proves the
stronger condition.

The completed independent Picard subaudit is in `notes/picard_subaudit.md`.
It independently reconstructed the free Pic^1 action, character maps, matrix
transport, twisted rational-lattice extension, determinant-weight correction,
derived restriction, unit pairing, and torsor Euler divisibility, and found no
counterexample or missing hypothesis. My own local check
of `06-descent.tex:289–344` agrees that the determinant correction has a purely
mixed first Chern class between the two Picard factors. The integral pairing
is unimodular. Top degree on the degree-one Picard product consumes top degree
on the degree-zero product, so only the rank of gamma survives the integral.
This is the relevant mechanism for recovering the module rank, rather than a
claim that an arbitrary Euler characteristic is its rank.

The torsor Euler lemma (`06-descent.tex:353–385`) was checked as an operator
argument on G_0: rank-zero tensoring lowers support dimension; hence d is
nilpotent. The projection formula gives `(a+d)d=0`, and inversion over Q shows
that d acts torsionly. Integral Euler characteristic kills that torsion.
The preceding free group action then yields the factor e_1, while cohomology
of a descended Delta_0-module contributes e_0^2 before idempotent reduction.

The topology-to-index comparison in `07-index.tex:34–85` uses an associated
bundle over a base of real dimension M+2h. A fiber that is (M+2h)-connected
allows a section and the relative homotopy over one additional dimension.
Freeness of H' on the target is not required. The later map to the quotient
uses the genuinely equivariant map, while its homotopy to Phi_0 is used only
nonequivariantly; I found no interchange of these two roles.

The integral pushforward (`:131–204`) is needed: rational Chern characters
alone would not establish `q_j in e_1 Z`. The paper constructs an integral
K-class by the complex Thom class and the projective bundle formula, with the
correct inverse Todd normal factor. K^0(P^h) is torsion-free, so the stated
uniqueness from the Chern character is valid.

The section comparison (`:211–341`) retains both distinct divisibilities.
The coefficients q_j get e_1 from the covering index. The Euler characteristics
on D_0 get e_0 e_1 from a Koszul finite difference of the original p(l).
The chosen D_0 need not be defined over k; after base change the Koszul terms
are still the same integer p(l-a). Derived restriction handles a coherent
class not flat over R or D_0. No equality of vector-bundle classes is inferred
solely from rational Chern characters; only the Euler characteristics needed
for the argument are compared.

## 5. Exact finite arithmetic checks and an edge counterexample

The p-adic truncation lemma is `07-index.tex:355–400`.
Its induction is correct: modulo p, `A=u^nu B` with B invertible forces the
coefficients of Q through degree h−nu to be divisible by p. Truncating there
and dividing by p gives the next congruence with both one less p-adic exponent
and nu fewer degrees. h>=v nu permits v iterations to reach Q(0).

The reproducible standard-library script `notes/companion_checks.py` exhausts
87,108 pairs of coefficient tuples at the threshold h=v nu, in these cases:

| p | v | nu | h | coefficient pairs | annihilating pairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 256 | 16 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 8,192 | 128 |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 13,122 | 162 |
| 2 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 65,536 | 128 |

Every annihilating pair has Q(0)=0 modulo p^v. It also checks the claimed
order `ord_u A_b(u) = p^(v_p(b))-1` modulo p for 396 cases with
p in {2,3,5,7} and 2<=b<=100. These are corroborating finite checks; the
general lemma is justified by the preceding induction, not by the samples.

There is an explicit counterexample if one weakens the dimension hypothesis:

`A=2+u`, `Q=2-u`, `p^v=4`, `nu=1`, `h=1`.

Their product is `4-u^2`, hence zero modulo `(4,u^2)`, but Q(0)=2 is not
divisible by 4. This confirms that losing the h>=v nu safeguard would break
the central conclusion. It does **not** falsify the stated lemma: the source
chooses h>=2 in this instance.

In the final Hilbert-series manipulation (`07-index.tex:402–475`), the finite
initial error R(t) is integral because q_j/e_1 is integral and the Euler
sequence agrees eventually with the Hilbert function. Multiplying by the
denominator, reducing coefficientwise, and substituting t=1−u is legitimate
for this polynomial identity. Cancelling the monomial u^s modulo a composite
integer is coefficient shifting, not cancellation of a possibly nonunit
coefficient. The chosen h depends on e_0 and b before M/e_1 are chosen, exactly
as required to avoid a circular parameter bound.

## 6. Assembly, fields, and limits of the dependency

The Veronese lemma (`08-assembly.tex:15–71`) uses all pair exchanges, which
indeed connect exponent allocations with the same total vector. This yields
`T_alpha^b=product D_j^(alpha_j)`, and `v^b=product D_j=z^b-u^b`, making every
new symbol integral. The later substitution is legitimate even without
coefficientwise commutativity inside one link: inputs centralize that fresh
matrix factor, and the two inputs commute. Fresh tensor factors centralize
the old coefficients. These are precisely the hypotheses needed by the
polynomial-algebra homomorphism in `:235–256`.

The nonzero coefficient c_i is not assumed. At `:201–216`, representing f_i
by a sum of pure powers gives a point on the cone where its class modulo V_b
is nonzero. Since X is geometrically integral, the same coefficient is
nonzero generically. If f_i was already in V_b, any nonzero coefficient can
be absorbed into V_b. The character recursion, the inverse point action on
blocks, and the coefficient transport formula give the same input/output
weights at `:288–320`; in particular lambda_M=1 makes the final t_i invariant.

Every center is finitely generated over the original C. An abstract embedding
into C exists because adding finitely many transcendental generators does not
change its infinite transcendence degree over Q. Such an embedding need not
fix the original C. The paper consistently reserves it for complex topology
and dimensions, not a specialization argument preserving division. This is
an important distinction; an embedding fixing the original C generally would
not exist for a transcendental extension.

For the **full-sequence** target over any characteristic-zero field F, one
does not need the partial-length Artinian reduction citation in Section 9.
Choose the finitely generated coefficient field F_0 over Q for the f_i and
the homogeneous ideal. Faithful flatness descends regularity to F_0. Embed
F_0 into C, apply the complex full-sequence assertion conditionally, and use
the same monomial exponents over F. Graded dimensions survive both field
extensions. Thus the field scope is not a separate obstruction once the
complex Artinian theorem is established.

The construction assumes all a_i>=2. Linear regular-sequence entries in a
general standard graded Artinian complete intersection must be eliminated by
a coordinate change first; the remaining complete intersection has degrees
at least two. If no variables remain, the algebra is the ground field and
the intended Sperner equality is elementary.

The family-200 theorem concerns homogeneous ideals and quotient Hilbert
functions. It does not by itself prove the target statement about the maximum
minimal generator count over **all** ideals. The matching/Sperner implication
and any passage from arbitrary ideals to homogeneous initial ideals must be
proved separately. A companion Betti claim is not a substitute for these
scope checks and was not used in this audit.
