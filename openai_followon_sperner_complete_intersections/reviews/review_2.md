## Original mathematical target and downstream reconstruction

The original claim is preserved exactly: for every characteristic-zero field, every full homogeneous regular sequence of positive degrees in a standard graded polynomial ring, and every ideal of its Artinian quotient, the largest minimal-generator number is the largest complete-intersection Hilbert coefficient. The claim includes the zero and unit ideals, nonhomogeneous ideals, empty variable/sequence case, all-linear case and mixed linear/nonlinear cases. No target was silently weakened to homogeneous ideals or algebraically closed fields.

1. **Linear elimination and order.** The defining ideal has height n. Dependent linear members would reduce its number of generators below n, contradicting the height theorem. A graded linear coordinate change kills the independent linear members. The remaining nonzero homogeneous images form a full homogeneous system of parameters in a polynomial ring and hence a regular sequence by Cohen–Macaulayness. A graded algebra isomorphism preserves all ideals, the maximal ideal, generator counts and Hilbert pieces. Removed degree-one Hilbert factors are 1. Permuting a homogeneous system of parameters is legitimate; the added degree-sorting justification therefore handles the upstream ordered-degree hypothesis. If no variables remain, A=k, its generator maximum is 1, attained by A, and the empty product is 1.

2. **Exact EGH input and fields.** The needed assertion is one pure-power overideal with the same *entire quotient Hilbert function* for every homogeneous overideal of the fixed full regular sequence. It is stronger than the already-known equality of the two complete-intersection Hilbert functions. No Betti bound or equality of ideal-generator counts is used. A fixed containing ideal and sequence involve finitely many coefficients. Their field over Q embeds into C; faithful flatness descends regularity, both scalar extensions preserve graded dimensions, and the output monomial exponent set defines the same counterpart over the original field. An arbitrary characteristic-zero field itself is never assumed to embed into C. Independently, the displayed flat tensor identity preserves mu for extensions of ideals and suffices to descend any upper bound.

3. **Matching, including plateaus.** The displayed rectangle chains partition the product of two chains, are saturated, and have endpoint rank sum equal to the total rank. Induction on factors gives symmetric chains and unimodality. If successive rank sizes are nondecreasing, every chain at the earlier rank has a successor, including a plateau at/above the middle: no chain starts there, so an ending would force a strict decrease. Leading monomials of a subspace in the pure-power quotient have an upper shadow contained in the leading monomials of its degree-one products. EGH applied to AV gives dimensions of the counterpart's degree-j and degree-(j+1) pieces; the latter may contain extra generators, and the ideal inclusion supplies the needed inequality. Thus matching transfers without asserting equal generator counts.

4. **Homogeneous bound and duality.** Removing an initial homogeneous layer below the last maximal Hilbert degree p changes mu by `dim(A_1 I_alpha)-dim(I_alpha)`, which matching makes nonnegative. For I contained in m^p, set J=Ann(mI). The Frobenius pairing identifies Ann(U) with the orthogonal complement of an ideal U, and `mJ` is contained in Ann(I), giving `mu(I)<=mu(J)`. Perfect graded pairings give `Ann(m^u)=m^(c-u+1)` at both endpoints as well as intermediate u. Consequently J contains m^(c-p), which contains m^p. Truncating J at p then yields `mu(J)<=mu(m^p)=h_p`. This proves the homogeneous upper bound without relying on an unexplained maximizing-ideal lattice argument.

5. **All ideals and equality.** Use lowest-degree initial parts under the finite intersection filtration. Multiplication gives `m gr(I)` contained in `gr(mI)`. Associated grading preserves total dimensions, so `mu(I)<=mu(gr(I))`; the direction is correct, and equality between the two product filtrations is unnecessary. The homogeneous bound therefore applies to every ideal. Finally `m^p/m^(p+1)=A_p`, giving the exact equality witness over the original field. The empty/all-linear case is handled with p=0 and m^0=A.
