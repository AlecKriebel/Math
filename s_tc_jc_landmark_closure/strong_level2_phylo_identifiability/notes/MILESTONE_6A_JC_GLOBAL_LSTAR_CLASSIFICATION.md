# Milestone 6A: global JC classification for \(\mathcal L_1\) and \(\mathcal L_*\)

## Conventions

For a rooted network, its semi-directed topology is obtained by forgetting
tree-edge directions, retaining each arrowhead entering a reticulation, and
suppressing only the degree-two artifact created by forgetting the global
root.  A **compatible rooting** inserts that artifact on a tree edge and
orients the undirected tree edges so that the binary, acyclic, strong
tree-child conditions and all retained reticulation arrowheads hold.

Let `R_root` denote changing between compatible rootings of the same
semi-directed topology.

**PROVED.** `R_root` preserves the complete open JC image.  For each fixed
parent choice, deleting unchosen reticulation edges gives the same unrooted
displayed tree under every compatible rooting.  The uniform stationary JC
tree distribution is the product over unrooted edge splits and is therefore
root independent.  The inheritance weight attached to each retained
reticulation arrow is unchanged.  Summing over parent choices proves the
claim.  Splitting or contracting the suppressed root edge uses positive
factors whose product is the old JC multiplier.

Before comparing bridge trees, collapse the invisible two-port root factor
`C_root`.  This is the only nontrivial unlabelled degree-two blob omitted by
the cut-split system.

## Global move system

For JC and the one-triangle-per-blob class, define

\[
\mathcal R_{\rm JC}
=\{R_{\rm root},T,C_{\rm root},R_3,\Theta,
\Omega_{\rm chain}\}.
\]

Here `T` may act in any admissible blob.  The other nontrivial topology moves
are root-local: after a compatible rerooting, they act in the unique blob
containing the global root artifact.

## Primary theorem

**PROVED.** Let \(N,N'\in\mathcal L_*\) have the same labelled leaf set.
Then

\[
\boxed{
N\bowtie_{\rm JC}N'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N\text{ and }N'\text{ are related by a finite sequence of moves in }
\mathcal R_{\rm JC}.
}
\]

Equivalently, there is a common **canonical backbone**, obtained by
collapsing `C_root`, normalizing every `T` class, and undoing any remaining
root-local move, such that:

1. the leaf-labelled homeomorphism-reduced bridge trees agree;
2. every backbone nonroot blob agrees up to port-labelled isomorphism and
   `T`;
3. each of \(N,N'\) is obtained from that backbone by choices of `T` and by
   at most one root-local expansion, at a compatible root site, from the
   class generated by
   \(C_{\rm root},R_3,\Theta,\Omega_{\rm chain}\).

The root sites used by \(N\) and \(N'\) need not be the same when the move is
an invisible two-port `C_root` insertion: both collapse to the same backbone.
For the other root moves, the retained reticulation arrowheads pin the unique
top nontrivial blob in which the move can occur.

The local stochastic correspondences are automatically compatible on a
nonempty regular open region.

**PROVED.** The same theorem holds for \(\mathcal L_1\), as a subclass.
This completes the mandatory JC if-and-only-if milestone for
\(\mathcal L_1\) and the stronger multi-blob class \(\mathcal L_*\).

The theorem classifies full-dimensional regular stochastic overlap.  It does
not claim equality of complete open images for moves other than those already
proved to have that stronger property.

## Fourier factorization across one bridge

Let a cut edge \(e\) induce \(A\mid B\).  For a zero-sum assignment put

\[
h=\bigoplus_{i\in A}g_i=\bigoplus_{j\in B}g_j.
\]

Conditional independence at the bridge and character orthogonality give

\[
\widehat p(g_A,g_B)
=P_A(g_A,h)\,x_e^{[h\ne0]}\,P_B(g_B,h).
\]

For each fixed \(h\), this is a positive rank-one matrix \(F_h\).  If
\((i_0,j_0)\) is any anchor, then

\[
L_i=F_h(i,j_0),\qquad
R_j={F_h(i_0,j)\over F_h(i_0,j_0)}
\]

satisfy \(F_h(i,j)=L_iR_j\).

**EXACTLY COMPUTED.** The verifier checks all eighteen \(2\times2\) minors
and all twelve anchor-reconstruction identities of a symbolic
\(3\times4\) block.

**PROVED.** A nonzero rank-one factorization is unique up to

\[
L\mapsto c_hL,qquad R\mapsto c_h^{-1}R.
\]

The zero-character normalization fixes \(c_0=1\).  The six automorphisms of
\(\mathbb Z_2^2\) act transitively on the three nonzero characters, and JC
equivariance forces one common nonzero scalar.  Thus every cut contributes
exactly one JC arm gauge.

## Recursive projective extraction

Contract every nontrivial blob to a node.  The bridges form a tree.  Peel a
leaf node of that tree, apply the rank-one anchor factorization at its unique
bridge, and continue recursively.

**PROVED.** On the positive open JC locus this terminates and determines every
local component tensor up to one reciprocal arm gauge on each incident
bridge.  Every anchor is nonzero because every JC Fourier coordinate is a
positive sum of positive displayed-tree monomials.

Conversely, multiplying one local port tensor by
\(c^{[h\ne0]}\) and the tensor on the other side by
\(c^{-[h\ne0]}\) leaves the global tensor unchanged.  These are all
factorization freedoms: uniqueness at each peeled rank-one block leaves no
cycle on which an additional gauge could circulate.

This is the projective cut-edge extraction missing from Milestone 5A.

## Why gauges cannot hide topology

Multiplying the arm at port \(i\) by \(z_i\) acts on a local Fourier tensor
as

\[
\widehat P(g_1,\ldots,g_r)
\longmapsto
\widehat P(g_1,\ldots,g_r)
\prod_i z_i^{[g_i\ne0]}.
\]

**EXACTLY COMPUTED.** Every one of the sixty exact four-port invariants used
in the local atlases is multihomogeneous in the four arm degrees.  The
verifier checks all 1,026 monomial terms.  Therefore an arm gauge multiplies
an invariant by one nonzero monomial and cannot change whether it vanishes.

The same conclusion holds for every support pullback because unselected
paths enter through positive independent edge products.  Thus two globally
equal projective factorizations have the same exact local signature at every
corresponding bridge-tree node.

## Ordinary tree vertices are not hidden blobs

A binary ordinary tree vertex has degree three in the reduced bridge tree.
The only nontrivial nonroot blob with the same minimum incidence is the
two-outgoing cycle, represented with its distinguished incoming port.

Put

\[
F=r_{12}r_{13}r_{23}-u_{123}^2.
\]

**EXACTLY COMPUTED.** The ordinary three-port tree satisfies \(F=0\), while
the complete minimal nonroot-cycle pullback is

\[
-p_0p_1p_2p_3^2p_4^2p_5^2p_6^2p_7
(p_1-1)^2(p_7-1).
\]

Here \(p_7\) is the inheritance probability.  Every factor except the
leading minus sign and \(p_7-1\) is positive on the complete open cube, so
the pullback is strictly positive.

**PROVED.** An ordinary degree-three tree node and a nontrivial blob have
disjoint open stochastic interiors; neither one-sided containment occurs.
Degree at least four already excludes an ordinary binary tree vertex
combinatorially.

## Necessity of the global theorem

Assume \(N\bowtie_{\rm JC}N'\).  Their irreducible closures have the same
dimension and share a full-dimensional set, hence are equal.

1. **Cut splits.** Milestone 5A supplies a common point outside both proper
   exceptional sets.  Exact flattening ranks recover the same complete cut
   split system and the same reduced bridge tree.
2. **Projective factors.** Recursive positive rank-one extraction identifies
   corresponding local tensors up to JC arm gauges.
3. **Tree/blob status.** The strict factor above distinguishes every
   potentially ambiguous degree-three node.
4. **Nonroot blobs.** Milestone 4D says arbitrary finite nonroot cycle/theta
   blobs overlap fully exactly under isomorphism and `T`.
5. **Root blob.** Milestone 5H says arbitrary finite one-triangle root blobs
   overlap fully exactly under
   \(T,C_{\rm root},R_3,\Theta,\Omega_{\rm chain}\).
6. **Root normalization.** In each topology, a root-only change can occur
   only in the component containing its global root artifact; treating the
   same changed core as a genuine nonroot blob is excluded by the complete
   incoming-port atlas.  Undo that one local move.  The remaining projective
   factors agree node by node with the common `T`-normalized backbone.
   Across the pair there can be two apparent sites only when each topology
   carries its own root expansion; both disappear on normalization.

The multihomogeneity certificate prevents reciprocal cut gauges from
altering any step.  Hence no coordinated change in two distant blobs can
escape all local tests.

## Sufficiency and independent composition

Suppose the stated structural conditions hold.  Use the common backbone as
the parameter hub.  At every site used by either topology, choose backbone
parameters in the nonempty local overlap domain of the corresponding root
move; sites in distinct components use independent local parameters, so
these conditions form a nonempty product-open set.  At one site, the exact
local atlas supplies a common regular point for its whole local class.
Choose common points for every `T` move and identical positive parameters in
every unchanged blob, then attach the same outside components and bridges.

**PROVED.** Fourier contraction preserves exact equality.  The positive
anchor factorization is a local real-analytic inverse to gluing modulo the
explicit arm gauges, so local ranks add with the bridge parameters and no
rank is silently lost.  The product of the local common neighborhoods is
therefore a common relatively open neighborhood of full global dimension.

Consequently all admissible `T` choices in distinct blobs compose
independently.  A non-`T` root move may be chosen at only one root site in any
one rooted topology.  Two topologies expanded at different invisible
`C_root` sites nevertheless overlap directly because both share the same
open backbone image.

## Sharp root-locality consequence

**PROVED.** Within \(\mathcal L_*\), every full-dimensional non-triangle
ambiguity is root-local.  No strongly tree-child network in this class can
carry two independently composable non-orientational ambiguity bits in two
different blobs.

This is the sharp alternative left open by the earlier failed strong stacking
of `Theta`.  It is a theorem from the complete nonroot generator atlas, not
an inference from one failed graft or a finite search.

An observational class may contain alternatives obtained by using different
compatible root sites in different topologies, especially different
two-port `C_root` insertions, but each individual topology differs
nontrivially from the backbone at at most one such site.  Therefore
non-orientational semi-directed multiplicity is additive over possible root
sites, while independent triangle redirections remain multiplicative.

## Exact topology multiplicity

Fix a regular stochastic chamber, so the set of local sheets containing the
input tensor is constant.  For each nontrivial bridge-tree node \(B\), let

- \(t_B\) be the number of port-labelled semi-directed local sheets in its
  nonroot `T` class;
- \(r_B\) be the number in its full root local class when \(B\) is a
  compatible root site;
- \(\mathcal A\) be the finite set of sites at which a compatible rooting and
  a root-local alternative exist.

Include an invisible degree-two `C_root` insertion as a virtual root site
with its certified local counts.  Put

\[
M_T=\prod_B t_B.
\]

**PROVED.** With port labels given by their descendant leaf blocks,
different local choices occur at different recovered bridge-tree nodes and
cannot be identified by a leaf-labelled automorphism.  Since at most one
root-extra choice can occur, the exact semi-directed multiplicity is

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{\rm sd}(p)
=M_T+
\sum_{B\in\mathcal A}
(r_B-t_B){M_T\over t_B}.
}
\]

The quantities \(t_B,r_B\) are read from the exact finite local atlas and its
stochastic sheet tests.  They are locally constant off the union of local
Jacobian, denominator, discriminant, and stochastic-boundary loci.

For a compatible semi-directed topology \(H\), let \(\kappa(H)\) be the
number of binary strong compatible rootings.  It is computed exactly by
inserting the root artifact on every candidate tree edge, orienting all tree
edges away from it, and checking the degree, acyclicity, and strong
tree-child conditions.  Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{\rm rooted}(p)=
\sum_{H\text{ counted by }\mu_{\rm sd}(p)}\kappa(H).
}
\]

This distinguishes root-location multiplicity, triangle redirection,
genuinely different semi-directed topology, and continuous parameter fibers.

## Canonical form

Define the canonical code of an observational class as follows.

1. Collapse every invisible `C_root` factor.
2. Reconstruct and canonically encode the leaf-labelled reduced bridge tree.
3. At every nonroot blob choose the lexicographically least code in its `T`
   class.
4. Enumerate every compatible root site and every local root move allowed by
   the exact atlas.
5. Choose the lexicographically least complete semi-directed coloured-graph
   code.

**PROVED.** The move system is finite on every finite network, every step
strictly reduces a finite lexicographic search space or performs finite
minimization, and the if-and-only-if theorem makes the resulting code equal
exactly on observationally equivalent networks.  Hence the canonical form is
well-defined and independent of rewrite order; confluence of an oriented
rewrite presentation is unnecessary.

## Structural reconstruction algorithm

Input an exact generic distribution known to arise from \(\mathcal L_*\).

1. Fourier transform it.
2. Test every nontrivial leaf bipartition by exact flattening rank and retain
   those of rank at most four.
3. Reconstruct the laminar split tree and collapse its unlabelled degree-two
   nodes.
4. Apply positive anchor factorizations recursively to obtain projective
   local tensors.
5. At a degree-three node, evaluate \(F\) to decide ordinary tree versus
   nontrivial cycle.  Degree at least four is not an ordinary binary vertex.
6. For every blob, choose representative leaves from incident descendant
   blocks and evaluate the exact bounded four-, five-, and six-port atlas
   signatures.  Reconstruct its core, port segments, and all segment orders.
7. Normalize `T` at nonroot blobs.  Enumerate compatible root sites and use
   the root atlas to normalize
   \(C_{\rm root},R_3,\Theta,\Omega_{\rm chain}\).
8. Return the least canonical code, the complete compatible topology list,
   and the two multiplicities above.

**PROVED.** The algorithm terminates.  It enumerates leaf splits rather than
all DAGs, and all later topology work uses bounded-size marginals and finite
tables.  Correctness and completeness follow step by step from the cut-split,
projective-factor, local-atlas, and global if-and-only-if theorems.

The direct split search uses \(O(2^n)\) candidate bipartitions, so no
polynomial-time claim is made.  Once the bridge tree is known, the local
stage uses \(O(n^6)\) bounded marginal selections and constant-size exact
atlas lookups.

## Biological consequence

**PROVED.** Under exact infinite-data JC observations, a strongly tree-child
level-2 history in \(\mathcal L_*\) reveals its complete cut-split tree and
every nonroot blob topology except triangle orientation.  All additional
unobservable structure is confined to one root site and is exactly one of
`C_root`, `R3`, `Theta`, or `Omega_chain`.

Thus the strong class has no independently stackable non-orientational bits:
its exponential ambiguity comes from independent triangle redirections,
with at most an additive root-local correction.  This sharply separates the
strong class from the near-tree-child class where `Theta` exponentiates in
every blob.
