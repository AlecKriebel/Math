# Prior-art attribution review

## Verdict: PASS after correction

A separate source audit compared the manuscript's closest-work table and
bibliography against the cited versions.

- Holtgrefe et al.: the manuscript cites their general weak/strong
  semi-directed tree-child framework (Theorem 5 and Corollary 3) and does not
  attribute the new binary-level-2 automatic triangle lemma to them.
- Ardiyansyah, arXiv:2104.12479 (2021): the revised manuscript now cites the
  earlier Fourier-invariant study of simple and semisimple level-2 network
  varieties, including four-leaf cases, as direct historical context. It does
  not portray that work as a global open-stochastic classification.
- Englander et al.: generic identifiability for binary triangle-free strongly
  tree-child level-2 semi-directed JC networks (Theorem 3.2 in the manuscript
  version consulted).
- The JC trinet polynomial is attributed to Englander et al.; the manuscript
  calls its use on restored support labels new, not the polynomial itself.
  Because the preprint numbering changed, the text explicitly labels
  Proposition 2.26 as April-2025-version numbering.
- Brits et al., arXiv:2607.12919v2: full level-1 identifiability modulo triangle
  redirection (Theorem 4.9) and the level-`k` JC trinet inequality (Lemma 5.5).
- Currie et al., arXiv:2606.26673: the cited semialgebraic description and
  full-dimensional triangle-model intersections/differences (Theorem 3.1,
  Corollary 3.3, Lemma 3.4, Theorem 3.5).

The manuscript's all-level-2 wording is always qualified by the binary
standard semi-directed `S_TC` class and JC. It does not claim priority for the
trinet inequality, full pointwise identifiability, richer substitution models,
level greater than two, a positive classification of `W_TC`, equality of
complete stochastic images, or pointwise membership of one input distribution
in every structurally redirected model.
