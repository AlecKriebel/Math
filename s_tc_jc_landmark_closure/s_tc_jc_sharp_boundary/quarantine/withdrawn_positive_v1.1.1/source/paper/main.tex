Jukes--Cantor Networks\\
\large A sharp boundary at weak tree-childness}

\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Phylogenetic networks extend trees by allowing reticulation events, but a topology is scientifically meaningful only to the extent that it is identifiable from the distribution induced at the observed taxa. We study the displayed-tree Jukes--Cantor model on binary standard semi-directed level-2 networks. A semi-directed topology is called strongly tree-child here when every admissible rooting is tree-child. We first prove a structural fact: any binary level-2 semi-directed topology that admits even one tree-child rooting automatically has at most one triangle in each blob. We then show that the exact generic leaf-pattern distribution determines every strongly tree-child level-2 topology up to ordinary triangle redirection, the only topology change that can remain observationally compatible on a full-dimensional model region. More precisely, one-sided full-dimensional stochastic containment occurs exactly when the labelled bridge trees agree and corresponding blobs are isomorphic or triangle-redirected. The proof combines Fourier cut factorization, pointwise cut-split recovery, projective extraction of local boundary tensors, bounded-support reconstruction of cycle and theta blobs, and exact finite atlases. The final atlas case consists of 192 seven-outgoing-port cycle-to-theta completions; established Jukes--Cantor trinet inequalities, applied to restored support labels, separate all of them. Sharpness holds at weak tree-childness: for every number of leaves at least four, two nonisomorphic and non-triangle-equivalent weakly but not strongly tree-child networks share a full-dimensional regular Jukes--Cantor model region. Exact graph, polynomial, sign, and Jacobian certificates accompany the proof.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} phylogenetic networks; identifiability; Jukes--Cantor model; level-2 networks; tree-child networks; algebraic statistics; semialgebraic geometry.
