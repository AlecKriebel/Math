## Why deletion cannot reduce theta-2 to the n=3 theorem

Every theta-2 minimal support has two mandatory reticulation-sink labels and
two repair labels occupying one minimum repair pair.  Delete any one outgoing
label:

- deleting a sink violates sink completeness; or
- deleting a repair leaves only one occupied repair segment, while every
  theta-2 minimum repair has size two.

The exact script checks all 12 single deletions across the three canonical
minimal records.  None retains the theta-2 strong primitive core.

The fixed-full restoration theorem starts from a source core-retaining rigid
support containing every source sink and a complete source repair.  Therefore
its hypothesis is false on every three-outgoing restriction of these sources.
Calling the deleted label a `+1` probe does not help: the audited `+1/+2`
coherence theorem presupposes an already common core-retaining rigid support;
it does not restore a lost source reticulation or source repair.

A different reduction theorem could in principle be proved, but none of the
reviewed gates supplies one.  Such a theorem would itself have to preserve
source-relative openness, bind the restored source role to the same full
relation, and recover the theta-2 core.  It would be mathematically equivalent
in load-bearing content to an n=4 extension gate.
