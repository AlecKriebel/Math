# Independent initial reconstruction

Recorded before any historical audit_a, audit_b, or acceptance verdicts are read. The family is analytic/Dirac/compactness; the claims below are a reconstruction of the candidate, not accepted premises.

The proposed universal strengthening is a normal-Gram rigidity theorem for bounded full-dimensional convex three-polytopes with fixed face lattice and weak interior-angle dominance. Its exact OWR consequence needs only reversal: if every angle of P exceeds its mate in Q, apply the strengthening to Q,P and contradict at least one strict inequality.

The analytic mechanism I reconstruct is the following. On smooth inner approximations of P, construct a smooth sphere map eta homotopic to the outward normal and with positive boundary defect W=(nuclear norm of d eta minus H)_+ tending to zero in L2(surface). Two-active-facet regions are products along the edge and one curved transverse direction; shortening normal arcs therefore makes the nuclear derivative no larger than H. The unavoidable vertex mismatch is hidden in logarithmic annuli whose 2-dimensional surface integral of 1/(r log delta)^2 tends to zero. This uses real dimension 3, where the critical trace is H1(volume) to L4(boundary).

The essential black box is a nonzero harmonic Clifford matrix A satisfying c(nu) A = A c(eta) and an energy inequality E <= (1/2) integral W |A|^2. It cannot be replaced by the polyhedral rigidity theorem, since doing that would assume another version of the central difficulty. To verify the smooth black box I must check the right representation including the transpose in the source, the index sign under eta homotopic to the Gauss map, the allowed flat convex patches and the exact regularity of the kernel. Constant A=I on the Euclidean ball for eta=nu is a necessary sign check.

Assuming the black box and uniform H1 trace/extension constants, normalize ||A||L2=1. Holder and the trace bound absorb the error to give E -> 0. A uniformly bounded extension to a fixed ball has a strongly L2-convergent subsequence. Almost-everywhere convergence of the inner-domain indicators and strong L2 prevent normalized mass from escaping into a narrowing boundary layer. The limit inside P is a nonzero constant C.

On every facet interior there is an unchanged flat patch in every sufficiently late approximation, eta is its corresponding target normal, and a fixed half-ball lies inside every approximation. There the fields converge strongly in H1 because E -> 0, so the boundary condition passes by the fixed-domain trace map. Thus c(n_i)C=Cc(m_i). Adjoint relations imply C*C commutes with the spanning target normal Clifford matrices, hence is a positive scalar identity. C is invertible; anticommutators give every Gram entry. The argument has no metric correspondence assumption and no simplicity assumption, but the geometric localization and smooth sphere-map homotopy must independently withstand nonsimple vertices.

Specific adversarial failure modes to resolve: opposite odd Clifford chirality/right-action convention, index for the wrong projection, smoothness unavailable at flat patches, mean curvature average versus sum, nonuniform trace under moving domains, unjustified boundary indicator convergence, and a weak bulk limit insufficient for facet traces. Finite numeric tests cannot prove the index or compactness assertions.
