2. A smooth flat convex cutoff of facet inequalities creates convex inner bodies, with active normals all making a uniformly positive scalar product with the position vector. On the level set the sum of active cutoff derivatives has a positive minimum. This supplies a regular smooth boundary, despite flat patches. Common inner/outer balls control the polar support functions and hence the raywise maps and inverse maps uniformly in Lipschitz norm. 3. The face-distance estimate can be derived directly from nearest-point projection: for each fixed face type the displacement belongs to the face normal cone and also satisfies every remaining active feasible inequality. Finitely many face types give a positive error-bound constant. Empty face intersections are uniformly separated on compact P. Thus three or more active actual facets are O(lambda^-1) from vertices, including nonsimple ones; a pair not meeting at an edge is likewise relegated to a vertex neighborhood.
4. Away from vertices, the source Gauss map follows one fixed minor great-circle arc for each active pair. Fractional arclength transport to the corresponding target arc has speed beta/alpha <= 1. The shape operator is positive semidefinite and has rank at most one, so its sole nonzero singular value is exactly H. The nuclear norm of the target derivative is therefore (beta/alpha)H. Smooth local angle coordinates, rather than arccos near an endpoint, plus flat vanishing of cutoff derivatives must establish all edge/facet gluing.
5. The fixed smooth map and every target arc lie in one hemisphere with a positive margin. Geodesic interpolation towards that fixed map contracts the derivative in the moving target endpoint: its radial and transverse factors are t and sin(t theta)/sin(theta), each <= 1 for theta < pi/2.
