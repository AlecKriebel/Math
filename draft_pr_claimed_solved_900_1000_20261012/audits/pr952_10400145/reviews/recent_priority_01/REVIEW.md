## Main modern comparisons

**Habiro–Lê, 2016.** The [journal final](https://msp.org/gt/2016/20-5/gt-v20-n5-p04-s.pdf), pp.2687–2693, was read for its exact quantifiers and conventions. Theorem 1.1, p.2690, produces a separate ordinary-Habiro-ring invariant for each simple Lie algebra; Proposition 1.9, p.2693, identifies its Taylor series with the Ohtsuki series. This is a fixed-g theorem. It neither asserts the printed S-valued rank interpolation nor supplies a negative answer in these inspected statements. The [accepted arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03549), dated 24 November 2015, was independently retrieved and hashed, but not close-read in this shard. The final controls this comparison.

**Beliakova–Gorsky, 2024 final.** The controlling source is [Selecta Mathematica 30:101](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-024-00990-y), published 30 October 2024, with the title using “link invariants.” Publisher and [DNB](https://d-nb.info/1355311616/34) downloads are byte-identical, 60 pages. Theorem 9.3 and its proof, p.32, explicitly identify the gl_N 3-manifold invariant with the sl_N Habiro–Lê invariant at fixed N. Proposition 11.5, p.49, concerns interpolation polynomials and includes a rank-dependent factor (−1)^(N choose 2) q^(N choose 3). Corollary 11.6, p.50, displays quantum-factorial denominators in the stable Schur coefficients. Consequently its stability statement cannot simply be renamed as an element of the printed ordinary S. These pages were visually checked. No equivalent negative 7.30 conclusion was identified there. The 43-page arXiv v2, 26 March 2021, is a distinct retained version.

**Fang–Zhou, August 2026.** [arXiv:2608.29585v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29585), submitted 30 August 2026, was inspected in its actual body, especially pp.38–43. Its rank coefficient ring is Iq = {f(A) in Q(q)[A±1] : f(q^n) in Z[q±1] for n≥2}; its inverse limit in §8.4 / Theorem 8.7, pp.39–40, completes in a color variable X with nodes Aq^(2r)+A^−1q^(−2r). This is a knot/symmetric-color object. The figure-eight example on p.40 and Proposition 9.1 on p.41 exhibit denominators, including unbounded order at q=1. This is a concrete ring difference, not evidence that the ordinary S claim succeeds or that its failure was previously established. The preprint's proofs were not certified by this audit.

**Fang, October 2026.** [Quantum Color Newton Rings, arXiv:2610.21102v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21102), has official submission time **15 October 2026, 02:05:19 UTC**. It is available before this audit date; its number was not used to guess its date. Proposition 4.1, p.26, permits arbitrary finite integral rank data through an integer-valued coefficient ring. Its displayed example gives b1(q²)=0 and b1(q³)=1. Proposition 4.2, pp.26–27, identifies the q=1 rank coefficients with Int(Z), and Proposition 4.3 / Theorem 4.4, pp.27–28, concern a color completion over that enlarged ring. These pages were visually checked. The example itself cannot be represented by an ordinary rank-interpolating element with constant Taylor value independent of rank; it illustrates why this is a different coefficient setting. It is not the standard homology-sphere family, so it is not an equivalent negative answer to 7.30. It postdates the packet's 8 October source-metadata checkpoint. No claim about its mathematical correctness is needed here.

**Gukov–Nawata–Saberi–Stošić–Sułkowski, 2016.** The [journal final of Sequencing BPS spectra](https://scoap3-prod-backend.s3.cern.ch/media/files/14609/10.1007/JHEP03%282016%29004_a.pdf), §5.2, printed pp.69–70 / physical pp.71–72, gives an a-deformed expression for a family of integral homology spheres. Its twist expression (5.8) contains a-dependent denominators. The inspected passage does not specify the printed ordinary S or prove an exact impossibility there. I have not inferred convergence, complete normalization agreement, or a general manifold theorem from that passage.
