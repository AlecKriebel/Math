# Independent reconstruction before reading prior family verdicts

This derivation was recorded after the complete final note was read and before family audit conclusions or original historical audit verdicts were read. Independence is clean context for this new whole-package review, not independence from the theorem statement, author-supplied coefficients, task brief, or shared trusted runtime.

The claim is existence of a reduced flat analytic family of nine affine lines in a fixed ball with finite simultaneous normalization, delta sum 36 and rough invariant rising from 7 to 12, together with a classical all-n extension. It concerns an unrestricted summed interpretation of the OWR question and does not satisfy the 2014 unibranch condition.

For three groups X=qY, Y=qZ, Z=qX, same-group pairs meet at three vertices and mixed pairs at [q_i q_j:q_j:1]. At a mixed point exactly the corresponding third line occurs. Nine such points plus three vertices are twelve ordinary triples. Counting 12*3=36 pairs exhausts all pairs of nine lines. L=X+2Y+4Z misses all of them because its values at vertices are nonzero and at mixed points have modulus at least 4-2-1=1. Therefore affine directions are distinct. Substituting Z=(t-x-2y)/4 into homogeneous products yields the displayed F up to a nonzero constant, with the exact listed factors and leading coefficient -65.

The total reduced hypersurface is reducible and nonnormal. Each component is a smooth graph over (y,t); distinct linear prime ideals intersect as their product in the regular local factorial ring. The quotient embeds into the finite product of the component quotients; that product is finite over the quotient, normal, and has its total fraction ring, so it is the integral closure. At fixed t, nine distinct affine lines persist, including the nine distinct directions at t=0. Restriction gives the normalization of every fiber and a smooth projection on the normalized total space. This does not assume irreducibility or unibranchness. Flatness follows independently from t-t0 being a nonzerodivisor: reducing sg=Fh modulo s forces h divisible by s in the ambient regular analytic ring, and cancellation proves torsion freeness over the base DVR.

At vertices norms are at most |t|; at mixed points at most sqrt(2)|t|, hence all intersections lie within radius 1/2 for |t|<1/4. For ax+by+ct=0 the Hermitian closest point is -ct*(conj(a),conj(b))/(|a|^2+|b|^2), perpendicular to v=(-b,a)/sqrt(|a|^2+|b|^2). For B the denominator squared is 1+|4+2q|^2>=5 and for C is |1+4q|^2+4>=13. Thus every line cuts the unit sphere transversely in a circle, with radius sqrt(1-d^2). Pair intersections inside the half ball imply disjoint boundary circles. This is a smooth family of nine boundary circles and nine normalized disks. Open-ball properness is unnecessary; finite normalization survives restriction.

For ordinary r>=3, one blowup separates r transverse branches. Adjunction with E^2=-1 gives K=E, and D=C'+E, so K(K+D)=r-2. Delta is C(r,2). An ordinary node is already normal crossing with no exceptional component and rough zero, agreeing with r-2. Consequently central r=9 gives delta36/rough7, twelve triples give delta36/rough12. K+D has nonnegative exceptional intersection r-2 for these cases.

For every n>=3, the exact same root-of-unity construction has three n-fold vertices plus n^2 triples. All pairs are accounted for by 3*C(n,2)+3*n^2=C(3n,2). The same infinity bound works uniformly and all affine directions are distinct. Component normalization/flatness/boundary proofs apply without a finite-range argument. The rough increase is 3(n-2)+n^2-(3n-2)=n^2-4. At n=2, three nodes plus four triples have delta15 and rough4; the ordinary sixfold central point has delta15 and rough4. No increase there. n=1 is outside the statement and merges nominal vertex/triple descriptions.
