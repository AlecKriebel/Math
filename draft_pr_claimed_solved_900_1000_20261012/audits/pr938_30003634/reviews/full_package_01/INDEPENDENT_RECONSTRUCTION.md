## Group and signature argument

The bound 6 is sufficient; no sharp bound is required. Homogenization is invariant under conjugacy: (tgt^-1)^r=tg^rt^-1 and two defect errors, divided by r, vanish. Thus q(h)=q(a), and q(b)=q(a) because the two Artin generators are conjugate.

For a homogeneous quasimorphism f vanishing on a normal subgroup N, normality gives (gu)^r=g^r u_r with u_r in N. One application of the defect to that product, plus homogeneity, gives |r f(gu)-r f(g)|<=D. No error growing with r is introduced. Dividing by r yields exact constancy on cosets, hence a well-defined homogeneous quasimorphism on G/N. For N=G^(k), that quotient is solvable and amenable; amenable rigidity makes the descent a homomorphism, which would make f a homomorphism. Therefore f is nonzero on every finite derived term. This is a deduction from known theory, not a new general lemma. In particular it does not assert existence in the infinite intersection.
