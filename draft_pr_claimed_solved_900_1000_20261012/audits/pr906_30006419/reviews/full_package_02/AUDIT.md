## A: flat belt and global scalar calibration

In a flat patch with coordinates (s,y), the smaller target set V must have y 1-Lipschitz for the distance of the entire target. Pointwise gradient length alone would be insufficient: a competitor or a shortest path might leave the flat patch. The manuscript explicitly supplies a sufficiently small strongly geodesically convex ball contained in the patch. Its ambient minimizing geodesics remain in the ball, so coordinate differences are bounded by ambient distance. Lifting the angular coordinate on this ball is legitimate.

For u(t,x)=(h(t),x), with 0≤h′≤1, the differential has operator norm one. McShane's infimum extension yields a finite global 1-Lipschitz scalar F in the compact target and agrees with y on V. For any matching-trace metric Sobolev competitor v, the scalar f=F∘v has trace x and squared scalar gradient bounded by the Reshetnyak density. Thus w=f−x belongs to W¹,²₀(Ω). Its weak x-derivative has zero integral by compactly supported scalar approximation, and the cross term vanishes:

`∫|∇f|² = |Ω| + ∫|∇w|² ≥ |Ω| = E⁺²(u;Ω)`.

This is a full comparison against competitors whose images escape V. It neither assumes target-valued smooth density nor imposes continuity, a homotopy constraint, or an energy ceiling. The scalar composition/trace facts are standard for the framework in use. The construction uses this lemma only where the ambient-distance premise is proved.

## A: conformal caps and critical Sobolev boundary invariance

The ordinary area form on a sphere is not globally exact. The cap proof does not pretend otherwise. It shrinks a source neighborhood until its compact image admits a smooth cutoff η equal to one near the image and having integral a less than half the total target area A. The coefficient b=a/(A−a) lies strictly below one. The form ω=((1+b)η−b)dA has coefficient in [−b,1], comass at most one, agrees with dA at the reference image, and has zero integral. On an oriented smooth sphere this last condition gives a global smooth primitive α.

Pointwise, v*ω≤|Jac v|≤‖dv‖op², with equality for the orientation-preserving conformal reference map. This signed comparison remains valid at orientation-reversing, rank-deficient, or zero derivatives. The remaining issue is equality of the form integrals for arbitrary W¹,² matching-trace competitors.

The paper gives a sufficient critical-regularity argument. A finite coordinate cover and subordinate cutoffs represent α as a finite sum of globally smooth scalar functions f_j dg_j. Each is bounded and Lipschitz because X is compact. Locally mollified bounded scalar W¹,² functions establish d(F dG)=dF∧dG distributionally. The one-form convergence follows from strong L² gradient convergence and bounded dominated multiplication; the gradient products converge in L¹. This is scalar approximation and does not require approximation by smooth X-valued maps.

The smooth target admits a smooth embedding; compactness makes the extrinsic and intrinsic Sobolev descriptions locally/comparably valid. Equal traces glue v inside Ω to u outside it as a target-valued W¹,² map on U. The difference β between its pullback of α and u*α lies in L² and is supported in the compact closure of Ω. Pairing dβ with a smooth cutoff equal to one there gives zero, because the cutoff derivative is disjoint from β's support. Its L¹ differential is the difference of the two ω pullbacks. Hence their integrals over Ω agree.

Target excursions and inserted bubbles are covered: exactness and trace gluing eliminate any net ω integral, without a local-image or homological premise. A nonexact area form would fail this argument; the constructed mean-zero exact form is therefore essential. ## B: arbitrary-seminorm angular stress and global holomorphic differential

The angular Jacobian of w=Av/|Av| is det A/|Av|². Homogeneity contributes another |Av|², and the change-of-domain factor contributes 1/det A. Therefore the transformed functional has exactly `(det A)⁻² |A⁻¹w|⁻⁴`, as printed. Both determinant and inverse-length exponents matter. The norm field s(w)² is fixed after the substitution, so differentiating the smooth matrix weight is valid even for nonsmooth, degenerate, and zero seminorms. Fixed-order derivative bounds by C M(s)² hold uniformly in a neighborhood of the identity.

The first derivative is T(s):D with the stated symmetric tracefree angular stress. The derivative is in the domain matrix, not a presumed differentiable norm or bilinear target tensor. Its measurability and L¹ integrability follow from the standard Sobolev approximate differential and finite energy.

For a compactly supported smooth vector field V in a minimizing chart, id+εV is a diffeomorphism for sufficiently small ε of either sign and equals the identity near the chosen subdomain boundary. Precomposition is an admissible matching-trace competitor. Changing variables first holds the differential field at the fixed new point, and the matrix Dφ_ε∘φ_ε⁻¹ has first derivative DV there. This avoids differentiating a merely measurable seminorm field. The derivative domination is integrable. Thus ∫T:DV=0 is justified.

Writing T=[[a,b],[b,−a]], its weak divergence equations are a_x+b_y=0 and b_x−a_y=0. These give holomorphicity of q=a−ib, with the correct sign convention, through distributional Weyl regularity. Scaling and oriented rotations give the unique tensor transformation law. In a holomorphic coordinate change the coefficient transforms by (dz/dw)². Almost-everywhere equal local representatives become a single holomorphic quadratic differential. Each pole has its own ordinary minimizing chart and weak equation; no unsupported puncture-removal inference is used. The entire-plane coefficient decays as O(|z|⁻⁴) by the infinity chart, and hence vanishes. This is the global sphere step that local stationarity on a planar region alone would not supply.

For the normalized nonzero balanced seminorm M=1, choose a direction of minimum m and its orthogonal direction of value L. The triangle inequality gives 1≤L²+m². Reverse triangle inequality gives |s(v)−L|v₁||≤m, and both compared seminorms are bounded by one on the unit circle. The squared difference is at most 2m; the stress kernel has operator norm two. This yields the printed 8m estimate. The rank-one comparison stress is L² diag(1,−1), so 1−m²≤8m and m≥√17−4. Nonzero rank-one seminorms are excluded; zero seminorms remain valid. The exact reciprocal identity gives 4+√17. A fourfold-symmetric non-Euclidean norm can be balanced, so roundness is properly not inferred.

## B: applicability of the older sharper pointwise bound
