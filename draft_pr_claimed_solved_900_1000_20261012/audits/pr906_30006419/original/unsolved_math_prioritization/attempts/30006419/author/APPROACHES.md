# Substantive author approaches

Problem 30006419 / OWR-14299521-012. Two substantive mathematical approaches were developed. Retrieval, source comparison, checking, artifact preparation and later audit are not counted as approaches.

## Approach 1: domain variation and angular stress

Attempted to derive a unique tracefree stress from local domain reparametrizations and then use the absence of holomorphic quadratic differentials on the sphere. The angular change-of-variables formula makes the Korevaar–Schoen density smooth in the domain matrix even when its target seminorm is nonsmooth. The stress vanishes globally. Comparing a normalized balanced seminorm with a rank-one seminorm gives the explicit bound (4+\sqrt{17}). The complete restricted positive argument is in KOREVAAR_SCHOEN.md.

The general-energy extension fails at the unique-stress step. The Reshetnyak density can have nondifferentiable domain variations; choices of local supporting stresses cannot simply be asserted to patch into a global holomorphic differential. The second approach shows that no extension proving the same universal conclusion can be correct.

## Approach 2: scalar-calibrated flat belt and conformal caps

Used the flat-target maps ((t,x)\mapsto(h(t),x)), with (0\leq h'\leq1), which minimize the operator-norm-squared energy through the harmonic scalar coordinate (x). Chose (h'=t^2) near the equator and (h'=1) outside a smaller belt, then compactified the target smoothly and extended the map conformally over both ends.

A global 1-Lipschitz scalar extension handles competitors leaving the flat patch. An exact two-form calibration handles arbitrary Sobolev competitors on conformal patches. The resulting sphere map is smooth, locally (E_+)-minimizing and essentially unbounded in distortion. Its positive global energy defect and a lowering Korevaar–Schoen variation distinguish the hypotheses. The complete counterexample is in PROOF.md.
