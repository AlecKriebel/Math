# PR905 priority audit: bounded historical verdict

Problem 30000717 / OWR-1465-011. Independent priority adversary. Literature cutoff: **12 October 2026**. Audit performed on that local date (UTC timestamps are in `RESEARCH_LOG.md`). The candidate and its frozen source packet were not edited. This is a bibliographic and claim-boundary audit, not a replacement for the independent proof and reproduction audits.

## Verdict

**The inspected primary sources do not supply a verified earlier statement of the exact all-constant tangency obstruction. Historical novelty, first resolution, and continued open status are not established.** The result can be described as an elementary explicit counterexample applying a classical zero-set obstruction, provided the classical ingredients and nearby positive theorems receive precise attribution and the access gaps below remain disclosed.

The original candidate already disclaims exhaustive priority. That disclaimer should remain in the paper, README, archive description and deposit metadata. Do not promote the catalogue label `claimed_solved` into a claim that this was a previously open problem in October 2026. It identifies the claimed answer and its provenance; it is not a verified historical status.

## Exact hypothesis tested

For the classical homogeneous Robinson sextic $R$, let $J_R$ be generated by the tangency minors $x_jR_i-x_iR_j$, and let $K_R$ be generated by the separate cross-products $x_jR_i,x_iR_j$, for $i<j$. The candidate proves, for either $L=J_R$ or $L=K_R$,

\[
R\geq0,\qquad R+\varepsilon\notin\Sigma\mathbb R[x,y,z]^2+I(V_{\mathbb R}(L))\quad\text{for every }\varepsilon\geq0.
\]

The ideal here is the **full real vanishing ideal**, not merely the generating ideal or ordinary radical. An earlier exact match must address this polynomial/variety and this unbounded-degree, finite-SOS nonexistence, or prove a theorem that directly yields it with checkable hypotheses. Ordinary non-SOS of $R$, fixed-degree numerical infeasibility, non-SOS of $R(x,y,1)$, a general existence of some non-SOS function on a curve, or a positive result with an inequality multiplier are not exact matches.

The separate diagnostic $Q=(1-xy)^2+y^2-1/2$ tests only the literal-product reading: its product zero set is the origin, although it takes negative values and has a nonattained infimum. Its underlying nonattaining example is classical. No discovery credit for that polynomial is supported.

## Original statement and attribution

The [publisher report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46100), DOI [10.4171/owr/2007/14](https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2007/14), was independently fetched and the relevant pages rendered. Printed p.810 (PDF page 58) has Theorem 1's exact ordinary-radical condition and the positive-epsilon generating-ideal condition. Printed p.811 (PDF page 59) displays separate products separated by commas and an ellipsis. It does not display tangency differences. Both explicit readings are therefore necessary; this audit does not authenticate an intended correction by Schweighofer. Removing the attainment assumption means allowing all polynomials, not restricting the conjecture to polynomials with a nonattained infimum.

Three ingredient attributions require precision:

The exact cubic lemma is classical, even though the candidate supplies its own calculation. |

The current author-hosted [Reznick reprint](https://reznick.web.illinois.edu/Paper34.pdf) is a 22-page scan with the 2000 AMS imprint and printed pagination 251–272. Its DOI is [10.1090/conm/253/03936](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/253/03936), authenticated independently by Crossref and the [Illinois institutional record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/some-concrete-aspects-of-hilberts-17th-problem/). An earlier 20-page seminar manuscript was also inspected, but it must not be confused with the publisher pagination. The main theorem's additional work is the restriction to zero **lines** and a positive coordinate axis, including handling the allowed constants for positive epsilon. This audit found no authenticated source making that exact application earlier; this does not establish that it originated here.

In the 2010 tangency definition, $M\in f(\mathbb R^n)$ occurs immediately before the shortened wording of Theorem 1.6. The latter must not be used to erase the preceding hypothesis. The more general optimization theorem also imposes hypotheses. In all of these positive certificates, the multiplier term changes what can hold on the candidate's coordinate axis. The candidate's failure modulo an ideal alone therefore does not refute these results.

The geometric obstruction already operates on the subvariety formed by the ten zero lines and one positive axis. The general curve literature supplies context for why noncompact reduced coordinate rings can fail to have positivity equal SOS. It cannot replace the candidate's specific all-epsilon argument. Conversely, replacing an ordinary radical by a real vanishing ideal is not a universal repair: the candidate's theorem already permits the latter. These are mathematical comparisons made in this audit, not historical priority claims.

The complete Hà–Pham 2009 article, DOI [10.1016/j.jpaa.2009.03.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2009.03.014): the publisher identifies open-archive status but its PDF returned 403; the content API returned metadata, not article body. The constrained/truncated scope is authenticated by its publisher abstract; exact contents beyond that were not verified.

4. No author-approved correction of the 2007 product display and no independent proof of continued open status through 2026.

These gaps limit historical promotion. They do not create a mathematical flaw in the explicit argument, and they are not filled by the failure of a search engine to find an exact match. Outside source access or expert historical input could improve coverage; no outreach was prepared or initiated.
