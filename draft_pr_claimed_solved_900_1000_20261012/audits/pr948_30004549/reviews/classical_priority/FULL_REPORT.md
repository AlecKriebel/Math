## Earlier DLZ context and predecessor bridge tests

The full 30-page arXiv:1104.2511v1 body was inspected, including proofs and examples, rather than relying on its abstract. Its unrestricted Conjecture 2.5 is distinct from Theorem 1.1's common-compatible-metric hypothesis. Proposition 3.7 bounds the intersection of anti-invariant spaces for metric-related structures; Corollary 3.11 then supplies bounds at most two. Theorem 3.12 treats non-Kähler or nontrivial-canonical-bundle complex surfaces and gives only zero or one in that metric-related family. Propositions 3.20–3.21 and Remark 3.22 account for the two-dimensional nonintegrable examples. Question 3.23 explicitly asks for three. The later openness and semicontinuity results do not preserve a lower bound of three under arbitrary nonintegrable perturbation.

The 2008 original arXiv:0812.3680v1 was separately inspected at its earlier metric-related examples and exact Question 3.27; the 2011 v2 identifies itself as the published subset, with other material expanded into 1104.2511. Its Example 2.18 retains one anti-invariant form using a fixed metric. Theorem 3.3 gives an upper bound h_J^- <= b^+ - 1 for tamed J; it is not an existence theorem attaining this bound. Large b^+ therefore supplies room, not an automatic counterexample.

Li–Zhang arXiv:0708.2520v4 introduces the cohomological groups and relates compatible and tamed cones. Its cone statements concern a given J and contain hypotheses about a nonempty compatible cone; they do not construct a nonintegrable J with any prescribed large h-minus. Fino–Tomassini arXiv:0811.4765 supplies four-dimensional nil/solvmanifold deformations with small cohomology; its explicit non-almost-Kähler example has a one-dimensional anti-invariant subgroup. Its general purity/fullness results do not provide a large anti-invariant dimension.

Angella–Tomassini arXiv:0909.3604's four-torus deformation is symplectically compatible and yields at most two classes. Its larger construction is six-dimensional. Angella–Tomassini–Zhang arXiv:1211.2928, Section 5, explicitly separates the compact-four-dimensional question from a six-torus family with h-minus four. Its full Theorem 5.4 and proof identify left-invariant cohomology on suitable solvmanifolds and yield h-minus <= n(n-1); in real dimension four this is at most two. No general-theorem bridge to the target was found.

These are earlier tools and related constructions, and should receive appropriate credit in contextual writing. Sharing holomorphic forms, positive planes, cohomological independence, or compact perturbation techniques alone does not establish that the candidate's qualifying existence statement is already in those papers.

## Hind–Tomassini version control

The inherited audit B cites the 19 September 2019 author PDF. That is a historical version, not the final text. The current arXiv record is v3, 7 July 2020; the publisher lists first publication 10 July 2020 and issue May 2021. Both were checked.

The 2019 text's noncompact infinite-dimensional example has superseded formulas. The final v3 Theorem 3.7 gives an explicit exponential sequence satisfying s^2+t^2+t=0, and the acknowledgment records the correction. Final v3 Theorem 3.8 supplies compactly supported nonintegrable perturbations on C^2 with only one closed anti-invariant form, and Corollary 3.9 supplies zero. These are noncompact form-space results, not closed-manifold cohomology examples.

Final Proposition 4.2 and its full proof retain exactly two classes on the Kodaira–Thurston manifold; Remark 4.3 says these examples cannot be metric related to an integrable structure. Proposition 5.1 and its full proof retain holomorphic two-forms from a product of two curves while changing an extra torus factor, obtaining arbitrarily large dimension in real dimension six. Reducing its base to a curve removes the holomorphic two-forms used by that proof. No direct four-dimensional specialization follows. This exact bridge test matters because the use of curves and a torus superficially resembles the candidate.

## Withdrawn Lejmi–Upmeier and genuine later journal work

The live arXiv:1507.00282 history lists v1, 1 July 2015, and withdrawn v2, 3 September 2015. The withdrawal specifically identifies a crucial sign error in the proof of Proposition 14. The full ten-page v1 was read. Its Theorem 1 assumes a compact connected symplectic four-manifold and an omega-compatible J; it claims the positive integrability implication in the almost-Kähler case. Proposition 14, printed p.7, compares the two formulas numbered (22) and (23), and the main theorem on p.8 explicitly uses its relations. Thus the defective dependency is inside the actual proof, not merely a bibliographic warning. No correction of that proof was attempted here.

The author's October 2026 official publication list and targeted searches were used to trace later joint work. The actual CAG 28(7) (2020), 1603–1645 publisher PDF, corresponding to arXiv:1703.01323v1, was inspected in its integrability section and other principal theorem statements. Theorem 3.2 gives integrated scalar-curvature inequalities, with equality characterizing Kähler structures; Theorem 4.8 has conformally almost-Kähler and scalar-curvature hypotheses. Theorem 6.4's ruled-manifold construction starts with a Kähler metric and conformally changes it; “non-Kähler” there does not mean nonintegrable J. This paper is not an authenticated repair or journal publication of the h-minus-at-least-three argument.

The same authors' arXiv:1709.05210v1, published in Tohoku Math. J. 72(4) (2020), 581–594, has actual Theorems 1.1–1.2 requiring constant Hermitian holomorphic sectional curvature (and, in the negative case, J-invariant Ricci curvature). These impose substantial geometric conditions. They supply neither the candidate's counterexample nor a corrected unrestricted h-minus theorem. The searched record did not yield a corrected successor to 1507.00282; this negative observation does not prove one does not exist.
