I first tried to break the construction at its global starting point. The affine curve is nonsingular since simultaneous vanishing of 2y and 6z^5 is incompatible with y²=z⁶−1. In the infinity chart q=y/z³, s=1/z, the two branches q=±sqrt(1−s⁶) extend smoothly. The connected double cover has six simple branch points and genus two. dz/y=dy/(3z⁵) at finite branch points; at infinity the forms are −s ds/q and −ds/q. No pole occurs; their nonconstant ratio z proves independence.

The real-class argument needs an actual global obstruction to exactness, not only pointwise form independence. For H=(a−ib)Ω₀+cΩ₁, Re(H)²=H∧conjugate(H)/2. In complex orientation this is nonnegative and strictly positive wherever H is nonzero: explicitly dz₁∧dz₂∧dbar(z₁)∧dbar(z₂)=4 real volume. A nonzero continuous H has a positive open set. Exactness of Re(H) on the closed oriented product would force its squared integral to vanish by Stokes. Thus all three real classes are independent. This argument uses neither the numerical checkers nor a count of pointwise rank.

For simultaneous normal coordinates, d(w/y)=dw/y−wy' dz/y² and wedging with dz cancels the extra term. Both Ω₀=dζ∧dξ and Ω₁=ζΩ₀ hold in the same chart. Hence γ=uα−vβ with α=du∧dx−dv∧dt and β=du∧dt+dv∧dx. A potential cutoff failure would be an extra derivative in d(Rv dv). It is absent: d(Rv dv)=v dR∧dv because dv∧dv=0. Both potentials have support strictly inside the ball and extend by zero with every jet. The exact perturbations preserve the classes, and the relation γ'=uα−vβ' holds on the entire cutoff region. Direct differentiation also gives dγ'=du∧α−dv∧β'=0, since the extra dv∧dR∧dv vanishes.

I independently expanded β'=du∧dt+(1−R_x)dv∧dx−R_t dv∧dt+R_u du∧dv. With V=du∧dv∧dx∧dt, the Gram entries are 2, R_t, 2(1−R_x), determinant 4D, D=1−R_x−R_t²/4. R_u causes no extra positivity condition; R_v disappears already in dR∧dv. For fixed compactly supported cutoff, all derivatives scale linearly with ε; taking |R_x|≤1/4 and |R_t|≤1 gives D≥1/2 for both signs of ε and at ε=0. These are uniform bounds on the whole ball, not merely the core.

Put s=R_t/2, a=sqrt(D). An explicit full-cutoff coframe, independently checked by expansion, is φ₁=du−s dv+i a dv, φ₂=dx+(i/a)(dt−s dx+R_u dv). Its product is exactly α+i(β'−sα)/a. The four real constituent forms have determinant one. Thus this is a genuine real J, smoothly varying jointly with point and ε, with the sign fixed to J₀ at ε=0. Equivalently its decomposable complex two-form has rank two; nonzero wedge with its conjugate ensures complementary kernels. No argument merely from an unoriented plane is needed. On a collar all perturbation jets vanish and the coframe is the original one, giving a smooth global extension. Zeros of Ω₀ elsewhere never enter the kernel construction. The uniform positive denominator and fixed support give convergence in each C^m norm, hence C-infinity convergence.

On the core, k=1−εx, φ₁=du+i sqrt(k)dv, φ₂=dx+i/sqrt(k)dt. I recomputed dφ₁∧φ₁∧φ₂=k'V/(2k), k'=−ε. Under the same convention, J∂u=k^(−1/2)∂v and J∂x=sqrt(k)∂t. For N(X,Y)=[JX,JY]−J[JX,Y]−J[X,JY]−[X,Y], N(∂u,∂x)=k'∂u/(2k), nonzero already at the origin for either parameter sign. Integrability would force the (0,2) part of dφ₁ to vanish and hence its wedge with the (2,0) coframe to vanish. No converse criterion is assumed. At ε=0 this obstruction vanishes and J=J₀, as required.

Two plausible exclusion theorems fail their hypotheses: the normalized imaginary form has derivative −k' dx∧du∧dt/(2k^(3/2)), so the complex volume form is not closed; and a common compatible positive metric would require g_uu=g_vv=k^(−1)g_vv where k≠1. Thus metric-related DLZ bounds do not apply. b⁺=5 of this Kähler product is compatible with a lower bound three and semicontinuity from initial real h-minus four. This does not establish an exact perturbed h-minus.
