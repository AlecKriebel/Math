# Complete classical specialization and independent checks

This document gives an audit-authored derivation from earlier published mathematical results. It is not represented as a verbatim proof found in a single earlier paper.

## Source premises and dates

T: Talagrand, *Compacts de fonctions mesurables et filtres non mesurables*, Studia Mathematica 67 (1980), Theorem 21, printed pp. 28–29. For a free filter F on a countable set I, F is meager as a subset of `2^I` exactly when there are pairwise disjoint finite sets `E_n` such that every member of F meets `E_n` for all sufficiently large n. The disjoint finite sets need not exhaust I. The original source also explicitly observes that ultrafilters are nonmeager.

L: Medini–Repovš–Zdomskyy, *Non-meager free sets and independent families*, arXiv:1508.00124v1, submitted 1 August 2015, Section 3, pp. 3–4, Corollary 5 and Lemma 8. In the surjective finite-to-one case used here, if F is a nonmeager free filter on J, the filter generated on I by the sets `psi^{-1}[A]`, `A in F`, is nonmeager. This includes arbitrary countably infinite I and J. No special ultrafilter assumption is present. The printed lemma omits a range qualification for its unrestricted wording; that boundary is recorded below and does not affect the surjective application.

M: Sipacheva, *Free Boolean Topological Groups*, arXiv:1612.04878v1, submitted 14 December 2016, Section 8, printed p. 25. The Mathias topology on the finite subsets of omega is the Boolean linear group topology with zero-neighborhood subgroups generated by filter members. This is the exact topology used below, not its invention.

## The exact countable example

Fix any free ultrafilter U on omega. Let `G=[omega]^{<omega}` with addition symmetric difference and zero the empty set. Give G the subgroup base `H_A=[A]^{<omega}` for `A in U`, as in M. Finite intersections of the H_A are H_(A intersection B), so this is a group topology. Cofinite members of U separate any two different supports: remove a coordinate at which they differ. Hence it is Hausdorff. G is countably infinite and every H_A is infinite.

Set `I=G\{empty}` and define its punctured neighborhood filter

    N = { B subset I : H_A\{empty} subset B for some A in U }.

It is a proper free filter. To see it includes all cofinite subsets of I, take a finite forbidden collection of nonempty supports and remove their finite union from omega; no forbidden support is then contained in A.

Define `m:I -> omega` by `m(s)=max(s)`. Its fiber over k has `2^k` elements, so m is finite-to-one and surjective (the singleton `{k}` lies in that fiber). Let

    L = { B subset I : m^{-1}[A] subset B for some A in U }.

Since U is nonmeager, source L gives that L is nonmeager. For every A in U,

    H_A\{empty} subset m^{-1}[A].

Consequently every member of L is a member of N, so **L is a subset of N**, and N is nonmeager as well. This uses monotonicity of meagerness for subsets in the same Cantor cube, not an unproved preservation assertion about arbitrary continuous images.

Suppose `(F_n)` were expansive. Removing the identity from every F_n preserves eventual intersection with each H_A: by pairwise disjointness the identity occurs in at most one block. Put `E_n=F_n\{empty}`. The E_n are pairwise disjoint finite subsets of I. Given B in N, choose A with `H_A\{empty} subset B`; every sufficiently late E_n meets B. By T, N is meager. Contradiction. This directly handles arbitrary finite disjoint blocks; it does not assume they form a partition or that their size is bounded.

Finally G admits a partition into infinitely many dense pieces. Choose pairwise disjoint unbounded subsets `S_j` of the nonnegative integers whose union is all nonnegative integers; for example `S_j={q: v_2(q+1)=j}`. Color a support s by the unique j with `|s| in S_j`. For each coset `s triangle H_A`, every sufficiently large support size q is attained by adding `q-|s|` fresh elements of the infinite set `A\s`. Each color therefore meets every basic coset. The colors are disjoint and cover G. This supplies the exact omega-resolvability hypothesis.

All conclusions are in ZFC. The general nonmeager-filter lemma and the standard topology, rather than only an ultrafilter definition, already provide the central obstruction. The explicit density coloring is an elementary completion of the specialization.

## Independent reconstruction of the published lifting lemma

Let `psi:I -> J` be surjective and finite-to-one, F a nonmeager free filter, and suppose its generated inverse-image filter were meager. Surjectivity makes each inverse image of a filter member nonempty and infinite, so the generated family is a proper free filter. By T there would be finite pairwise disjoint E_n in I eventually meeting every inverse image `psi^{-1}[A]`, A in F. Thus `psi[E_n]` eventually meets every A. For each j in J, membership of j among these images occurs only finitely often, since its fiber is finite and the E_n are disjoint. Greedily choose an infinite subsequence of the images which are mutually disjoint: a finite union of previously chosen finite images forbids only finitely many later indices. The resulting disjoint finite sets eventually meet every A in F, making F meager by T, a contradiction. This proves L independently, including the point-finite-to-disjoint step.

## Boundary and direction checks

1. The identity must be punctured before talking about a free filter. Leaving it in every neighborhood would create a principal common intersection and invalidate the application.
2. Finite-to-one, not merely finite-valued, is needed. For `max` the exact finite fiber count is `2^k`; the `min` map has infinite fibers and would not justify L.
3. The inverse-image-generated filter is contained in N; equality is unnecessary and generally false. Nonmeagerness passes to supersets in a fixed ambient Cantor cube.
4. A failure of H-separability for varying dense sets is not the statement used. The contradiction concerns every finite-block candidate for the constant underlying group.
5. A conditional selective-ultrafilter example is unnecessary. T and L apply to every free ultrafilter.
6. The printed unrestricted Lemma 8 needs its pullback family to be a proper filter. For example, map omega injectively onto the evens and take an ultrafilter concentrated on the odds; the inverse image of the odds is empty. Surjectivity (as for max) removes this defect; more generally a filter-positive range suffices. This is a source-formulation boundary, not a gap in the exact specialization.
7. The principal-ultrafilter case is outside the free-filter hypotheses. The proof does not pretend a finite computation produces a free ultrafilter.

## Optional full equivalence for the standard topology

For any free filter F on omega, the Mathias Boolean group has an expansive sequence **if and only if F is meager**. The nonmeager direction is the argument above. For the converse, T supplies finite disjoint sets I_n of integers eventually meeting every A in F. The finite sets `E_n={{k}:k in I_n}` then eventually meet each H_A. Enumerate G as `(g_j)`. Recursively choose increasing indices `k_n` so that

    B_n = union_(j<=n) (g_j triangle E_(k_n))

avoids all earlier B_i. This is possible because that union is finite and each fixed point can occur in only finitely many translated E_k for the finitely many j involved. For each basic coset `g_j triangle H_A`, B_n meets it once n is large enough that `j<=n` and `k_n` exceeds the threshold for A. Thus the B_n are expansive and mutually disjoint. This equivalence is an audit deduction, not a claim that the full wording appears in an earlier source.

## Why the Cohen-space group-hull route fails

Every infinite countable precompact topological group has expansive blocks. Enumerate it increasingly by finite sets `K_n` and choose translates `B_n=K_n x_n` disjoint from earlier blocks, avoiding finitely many forbidden x_n at each step. Each nonempty open U is left syndetic: `G=F U` for a finite F. For sufficiently large n, `F^{-1} subset K_n`, so every translate K_n x_n meets U. Hence the B_n are expansive.

The Boolean subgroup generated by the Cohen points in the Cantor cube is countable and precompact. The no-groupable-subset theorem for the original space therefore cannot be promoted to the generated group by an inheritance assertion. A new topology and a new obstruction proof would be required. None is supplied by that source theorem.
