# Independent reconstruction and falsification boundaries

This is audit reasoning for the externally pinned report, not a new paper or a priority claim. The claim tested is existence in ZFC of a countably infinite Hausdorff nondiscrete Boolean topological group admitting a partition into infinitely many countably indexed dense sets, with no pairwise disjoint finite-block sequence eventually meeting every nonempty open set. The mathematical conclusions here are deductions. The attached computations only check finite identities and bounded software behavior.

## Coordinate algebra and topology

Identify a finite subset of the natural numbers with its finitely supported indicator function into the field with two elements. Symmetric difference is coordinatewise addition. The field identities at every coordinate give associativity, commutativity, identity the empty support, and self-inversion. Finite initial power sets cover the group; singleton supports witness infinitude. Thus the group is countably infinite.

For any coordinate set A, put H_A = {s finite: s is contained in A}. Coordinatewise cancellation proves that H_A is a subgroup, H_A intersect H_B = H_(A intersect B), and H_A + H_B = H_(A union B). For the latter reverse inclusion, divide a support contained in A union B between A and B, placing coordinates in their intersection on either side. A coset s + H_A consists exactly of the supports x with x outside A equal to s outside A: equivalently x symmetric-difference s is contained in A. Hence two cosets for the same A are equal or disjoint. If s + H_A and t + H_B meet at x, translating by x reduces their intersection to H_A intersect H_B, so their intersection is x + H_(A intersect B). These are universal identities, not extrapolations from the finite check.

Fix a free ultrafilter U on the natural numbers. In ZFC, the cofinite filter is proper; the union of a chain of its proper extensions is a proper filter because any finite collection of members belongs to one extension in the chain. Zorn gives a maximal proper extension. If C is absent, the filter generated by adjoining C is improper, so some old member is disjoint from C. Upward closure puts the complement of C in the maximal filter. Both C and its complement cannot belong. No finite member can belong because its cofinite complement already belongs. This proves precisely the free-ultrafilter existence and decision facts used below.

The cosets with A in U cover the group and refine at intersections by the identity above and finite-intersection closure of U. They therefore form a base. For a basic neighborhood of s+t, represented as (s+t)+H_A, the sum of s+H_A and t+H_A is exactly (s+t)+H_A. This is joint continuity for the product topology, and inversion is the identity. For distinct supports s,t, choose a coordinate k in their difference; the cofinite set excluding k belongs to U. Its two cosets are disjoint, proving Hausdorffness. Each A in U is infinite, so H_A contains infinitely many distinct singleton supports; every basic neighborhood is infinite. Thus the group is nondiscrete. A coset complement is a union of the other cosets of that subgroup, so these neighborhoods are clopen.

The finite signature test separately verifies membership by fixed coordinates and the mixed intersection and sum formulas. In a finite coordinate universe, compatibility of s+H_A and t+H_B is exactly agreement of s and t outside A union B; a compatible representative is (s outside A) union (t outside B). The test does not assign ultrafilter membership to any finite coordinate set.

## Support-size coloring

Every finite support s has exactly one color v_2(|s|+1), a nonnegative integer; this includes the empty support with color zero. The color classes are a partition. For fixed j the admissible sizes are exactly q = 2^j-1 modulo 2^(j+1), with q nonnegative. This arithmetic progression is unbounded. Given a basic coset s+H_A, choose any such q at least |s| and choose q-|s| distinct coordinates of A outside s. This is possible because A is infinite and s finite. Their finite support t belongs to H_A, is disjoint from s, and s+t has size q. If q=|s|, t is empty and the argument still holds. Thus every class meets every basic coset and is dense; each is nonempty. There are exactly countably infinitely many classes.

The new finite test checks a broader elementary size identity: the sizes in s+H_A, for finite A, are exactly the consecutive integers from |s outside A| to |s outside A|+|A|. Indeed the fixed outside coordinates contribute the first summand, while the coordinates inside A can be any subset of A. This is why a finite truncation cannot establish density of all colors: all 65,536 eight-coordinate finite cosets in the new test miss color 4. The closed-form least admissible q at least m is m + ((2^j-1-m) modulo 2^(j+1)). Minimality and its color were checked for 129 sizes and ten colors, but the general modular identity supplies its proof.

## Finite fibers and the universal obstruction

The following abstract lemma isolates the mechanism. Let f:X to N have finite fibers. Let (F_n) be pairwise disjoint finite nonempty subsets of X. The finite nonempty images M_n=f[F_n] are point-finite: for fixed k, each index with k in M_n consumes a distinct point of f^(-1){k}, so at most the fiber size many such indices occur. If J is a finite collection of labels, only finitely many M_n meet J, by a finite union of these bounds.

Consequently there are strictly increasing indices n_i whose M_(n_i) are pairwise disjoint. After choosing finitely many, their union J is finite. The indices whose image meets J, together with the finite initial interval ending at the previous selected index, form a finite forbidden set. Choose any later index outside it; a least one exists in N. This constructs the full infinite sequence by ordinary recursion. It does not choose a set in U, take a countable intersection in U, or use selectivity. No uniform fiber bound and no uniform bound on |F_n| is needed.

For the support group remove the identity before applying f(s)=max(s). Exactly 2^k nonempty finite supports have maximum k, via the bijection t maps to t union {k} from the power set of {0,...,k-1}. This proves finite fibers, including fiber size one for k=0. Distinct supports with the same maximum can occur in different blocks; they give repeated M_n labels up to that bound. The proof never assumes disjoint images before thinning.

For an arbitrary sequence of pairwise disjoint finite group blocks, infinitely many empty blocks are missed by G itself. Otherwise remove a finite initial segment containing all empty blocks. Pairwise disjointness puts the identity into at most one block, so delete through that index also, if it exists. The surviving sequence is infinite and meets the lemma's hypotheses. These deletions do not change the existence of infinitely many unbounded missed original indices.

Apply the lemma and form C_0 and C_1 by uniting images at even and odd selected positions. They are disjoint. A proper filter cannot contain both, so some C_epsilon is not in U; the ultrafilter decision property puts A=N minus C_epsilon in U. Each support of each block on that parity has its maximum outside A, so it is not contained in A. Thus H_A misses each such entire block. H_A is one fixed, nonempty open set for the original sequence, and the selected parity has infinitely many increasing, hence unbounded, original indices. This proves the negation of eventual intersection with every nonempty open set. U was fixed before the sequence, while A is allowed to depend on the sequence, as the target quantifiers require.

The new abstract finite-fiber test exhausts all 81 maps from four points to three labels and all 256 assignments of those points to three disjoint blocks or to an unused label. This is 20,736 map/assignment pairs, independently of any maximum representation. Another fixture has 400 consecutive blocks of sizes 1 through 400, partitioning support encodings 1 through 80,200. Its maxima repeat in many different blocks; greedy selection yields 17 indices from 0 to 362. This finite prefix is a stress test for repeated labels and growing sizes, not a certificate of infinite thinning.

## Boundary cases and failed substitutes

If disjointness is dropped, the constant sequence of supports {{0}} has the same image {0} at every index: the point-finite inference and thinning fail. If point-finiteness is dropped in the abstract lemma, M_n={0} gives the same failure. If image sets can be infinite, point-finiteness alone is also insufficient: encode one label for each unordered pair {i,j} of indices and let M_i consist of all pairs incident to i. Each label occurs twice, but every two image sets meet. Finiteness of every block is used centrally.

A principal ultrafilter is an invalid replacement: if it is principal at k, {k} lies in every subgroup H_A, so the topology cannot separate the identity from {k}. The neighborhood H_{k} also has only two elements and misses sufficiently high support-size colors. These failures were detected in the new finite boundary test. No finite principal ultrafilter is counted as evidence for freeness, its ZFC existence, nondiscreteness, Hausdorffness, or any infinite universal claim.

If one uses only a free proper filter, the group and coloring steps survive, but the last step need not: C outside the filter does not imply its complement belongs. This audit does not replace that central decision property with an unsupported lemma. The ultrafilter's existence is nonconstructive; no numerical experiment in this package constructs one.

## Strongest result and exact gap

The self-contained reconstruction finds no gap in the specified infinite construction or its universal finite-block obstruction under ZFC. This conclusion rests on the arguments above and the pinned report's equivalent mechanism. The new computations verify finite algebra, sizes and finite-fiber consequences only. They do not formally mechanize this argument.
