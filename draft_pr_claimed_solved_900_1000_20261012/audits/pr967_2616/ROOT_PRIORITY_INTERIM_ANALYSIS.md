## Exact selection terminology and implications

For any space X, an expansive sequence of disjoint finite sets exists if and only if X has a groupable dense subset in the ordinary finite-partition sense. In the forward direction delete the finite prefix containing all empty blocks (infinitely many empty blocks defeat X itself); the remaining nonempty blocks partition their union D, and eventual hitting proves D is dense and groupable. Conversely the defining finite partition of a groupable dense subset is the desired sequence. For a countably infinite X, existence of such D is equivalent to X itself being groupable: enumerate X minus D and attach at most one leftover point to each block. Blocks remain finite/disjoint and eventual intersection is preserved. These statements do NOT claim that an arbitrary dense subspace inherits groupability.

H-separability is a stronger selection condition involving EVERY sequence of dense sets. H-separability plus a countably infinite dense partition gives expansive blocks: take finite selections from each of the distinct dense pieces, which makes the blocks disjoint. A source showing only failure of H-separability does not supply the needed failure of expansiveness. A source showing a general nongroupable countable space has not supplied the group structure required here.

## An additional fully proved positive boundary

Every countably infinite precompact group admits expansive disjoint finite blocks. Enumerate G and choose increasing finite E_n exhausting G. Inductively choose x_n so E_n x_n avoids the finite union of previous blocks; only finitely many x_n are forbidden. Every nonempty open U in a precompact group is left syndetic: pick uV contained in U, with V an identity neighborhood; finite K with KV=G gives (K u^{-1})U=G. If F U=G with F finite, then each E_n containing F^{-1} has E_n x intersect U for every x. Therefore E_n x_n eventually meet U. This boundary is an elementary root deduction, not a claim of novelty and not an additional author proof attempt. It shows that a precompact-group result alone needs careful hypothesis checking before it can threaten or reproduce the present nonprecompact counterexample.
