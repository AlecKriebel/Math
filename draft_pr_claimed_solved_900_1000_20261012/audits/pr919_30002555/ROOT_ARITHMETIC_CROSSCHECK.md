# A distinct algebraic check of infinite order

The exact matrix A has trace6/5, determinant1 and A^T A=I. If A had finite order, its two complex eigenvalues would be roots of unity. They would be algebraic integers, so their sum trace(A)=6/5 would be a rational algebraic integer. Every rational algebraic integer is an integer: if a/b in lowest terms solves a monic integer polynomial, clearing denominators forces b to divide a^d, hence b=1. Since6/5 is not an integer, finite order is impossible. This is a universal proof using elementary algebraic-integer closure, not an angle approximation or finite enumeration. The original explicit trace recurrence mod5 independently proves the same fact without algebraic-integer language.

Negative controls matter: rational quarter-turns do have finite order and determinant1 alone does not imply orthogonality. The original arithmetic checkers correctly include these. All powers of the particular A are rotations by the group law in SO(2), so every positive, zero and negative power is admissible. Density of the resulting rotation subgroup is not required in the contradiction and is not being inferred by sampling.

The topological/conformal proof, actual source formulation and priority remain separate from this arithmetic observation. This crosscheck adds no author proof-search turn or new claim of novelty.
