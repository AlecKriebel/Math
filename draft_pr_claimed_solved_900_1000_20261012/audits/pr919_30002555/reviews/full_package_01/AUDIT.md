## Exact theorem and independent mathematical audit

Let `X={0,2/3,1}` and `A=(1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]]`. The claim is that `<A>` is a countably infinite, norm-preserving subgroup of `GL+(2,R)` and no tame infinite-genus translation surface with precisely three ends, all accumulated by genus, has full matrix Veech group containing A. Equality with the prescribed group is therefore impossible. This is an embedded-matrix assertion.

The stronger verified intermediate statement is: on an infinite-genus Riemann surface with finitely many n>=3 nonplanar ends, the full holomorphic automorphism group is finite; for every compatible translation structure the complete rotational affine preimage and its derivative image are finite. This does not say the full affine group or full matrix Veech group is finite.

1. **Uniformization and normalizer.** Infinite genus implies nonabelian fundamental group. A nontrivial free deck group cannot act holomorphically on the sphere, because every nonidentity Möbius map has a fixed point. A free holomorphic deck action on the plane consists of translations, hence is abelian. Thus the universal conformal cover is H and `S=H/Lambda` with nonabelian discrete deck group. Automorphisms lift, giving `Aut(S)=N(Lambda)/Lambda`. If distinct nonidentity normalizers approached identity, discreteness of Lambda would make them eventually centralize fixed noncommuting `a,b` in Lambda. The centralizer of any one nonidentity element of PSL(2,R) is abelian, by its elliptic/hyperbolic/parabolic normal forms. Therefore a and b would commute, contradiction. This is a valid discreteness argument; it does not assume finite generation of Lambda.
2. **Closedness and compact transporters.** Identity isolation for a subgroup implies it is closed: a convergent sequence has pairwise quotients in the identity-isolating neighborhood, so is eventually constant. Properness of the PSL(2,R) action on H follows from its compact rotation stabilizer. A compact-set transporter therefore meets the closed discrete normalizer in finitely many elements. Downstairs, choose finitely many relatively compact `W_j` covering compact K with their closures in evenly covered `V_j`, and choose one local section on each V_j. The finite union of section-images of the compact closures is compact and covers K under projection. It is neither a global section nor the full inverse image of K. A normalizer coset carrying K to intersect K has a representative carrying this compact lift to intersect itself. This proves finiteness of the compact-set automorphism transporter without flat completeness, bounded area or a fundamental-domain compactness assumption.
3. **Finite-ended core under arbitrary homeomorphisms.** Classification identifies the surface with a compact n-holed genus-zero core joined to n one-ended infinite-genus arms. The open arm has an additional intrinsic collar end; the proof explicitly counts only ambient ends of S. If a homeomorphic image of the connected core missed it, the image would lie in one arm. The union of K and all the other **closed** arms is connected, meets K at their cuffs, is disjoint from the image, and carries n-1 ambient ends. It must lie in a single complementary component of the image. Each such component is the homeomorphic image of an original arm, so has exactly one ambient end, even if the homeomorphism permutes all ends. Since n-1>=2 this is impossible. The proof neither assumes every compact subsurface is nondisplaceable nor invokes an infinite-end hypothesis.
4. **The same underlying surface and cone extension.** Translation charts give the complex structure on the actual S. An included finite cone of angle2*pi*k admits a local parameter w with developing coordinate proportional to w^k. A rotational affine homeomorphism is holomorphic on the punctured chart and bounded in a target coordinate near the vertex; removability, and the same argument for its inverse, gives a local biholomorphism. Included finite cones may be infinite in number but discrete. Omitted points stay omitted, and infinite-angle completion points are not inserted as ordinary points. Thus no end space is silently changed by completion or puncture filling. The flat length metric may be incomplete; all analytic arguments concern this same complex surface.
5. **Full preimage, arbitrary kernel, no splitting.** For the derivative homomorphism D, put `I=D^{-1}(SO(2))`. Every element of I is holomorphic by its local unit-complex-linear formula and the cone extension. The identity `D(I)=Gamma(S) intersect SO(2)` follows directly from image/preimage definitions. Taking the full preimage includes the entire translation kernel, and does not require a faithful lift or a section of D. A nonsplit cyclic extension C4->C2 provides an additional exact algebraic control illustrating why a chosen section would be an invalid extra premise. Core nondisplaceability and the finite compact-set automorphism transporter make Aut(S), I and the rotational derivative image finite.
6. **Exact infinite order and admissibility.** `A^t A=I` and `det(A)=1`; all integer powers preserve every Euclidean norm. For `B=5A`, Cayley--Hamilton gives `B^2-6B+25I=0`, so the traces satisfy `u_0=2,u_1=6,u_(m+1)=6u_m-25u_(m-1)`. Modulo5, every positive-index term is1. If `A^m=I` for positive m then `u_m=2*5^m=0 mod5`, contradiction. Negative m reduce by inversion, so all nonzero integer powers are nonidentity and the group is countably infinite. Independently, finite order would make both eigenvalues roots of unity and their sum6/5 a rational algebraic integer, impossible because it is not an integer. The three points are retained Cantor endpoints, form a nonempty finite closed subset, and avoid any empty-end degeneracy.

Falsification boundaries were checked explicitly. For n=1 or2, the end-count contradiction disappears. Finite rotation groups of orders1,2,4 are valid negative controls for an alleged universal infinite-order inference. Determinant1 alone does not imply rotation (for example diagonal2,1/2). The argument excludes this prescribed embedded cyclic rotation group and not every abstract copy of Z with a nonorthogonal generator. No Jacob's-ladder lattice, finite-area lattice, construction, general classification or current-openness conclusion is drawn. I found no circularity or unsupported equivalent reformulation carrying the central difficulty away.
