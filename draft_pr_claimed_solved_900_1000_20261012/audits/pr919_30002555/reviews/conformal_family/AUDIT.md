## Strongest independently verified conclusion

Let R be an infinite-genus Riemann surface. Its full holomorphic automorphism group has finite compact-set transporters. Consequently, if its underlying topological surface has a connected compact nondisplaceable set K, then Aut_hol(R) is finite. For a translation structure on the exact underlying surface, **every** affine automorphism with derivative in SO(2), including every derivative-kernel element, is holomorphic. Therefore I=D^{-1}(SO(2)) is finite, and its image Gamma(S) intersect SO(2) is finite. A three-ended surface with all ends accumulated by genus has the required K. Hence A cannot even belong to Gamma(S).

This conclusion does not require a complete flat metric, finite area, finitely many cone points, a trivial translation kernel, a splitting of the derivative sequence, or an action of the matrix image itself on S. The conformal argument uses the complete universal hyperbolic metric, not the flat completion.

## Detailed adversarial verification

### Uniformization and nonabelian deck group

Uniformization applies to the connected, orientable, boundaryless underlying Riemann surface. Infinite genus cannot be a sphere quotient: a nonidentity Möbius map has a fixed point, whereas deck transformations act freely. A free plane deck group contains only translations, because z -> az+b with a != 1 has a fixed point. Discrete plane-translation groups have rank at most two, so their quotients have genus at most one. Thus the universal cover is H.

The deck group Lambda is discrete and free. It is nonabelian. Equivalently, if it were abelian, the classification of Möbius centralizers would put a torsion-free discrete Lambda in a one-parameter hyperbolic or parabolic group, hence make it cyclic (or trivial). Its quotient would have genus zero. This supplies a direct justification of the author's standard surface-group fact and does not assume an arbitrary compact subsurface injects on fundamental groups.

### Normalizer: nondiscrete and nonclosed subgroup pitfalls

Every holomorphic automorphism lifts to H, giving Aut_hol(R)=N(Lambda)/Lambda. No finite-generation assumption on Lambda is used. Choose just two noncommuting elements a,b in Lambda.

If N were nondiscrete, its identity would be an accumulation point, since it is a subgroup of the metrizable Lie group PSL(2,R). Choose nonidentity n_j -> 1. Conjugation keeps n_j a n_j^{-1} in discrete Lambda and makes it converge to a, so it eventually equals a. The same holds for b. Then a,b lie in the centralizer of n_j. The centralizer of any nonidentity element of PSL(2,R) is abelian: conjugate the element to a rotation, positive diagonal map, or parabolic translation. This also covers possible elliptic elements of N; the deck group itself has no elliptics. We obtain a contradiction. Therefore N is discrete.

An arbitrary subgroup of PSL(2,R) need not be closed or discrete. This proof establishes the needed property specifically for the normalizer. A subgroup with isolated identity is also closed: a convergent sequence of its elements must eventually be constant, since pairwise quotients eventually lie in an identity neighborhood containing no other subgroup element. The author leaves this standard fact implicit in the next step. That omission is expository, not a gap.

### Compact transporters upstairs and downstairs

For z=x+iy in H, let s_z be the matrix [[sqrt(y),x/sqrt(y)],[0,1/sqrt(y)]], which sends i to z continuously. If n sends c in compact C into d in compact D, then n belongs to s_D SO(2) s_C^{-1}, a compact set. The transporter is closed, hence compact. Since N is closed and discrete, its transporter is finite.

Every compact K in R has a compact covering lift L in H. More explicitly, cover K by finitely many W_j with closure(W_j) contained in an evenly covered coordinate neighborhood V_j, choose one local section s_j of each V_j, and take L to be the finite union of s_j(closure(W_j)). This is a compact covering lift, not a claim that K admits a global section.

For f(K) intersect K nonempty, choose any normalizing lift n and witness lifts u,v in L. Then nu=lambda v for some lambda in Lambda, so lambda^{-1}n sends L to meet L. Finiteness upstairs gives only finitely many cosets downstairs. Quotient finite transporters follow without any properness assumption on the original flat metric or any preexisting closedness assertion about the automorphism group.

### Exact surface, finite cones, infinite-angle points, and boundary

On a regular translation chart, every element of I has formula z -> e^{i theta}z+c and is holomorphic. If finite cone points belong to S, use the ordinary coordinate w with z=w^k near an apex. A rotation-affine map preserves cone angle and is holomorphic on the punctured w-disk. Its underlying homeomorphism is continuous at the apex, so the target coordinate is bounded near the puncture; the removable-singularity theorem extends it. Applying the same argument to the inverse gives a biholomorphic extension. This works for any discrete set of included cone points and makes no finiteness assumption on that set.

If finite cone points are instead excluded from S, the translation charts already define a Riemann surface there; they are not silently filled. Infinite-angle completion points have no ordinary surface neighborhoods and are not adjoined. Compactness and ends are always those of the same S to which the core argument is applied. Thus a potential change of ends from filling punctures does not enter the proof. The sources use surfaces without boundary; no boundary reflection or boundary uniformization is smuggled in.

### Core, derivative image, and the matrix witness

Classification identifies the required three-ended, no-planar-end surface with a compact connected three-holed sphere joined to three one-ended infinite-genus pieces. Let K be the central compact subsurface. If h(K) missed K, it would lie in one component of S minus K. The other two ends would remain in the single component of S minus h(K) containing K and the other two arms. This contradicts the three one-end components transported by h. The argument is for arbitrary homeomorphisms, including ones permuting the ends.

Each element of I sends K to meet K. Finite compact transporters therefore make I itself finite. The exact identity D(I)=Gamma(S) intersect SO(2) is immediate from taking the full inverse image; no lifts of a group presentation are chosen.

A is orthogonal of determinant one, so all integral powers preserve every Euclidean vector norm. Infinite order follows independently because (3+4i)/5 cannot be a root of unity: its sum with its inverse is 6/5, a rational noninteger algebraic integer if it were one. The author's integer trace recurrence gives a second valid proof for all positive exponents; negative exponents reduce to positive ones. `CHECKS.py` checks the arithmetic identities and a finite recurrence sample only; the induction and algebraic-integer argument establish the universal claim. A finite three-point closed subset of the Cantor set is permitted.

### Conjugacy and tameness

The optional conjugacy paragraph is correct. For B in GL+(2,R), postcomposition of charts conjugates derivatives by B and preserves the topology. Global norm bounds m|v| <= |Bv| <= M|v| give a bilipschitz identification of completions, but that alone would not establish the required exact cone models.

The extra local argument supplies them: a finite or universal cyclic cover of a punctured disk develops, after B, to the corresponding cover of a punctured ellipse. A sufficiently small round disk centered at the puncture lies inside the ellipse. Its inclusion is a homotopy equivalence of punctured domains, so the restricted cover remains connected with the same finite or infinite degree. In the new pulled-back Euclidean metric, adjoining its apex gives exactly the standard cone neighborhood. The lower bilipschitz bound separates this sufficiently small neighborhood from paths leaving the old cone neighborhood. Thus no hidden global shortcut changes the local model. No conjugacy argument is necessary for the explicitly prescribed A.
