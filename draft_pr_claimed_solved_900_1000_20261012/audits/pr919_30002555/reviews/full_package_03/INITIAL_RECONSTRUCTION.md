The target is universal over embedded countable subgroups of GL+(2,R) without strictly norm-contracting elements and closed Cantor subspaces X, for a tame infinite-genus surface having exactly X as its end space and no planar ends. One admissible pair suffices to negate it. I reconstruct the following stronger obstruction: for any translation structure on a surface with finitely many n>=3 ends, all accumulated by genus, the full inverse image I=D^{-1}(SO(2)) in the affine group is finite; therefore its derivative image is finite. This does not require a section of D or a faithful action by the matrix group.

1. Keep precisely the underlying surface specified by the end condition. Its regular translation charts define a Riemann surface, and included finite cone points extend through a parameter w for which the developing coordinate is proportional to w^k. An affine rotation is holomorphic on the regular locus, extends by removability at those included points, and has holomorphic inverse. Omitted points are not filled and infinite-angle completion points are not made manifold points. The original flat metric can be incomplete.
2. Infinite genus gives nonabelian fundamental group. Uniformization rules out the sphere because its nonidentity holomorphic deck maps have fixed points, and the plane because a fixed-point-free holomorphic affine deck map must be a translation, forcing abelian deck group. Thus S=H/Lambda, Lambda a free, discrete, nonabelian subgroup of PSL(2,R).
3. Every conformal automorphism lifts to a normalizer element; Aut_hol(S)=N(Lambda)/Lambda. If distinct nonidentity normalizer elements approach identity, conjugation eventually fixes two noncommuting elements of Lambda. A nonidentity PSL(2,R) element has abelian centralizer; hence such a sequence cannot occur. The identity is isolated in N. A subgroup with identity isolated is closed as well as discrete: convergent sequences have pairwise quotients eventually in that identity neighborhood and hence are eventually constant.
4. PSL(2,R) acts properly on H because the stabilizer is compact; compact-set transporters meet closed discrete N in finitely many points. A compact K downstairs has a compact lift-cover L made from finitely many sections on relatively compact evenly covered neighborhoods. This need not be a global section. If an automorphism sends a point of K into K, one representative modulo Lambda sends a point of L into L. Hence only finitely many such automorphisms exist.
5. The classification of orientable surfaces identifies this topological type with a genus-zero compact n-cuff core K and n one-ended infinite-genus arms. If h(K) avoided K, it would lie within one open arm. K plus the other closed arms is connected, disjoint from h(K), and carries n-1>=2 ambient ends. It must lie in one complement component of h(K), whereas every such component carries exactly one ambient end because h permutes the ends. Contradiction. Intrinsic collar ends of the arms do not alter ambient end counts. Thus every homeomorphism moves K to meet K.
6. Every element of I is a conformal automorphism and so has the compact transporter property in step 4, while step 5 says every element meets that transporter. Therefore I and D(I) are finite. A possible infinite translation kernel is addressed by the same argument, rather than assumed away.
7. For B=[[3,-4],[4,3]], B^2-6B+25Id=0 and tr(B^0)=2,tr(B)=6. The trace recurrence gives tr(B^m)=1 mod 5 for all m>=1 by induction. Thus (B/5)^m cannot be identity because it would have trace 2*5^m=0 mod5. The displayed A is orthogonal, determinant 1, hence all its integer powers preserve lengths; its subgroup is countably infinite and admissible. X={0,2/3,1} is a closed three-point Cantor subset. Containment of A forces containment of its infinite rotation subgroup, contradicting step 6.

Boundary checks: n=1 and n=2 fail the n-1>=2 step; the argument makes no finiteness assertion there. Finite rotation groups are compatible. Abstract infinite cyclic groups with another nonorthogonal matrix generator are outside the obstruction. No Jacob's-ladder lattice, finite-area, or global realization classification follows.

Optional linear-conjugacy extension: an invertible orientation-preserving linear change of translation charts conjugates derivatives and preserves topology. Bilipschitz equivalence identifies completions but alone does not prove exact conical tameness. A cyclic developing cover of a punctured round disk instead becomes a cyclic developing cover of a punctured ellipse. A sufficiently small round disk inside that ellipse gives a connected restriction of the same covering degree, since its inclusion induces the same fundamental-group generator; pulling back Euclidean distance gives the exact finite/infinite conical germ. Thus this additional local argument supplies the needed tameness bridge.
