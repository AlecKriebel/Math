The question quantifies over actual countable matrix subgroups of GL+(2,R), and
the Veech group is the derivative image, not just an abstract isomorphism type.
OWR Question 4 is on printed page 887 (fresh full PDF page 19). RMV2017 restates
a stronger nested-end question at printed page 530, Question 0.2, and explicitly
says at printed page 532 that the finite-end n >= 2 case remains unknown there,
followed by a positive conjecture. Those are historical 2014/2017 statements.

An independent direct prior bridge is already available in ARSVW v1 (submitted
31 October 2023, announced in the November listing): Theorem 1.6, page 3, says
every translation structure on an infinite-type surface with a finite-type
nondisplaceable subsurface has finite Isom. Section 3.1, page 12, defines Isom as
the full inverse image of SO(2) under the derivative. Thus finiteness immediately
implies finite Gamma intersect SO(2), without a section or trivial kernel.
Theorem 1.8 / Section 4.4 in v1 additionally explicitly covers characteristic
system (alpha,n) with n >= 3; finite n-point end spaces have alpha = 0.
The v1 proof uses proper discontinuity (Corollary 2.21) and Lemma 2.19(2),
the same compact-core mechanism as the candidate. V2 renumbers these as
Theorem 1.8, Corollary 2.19, and Theorem 2.17(2), with the same substance.

For the candidate rotation A = (1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]], A is orthogonal with determinant
1. Its powers are noncontracting in the PSV convention. Its eigenvalue
(3+4i)/5 is not a root of unity: a root of unity plus its inverse is an algebraic
integer, whereas here the sum 6/5 is rational and not an integer. Equivalently
the candidate's integer trace recurrence gives a mod-5 obstruction. Hence an
infinite rotational subgroup contradicts the already stated finite Isom theorem
on any three-ended surface of the required topology.

This establishes direct corollary status from pre-existing results by 2023. It does
not yet establish that an earlier source literally states the OWR negative answer,
uses this rational rotation, or even explicitly states finite Gamma intersect SO(2)
for finite-ended surfaces. Searches for those distinct priority claims continue.
ARSVW positive Veech statements say “isomorphic” and must not be interpreted as
prescribing the original matrix subgroup; the SO(2) obstruction would invalidate
that stronger reading on nondisplaceable surfaces. The negative OWR question and
the possible abstract group realization conjecture therefore need separate framing.
