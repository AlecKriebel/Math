# Complete bridge from earlier results to the exact counterexample

This is an audit derivation, not a new author proof-search turn or a claim that the cited authors printed this Veech counterexample. The robust historical bound is the published APV paper (2021); the same needed lemmas are in its public arXiv v1 (4 July 2020).

Let S be any surface permitted by OWR2014 Question 4 with end space X={0,2/3,1}. Thus S is connected, orientable, without boundary, of infinite genus, and has exactly three ends, all accumulated by genus. The underlying S in this statement is retained throughout.

1. **Compact core.** A connected compact genus-zero core K with three boundary circles can be chosen so that each component U_i of S minus K carries exactly one end and infinite genus. This follows from the usual orientable-surface classification; alternatively APV Lemma 4.1 applies directly because the three-point end space is countable with Cantor–Bendixson degree three. APV defines nondisplaceability using all self-homeomorphisms, not just a subgroup fixing ends. For a direct check, if h(K) misses K, its connected image lies in one U_i. The connected set K together with the other two U_j lies in a single component of S minus h(K), and carries two ends. But h induces a permutation of ends, so each component of S minus h(K) carries exactly one end. Contradiction. Thus every self-homeomorphism carries K to meet itself. No hypothesis that the affine maps fix the ends individually is needed.

2. **The Riemann surface is the same S.** Translation coordinate changes are holomorphic. In the convention including finite cone points, an angle 2πk has a local parameter w in which the one-form is a nonzero constant times w^(k-1)dw; this extends the complex structure across the point. In a convention omitting such points, use the complex structure on the stated S without adjoining points or changing its ends. Infinite-angle completion points are not ordinary surface points and are not adjoined. No completeness of the flat length metric is assumed. PSV2011 p676 makes the included-finite-cone convention explicit; RMV2017 p539 uses its own S and metric completion separately. These distinctions do not change this argument.

3. **A complete hyperbolic metric is available for this complex structure.** Infinite genus gives a nonabelian fundamental group. By Poincaré–Koebe uniformization, the simply connected conformal cover is the sphere, plane, or disk/upper half-plane. The sphere permits no nonidentity fixed-point-free deck transformation. Every fixed-point-free holomorphic automorphism of the plane is a translation; hence a freely acting deck group there is abelian. Thus only the disk remains. Its complete Poincaré metric descends to a complete hyperbolic metric q on S. Completeness here concerns q, not the original flat metric. A holomorphic automorphism of S lifts to a disk automorphism and therefore preserves q. Winkelmann p414 states precisely this classical properness/isometry bridge; DHV Theorem 3.1.4 supplies a full normalizer proof of the exceptional cases.

4. **Apply the older compact-core theorem.** APV Lemma 4.2 states that a subgroup Λ of the isometry group of a complete hyperbolic surface with nonabelian fundamental group is finite whenever there is a compact set nondisplaceable with respect to Λ. Its proof uses the compact-set proper discontinuity in Lemma 2.6. Apply it to Λ=Aut_hol(S) inside Isom(S,q), using K from step 1. Every hypothesis has now been verified. Therefore Aut_hol(S) is finite. This is also the relevant necessary direction of APV Theorem 4.3/Theorem 6.2(3). The proof does not require any choice of a special hyperbolic realization metric from their constructive sufficiency results.

5. **Use the full affine preimage.** Let D:Aff^+(S)→GL^+(2,R) be the derivative map, Γ its image, and I=D^(-1)(SO(2)). Every element of I has local formula z↦λz+b with |λ|=1; it is holomorphic, including across any included finite cone point by removability in local parameters. Thus I⊂Aut_hol(S), and D(I)=Γ∩SO(2). This equality is about the full preimage and works with arbitrary translation kernel. Neither an action of Γ on S, a splitting of D, nor a faithful chosen lift is assumed. I and its derivative image are finite by step 4.

6. **Exact admissible matrix.** Let B=[[3,-4],[4,3]] and A=B/5. Then AᵀA=Id and det(A)=1, so every integral power is a norm-preserving rotation. Put u_n=tr(B^n). Cayley–Hamilton gives u_0=2, u_1=6 and u_(n+1)=6u_n−25u_(n−1). Consequently u_n≡1 (mod 5) for all n≥1. A^n=Id would give u_n=2·5^n≡0 (mod 5), impossible. Negative powers reduce to positive powers by inversion. Hence <A> is countably infinite and has no strictly contracting element, under either the source Euclidean-shortening convention or the spectral convention in DHV. X is a closed nonempty subset of the middle-thirds Cantor set: 0, 1, and the endpoint 2/3 all belong to it.

7. **The exact universal assertion fails.** If Γ contains A, then Γ∩SO(2) contains the infinite subgroup <A>, contradicting step 5. This excludes even containment of this prescribed matrix subgroup, hence excludes its realization as the full matrix Veech group. An abstract infinite cyclic group generated by a different, nonorthogonal matrix is a different assertion. For conjugate prescribed matrices, either postcompose the translation charts and check the cone models, as the candidate does, or run step 5 using the preserved positive-definite quadratic form.

This completes a direct earlier-corollary proof, with no remaining mathematical bridge gap for the exact claimed pair. It does not show that APV, Winkelmann, DHV, or ARSVW literally stated a negative answer to the OWR/RMV question.

The shorter earlier route is ARSVW v1 Theorem 1.6 (31 October 2023), Section 3.1 and the compact core. Its Isom group is explicitly D^(-1)(SO(2)); the v2 numbering is Theorem 1.8. Their geometric definition has finite cone points included, a discrete cone set, and no flat-completeness requirement. The preceding conformal route also covers the omitted-point convention without altering S.
