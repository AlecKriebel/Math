## Actual primary target

[OWR 2014/15](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46504), DOI [10.4171/OWR/2014/15](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2014/15), has Valdez's contribution, joint with Camilo Ramírez Maluendas, on printed pp.885–888. Question 4 is on p.887. I read the whole contribution and actually inspected rendered images of those four pages, including its definitions and neighboring claims.

The question quantifies over **any closed Cantor subspace** X and **any countable prescribed subgroup** G of GL+(2,R) without contracting elements. It asks for a tame surface of infinite genus, with no planar ends and end space homeomorphic to X. It has no self-similarity, translatability, characteristic-system, finite-area, or completeness restriction. A single admissible pair with no realization disproves this universal assertion; a classification of all pairs is unnecessary.

The Veech group is defined there as the matrix image of all orientation-preserving affine derivatives. The adjacent uncountable-group statement expressly allows conjugates; the neighboring countable realization statement specifies equality with G. [RMV2017](https://msp.org/agt/2017/17-1/agt-v17-n1-p15-s.pdf), pp.529–532 and 539, retains the actual derivative-image convention. An abstract group isomorphism is weaker and would not suffice. Its Question 0.2 is broader in allowing a nested pair of end/genus-end spaces; its all-nonplanar specialization contains this exact target.

The witness X={0,2/3,1} is a closed nonempty finite subset of the usual Cantor set. In ternary, 2/3 has expansion 0.2; the endpoints are also retained. Thus this is neither an empty-space artifact nor a forbidden topology. A three-point space is not self-similar: a partition into clopen pieces of cardinalities one and two cannot have a piece containing a copy of the three-point space. None of the self-similar positive results can be silently applied here. RMV's positive Theorem 0.6 has characteristic system (k,1); X has finite characteristic system (0,3), outside that theorem.

This is visible in the page image, not an extraction error. RMV Theorem 0.1 states absence of contracting elements but does not itself define the norm convention explicitly.

To bind that convention I additionally retrieved the cited original [PSV2011](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.2625.pdf), DOI [10.5802/aif.2625](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.2625). Its abstract identifies the excluded set with operator norm less than one; p.674 defines the strict-contraction set U and Theorem 1.1(i) requires disjointness from U. The pointwise strict inequality is understood for nonzero vectors, as the zero vector cannot satisfy a strict inequality. Compactness of the unit circle makes strict shortening in every direction equivalent to Euclidean operator norm less than one. This is not a determinant test and is not a prohibition on shortening even one direction.

For A=(1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]], A-transpose times A=I and determinant A=1. Every integer power therefore preserves every vector length and has operator norm one. The candidate satisfies the actual source convention regardless of the erroneous OWR summary clause.

## Underlying surface, cone points, ends, and affine maps

OWR and RMV compress the distinction between a translation atlas's regular locus and its underlying singular surface. They should not be read as authorizing a change in the end-space condition. The explicit model in their cited PSV2011 §2, printed p.676, is decisive: start with a connected Riemann surface S and a nonzero holomorphic one-form; its zeros are finite-angle singularities **in S**. Tameness says that the missing points of the metric completion are infinite-angle singularities. Infinite-angle completion points are not ordinary points of the conformal surface. In this model ends and genus are those of S, including its finite cone points.

The original proof respects this convention. A finite cone of angle 2πk has a local coordinate w in which the translation developing coordinate is proportional to w^k; its complex structure extends across the vertex. A rotational affine map is holomorphic off the cone set. As a homeomorphism it is bounded in a small target coordinate near an included cone vertex, so removability extends it holomorphically, and the same holds for its inverse. Infinite-angle points need not be added. If a convention instead omits finite cones, the actual punctured surface is already conformal; the proof explicitly does not fill it silently. Under the standard tame model an omitted isolated finite cone vertex gives a planar puncture end, inconsistent with this target's no-planar-end hypothesis.

This bridge uses the same underlying surface in the topological and conformal arguments. It does not require a complete flat metric, a compact metric completion, finitely many cones, positive lower cone separation, finite area, or unit area. Finite cones form a discrete subset of the actual surface, even if they accumulate toward ends. The local argument remains valid in that case.

The relevant source is [ARSVW v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00158v2), §3 and §3.1, pp.13–14. Its Definition 3.1 includes a discrete finite cone set in the connected topological surface, and its Remark 3.2 gives the equivalent holomorphic-form model. Section 3.1 defines its orientation-preserving isometry group precisely as D^{-1}(SO(2)). Consequently the candidate's I(S) is the full group needed by the theorem. It is not merely the image Γ(S)∩SO(2), and it does not require a splitting or trivial translation kernel. By elementary image/preimage algebra, D(I(S))=Γ(S)∩SO(2); an infinite derivative intersection forces an infinite preimage.

## Credited finiteness theorem: exact applicability

ARSVW Theorem 1.8, p.3, says that an infinite-type surface with a nondisplaceable finite-type subsurface has finite isometry group for every translation structure on it. There is no self-similarity, all-nonplanar-end, tameness, finite-area, or complete-flat-metric assumption in this theorem. Theorem 2.18 and **Corollary** 2.19, p.13, give the underlying conformal proper-discontinuity principle; §2's convention, p.9, is exactly finiteness of compact-set transporters. The proof of Theorem 1.8 spans pp.16–17 and invokes the compact nondisplaceability argument of Lemma 2.17(2), p.12. Some internal prose calls 2.19 a theorem; its displayed label is a corollary.

Theorem 2.18 classifies conformal surfaces whose full analytic automorphism group fails this compact-set properness: sphere, plane, half-plane, annulus (including the exterior-disk punctured-disk case), punctured plane, or torus. Infinite genus excludes every listed case. No inference from completeness of the flat metric is used. The original proof separately supplies the required infinite-genus uniformization and normalizer argument, so it does not depend on extending ARSVW's surface convention to a completion.

ARSVW Theorems 1.12–1.14 on Veech realization are phrased up to isomorphism and have additional topology restrictions. They are not universal prescribed-matrix realization theorems. This distinguishes their positive results from the finiteness obstruction already credited by the candidate. The obstruction's underlying theorem is existing literature; no novelty conclusion follows from extracting this rational witness.

## Full original proof crosscheck

1. **Admissibility and all powers.** The exact orthogonality and determinant identities check. For B=5A, the identity B²−6B+25I=0 gives u(n+1)=6u(n)−25u(n−1) for trace u(n). The bases u(0)=2 and u(1)=6 imply u(n)=1 modulo 5 for every n≥1. If A^n=I, trace B^n=2·5^n is zero modulo 5, a contradiction. Negative exponents reduce to positive ones by inversion. Thus the group is countably infinite without a numerical rotation-angle assumption.

2. **Core.** RMV's classification theorem at p.534 applies to connected orientable boundaryless surfaces of equal genus and equal nested end/genus-end spaces. Attaching one-ended infinite-genus surfaces along the boundary circles of a compact n-holed sphere makes a model with exactly n nonplanar ends. It is homeomorphic to every target with those invariants. If a homeomorphic image of the connected core K misses K, it lies in one complementary component. The union of K with the other n−1 end components is connected and disjoint from that image, so a single component of the image's complement carries at least n−1 ends. Each complementary component of an image core must instead carry exactly one end. For n≥3 this is a contradiction. The proof handles arbitrary homeomorphisms, including permutations of ends. The argument has no such contradiction for n=1 or n=2.

3. **Proper discontinuity.** Infinite genus forces nonabelian fundamental group. A sphere cover cannot have a nontrivial free deck group, and a freely acting deck group of the complex plane is translations and abelian. Hence the conformal universal cover is the disk/half-plane with a nonabelian discrete deck group Λ. Its normalizer N is discrete: a sequence of nonidentity normalizers tending to identity eventually centralizes two fixed noncommuting deck elements, impossible because a nonidentity element of PSL(2,R) has abelian centralizer. Aut(S)=N/Λ. Compact lifts from finitely many evenly covered neighborhoods reduce a compact transporter in S to finitely many elements of the discrete group N transporting one compact set in the half-plane to itself. This is a valid compact-set proof even if the original flat metric is incomplete.

4. **Conclusion and kernel.** Every element of I(S) is a conformal homeomorphism and moves K to meet K. Proper discontinuity makes I(S) finite. Its image Γ(S)∩SO(2) is then finite. If Γ(S) contains A it contains all of its infinite cyclic rotation subgroup, a contradiction. Exact equality with G is impossible, and a nontrivial derivative kernel cannot evade the argument.

5. **Conjugacy statement.** If Γ(S)=C G C^{-1}, postcomposing charts by C^{-1} conjugates the Veech group back to G and preserves topology. Bilipschitz equivalence identifies metric completions. The proof correctly adds the necessary stronger local observation: the linear image of a cyclic developing cover over a punctured disk is the same cyclic cover over a punctured ellipse; restriction to a smaller round disk gives the required finite or infinite cone model for the new metric. It does not infer exact conical tameness from bilipschitz equivalence alone. This optional conjugacy extension is sound. Abstract infinite cyclic realizability remains unaddressed.

These checks found no exact remaining source/model or mathematical gap. They preserve the positive one-ended and Cantor-ended cases and do not make any assertion for Jacob's ladder or finite-area lattices.
