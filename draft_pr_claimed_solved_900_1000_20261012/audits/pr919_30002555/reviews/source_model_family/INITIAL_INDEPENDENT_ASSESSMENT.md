## Reconstructed target and success criteria

OWR 2014/15, Valdez contribution (joint with Camilo Ramírez Maluendas), printed pp. 885–888, Question 4 at p. 887, asks a universal existence question over every closed subspace X of the Cantor set and every countable prescribed subgroup G of GL+(2,R) without contracting elements. The requested surface has infinite genus, no planar ends, and end space homeomorphic to X. The whole contribution and its surrounding positive results were read, and pp. 885–888 were actually rendered and visually inspected. No self-similarity condition restricts Question 4. Its preceding theorem permits conjugates for particular uncountable groups; its following theorem says the countable Veech group is precisely G. The report defines the Veech group as the differential matrix image of all orientation-preserving affine diffeomorphisms. Thus merely realizing an abstract group isomorphic to G cannot answer this question.

X={0,2/3,1} is an admissible nonempty closed subspace of the usual ternary Cantor set (2/3=0.2 in base three; endpoints 0,1 are in it). No empty-space convention is needed. Any surface with three nonplanar ends is infinite type. The candidate A=(1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]] is visibly in SO(2); hence every integer power has Euclidean operator norm 1. Countability is automatic for its cyclic subgroup, while infinite order still needs an all-exponents proof. The source excludes strict metric contractions, not matrices with determinant below 1 and not every matrix contracting some direction.

## Source errors and model bridge needing explicit handling

The printed OWR Theorem 1(1), p.886, literally describes matrices satisfying a strict shortening inequality and parenthetically calls this absence of contractions. This is an internal sign/wording error; taken literally it excludes the identity. PSV2011 Theorem 1.1(i), p.674, instead requires disjointness from the strict-contraction set U; its introduction identifies this with norm less than 1. The pointwise inequality should of course be read for nonzero vectors. The candidate is admissible independently of this transcription error because every power preserves every vector length.

OWR and RMV2017 use compressed atlas/metric-completion language. Finite cones and infinite cones must not silently be treated as the same topological objects. The explicit cited model in PSV2011 §2, p.676, starts with a connected Riemann surface S and a nonzero holomorphic form: finite cone points are the zeros in S, while infinite-angle points belong only to the metric completion. Ends and genus of S concern this actual surface, not its entire metric completion and not the regular chart locus with zeros removed. This is consistent with RMV2017's slit constructions, which retain their finite-angle endpoints while claiming no planar ends. If one instead literally uses the regular locus, filling its finite cone punctures is an explicit change of surface and must be justified; in a no-planar-end three-ended target, omitted isolated finite cone punctures cannot occur. There is no basis for inserting infinite-angle completion points into the conformal surface.

ARSVW v2 (arXiv:2311.00158v2; version submitted 2026-02-23, title-page date 2026-02-20) explicitly includes a discrete set of finite conical singularities in its topological surface, with the atlas on its complement. §3.1, p.14, defines Isom(M) as the full preimage D^{-1}(SO(2)), not a chosen lift or split subgroup. It states the conformal extension over finite cone points. Its Theorem 1.8, p.3, requires an infinite-type surface with a nondisplaceable finite-type subsurface; it has no self-similarity, no-planar-end, finite-area, tameness, or metric-completeness hypothesis. Theorem 2.18 and Corollary 2.19, p.13, supply proper discontinuity through conformal uniformization. Corollary 2.19 is a corollary, despite being called a theorem within some proofs. A direct use of this finiteness result therefore depends on verifying the topology and the finite-cone/conformal bridge, not on assuming flat completeness.

ARSVW's Veech realization Theorems 1.12–1.14 are phrased up to group isomorphism, and Theorem 1.12 additionally requires self-similarity and all finite-type subsurfaces displaceable. They do not positively settle the unrestricted prescribed-matrix question. Conversely Theorem 1.8 already makes the proposed rotational obstruction a short consequence once the candidate topology is checked. This observation is source binding and credit identification, not a priority or novelty finding.

## Initial independent assessment

The exact-source binding is strong: the candidate addresses a genuine permitted finite end space and a prescribed embedded matrix group. The main model risk is imprecise treatment of singularities and the source's own compressed notation; the explicit PSV definition resolves the intended standard model. The proposed obstruction is plausible in this model. To accept it mathematically still requires independent checks that a compact connected three-ended core intersects every homeomorphic image, that all rotational affine maps are conformal on the same surface whose ends are prescribed, that conformal automorphisms act properly discontinuously for infinite genus even with an incomplete flat metric, and that A has infinite order. An exact equality up to GL+ conjugacy would also be obstructed after applying a global chart change and proving tameness under that change; abstract isomorphism alone would not be obstructed.

No conclusion about novelty, current openness, separate Jacob's-ladder lattice realizability, or historical author receipts is made.
