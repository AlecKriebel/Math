# Independent reconstruction before consulting prior review verdicts

The exact target is containment of the displayed orthogonal matrix in the full derivative image, on the original connected boundaryless surface with three ambient ends all accumulated by genus. The universal realization statement needs only one counterexample. I treat genus and end hypotheses as essential; the topology does not exclude one/two ends or finite rotation groups. No finite calculation can prove the analytic assertions below.

1. A translation atlas defines a complex atlas on regular points. At an included cone of angle 2πk the local flat coordinate is c w^k; compatibility induces the standard complex parameter w. An affine rotation extends holomorphically: its punctured-neighborhood representation is holomorphic and bounded in the target cone parameter near the mapped cone, hence removable. Its inverse has the same property. An omitted completion point remains omitted. Infinite-angle points are not ordinary complex points. Thus the full SO(2) derivative preimage embeds in holomorphic automorphisms of the SAME surface; incompleteness of the flat metric is immaterial.

2. Infinite genus forces a nonabelian fundamental group. The simply connected uniformization cannot be sphere (a nontrivial Möbius transformation has a fixed point), or plane (a fixed-point-free nonidentity holomorphic affine transformation is a translation; a freely acting plane deck group is abelian). Hence R=H/Λ with nonabelian discrete torsion-free Λ. Choose noncommuting a,b in Λ. A normalizer sequence tending to identity eventually centralizes a and b, by discreteness. A nonidentity element of PSL2R has abelian centralizer (one rotation subgroup, one dilation subgroup, or one parabolic translation subgroup by normal form). If a nonidentity normalizer element centralized a,b then a,b lie in its abelian centralizer, contradiction. Thus identity is isolated in the normalizer. A subgroup with isolated identity is closed: two sufficiently late members of a convergent sequence have quotient in the identity-isolating neighborhood, and coincide. This extra closedness is necessary for finiteness in compact transporters.

3. PSL2R acts properly on H: for compact K,L the elements mapping K to meet L lie in a compact set, using compact point stabilizer and local sections of the transitive action. A closed discrete subgroup therefore has finite compact transporters. To descend, a compact subset of R admits a compact covering lift built from finitely many local sections on relatively compact evenly covered neighborhoods. If a normalizer coset sends K to meet K, multiply its representative by an appropriate deck transformation so that it sends this compact lift to meet itself. Finitely many normalizer representatives imply finitely many cosets. This proves the exact compact-intersection finiteness claimed; mere abstract discreteness would not suffice.

4. Surface classification with n finite nonplanar ends permits a genus-zero compact n-cuff core and n one-ended infinite-genus arms: each arm absorbs all finite genus. For n≥3, suppose a homeomorphic copy of the core avoids the core; connectedness puts it in one arm U_i. The core together with every other closed arm is connected and avoids the copy, so it lies in one component of the complement of the copy. That component contains n−1≥2 ORIGINAL ambient ends, whereas every complement component of a homeomorphic core has exactly one ambient end. The homeomorphism may permute all ends: the count remains one. The intrinsic collar end of an open arm must not be counted as an ambient end. Hence all homeomorphisms have core intersection.

5. Combine 3 and 4 for the full group I=D^{-1}(SO2), which is a subgroup of Aut_hol(S). Every element moves the compact core to intersect itself, so I is finite. Its derivative image is exactly Γ(S)∩SO2, with arbitrary kernel allowed. No splitting or faithful Γ-action is required. If A is in Γ, then all integral powers are in this finite image.

6. For B=[[3,-4],[4,3]], det B=25, trace B=6, Cayley–Hamilton gives u_(m+1)=6u_m−25u_(m−1). With u0=2,u1=6, every m≥1 has u_m≡1 mod5. If A^m=I then u_m=2·5^m≡0 mod5, contradiction. Thus no positive power is identity; negative powers supply inverses, so G is infinite cyclic and countable. A^tA=I and det A=1 imply EVERY power preserves EVERY Euclidean norm. The Cantor membership follows from ternary 0, 0.2, 1=0.222… and finite closedness.
