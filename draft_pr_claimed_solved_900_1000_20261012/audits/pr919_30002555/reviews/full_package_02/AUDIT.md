The exact claim is a prescribed embedded-matrix obstruction: `X={0,2/3,1}` is a nonempty closed Cantor subspace; `A=(1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]]` generates a countably infinite norm-preserving group; no allowed three-ended infinite-genus tame surface with all ends accumulated by genus can even have A in its full matrix Veech group. A single pair disproves the universal existence assertion. A classification of other pairs is unnecessary.

The strongest verified result in the stated setting is that an infinite-genus Riemann surface with finitely many n≥3 nonplanar ambient ends has finite full holomorphic automorphism group. For every compatible translation structure on that same S, its full SO(2) affine preimage and its rotational derivative image are finite. The proof does not show that the whole affine group or whole Veech group is finite.

1. Infinite genus forces nonabelian fundamental group. Sphere uniformization cannot have a nontrivial free holomorphic deck group, because every nonidentity Möbius transformation has a fixed point. A freely acting plane deck group consists of translations and is abelian. Thus S=H/Λ, with nonabelian discrete deck group Λ. Lifting identifies Aut_hol(S) with N(Λ)/Λ. If nonidentity normalizers tended to identity, discreteness would eventually make them centralize fixed noncommuting a,b in Λ. The centralizer of any nonidentity PSL2R element is abelian, from its elliptic/hyperbolic/parabolic normal form. This is a contradiction. Identity isolation makes N discrete and closed: pairwise quotients of a convergent sequence eventually lie in the identity-isolating neighborhood, so the sequence is eventually constant.
2. The transitive PSL2R action on H has compact rotation stabilizer and is proper. More explicitly, continuous matrices taking i to z confine a compact-set transporter to `s(L) SO(2) s(K)^−1`, a compact set. Intersecting with closed discrete N is finite. A compact subset downstairs has a compact covering lift assembled from finitely many local sections on relatively compact evenly covered neighborhoods. A normalizer coset taking the compact set to meet itself has a deck-adjusted representative taking that compact lift to meet itself. Thus the compact transporter downstairs is finite. There is no global-section or compact-fundamental-domain assumption.
3. Classification gives a compact n-cuff genus-zero core and n one-ended infinite-genus arms. If a homeomorphic image of the connected core avoids it, the image lies in one arm U_i. The core together with all the other closed arms is connected, avoids that image and carries n−1 ambient ends. It lies in one complementary component of the image, yet every such component is h(U_j), carrying exactly one ambient end. Arbitrary end permutations do not change that singleton count. The intrinsic collar end of an open arm is never counted as an ambient end. Since n−1≥2, displacement is impossible. This proof is independent of any properness hypothesis and is not circular.
4. Translation charts supply the complex structure on the actual S. At an included finite cone of angle2πk, use a local parameter w with developing coordinate proportional to w^k. A rotation-affine homeomorphism is holomorphic off the apex and bounded in a target parameter by continuity, hence removable there; the same argument applies to its inverse. Discrete finite cones need not be finite in number. Omitted points stay omitted; infinite-angle completion points are not adjoined as surface points. The incomplete flat metric is not replaced by its completion. Hence `I=D^−1(SO2)` lies in Aut_hol(S), with `D(I)=Γ(S)∩SO2` by image/preimage algebra. The full preimage includes arbitrary translation kernel and needs neither a splitting nor a faithful Γ-action. Compact nondisplaceability and finite transporters make Aut, I and its derivative image finite.
5. Direct multiplication gives A^tA=I and det A=1, so every integral power preserves every Euclidean vector norm. For B=5A, Cayley–Hamilton gives the trace recurrence `u0=2,u1=6,u(m+1)=6u(m)−25u(m−1)`. Induction modulo5 gives u(m)=1 modulo5 for every positive m. If A^m=I, then u(m)=2·5^m=0 modulo5, impossible. Negative powers reduce by inversion. Independently, finite order would make the eigenvalue and inverse roots of unity, whose sum6/5 would be a rational algebraic integer and hence an integer, another contradiction. These are all-exponent proofs, not numerical angle checks. Cantor membership uses ternary expansions 0,0.2,0.222… and finite closedness.
