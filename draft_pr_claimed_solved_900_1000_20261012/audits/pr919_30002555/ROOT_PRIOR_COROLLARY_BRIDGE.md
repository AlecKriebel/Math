## Journal route already available in2021

1. Any eligible tame translation surface S is the exact connected orientable boundaryless Riemann surface with finite cone points included, not its possibly nonmanifold metric completion. This analytic model and full derivative-image convention are explicit in PSV2011p676. Infinite-angle completion points are not added as manifold points. Rotational affine maps are holomorphic; extension across included finite cones uses a root coordinate and removability. Thus I=D^{-1}(SO2) lies in Aut_hol(S), with D(I)=Gamma(S)intersectSO2 regardless of derivative kernel.
2. Infinite genus gives nonabelian fundamental group. Classical uniformization supplies S=H/Lambda. The quotient of the complete Poincare metric is a complete hyperbolic metric on this same underlying surface: every geodesic lifts to a complete geodesic upstairs. Every holomorphic automorphism lifts to a real Möbius automorphism of H and preserves that metric. This auxiliary completeness says nothing about completeness of the given flat metric.
3. The finite3-point endspace is countable, its first Cantor–Bendixson derivative is empty, and its degree is3. Aougab–Patel–Vlamis, MathematischeAnnalen381(2021)459–498, DOI10.1007/s00208-021-02164-z, **Lemma4.1p477**, already produces a compact nondisplaceable subsurface. Its proof is the same finite-end partition contradiction. Use its degree criterion, not the adjacent Corollary4.4rank wording.
4. Their **Lemma4.2p477** states that a subgroup of the isometry group of a complete hyperbolic surface with nonabelian fundamental group is finite when it has a compact nondisplaceable set. Apply it to Aut_hol(S), viewed inside the isometry group of the canonical uniformizing metric from step2. All of Homeo(S) cannot displace K, so neither can that subgroup. Aut_hol(S), hence I and its derivative image, is finite.
5. The matrix A=(1/5)[[3,-4],[4,3]] is an infinite-order rotation by the elementary all-exponents modular proof (or rational noninteger trace algebraic-integer argument). All its powers are noncontracting. If Gamma(S) contained A, Gamma(S)intersectSO2 would be infinite, contradiction. The explicit admissible pair X={0,2/3,1},G=<A> cannot be realized.
