# Fresh literature-positioning audit

**Audit date:** 27 August 2026  
**Article audited:** revised K3P level-2 manuscript in the referee package  
**Manuscript location:** `package_copy/proof_package/manuscript/sections/01_introduction.tex`  
**Method:** adversarial comparison against primary sources only: publisher article pages/full text, arXiv or bioRxiv versions, and an institutional repository where useful. No author was contacted. The package was not modified.

## Bottom line

The revised introduction is substantially current and generally distinguishes the surrounding results correctly. I found two actionable literature-positioning issues:

1. **Substantive but local novelty overstatement (must correct):** introduction lines 48--52 include “equality of the three orientation closures” among the “new exact local contribution[s].” Gross et al. (2021), already cited by the manuscript, explicitly state that under JC, K2P, and K3P the ideals of all three-leaf semi-directed triangle networks are identical. Equality of the corresponding Zariski closures is therefore not new. The manuscript’s exact normalized-rank certificate, explicit irreducible eight-term quartic, and common strict smooth physical germ can still be positioned as new refinements.
2. **Minor scope overstatement (should correct):** introduction lines 71--74 can naturally be read as saying that Allman et al. identify the whole class of galled tree-child networks of arbitrary level. Their theorems concern explicitly restricted large subclasses, with binary/topological, generic-parameter, model, and sampling hypotheses. The classes include networks of arbitrary level, but do not comprise all galled tree-child networks.

I found **no uncited 2025--2026 primary source that directly claims the manuscript’s complete K3P, triangle-inclusive, strongly tree-child level-2 containment classification**. That is a negative search result, not a proof of exhaustive novelty. One optional bibliographic update is the 14 January 2026 correction to the Holtgrefe et al. paper; the correction restores an omitted figure panel and does not change its mathematical results.

## Actionable findings

### LIT-1 — A previously published closure equality is presented as new

**Priority:** substantive, local, and readily repairable  
**Confidence:** very high

**Manuscript text:** `sections/01_introduction.tex`, lines 48--52:

> “the new exact local contribution here is a certified normalized rank-14 theorem, an explicit irreducible eight-term defining quartic, equality of the three orientation closures, and a common strict smooth physical germ”

**Primary-source evidence:** Gross, van Iersel, Janssen, Jones, Long, and Murakami, *Distinguishing level-1 phylogenetic networks on the basis of data generated by Markov processes*, Journal of Mathematical Biology 83, 32 (2021), publisher full text: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-021-01653-8>.

In the published full text, the authors state that, under JC, K2P, and K3P, the variety of a triangle or double-triangle semi-directed network is determined by its unrooted skeleton, explaining this by the identical ideals of all three-leaf semi-directed triangle networks. Identical vanishing ideals give identical Zariski-closed varieties. This covers the bare equality of the three oriented triangle closures now listed as new.

The point is independently reflected in the current Brits et al. preprint, which says that for group-based models the leaf-pattern distribution of a three-sunlet is unchanged by placement of the reticulation vertex and cites Gross et al. (2021): <https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12919v3#S4.SS3>.

**Assessment:** The main theorem and the stronger local geometry claimed by the new paper are not displaced. Gross et al. do not, in the passage located, supply the manuscript’s normalized rank-14 certificate, identify the common closure as the particular irreducible eight-term quartic $H_{14}$, or prove the common strict smooth physical germ relative to that hypersurface. Gross--Krone--Martin also only computationally recorded the K3P dimension deficiency, which the introduction already acknowledges at lines 35--38. The correction needed is therefore one of priority and attribution, not abandonment of the local result.

**Recommended replacement for lines 48--52:**

> In the Klein-four/K3P case, we recover the known equality of the three orientation closures and sharpen it by giving a certified normalized rank-14 theorem, identifying their common closure as an explicit irreducible eight-term quartic hypersurface, and proving a common strict smooth physical germ relative to that hypersurface. The global contribution is to place this local geometry inside a complete level-two containment classification.

If a difference in cleanup convention is intended to make the closure-equality theorem genuinely distinct from Gross et al.’s statement, that distinction must be stated and justified explicitly; the present prose makes no such limitation.

### LIT-2 — Allman et al. are described too broadly

**Priority:** minor-to-moderate positioning correction  
**Confidence:** high

**Manuscript text:** `sections/01_introduction.tex`, lines 71--74:

> “Allman et al. obtain identifiability for galled tree-child networks of arbitrary level from quartet concordance factors under gene-tree models”

**Primary-source evidence:** Allman, Ané, Baños, and Rhodes, *Beyond Level-1: Identifiability of a Class of Galled Tree-Child Networks*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 87, 166 (2025), published 22 October 2025: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8>.

The abstract says the results cover “large sub-classes,” not the entire galled tree-child class. The main practical theorem (Theorem 5.7) assumes binary semi-directed networks in the paper’s classes \(\mathfrak C_4\) or \(\mathfrak C_5\), generic numerical parameters, specified gene-tree models, and model-dependent sampling conditions (notably two samples per taxon for the broadest coalescent result). Proposition 5.8 imposes the additional condition that no two hybrid edges attach to the same skeleton-forest edge. The discussion summarizes the identifiable classes as essentially binary, galled, blobwise tree-child, and without small cycles. These classes **include** networks of arbitrary level and non-planar examples; arbitrary level is not a claim that every galled tree-child network is covered.

**Assessment:** The next sentence in the manuscript correctly distinguishes quartet concordance factors/gene-tree models from displayed-tree K3P site-pattern containment. The only problem is the breadth of the topology claim.

**Recommended replacement:**

> Complementarily, Allman et al. obtain identifiability for large, explicitly restricted subclasses of binary galled tree-child networks—including networks of arbitrary level—from quartet concordance factors under several gene-tree models, subject to genericity and model-dependent sampling hypotheses; their data and model are different and do not subsume displayed-tree K3P site-pattern containment.

## Source-by-source scope check

### Gross, Krone, and Martin — three-sunlet dimension deficiency

**Source/status:** *Dimensions of Level-1 Group-Based Phylogenetic Networks*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 86, 90; published 17 June 2024. Official article: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-024-01314-z>. Official Table 1: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-024-01314-z/tables/1>.

**Exact scope found:** The proved general dimension formula is for triangle-free level-1 group-based networks. The paper’s computational table records, for the three-sunlet with general group-based model on \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), deficiency one. The paper identifies this general Klein-four model with K3P.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 35--38 say that Gross--Krone--Martin “computationally record the K3P three-sunlet’s one-dimensional deficiency.” This is exact and appropriately labels the result computational rather than proved in that paper.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Cox, Gross, and Martin — odd-order theorem and even-order computations

**Source/status:** *Group-based phylogenetic models on 3-sunlet networks*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 87, 132; published 18 August 2025. Official article: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01506-1>.

**Exact scope found:** Theorem 1.1 proves the expected affine dimension for the general group-based model on a three-sunlet when the finite abelian group has odd order at least five. Section 4 computationally examines small odd- and even-order groups. The discussion expressly leaves a full even-order understanding open because the proof uses the absence of order-two elements.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 37--38 accurately distinguish a proved odd-order theorem from computational study of even-order cases. They do not imply that the paper proved the K3P/Klein-four case.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Gross et al. — level-one identifiability and triangle orientation equality

**Source/status:** *Distinguishing level-1 phylogenetic networks on the basis of data generated by Markov processes*, Journal of Mathematical Biology 83, 32; published 4 September 2021. Official article: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-021-01653-8>.

**Exact scope found:** The headline identifiability theorem is generic and triangle-free under JC, K2P, and K3P. Separately, the full text explicitly says all three-leaf semi-directed triangle-network ideals are identical under those three models and propagates skeleton-based equality to triangle/double-triangle varieties.

**Manuscript comparison:** The collective level-one summary at lines 31--35 is fair. The omission is in novelty attribution at lines 48--52, as detailed in LIT-1.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Hollering and Sullivant — algebraic-matroid identifiability

**Source/status:** *Identifiability in Phylogenetics Using Algebraic Matroids*, Journal of Symbolic Computation 104 (2021), 142--158. Official arXiv full text: <https://arxiv.org/html/1909.13754>; publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2020.04.012>.

**Exact scope found:** Corollary 5.10 gives generic identifiability results for sufficiently large cycles (cycle length at least four) under K2P and K3P in the one-reticulation/level-one setting. It does not resolve three-cycles or level two.

**Manuscript comparison:** Its inclusion in the broad level-one background at lines 31--35 is accurate. It is not a direct competitor to the triangle-inclusive level-two theorem.

**Confidence:** high.

### Cummings and Hollering — multigraded implicitization

**Source/status:** *Computing Implicitizations of Multi-Graded Polynomial Maps*, Journal of Symbolic Computation 132 (January--February 2026), article 102459. Publisher page: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717125000410>; official arXiv full text: <https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07678>; institutional record: <https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/computing-implicitizations-of-multi-graded-polynomial-maps/>.

**Exact scope found:** The paper computes all degree-two and degree-three K3P invariants for the four-leaf network (reported as 12 minimal quadrics and 64 minimal cubics) and all degree-two invariants for the five-leaf case (648 minimal quadrics). It reports that a prior degree-limited Gröbner computation had failed to obtain the degree-three generators after 100 days.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 44--48 accurately describe low-degree four- and five-leaf K3P sunlet calculations and the 100-day comparison. The manuscript does not misrepresent this as a full ideal or as a level-two classification.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Currie et al. — semialgebraic triangle ambiguity under JC

**Source/status:** *Semialgebraic Conditions for Identifying Triangles in Phylogenetic Networks*, arXiv:2606.26673v1, submitted 25 June 2026: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26673v1>.

**Exact scope found:** The preprint gives complete semialgebraic descriptions of the three oriented, three-leaf JC triangle models and shows that each pairwise intersection and the corresponding set differences are full-dimensional. Thus the oriented models are algebraically indistinguishable but neither identical as physical sets nor generically identifiable from their union.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 39--43 are accurate and importantly distinguish this ambient JC semialgebraic geometry from the K3P germ relative to $H_{14}$. The reference status as an arXiv preprint is correctly encoded.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Brits et al. — pointwise level-one full identifiability

**Source/status:** *On Tree--Network Distinguishability and Full Identifiability of Phylogenetic Networks*, arXiv:2607.12919v3, version dated 25 August 2026: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12919v3>; full HTML: <https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12919v3>.

**Exact scope found:** Theorem 4.9 states pointwise disjointness of the physical model images for distinct level-one semi-directed networks, modulo reticulation placement in triangles, under JC, K2P, and K3P on its biologically reasonable positive/stochastic parameter space with nontrivial mixing. The paper defines semi-directed networks using exhaustive suppression of degree-two vertices and identification of parallel edges. Corollary 4.10 describes intersections when mixing boundaries are admitted.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 53--57 accurately state the theorem’s pointwise scope, models, and triangle quotient, while explicitly flagging the cleanup-convention translation and standard-admissibility restriction. I found no literature overclaim there.

**Confidence:** high for the cited theorem; medium-high for exact equivalence of conventions, because that equivalence also depends on the revised manuscript’s internal graph definitions rather than literature alone.

### Holtgrefe et al. — level-two distinguishability from displayed quartets

**Source/status:** *Distinguishing Phylogenetic Level-2 Networks with Quartets and Inter-Taxon Quartet Distances*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 87, 168; published 23 October 2025. Official article: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01549-4>. Correction published 14 January 2026: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01564-5>.

**Exact scope found:** The paper studies semi-directed, outer-labelled planar, galled level-two networks. A canonical form characterizes exactly when members of that class are distinguishable by displayed quartets; the canonical form captures most, but not always all, network features. The inter-taxon quartet distance is shown circular decomposable in the relevant setting, providing foundations for inference from quartet information.

**Manuscript comparison:** Calling this a “complementary partial result” at lines 59--62 is accurate. It is combinatorial/quartet-based, class-restricted, and does not give the K3P site-pattern containment classification claimed here.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Englander et al. — triangle-free strongly tree-child level two under JC

**Source/status:** *Identifiability of Phylogenetic Level-2 Networks under the Jukes--Cantor Model*, bioRxiv DOI 10.1101/2025.04.18.649493. Version history in the official bioRxiv API: <https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493/na/json>. Version 4, posted 4 July 2026: <https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493v4>. Institutional record: <https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/identifiability-of-phylogenetic-level-2-networks-under-the-jukes-/>.

**Exact scope found:** The main full-topology result is generic identifiability under JC for binary, semi-directed, triangle-free, strongly tree-child level-two networks. The paper also proves pointwise distinguishability under JC and K2P when displayed-quartet sets differ, yielding recovery of features such as the tree of blobs and circular orders in broader classes. The bioRxiv API lists v1 (24 April 2025), v2 (9 October 2025), v3 (23 December 2025), and v4 (4 July 2026), with no journal publication recorded as of the audit date.

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 62--67 state the main model/class restrictions and accurately describe the pointwise JC/K2P quartet tools. “Removing triangle-freeness and classifying the resulting triangle ambiguity” is a defensible statement of the present advance. The bibliography accurately labels v4 and its revision date.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Martin et al. — practical four-taxon invariant method

**Source/status:** *Algebraic Invariants for Inferring 4-Leaf Semi-Directed Phylogenetic Networks*, Systematic Biology 75(4), 657--672; first published online 14 October 2025, issue publication July 2026. Official article: <https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/75/4/657/8285810>.

**Exact scope found:** The paper develops an algebraic-invariant method for inference of four-leaf/four-cycle semi-directed networks under JC and K2P, evaluates it on simulations, and applies it to biological sequence data (Xiphophorus).

**Manuscript comparison:** Lines 68--71 accurately position it as practical finite-data inference rather than an exact local-to-global K3P containment classification.

**Confidence:** very high.

### Allman et al. — galled tree-child identifiability from concordance factors

**Source/status:** *Beyond Level-1: Identifiability of a Class of Galled Tree-Child Networks*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 87, 166; published 22 October 2025. Official article: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8>.

**Exact scope found:** Strong identifiability results hold for large subclasses of binary galled networks whose blobs satisfy tree-child and cycle/attachment restrictions. Theorem 5.7 is stated for classes \(\mathfrak C_4\) and \(\mathfrak C_5\), with generic numerical parameters and model-specific sampling/data assumptions under the displayed-tree and two network multispecies-coalescent variants. Proposition 5.8 has an additional attachment restriction. The covered classes can have arbitrary level and need not be planar.

**Manuscript comparison:** The model/data non-subsumption statement at lines 73--75 is correct. The class description at lines 71--73 should be narrowed as in LIT-2.

**Confidence:** very high.

## Direct 2025--2026 competitor search

I searched primary-source domains for combinations of “K3P,” “Kimura 3-parameter,” “level-2,” “semi-directed,” “strongly tree-child,” “identifiability,” “containment,” and “phylogenetic network,” with particular attention to arXiv, bioRxiv, Springer/Bulletin of Mathematical Biology, Journal of Mathematical Biology, Journal of Symbolic Computation, and Systematic Biology. The closest current works found were:

- Brits et al. (level one, JC/K2P/K3P, pointwise full identifiability);
- Englander et al. (level two, full topology under JC only and triangle-free; broader pointwise quartet tools under JC/K2P);
- Holtgrefe et al. (displayed-quartet canonical form for outer-labelled planar galled level-two networks);
- Allman et al. (quartet concordance factors under gene-tree models for restricted galled tree-child classes, including arbitrary-level examples);
- Martin et al. (four-taxon practical JC/K2P inference);
- Currie et al. (three-leaf JC semialgebraic triangle geometry);
- Cummings and Hollering (low-degree K3P sunlet implicitization); and
- the older Ardiyansyah preprint on invariant distinguishability for restricted simple/semisimple level-two networks, already cited by the article: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12479v1>.

None of these primary sources claims a complete regular full-dimensional containment classification for binary standard semi-directed strongly tree-child level-two networks under K3P, including triangles and a continuous-time counterpart. I did not locate a missing direct 2025--2026 competitor. Because search indexing and unpublished work can never establish a universal negative, confidence in this conclusion is **high but not absolute**.

## Referee conclusion on literature positioning

Subject to correcting LIT-1 and narrowing LIT-2, the revised literature discussion supports the manuscript’s claimed position: the strongest neighboring level-two site-pattern theorem is triangle-free and JC-only; the K3P triangle-local algebra had dimension evidence and known orientation-closure equality but lacked the manuscript’s claimed exact hypersurface-and-germ refinement; and the other higher-level results use displayed quartets or gene-tree concordance factors on different topology classes. No additional literature-based obstacle to the global K3P novelty claim was found.
