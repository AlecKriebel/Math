# Attempt 2: Induced linear characters and exact Fourier fibers

Status: no general resolution. The attempt makes an induced-character route precise, proves two positive cases, and isolates the exact inequality that is missing for the remaining case. It does not assume that Adams operations commute with induction; they do not do so in this naive form.

Let H <= G and let lambda be a linear character of H. With A_G as in Attempt 1, the induction formula, conjugation equivariance of primary decomposition, and an interchange of finite sums give

A_G(Ind_H^G lambda)
 = |H|^{-1} sum_{x in G, x_{p'} in H} conjugate(lambda(x_{p'})).    (1)

For completeness, evaluate the induced character at x_{p'} as |H|^{-1} times the sum of lambda(a^{-1}x_{p'}a) over a carrying x_{p'} into H. In the outer average over x, replace x by axa^{-1}. Each a then contributes the same sum; its |G| occurrences cancel the outer denominator |G|. This proves (1).

The p-power-order part of lambda is identically 1 on p-regular elements. Let lambda_0 be the p'-order part, of order m. Fix a primitive m-th root zeta. For j mod m, let

T_j = #{x in G : x_{p'} in H and lambda_0(x_{p'}) = zeta^j}.

Equation (1) becomes the finite Fourier coefficient

A_G(Ind_H^G lambda) = |H|^{-1} sum_j T_j zeta^{-j}.    (2)

## Galois symmetry produces a concrete integer inequality

If a is coprime to |G|, then x -> x^a is a bijection of G, its inverse being an appropriate power map. It maps T_j to T_{aj}, since primary decomposition commutes with powers. Every unit modulo m can be lifted to a unit modulo |G|: choose residues avoiding primes dividing |G| but not m, using the Chinese remainder theorem. Thus T_j depends only on gcd(j,m).

For d dividing m, put t_d = T_{m/d}; this is the count for each individual primitive d-th root of unity in the image of lambda_0. Summing the primitive roots gives the Mobius function, so

A_G(Ind_H^G lambda) = |H|^{-1} sum_{d|m} mu(d) t_d.    (3)

This is an integer because Psi_G is a generalized character and Ind_H^G lambda is a character. Its integrality must not be confused with nonnegativity.

When m is a prime ell, the condition is particularly sharp:

A_G(Ind_H^G lambda) = (t_1 - t_ell)/|H|.

Thus this induction route would need a fiber-domination statement t_1 >= t_ell. A bare appeal to nonnegative fiber sizes does not give that inequality.

## Cases that the formula actually proves

1. If lambda has p-power order, then m=1, every summand in (1) is 1, and the coefficient is a nonnegative integer. In particular, an irreducible character induced from a p-power-order linear character cannot be a negative-coefficient witness. The same holds if a positive integer multiple of an irreducible character is so induced, by dividing its nonnegative coefficient by that positive integer.

2. If H is a p'-group, the restriction of Psi_G to H is the permutation character of the conjugation action of H on the set G_p of p-elements of G. Indeed, every h in H is p-regular and its fixed points on G_p are exactly C_G(h)_p. Frobenius reciprocity now identifies (1) with the multiplicity of lambda in this permutation character. This is nonnegative. It proves the fiber inequalities for this case, but relies essentially on every element of H being p-regular.

These arguments do not imply that every ordinary irreducible character is a nonnegative combination of these induced characters. Brauer induction permits negative coefficients, and its signed expression cannot establish the required positivity.

## Exact, small examples

The accompanying checker builds permutation groups directly, rather than using an externally supplied character table.

- G=S4, H=A4, p=2, lambda a nontrivial character of A4/V4 of order 3: T=(16,4,4), so the induced-character coefficient is (16-4)/12=1. The induced character here is the degree-2 irreducible of S4. The labeling of A4/V4 is verified multiplicatively for every pair of elements.
- G=S5, H=C6 generated by a disjoint 3-cycle and transposition, p=2, lambda of order 3: T=(56,2,2), giving coefficient (56-2)/6=9. This checks the formula even when H contains p-elements; it does not prove it is positive for all such H.

For S3, S4 and S5 at every prime divisor of their orders, the checker also compares every primary-map fiber with the independent centralizer p-element count. These finite checks validate the formulas and normalizations, not the universal conjecture.

## Remaining obstruction

For a general subgroup H containing p-elements and a linear character with nontrivial p'-part, the conjugation permutation character on G_p agrees with Psi_G only on H's p-regular elements. Deleting its values at p-singular elements is not an operation known to preserve the positive character cone. Formula (3) expresses the exact missing positivity as an alternating Mobius sum; no injection or representation-theoretic decomposition proving the needed fiber domination has been constructed here.
