# Low-twist branch-integral symmetric differentials on the plane

Problem 30003253, queue row 1830, OWR-15170-020. Research date: 20 October 2026.

## Status and scope

**PARTIAL PROGRESS; the original open-ended surface criterion is not claimed resolved.** Five substantive avenues below yield exact existence algorithms, sharp subclass results, and explicit geometric sufficient conditions. These do not characterize when an arbitrary surface morphism has a useful differential, do not decide every positivity cone, and do not provide a general list of integral curves. No novelty priority is asserted.

The authentic question is Garcia-Fritz's Question 1 on printed p. 2841 of *Definability and Decidability Problems in Number Theory*, Oberwolfach Reports 13 (2016), no. 4, 2793–2866, [official report](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/15170), [official PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46652). It asks for hypotheses on a complex surface and a morphism to P² ensuring a sufficiently low-twist symmetric differential integral along every branch component, in order to control curves of geometric genus bounded by a specified g. It does not fix an order or a numerical inequality. The catalogue's scalar-section reformulation is not equivalent. The higher-dimensional question is separate.

Throughout, B is a nonempty reduced plane divisor with homogeneous equation F of degree b. Its irreducible components have equations F_i. Write Ω=Ω¹_P². An order-r differential of twist k means a nonzero element of H⁰(P²,Sym^r Ω(k)). A curve is integral when its pullback to the normalization, then to the r-th power of the normalization's canonical bundle, is zero. This is equivalent to generic tangent vanishing, also for a singular curve. It does not mean all coefficients vanish on the curve, and is not a statement about torsion in singular Kähler differentials.

The main exact optimization studied here is the **minimum absolute twist**

k_min(B)=min{k : some r≥1 and nonzero ω∈H⁰(Sym^r Ω(k)) make B integral}.

This is our precise auxiliary problem, not a quantity specified in the informal source. It differs from minimizing k/r and from optimizing an upstairs genus/ramification inequality.

## Avenue 1: first-order logarithmic obstruction and smooth-branch sharpness

### 1.1 Exact first-order result for SNC divisors

Suppose B=Σ_{i=1}^c B_i is simple normal crossings, with degrees b_i and total b. Set K_B=ker(Ω→⊕_i Ω¹_{B_i}), viewing the target as pushed forward to P². Then

K_B=Ω(log B)(−B).

This is an equality of subsheaves of Ω. At a smooth branch point B=(x=0), both sides are generated by dx and x dy. At a crossing B=(xy=0), both are generated by y dx and x dy. Off B there is nothing to check.

Twist the residue sequence by t=k−b:

0→Ω(t)→Ω(log B)(t)→⊕_i O_{B_i}(t)→0.

For t<0 both outer H⁰ spaces vanish, so no integral first-order differential exists for k<b. At t=0 the connecting map C^c→H¹(Ω)=C sends (a_i) to Σ_i b_i a_i times the hyperplane class. Thus

h⁰(K_B(b))=c−1.

The Euler sequence gives H⁰Ω(t)=0 for t≤1, H¹Ω=C, and H¹Ω(1)=0. Therefore, for one smooth irreducible branch B,

min{k:H⁰K_B(k)≠0}=b+1;

for c≥2 SNC components the minimum is b.

At k=b the complete residue family is

ω=Σ_i a_i(F/F_i)dF_i,   Σ_i b_i a_i=0.

At k=b+1, the first-order space has dimension 2 per line component and 3 per component of degree at least two. For a smooth irreducible B, it is spanned by

ω_ℓ=ℓ dF−bF dℓ.

Choose ℓ not proportional to F when b=1. For b≥2 smooth irreducible, every nonzero ℓ works. This form has coefficient degree b, zero Euler contraction, and restricts tangentially to zero on B.

The residue boundary's Chern-class interpretation is classical; see Graf–Kovács, Lemma 3.5, [*An Optimal Extension Theorem for 1-Forms and the Lipman–Zariski Conjecture*](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/DMJDMV/vol-19/27.pdf). These first-order facts are not asserted as new.

### 1.2 Independent Jacobian description, including singular B

Write ω=A·dx with homogeneous coefficients deg A_i=k−1 and x·A=0. For every reduced F, generic tangent vanishing on B is equivalent to componentwise divisibility

F | A×∇F.

Euler's identity x·∇F=bF gives mutually inverse maps

A=x×Q,   Q=(A×∇F)/(bF),   Q·∇F=0.

Thus the first-order integral space is exactly AR(F)_{k−2}, the Jacobian-syzygy module in degree k−2. This remains valid for singular reduced B, with integrality understood on normalization. For smooth B of degree at least two, the three partials form a regular sequence, and Koszul syzygies first occur in degree b−1, independently confirming k=b+1. See Dimca–Sernesi, equation (3.1), [*Syzygies and logarithmic vector fields along plane curves*](https://jep.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jep.10.pdf), for the standard logarithmic-syzygy framework.

### 1.3 Higher-order extension for a single smooth branch

Do not replace K_B by Sym^r Ω(log B)(−B): that is wrong for r>1. Locally at x=0 the actual kernel is generated by

x(dy)^r, dx(dy)^{r−1}, …, (dx)^r,

whereas the proposed logarithmic expression introduces poles in terms with at least two dx factors.

For smooth B, the correct exact sequence is

0→Sym^r Ω(k−b)→K_{B,r}(k)
 →i_*[O_B(k−b)⊗Sym^{r−1}(Ω|_B)]→0,

where K_{B,r}=ker(Sym^r Ω→i_*(Ω¹_B)^{⊗r}). The sequence follows by restricting to B and taking the kernel of the symmetric power of the rank-two conormal quotient. The conormal line is O_B(−b); multiplication by it injects its tensor product with Sym^{r−1}(Ω|_B).

For r≥2, Euler's injection Ω|_B→O_B(−1)^3 embeds the last sheaf into O_B(k−b−r+1)^N. It has no sections for k<b+r−1. At the boundary k=b+r−1, a section is a constant tensor T∈Sym^{r−1}C³ lying in Sym^{r−1}(ker x) for every point x∈B. Equivalently x⌟T=0 for every x. A nonlinear irreducible plane curve spans P², so contraction by every vector vanishes. Since r−1>0, T=0. The first sheaf also has no sections: its twist k−b≤r−1 is below the universal threshold 2r proved below. Consequently

k≥b+r  for every nonzero branch-integral order-r form when r≥2 and B is smooth nonlinear.

The r=1 boundary is handled by the residue argument above, not by the constant-tensor argument, which would be invalid for degree-zero tensors. Together with k≥2r for a line, this proves the all-orders result

**For every smooth irreducible plane curve B of degree b, k_min(B)=b+1.**

This is an exact obstruction to reducing absolute twist by increasing order in that class. It does not by itself preclude a useful genus-zero inequality upstairs.

## Avenue 2: finite exact optimization, for fixed order and over all orders

### 2.1 Complete incidence-polynomial model

Let x=(x₀,x₁,x₂), let a=(a₀,a₁,a₂), and put η=x×dx. Each η_i is a section of Ω(2), and x·η=0. Euler and the rank-two identity T≅Ω(3) give

0→O(−1)→O³→Ω(2)→0.

Taking the r-th symmetric power of the quotient by a line, and twisting by d, yields

0→Sym^{r−1}C³⊗O(d−1)
 →Sym^r C³⊗O(d)→Sym^r Ω(2r+d)→0.

The first map is multiplication by x·a. Since H¹(P²,O(n))=0 for every integer n, the global sections are exactly the bihomogeneous polynomials P(x,a) of bidegree (d,r), modulo (x·a)Q of bidegree (d−1,r−1), mapped to P(x,x×dx).

For d<0, both polynomial spaces have no sections. Therefore

**H⁰(Sym^r Ω(k))=0 for k<2r.**

For k=2r+d, d≥0, the dimension is

binom(r+2,2)binom(d+2,2)−binom(r+1,2)binom(d+1,2)
=(r+1)(d+1)(r+d+2)/2.

There is also an elementary proof of the lower bound: restricting tangentially to any line gives O_P¹(k−2r). If k<2r it is zero, and every point and tangent direction arises from a line, forcing the original symmetric tensor to be zero.

### 2.2 Exact branch-integrality equations

For an irreducible reduced homogeneous equation F_i, one has

B_i is ω-integral  ⇔  F_i divides P(x,∇F_i).

At a smooth point x of B_i, its tangent-line dual coordinates are ∇F_i(x). The cross product x×v for a nonzero tangent vector v is a nonzero scalar multiple of these coordinates. Homogeneity of degree r in a proves the equivalence on a dense open set. Normalization-integrality follows because a regular section on a smooth curve vanishing generically vanishes identically. The incidence relation causes no ambiguity: x·∇F_i=b_i F_i, so changing P by (x·a)Q changes the evaluation by a multiple of F_i.

All these divisibility conditions are linear in the coefficients of P. A linear Gröbner normal-form map modulo each F_i gives a finite exact matrix. A merely univariate remainder routine with nonconstant leading coefficient is not an adequate substitute: the normal-form map must be linear over the coefficient field. Equivalently, one may use the entire reduced F and impose F|P(x,∇F); on each component the extra factors in ∇F are generically nonzero. Factoring is convenient but not logically essential.

A basis of the incidence quotient is given by the bidegree-(d,r) monomials not divisible by x₀a₀, using x₀a₀=−x₁a₁−x₂a₂. Kernel dimension greater than zero is necessary and sufficient for existence. Alternatively, one can work with arbitrary homogeneous symmetric coefficients and linear Euler horizontality constraints; the incidence model removes those relations in advance.

### 2.3 Termination and exact global minimum

For any reduced nonempty B, choose a linear ℓ so that ℓdF−bF dℓ≠0. This gives a first-order form of twist b+1 integral on every component. Its product with r−1 nonzero pencil forms of twist 2 gives a nonzero order-r form of twist b+2r−1. The product is nonzero because symmetric forms over the function field form a polynomial domain.

Thus the minimum twist for any fixed r is found by the finite search

2r≤k≤b+2r−1.

More strongly, the global minimum absolute twist over all r is found by the finite search

2≤k≤b+1,   1≤r≤floor(k/2).

The first successful k is precisely k_min(B). The argument is not limited to fixed r: the universal upper bound b+1 and the universal lower bound 2r jointly bound the order of any minimizer.

This is an effective theorem for equations over a computable algebraic-number field, with exact polynomial and linear algebra. It is not an algorithm for arbitrary unspecified, uncomputable complex coefficients. The supplied script implements rational coefficients only; extending its backend to algebraic-number fields is not claimed implemented. It rejects empty, nonhomogeneous, constant, nonreduced, shared-component, and zero-order inputs. Squarefree reducible equations are accepted. Negative controls and two invertible coordinate changes on three examples are recorded separately.

### 2.4 Exact authored examples

`checks/branch_integrality.py` computes exact rational ranks and witnesses, saved to `checks/exact_results.json`. It is a small reproducible realization, not a production general-purpose CAS. Cases include:

- a line: minimum 2, order 1;
- a smooth conic: minimum 3, order 1;
- Fermat cubic, quartic, quintic: minima 4, 5, 6, order 1;
- coordinate triangle: minimum 3, order 1;
- five or seven distinct tangent lines to a smooth conic: minimum 4, order 2;
- five concurrent lines: minimum 2, order 1;
- six lines x+t y+t³ z=0, t=1,…,6: minimum 5, order 2;
- the two smooth conics x²+y²+z² and x²+2y²+3z²: minimum 4, order 1;
- ten lines x+t y+t⁴z=0, t=1,…,10: minimum 7, order 2 (order 3 also has existence by the dimension bound).

The six-line example has no triple intersections: the determinant of three dual points (1,t,t³) is a nonzero Vandermonde factor times the positive sum of the three parameters. Its six dual points lie on no conic: the evaluation exponents 0,1,2,3,4,6 give a nonsingular generalized Vandermonde matrix at distinct positive parameters. The rank calculations independently verify the relevant nonexistence spaces. The ten-line example is likewise SNC: three-point determinants are a Vandermonde factor times the positive complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree two. Its exact lower-twist ranks certify nonexistence for every k≤6, not just a dimension prediction.

These examples verify the declared finite instances, not any uncomputed genericity or asymptotic assertion.

## Avenue 3: duality, sharp normalized twist, and explicit complete classes

### 3.1 All forms at the universal threshold

At k=2r, the coefficient model reduces to ω=H(x×dx), with H a homogeneous degree-r polynomial in three dual variables. There are no incidence relations in x-degree zero.

For a nonlinear irreducible branch C, let C^∨ be its dual curve and G_C its reduced defining equation. Then C is ω-integral exactly when G_C divides H. For a line L, its tangent-line dual coordinates are constant, namely the dual point [L], and integrality is exactly H([L])=0.

Suppose B has nonlinear components C₁,…,C_s and line components L₁,…,L_t. Set G=∏G_{C_i}, q=deg G. Let Z be the dual points [L_j] not already on G=0. Then all threshold forms are

H=GJ, with J vanishing at every point of Z.

If α(Z) is the minimum degree of a nonzero homogeneous polynomial vanishing at Z, with α(empty)=0, the least threshold order is q+α(Z), except that r must always be positive. For nonempty B this expression is already positive. Distinct nonlinear components have distinct dual curves by biduality in characteristic zero.

Consequently **every reduced plane branch divisor admits a nonzero branch-integral differential with k/r=2**, and no smaller normalized twist is possible. This answers that auxiliary slope problem exactly. It does not make the absolute k small: a smooth degree-b curve has dual degree b(b−1), so its direct tangent web has large order and twist, while first order already realizes k=b+1.

The degree-zero web interpretation is classical; Cavalier–Lehmann, *Global structure of holomorphic webs on surfaces*, Banach Center Publications 82 (2008), Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 7.3, give the degree/twist convention and incidence description: [publisher PDF](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/86271). Our elementary coefficient proof does not require a nonzero discriminant assumption or remove repeated factors; the original symmetric-differential question permits those.

### 3.2 Conic tangents: sharp all-orders minimum 4

For at least five distinct lines tangent to a smooth conic, the dual points lie on a smooth dual conic. Its equation H gives an order-two form of twist 4 integral on every line. No three of the tangent lines are concurrent, since from a point there are at most two tangents to a smooth conic. Thus the arrangement is SNC.

First order requires twist b, where b is the number of lines. For k<4 only r=1 is possible, by k≥2r, and that first-order space is zero when b≥5. Therefore k_min(B)=4 exactly. This gives an explicit class in which higher symmetric order strictly beats the first-order SNC bound.

All integral curves of H(x×dx) are the tangent lines together with the envelope conic: a nonlinear integral curve has its dual contained in the dual conic, so biduality forces it to be the envelope. This is not just a statement about generic nonsingular leaves; the discriminant/envelope curve must be retained.

This very conic-tangent differential and its integral curves appear in Garcia-Fritz–Urzúa, [*Families of explicit quasi-hyperbolic and hyperbolic surfaces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07671), §4, Corollary 4.3, published Math. Z. 296 (2020), 573–593. That corollary imposes cyclic-cover conditions and b>4m. It is an established geometric sufficient family, not a newly solved general question.

A caution against automatically upgrading integrality to hyperbolicity: for an even number of tangent lines, their product restricted to the envelope conic is a square. The double cover therefore has two rational components over the conic. Resolving the crossings of the branch lines does not eliminate these curves. In particular, the envelope cannot be discarded merely because it is not a branch component.

### 3.3 Dimension-based abundance for line arrangements

For a line L, the restriction map from H⁰(Sym^r Ω(2r+d)) to H⁰(L,O_L(d)) imposes at most d+1 linear conditions. Therefore b distinct lines always admit a nonzero integral form whenever

(r+1)(d+1)(r+d+2)/2 > b(d+1),

or equivalently (r+1)(r+d+2)/2>b.

This is a sufficient dimension count, not a statement that all line constraints are independent. For example r=3,d=1 gives 24 unknowns and at most 20 constraints for ten lines, hence twist 7. It illustrates why first-order residue obstructions cannot be extended unchanged to higher symmetric order.

## Avenue 4: pencils and actual smooth surface morphisms

For coprime homogeneous P,Q of the same degree d, define

ω=Q dP−P dQ ∈H⁰(Ω(2d)).

Its Euler contraction vanishes. It is nonzero because P/Q is a nonconstant rational function in characteristic zero. Every irreducible component of every pencil member λP+μQ=0 is integral. Divide the coefficient gcd G to obtain a primitive form of twist 2d−deg G. Off the finite base locus and coefficient degeneracy set, integrality is exactly d(P/Q)=0, so every integral irreducible curve is a pencil-fiber component. A product of primitive pencil forms has integral curves exactly the union of the constituent fiber-component families.

Thus arbitrarily many branch fibers can be integral for a fixed twist. Their common basepoints are essential, however: a large plane branch degree does not justify ignoring exceptional divisors after resolution.

A smooth generically finite sufficient family is supplied by a fibration base change. Let β:S→P² resolve a degree-d pencil with simple basepoints, f:S→P¹ the resulting fibration. Take a finite map h:C→P¹ from a smooth curve of genus h_C>g, ramified away from critical values of f. Then X=S×_{P¹}C is smooth, and π:X→P² is a generically finite morphism. The branch components are the pencil members over branch values of h. Any curve Γ⊂X mapping nontrivially to C has normalization mapping finitely to C, so Riemann–Hurwitz gives g(Γ)≥h_C when h_C≥1. Hence every curve of genus at most g is contained in a fiber.

For the line pencil this is a smooth ruled surface over C. The exceptional section is not rational when h_C≥2; it has genus h_C. Direct local calculation gives the saturation of π*ω after removing twice the exceptional section plus the pullback of Ram(h), leaving the differential f_X*Ω¹_C inside Ω¹_X and twist f_X*K_C^{-1}. This is precisely the correction concealed by a naive plane-degree argument.

For a general degree-d Lefschetz pencil whose singular members are irreducible one-nodal curves, all fiber components have genus at least (d−1)(d−2)/2−1. If this lower bound is greater than g, the same construction has no curves of genus at most g. This requires the stated pencil properties, not merely the phrase “general branch divisor.” The morphism π is not finite, because the pulled-back exceptional sections contract to pencil basepoints. The source question does not explicitly require finiteness, but a theorem for finite morphisms cannot silently use this construction.

See the separate `PENCIL_COVER_AUDIT.md` for the expanded saturation calculation and exact hypotheses. These are a concrete sufficient class, not a general classification or a priority claim.

## Avenue 5: ramification valuations and a bounded genus-budget decision criterion

### 5.1 Exact local order, and why order r cannot be multiplied in blindly

In a local product ramification model x=u^e,y=v, write

ω=Σ_{j=0}^r a_j(x,y)(dx)^j(dy)^{r−j}.

If m_j=ord_{x=0}(a_j), with infinity for a zero coefficient, the exact divisorial vanishing order of the pullback is

γ=min_j {e m_j+j(e−1)}.

The differential monomials in du,dv are independent, so different j cannot cancel. Branch integrality means m₀≥1; therefore γ≥e−1. But γ need not be r(e−1): dx(dy)^{r−1} already has exact order e−1. Even a form with only a high normal term and an x-multiple tangent term can have its gain limited by the latter's order e. Thus the upstairs improvement depends on the full valuation data, not on r alone.

The basic e−1 theorem and the degree-negativity mechanism predate the source question: Garcia-Fritz's 2015 thesis, Proposition 3.42 and Theorem 3.87, [institutional full text](https://qspace.library.queensu.ca/bitstreams/007eb1de-ecf4-4747-a756-f7cac789ba56/download). We use them as established transfer tools, not as a new solution to existence.

### 5.2 A precise conditional finite search

Let π:X→P² be a dominant generically finite morphism from a smooth projective surface, H=π*O(1), and let R be an effective divisor for which branch-integrality guarantees π*ω vanishes along R. For example, one may use the sum of (e_D−1)D over divisors mapping onto branch components. Suppose a rational number a is given together with a verified nefness certificate for R−aH. Curves contracted by π are treated separately.

For a noncontracted curve Γ with normalization of genus q≤g, the pulled-back differential after dividing by R has target line degree at most

(k−a)(H·Γ)+2r(q−1).

For g≥1, the strict inequality

k+2r(g−1)<a

is sufficient to make this negative, since H·Γ is a positive integer. Hence every such Γ is integral for the saturated differential. Pullback along a nonconstant map of normalized curves in characteristic zero is generically injective, so its image in the plane is integral for ω. Ramification and contracted components are not forgotten; the latter require independent treatment.

Given B, g≥1, and such a certified numerical budget a, existence of a branch-integral differential meeting this **specific sufficient inequality** is decidable by finite exact linear algebra. Indeed k≥2r implies 2rg<a, so only finitely many r and k need testing. A negative result means this specified budget criterion cannot be met; it does not prove that every version of the differential method fails. Nefness of R−aH is an input here, not a generally solved computation.

For g=0, k≤a is a sufficient uniform condition because the canonical contribution is −2r. This criterion also has a finite search, because 2r≤k≤a. Positive k−a cannot be compensated uniformly without bounding H·Γ; no unjustified degree bound is assumed.

The cyclic tower theorem of Garcia-Fritz–Urzúa 2020 supplies one rigorously established way to control resolution discrepancies under its SNC and congruence hypotheses. Its positivity coefficient is the one in its theorem, not a casually substituted smooth-cover coefficient. It assumes the branch-integral differential exists. The incidence algorithm supplies an exact test for that part once the other hypotheses and a bound are specified.
