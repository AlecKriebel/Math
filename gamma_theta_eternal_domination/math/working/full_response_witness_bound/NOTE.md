# A five-vertex witness bound for a full response at \(k=3\)

## Status and scope

Date: 2026-07-27 (PDT)

This note records a human proof extracted from a small UNSAT core.  The
proof is independent of the SAT calculation.  It uses only ordinary
domination and the fact that three specified triples dominate; it does not
use eternal closure beyond the usual reason those triples are present.

All complements below are denoted by

\[
H=\overline G.
\]

The theorem is conditional.  Its specialization to the exact
separated-port core gives an order floor for that one response pattern, not
a global counterexample-order exclusion.

## 1. Neutral vertices

Let

\[
S=\{a,b,c\}
\]

be a dominating set of \(G\), and let \(x\notin S\).  Assume:

1. \(\gamma(G)\geq 3\);
2. \(x\) is adjacent in \(G\) to each member of \(S\); and
3. each of

   \[
   \{x,a,b\},\qquad \{x,a,c\},\qquad \{x,b,c\}
   \tag{1.1}
   \]

   dominates \(G\).

Define the set of vertices outside \(S\) that are \(G\)-complete to \(S\):

\[
Q_S=\{q\in V(G)-S: qs\in E(G)\text{ for every }s\in S\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus \(x\in Q_S\).

### Theorem 1.1 (five-vertex witness bound)

Under the assumptions above,

\[
\bigl|V(G)-(S\cup Q_S)\bigr|\geq 5.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
|V(G)|\geq |Q_S|+8.
\tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

For each \(i\in S\), the pair \(\{x,i\}\) does not dominate \(G\), because
\(\gamma(G)\geq3\).  Hence there is a vertex

\[
u_i\in N_H(x)\cap N_H(i).
\tag{1.5}
\]

No \(u_i\) lies in \(Q_S\), since every member of \(Q_S\) is
\(G\)-adjacent to \(i\).  Moreover, for every \(j\in S-\{i\}\),

\[
u_i j\notin E(H).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Indeed, otherwise \(u_i\) would be adjacent in \(H\) to all three members
of the dominating triple \(\{x,i,j\}\) from (1.1), so that triple would
miss \(u_i\) in \(G\).  It follows in particular that

\[
N_H(u_i)\cap S=\{i\}
\tag{1.7}
\]

and that \(u_a,u_b,u_c\) are pairwise distinct.

Apply \(\gamma(G)\geq3\) again, now to each pair \(\{i,u_i\}\).  Choose

\[
z_i\in N_H(i)\cap N_H(u_i).
\tag{1.8}
\]

The vertex \(z_i\) is not:

- a member of \(S\), by (1.7) and the absence of loops;
- \(x\), because \(xi\in E(G)\);
- a member of \(Q_S\), because every member of \(Q_S\) is
  \(G\)-adjacent to \(i\); or
- one of \(u_a,u_b,u_c\), again by (1.7) and the absence of loops.

Consequently, the three vertices \(u_a,u_b,u_c\) and every \(z_i\) lie in

\[
A=V(G)-(S\cup Q_S),
\tag{1.9}
\]

with

\[
z_i\notin\{u_a,u_b,u_c\}.
\tag{1.10}
\]

Suppose \(|A|\leq4\).  There is then at most one member
\(z\in A-\{u_a,u_b,u_c\}\).  Equation (1.8) forces

\[
z_a=z_b=z_c=z.
\]

Thus \(z\) is adjacent in \(H\) to all of \(a,b,c\), which says exactly
that the dominating set \(S\) misses \(z\) in \(G\).  This contradiction
proves \(|A|\geq5\). \(\square\)

## 2. Eternal-family interpretation

Suppose now that

\[
\gamma(G)=\alpha(G)=\gamma^\infty(G)=3
\]

and \(S=\{a,b,c\}\) is an independent triple.  Every maximum independent
set belongs to every eternal triple-family, so \(S\) is retained and
dominates.  If \(x\) has the full response list at \(S\), then all three
one-guard successors in (1.1) belong to the family and therefore dominate.
The moves also show that \(x\) is \(G\)-adjacent to every member of \(S\).
Thus Theorem 1.1 applies to every full-response vertex.

This formulation is deliberately family-relative: “full response” means
that all three actual successor states are retained, not merely that the
three graph edges from \(x\) to \(S\) exist.

## 3. Exact separated-port consequence

In the exact nine-vertex separated-port core, the outside vertices are

\[
x,r,s,q,v_0,v_1,
\]

and the prescribed induced complement has no edge from \(S\) to any of
these six vertices.  Equivalently,

\[
\{x,r,s,q,v_0,v_1\}\subseteq Q_S.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If \(x\) has the full response list and \(\gamma(G)=3\), Theorem 1.1 gives

\[
|V(G)|\geq |S|+|Q_S|+5\geq3+6+5=14.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Corollary 3.1

No graph of order at most \(13\) can realize the exact induced
separated-port core together with \(\gamma(G)=3\) and the full response
list at \(x\).

This improves the earlier order-\(11\) cap-and-escape floor for this exact
pattern.  It does not exclude other response-list obstructions at order
\(13\), and it does not raise the campaign's global certified frontier.

## 5. Exact boundary

The proof forces five non-neutral witnesses but does not characterize their
mutual adjacencies.  It therefore does not by itself:

- rule out the exact core at order \(14\);
- connect the witness layer to the terminal 2-SAT obstruction;
- eliminate the even bow-tie/cycle return found in the cap ladder; or
- prove the complete \(k=3\) case.

The next useful question is whether the dynamic response constraints force
one of the five witnesses to play two incompatible terminal roles.  That
would convert the static counting bound into a genuine contradiction
rather than only an exact-pattern order floor.
