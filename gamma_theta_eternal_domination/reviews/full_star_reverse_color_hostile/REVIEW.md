## 1. Dependency, model, and quantifier audit

Let

\[
 \gamma(G)=\alpha(G)=\gamma^\infty(G)=3,
 \qquad H=\overline G,
\]

let \(\mathcal F\) be an arbitrary optimal eternal family of dominating
triples, and let

\[
 S=\{s_0,s_1,s_2\}\in\mathcal F
\]

be independent.  Suppose \(x\notin S\) has all three retained responses

\[
 S-\{s_i\}+\{x\}\in\mathcal F
 \qquad(0\le i\le2).
\]

Membership of these successors also forces \(xs_i\in E(G)\): after
removing \(s_i\), the other two anchors are nonadjacent to \(s_i\), so the
retained successor can dominate \(s_i\) only through \(x\).

Put

\[
 B=N_H(x).
\]

Then \(B\cap S=\varnothing\).  It is nonempty because otherwise \(x\)
would dominate \(G\).  Moreover \(H[B]\) has no isolated vertex: for
\(b\in B\), the pair \(\{x,b\}\) cannot dominate \(G\), so it has a common
open \(H\)-neighbor \(c\), and \(c\in B\) with \(bc\in E(H)\).

The candidate uses the accepted inputs in their correct scopes.

- C-058 supplies the static viable-list Hall precursor, while the actual
  nonemptiness proof correctly uses the stronger family-response Hall

- C-064 and C-073 provide the established cross-state and physical-link
  context; no path-independent guard labels are assumed.
- C-108 applies to arbitrary retained independent states in the same
  eternal family and propagates one named responder at one fixed target.
- C-132 and C-139 concern the same physical set \(B=N_H(x)\), not the
  possibly larger dynamically inactive set.  The new result strengthens
  their componentwise picture without using its own conclusion
  circularly.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of the global reverse set

Every edge \(bc\in E(H[B])\) gives an independent triple

\[
 T_{bc}=\{x,b,c\}.
\]

Because \(\alpha(G)=3\), it is a maximum independent set.  Maximum
independent states belong to every eternal triple-family, so
\(T_{bc}\in\mathcal F\).

For such an edge define

\[
 R_{bc}=\{i:\{s_i,b,c\}\in\mathcal F\}.
\]

Fix \(i\) and take any two physical-link edges \(bc,de\), even from
different components.  The retained independent states \(T_{bc}\) and
\(T_{de}\)

- both avoid the attacked vertex \(s_i\);
- share the guard position \(x\); and
- have \(xs_i\in E(G)\).

C-108, with target \(s_i\) and shared responder \(x\), therefore gives

\[
 \{s_i,b,c\}\in\mathcal F
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{s_i,d,e\}\in\mathcal F.
\]

Thus \(R_{bc}\) is one global set \(R\), independent of both the edge and
its link component.

To prove \(R\ne\varnothing\), fix one edge \(bc\) and use \(T_{bc}\) as
the independent reference state.  The root \(S\) is an independent
three-set disjoint from \(T_{bc}\).  C-059's family-response Hall theorem
gives

\[
 \left|
 \bigcup_{i=0}^2 L_{T_{bc}}^{\mathcal F}(s_i)
 \right|\ge3.
\]

The union is contained in the three guard positions \(\{x,b,c\}\), so it
equals that set.  In particular \(x\) occurs in at least one response
list.  Its successor is \(\{s_i,b,c\}\), proving \(i\in R\).

This argument needs family membership, not merely domination of proposed
swaps.  It also proves globality across disconnected components of
\(H[B]\), which ridge covariance alone would not do.

## 3. Exact response-row formula

For \(z\in B\), write

\[
 P(z)=\{i:\{x,s_i,z\}\in\mathcal F\}.
\]

From \(T_{bc}\), attack the unoccupied anchor \(s_i\).

- The guard at \(x\) answers exactly when \(i\in R\).
- The guard at \(b\) has successor \(\{x,s_i,c\}\), so a retained
  \(b\)-response implies \(i\in P(c)\).  Conversely, if \(i\in P(c)\),
  the retained state \(\{x,s_i,c\}\) dominates \(b\).  Both \(x\) and
  \(c\) miss \(b\) in \(G\), since \(b\in B\) and \(bc\in E(H)\).
  Therefore \(s_ib\in E(G)\), making the move \(b\to s_i\) legal.
- The same argument with \(b,c\) interchanged handles the guard at \(c\).

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 L_{T_{bc}}^{\mathcal F}(s_i)
 =
 (\{x\}:i\in R)
 \cup(\{b\}:i\in P(c))
 \cup(\{c\}:i\in P(b)).
}
\]

No implication silently drops the required move edge.  The clean checker
reconstructs every actual row and compares it to this formula.

## 4. Feasible colors and the greatest-family qualification

Fix anchor colors by \(\kappa(s_i)=i\).  A color \(r\) is feasible at
\(x\) exactly when a proper three-coloring of \(H-x\) avoids \(r\) on
\(B=N_H(x)\).  Such a coloring extends by \(\kappa(x)=r\).

For the implication

\[
 C_x\subseteq R,
\]

the candidate correctly switches from an arbitrary optimal family to the
literal greatest eternal family \(\mathcal F^\star\).  A proper
three-coloring of \(H\) partitions \(G\) into three cliques.  The family of
all triples choosing one vertex from each clique is eternal: every
transversal dominates, and an attack is answered by the guard in the
attacked vertex's clique.  Therefore this clique-fiber family is contained
in \(\mathcal F^\star\).

If \(bc\) is a physical-link edge and \(x\) has color \(r\), then both
\(b,c\) avoid \(r\), while \(bc\in E(H)\) makes their colors distinct.
They use the other two colors, so \(\{s_r,b,c\}\) is a clique-fiber
transversal and lies in \(\mathcal F^\star\).  Hence \(r\in R\).

This proof does not apply to an arbitrary proper eternal subfamily, and the
candidate states that qualification explicitly.

## 5. What the restricted kernel proves

For color \(r\), let

\[
 \mathcal B_r=
 \{S-\{s_r\}+\{y\}:y\in B\}.
\]

Start with all dominating triples outside \(\mathcal B_r\), and repeatedly
delete every state having an unoccupied attack with no one-edge successor
remaining in the current set.  The fixed point \(\mathcal K_r\) is exactly
the greatest eternal triple-family avoiding the named banned states.

Thus

\[
 S,\ S-\{s_r\}+\{x\}\in\mathcal K_r
\]

means that one closed future strategy contains both states while avoiding
every state in \(\mathcal B_r\).  This is genuinely coinductive and
future-stable.

It does **not** mean that physical guards carry persistent colors or that
an arbitrary state of \(\mathcal K_r\) has a color-transversal
interpretation.  The precise safe formulation in Section 4 is correct;
the broader “global color prohibition” phrase should not be used without
this qualification.

If \(r\) is feasible, its clique-fiber family contains \(S\) and
\(S-s_r+x\).  A vertex \(y\in B\) cannot have color \(r\), so
\(S-s_r+y\) is not a transversal.  The clique-fiber family therefore
avoids \(\mathcal B_r\) and lies in \(\mathcal K_r\).  This proves the
one-way implication

\[
 C_x\subseteq R\cap K_x.
\]

No converse is claimed.

## 9. Correct frontier

The audited theorem proves that every full target has at least one global
one-step reverse color.  The equality control shows that choosing a
reverse color is insufficient: two of its three reverse colors fail after
future attacks.

The next honest target is therefore:

1. prove that at least one reverse color survives its exact restricted
   greatest kernel in the equality-critical branch; and
2. prove that a surviving color yields a compatible anchored coloring.

The first is a coinductive selection problem.  The second is a global
gluing problem.  Neither follows from the present theorem or control.
