# A universal diameter vertex and acute sets on \(S^3\)

**Checkpoint:** 2026-08-01
**Scope:** first-principles positive-route analysis; no web or literature
search.

## Status

The full universal-vertex theorem is not proved here.  The following exact
nontrivial regimes are proved, including a one-sided neighborhood of the
sharp closest-cap bound:

> Let \(X\subset S^3\) be compact and satisfy
> \(\langle x,y\rangle\ge1/2\) for all \(x,y\in X\).  Let \(p\) be the
> closest point of \(\operatorname{conv}X\) to the origin and put
> \(h=\lVert p\rVert\).  Then \(X\) has a partition into four parts and a
> number \(\delta>0\) such that same-part pairs have inner product at least
> \(1/2+\delta\) in either of the following cases:
>
> 1. \(5/8\le h^2<5/8+\varepsilon\), for one universal but nonexplicit
>    \(\varepsilon>0\);
> 2. \(h^2>(1+\sqrt3)/4\).

The second case includes every configuration for which the closest exposed
face needs at most two support points.

In addition, an adaptive nearest-contact partition works throughout the
intermediate interval when the four transverse support directions form a
regular tetrahedron.  The closest-cap method still leaves a compact
nonregular middle interval: the existence proof does not quantify
\(\varepsilon\).

A fixed lexicographic tetrahedral transverse partition fails even at the
sharp endpoint.  More subtly, nearest-contact sectors adapted to the actual
support tetrahedron can fail for a distorted support; an exact rational
support and algebraically defined threshold pair are given below.  Thus the
remaining proof must use a more flexible support-dependent partition or more
than one closest-support layer.

Before the nonexplicit low-cap neighborhood is invoked, the raw metric gap is

\[
 \frac58<h^2\le\frac{1+\sqrt3}{4}.
\]

## 1. Reduction from a universal diameter vertex

Suppose a compact set in \(\mathbb R^4\), scaled to diameter one, contains a
point \(a\) at distance one from every other point.  Translate \(a\) to the
origin.  Every residual point is a unit vector, and the global diameter bound
is

\[
 \lVert x-y\rVert^2=2-2\langle x,y\rangle\le1,
\]

or

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle\ge\frac12.                         \tag{1}
\]

Residual diameter edges are exactly the pairs attaining equality in (1).
A uniform four-partition of the residual set, together with a fifth singleton
part for \(a\), eliminates the universal vertex from any counterexample.

Here “uniform” means that one \(\delta>0\) works for every same-part pair.
This is the condition needed for compact, rather than merely finite, sets.

## 2. The closest cap

Let \(C=\operatorname{conv}X\), let \(p\in C\) minimize norm, and write

\[
 h=\lVert p\rVert,\qquad n=p/h.
\]

The pairwise lower bound implies \(p\ne0\).  The closest-point inequality is

\[
 \langle p,x\rangle\ge h^2\qquad(x\in X),               \tag{2}
\]

so

\[
 \langle n,x\rangle\ge h.                               \tag{3}
\]

The exposed closest face lies in a three-dimensional affine hyperplane.
Thus \(p\) is a convex combination of at most four contacts
\(a_1,\ldots,a_m\in X\).  If their positive weights are \(\lambda_i\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 h^2
 &=\left\lVert\sum_i\lambda_i a_i\right\rVert^2\\
 &\ge \sum_i\lambda_i^2
     +\frac12\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\lambda_j\\
 &=\frac12+\frac12\sum_i\lambda_i^2
 \ge\frac58.                                             \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently every point can be written

\[
 x=(\sin r\,u,\cos r),\qquad
 u\in S^2,\quad 0\le r\le\rho=\arccos h,                 \tag{5}
\]

relative to the axis \(n\).

## 3. What tetrahedral transverse sectors actually give

Let \(t_1,\ldots,t_4\in S^2\) be a regular tetrahedron:

\[
 \langle t_i,t_j\rangle=-\frac13\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Let \(Q_i\) be the closed spherical Voronoi cell

\[
 Q_i=\{u:\langle u,t_i\rangle\ge\langle u,t_j\rangle
       \text{ for every }j\}.
\]

Assign ties in any deterministic way.  A same-colored pair lies in one
closed cell.

### 3.1 Exact cell diameter

The sharp cell bound is

\[
 \langle u,v\rangle\ge-\frac1{\sqrt3}
 \qquad(u,v\in Q_i).                                    \tag{6}
\]

It is not \(-1/3\).  To see the geometry explicitly, take

\[
\begin{aligned}
 t_1&=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3,&t_2&=(1,-1,-1)/\sqrt3,\\
 t_3&=(-1,1,-1)/\sqrt3,&t_4&=(-1,-1,1)/\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The cell \(Q_1\) is the spherical section of the cone generated by
\(-t_2,-t_3,-t_4\).  Both

\[
 u=(0,0,1),\qquad v=-t_4
\]

belong to \(Q_1\), and

\[
 \langle u,v\rangle=-\frac1{\sqrt3}.                    \tag{7}
\]

For completeness, (6) follows from a short face calculation.  If
\(a,b,c\) are the three cone generators, their mutual products are
\(-1/3\).  A negative minimum on the spherical cone cannot have either point
in the relative interior, since the spherical first-order equation would
force the other point to be its antipode.  Hence both points lie on boundary
arcs.  The same-arc minimum is \(-1/3\).  For two distinct arcs write

\[
 u=\frac{(1-t)a+tb}{\lVert(1-t)a+tb\rVert},\qquad
 v=\frac{s b+(1-s)c}{\lVert s b+(1-s)c\rVert}.
\]

When the numerator is negative, squaring the desired inequality reduces to

\[
 3(1-4ts)^2
 \le
 (8t^2-8t+3)(8s^2-8s+3),
 \qquad 0\le t,s\le1,\quad4ts\le1.
\]

Here is a fully algebraic verification.  Subtract the left side from the
right and call the result \(D(t,s)\).  As a quadratic in \(t\),

\[
 D(t,s)=A(s)t^2+B(s)t+C(s),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A(s)&=8(2s^2-8s+3),\\
 B(s)&=-8(8s^2-11s+3),\\
 C(s)&=6(2s-1)^2,
\end{aligned}
\]

and its discriminant factors as

\[
 B(s)^2-4A(s)C(s)
 =64s(5s-2)(8s^2-8s+3).                                \tag{8}
\]

Put \(a=2-\sqrt{10}/2\), the smaller root of
\(2s^2-8s+3\).  If \(0\le s\le2/5\), then \(A>0\) and the
discriminant in (8) is nonpositive.  If \(2/5<s<a\), then \(A>0\) and
\(B>0\), so \(D\) is increasing for \(t\ge0\) and
\(D(t,s)\ge C(s)\).  Finally, if \(a\le s\le1\), then \(A\le0\).
The condition \(4ts\le1\) gives
\(0\le t\le1/(4s)\), and concavity reduces the minimum to the two
endpoints.  At the first endpoint

\[
 D(0,s)=6(2s-1)^2\ge0,
\]

while at the second the subtracted term vanishes and

\[
 D(1/(4s),s)
 =(8s^2-8s+3)
  \left(8(4s)^{-2}-8(4s)^{-1}+3\right)>0.
\]

The quadratic \(8z^2-8z+3\) is positive because its discriminant is
negative.  Equality is attained at \((t,s)=(0,1/2)\), and symmetrically at
\((1/2,0)\), namely a cone ray and the midpoint of the opposite boundary
arc.  This proves (6) and its sharpness.

### 3.2 A high-cap theorem

For same-cell transverse directions, (5), (6), and monotonicity in both
radial variables give

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle
 \ge h^2-\frac{1-h^2}{\sqrt3}.                           \tag{9}
\]

The right side is strictly greater than \(1/2\) exactly when

\[
 h^2>\frac{1+\sqrt3}{4}.                                \tag{10}
\]

Thus tetrahedral Voronoi coloring proves a uniform four-partition in the
regime (10).  The margin is the positive difference between the two sides of
(9) and \(1/2\).  Polar points are harmless because their product with every
point in the cap is at least \(h>1/2\).

If the closest face is supported on \(m\) points, (4) strengthens to

\[
 h^2\ge\frac12+\frac1{2m}.
\]

In particular \(m\le2\) gives \(h^2\ge3/4\), which lies strictly in the
high-cap regime.

### 3.3 Exact failure of the naive fixed coloring

At the universally guaranteed endpoint \(h^2=5/8\), (7) would give only

\[
 \frac58-\frac3{8\sqrt3}
 =\frac{5-\sqrt3}{8}<\frac12.
\]

There is also an exact admissible configuration showing that fixed
lexicographic tie-breaking is not a technical cure.  Keep the fixed
tetrahedron \(t_i\), use the opposite tetrahedron \(-t_i\) as transverse
directions, and lift it to the outer cap:

\[
 x_i=\left(\sqrt{\frac38}(-t_i),\sqrt{\frac58}\right).
\]

Then

\[
 \langle x_i,x_j\rangle
 =\frac58+\frac38\left(-\frac13\right)=\frac12
 \quad(i\ne j).                                         \tag{11}
\]

Their average is \(\sqrt{5/8}\,n\), so this is genuinely the closest-cap
normalization, not an arbitrarily chosen cap.  Under the rule “choose the
least index maximizing \(\langle u,t_i\rangle\),” the four transverse
directions receive colors

\[
 2,1,1,1
\]

in one-based notation.  Three vertices of the equality \(K_4\) are
monochromatic.  An adapted partition can color them, but a fixed
nearest-tetra rule cannot be accepted without additional structure.

## 4. The sharp closest-cap case is nevertheless four-colorable

Assume now

\[
 h^2=\frac58.                                            \tag{12}
\]

Every inequality in (4) is then equality.  Therefore:

1. the chosen positive supporting representation uses four contacts
   \(a_1,\ldots,a_4\);
2. its four weights are \(1/4\);
3. \(\langle a_i,a_j\rangle=1/2\) for \(i\ne j\).

This does not assert that the exposed closest face has no additional
contacts; only the chosen Caratheodory representation is being described.

The contacts form a basis of \(\mathbb R^4\).  For \(x\in X\), define

\[
 s_i(x)=2\langle a_i,x\rangle-1\ge0,\qquad
 S_x=\sum_i s_i(x),\qquad r_x=S_x-1.                    \tag{13}
\]

The contact Gram matrix is

\[
 G=\frac12(I+J),\qquad G^{-1}=2I-\frac25J.
\]

Substitution gives the two exact identities

\[
 \lVert s(x)\rVert^2=1+\frac{r_x^2}{5},                 \tag{14}
\]

and

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle-\frac12
 =\frac{5\langle s(x),s(y)\rangle-r_xr_y}{10}.           \tag{15}
\]

### 4.1 Range of the scalar parameter

Since \(s_i\ge0\), one has
\(\lVert s\rVert^2\le S^2\).  Equations (13), (14) then force
\(r_x\ge0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in the other direction gives

\[
 (1+r_x)^2\le4\left(1+\frac{r_x^2}{5}\right),
\]

and hence

\[
 0\le r_x\le R:=2\sqrt{10}-5<\frac75.                  \tag{16}
\]

### 4.2 Four safe coordinate regions and a safe core

Put

\[
 f_i(x)=s_i(x)-\frac{r_x}{\sqrt5}.
\]

If \(f_i(x)>0\) and \(f_i(y)>0\), then

\[
 5\langle s(x),s(y)\rangle
 \ge5s_i(x)s_i(y)>r_xr_y,
\]

so (15) gives \(\langle x,y\rangle>1/2\).

It remains to handle points for which every \(f_i\le0\).  Call these points
the core.  For a core point,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 s_i
 &=1+r-\sum_{j\ne i}s_j\\
 &\ge1+r-\frac{3r}{\sqrt5}
 =1-r\left(\frac3{\sqrt5}-1\right)>\frac12.             \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]

The final strict inequality follows from the exact rational bounds

\[
 R<\frac75,\qquad \frac3{\sqrt5}-1<\frac5{14}.
\]

If \(x\) is a core point and \(y\in X\), then

\[
 \langle s(x),s(y)\rangle
 >\frac12\sum_i s_i(y)=\frac{1+r_y}{2}.
\]

Using (16) in (15) yields

\[
 5\langle s(x),s(y)\rangle-r_xr_y>\frac52,
\]

so in fact

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle>\frac34.                            \tag{18}
\]

Thus every core point is uniformly safe with every point of \(X\).

### 4.3 Obtain a uniform compact partition

Let

\[
 F(x)=\max_i f_i(x),\qquad Z=\{x\in X:F(x)\le0\}.
\]

If \(Z\) is nonempty, it has, by (18), a positive uniform product margin
with all of \(X\).  Continuity and compactness therefore give an
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that every point with
\(F(x)\le\varepsilon\) retains a uniform margin with all of \(X\).  If
\(Z\) is empty, compactness gives \(\min_XF>0\), and we instead choose
\(\varepsilon<\min_XF\), making the safe band empty.

Put all such points into color 1.  For every remaining point, choose a color
\(i\) attaining \(F(x)\); then

\[
 f_i(x)>\varepsilon.
\]

Two nonsafe-band points of the same color satisfy

\[
 5s_i(x)s_i(y)-r_xr_y\ge5\varepsilon^2,
\]

in addition to nonnegative cross terms.  Pairs involving the safe band have
the compactness margin already obtained.  Taking the smaller of these two
margins gives one \(\delta>0\) for the whole four-partition.

This proves the compact uniform theorem at the sharp endpoint (12).  The
adaptation to the actual four contacts is precisely what the fixed
tetrahedral counterexample in Section 3.3 lacked.

### 4.4 A universal one-sided neighborhood of the endpoint

The endpoint result automatically persists on a universal, although
nonexplicit, interval.  This is a compactness statement, not a perturbative
assumption about a preselected support simplex.

Let \(\mathcal K\) be the space of nonempty compact subsets of \(S^3\), with
Hausdorff distance.  It is compact.  The subspace

\[
 \mathcal A=\{X\in\mathcal K:
       \langle x,y\rangle\ge1/2\text{ for all }x,y\in X\}
\]

is closed.  Moreover

\[
 h(X)=\operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{conv}X)
\]

is continuous: taking convex hull does not increase Hausdorff distance, and
distance from a fixed point is 1-Lipschitz in Hausdorff distance.  Hence

\[
 \mathcal A_0=\{X\in\mathcal A:h(X)^2=5/8\}
\]

is compact.

Partitionability with a positive product margin is Hausdorff-open.  Indeed,
suppose \(X=X_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_4\) and same-part products are at least
\(1/2+\delta\).  If \(d_H(X,Y)<\eta\), choose for each \(y\in Y\) a point
\(\pi(y)\in X\) with \(\lVert y-\pi(y)\rVert<\eta\), and give \(y\) the
color of \(\pi(y)\).  For a same-colored pair,

\[
 |\langle y,y'\rangle-
   \langle\pi(y),\pi(y')\rangle|<2\eta.
\]

Thus \(\eta<\delta/4\) transfers a margin of at least \(\delta/2\).
Section 4.3 supplies such a neighborhood for every member of
\(\mathcal A_0\).

It follows that there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that every
\(X\in\mathcal A\) with

\[
 \frac58\le h(X)^2<\frac58+\varepsilon                 \tag{19}
\]

has a uniform four-partition.  Otherwise there would be a sequence of
counterexamples with \(h(X_k)^2\downarrow5/8\).  A Hausdorff-convergent
subsequence would limit to a member of \(\mathcal A_0\), and eventually lie
in its open partitionable neighborhood, a contradiction.  No numerical
value of \(\varepsilon\) is extracted by this argument.

## 5. Adaptive cells from the active contact simplex

The closest support gives a natural three-dimensional coordinate system in
which adaptive partitions can be tested exactly.  Put

\[
 H=h^2,\qquad c=\frac12,\qquad \alpha=\sqrt{1-H},
\]

and suppose a chosen positive support representation has four contacts

\[
 a_i=(\alpha t_i,h),\qquad t_i\in S^2,\qquad
 \sum_i\lambda_i t_i=0,\quad \lambda_i>0,\quad
 \sum_i\lambda_i=1.                                    \tag{20}
\]

### 5.1 Weighted polar reduction

Write a point of the closest cap as \(x=(v,z)\), and define

\[
 \bar k=hz,\qquad u=\bar k-c,\qquad
 w=\frac{\alpha v}{u}.                                 \tag{21}
\]

Here \(u\ge H-c>0\).  The contact inequalities become

\[
 \langle a_i,x\rangle
 =c+u(1+\langle t_i,w\rangle)\ge c,
\]

so \(w\) lies in the polar tetrahedron

\[
 P=\{w\in\mathbb R^3:\langle t_i,w\rangle\ge-1
       \text{ for }i=1,\ldots,4\}.                    \tag{22}
\]

The unit equation is

\[
 \frac{(c+u)^2}{H}
 +\frac{u^2\lVert w\rVert^2}{1-H}=1.                  \tag{23}
\]

For fixed \(w\), this has one positive root \(u\).  If

\[
 d=H-c,\qquad R=\frac{1-H}{d},
\]

then \(u\ge d\), equivalently \(z\ge h\), holds exactly when
\(\lVert w\rVert\le R\).  Indeed, substitution of
\((u,\lVert w\rVert)=(d,R)\) makes the left side of (23) equal to one,
and that left side is strictly increasing in positive \(u\).

Coloring a point by a contact maximizing \(\langle a_i,x\rangle\) is now
the partition of \(P\cap B(0,R)\) into

\[
 P_i=\{w\in P\cap B(0,R):
       \langle t_i,w\rangle\ge\langle t_j,w\rangle
       \text{ for every }j\}.                          \tag{24}
\]

If \(w,w'\) have radial parameters \(u,u'\), their lifted product is

\[
 \Phi_H(w,w')
 =\frac{(c+u)(c+u')}{H}
  +\frac{uu'}{1-H}\langle w,w'\rangle.                 \tag{25}
\]

Thus the adaptive nearest-contact question is an exact optimization on four
truncated polar cells in \(\mathbb R^3\); no floating-point Gram
reconstruction is involved.

### 5.2 The regular-support family is safe throughout the gap

Suppose the transverse contacts in (20) are a regular tetrahedron.  Then

\[
 \sum_i t_i=0,\qquad
 \sum_i t_it_i^{\mathsf T}=\frac43I.
\]

For \(w\in P_i\), put \(q_j=\langle t_j,w\rangle\).  The corresponding
cell in \(q\)-coordinates is

\[
 \mathcal P_i=\{q\in\mathbb R^4:
      \sum_jq_j=0,\ q_j\ge-1,\ q_i\ge q_j\text{ for all }j\}.
\]

With \(p_j=(q_j+1)/4\), this is the part of the standard simplex where
\(p_i\) is a largest coordinate.  Its vertices are the uniform probability
vectors on nonempty subsets \(S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}\) containing \(i\).
Indeed, at a vertex every positive coordinate must be tied with \(p_i\);
otherwise one can perturb inside the positive support while preserving the
strict inequalities.  Conversely, the zero-coordinate equations and the
ties \(p_j=p_i\) on \(S\) isolate each listed point.
At the corresponding \(q\)-vertex,

\[
 q^S_j=\frac4{|S|}\mathbf 1_{j\in S}-1.
\]

Consequently, for two such vertices,

\[
 \langle q^S,q^T\rangle
 =\frac{16|S\cap T|}{|S||T|}-4\ge-\frac43.             \tag{26}
\]

For completeness, if \(|S||T|\le6\), the common index \(i\) gives the
bound immediately.  The remaining size pairs are
\((2,4),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4)\), where the intersection has size at least
\(2,2,3,4\), respectively.  A bilinear function on a product of two
polytopes attains its minimum at a vertex pair, so (26) holds throughout
one cell.  The tight-frame identity then gives

\[
 \langle w,w'\rangle
 =\frac34\langle q,q'\rangle\ge-1.                     \tag{27}
\]

It remains only to control the two radial parameters.  Equation (21) gives

\[
 H-c\le u,u'\le h-c.
\]

Using (27) in (25), set

\[
 L_H(u,u')=\frac{(c+u)(c+u')}{H}-\frac{uu'}{1-H}.
\]

For \(5/8\le H\le(1+\sqrt3)/4\), this function is increasing in each
variable on the displayed rectangle.  Indeed,

\[
 \frac{\partial L_H}{\partial u}
 =\frac{c(1-H)-u'(2H-1)}{H(1-H)},
\]

and its numerator is bounded below by

\[
 \frac12(1-h^2)-(h-\tfrac12)(2h^2-1)
 =h\left(1+\frac h2-2h^2\right)>0.                     \tag{28}
\]

The last sign is exact: \((1+\sqrt3)/4<25/36\), hence \(h<5/6\), while
\(4h^2-h-2\) is increasing here and has value \(-1/18\) at \(h=5/6\).
It follows that a same-colored pair satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle x,y\rangle
 &\ge L_H(H-c,H-c)\\
 &=c+\frac{(H-c)(3/2-2H)}{1-H}>c.                      \tag{29}
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves a uniform four-partition for every regular transverse support
tetrahedron throughout the entire unresolved cap interval, including both
endpoints.

### 5.3 Exact failure for a distorted support

Inequality (27) is special to the regular support.  In fact, nearest-contact
coloring can put an exact threshold pair in one cell for a distorted closest
support, even though all support contacts are mutually strictly above the
threshold.

Take

\[
 H=\frac{17}{25},\qquad h=\frac{\sqrt{17}}5,
 \qquad \alpha=\frac{2\sqrt2}{5},
\]

and the following rational unit transverse directions:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 t_1&=(6,9,-2)/11,&
 t_2&=(-9,-8,-12)/17,\\
 t_3&=(-12,0,-5)/13,&
 t_4&=(1,-4,8)/9.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{30}
\]

They contain the origin with positive weights

\[
 (\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\lambda_4)
 =\frac1{37382}(12364,10251,2860,11907).                \tag{31}
\]

Their smallest mutual product is \(-6/11>-9/16\).  Since
\((c-H)/(1-H)=-9/16\), the lifted contacts
\(a_i=(\alpha t_i,h)\) have mutual products at least
\(139/275>1/2\), and their weighted mean is \(hn\).

Consider the two rational polar points

\[
 w_1=(9,-4,-6)/7,
 \qquad
 w_2=(-11,20,9)/16.                                    \tag{32}
\]

Their four support scores are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &t_1&t_2&t_3&t_4\\ \hline
 w_1&30/77&23/119&-6/7&-23/63\\
 w_2&6/11&-169/272&87/208&-19/144
\end{array}                                             \tag{33}
\]

so both lie strictly in color 1 and all scores are at least \(-1\).  Also

\[
 \lVert w_1\rVert^2=\frac{19}{7},\qquad
 \lVert w_2\rVert^2=\frac{301}{128},\qquad
 \langle w_1,w_2\rangle=-\frac{233}{112}.
\]

Let \(u_1,u_2\) be the positive roots required by (23).  After clearing
denominators, they are defined exactly by

\[
 9475u_1^2+1400u_1-602=0,
 \qquad
 153525u_2^2+25600u_2-11008=0.                         \tag{34}
\]

Exact rational sign checks isolate them in

\[
 \frac{18878}{10^5}<u_1<\frac{18879}{10^5},
 \qquad
 \frac{19707}{10^5}<u_2<\frac{19708}{10^5}.
\]

In particular both exceed \(H-c=9/50\), and the displayed squared norms are below
\(R^2=(16/9)^2\).  Define their lifts explicitly by

\[
 x_j=\left(\frac{u_j}{\alpha}w_j,
            \frac{c+u_j}{h}\right)\qquad(j=1,2).
\]

They lie in the closest cap.  Substitution in (25), using rational interval
arithmetic, gives

\[
 \frac{23}{50}<\langle x_1,x_2\rangle
 <\frac{93}{200}<\frac12.                              \tag{35}
\]

This strict subthreshold pair yields an exact threshold obstruction without
putting a subthreshold pair into the final acute set.  Follow the normalized
chord

\[
 y(t)=\frac{(1-t)x_1+tx_2}
 {\lVert(1-t)x_1+tx_2\rVert},\qquad0\le t\le1.
\]

All contact inequalities, the closest-cap inequality, and the strict color-1
comparisons persist along this chord: their unnormalized versions are linear,
and normalization can only increase the positive lower bounds because the
chord has norm at most one.  Since
\(\langle x_1,y(0)\rangle=1\) and (35) holds at \(t=1\), there is a
\(t_*\in(0,1)\) with

\[
 \langle x_1,y(t_*)\rangle=\frac12.
\]

The parameter is algebraic: if \(g=\langle x_1,x_2\rangle\) and
\(s=1-g\), it is the unique root in \((0,1)\) of

\[
 3-6st+2s(2s-1)t^2=0.                                  \tag{36}
\]

Indeed, (35) gives \(1/2<s<1\).  The quadratic is positive at zero and
negative at one, has positive leading coefficient and two positive roots,
so exactly one root lies in \((0,1)\).

Therefore

\[
 \{a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,x_1,y(t_*)\}
\]

is an exact acute set with the prescribed closest support and with one
threshold pair strictly inside a single nearest-contact cell.  This does not
rule out a different adaptive four-partition, but it rules out the simplest
one-cell-per-contact theorem.

## 6. Tangent and gnomonic reductions

Both standard projections clarify the remaining obstacle but do not close
it.

### 6.1 Orthogonal tangent projection

Write

\[
 x=(w,\sqrt{1-\lVert w\rVert^2}),\qquad
 \lVert w\rVert\le\sqrt{1-h^2}.
\]

Then a threshold pair satisfies

\[
 \lVert w-z\rVert^2+
 \left(\sqrt{1-\lVert w\rVert^2}
       -\sqrt{1-\lVert z\rVert^2}\right)^2=1.            \tag{37}
\]

Its projected distance can be strictly below one when the two heights
differ.  Therefore a diameter coloring of the orthogonal projection does not
automatically color all original threshold pairs.

### 6.2 Gnomonic projection

With

\[
 x(z)=\frac{(z,1)}{\sqrt{1+\lVert z\rVert^2}},
\]

the cap becomes

\[
 \lVert z\rVert^2\le h^{-2}-1\le\frac35,                \tag{38}
\]

and the threshold condition is

\[
 2(1+\langle z,w\rangle)
 \ge
 \sqrt{(1+\lVert z\rVert^2)(1+\lVert w\rVert^2)}.        \tag{39}
\]

The inverse gnomonic map has differential norm at most one, so a
four-partition of the projected set into Euclidean pieces of diameter
strictly below one would suffice.  But the guaranteed ball in (38) has radius
\(\sqrt{3/5}\), and the straightforward tetrahedral cone pieces have squared
diameter

\[
 \frac65\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)>1.
\]

Thus the direct Euclidean-diameter shortcut is quantitatively too weak.
Equation (39), rather than ordinary projected diameter alone, must be used in
any successful gnomonic proof.

## 7. Remaining target

After shrinking the nonexplicit constant in (19), if necessary, the
unresolved closest-cap range is the compact band

\[
 \boxed{\frac58+\varepsilon\le h^2
        \le\frac{1+\sqrt3}{4}}.
\]

The sharp endpoint and a one-sided neighborhood are controlled by the rigid
regular-contact limit and hyperspace compactness, while sufficiently large
\(h\) is controlled by the metric diameter of tetrahedral transverse cells.
Regular four-contact supports are controlled across the whole gap by (29).
For distorted supports, however, the raw cap is still too wide for the
universal tetrahedral bound and Section 5.3 rules out the simplest adaptive
replacement.

Promising next steps are:

1. subdivide the weighted polar cells (24), rather than assigning one whole
   cell to each contact;
2. quantify the compactness neighborhood (19), which would reduce the
   remaining semialgebraic parameter range;
3. treat the three-contact boundary separately as a planar support plus one
   free transverse coordinate;
4. exploit the pair-dependent inequality (39), rather than replacing it by
   a Euclidean diameter bound.
