# Nontechnical explanation (150 words)

The Lalonde graph is classically four-colorable, yet adding one universal vertex raises its classical chromatic number to five. The open question was whether quantum measurements could use four colors by replacing color choices with higher-dimensional subspaces. We prove they cannot. First, we average over color names, which makes every subspace have the same dimension without assuming that property. For each color, the graph’s four triangles then force thirteen vertex subspaces into a rigid three-coordinate pattern. The remaining six vertices are described by one invariant plane, including missed degenerate cases that are not graphs of matrices. Comparing two colors forces two pieces of these planes to occupy mutually orthogonal subspaces. Across all colors, those pieces would require three units of space for every two units available, an impossible dimension count. Thus the unrestricted quantum chromatic number is five. The same argument directly proves the conjectured value n+1 for the joined family.
