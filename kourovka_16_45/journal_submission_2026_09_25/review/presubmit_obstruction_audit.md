## Falsification routes and checkable reasoning

1. **Could nonfaithfulness create a four-point base?** For every finite group,
   \(b(X)=\max_{N\lhd X}b_{\mathrm f}(X/N)\): passing to an action's kernel makes precisely its image action faithful, and every quotient action pulls back. The converse subgroup construction has kernel \(\bigcap_i\operatorname{Core}_X(L_i)=\operatorname{Core}_X(\bigcap_iL_i)\), equal to the given intersection exactly when that intersection is normal. Thus the condition in manuscript lines 119–141 is sufficient as well as necessary. The empty family handles the trivial case correctly.

2. **Could witness independence or the Boolean formulation overstate the result?** A witness \(x_i\in\bigcap_{j\ne i}L_j\setminus L_i\) cannot lie in the subgroup generated by the other witnesses, since all those others lie in \(L_i\). Conversely, the omission-generated subgroups of an independent set have the original elements as witnesses. For \(F(J)=\bigcap_{i\notin J}L_i\), one has \(F(J)\cap F(K)=F(J\cap K)\), \(F(\varnothing)=\bigcap_iL_i\), and \(F([t])=X\). Also \(x_i\in F(J)\) exactly when \(i\in J\), proving injectivity. No unsupported join-preservation assumption occurs.

3. **Attempted faithful size-four counterexample.** Suppose four subgroups have irredundant intersection one. At least one member \(K\) misses \(V\). Restrict the other three members to \(K\); their witnesses remain in \(K\), giving an irredundant three-member family with trivial intersection there. There are exactly two possibilities for \(K\cap V\), both fatal to this attempt:
   - If \(K\cap V=1\), projection embeds \(K\) in \(H\). Every even-order subgroup of \(H\) contains its unique involution \(-I\); every odd-order subgroup is trivial, cyclic of order three, or cyclic of order five. A family with intersection one must therefore contain an odd-order member. A trivial member settles the intersection alone; a prime-order member needs at most one additional member. Hence \(b_{\mathrm f}(H)=2\), and the same upper bound holds for every subgroup.
   - If \(K\cap V=W\) is a line, then \(\pi(K)\le H_W\). The scalar map \(H_W\to\mathbf F_{29}^{\times}\) is injective: its kernel would consist of nonidentity unipotents of order 29, incompatible with \(|H|=120\). Thus \(H_W\) is cyclic of order dividing four. A Sylow 2-subgroup splits \(K\) as \(C_{29}\rtimes C_{2^a}\), \(0\le a\le2\), with faithful scalar action. In each subgroup containing \(C_{29}\), a generator of the cyclic quotient together with any generator outside its complement generates the subgroup. Subgroups missing \(C_{29}\) are cyclic 2-groups. Therefore \(\mu'(K)\le2\), including the trivial-complement boundary case.
   Consequently \(b_{\mathrm f}(G)\le3\). This route is closed by a direct contradiction; it does not transfer the central claim to an unproved subgroup classification of \(G\).

4. **Attempted normal-bottom size-four counterexample with nontrivial bottom.** Irreducibility implies \(N\cap V=1\) or \(V\) for normal \(N\). In the first case \([N,V]=1\), so \(N\le C_G(V)=V\), using faithfulness of the matrix action; then \(N=1\). Thus every nontrivial normal bottom contains \(V\), and quotienting its whole family by \(V\) preserves irredundancy. This would produce a size-four family in \(H\), contradicting \(\mu'(H)=3\). Equivalently every nonfaithful action factors through \(H\), where \(b(H)=3\). No quotient was omitted.

5. **Could \(b(H)\) and \(b_{\mathrm f}(H)\) have been confused?** They differ here. The degree-five action of \(H/Z\cong A_5\) has minimal bases of size three (two fixed letters leave a 3-cycle, three fixed letters leave the identity), and pulls back with kernel \(Z\). In contrast the unique-involution argument gives \(b_{\mathrm f}(H)=2\). To check \(\mu'(H)\le3\), if an independent set becomes dependent modulo \(Z\), one omitted generator lies in \(KZ\setminus K\). Then \(Z\not\le K\), so \(K\) has odd prime order or is trivial, and the independent set has size at most two. Otherwise its images are independent in \(A_5\), whose proper maximal subgroup types \(A_4,D_{10},S_3\) have invariant two. This establishes the required upper bound without assuming the desired conclusion. Identifying the central double cover abstractly with \(\mathrm{SL}_2(5)\) is not needed for this argument.

6. **Could the three-point lower bound or rank-four obstruction fail arithmetically?** The three affine subgroups have orders 58. Their pair intersections are exactly the three distinct involution subgroups printed in the manuscript, and their total intersection is one. Their disjoint coset action has degree \(3\cdot100920/58=5220\) and kernel one. The four omission-generated subgroups have orders \(120,6728,10092,16820\), intersection \(Z_0=\{1,(0,-I)\}\), and each has its stated omission witness. Translation by \(e_1\) takes \((0,-I)\) to \((2e_1,-I)\), proving nonnormality. The cores are as stated: the three preimages have core \(V\rtimes Z\), while the complement's core is one; their cores are not an irredundant family.
