## Mathematical verification

### 1. Definitions and subgroup formulations

The kernel convention is correct. The point stabilizers of a minimal base have a normal total intersection equal to the action kernel, and every member is essential. Conversely the identity cosets in the disjoint union of the coset actions give a minimal base because the intersection of their cores equals the core of their intersection. When the latter is already normal, this is exactly the stipulated intersection.

Factoring by the kernel proves the maximum-over-quotients formula. Trivial actions have the empty minimal base; the empty family has ambient-group intersection. Allowing infinite point sets changes nothing, because each nonidentity element of the finite image is excluded by some point of a given base, so finitely many chosen points already constitute a base.

For an arbitrary irredundant intersection family, a witness for the ith subgroup lies in every other subgroup and outside the ith one. These witnesses are distinct and independent. Conversely, the subgroups generated by all but one member of an independent set have the stated witnesses. This proves the breadth identity without a hidden assumption that the independent set generates the ambient group.

Restriction to any family member preserves the witnesses for the other members and preserves the total intersection. Thus the stated independence and faithful restriction bounds both follow. For a one-member family, the resulting empty family is correctly interpreted inside that member. Subgroup monotonicity of the two invariants used in the proof follows from the same descriptions.

The map indexed by complements of subsets preserves meets because complements turn intersection into union of indexing sets. Its witnesses distinguish every pair of distinct subsets; its top is the ambient group and its bottom is the total intersection. No unjustified join-preservation claim is made.

### 2. The order and invariants of H

Both generating matrices have determinant one. The displayed triangle identities put the central order-two subgroup inside H and give a surjection from the (2,3,5) spherical triangle group A5 to H/Z. The image of A is nontrivial, so simplicity makes the quotient A5. This yields order 120 without enumerating H.

The proper-subgroup argument for A5 is valid. Intransitive subgroups either fix a point or preserve a 2+3 partition, giving containment in A4 or S3. A proper transitive subgroup has order 5, 10, 15, 20, or 30. The Sylow count forces a normal subgroup of order five in the middle two cases, contradicting the order-ten normalizer; simplicity excludes order 30. The remaining cases lie in a normalizer of order ten. The asserted simplicity can be checked from the supplied conjugacy-class sizes.

A generating set of A4 includes an element of order three and an element outside the cyclic subgroup it generates. The pair generates A4 because there is no subgroup of order six. Proper subgroups have independence invariant at most two. The prime-order dihedral cases are equally valid, including S3. Deleting an element of an independent set in A5 leaves a set generating a proper subgroup, so its size is at most two; the natural action supplies the matching lower bound of three.

Over odd characteristic the only involution in SL2 is minus the identity: the polynomial x squared minus one has distinct roots, and determinant one excludes mixed signs. Consequently every even-order subgroup of H contains Z, and every subgroup missing Z is trivial or cyclic of order three or five.

The projection argument for an independent set in H handles the important failure case correctly. If projected independence fails, an omitted element lies in KZ outside K; hence K cannot contain Z and has independence invariant at most one. This bounds the original set by two in that case and by three in the other case. The pulled-back degree-five action supplies b(H)=3. A faithful intersection family must contain an odd-order subgroup; a prime-order member requires just one other member to cut its intersection to one. Subgroups of orders three and five exhibit the faithful lower bound two. Thus all three stated invariants of H are proved.

### 3. Explicit independent triple

Fresh modular arithmetic confirms both words for R2 and R3, every square, and product orders 4, 3, and 10. The corresponding projective products have orders 2, 3, and 5. Two involutions whose product has order n generate a dihedral group of order 2n; lifting doubles the order because the lifts contain Z. Hence the pair orders are 8, 12, and 20.

The least common multiple of these pair orders is 120, so the triple generates H. Every pair is proper, which proves independence. The first two pair subgroups intersect in the subgroup generated by R3, by divisibility and its order four. The remaining pair does not contain R3 but contains Z, giving exact total intersection Z. None of these conclusions depends on identifying the pair groups by a possibly ambiguous binary-dihedral name.

### 4. Subgroups missing V

The prime-field condition is used correctly: every additive subgroup of V is a vector subspace. Thus a subgroup missing V has translation intersection either zero or a line; there is no omitted intermediate additive possibility.

For a line stabilizer in H, the kernel of scalar restriction would consist of unipotent matrices of order 29, unless trivial. Since 29 does not divide 120, scalar restriction is injective. The stabilizer therefore embeds in the multiplicative field group and has order dividing gcd(120,28)=4. In particular H cannot fix a line.

For a subgroup K with a translation line W, projection lands in this cyclic 2-group. A Sylow 2-subgroup of K has the full quotient order and trivial intersection with W, so it is a complement without needing an unstated splitting theorem. Its action on W is faithful by the same scalar-injectivity argument.

The scalar semidirect-product lemma covers the trivial complement as well as nontrivial cyclic 2-groups. A subgroup with zero translation intersection embeds in a cyclic prime-power group. Otherwise it contains the whole prime-order translation group. Any generating set projects to a generating set of a cyclic prime-power quotient, so one projected element generates that quotient. Its geometric-sum power vanishes, making it a complement generator. Any generator outside that complement forces the whole prime-order translation subgroup. Thus every subgroup has no independent set larger than two. The argument is uniform over all the subgroups it needs to cover.

When K has zero translation intersection it embeds in H, giving the different bounds needed later: independence at most three and faithful base invariant at most two. The manuscript does not confuse these two bounds.

### 5. Universal upper bounds and lower witnesses

A faithful irredundant intersection family in G must contain a member missing V. Restriction therefore bounds its size by three. For a nontrivial normal subgroup N, irreducibility gives N intersect V equal to zero or V. In the zero case the commutator of N with V lies in both, so N centralizes V. Faithfulness of the linear action gives the centralizer of V equal to V, forcing N trivial. Thus every nontrivial kernel contains V, and every nonfaithful action factors through H. This proves the all-actions bound three, not merely the faithful bound.

The four displayed affine elements are independent: projection excludes each linear element from the subgroup generated by the remaining ones, and the pure translation is outside the complement. Their subgroup contains the H-orbit of a nonzero vector; irreducibility and the prime field imply that this spans all of V. For the reverse independence bound, either an irredundant subgroup family quotients by V to one in H, or restriction to a member missing V bounds its size by four. This is not circular: the breadth identity was established before these bounds and does not rely on the constructed four-set.

Each faithful witness subgroup consists of its translation line together with a coset having linear part minus the identity. The three translation lines have pairwise zero intersections. Comparing the three affine cosets gives precisely the three distinct order-two pairwise intersections written in the paper and trivial total intersection. All members are therefore essential. The disjoint coset action has degree 3 times 100920/58 = 5220, and supplies the matching faithful lower bound three.

### 6. Normality obstruction

Every pair subgroup of H has order greater than four, so it cannot stabilize a line. Its orbit of e1 therefore spans V, proving the asserted preimage description of the three subgroups obtained by omitting a linear generator. Intersecting them with the complement reduces the total intersection to Z0. Direct affine conjugation sends the nontrivial element of Z0 to the displayed element with nonzero translation part, proving nonnormality.

The cores of the pair subgroups in H are Z, because their proper images in the simple quotient have trivial core. Taking preimages gives cores V semidirect Z in G. The complement has trivial core by the normal-subgroup argument. Thus the core family really loses irredundancy in the specified way. This explains the obstruction without asserting that taking cores is the only possible way to seek a normal-bottom embedding.
