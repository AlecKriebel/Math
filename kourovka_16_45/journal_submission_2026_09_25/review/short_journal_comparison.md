## Conclusion

Among Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society, Glasgow Mathematical Journal, Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society, and Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra, the Australian Bulletin remains a defensible first choice for the present seven-page, self-contained counterexample. Its specific compact-paper emphasis and record of short Kourovka solutions support that choice. Proceedings AMS is an equally natural format alternative. Neither scope nor available public information establishes that the Australian Bulletin maximizes acceptance probability; its explicit capacity statement shows considerable competition. Glasgow is a plausible alternative, and the other two are within scope but less specifically matched to a short counterexample, in my judgment. A group-theory specialist or Bulletin LMS needs separate comparison; this review does not establish a best journal across all venues.

## Evidence and limitations

### Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society

The journal emphasizes quick publication, competitive submissions, and a presentation sufficiently finished to require no revision. It reports receiving more than five times the material it can publish, with decisions often within a month. That supports fast initial triage, not an easy-acceptance claim, and the capacity ratio must not be presented as this paper's acceptance rate. [Official focus and review policy](https://journal.austms.org.au/ojs/index.php/Bulletin/about)

Two genuine research-paper precedents are John Cossey, *Special maximal subgroups of p-groups*, 89 (2014), 415–419, addressing Kourovka 16.13, and Ballester-Bolinches–Calvo–Esteban-Romero, *A question from the Kourovka notebook on formation products*, 68 (2003), 461–470. They demonstrate that compact solutions to specific finite-group questions are at home here. [Cossey, DOI 10.1017/S0004972713000658](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972713000658); [formation-products paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/question-from-the-kourovka-notebook-on-formation-products/68696698D8E7BDAB3252B1F16A0A4D25)

The seemingly close Baykalov item, *Intersection of conjugate solvable subgroups in finite classical groups*, 105 (2022), 507–510, is classified as an Australasian PhD thesis abstract, so it should **not** be used as a research-article acceptance precedent.

The present package's detailed AI declaration is compatible in principle with the journal-specific declaration framework. This is not unique to this journal. [Preparation and AI policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials)

### Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society

AMS describes the journal as devoted to shorter research articles across pure and applied mathematics. The present contribution fits that format. [Official AMS issue page carrying the journal scope](https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/2016-144-05/home.html)

A historically relevant example is Göbel–Shelah's short refutation of Plotkin's geometric-equivalence conjecture, Kourovka 14.71. It demonstrates that a specific conjecture counterexample can qualify for Proceedings; its infinite-group subject is less close than this paper's finite-group setting. [Author-hosted published paper](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95567/741.pdf)

The current official AMS scope and AI-policy landing pages returned HTTP403 using both the web tool and a direct public request. The 2024 AMS Notices discussion reports that AMS adopted COPE's disclosure/accountability framework; this is historical primary evidence, **not certification that the policy remains unchanged in September 2026**. Do not discard Proceedings as incompatible, but do not certify a submission there without reading its current agreement. [Current policy endpoint, retrieval blocked](https://www.ams.org/publications/journals/policies/UseofArtificialIntelligence); [2024 Notices discussion](https://www.ams.org/notices/202401/rnoti-p93.pdf)

No reliable current primary acceptance-rate or comparable processing-time statistic was located. My judgment: credible alternative, no evidenced gain in acceptance probability over the Australian Bulletin.

### Glasgow Mathematical Journal

The journal publishes original work across pure and applied mathematics and explicitly includes algebra, representation theory, and geometric group theory among its active areas. Its scope does not express the Australian Bulletin's short-note emphasis. [Official scope](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/glasgow-mathematical-journal/information/author-instructions)

There is a related finite-group invariant paper, *Finite groups with the same join graph as a finite nilpotent group*, 63 (2021), involving subgroup structure, irredundancy, and connections to base-size literature. This is relevant evidence of audience, but not a direct precedent for this precise counterexample. [Published article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/glasgow-mathematical-journal/article/finite-groups-with-the-same-join-graph-as-a-finite-nilpotent-group/03DE1FDFE1AA0979C066EBD1114B1BB1)

Its current journal-specific policy covers AI-generated text and analysis/insights, requires disclosure, excludes AI authorship, and places responsibility on human authors. There is no reviewed blanket prohibition on substantial AI writing. [Preparation and AI policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/glasgow-mathematical-journal/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials)

My judgment: plausible alternative, not demonstrably easier or more specifically suited than the Australian Bulletin. No verified acceptance probability is supplied.

### Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society

Its scope considers original advances in pure or applied mathematics; the work is in scope. Its current journal-specific AI policy requires disclosure of generated text and extracted insights with human accountability, like the other reviewed Cambridge journals. [Scope, preparation, and AI policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematical-proceedings-of-the-cambridge-philosophical-society/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials)

My judgment: legitimate possibility, but no format, audience, or acceptance evidence found that warrants preferring it for this particular seven-page finite-group construction.

The managing editor's journal page describes an emphasis on algebra with broader relevance, immediate applications, or theories expected to enable later applications.
