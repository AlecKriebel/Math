# Journal target reassessment

Checkpoint: 2026-09-26 04:33:56 UTC. Bounded comparison **100% complete** after two independent journal-comparison paths and coordinator source checks. This percentage concerns the comparison task, not acceptance likelihood. The submission kit is unchanged.

## Correction to the earlier recommendation

Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society is a defensible practical first target, not a demonstrated maximizer of acceptance probability. Policy compatibility establishes that a transparently disclosed submission can be considered; it does not establish editorial interest or favorable odds. The comparison found several legitimate alternatives. No numerical manuscript-specific acceptance probabilities are available.

The case for the Bulletin is the combination of a seven-page self-contained note, an explicit named-problem resolution, relevant group-theory publication history, compatible disclosure rules and an explicitly quick screening process. Its own policy says submissions exceed publication capacity by more than fivefold. This is evidence of selectivity, not an exact acceptance rate and not a basis to call it easy.

## Evidence and alternatives

- **Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society:** [official scope](https://journal.austms.org.au/ojs/index.php/Bulletin/about) asks for clear papers of about twelve pages or fewer in publishable form and says decisions are often within a month. A genuine five-page research-article precedent is J. Cossey, *Special maximal subgroups of p-groups*, 89 (2014), 415–419, answering Kourovka Problem 16.13: [official article record](https://journal.austms.org.au/ojs/index.php/Bulletin/article/view/7138). This shows that the format and contribution type are at home there; it does not predict this paper's outcome.
- **Journal of Algebra:** a closer specialist audience and credible alternative. Its [official scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-algebra/0021-8693) asks for substantial lasting contributions and innovative techniques. Seven pages and a counterexample are not disqualifications: Manuel Staiger, *On the almost-palindromic width of free groups*, 659 (2024), 475–481, answers another Kourovka question negatively ([publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002186932400382X)). [Elsevier's June 2026 AI policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals) permits broader responsible use than the older language-editing-only formulation; declarations and authentic human intellectual oversight remain essential. No better acceptance chance is established.
- **Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society:** a credible more ambitious concise-paper option, with a particularly relevant recent base-theory readership. [Scope](https://www.lms.ac.uk/publications/blms/submission) specifies concise significant advances, at most twenty pages, and an introduction understandable outside the immediate specialty. A recent subject precedent is Brenner–del Valle–Roney-Dougal, *Irredundant bases for soluble groups* (2025), DOI [10.1112/blms.70137](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.70137); its ordered irredundant bases are not identical to our inclusion-minimal bases. [LMS June 2026 AI policy](https://www.lms.ac.uk/publications/policies/AI) explicitly covers declared use in idea generation, calculations, and constructing or verifying proofs, while preserving human accountability and editorial discretion. Stronger ambition is an editorial judgment, not a quantified rejection prediction.
- **Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society:** equally natural short-article format. No evidence establishes better odds. The live AMS AI policy endpoint could not be inspected in this bounded comparison, so eligibility of this exact provenance is not certified here.
- **Journal of Group Theory:** strongest narrow topical match, but [De Gruyter Brill publishing ethics](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/publishing/for-authors/author-policies/publishing-ethics) says AI-generated papers are not accepted. Given this manuscript's substantive AI drafting and mathematical development, it should not be treated as an eligible option without resolving that restriction. No attempt to hide or relabel the provenance is appropriate.
- **Glasgow Mathematical Journal:** plausible broad pure-mathematics alternative with compatible public disclosure provisions, but this comparison found no specific advantage over the Bulletin for this short note.

The Baykalov 2022 Bulletin item on conjugate solvable subgroups is an Australasian PhD thesis abstract, so it was expressly excluded as evidence of acceptance of a normal research article of the same length. Impact factors and author affiliation were not used to invent an acceptance forecast. No outside person was contacted.

Journal of Algebra's journal-specific author guide was inaccessible during this comparison; the current general publisher policy is verified, not every journal-level or logged-in condition. That guide and the appropriate disclosure format would need a final check before retargeting the package. Its topic fit is additionally supported by Gill–Lodà, *Statistics for S_n acting on k-sets*, Journal of Algebra 607 (2022), 286–299, which treats maximum minimal-base sizes ([publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021869321005366)).

## Decision

Retain the prepared Bulletin package as a pragmatic short-paper first submission, while explicitly withdrawing any claim that it uniquely or provably maximizes acceptance. Journal of Algebra deserves serious consideration if specialist audience is the priority; Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society is a credible first attempt if the author wants a more ambitious significance judgment. Choosing among those strategies is separate from theorem validation. This advisory comparison does not silently retarget or rebuild the user's finalized kit.
