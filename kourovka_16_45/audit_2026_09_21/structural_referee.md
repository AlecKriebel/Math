## Independent derivation of the structural bounds

### 1. Normal-bottom and unrestricted intersection conventions

For any finite group X, an irredundant family of point stabilizers from a
minimal base has total intersection N equal to the action kernel, which is
normal in X. Conversely, if subgroups L_i have normal, irredundant total
intersection N, the action on the disjoint union of the left coset spaces
X/L_i has kernel

\[
\bigcap_i\operatorname{Core}_X(L_i)
=\operatorname{Core}_X(\bigcap_iL_i)
=\operatorname{Core}_X(N)=N.
\]

The identity cosets are a minimal base because deleting the i-th stabilizer
enlarges the intersection. Setting N=1 gives the faithful version; factoring
through a kernel yields b(X)=max_N b_f(X/N). This is valid for nontransitive
actions and does not replace the family by its cores. Every minimal base is
finite because the finite permutation image has a finite base, even when the
acted-on set is infinite.

For unrestricted irredundant intersections choose witnesses
x_i in the intersection of all L_j for j unequal to i but outside L_i.
All other x_j lie in L_i, so x_i is not generated by them. Conversely the
omission-generated subgroups of an independent set form an irredundant family.
This proves c_cap(X)=mu'(X), without a normality assertion on the bottom.

For the Boolean map A -> intersection_{i not in A} L_i, intersection of index
sets corresponds to intersection of subgroups. If i belongs to A but not B,
the witness x_i lies in the image of A and not the image of B. Hence the map
is injective, maps the top to X, and maps the bottom to the total intersection.
There is no join-preserving requirement in this argument.

Finally, restricting the other family members to L_i preserves all their
witnesses. Therefore t-1 <= mu'(L_i); when the total intersection is trivial,
t-1 <= b_f(L_i). These statements include t=1 and trivial subgroup edge cases.

### 2. Identification and subgroup facts for H

The determinant and triangle relations are exact. Since -I=A^2 belongs to H,
Z={I,-I} is a central subgroup of H. The images of A and B in H/Z satisfy
the (2,3,5) presentation and are nontrivial. The standard presentation of A5
and its simplicity imply H/Z is A5 and therefore |H|=120.

Independently, the checker constructs H by exact matrix multiplication, finds
120 elements, and checks the claimed map from SL(2,5) by a paired Cayley
traversal. The set of pairs has size 120 and both projections are bijections;
the first projection is the full set of determinant-one matrices over F5.
Thus this identification also has a small, reproducible direct certificate.
The abstract isomorphism H = SL(2,5) is not needed for the ensuing argument.

The manuscript's elementary proper-subgroup classification for A5 is valid:

- An intransitive subgroup either fixes a letter (and lies in A4) or has orbit
  partition 2+3 (and lies in the parity-preserving subgroup isomorphic to S3).
- A proper transitive subgroup has order 5, 10, 15, 20, or 30 by orbit-stabilizer
  and Lagrange. Orders 15 and 20 would have a normal Sylow-5 subgroup, whose
  normalizer in A5 has order only 10. Order 30 would be index two, impossible
  since a map A5 -> C2 kills all 3-cycles and those generate A5.
- Groups of the remaining orders 5 and 10 lie in a Sylow-5 normalizer D10.

The normalizer calculation is correct: N_S5(C5) has order 20 and contains odd
permutations, so its intersection with A5 has order 10. A4, D10, and S3 all have
independence number 2. For A4, a generating set needs a 3-cycle and an element
outside its cyclic subgroup; the pair generates A4 because no order-six
subgroup exists. Proper subgroups of A4 have independence number at most two.
For the dihedral cases of twice prime order, a generating pair can be taken
as a nonidentity rotation with a reflection or as two distinct reflections.
Deleting an element of an independent set in A5 leaves an independent set
generating a proper subgroup, and hence mu'(A5)<=3. Three points in the
natural A5 action on five letters give a faithful minimal base, giving equality.

### 3. The two base invariants of H really differ

An involution in SL(2,29) is diagonalizable with eigenvalues in {1,-1};
determinant one excludes the mixed pair. Thus -I is its unique nonidentity
involution. Every even-order subgroup of H contains Z by Cauchy's theorem.
An odd-order subgroup injects into A5 and, using the classification above,
is trivial or cyclic of order 3 or 5.

For an independent set S in H, if its indexed images modulo Z are independent,
|S|<=3. Otherwise, some s is in KZ but outside K, where K is generated by the
other elements. This forces Z not to be contained in K, so K is an odd-order
subgroup of the preceding type. The other independent elements number at most
one, giving |S|<=2 in this case. Thus mu'(H)<=3.

Any subgroup family with intersection one has an odd-order member. If this
member is trivial, irredundancy allows only that member. If it has prime order,
some other member intersects it trivially; irredundancy then permits at most
two members. The cyclic subgroups of orders 3 and 5 have trivial intersection,
so b_f(H)=2.

The independent checker verifies every displayed R_i, its word in A and B,
its square -I, the pair orders 8, 12, 20, and that all three generate H.
The least-common-multiple argument independently explains the last fact:
lcm(8,12,20)=120. Each omitted generator is outside its pair subgroup.
The first two pair subgroups intersect in <R3>, since their intersection has
order dividing gcd(8,12)=4 and contains R3 of order four. The third pair
subgroup cannot contain R3 because that would make it contain all of H.
Their common intersection is therefore exactly Z, and the three-element
family is irredundant. Consequently b(H)=mu'(H)=3.

### 4. Every subgroup not containing V has faithful base invariant at most two

Let W be a line in V. Restriction to W maps H_W into F_29^*. An element in the
kernel, written in an adapted basis, is [[1,u],[0,1]]. If u is nonzero its
order is 29, which cannot divide 120. Hence the map is injective, H_W is
cyclic, and |H_W| divides gcd(120,28)=4. In particular H cannot preserve a
line, so its action on V is irreducible. Independently all 30 line stabilizers
were checked and all have order exactly four.

Now let K be a subgroup of G not containing V. Because 29 is prime, every
additive subgroup of V is an F_29-subspace. Thus K intersect V has dimension
zero or one.

If K intersect V is trivial, projection embeds K in H. Both mu' and b_f are
monotone under taking subgroups: for b_f, the same subgroups with trivial
intersection remain a valid faithful coset-action family in the larger group.
It follows that mu'(K)<=3 and b_f(K)<=2.

If K intersect V is a line W, it is normal in K and the projected group D is
contained in H_W. Therefore |K|=29|D| with |D| in {1,2,4}. A Sylow-2 subgroup
has order |D|, meets W trivially, and maps isomorphically onto D. It is a
complement to W; conjugation on W agrees with the faithful scalar action of
D. This proves K is isomorphic to F_29 semidirect D. It does not presume a
complement theorem for arbitrary extensions or overlook translated complements.
The case D=1 simply gives the cyclic group W.

For F_p semidirect C with C a faithful cyclic 2-group, every subgroup missing
the p-group embeds in C and is a cyclic 2-group. A subgroup meeting the
p-group nontrivially contains that whole group and equals its full preimage
of some D<=C. When D is nontrivial, a generating set contains an element
whose projection generates D, since D has a unique maximal subgroup. Such an
element has order |D| by the geometric-series calculation; its cyclic subgroup
is a complement. A second generator outside that complement, together with
the first, generates the whole subgroup: otherwise their intersection with
the p-group would be trivial and their subgroup would equal that complement.
Thus every subgroup has independent generating sets of size at most two,
which is exactly the needed mu'<=2 conclusion. The trivial C and D cases
are covered separately by the prime-order cyclic group.

### 5. Faithful and all-actions bounds for G

A faithful irredundant family has intersection one, so some member K misses V.
Restriction to K and the preceding result give t-1<=b_f(K)<=2, whence
b_f(G)<=3. This argument covers arbitrary subgroup families without maximality,
conjugacy, or transitivity assumptions.

If N is a nontrivial normal subgroup of G, irreducibility gives N intersect V
in {1,V}. In the first case [N,V] is contained in N intersect V and is trivial.
Hence N centralizes V. An affine element (v,h) centralizes all translations
exactly when h acts identically, and the matrix action is faithful; thus
C_G(V)=V. This forces N<=V and then N=1, a contradiction. Therefore every
nontrivial normal subgroup contains V. Every nonfaithful action consequently
factors through H and has minimal bases of size at most b(H)=3. Together
with the faithful bound this gives b(G)<=3.

The exact independence upper bound has a similar, but importantly distinct,
argument. An unrestricted irredundant family all of whose members contain V
passes to an irredundant family in H, of size at most three. Otherwise choose
K missing V and apply t-1<=mu'(K)<=3. Thus mu'(G)<=4 without assuming its
bottom is normal.

### 6. Lower bounds, obstruction, and cores

The three linear elements corresponding to the R_i remain indispensable by
projection onto H; the nonzero translation is indispensable because the other
three lie in the zero-translation complement. This proves the displayed
four-element set is independent. It generates G because it contains H and
a nonzero vector whose orbit spans V by irreducibility. Each two-generator
linear subgroup has order greater than four, so none can preserve a line;
its affine omission subgroup has order 841 times 8, 12, or 20 as stated.

The faithful three-family can be inspected directly: each consists of its
translation line with linear part I and one coset of that line with linear
part -I. Enumerating these 58-element sets confirms every displayed pairwise
intersection and the trivial triple intersection. Therefore b_f(G)>=3, and
the coset action degree is indeed 3*100920/58=5220.

For the four-element independent set, the omission family has bottom Z_0 in
the zero-translation complement. Conjugation by translation e1 sends its
nonidentity element to (2e1,-I), so Z_0 is nonnormal. This is a concrete
failure of the normal-bottom requirement, consistent with the upper bound.

Each proper pair subgroup of H contains Z, and its core is exactly Z because
its quotient core is a proper normal subgroup of A5. The corresponding
preimage subgroups in G therefore have core V semidirect Z. The complement's
core is trivial by the normal-subgroup argument; also the asserted stronger
fact that H meets its e1-translate conjugate trivially is true, since such an
intersection corresponds to elements fixing e1. A nonidentity element fixing
e1 would be nontrivial unipotent of order 29. The checker confirms that only I
fixes e1. Thus the displayed list of cores is correct and destroys irredundancy.

## Adversarial boundary checks and editorial recommendations

1. The characteristic-11 example is internally valid. The independent checker
   constructs its order-120 complement, checks membership and order ten of C,
   checks both invariant lines, and verifies the total/deletion intersection
   orders 1 and (11,11,5,2). Therefore the line-stabilizer restriction is an
   actual necessary feature of this proof strategy, not cosmetic.
2. The statement about the complement meeting its conjugate trivially would be
   clearer with the one-sentence unipotent argument just given. This is an
   expository improvement, not a missing hypothesis.
3. The term “generating set” in the A4 discussion means one generating A4 itself;
   the next sentence handles proper subgroups. This is logically valid as
   written, but saying “a generating set of A4” removes possible ambiguity.
4. Keep the distinction among mu', b, and b_f explicit throughout. In particular
   b_f(H)=2 does not imply b(H)=2, and b(H)=3 is essential to the quotient step.
5. The exact verifier and manuscript should agree on the corrected product
   orders. Do not retain the erroneous value six merely because the resulting
   subgroup happens to have order twelve.
