                                                  Abstract
          Let b(G) be the largest size of an inclusion-minimal base, over all permutation representations
      of a ﬁnite group G, with bases taken for the permutation image. Let µ0 (G) be the largest size of
      an independent subset of G, not necessarily generating G. We construct an explicitly speciﬁed
      aﬃne group of order 100920 for which

                                           b(G) = 3 < 4 = µ0 (G).

      The upper bound covers faithful and nonfaithful actions and does not depend on enumerating
      the subgroups of the aﬃne group. Exact, independently implemented Python and C++ veriﬁers
      certify the small matrix-group inputs and the explicit witnesses. The obstruction is a central
      involution of a complement which is not normal in the ambient aﬃne group.


1     Introduction and conventions
Cameron’s Problem 16.45 in the Kourovka Notebook asks whether two invariants of every ﬁnite
group coincide: the largest size of an inclusion-minimal permutation base, and the largest size
of an independent subset. The inequality b(G) ≤ µ0 (G) is known. We give a negative answer by
constructing an aﬃne group G = F229 ⋊H, where H = ∼ SL2 (5) acts through explicitly given matrices,
and proving
                                 bf (G) = b(G) = 3 < 4 = µ0 (G).
Here bf restricts the base invariant to faithful actions. No minimum-order assertion is made.
    The distinction between faithful and arbitrary actions is essential to the proof: for the comple-
ment, bf (H) = 2 whereas b(H) = 3. Every subgroup of G not containing its translation subgroup
has faithful base invariant at most two. Restricting an irredundant intersection family to such a
subgroup bounds faithful bases in G; the normal-subgroup structure then covers all nonfaithful
actions. The upper bound is structural and requires no enumeration of the subgroup lattice of the
order-100920 aﬃne group.

1.1   Deﬁnitions and relation to earlier work
A subset S of a ﬁnite group X is independent if x ∈/ hS \ {x}i for each x ∈ S; the empty subset is
allowed. Write µ0 (X) for its maximum possible size. For an action ρ : X → Sym(Ω), a base has
pointwise stabilizer ker ρ in X. Minimal means inclusion-minimal. Write b(X) for the maximum
size of a minimal base over all actions, and bf (X) for the corresponding maximum restricted to

faithful actions. Even if Ω is inﬁnite, a base contains a ﬁnite subbase: choose a point moved by
each nonidentity element of the ﬁnite permutation image. Consequently every inclusion-minimal
base is ﬁnite.
    These conventions give b(1) = bf (1) = µ0 (1) = 0. Cameron’s 2010 exposition uses b2 for our b
and µ∗ for our µ0 ; his 2014 preprint uses b2 and µ0 , and his 2024 published account uses b2 and µ↑ ,
respectively [2, 3, 4]. These sources allow nonfaithful representations. The foundational subgroup
and Boolean meet-semilattice formulations below are due to Cameron [3, 4]; we include proofs to
ﬁx the normality and action-kernel conventions used in our construction.
    The September 2026 notebook lists Problem 16.45 without an attached solution [1]. A focused
search of the notebook, its update, and the cited primary literature found no earlier resolution.
This reports the scope of the search, not a guarantee of bibliographic priority.

    We record the elementary group facts needed below. Every proper subgroup of A5 lies in a
subgroup isomorphic to A4 , D10 , or S3 . Here D10 denotes a group of order 10. One way to see this
is to use the natural action on ﬁve letters. An intransitive subgroup either ﬁxes a letter or has orbit
sizes 2 + 3. A proper transitive subgroup has order 5, 10, 15, 20, or 30. Orders 15 and 20 would
give a normal Sylow 5-subgroup, whose normalizer in A5 has order 10. Order 30 is impossible: an
index-two quotient is trivial on every 3-cycle, and 3-cycles generate A5 . The remaining cases lie in

a Sylow-5 normalizer. This normalizer has order 10 because the order-20 normalizer in S5 contains
odd permutations.
    Each of A4 , D10 , S3 has independence number 2. For A4 , a generating set contains a 3-cycle
and some element outside its cyclic subgroup; these two generate A4 , since there is no subgroup of
order 6. Its proper subgroups have independence number at most 2. For a dihedral group of order
2q, q prime, a generating set contains either a nontrivial rotation and a reﬂection, or two distinct
reﬂections, and two such elements suﬃce. It follows, by deleting one element from any independent
set in A5 , that
                                            µ0 (A5 ) ≤ 3.                                        (3)
The natural degree-ﬁve action has a minimal base of size 3, so equality holds. Simplicity, used above
and below, also has an elementary check: the nonidentity conjugacy-class sizes are 15, 20, 12, 12,
and no proper nontrivial union of classes together with the identity has order dividing 60.

Lemma 4. The complement satisﬁes

                                 µ0 (H) = b(H) = 3,      bf (H) = 2.

Proof. The only involution in SL2 (29) is −I: a matrix squaring to I is diagonalizable with eigenval-
ues ±1, and determinant one forces both signs to agree. Therefore every even-order subgroup of H
contains Z. Every odd-order subgroup injects into A5 and, by the preceding subgroup description,
is trivial or cyclic of order 3 or 5.
    Let S be independent in H. If its images in H/Z, as an indexed family, are independent, then
|S| ≤ 3. Otherwise some s ∈ S lies in KZ, where K = hS \ {s}i, but does not lie in K. Thus Z
is not contained in K, so K has odd order and is trivial or cyclic of prime order. Since S \ {s} is
independent, |S| ≤ 2. This proves µ0 (H) ≤ 3.
    For the faithful invariant, a subgroup family with intersection 1 must have an odd-order member,
since all even-order members contain Z. If this member is trivial, a minimal such family has size
1. If it has prime order, some other member intersects it trivially, so a minimal family has size at
most 2. Nontrivial subgroups of orders 3 and 5 give a faithful family of size 2, proving bf (H) = 2.

4     The aﬃne counterexample and the all-actions upper bound
Set
                                      G = V ⋊ H,          V = F229 ,
with multiplication
                                      (v, h)(w, k) = (v + hw, hk).
The group order is 292 · 120 = 100920; write π : G → H for projection.        This description speciﬁes
the action, not just the abstract factors of a semidirect product.
Lemma 7. If K ≤ G does not contain V , then bf (K) ≤ 2. More precisely, if K ∩ V = 1, then
µ0 (K) ≤ 3 and bf (K) ≤ 2; if dimF29 (K ∩ V ) = 1, then µ0 (K) ≤ 2.
Proof. Every additive subgroup of V is an F29 -subspace, since 29 is prime. If K ∩ V = 1, then π
embeds K in H. Both µ0 and bf are monotone for subgroups: for the latter, use the same subgroup
family with trivial intersection in the larger group. Apply Lemma 4.
    Otherwise W = K ∩ V is a line, normal in K, so π(K) ≤ HW is cyclic of order dividing 4. A
Sylow 2-subgroup of K maps isomorphically onto π(K) and complements W . Its action on W is
faithful by Lemma 6. Thus K is isomorphic to a group covered by Lemma 5, which gives µ0 (K) ≤ 2
and hence bf (K) ≤ 2. These are all possibilities for a subgroup not containing V .
Proposition 8. bf (G) ≤ 3.
Proof. Let K1 , . . . , Kt be any meet-irredundant family with total intersection 1. Some Ki does not
contain V , since otherwise the intersection would contain V . Lemmas 3 and 7 give
                                          t − 1 ≤ bf (Ki ) ≤ 2.
No transitivity, maximality, or conjugacy assumption on the subgroups was made.

Proposition 9. b(G) ≤ 3, including all nonfaithful actions.
Proof. Every nontrivial normal subgroup N ◁ G contains V . To prove this, irreducibility gives
N ∩ V = 1 or V . In the ﬁrst case [N, V ] ≤ N ∩ V = 1, so N ≤ CG (V ). The matrix action is faithful,
hence CG (V ) = V , forcing N = 1. This contradicts the choice of N .
   It follows that every nonfaithful action of G factors through G/V = H, and its minimal bases
have size at most b(H) = 3. Faithful actions are covered by Proposition 8. This proves the stated
bound for every representation.


5    Explicit lower bounds and the exact independence number
Let e1 = (1, 0)T and e2 = (0, 1)T . With the matrices in (4), deﬁne

                              S = {(e1 , I), (0, R1 ), (0, R2 ), (0, R3 )} ⊆ G.

The three linear elements project to the independent triple in H. Thus no one of them is gener-
ated by the other three elements of S, since its projection would then be generated by the other
two matrices. The translation (e1 , I) is not in the zero-translation complement. Therefore S is
independent and µ0 (G) ≥ 4.
    In fact S generates G: it contains generators for H, and the orbit of a nonzero vector under an
irreducible group spans V . Its omission subgroups have the following explicitly veriﬁed orders:

                 Omitted element      Order of the subgroup generated by the others
                 (e1 , I)                                       120
                 (0, R1 )                                 841 · 8 = 6728
                 (0, R2 )                                841 · 12 = 10092
                 (0, R3 )                                841 · 20 = 16820

The latter three formulas also follow structurally: each corresponding pair subgroup of H has order
greater than 4, so cannot stabilize a line and is irreducible on V .
Proposition 10. µ0 (G) = 4.
Proof. It remains to give the upper bound. By Lemma 2, consider any meet-irredundant family
K1 , . . . , Kt , with no assumption on its total intersection. If all members contain V , quotienting by
the genuinely normal subgroup V gives an irredundant family in H, so t ≤ 3. Otherwise choose
Ki 6⊇ V . If Ki ∩ V = 1, then t − 1 ≤ µ0 (Ki ) ≤ 3. If Ki ∩ V is a line, then t − 1 ≤ µ0 (Ki ) ≤ 2. In
either case t ≤ 4.

    There is also a faithful minimal base of size 3. Deﬁne subgroups

normal bottom.
    Taking normal cores does not repair this example. Simplicity of H/Z =     ∼ A5 implies that the
three proper pair subgroups of H have core Z. Since M2 , M3 , M4 contain V , their cores in G are
V ⋊ Z. The complement M1 = H has trivial core, since every nontrivial normal subgroup of G
contains V , while H ∩ V = 1. Hence the four cores are

                                     1,   V ⋊ Z,     V ⋊ Z,     V ⋊ Z.

The ﬁrst member alone gives their total intersection. All four-way essentiality has been lost. No
quotient by the nonnormal subgroup Z0 is used anywhere in the argument.

and agree.
