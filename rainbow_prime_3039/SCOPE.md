# Exact target and public sources

For every sufficiently large prime p, does every partition of Z/pZ into four classes, each of cardinality floor(p/4) or ceil(p/4), contain a modular arithmetic progression a,a+d,a+2d,a+3d with d nonzero and all four colors distinct? The phrase “large prime” is interpreted asymptotically, with no explicit threshold specified. A single small-prime example would not settle this question.

Original source: David Conlon, *Rainbow solutions of linear equations over Z_p*, Discrete Mathematics 306 (2006), 2056–2063. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2006.03.070 . Author manuscript: https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dconlon/RE.pdf , Section 5, Problem 6, manuscript page 9. The floor/ceiling cardinality condition was visually checked in the manuscript during source recovery on 2026-10-19/20 UTC; text extraction was unreliable. The source document and page images are not distributed here.

Public catalog: https://garden.irmacs.sfu.ca/op/rainbow_ap_4_in_an_almost_equinumerous_coloring .

Related background: Conlon–Jungic–Radoicic, *On the existence of rainbow 4-term arithmetic progressions*, Graphs and Combinatorics 23 (2007), 249–254, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-007-0723-2 . This is interval/composite-modulus background, not a resolution of the target.

Jana–Das, *A Note On Rainbow 4-Term Arithmetic Progression*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01086 , inspected version https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01086v1 , supplies the four interval words examined in the computation report. Interval and composite-modulus results cannot be substituted for an asymptotic prime-modulus theorem. No general correctness endorsement of that preprint is made.

A bounded literature check found no resolution of this exact target. This is not an exhaustive literature-status certificate. This package does not solve the target and makes no novelty claim.
