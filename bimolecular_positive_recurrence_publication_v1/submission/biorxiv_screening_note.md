# Screening note for a possible bioRxiv submission

**Proposed category:** Systems Biology

This manuscript presents a new theorem about stochastic biochemical reaction
network models. Continuous-time Markov chains with stochastic mass-action
rates are used in systems biology to model low-copy-number intracellular,
signaling, enzymatic, gene-regulatory, and synthetic biochemical systems. The
paper proves that, for every positive rate vector, each closed communicating
class has a unique stationary probability law when the finite reaction
network is weakly reversible, has one linkage class, and has molecularity at
most two.

The biological relevance is the existence of a stationary molecule-count
distribution and long-run state frequencies within the stated class. Finite
unbounded molecule-count moments require additional integrability and are not
claimed. The paper does not claim that all biochemical networks satisfy its
structural assumptions, and its motivating cycles are abstract
reaction-network motifs, not experimentally validated molecular mechanisms.
It supplies no clinical, organism-specific, or empirical claim.

The result is proved analytically. A deterministic verification package
accompanies the manuscript and checks exact identities, boundary examples,
finite calibration chains, and reproducibility. These outputs are
falsification aids and do not replace the universal proof. No empirical
biological dataset was collected or analyzed.

This note is intended to make the manuscript's direct systems-biology model
relevance and its limitations transparent. It does not presume or guarantee
that bioRxiv will determine the mathematical work to be in scope.
