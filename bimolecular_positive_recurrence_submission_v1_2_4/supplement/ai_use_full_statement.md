## Treatment of outputs and rejected approaches

AI output was treated as working material rather than scholarly authority or
independent expert validation. Literature leads, bibliographic metadata,
policy statements, and priority claims were checked against the primary
publisher, preprint, conference, or institutional records identified in the
manuscript and supplements. Mathematical identities and finite calibrations
used in the released argument are exposed in the proof or deterministic
verification package so that they can be checked independently.

Rejected AI-assisted approaches are not part of the final proof. These
included universal one-step Lyapunov claims, rate-changing cycle comparisons
without a valid uniform bound, and incomplete fast--slow hierarchy arguments.
The released proof is the marked-channel, carried-target,
residual-log-factorial, finite-path argument presented in the manuscript.

No AI-generated raster figure appears in the Version 1.2.4 manuscript. The
decorative schematic used in Version 0.3 was removed before Version 1.0.

## Direction, authorship, and responsibility

The work was developed through an iterative, human-directed research process.
The author determined the scope and claims released here, curated the package,
and assumes responsibility for the manuscript and verification materials.

No AI system is an author. The proof, source, exact verifier, canonical
reports, manifests, and audit materials are supplied to make the AI-mediated
research independently checkable. No claim of prior independent expert human
validation is made.

## Cambridge/Applied Probability policy alignment

This record was rechecked on 16 August 2026 against the *Journal of Applied
Probability* and Applied Probability Trust instructions. Those instructions require
declaration and description when AI generated manuscript text or was used to
analyze or extract insights. They ask, to the extent reasonably possible, for
the tool name and version, dates, access method, a full description of use,
and appropriate acknowledgement of any third-party material. They also place
accountability on the author and do not permit AI tools to be listed as
authors. The manuscript provides the concise reader-facing declaration; this
supplement provides the fuller chronology and roles.

The Version 1.2.2 preparation also checked the then-current bioRxiv scope,
screening, article-category, permanence, and licensing information and the
current *Stochastic Processes and their Applications* submission and AI
policies. The bioRxiv scope, screening, and DOI guidance were rechecked on 22
August 2026; the remaining policy notes retain their stated historical check
dates. bioRxiv is the intended first preprint route; Applied Probability is
the recommended journal route, with SPA retained as a credible alternative.
