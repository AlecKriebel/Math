## What the generated specialization actually assumes

This is a reconstruction of the supplied attempt, not wording attributed to AIM. It assumes a smooth projective toric variety X with fan Sigma, polynomial Cox ring S=k[x_rho], and irrelevant ideal B generated by chart monomials u_sigma=product of x_rho outside a maximal cone sigma. The affine toric chart is Spec((S[u_sigma^(-1)])_0); the full localization is its Cox-space counterpart, not literally the coordinate ring of the chart. Smoothness is material to passing between sheaf exactness and all graded localization strands.

The input is a finite regular CW complex with monomial cell labels divisible by face labels, augmented to the ideal generated by vertex labels. Virtual exactness means the augmented complex resolves the associated ideal sheaf, allowing irrelevant-locus homology over S. This is not the assertion that every virtual resolution is cellular, and not the ordinary exactness of the complex over S.

The proposed finite criterion tests reduced homology of label subcomplexes on each maximal cone, ignoring exponents of inverted variables; thresholds range over a finite Cartesian product of coordinate exponent sets. Testing just the distinct whole restricted label vectors is not equivalent without an additional argument: the attempt’s next-step shorthand must not replace its Cartesian-product theorem statement.

A local Morse matching must be acyclic and match incidences whose coefficients are units in the chosen localization. For the monomial regular-cell setting this is equality of exponents on the non-inverted variables, with invertible incidence signs. Equality only of Cox divisor classes is not this condition. One matching homogeneous on every chart forces equal full monomial labels. This elementary observation is narrower than an impossibility theorem for all global virtual minimization or all homological perturbation methods. Local critical summands and local differentials do not automatically descend to one smaller global free Cox complex.

## Published positive analogue

Hanlon–Hicks–Lazarev, *Resolutions of toric subvarieties by line bundles and applications*, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 12 (2024), e24, https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2024.21 ; inspected arXiv v3: https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03763v3 . Appendix A, Theorem A.8, gives chain-homotopy invariance for an acyclic partial matching on an oriented Morse quiver with an abelian-category-valued sheaf, provided matched arrows become isomorphisms. Section 4.2 applies this after toric restriction. Theorem A constructs line-bundle resolutions for closed toric substacks in stacks covered by smooth stacky charts; positive-characteristic local-model qualifications must be retained (Remark 1.2). Corollary C gives length at most dim(X) virtual resolutions for finitely generated Pic(X)-graded B-saturated Cox modules when X is smooth projective. These are constructive positive analogues, not a theorem that an arbitrary supplied virtual free complex admits a globally minimal Morse reduction.

## Later, more specific minimization

Zengrui Han, *Minimal resolutions of toric substacks by line bundles*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14384v3 , inspected v3 dated 17 September 2026. Inspected full PDF and HTML, especially §§1.2–1.4 and Theorem 1.2. It constructs strong deformation retracts of the HHL cellular complexes via homological perturbation and Moore–Penrose inverses. Its Cox formulation concerns a **normal closed toric subvariety of a simplicial projective toric variety**, and resolves S/I_Y virtually. This is not restricted merely to smooth varieties, but is also not a theorem about arbitrary modules or arbitrary starting complexes. Minimal virtual resolutions need not be unique. The inspected manuscript remains a preprint for this audit; no publication or acceptance claim is made here. Its Moore–Penrose construction uses complex coefficient spaces and standard Hermitian inner products; arbitrary-field generality is not claimed. Its examples discuss perfect Morse matchings, but their existence is not a universal hypothesis-free minimization theorem.
