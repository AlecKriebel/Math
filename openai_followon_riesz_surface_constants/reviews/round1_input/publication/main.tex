\begin{abstract}
We deduce the exact two-dimensional hypersingular minimal Riesz-energy
constant for every real $s>2$ from OpenAI's recently released triangular-lattice
universal-optimality theorem. The constant is the Epstein sum of the
covolume-one triangular lattice. An explicit gap-periodization argument
relates the finite square problem to the source theorem's centered-disk
lower energy, with an inter-box error of order $N^{1-s/2}$ for each fixed gap
parameter. A lattice crop gives the matching upper bound. The corrected
Hardin--Saff universality theorem then gives the ordered-pair leading
asymptotic on every smooth compact embedded surface of positive area,
including surfaces with smooth boundary, for ambient Euclidean distance.
This resolves the two-dimensional $s>2$ case of the explicit lattice-constant
conjecture recorded by Brauchart, Hardin and Saff. The universal-optimality
breakthrough, finite-to-infinite reduction and manifold theorem are inherited
results; this note gives a self-contained account of the known passage and
records its planar surface consequence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, provenance and conventions}
Put $b=\sqrt3/2$ and
\[
 \Lambda=b^{-1/2}\{j(1,0)+k(1/2,b):j,k\in\Z\},\qquad
 Z_s=\zeta_\Lambda(s)=\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\abs{v}^{-s}.
\]
The lattice has covolume one. For a compact set $K\subset\R^p$, define
\[
 \Es(K,N)=\min_{x_1,\ldots,x_N\in K}
        \sum_{i\ne j}\abs{x_i-x_j}^{-s},
\]
with collisions assigned energy $+\infty$. All pair sums in this note are
\emph{ordered}. Distances are Euclidean distances in the ambient space,
not intrinsic geodesic distances. The Hausdorff measure $\mathcal H^2$ is
normalized to agree with planar Lebesgue area.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every real $s>2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
 C_{s,2}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Es([0,1]^2,N)}{N^{1+s/2}}=Z_s.
\end{equation}
If $M\subset\R^p$ is a smooth compact embedded two-dimensional manifold,
with or without smooth boundary, and $A=\mathcal H^2(M)>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surface}
 \Es(M,N)\sim Z_s A^{-s/2}N^{1+s/2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the unit sphere in $\R^3$, with chordal distance, has
coefficient $Z_s/(4\pi)^{s/2}$.
\end{theorem}

The candidate constant in dimension two goes back to Kuijlaars and
Saff~\cite[Conjecture 1]{KS1998}, whose unordered-pair coefficient must be
doubled in our convention; Brauchart--Hardin--Saff~\cite[Conjecture 2]{BHS2012}
state the formula $C_{s,d}=\covol(\Lambda_d)^{s/d}\zeta_{\Lambda_d}(s)$
for $s>d$ in dimensions $2,4,8,24$. We establish only its $d=2$, $s>2$
part. The result concerns the leading minimum value, not microscopic
crystallization or uniqueness of finite minimizers. It makes no new assertion
for $s\le2$ or about a second-order term.

The new pivotal input is OpenAI's infinite-plane theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{OAIUniversal}, also proved in its atomic companion
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{OAIAtomic}. We use the exact source version at repository
commit \texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}.
For a locally finite $\mathcal C\subset\R^2$, let $B_R$ be the closed disk
centered at zero and $n_{\mathcal C}(R)=\#(\mathcal C\cap B_R)$. The source
uses centered density $n_{\mathcal C}(R)/(\pi R^2)\to1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinite}
 \Hs(\mathcal C)=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac1{n_{\mathcal C}(R)}
   \sum_{\substack{x,y\in\mathcal C\cap B_R\\x\ne y}}\abs{x-y}^{-s}.
\end{equation}
The imported theorem says that $\Hs(\mathcal C)\ge Z_s$ for every such
configuration. Indeed $g(t)=t^{-s/2}$ satisfies its smooth nonnegative
complete-monotonicity hypotheses, since
\[
 (-1)^r g^{(r)}(t)=(s/2)_r t^{-s/2-r}>0\quad(t>0,\ r\ge0).
\]
Here $(s/2)_0=1$. Section~\ref{sec:periodic} proves the exact finite transfer;
only the square-periodic special case of the imported theorem is needed.
The finite/periodic asymptotic equivalence was proved by Hardin, Saff and
Simanek~\cite[Theorem 3.2]{HSS2014}. Hardin, Lebl'e, Saff and
Serfaty~\cite[Proposition 3.1]{HLSS2018} identify the thermodynamic minimum
with $C_{s,d}$; their published note explicitly draws the analogous
$d=8,24$ consequence of universal optimality. Periodic centered-ball
limits also appear in Cohn--Kumar~\cite[Lemma 9.1]{CK2007}. The passage
proved below is this established reduction in the present conventions.
This is an explicit newly available consequence of the planar theorem,
not an independently discovered base breakthrough. We do not claim an
independent proof of planar universal optimality, invention of these
reductions, or first public priority for the corollary.

\section{Periodic disk limits and a gap-periodization bound}\label{sec:periodic}
We first justify the disk-energy limit for the periodic configurations used
below, so there is no implicit change of averaging convention.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:periodic}
Let $L>0$, $q\ge1$, and $F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_q\}\subset[0,L)^2$ consist of distinct
points. For $\mathcal C=F+L\Z^2$ and $s>2$, the centered density is $q/L^2$,
and the lower limit in \eqref{eq:infinite} is an ordinary finite limit equal to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rows}
 \frac1q\sum_{i=1}^q h_i,\qquad
 h_i=\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le q,\ k\in\Z^2\\(j,k)\ne(i,0)}}
       \abs{f_i-f_j-Lk}^{-s}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Representations are unique in the half-open fundamental square, and the set
is locally finite. For $m=\norm{k}_\infty\ge2$, each denominator in the
$i$th row is at least $L(m-1)\ge Lm/2$. There are $8m$ such translations,
so the tail is bounded by $8q(2/L)^s\sum_{m\ge2}m^{1-s}<\infty$.
The remaining finitely many denominators are nonzero. Thus $h_i<\infty$;
all rearrangements of these nonnegative sums are justified by Tonelli.

Let $n_i(R)=\#((f_i+L\Z^2)\cap B_R)$ and $d=L\sqrt2/2$.
The side-$L$ cells centered at these points give
\[
 \pi(R-d)^2/L^2\le n_i(R)\le\pi(R+d)^2/L^2\quad(R>d).
\]
Their union contains $B_{R-d}$ and is contained in $B_{R+d}$.
Consequently $n_i(R)=\pi R^2/L^2+O(R/L+1)$ and
$n_i(R)/n_{\mathcal C}(R)\to1/q$.

Write $S_R$ for the numerator in \eqref{eq:infinite} and let $h_i^{(T)}$
be the row restricted to displacements of length at most $T$.
For $R>T$, completing outgoing rows and, respectively, retaining only
rows from $B_{R-T}$ give
\[
 \sum_i n_i(R-T)h_i^{(T)}\le S_R\le\sum_i n_i(R)h_i.
\]
Divide by $n_{\mathcal C}(R)$ and take $R\to\infty$ at fixed $T$.
Then $T\to\infty$ gives $h_i^{(T)}\uparrow h_i$ by monotone convergence,
proving \eqref{eq:rows}. This also proves the asserted density.
\end{proof}

For $\lambda>0$, direct substitution of $R/\lambda$ in the definitions gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 \operatorname{dens}(\lambda\mathcal C)=\lambda^{-2}\operatorname{dens}(\mathcal C),
 \qquad \Hs(\lambda\mathcal C)=\lambda^{-s}\Hs(\mathcal C).
\end{equation}
Let $a_N=N^{-1-s/2}\Es([0,1]^2,N)$, and distinguish the Riemann zeta
function $\zeta_{\mathrm R}(u)=\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-u}$ from the lattice sum.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
For each $s>2$, $N\ge1$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitebound}
 a_N\ge(1+\varepsilon)^{-s}Z_s
       -8\varepsilon^{-s}\zeta_{\mathrm R}(s-1)N^{1-s/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take any distinct $N$-point set $X=\{x_i\}\subset[0,1]^2$, set
$y_i=\sqrt N x_i$, $L=(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N$, and repeat
$F=\{y_i\}$ by $L\Z^2$. The strict gap makes $F\subset[0,L)^2$, even
when $X$ contains boundary points, and the density is $(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:periodic}, its energy per point is
\[
 N^{-1-s/2}\sum_{i\ne j}\abs{x_i-x_j}^{-s}+I_{N,\varepsilon},\qquad
 I_{N,\varepsilon}=\frac1N\sum_{i,j=1}^N\sum_{k\ne0}
                  \abs{y_i-y_j-Lk}^{-s}.
\]
The cross-cell sum includes $i=j$ for nonzero translations.
For $m=\norm{k}_\infty\ge1$, a coordinate with $\abs{k_a}=m$ gives
\[
 \abs{y_i-y_j-Lk}\ge\sqrt N\big((1+\varepsilon)m-1\big)
                       \ge\varepsilon\sqrt N\,m.
\]
Counting the $8m$ translations in that shell yields the uniform bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross}
 0\le I_{N,\varepsilon}\le
 8\varepsilon^{-s}\zeta_{\mathrm R}(s-1)N^{1-s/2}.
\end{equation}
No separation or occupancy bound within $X$ was used.

Compress the repeated configuration by $\lambda=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}$.
It now has centered density one. Equations~\eqref{eq:rows}--\eqref{eq:scaling}
and the imported universal-optimality theorem give
\[
 Z_s\le(1+\varepsilon)^s\left[
       N^{-1-s/2}\sum_{i\ne j}\abs{x_i-x_j}^{-s}+I_{N,\varepsilon}\right].
\]
Combine with \eqref{eq:cross} and minimize over $X$ to prove
\eqref{eq:finitebound}. Finite minimizers exist by lower semicontinuity
on the compact product after assigning $+\infty$ to collisions.
\end{proof}

For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the error in \eqref{eq:finitebound} tends to zero
because $s>2$. Hence
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}a_N\ge(1+\varepsilon)^{-s}Z_s,
 \qquad \liminf_{N\to\infty}a_N\ge Z_s
\]
after $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The limit order is the disk radius first
at each fixed $N,\varepsilon$, then $N\to\infty$ at fixed $\varepsilon$,
then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. There is no uniform-in-$N$ disk-limit
assumption and no periodization with zero gap.

\section{The matching lattice upper bound}
The sum $Z_s$ converges for $s>2$: if $\Lambda=B\Z^2$ with $B$ invertible,
then $\abs{Bk}\ge c\norm{k}_\infty$ for some $c>0$, and
$Z_s\le8c^{-s}\sum_{m\ge1}m^{1-s}<\infty$.
Take a bounded fundamental parallelogram $P$ of $\Lambda$, contained in
$[-D,D]^2$ for some $D>0$. Its translates tile the plane up to cell
boundaries and each has area one. For $t\ge2D$, every point of
$[D,t-D]^2$ lies in a cell whose lattice representative belongs to
$[0,t]^2$. Therefore
\[
 \#(\Lambda\cap[0,t]^2)\ge(t-2D)^2.
\]
With $t_N=\sqrt N+2D$, select exactly $N$ points $Y_N$ from this crop.
Each outgoing row of the finite set is at most the full lattice sum $Z_s$.
Scale by $1/t_N$ into the unit square to obtain
\[
 \Es([0,1]^2,N)\le t_N^s N Z_s,\qquad
 a_N\le Z_s\left(1+\frac{2D}{\sqrt N}\right)^s.
\]
Thus $\limsup a_N\le Z_s$, proving \eqref{eq:square}.
Selecting an exact-$N$ subset avoids any assumption that a lattice crop
can be chosen with exactly $N$ sites. No tail is discarded with an
uncontrolled error.

\section{Transfer to surfaces and verification scope}
Hardin and Saff~\cite[Theorem 2.4]{HS2005} prove that, for $s>d$, a compact
$d$-rectifiable manifold $K\subset\R^{d'}$ of positive $\mathcal H^d$
measure satisfies
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Es(K,N)}{N^{1+s/d}}
          =\frac{C_{s,d}}{\mathcal H^d(K)^{s/d}}.
\]
Their definition (19) is a finite union $K=\bigcup_k\varphi_k(K_k)$,
where each $K_k\subset\R^d$ is compact and $\varphi_k$ is bi-Lipschitz
on an open set $G_k\supset K_k$. The theorem uses ambient Euclidean
distance, ordered pairs, and the unit-cube-normalized constant.
We use the corrected proof, with the addendum included in the cited
arXiv version, rather than the uncorrected neighborhood assertion in
Proposition 8.3.

A smooth compact embedded surface with smooth boundary has finitely many
such compact chart pieces. At a boundary point, its smooth chart on a
half-disk extends to an open planar neighborhood; after shrinking, the
extension is bi-Lipschitz by the full-rank derivative and local graph
representation. The pieces on the half-disk remain compact subsets of
that open neighborhood. Thus the $s>d$ theorem allows boundary here.
The extra containment in a $C^1$ manifold without boundary in the source's
$s=d$ clause is not needed for $s>2$.

For completeness, the $s>d$ addendum's neighborhood lemma is used only
inside a \emph{compact} regular thickening: cover each chart piece by
finitely many closed cubes contained in $G_k$ and take the union of their
bi-Lipschitz images. This is a compact $d$-regular set containing $K$.
Consequently the closure of each relative neighborhood is compact and
its positive-distance step is valid. No unrestricted unbounded-neighborhood
version of that lemma is invoked. Applying the theorem with $d=2$ and
\eqref{eq:square} proves \eqref{eq:surface}; the sphere has area $4\pi$.
