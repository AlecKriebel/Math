# Independent mathematical audit: row 1848

## Verdict and scope

## Automaton and exhaustive checks
The eight compatible separable forbidden patterns of length four are exactly 1342, 2314, 2341, 2431, 3241, 3412, 3421, 4231. Direct enumeration of all permutations through length seven, with independent subsequence standardization and avoidance tests, agrees with all 255 nonempty relative-basis coefficient lists. Ambient counts are 1,1,2,6,22,90,394,1806. The audit also constructed and checked 2,321 distinct nonempty canonical trees through that length.

An additional independent witness check verifies every one of 124,258 automaton transitions across 9,218 reachable states (counting states separately for each basis): direct subsequence masks of composed witness permutations equal the computed masks and avoid all basis members.

The binary convention is sound: take the first indecomposable component for the root operation as the left child. Its root cannot have the same sign as the parent. The right child represents the remaining components and may have either sign. Sum- and skew-decomposability are mutually exclusive for a nontrivial permutation. These facts give a unique parse and prevent associativity overcounting.

For every tracked pattern, an occurrence either lies in one child or crosses the split. In the latter case, its indices split into a prefix and suffix, with all values ordered across the split according to the parent sign. The transition's tests of every sum/skew cut are therefore necessary and sufficient. The set of all subpatterns of basis members includes every factor needed by a cut. Rejecting a forbidden-pattern bit is exact. Reachability terminates in a finite state space, and the final stable pass visits all state pairs. The positive-size coefficient recursion is well-founded and does not introduce empty children. This reasoning certifies the automaton independently of the finite numerical tests.

The implementation is used here only for nonempty bases of length four. It should not be advertised as handling an empty basis without modification, since the empty union construction would fail.

## Exact Av(3412) check
Let G include the empty permutation and A count sum-indecomposables. Then G=1/(1-A). A consists of a singleton or a skew-root object. In a canonical skew decomposition each child is singleton or sum-decomposable. Every nonsingleton child therefore contains 12; two such children create 3412. Conversely 3412 cannot cross children unless at least two contain 12. Its two ascending pairs cannot span a skew split. Consequently

A=x+x²/(1-x)+(G-1-A)((1-x)^(-2)-1).

Elimination gives x(x-2)G²+(x+1)G-1=0. Its discriminant is (1-x)(1-5x). The correct branch is
G=(-1-x+sqrt((1-x)(1-5x)))/(2x(x-2)),
with G(0)=1. It begins 1,1,2,6,21,79,311,1265 and agrees with brute force. Setting G=1+F gives exactly the research report's polynomial for nonempty F. Simple zeros at x=1 and x=1/5 show that the discriminant is not a square in Q(x); the quadratic is irreducible over Q(x), certifying degree two and nonrationality. This is a known symmetry-related example, not a claimed new solution.

## Actual-class obstruction to a dominant-square-root proof
For N≥3 let C_N be the direct-sum closure of Av(231) union all separable permutations of size at most N. A pattern of a direct sum is a direct sum of patterns of the summands, after omitting empty selections. Thus C_N is downward closed, remains within Sep, and contains Av(231). This is not an arbitrary positive grammar.

Av(231) is sum closed since 231 is sum indecomposable. Its sum-indecomposable generating function is x Cat(x). Among separables of length n≥2, exactly half are sum indecomposable: each is uniquely sum- or skew-decomposable and complement exchanges the two cases. Hence the additional allowed indecomposables have polynomial
P_N=Σ(n=3..N)(s_n/2-Cat_(n-1))x^n,
and G_N=1/(1-x Cat(x)-P_N). There are no further indecomposables generated by taking direct sums.

For N=11 the coefficients of P_N at degrees 3 through 11 are 1,6,31,155,771,3850,19363,98187,502063. Exact evaluation gives P_11(1/4)=2097931/4194304=1/2+779/4194304. Thus I(x)=x Cat(x)+P_11(x) increases continuously from 0 to a value greater than 1 before the Catalan boundary. There is a unique r in (0,1/4) with I(r)=1; its derivative is positive and finite, making the pole simple. Nonnegative coefficients imply no singularity inside |x|<r. The positive coefficient at degree one forces equality in the triangle bound on |x|=r only at x=r, so the dominant pole is unique. Numerically r≈0.24999996572051372455.

Nevertheless G_N is not rational: otherwise x Cat(x)=1-P_N-1/G_N would be rational, a contradiction. This rigorously refutes a universal claim that the dominant singularity must be square-root shaped, but does not refute the original conjecture. A hidden subdominant branch point suffices for nonrationality.

## Five-avenue scope audit
1. The source report and the strong-rationality theorem have distinct logical scopes. Nonrationality of an included Catalan class proves failure of strong rationality, not failure of rationality of the containing class.
2. Finite relative basing follows from well-quasi-ordering. It gives no uniform maximum basis length, so length-four exhaustion is not exhaustion of the target.
3. Modular recurrence screening is evidence only. Failure to detect a short recurrence cannot prove nonrationality, and a finite detected recurrence would still need an exact generating-function proof.
4. Positivity does not itself prevent algebraic cancellation; the genuine C_11 example additionally defeats the dominant-singularity shortcut.
5. Neither sum nor skew closure follows from being an arbitrary subclass. The workshop structural conjecture remains a conjecture. Closure-based sufficient theorems cannot replace their hypotheses with mere Catalan containment.

The reviewed report preserves these restrictions. Further symbolic elimination outputs, if added later, require a separate exact audit; this report does not pre-approve them.

## Addendum: exact Sep intersect Av(4231) certification
The newly hand-derived case is accepted. This acceptance does not include the stopped, inconclusive 1342 symbolic elimination.

Write T for the nonempty Catalan series, uniquely determined by T=x(1+T)^2 and T(0)=0. A canonical skew decomposition has at least two skew-indecomposable children. Each nonsingleton child is sum-decomposable and contains an ascent. A nonsingleton interior child would supply the middle ascent in 1 skew-sum 12 skew-sum 1=4231, using one point from each side. Thus nonsingleton children can appear only at the two endpoints. A first nonsingleton child must avoid 312 because a trailing point would complete 312 skew-sum 1=4231. A last nonsingleton child must avoid 231 because a preceding point would complete 1 skew-sum 231=4231.

These conditions are sufficient as well as necessary. Any 4231 occurrence crossing children either splits at one skew cut (4|231 or 312|1) or occupies at least three children with its central increasing pair in one child. The pair cannot cross a skew boundary. In the latter case that child is interior and nonsingleton, which is forbidden. In the former cases the endpoint restrictions exclude the occurrence, while singleton children cannot supply either three-point pattern. An occurrence within an endpoint is excluded because avoiding 312 or 231 also excludes 4231. This covers every occurrence.

Both endpoint types are therefore nonsingleton sum-decomposable permutations in Av(312) or Av(231). Both are Catalan classes and are sum closed. Their sum-indecomposable series is x(1+T): indeed the Catalan equation implies T/(1+T)=x(1+T). Hence the common endpoint series is Q=T-x(1+T)=(1-x)T-x.

The skew-decomposable series is exactly
M=x²/(1-x)+2xQ/(1-x)+Q²/(1-x)=(1-x)T².
The three terms respectively count all-singleton children, exactly one nonsingleton endpoint, and two nonsingleton endpoints; the geometric factor counts the intervening or remaining singletons without overlap. Since 4231 is sum indecomposable, its avoidance class inside Sep is sum closed and the total empty-inclusive series is
G=1/(1-x-M)=1/((1-x)(1-T²)).

Exact elimination of T gives
(4x³-9x²+6x-1)G²+(4x²-5x+1)G+x²=0.
The resultant equals this polynomial exactly. Independently, substitution of the stated rational expression in T yields a numerator with zero remainder modulo T-x(1+T)^2. Thus this is an algebraic identity, not a fitted equation. The discriminant is (1-x)²(1-2x)²(1-4x), nonsquare in Q(x) because its valuation at x=1/4 is odd. The polynomial is irreducible over Q(x), so G has degree two and is nonrational. At x=0 the polynomial is G-G², and its derivative at G=1 is -1. Therefore G(0)=1 selects a unique formal branch; the Catalan expression supplies it. Initial coefficients through degree twelve also agree with the automaton as a secondary check.
