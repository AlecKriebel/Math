## Exact claim and scope

The proposed claim is that the minimum matrix dimension of an exact **real** PSD lift of the symmetric Hamiltonian-cycle polytope of the complete graph on N cities is at least 2^(cN), for a fixed c > 0 and all sufficiently large N. If an explicit 2^O(N) formulation is checked, the order is 2^Theta(N). General unrestricted PSD matrices are essential: statements only about symmetry, bounded blocks, approximation, or a fixed hierarchy do not settle this claim.

Priority assessment must distinguish a new base lower-bound argument from an immediate consequence of an available base theorem. If family 126 is valid, the principal new lower-bound mechanism is OpenAI's matching result. Yannakakis's reduction and standard monotonicity then supply the TSP consequence. The present project's useful contribution can be an explicit, independently checked account of the consequence and conventions, not an independently discovered lower-bound method.

## Primary-source comparison

**Lee–Raghavendra–Steurer (LRS).** [arXiv:1411.6317v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6317v1), first submitted November 24, 2014; STOC 2015, DOI [10.1145/2746539.2746599](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746599). Section 1.1 measures size by k for an affine section of real symmetric k-by-k PSD matrices and a linear image. Theorem 1.1 states COR(n) >= 2^Omega(n^(2/13)); Corollary 1.2 states symmetric TSP(N) >= 2^Omega(N^(1/13)). Proposition 5.2 gives the quadratic-size correlation-to-TSP face projection. Thus this paper gives unrestricted exact stretched-exponential lower bounds, not the target linear exponent. Its arXiv history presently lists only v1. The downloaded v1 is pinned by hash in the evidence manifest.

**Later quantitative correction.** Troy Lee, Anupam Prakash, Ronald de Wolf, and Henry Yuen, [On the Sum-of-Squares Degree of Symmetric Quadratic Functions](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2016.17), CCC 2016, pp. 17:1–17:31; arXiv [1601.02311](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02311), submitted January 11, 2016; publisher date May 19, 2016. Section 1.3.1, p. 17:5, explicitly records COR(n) >= 2^tildeOmega(n^(2/11)) and TSP(N) >= 2^tildeOmega(N^(1/11)). Footnote 3 corrects the earlier pseudo-density calculation, replacing an error bound of order 2^n/n^(3/2) by 2^n/sqrt(n); Section 3.1 gives the resulting rank calculation. Preserve the tilde: polylogarithmic losses are suppressed. This later paper is a necessary citation if discussing the strongest quantitative bound found here. Calling 1/13 the best prior bound would be inaccurate.

**Known matching-to-TSP reduction.** Mihalis Yannakakis, [Expressing combinatorial optimization problems by linear programs](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(91)90024-Y), JCSS 43(3), 441–466 (1991), Theorem 2 proof, printed p. 454. The source constructs three equal vertex sets L, M, R, cliques on L and R, and paths l_i–m_i–r_i. Its notation has matching size 2m and TSP size 6m; hence matching on an even n vertices is obtained from TSP on **3n** cities. Missing-edge coordinates are set to zero. Middle vertices force their two incident edges; restriction to L is a perfect matching, and every L matching extends to a Hamiltonian cycle. This geometry is independent of the symmetry used in the paper's LP lower-bound theorem. The original PDF was successfully downloaded and its p. 454 inspected in extracted text.

**Rothvoss corroboration, not original reduction credit.** Thomas Rothvoss, [arXiv:1311.2369](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2369), first submitted November 11, 2013, current v4 dated March 17, 2017; JACM 64(6), 41:1–41:19 (2017), DOI [10.1145/3127497](https://doi.org/10.1145/3127497). The paragraph before Corollary 2 explicitly attributes the linear-size face projection to Yannakakis. Corollary 2 gives the linear **LP** extension-complexity bound 2^Omega(N). It does not supply an SDP lower bound. Cite both sources if explaining why the same reduction can transfer a newly available PSD obstruction.

**Restricted classes and potentially misleading wording.** Fawzi–Parrilo's [arXiv:1311.2571](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2571) concerns products of PSD cones of a fixed block size; it is not a theorem for an unrestricted single PSD cone. Braun et al.'s [arXiv:1504.00703v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00703v5), Definition 2.2 and the subsequent coordinate-symmetry convention, restrict the admissible action on lift coordinates; their matching Theorem 1.2 gives a linear exponent in that setting. Their asymmetric metric TSP statements have different scope. Some introductions call LRS's stretched-exponential bounds “exponential.” Exponents and quantifiers, not this word, determine whether a cited result settles the target. Gribling–de Laat–Laurent's [arXiv:1708.01573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01573) develops rank lower-bound hierarchies; its introduction still describes the unrestricted matching question as open. None of these inspected sources establishes the proposed unrestricted linear-exponent TSP theorem.

## Priority conclusion and permitted framing

The strongest relevant inspected pre-OpenAI exact SDP TSP statement is the corrected stretched-exponential bound 2^tildeOmega(N^(1/11)). The target linear exponent would follow from the family-126 exponential matching theorem by the already printed Yannakakis construction. All substantial lower-bound novelty should therefore be attributed upstream. The diagonal PSD embedding of a subset dynamic-programming LP upper bound is also familiar machinery; a detailed verification of it can make the note useful without being presented as a new algorithm.

Safe framing, **only after the matching proof passes mathematical audit**: “We record the symmetric TSP consequence of OpenAI's matching lower bound, using Yannakakis's linear-size face projection. We give explicit reduction, padding, and lift-size details and verify the matching exponential upper bound.” This avoids a claim of first priority or independent solution of the matching conjecture. A suitable title identifies a consequence or corollary rather than advertising a new lower-bound method.

The searches found no primary source containing the whole proposed new TSP note; that finite negative finding cannot establish novelty. The logical implication itself was already available once the matching source became public. If another public manuscript explicitly states this exact TSP result and already provides the same argument, a duplicate solution preprint must be withheld under the user's instruction. The parent should check that condition again with the final candidate's precise statement and any newly discovered companion evidence.
