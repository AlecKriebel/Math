## Main conclusion

The ROOT assessments generally preserve the manuscripts' actual claim boundaries. I found no claim-level evidence that warrants rejecting a theorem in these files. The important risk is **presentation of inherited conditional breakthroughs as the author's added achievement**, followed by treating an editorial recommendation as proof/dependency clearance. The two-score design prevents this if its labels remain explicit.

The user's rubric asks the importance of the *stated theorem assuming correctness, novelty and publication*. Thus an 8 or 9 for an inherited consequence is not automatically a scoring error. It must be read beside the lower added-contribution score and the attribution sentence. Fourteen of the 19 follow-ons have conditional impact at least 8; none has added contribution above 7. This is a portfolio of consequential deductions with a few substantial extensions, not fourteen independently established major breakthroughs by this author. Do not use the high conditional column alone to name the author's most important original result.

The same importance rubric applies to added contribution after crediting imported inputs. It should ordinarily not exceed the conditional impact of the whole paper. ROOT's 34 audited rows already satisfy that inequality. No blanket score reduction is recommended merely because a manuscript is unrefereed: that would conflate correctness probability with the user's conditional importance question.

## Independently selected three-paper submission shortlist

These three are from other reviewers' partitions; none is from my original 64. I selected them after reading their actual theorem/proof texts, rather than merely adopting their recommendation labels. These are plausible **specialist submissions**, not guarantees of acceptance at a general journal.

### 1. Nonconvexity of fixed odd-power sum-of-squares cones — 23191301

Conditional impact 7; added contribution 6.5 remains reasonable. This is the cleanest compact theorem-and-proof package of the three. The main new mechanism is the finite disjoint-copy obstruction at one prescribed odd power. A product truncated functional stays positive on the required squares, while its value on the qth power of the sum is a polynomial in the copy count with negative leading coefficient `L(p)^q`. The classical modified Motzkin seed supplies membership of every individual summand. The explicit q=3 construction then replaces “sufficiently many” by a checkable rational certificate.

Adversarial checks: the proof uses finite tensor powers only, does not assume a representing measure, does not reuse a degree-18 functional for arbitrary q, and explains why SOS summands cannot have excessively high degree through cancellation. Those address the obvious shortcuts that would invalidate the mechanism. The displayed dimension is **3 times 10^62**, not 3,186; PDF text extraction can lose exponents. It is a sufficient finite bound, not a low-dimensional example. The seed, its SOS cube, separation and product positivity retain prior credit.

Remaining gate: independent review of the product/separation proof and exact local certificate, plus the disclosed priority gaps. No ternary counterexample, smallest dimension, or common dimension for all q is proved. I did not rerun the 220-by-220 rational certificate in this checkpoint; the older reviewer records a fresh replay. Recommendation: retain strong submission candidate, with the disjoint-copy lemma central and the giant numerical certificate supplementary.

### 2. Three anti-invariant classes on a nonintegrable four-manifold — 23422055

Conditional impact 7 is reasonable; added contribution should use the same importance scale and be at most 7. This is a short explicit counterexample construction with a sharply stated target. On the genus-two-curve times elliptic-curve product, the three cohomology classes are globally independent although their local representatives span only a real two-plane. Compactly supported exact perturbations retain the local relation `gamma_epsilon = u alpha - v beta_epsilon`; the positive plane defines the new almost complex structure, and an explicit nonzero `(0,2)` term establishes nonintegrability.

Adversarial checks: the construction distinguishes cohomological independence from pointwise independence; normalization of the closed pair is not assumed closed; zeros of the holomorphic form are avoided by doing the kernel construction only in one nonvanishing coordinate ball; the compact cutoff identities are included, not merely the core formulas. I tracked the local wedge and normalization mechanism against the displayed forms. This is not a verification of every global/source interface.

Remaining gate: confirm the exact primary conjecture scope and compare the older nonintegrable product examples; the paper honestly records journal-final and historical coverage gaps. It establishes at least three classes, not the exact anti-invariant dimension, compatibility with a fixed symplectic form, or a contradiction to generic vanishing. Recommendation: retain strong submission candidate. It is particularly suitable for a concise geometry submission with the explicit construction first.

### 3. An explicit odd-order p-group with as many automorphisms as elements — 22770864

Conditional impact 7.5; added contribution 7 remains reasonable. This has the clearest named existence target and the strongest explicit computational infrastructure among the three, but also the largest specialist-proof burden. The contribution is not merely a Smith computation: it constructs a flag-rigid finite-characteristic Lie algebra, an asymmetric lattice, and a full-set logarithm/exponential bijection from automorphisms to a derivation kernel. The chosen modulus then equates group order and full automorphism order.

Fresh limited arithmetic checks in this checkpoint confirmed the displayed exponent accounting: `31*1689 = 30*1689+1689 = 52359`; Smith multiplicities total 931 and their weighted sum is 1689; the stated class bound 846 is below p=1009. These are elementary consistency checks only. The full manuscript separately argues finite-field complement/stabilizer classification, lattice lifting, congruence-preserving log/exp and the Lazard range. Counting only inner automorphisms or a Sylow subgroup would not suffice, and the manuscript explicitly avoids that mistake.

Remaining gate: a group/Lie specialist must check those nonlinear-to-linear and finite-characteristic lemmas; rerunning the matrix certificate cannot certify them. No smallest prime, smallest order, every-odd-prime statement, or expanded Cayley table is supplied. Recommendation: retain strong submission candidate, but package the conceptual proof and independently replayable finite certificate together.
