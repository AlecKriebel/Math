Finding
No additional primary paper containing a complete unrestricted binary-rational hafnian FPRAS together with the proposed full gadget/sampling proof was located. This is a search outcome, not evidence that the work is novel. The mathematical implication from an unweighted general-graph FPRAS to a binary-rational weighted FPRAS is already established machinery. Any claim of independently resolving a previously harder weighted approximation problem would therefore be misleading; the defensible framing is an explicit reduction and newly available consequence of the upstream FPRAS. Strong positive equivalence evidence
1. Colin McQuillan, Approximating Holant problems by winding, arXiv:1301.2880v1, public submission 14 January 2013. Primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.2880v1 ; dated record: https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2880 . Section 7.2, Lemma 25 constructs an unweighted two-port circuit with signature diag(p,q) from binary integers. Its proof uses the binary expansion and doubled alternating path edges. Lemma 27 explicitly permits rational edge weights/fugacities encoded by binary numerator and denominator, and builds a simple unweighted graph whose count equals a computable positive integer times the partition function. Ordinary weighted perfect matching is the zero-fugacity special case. Thus the reduction applies to arbitrary nonnegative rational hafnians uniformly, rather than only fixed finite weight sets. Its stated construction is polynomial; counting its separate paths gives O((log p)^2+(log q)^2) gadget size. It does not claim the proposed O(log W) recurrence. This is an exact scaled-count reduction and does not itself establish that the base unweighted FPRAS exists.

2. Jin-Yi Cai and Tianyu Liu, Counting Perfect Matchings and the Eight-Vertex Model, ICALP 2020, paper 23, p.23:4 (printed page). Primary proceedings PDF: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol168-icalp2020/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23.pdf . The discussion after Theorem 4 explicitly identifies the lack of extra approximation power from nonnegative edge weights and cites McQuillan Proposition 5 (reference 18). It does not announce an unrestricted FPRAS: p.23:5 still describes #PM as open. Correction verified 2026-10-07: the 2019 arXiv v1 HTML, versioned v1 PDF, and versioned source all explicitly include the McQuillan Proposition 5 attribution immediately after Theorem 1.2. The current/unversioned PDF is byte-identical to the versioned v1 PDF. The proceedings adds the words “to the unweighted #PM” and changes citation numbering; there is no omission-versus-addition discrepancy. The previous report sentence claiming that the 2019 arXiv text lacked the attribution was false and has been withdrawn. See CAI_LIU_VERSION_RECONCILIATION.txt and exact source/download evidence JSON files.

3. Jerrum, Valiant, and Vazirani, Random generation of combinatorial structures from a uniform distribution, TCS 43 (1986), 169–188. Primary paper copy: https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/ConvergeRates/RandomizedAlgs/JerrumValiantVaziraniTCS1986.pdf ; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(86)90174-X . Theorem 6.3 proves fully polynomial approximate counting implies fully polynomial almost-uniform generation for self-reducible p-relations; p.184 explicitly names graph 1-factors as an application. The original generator allows no output, so an always-output bounded-time claim needs its wrapper/fallback analysis. Combining a correctly specified uniform sampler with a positive exact edge-gadget reduction and its fibre multiplicities gives the weighted law by pushforward; TV contracts under projection. This last application is an inference, not a separately named theorem found in the paper. Exact zero detection comes from support perfect-matching feasibility, separately from approximation.

Older compact-gadget chain
Curticapean, Parity Separation: A Scientifically Proven Method for Permanent Weight Loss, primary arXiv PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07480 and published proceedings copy https://d-nb.info/1365619907/34 , surveys positive-integer weight removal and compact gadgets in the modular/interpolation reductions; the published text references Zanko and earlier counting work. Its signed parity separation is not a positive AP reduction, because subtraction may cause cancellation. The elementary W-parallel-edge observation is unary and cannot alone support binary polynomial time. The compact citation chain is relevant prior art even though this paper is not an unrestricted FPRAS proof.
Ben-Dor–Halevi, Zero-One Permanent is #P-Complete, A Simpler Proof, author primary PDF https://people.csail.mit.edu/shaih/pubs/01perm.pdf , dated 22 February 1995 in the checked copy (conference version cited as ISTCS 1993). Sections 5.2–5.3 remove nonnegative integer directed-graph weights using a sum of powers of two and path gadgets with internal self-loops. They prove polynomial dependence on log W. This supplies old DAG/path weight-simulation mechanics; it is not a hafnian FPRAS paper, and the present search did not show the exact requested recurrence there. Zanko 1991 DOI https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129054191000066 was identified, but full primary PDF access returned an error; its exact construction was not verified. McQuillan also points to Goldberg–Jerrum 2008: https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0605140 , Lemma 7 proof, pp.30–31, uses a rational-weight terminal gadget for a fixed Tutte parameter; that source alone does not address arbitrary binary variable weights.

Recent candidate leads checked and excluded by their theorem scope
- Zihong Yi, Diffuse Gaussian Truncation For Deterministic Approximate Counting, arXiv:2609.04079v1, public 3 September 2026: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04079 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04079 . Theorem 1.1(a) is a deterministic FPTAS for support minimum degree at least (1/2+gamma)n and positive entries in [theta,1], with fixed gamma>0 and theta>0. Arbitrary sparse supports and arbitrary binary dynamic range are outside it. Not an unrestricted duplicate; proof validity not audited.
- Anand–Chen–Cryan–Freifeld–Goldberg–Guo–Zhang, Simulating Gaussian boson sampling on graphs in polynomial time, arXiv:2511.16558 (v1 public 20 November 2025; checked v2 dated 25 June 2026): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16558 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16558 . Theorem 1.1 samples vertex subsets S with probability proportional to c^(2|S|) PM(G[S])^2, not perfect matchings of a given arbitrary weighted G. Its Section 2 expressly states that its general-graph weighted-matching sampler covers all matching sizes and that conditioning on perfect matchings requires a controlled near-perfect/perfect ratio. An efficient sampler for its different subset law does not supply the requested weighted-perfect-matching law by arbitrary conditioning. Theorem 1.3 concerns nonnegative rectangular permanent/Boson sampling, so also is not the target.
- Youngrong Lim and collaborators, Approximating outcome probabilities of linear optical circuits, npj Quantum Information 9 (2023), primary https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00791-9 ; supplement https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/10173/files/41534_2023_791_MOESM1_ESM.pdf?ln=en . Theorem 3 labelled FPRAS for hafnian assumes a special block matrix built from common unitary decompositions and squeezed-thermal parameters satisfying an explicit lower bound. It does not apply to every nonnegative rational symmetric matrix.
- Uvarov–Vinichenko QTML 2024 primary poster https://indico.qtml2024.org/event/1/contributions/35/attachments/36/423/QTML_2024_poster_Hafnians.pdf discusses estimator variance and calls general hafnian FPRAS existence an open question. It is not a full unrestricted proof. Exact or exponential-time hafnian papers returned by searches are outside the approximation target.
