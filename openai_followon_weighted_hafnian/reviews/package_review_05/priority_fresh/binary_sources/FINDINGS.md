## McQuillan: binary rational weighted counting already reduces to simple #PM

Primary source: Colin McQuillan, *Approximating Holant problems by winding*, [arXiv:1301.2880v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2880v1). Section 7.2 is printed pp. 26-28. Its defined target #PerfMatch takes a simple graph. Its intermediate #FugacityWeightedPM takes a closed matching circuit whose fugacities and edge weights are ratios of nonnegative integers supplied in binary; rational denominators must, of course, be positive.

The displayed integer gadget is **Lemma 25**, not Proposition 5. For binary integers p,q >= 0 it constructs a zero-fugacity, unit-edge-weight fragment with two dangling edges and signature diag(p,q). Lemma 27 then reduces the entire binary rational weighted problem to simple #PerfMatch. Proposition 5 is the enclosing finite-signature Holant consequence; its fixed finite set of signatures must not be mistaken for a restriction on the variable binary weights explicitly accepted by Lemma 27.

The integer mechanism can be reconstructed directly from the proof. For p = sum_i 2^(n_i), the fragment G_(p,1) has terminals s,t. A set bit n_i = 0 supplies one direct edge. A set bit n_i >= 1 supplies a separate s-t path of length 2n_i-1, with a doubled edge at every odd position. All internal vertices of different paths are distinct. If both dangling edges are used, s,t are already covered and the even-position edges supply one matching. If neither dangling edge is used, exactly one path uses its odd-position edges; its n_i doubled choices supply 2^(n_i) matchings. Exactly one dangling edge cannot be used by parity. Thus its four entries are (p,0,0,1), including p=0. A degree-two zero-fugacity vertex has signature [[0,1],[1,0]]. Serially composing G_(p,1), that vertex, G_(q,1), and another such vertex produces diag(p,q). The q=1 case needs no composition.

This particular printed construction has worst-case O((log(p+1))^2 + (log(q+1))^2) size: when all bits are set, the total length is proportional to the sum of their positions. The paper claims polynomial-time construction from binary inputs; it does **not** claim a linear-logarithmic bound for this gadget. It uses only nonnegative integer multiplicities, not p or q parallel copies of a single edge.

Lemma 26 removes vertex fugacities by a weight-preserving extension using a chain of triangular attachments and one parity edge. Lemma 27 uses that extension and replaces each rational weighted edge p_e/q_e by its integer-signature fragment. It obtains an unweighted multigraph whose exact matching count is C times the original weighted value, where C = product_e q_e. It deletes loops and gives every remaining multiedge its own three-edge subdivision. A used edge has the unique extension selecting the two outer subdivision edges; an unused edge has the unique extension selecting the middle edge. Hence the resulting graph is simple and its count is still exactly C times the original value. The bit length of C is at most the sum of denominator bit lengths. This is an exact scaled reduction, stronger than an abstract approximation-preserving promise alone.

Consequently, setting all original vertex fugacities to zero specializes the source to binary nonnegative rational weighted perfect matchings on arbitrary graphs. This specialization is a deduction from the explicit input class and proof. No assumption that weights are bounded, above one, or encoded in unary is needed. A general unweighted #PM FPRAS, if available, transfers through this polynomial exact reduction and exact division by C. Neither this section nor Proposition 5 is itself an unconditional FPRAS for arbitrary #PM; the paper clearly states the conditional implication. This section also does not state the full variable-weight approximate sampling theorem or its total-variation/time guarantee.

## Dell 2010 and ECCC 2012: linear-logarithmic binary path construction

Primary sources: Holger Dell, Thore Husfeldt and Martin Wahlén, [ECCC TR10-078 original](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/078/), printed p. 8, Fig. 3; and Holger Dell, Thore Husfeldt, Dániel Marx, Nina Taslaman and Martin Wahlén, [ECCC revision 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/078/revision/1/download/), printed p. 12, Fig. 3. The ECCC 2012 PDF was downloaded directly for this check, rather than inferred to be identical to arXiv v1. The arXiv v1 PDF and its freshly downloaded primary TeX were also inspected as a separate version.

Both actual figures contain the same arithmetic mechanism. The left panel replaces a weight-2 arc u->v by a direct unit arc plus a two-arc detour through a fresh vertex. The fresh vertex has one unit self-loop. There are two choices when the replaced arc participates in a cycle cover and one choice, its loop, when it does not.

In the right panel, let a = sum_(i=0)^k a_i 2^i with a_i in {0,1}. A directed spine x_0=u, x_1,...,x_k has weight-2 arcs between consecutive vertices. Each x_i has a weight-a_i exit to v; the zero exits can be deleted. Each internal spine vertex has a unit self-loop. Replacing each spine weight-2 arc by the left-panel gadget gives O(k) vertices and arcs, all with unit weights. A u-to-v route exiting at x_i has exactly 2^i choices, so the total number of non-loop routes is a. Unused internal vertices have exactly one completion by their loops. Removing those internal loops leaves a simple DAG: it has k spine vertices, k doubling-detour vertices and the two terminals, and 3k + popcount(a) arcs. The degenerate a=1 case is a single arc; a=0 is deletion of the original edge.

This is a genuine logarithmic positive-integer simulator, even though its illustrated arithmetic graph temporarily labels spine arcs by 2. The left panel explicitly eliminates those labels with constant overhead. Its proof application concerns directed cycle-cover permanent counting after interpolation: the queries have a <= n, with nonunit weights on loop edges. The text records exact equality per(G')=per(G_a) and O(m+n log a) edges. The caption and mechanism apply to arbitrary binary nonnegative integers; removing the a<=n restriction for this local construction is a deduction from the figure, not a new theorem asserted in the paper.

The original 2010 paper transfers its permanent lower bounds to bipartite perfect matching counting on the next page. It does not formulate the later general-graph rational hafnian FPRAS or sampling guarantee. A direct split-vertex realization of the local DAG is nevertheless checkable: keep L_u and R_v as terminals; split each internal vertex x into L_x,R_x with an identity edge; and replace every DAG arc x->y by L_x-R_y. With both terminals present, every matching chooses one directed u-v path and identity edges away from it, because a non-path component would require a directed cycle in the DAG. With both terminals removed, only identity edges remain. Removing exactly one terminal leaves unequal bipartition sizes. This yields signature (a,1,0,0) in the order both present, both absent, only left absent, only right absent. This paragraph is an explicit reconstruction/deduction, not a quotation of a Dell statement about general nonbipartite gluing.

The 2012 diagram draws circles at its original terminals; the 2010 diagram leaves terminal labels uncircled. **Neither version has an extra sink-v self-loop.** An apparent hook in a reduced-resolution rendering of the 2012 figure is overlapping incoming arrowheads. A 300-dpi crop of the actual ECCC revision PDF resolves it; the primary arXiv TeX independently has loops only at the internal spine vertices (paper.tex lines 1273-1276). No terminal loop is added in the reconstructed gadget.

## Cai-Liu v1: exact citation and what it establishes

Primary source: Jin-Yi Cai and Tianyu Liu, *Counting perfect matchings and the eight-vertex model*, [arXiv:1904.10493v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10493v1). The introduction after Theorem 1.2 (printed p. 4; intro.tex line 191) says that nonnegative edge weights do not add computational power and cites **[McQ13, Proposition 5]**. Its bibliography identifies McQ13 as Colin McQuillan, *Approximating Holant problems by winding*, CoRR abs/1301.2880, 2013 (paper.bbl lines 92-95).

This is an explicit later public citation of McQuillan's weighted-to-unweighted mechanism, not an inference from shared terminology. Its context is reducing fixed eight-vertex signatures implemented by constant-size nonnegative matchgates to #PerfectMatchings. McQuillan's §7.2 supplies the precise uniform binary-rational machine model. Cai-Liu's broad prose about nonnegative edge weights should not be stretched into a bit-complexity claim for arbitrarily presented real weights.
