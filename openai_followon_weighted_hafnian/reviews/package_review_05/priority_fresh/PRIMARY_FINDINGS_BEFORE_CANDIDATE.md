## Strongest supported attribution reconstruction

1. McQuillan, arXiv:1301.2880v1, public submission 2013-01-14 08:02:59 UTC. Section 7.2 explicitly defines Fugacity-Weighted-PM with fugacities and edge weights as ratios of nonnegative integers written in binary. Lemma 26 (TeX label lem:pmwtsim) realizes diag(p,q), using binary powers in paths with doubled odd edges; the total worst-case size is polynomial in bit length, with O(log^2 p+log^2 q) from this literal construction. Lemma 28 (lem:fwpmp) constructs a simple graph with C times the matching-circuit value, C the product of denominators, by eliminating fugacities and replacing weighted edges; deleting loops and subdividing edges into length-three paths removes multiedges. Proposition 5 is a broader Holant reduction and contains this machinery. Binary nonnegative rational weighted perfect-matching approximation reduces to simple unweighted #PM already here; there is no new computational expressiveness claim to make.

2. Dell--Husfeldt--Wahlen, ECCC TR10-078 original, public publication 2010-04-27 23:50 per ECCC. Figure 3 and Section 3 p8 depict weight-two path/direct-arc replacement and a directed binary chain of doubling arcs with bit-selected exits. Identity loops complete unused internal vertices into a cycle cover. The text gives O(log a) overhead per simulated weight. Thus logarithmic binary path weight removal is older than McQuillan's literal quadratic-log circuit. The 2012 expanded Dell--Husfeldt--Marx--Taslaman--Wahlen version repeats Fig3 p12; arXiv v1 submission 2012-06-08 14:29:52 UTC and ECCC rev1 accepted 2012-06-08 17:51. Their use in the hardness reduction restricts variable weights to loop edges with a<=n; the displayed gadget itself uses arbitrary binary integer a. Splitting directed vertices converts cycle-cover paths/identity loops to matching arcs/identity edges. A self-contained terminal signature/gluing proof is useful exposition, but logarithmic binary path machinery is inherited rather than a novelty claim.

5. Rudelson--Samorodnitsky--Zeitouni arXiv:1409.3905v1 (public 2014-09-13; manuscript date September4 is not disclosure), title Random Gaussian matrices and Hafnian estimators. Its adjacency theorem assumes d>=alpha n+2 and strong expansion up to n(1-alpha)/(1+kappa/4), with log error C n^(1-epsilon), epsilon<1/5, and n^-D failure, D>4; a spectral gap sharpens log error to C sqrt(n log n). General stochastic version also requires a large-entries graph, minimum degree, strong expansion and max entry n^-vartheta. v2 (public 2016-09-02), Hafnians, perfect matchings and Gaussian matrices, preserves these restricted, subexponential multiplicative-error guarantees. These are not an all-input relative-epsilon FPRAS.
