## Strongest verified finding

The general exact reduction from binary nonnegative rational weighted perfect matching counts to counts on finite simple unweighted graphs was explicitly public by McQuillan's arXiv:1301.2880v1 (14 January 2013), §7.2, Lemmas 25 and 27. The logarithmic integer compression mechanism was explicitly public earlier in Dell–Husfeldt–Wahlén's original ECCC TR10-078 (27 April 2010), Figure 3 and proof of Theorem 2(iii). Its graph is, after reversal and relabeling, the directed construction requested in USER_REQUEST. I verified this graph correspondence algebraically and by exact checks for W=1,...,256. This does not establish absolute first priority: it gives a verified public upper bound for when the machinery existed.

## Primary-source ledger and exact scopes

1. **Valiant 1979**, *The complexity of computing the permanent*, Theoretical Computer Science 8, 189–201, DOI [10.1016/0304-3975(79)90044-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(79)90044-6). [Inspected original PDF](https://www.cs.bu.edu/faculty/gacs/courses/cs535/papers/Valiant_permanent.pdf). Lemma 3.3, printed p.193, gives an exact permanent-preserving transformation from matrices with entries in {0,...,m} to 0/1 matrices, with running time polynomial in **m and matrix order**. Its proof, printed pp.196–197 and Figure 2, uses a directed cycle-cover simulator for weight k; when the original edge is used, a remaining cycle-cover choice picks one among k distinguished self-loops; when unused, the extension is unique. The inspected statement does **not** claim polynomial dependence on log m. Therefore this particular lemma is not direct primary evidence for succinct binary weights. It establishes earlier exact edge simulation, but not the required bit bound. The paper says received October 1977; receipt of a manuscript does not establish public disclosure in 1977.

2. **Dell–Husfeldt–Wahlén 2010**, *Exponential Time Complexity of the Permanent and the Tutte Polynomial*, [original ECCC TR10-078 metadata](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/078/), [original PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/078/download/). The original has three authors. Relevant location is Figure 3 and proof of Theorem 2(iii), PDF/printed p.8; it is **not a standalone lemma**. The figure gives a binary chain realizing a=sum a_i 2^i with a_i in {0,1}. Doubling edges are expanded using a direct arc and a two-step route, each new internal vertex having a self-loop. Bit-controlled exits lead to the fixed sink. The text gives exact equality per G'=per G_a and a logarithmic edge increase O(m+n log a). The surrounding hardness application assumes a<=n and nonunit weights only on loops, but the displayed **local simulator itself** works for arbitrary positive integer a on a directed arc. The distinction matters: arbitrary binary input is an elementary extension of the figure, not the quantified hardness theorem's stated domain. The ECCC page records original acceptance 27 April 2010 17:55 and publication 27 April 2010 23:50. These archive timestamps support public disclosure by that date, not an assertion that no earlier simulator existed.

3. **Dell–Husfeldt–Marx–Taslaman–Wahlén**, [arXiv:1206.1775v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775), submitted 8 June 2012 14:29:52 UTC; journal metadata identifies ACM Transactions on Algorithms 10(4), article 21 (2014), [DOI 10.1145/2635812](https://doi.org/10.1145/2635812). I inspected the arXiv v1 PDF (the unversioned PDF currently has the same SHA-256). Figure 3 and proof of Theorem 1.3(ii), printed p.12, repeat the binary simulation and logarithmic edge bound. The first page identifies earlier ICALP 2010 and IPEC 2010 versions. I did not obtain the publisher's final 2014 PDF, so this report does not silently call the inspected arXiv v1 the final journal text.

4. **McQuillan 2013**, [*Approximating Holant problems by winding*, arXiv:1301.2880v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2880). Primary archive metadata records submission 14 January 2013 08:02:59 UTC and only v1. I inspected PDF plus versioned TeX source. §7.2 defines #PerfMatch on **simple graphs** and #FugacityWeightedPM with vertex fugacities and edge weights given as ratios of nonnegative integers in **binary**. Lemma 25 (printed p.27) gives an all-unit-edge, zero-fugacity two-external-edge circuit with exact signature matrix diag(p,q), for arbitrary binary nonnegative integers p,q. Lemma 27 (printed p.28) builds a simple graph G with C times the weighted partition function perfect matchings, C the product of edge denominators after eliminating fugacities. Parallel edges are removed by length-three subdivision; loops are deleted. Thus it is incorrect to describe this source as covering merely unary weights, merely constant weights, or only a final multigraph.

   The displayed G_{p,1} construction uses a separate doubled-odd-position path for every set bit p=sum 2^{n_i}. A path for exponent n_i has length 2n_i-1 and a parallel edge at every odd position. This explicitly has O((log p)^2) size in the worst case (sum of all occupied exponents), which remains polynomial in the binary input length. It is not the most efficient O(log p) chain simulator. Serial composition with two single-vertex NOT circuits produces diag(p,q).

   The currently served PDF's title date is September 5, 2018, while its archive banner is v1 14 January 2013. The TeX source uses date=\\today; this is a recompilation date, not contrary evidence to the archive's 2013 disclosure. Versioned archive identifiers and history are the appropriate evidence. I did not identify a separately titled 2014 or 2016 McQuillan edition during the bounded search. This is not proof that none exists. The relevant verified primary citation is the 2013 arXiv version.

5. **Cai–Liu 2019/2020**, [*Counting perfect matchings and the eight-vertex model*, arXiv:1904.10493v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10493), submitted 23 April 2019 18:52:15 UTC, only v1; [ICALP 2020 PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol168-icalp2020/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23.pdf), DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.23). I inspected both full arXiv PDF/TeX and conference PDF. The arXiv introduction on printed p.4 explicitly credits McQuillan Proposition 5 for the fact that nonnegative edge weights add no computational power; the conference introduction on p.23:4 explicitly compares with **unweighted #PM** and cites the same proposition. Their own 4-ary matchgate characterization is a separate result, and does not supply the original binary edge simulator. Their Definition 4.1 (conference Definition 13) uses nonnegative edge weights and fixed dangling-edge assignments; it should not be treated as an arbitrary-real bit-encoding theorem. McQuillan's explicit rational/binary Lemma 27 is the suitable source for that computational statement.

6. **Goldberg–Jerrum citation chain**, [*Inapproximability of the Tutte polynomial*, arXiv:cs/0605140v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605140), submitted 30 May 2006, v2 30 July 2007; journal 2008. McQuillan cites a similar reduction here. I inspected both PDFs, especially v1 Lemma 7 proof, pp.30–31. That proof realizes a **fixed rational parameter** n1/n2 by literal bundles of n1 and n2 parallel edges, and scales by n2^m. It does not state a compact reduction uniform in binary-variable n1,n2. Its p.13 remark removes loops/multiple edges by 3-stretch. Hence its earlier exact local-weight idea is appropriate historical context, but not direct evidence for the full binary-size bound.

## Independent terminal-signature and graph-correspondence derivation

For McQuillan G_{W,1}, fixed dangling bits (0,0) mean the terminal vertices must be matched internally, yielding W completions. Fixed bits (1,1) consume both terminals externally, leaving one internal completion. One dangling edge selected leaves odd parity, hence zero. After removing the dangling edges, in endpoint-deletion notation the counts are

    (#PM(T), #PM(T-{s,t}), #PM(T-{s}), #PM(T-{t})) = (W,1,0,0).

This correspondence prevents a convention error: attaching dangling ends to original vertices and identifying gadget terminal vertices with original vertices are different gluing conventions. For rational pe/qe, an endpoint-sharing gadget with deletion signature (pe,qe,0,0) gives local pe for an edge used in the original matching, qe for an edge unused. Therefore each original matching M has exactly

    product(e in M) pe * product(e not in M) qe
      = C * product(e in M) (pe/qe),  C=product(all e) qe

preimages. C has at most the sum of denominator bit lengths plus a constant many bits and is polynomial-time computable. Binary integer weights set qe=1. Positivity/zero and disconnected support create no obstruction to this exact identity. Counting the empty graph gives 1.

For the Dell gadget, remove its internal self-loops and view its remaining directed graph as a DAG. Every directed cycle-cover extension uses an endpoint-to-endpoint path if the original arc is selected, and otherwise all internal self-loops. The DAG forbids additional internal nontrivial cycles. Splitting each internal vertex i into L_i,R_i turns its self-loop into the identity edge L_i R_i, and turns each remaining arc i->j into L_i R_j, retaining only source-L and sink-R at the endpoints. This is a simple unweighted bipartite local gadget with deletion signature (W,1,0,0), even when attached to a nonbipartite original graph. Its internal vertices/edges are O(log W). No expansion into W edges is needed.

The USER_REQUEST recurrence constructs vertices s,t_0 and, for each suffix bit b_i, adds a_i,t_i and arcs t_{i-1}->t_i, t_{i-1}->a_i, a_i->t_i, plus s->t_i when b_i=1. Let k=bitlength(W)-1. Reverse all arcs and map

    s -> fixed sink v,
    t_i -> top-chain vertex z_{k-i},
    a_i -> doubling-detour vertex h_{k-i+1}.

This is exactly Dell Figure 3's binary chain read from low bits to high bits, with the highest-bit exit always present. The two split undirected gadgets are consequently isomorphic after also exchanging left/right copies and terminal order. The matching mechanism is not merely asymptotically comparable; the displayed directed constructions correspond explicitly.

`reconstruct_primary_gadgets.py` contains independently written literal reconstructions and an exact matching enumerator. `reconstruction_results.json` records W=1,...,256: all four signatures and all explicit reversal/relabeling correspondences passed. Finite checks supplement the symbolic argument; they do not certify any upstream approximation theorem.

## Attribution/priority consequences and limitations

- The binary-rational exact reduction and its finite-simple-graph scope are established machinery. Their presentation may be useful, but cannot be advertised as a new general weight-removal theorem.
- The requested O(log W) DAG construction is an explicit reconstruction of the 2010 binary edge simulator. No new compression claim is supported by this audit.
- A newly available general unweighted FPRAS can have newly available weighted corollaries. This source audit alone does not determine whether a particular follow-on consequence is new or whether the upstream FPRAS is sound.
- I established public disclosure **by** 27 April 2010 for logarithmic integer simulation and **by** 14 January 2013 for the explicit binary-rational/simple-graph theorem. I did not establish absolute earliest priority, nor exhaust every textbook, preprint or companion work.
- Bounded retrieval limitation: the 2013 Liverpool thesis request timed out and was not inspected. All relevant McQuillan results above were checked directly in its primary versioned arXiv PDF and TeX. Final 2014 Dell publisher text was not inspected. These limits do not weaken the directly verified earlier disclosures.
