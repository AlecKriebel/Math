## Findings

1. Binary integer weight elimination is established. Dell, Husfeldt, Wahlén, ECCC TR10-078 (2010), Figure 3 and page 8 give an integer-weight gadget with logarithmic size. It is a directed cycle-cover/permanent construction. The expanded Dell, Husfeldt, Marx, Taslaman, Wahlén work (arXiv:1206.1775v1, posted 8 June 2012; journal ACM Transactions on Algorithms 10(4), article 21 (2014)) repeats this in Figure 3 and page 12. Do not attribute the general binary-gadget idea, logarithmic blowup, or weighted-to-unweighted reduction as new. Exact publisher text was unavailable via DOI (403); the inspected primary source is the public expanded author arXiv v1, not an invented v2 (v2 fetch returned 404).

2. The strongest direct predecessor is McQuillan, arXiv:1301.2880v1, Section 7.2. The problem immediately before Lemma 25 explicitly permits nonnegative rational fugacities and edge weights encoded by binary integer ratios. Lemma 25 produces unit-edge, zero-fugacity two-external-edge circuits with diagonal signature (p,q). Its proof uses the binary expansion of p, with doubled odd-position edges, then serial composition for q. Lemma 27 constructs a simple unweighted graph whose perfect-matching count is C times the original rational partition function. It also removes loops and makes a multigraph simple by length-three edge subdivision. This is a genuine polynomial-time binary rational reduction, stronger in weight scope than merely unary expansion. Source and rendered PDF Lemmas 25 and 27 agree. Its p-construction is O((log p)^2), while the earlier permanent construction demonstrates O(log p) arithmetic simulation. The new requested DAG proof may be a different explicit realization, but no novelty is established by that difference.

3. Jerrum, Valiant, Vazirani, TCS 43 (1986), Section 6, Theorem 6.3 gives counting-to-almost-uniform-generation for self-reducible p-relations and explicitly includes 1-factors in undirected graphs. Thus the existence of approximate weighted perfect-matching sampling follows from established counting/sampling machinery together with rational weight elimination. A direct self-reduction with explicit bit/TV/error accounting can still be useful exposition. Do not claim that an FPRAS alone, without self-reducibility and probability analysis, supplies an arbitrary requested sampler guarantee.

Rudelson, Samorodnitsky, Zeitouni, arXiv:1409.3905v2 (2 September 2016; journal Annals of Probability 44(4), 2858–2888) proves concentration of a Gaussian determinant estimator under strong expansion/degree and entry-size assumptions (Theorems 1.2, 1.5, 1.9, 1.10), with logarithmic errors of positive powers of n. This is restricted subexponential-factor evidence, not an unrestricted relative-epsilon FPRAS.

5. Barvinok, arXiv:1601.07518v1 (27 January 2016), Theorem 1.5, is a deterministic quasipolynomial relative approximation when all relevant entries lie in [delta,1] for fixed delta>0. The latest v5 (13 January 2017), with title Approximating permanents and hafnians, must be distinguished from the earlier title Approximating permanents and hafnians of positive matrices. Version-specific theorem numbering must be verified when cited.

Its bit guarantee is polynomial with exponents depending on these fixed constants. It does not cover arbitrary support or unrestricted tiny nonzero entries.
