              Binary rational hafnians: an exact reduction and
                 approximation and sampling consequences
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           October 6, 2026


                                                Abstract
         We give a self-contained implementation of classical positive-integer weight removal for per-
     fect matchings. A binary directed acyclic graph with W paths gives a simple two-terminal
     graph with matching signature (W, 1, 0, 0) using 4 bits(W ) − 2 vertices. Gluing these gad-
     gets and clearing denominators reduces every nonnegative binary rational hafnian to an un-
     weighted simple-graph perfect-matching count with polynomial encoding length. Consequently,
     the general-graph FPRAS of OpenAI gives a uniform FPRAS for this rational model, with exact
     zero detection. We specify a bounded-bit counting self-reduction sampler with explicit total-
     variation and runtime bounds. The approximation breakthrough, logarithmic weight removal,
     and count-to-sample framework are inherited; this note supplies explicit proofs, bit bounds,
     boundary cases, and reproducible ﬁnite checks. Its approximation conclusions rely on the cited
     general-graph theorem and do not concern signed or complex hafnians.

    The external approximation input is OpenAI’s Theorem 1.1 [1], pinned at repository commit
adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a. It states a uniform FPRAS for every ﬁnite simple
undirected graph, returning a nonnegative rational estimate with relative error ϵ and failure prob-
ability at most δ, for rational 0 < ϵ < 1, 0 < δ < 1/2. Its worst-case bit time on every execution
is polynomial in input length, ϵ−1 , and log δ −1 . On infeasible inputs every execution returns zero.
This one-way zero property does not certify zero on positive inputs. We separately use Edmonds’
polynomial-time matching algorithm [3] for exact feasibility and witnesses.
    Logarithmic integer-weight removal predates this note. Dell–Husfeldt–Wahlén [4], and its ex-
panded version [5], give binary weight gadgets for permanents. McQuillan [6, Section 7.2, Lem-
mas 25 and 27] treats nonnegative binary rational fugacities and reduces them to simple unweighted
perfect matching counts with an exact computable factor. Our Horner DAG is a self-contained vari-
ant of established path and identity-edge machinery, not a new weighted-to-unweighted reduction.
Counting self-reduction is likewise classical [7]; [1] gives an explicit unweighted deletion sampler.

    The newly available general-graph FPRAS is entirely credited to [1]. Its stated applications
explicitly exclude compressed weights. Its entropy companion [2] includes weighted sums, but
only a deterministic factor 512n approximation, not a relative FPRAS. The rational complexity
consequence here was already implicit in the classical equivalence once the unweighted theorem
became available. We make no ﬁrst-publication or new complexity-classiﬁcation claim. This note
records the consequence, proof variant, explicit bit model, and veriﬁcation package.
    Earlier hafnian approximation results address restrictions: Rudelson–Samorodnitsky–Zeitouni
[8] analyze a Gaussian determinant estimator under expansion and spectral hypotheses, with subex-
ponential multiplicative errors; Barvinok [9] gives deterministic approximation on matrix classes
admitting a zero-free interpolation region. Neither provides the present unrestricted binary rational
FPRAS. Yi [10] gives deterministic relative approximation for dense supports with ﬁxed positive
weight bounds; this note has neither promise.


2    A logarithmic exact gadget
Lemma 1. For each integer W ≥ 1, there is a simple graph HW with distinct terminals u, v whose
matching signature is
        (# PM(HW ), # PM(HW − {u, v}), # PM(HW − u), # PM(HW − v)) = (W, 1, 0, 0).
For ℓ = bits(W ) = 1 + blog2 W c, it has 4ℓ − 2 vertices, 4ℓ − 4 of them internal, and at most 6ℓ − 5
edges.
Proof. Start with a DAG containing s → t, representing the leading binary digit 1. For each
subsequent digit b, introduce fresh a, z, add
                           t → z,    t → a,   a → z,    and s → z if b = 1,
and replace the sink t by z. Each old path has two extensions, and the additional direct path is
present exactly for b = 1, so the recurrence is w 7→ 2w + b. The ﬁnal path count is W . Put each
new a after the old vertices and z after a: every arc goes forward. The source has indegree zero,
the ﬁnal sink outdegree zero, and there are no duplicate arcs. The DAG has 2 + 2(ℓ − 1) vertices
and at most 1 + 4(ℓ − 1) arcs.
    Split every internal vertex i into Li , Ri with an identity edge Li Ri . Keep only Ls = u and
Rt = v at the terminals. Replace each arc i → j by an undirected edge Li Rj . The result is simple
and bipartite with the asserted sizes.
    In a full perfect matching, the chosen nonidentity edges give directed arcs. Each internal vertex
either uses its identity edge or has one chosen incoming and one chosen outgoing arc. The source
has one chosen outgoing arc, and the sink one chosen incoming arc. Such arcs form an s–t path and
possibly disjoint directed cycles; acyclicity excludes all cycles. Conversely, a path ﬁxes its arc edges
and all identity edges oﬀ the path uniquely. This proves the full count W . After removing both
terminals, the chosen arcs have equal in- and out-degree at every vertex. Any nonempty such ﬁnite
set contains a directed cycle, hence none can occur. The all-identity matching is unique. Removing
exactly one terminal leaves odd order and cannot have a perfect matching.

3    Approximation and exact zero
Corollary 3. Using [1], there is a uniform FPRAS for ZA for rational 0 < ϵ, δ < 1, with relative
error at most ϵ and failure probability at most δ. Its every-execution bit time is polynomial in the

5    Veriﬁcation and limits
The supplementary code constructs the gadgets, enumerates the four signatures for W = 1, . . . , 64,
and compares exact rational hafnians with expanded matching counts on small boundary instances.
It also integrates ﬁnite-bit sampling laws, including perturbed estimates and failure histories, to
check TV bounds. Finite checks support the construction and wrapper but do not replace proofs or
certify the external FPRAS. Exact enumeration helpers are exponential-time test tools; the sampler
wrapper requires supplied polynomial counting and feasibility algorithms.
    Disconnected support, zero counts, weights below one, and large denominators are covered.
The nonnegative rational scope is essential. There is no signed or complex hafnian conclusion or
unrestricted Gaussian-boson-sampling consequence.
    The project records separate manuscript, formal-scope, priority, gadget and sampling audits
and complete-package reviews. The real upstream Lean declaration was located and its state-
ment’s semantics checked, but an independent kernel rebuild has not been reproduced here. No
formalization of this follow-on theorem is claimed. AI tools were used extensively in research,
drafting, implementation and automated adversarial veriﬁcation. This preprint has not undergone
