# Adversarial check of the prior theorem's proof

The exact published Theorem 6.1 statement and its smooth hypotheses support the submitted necessity application. Its full proof was read, including the polynomial-reduction paragraph on PDF p43. There is a nonblocking imprecision in that paragraph which should not be inherited as an independent argument: polynomial spectral mapping need not remove only one nonreal pair. For example, the constant real operator made of rotation blocks with eigenvalues +/-i and +/-i/2 maps under q(z)=z^2+1 to eigenvalues 0 and 3/4, all real. Thus the line claiming that all-real spectrum of q(L) means the only nonreal eigenvalues of L are the chosen pair is not literally correct. This is a source-proof observation, not a counterexample to Theorem 6.1, and not a submitted construction defect.

The exact compact-support application can be independently justified by the first maximum-principle phase alone, without the disputed reduction sentence. Here is the argument, using the primary local splitting and complex-holomorphic results (Theorems 3.1 and 3.3) as prior lemmas.

Suppose a smooth Nijenhuis tensor on a closed connected manifold has a zero point and some nonreal spectrum. Define mu(x) as the largest positive imaginary part among the spectrum, or zero when all roots are real. For a real matrix, nonreal roots come in conjugate pairs, so the maximum mu_0 on the compact manifold is strictly positive. Root continuity gives continuity of mu.

Inside the compact nonempty maximum set M_0, take the largest algebraic multiplicity m_0 of a root with imaginary part mu_0. The set M_tilde of points attaining this multiplicity is nonempty and closed: along a convergent sequence choose such a root; a subsequence converges to a root of imaginary part mu_0 whose multiplicity is at least m_0 by continuity of the characteristic polynomial and its derivatives, hence exactly m_0 by maximality.

At p in M_tilde, choose lambda_0 with that multiplicity. Local splitting isolates the conjugate pair clusters and gives a monic degree-m_0 polynomial P whose coefficients are holomorphic in the corresponding complex leaf coordinates, and independent of the other coordinates. At p, P=(z-lambda_0)^m_0. Everywhere its roots have imaginary part at most mu_0. Their sum has imaginary part -Im(a_1), where a_1 is the first coefficient. This quantity attains its possible upper bound m_0*mu_0 at p. The holomorphic maximum principle makes a_1 constant on the connected neighborhood. Therefore every root of P throughout the neighborhood has imaginary part exactly mu_0, because their sum attains the common upper bound.

To avoid an assumption about smooth individual root branches, shift the polynomial by i*mu_0. All roots of P(w+i*mu_0) are real, so every coefficient of this shifted polynomial is real valued. These coefficients are also holomorphic and independent of the remaining coordinates, and therefore constant. Consequently P remains (z-lambda_0)^m_0 throughout the neighborhood, which lies in M_tilde. Thus M_tilde is open as well as closed. Connectedness forces M_tilde to equal the whole manifold. A zero point cannot lie in M_tilde because mu_0>0, a contradiction.

This proves the exact weaker obstruction needed by the submission: a closed connected smooth Nijenhuis field with a zero point has only real spectrum everywhere. Applying it to the compact-support zero extension on S^n resolves the necessity direction. No unsupported global Jordan constancy, analytic hypothesis, regularity assumption or smooth-eigenvalue parametrization is used. The full global constancy theorem remains a credited prior result; this review does not certify every step of that stronger source proof or claim to reprove it in full.
