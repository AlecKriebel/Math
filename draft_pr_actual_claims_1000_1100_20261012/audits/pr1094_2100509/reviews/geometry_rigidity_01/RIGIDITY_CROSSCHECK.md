# Full prior rigidity scope, with independent maximum-principle crosscheck

Fresh author version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04603v2, Theorem 6.1 on printed/PDF page 41; complete proof on pages 42-43. The preceding real-analytic Artin argument ends the earlier proposition and is not a hypothesis of Section 6.1. The general setup is a smooth real endomorphism field on a smooth manifold (page 7). Exact assumptions here: Nijenhuis, closed, connected. No global regularity, fixed Segre type, diagonalizability, constant rank, or analyticity is imposed.

The dependencies were read in full where used: analytic matrix functions and Proposition 3.1 on pages 17-19; splitting Theorem 3.1 and proof on pages 19-20; general splitting conclusion on page 21; no-real-spectrum Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 with complete proof on pages 21-24. Local splitting explicitly permits singular points and variable algebraic structure. The no-real-spectrum assumption of Theorem 3.3 is applied to a separated local real block corresponding to one nonreal eigenvalue and its conjugate, not to the entire original operator. This is the crucial complex-block scope check. Holomorphicity is obtained from the smooth Nijenhuis condition via the canonical complex structure; it is not an extra hypothesis of the original field. The Sylvester equation has disjoint upper and lower half-plane spectra, irrespective of Jordan sizes.

The full source proof proceeds by a maximum-imaginary-part argument, then a polynomial reduction. For the submitted necessity only the first persistence argument suffices: any nonreal spectrum would force a positive maximal imaginary part at every point, incompatible with a zero point. The following independent residual-characteristic-polynomial argument also verifies the full stated theorem and avoids relying on the terse reduction language at the end of the source proof.

Let R_x(t) initially be the full characteristic polynomial, then successively the polynomial left after removing globally constant nonreal pairs. It is a globally smooth monic real polynomial. Its degree is finite and its roots are roots of the full characteristic polynomial. Let beta > 0 be the maximum, over compact M, of the imaginary parts of its roots. Select a residual root lambda with Im lambda=beta and maximal algebraic multiplicity m among roots on that top horizontal line. Prior removed constants do not equal lambda: each removed factor had its full multiplicity removed everywhere. Thus the full operator has exactly m copies of lambda at the selected point.

Use local splitting for lambda and its conjugate, and the canonical complex structure on that block. On a product neighborhood, its complex characteristic factor P(t)=t^m+a_1 t^(m-1)+...+a_m has holomorphic coefficients in its own complex variables and is independent of all other coordinates. At the selected point, P=(t-lambda)^m. Take the neighborhood small enough that all roots of P avoid the finite set of prior removed constants; then they are residual roots and have Im <= beta throughout. Therefore -Im a_1 <= m beta with equality at the selected point. The maximum principle makes a_1 constant locally, so all roots of P have Im exactly beta. The shifted polynomial P(w+i beta) has only real roots at every point. All its coefficients are consequently real-valued holomorphic functions, and hence locally constant. P=(t-lambda)^m throughout the neighborhood.

Now S={x: lambda has residual multiplicity at least m} is closed (polynomial and derivative vanishing conditions), nonempty, and open by the same local argument at every point of S. Multiplicity cannot exceed m by its maximal choice on the beta line. Connectedness gives S=M, with exactly m copies everywhere. Remove the globally constant real factor ((t-lambda)(t-conjugate(lambda)))^m from R and repeat. Smooth polynomial division by a fixed monic factor preserves smooth coefficients. The residual polynomial cannot acquire additional copies of the just removed root because its full multiplicity was exactly m everywhere. At every iteration the degree falls by 2m; thus after finitely many steps no nonreal roots remain. Every nonreal eigenvalue of the original field is among the constant pairs removed, with constant full algebraic multiplicity. This proves the exact full source statement using its local splitting and holomorphicity results without a regularity assumption.

For a compactly supported field on R^n, the standard chart in S^n carries its support to a compact subset missing infinity. Define it to be zero outside that compact neighborhood. This produces a smooth real Nijenhuis field on closed connected S^n, zero on a whole neighborhood of infinity. A persistent nonreal number cannot be an eigenvalue of zero. The deduction holds for every n >= 1; the necessity does not need an almost-complex-structure obstruction on any particular sphere dimension.
