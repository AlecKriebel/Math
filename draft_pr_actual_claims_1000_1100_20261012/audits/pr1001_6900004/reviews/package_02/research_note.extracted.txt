                Full stress stratiﬁcations determine a labelled graph
                                                    Alec Kriebel∗

                                        12 October 2026 (research note)



                                                        Abstract
              For a simple labelled graph, consider the full conﬁguration space, including vertex collisions,
          partitioned into connected components of coordinate-sign equivalence classes of self-stress spaces.
          We prove that two graphs induce identical partitions precisely when their edge sets agree. More
          explicitly, an edge is recovered by asking whether its collision diagonal is a union of strata. This
          answers the literal equality formulation of Karpenkov’s Problem 4. The proof combines the
          known singleton-support collision criterion with an explicit positive diagonal transport along a
          collinear path. The comparison convention is essential: nonisomorphic trees can have ambient-
          linearly equivalent stratiﬁcations.

Fix integers n, d ≥ 1. A graph below is simple and has the common labelled vertex set [n]; unused
vertices are isolated. Its conﬁguration space is X = (Rd )n . For P = (p1 , . . . , pn ) and a graph G,
put                                                                                

There is one real coordinate per unordered edge, so wij = wji . Two conﬁgurations for the same
graph are equivalent when a homeomorphism between their stress spaces preserves the sign of every
edge coordinate, including zero. Let Sd (G) be the partition of X into connected components of
these equivalence classes. We retain the entire Cartesian space: collisions, self-intersections and
deﬁcient aﬃne spans are allowed. These are the full-space conventions of [1, 2].
    Equality of partitions means equality as collections of subsets of this ﬁxed labelled space. We do
not directly compare stress spaces belonging to diﬀerent graphs. Write ∆ij = {P ∈ X : pi = pj }
for a collision diagonal.

Theorem 1. For every simple labelled graph G and every i ̸= j,

                           {i, j} ∈ E(G)     ⇐⇒       ∆ij is a union of strata of Sd (G).

Consequently, for simple graphs G, H on [n],

                                      Sd (G) = Sd (H)     ⇐⇒       E(G) = E(H).

Proof. If e = {i, j} is an edge, the stress positive only on e belongs to WG (P ) exactly when
pi = pj : its only possibly nonzero forces are pj − pi and pi − pj . Thus a coordinate-sign-preserving
homeomorphism of ﬁbres cannot identify a collapsed and a noncollapsed placement of e. The
collision diagonal is therefore a union of whole equivalence classes, hence of strata. This elementary
collision detector is already recorded in Panina’s Lemma 3.1 (preprint Section 3, Lemma 4) [3]; we
claim no novelty for it.
    Conversely, suppose {i, j} is not an edge of G. Choose a ∈ Rd \ {0} and, for −1/2 ≤ t ≤ 1/2,
set
                pk (t) = xk (t)a,   xi (t) = t, xj (t) = 0, xk (t) = k + 1 (k ∈ / {i, j}).
The only pair that can collide on this path is {i, j}, so no G-edge collapses. Orient the edges
arbitrarily and let BG be the vertex-edge incidence matrix, with +1 at the initial endpoint and −1
at the ﬁnal one. For an oriented edge (u, v) put Dt = diag(xv (t) − xu (t)). Since a ̸= 0,

                 WG (P (t)) = ker(BG Dt ),         Tt = Dt−1 D0 ,      BG Dt Tt = BG D0 .

Every entry of Dt stays nonzero with constant sign. Hence Tt is a positive diagonal linear homeo-
morphism from WG (P (0)) onto WG (P (t)); its inverse is D0−1 Dt . The construction is independent
of edge orientation and preserves zero coordinates as well as both nonzero signs. For an edgeless
graph it is the unique map between zero stress spaces.
    The entire continuous path thus lies in one ﬁbre-equivalence class. Its connected image lies in
one connected component of that class, hence in one stratum. But P (0) ∈ ∆ij and P (1/4) ∈   / ∆ij .
The diagonal cannot be a union of whole strata. This proves the equivalence. Identical partitions
now recover identical edge sets; the reverse implication is immediate. For n = 1 there is only the
empty simple graph.

     The collinear path is a witness inside the full conﬁguration space; the theorem is not a classiﬁca-
tion restricted to collinear placements. Stress-space dimension alone does not supply the argument:
it is the labelled coordinate support that detects a collapsed edge.

Corollary 2. A speciﬁed permutation of vertex labels carries Sd (G) onto Sd (H) exactly when it
carries G onto H.

Proof. Reindexing points and edge weights carries the equilibrium equations for G to those for the
relabelled graph. Apply Theorem 1.


2    The original question and the comparison convention
Karpenkov’s Problem 4 [2, p. 22] asks which subgraphs of Kn deﬁne the same stratiﬁcations. Its
preceding deﬁnitions use connected strata in the full labelled Cartesian conﬁguration space. Under
literal equality of these deﬁned partitions, Theorem 1 gives the complete answer: each equality class
contains exactly one labelled graph. The source does not separately deﬁne a cross-graph abstract
or ambient equivalence. We do not infer an author’s unwritten intention or claim to classify such
diﬀerent comparisons, generic-only spaces, or collision-free spaces.
    The distinction has a concrete mathematical eﬀect. Let G be the four-vertex path with edges
12, 23, 34, and H the star with edges 12, 13, 14. For any forest, leaf elimination shows that stresses
are supported exactly on its collapsed edges; those coordinates are arbitrary and independent. The
invertible linear transformation

                    q1 = p 1 ,   q 2 = p2 ,   q 3 = p1 + p3 − p2 ,   q 4 = p1 + p4 − p3

turns the path’s three edge diﬀerences into the star’s three edge diﬀerences. It therefore maps their
full stress-equivalence classes, and their connected components, bijectively under that edge corre-
spondence. The graphs have diﬀerent degree multisets, so they are nonisomorphic, although their
stratiﬁcations are ambient-linearly equivalent. Their partitions are unequal in the ﬁxed labelled
space by the theorem.
