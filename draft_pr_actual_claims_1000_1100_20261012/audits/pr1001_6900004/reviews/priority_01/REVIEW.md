## Claim tested for priority

The data are the connected coordinate-sign stress partition of the **entire** fixed labelled space `(R^d)^n`, for a simple graph on `[n]` and `d >= 1`. Equality means the same collection of subsets of that space. The claim is that equality holds if and only if the edge sets are equal. The stronger reconstruction formulation says that `{i,j}` is an edge exactly when the collision diagonal `p_i=p_j` is a union of whole strata.

A prior-result match would require this comparison data, full collision-inclusive base, connected strata, all simple graphs (including isolated vertices), and all positive dimensions, or an earlier proof that implies the same universal assertion without adding an unsupported step. Merely detecting a collapsed edge **within a known graph**, comparing dimensions, comparing a single realization space, giving generic stressability conditions, or studying an affine quotient does not meet that criterion.

## What is already established

1. **The framework and sign-preserving partition are foundational.** Doray–Karpenkov–Schepers, [arXiv:0806.4976v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.4976v2), Definitions 2.1–2.6 and Theorem 2.8, provide the full Cartesian base, symmetric edge stresses, zero nonedge coordinates, connected sign-equivalence strata, and semialgebraicity. The [final journal PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00454-009-9229-4.pdf) was also recovered and inspected: its relevant numbering is Definition 2.7, Theorem 2.10 and Lemma 2.12. Section 4 gives conditional fiber/dimension comparisons. Proposition 4.1 cancels an edge using a supported stress on an induced subgraph and restricts the domain to `Sigma_d(G,H)`. Theorem 5.5 concerns geometric descriptions of dimension-threshold loci; Problem 2 asks for graphs associated with given such loci. These do not state full-partition reconstruction. Publication was 18 November 2009, with a 2010 issue.

2. **The collision detector is explicit prior art.** Panina's [2019 v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.07212v4), Section 3, Lemma 4, detects a degenerate edge by its positive singleton-support stress sign vector. The actual 2020 journal body uses **Lemma 3.1**, p.144, for this result and **Proposition 3.2**, p.145, for strong/weak equivalence; these are not the preprint's numbering. Section 1 excludes degenerate graph edges and uses an affine quotient; Section 3 explicitly returns to the unnormalized collision-inclusive stress convention. Appendix A.3, p.148, already observes collinear stress-matroid dependence on vertex order. Appendix A.1 gives another graph with the same particular realization space. These are important antecedents, not the tested full-partition classification. The published body was inspected in its [article-text mirror](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346720911_A_universality_theorem_for_stressable_graphs_in_the_plane); DOI journal access failed. Cite the stable [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.26493/1855-3974.641.e06) and version-specific theorem numbers.

3. **Collinear incidence normalization is elementary established machinery.** The explicit positive diagonal transport in the PR is useful for checking the component argument, but should not be advertised as a new stress-space equivalence technique. Independently, Clay–Jordán–Tóth, [Minimally Rigid Tensegrity Frameworks](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-025-00786-7), Section 2, state that a one-dimensional rigidity matrix is obtained from a graph incidence matrix by nonzero row scalings. Their surrounding discussion concerns proper-stress supports and rigidity bounds. This supports established normalization machinery, not an earlier full-partition reconstruction theorem. Publication was 11 October 2025; the issue is 2026.

The potentially publishable contribution is the **specific consequence for comparison of entire partitions**: an existing edge makes its collision diagonal a union of strata, while a nonedge permits a collinear path through that diagonal within one stratum. Combining these facts reconstructs the edge set. No exact earlier statement or proof of this consequence was located. Calling the result an elementary reconstruction observation is more defensible than claiming novel collision detection, oriented-matroid theory, or transport machinery.

## Relationship to original Problem 4

Karpenkov's [2018 journal paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40598-018-0080-7) was inspected in the full publisher text and [journal PDF](https://armj.math.stonybrook.edu/pdf-Springer-final/018-0080.pdf). Definitions 1.1–1.3 on p.20 prescribe labelled vertices, positive dimension, the full Cartesian base, coordinate-sign-preserving homeomorphisms, and connected strata. Examples 1.4–1.5 and the example after Problem 5 include collisions. Problem 4 on p.22 asks: “Which subgraphs of K_n define the same stratifications?” No distinct cross-graph equivalence relation is specified. Section 2 also emphasizes combinatorial dimensions and adjacency, and discusses coincident individual strata. Publication online was 2 February 2018.

**Inference from those bodies:** literal equality of the defined partitions is a natural mathematically exact reading and the submitted theorem completely answers that reading. The source does not warrant silently replacing it with an abstract comparison; nor does its lack of a separate relation prove that no broader comparison was intended. Therefore the comparison must appear in the title/abstract/introduction and metadata wherever a Problem 4 solution is claimed. This is scope discipline, not a defect in the literal theorem.

The fresh mathematical review's nonisomorphic four-vertex path/star construction is a checkable reason to retain the distinction. Arbitrary ambient linear equivalence identifies their full stratified spaces, while literal partition equality does not. That alternative cannot be claimed solved by uniqueness of the labelled edge set. Likewise, collision-free/generic restrictions, adjacency-poset comparison, stressability loci alone, and pure-condition polynomials are different data. None is settled here. The note need not speculate about what the original author had in mind.

## Adversarial priority tests and limits

- The strongest known ingredient, Panina's collision detector, was not treated as novel. The detector alone does not identify which collision diagonals fail to be unions of strata for nonedges; the submitted connected path supplies that missing direction.
- Earlier full sign-set and strong-equivalence machinery was not repackaged as a new equivalence theorem. The new proof directly constructs the needed positive linear equivalences.
- Collinear vertex-order invariance is also prior. The explicit formula is appropriate as a transparent derivation, with no claim of priority for the formula or normalization technique itself.
- Fiber isomorphisms in the surgery literature were checked against configuration-domain and graph hypotheses. They do not compare complete partitions, and abstractly isomorphic fibers do not in general preserve every coordinate sign.
- Panina's equal selected realization spaces and White–Whiteley's reconstruction conjecture concern weaker/different comparison data. They do not refute or preempt the exact theorem; the exact theorem does not resolve them.
- Search snippets, author-list pages, titles, and bibliographic databases were discovery aids. Only inspected original paper bodies support the substantive scope exclusions. Failed full-text retrievals remain listed as failures.
