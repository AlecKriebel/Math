# Fresh Langlands/noninduction derivation

Pinned claim: PR1032, problem 30001739, head `810d52306312623ecfe01ecbc5f5f0dd360972c3`.
This derivation concerns the representation and noninduction hypotheses. It does not calculate a unitary period.

## 1. Exact question and category

Write `G=GL_n(E)` and `X` for the nonsingular Hermitian matrices. The original Conjecture 4 is the assertion

\[
 \pi=\pi_1\times\cdots\times\pi_k\ \text{irreducible},\quad
 \pi_i^\tau\simeq\pi_i,\quad
 \pi_i\ \text{not a whole proper parabolic induction}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,\pi^*)=2^k.
\]

The report identifies the last space with the sum over the `G` orbits of `X` of the stabilizer-period spaces. For a quadratic extension of p-adic fields there are two Hermitian isometry classes. Thus the MODEL's summed multiplicity is the original multiplicity, including when the two stabilizer groups happen to be isomorphic. At `k=1` the target product is just the factor `pi_1=pi`, so irreducibility of the target product is automatic. A definition of `pi` as a quotient of another induction does not violate the whole-product hypothesis.

Source authentication: [EMS published report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46327), PDF page 16, printed p.734, section 5.3; the [MFO report](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/3229/OWR_2011_14.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) has the same conjecture on PDF page 16, printed p.736. The period-space identification is on PDF page 13 (EMS p.731; MFO p.733). The full original statement does not restate every category assumption. The candidate field `Q_3` is squarely within the local-field setting, and the adopted smooth complex category matches the cited local representation theory. This is sufficient to test the original universal assertion without extending the result to other categories.

## 2. Representation and quotient conventions

Fix the trivial character `rho=1` of `E^*`. On its cuspidal line, write a segment as the finite interval of characters

\[
 [a,b]=\{\nu_E^a,\nu_E^{a+1},\ldots,\nu_E^b\}.
\]

Its length is `b-a+1`; the Langlands segment representation is

\[
 L([a,b])=\operatorname{cos}(\nu_E^a\times\cdots\times\nu_E^b)
 =\operatorname{St}_{b-a+1}(E)|\det|_E^{(a+b)/2}.
\]

The quotient convention is essential. In the same notation `Z([a,b])` is the socle of the ascending character product and is a different representation in general. No replacement of `L` by `Z` at this step is allowed.

Put

\[
 A=[0,2],\ B=[1,4],\ C=[3,3],\ D=[2,5],\ E_0=[4,6],
 \quad m=A+B+C+D+E_0.
\]

The ordered segment products in the proof are

\[
 O_1=(E_0,D,C,B,A),\quad
 O_2=(E_0,C,D,B,A),\quad
 O_3=(E_0,D,B,C,A).
\]

For intervals on one line, direct unpacking of linkage gives

\[
 [a,b]\prec[c,d]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a<c\le b+1\ \text{and}\ b<d.
\]

The independent checker examines every earlier/later pair in all three orders and finds no precedence violation. `O_1` also has decreasing real exponents `5,7/2,3,5/2,1`; hence it is the usual analytic standard order. The total degree is `3+4+1+4+3=15`. The three products are the same Langlands standard module up to the usual unlinked reorderings. Classification supplies

\[
 \pi=L(m)=\operatorname{cos}(\lambda(m)),
\]

an irreducible smooth admissible representation with multiplicity one in the standard module. The finite-length cosocle being simple implies that the standard module has a unique maximal proper submodule: every simple quotient factors through that cosocle, and the quotient maps to that simple object differ by a scalar. This does not assert that the maximal submodule is generated by particular rank-one kernels.

All characters on the chosen line are fixed by `tau`, because the normalized absolute value is fixed. The Galois twist commutes with induction and with passage to the unique irreducible quotient, so `pi^tau` is isomorphic to `pi`.

The classification interfaces used here are in [LM v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.6310v3), Definition 2.2 and sections 2.2–2.3, pp.8–11. Full-source hash and version are recorded in `sources/SOURCE_RECEIPTS.json`.

## 3. Universal reduction of proper induction to a finite partition

Suppose, for a contradiction, that this `pi` were itself induced from a proper Levi. Conjugating the parabolic to standard form gives a product of at least two positive-degree factors. Normalized parabolic induction is exact and preserves nonzero objects. In the compact model, a nonzero inducing vector yields a nonzero section supported near the identity coset; consequently a nonzero proper submodule or quotient of an inducing factor remains nonzero after induction. Thus simplicity of the full induced representation forces the inducing data to be simple. An irreducible representation of this Levi is an external tensor product of irreducible admissible representations of its general-linear blocks.

If there are more than two blocks, group all but the last one. The grouped factor must itself be irreducible: a nonzero proper subrepresentation of it, and the corresponding nonzero quotient, would survive further induction with the last factor, contradicting simplicity of `pi`. We therefore obtain an entire irreducible product

\[
 \pi=\sigma\times\omega,
 \qquad \deg\sigma>0,\quad\deg\omega>0.
\]

Classification writes `sigma=L(m_1)` and `omega=L(m_2)`. The multiplicity-one additive constituent property gives

\[
 [L(m_1+m_2)]\le [L(m_1)\times L(m_2)].
\]

Because the right side is the class of a single irreducible object, `pi` must equal `L(m_1+m_2)`. Injectivity of Langlands classification then gives the equality of **multisets of segments**

\[
 m_1+m_2=m.
\]

This is stronger than equality of supercuspidal supports. It rules out splitting, joining, or replacing segments by other segments with the same support. Since the five occurrences in `m` are distinct and both degrees are positive, `m_1,m_2` are exactly a partition into two nonempty subsets of `A,B,C,D,E_0`. There are `(2^5-2)/2=15` unordered possibilities, represented by the five singleton sides and ten two-element sides. The argument is about the whole induced object; a reducible induction having `pi` among its constituents does not supply a counterexample to this reduction.

## 4. Why a criterion stated for Z applies to these L parameters

Let `T` be the Zelevinsky involution on the Grothendieck ring. Its relevant properties are

\[
 T([\alpha\times\beta])=T([\alpha])T([\beta]),\quad
 T([L(n)])=[Z(n)],\quad T([Z(n)])=[L(n)].
\]

It permutes the basis of irreducible classes. A finite-length representation is irreducible precisely when its positive irreducible expansion has total multiplicity one. Applying a basis permutation preserves that total multiplicity, including repeated occurrences. Therefore

\[
 L(m_1)\times L(m_2)\text{ irreducible}
 \iff Z(m_1)\times Z(m_2)\text{ irreducible}.
\]

This is an argument in the representation ring together with positivity and finite length. It does not require a functorial isomorphism between the two induced modules and does not replace `m_i` by the combinatorial dual multisegment `m_i^t`. LM section A.5, pp.52–53, states the needed ring and irreducible-basis properties.

## 5. Ladder hypothesis and exact LC graph

A same-line multisegment is a ladder for the LM criterion when no segment occurrence contains a different occurrence; equivalently, both endpoints are strictly monotone in the same order. Adjacent linkage is not required. Every singleton is a ladder. Among two-element sides of this candidate, only `BC` and `CD` fail this condition. Their complements `ADE` and `ABE` are ladders. Every one of the 15 partitions therefore has at least one ladder side.

For ordered lists of segment **occurrences** `m_1=(u_i)` and `m_2=(v_j)`, define

\[
 X_{m_1,m_2}=\{(i,j):u_i\prec v_j\},\qquad
 Y_{m_1,m_2}=\{(i,j):(u_i-1)\prec v_j\}.
\]

Here `u_i-1` means subtract one from both endpoints of the first segment. A `Y` vertex `(i_2,j_2)` is a neighbor of an `X` vertex `(i_1,j_1)` exactly when

\[
 (i_1=i_2\ \text{and}\ v_{j_2}\prec v_{j_1})
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (j_1=j_2\ \text{and}\ u_{i_1}\prec u_{i_2}).
\]

Thus the first coordinate of `X` precedes the first coordinate of its neighbor, whereas the neighbor's second coordinate precedes the second coordinate of `X`. Reversing both arrows is a different graph. LC means an injection from `X` to `Y` using these neighbors. The source theorem with one ladder side tests **both** `LC(m_1,m_2)` and `LC(m_2,m_1)`. It applies when the failing direction has the nonladder side first, because the second tested condition is explicitly included in the theorem. It does not require both sides to be ladders.

If one `X` vertex has no neighbor, a singleton subset has Hall defect one and no covering injection exists. The independent checker enumerates the complete graphs in both directions, every subset for Hall's condition, and a separate exhaustive covering-injection search. The following table is one independently selected obstruction per partition. Segment label `E` means `E_0`.

For example, in `C → ABDE`, `C=[3,3]` precedes `E=[4,6]`, so `(C,E)` belongs to `X`. The shifted `C=[2,2]` precedes none of `A,B,D,E`, hence `Y` is empty and the obstruction is immediate. The finite failures, the ladder hypothesis, the two-direction source theorem, and the ring-involution transfer prove that no entire proper induced realization of `pi` exists.

## 6. What follows and what does not

The assigned family establishes that `pi` is an irreducible smooth admissible complex representation of `GL_15(E)`, is `tau` invariant, and is not itself any proper parabolic induction. These are the original conjecture's `k=1` hypotheses. It also checks the ordered/cosocle prerequisites used in the proposed period proof.

It does **not** prove `Hom_{U(V)}(pi,C)=0`. That conclusion additionally needs the complete period-basis theorem at all three orders, correctly normalized holomorphic intertwiner and gamma-ratio specialization, label-diagonal invertible transport across both nested swaps, and linear independence after each linked swap for arbitrary Hermitian `V`. If that independent analytic chain holds, the verified candidate is an exact-scope counterexample with total multiplicity zero instead of two. Finite matching PASS alone cannot discharge any of those analytic obligations.
