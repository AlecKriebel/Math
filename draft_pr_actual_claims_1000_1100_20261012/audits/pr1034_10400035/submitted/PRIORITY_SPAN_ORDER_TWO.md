# Stanford Q2.13: focused priority and context check

Checked 9 October 2026. Scope: the proposed ordinary, unframed, rational, two-string, order-two failure of the Milnor/free-exterior spanning statement. This is a bounded literature and attribution check, not an independent proof audit and not a claim of mathematical priority.

## Conclusion

No exact earlier statement of this degree-two counterexample, and no theorem contradicting it in the same category, was located. The proposed result combines established ingredients; the checked sources do not themselves state their consequence for Stanford Q2.13. Search absence is not evidence that nobody previously observed the consequence. Appropriate description, conditional on proof acceptance: **a negative answer to the spanning clause at (k,n)=(2,2), derived from published low-degree classification and a published free-tangle example; no novelty claim.** The nontriviality clause for N_n remains separate.

## Controlling question

T. Stanford's contribution to T. Ohtsuki (ed.), *Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), §2.6, p.408, fixes the strand count and asks whether Milnor invariants/products together with invariants vanishing on all free-group-complement string links span the finite-type space. It does not require a direct sum. Its motivation concerns local knots, not arbitrary knotting of a component after other strands are deleted. The following Polyak discussion belongs to a different question. The original page and its preceding setup were checked directly. [Published source](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf)

## Ingredient attribution

1. **Complete ordinary order-two classification:** Jean-Baptiste Meilhan, *On Vassiliev invariants of order two for string links*, Theorem 2.4, with Definitions 1.1–1.3 and §2.1. For two strings its expression uses constants, linking, linking squared, the two component Conway coefficients, and the plat-derived V_2. The theorem and proof on preprint pp.4–6 were read, including the normalization on p.3. Remark 2.2 credits earlier degree-two dimension computations to Bar-Natan; the introduction credits Murakami's closed-link analogue. Do not describe these ingredients as new. Meilhan's article does not state the Stanford free-exterior consequence. The inspected version is arXiv v2, 10 November 2004; journal: J. Knot Theory Ramifications 14 (2005), 665–687. [Versioned source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402036v2)

2. **All-length based Milnor concordance invariance:** Meilhan–Yasuhara, §5.1, explicitly states this in its fixed-endpoint string-link category and cites A. J. Casson, *Link cobordism and Milnor's invariant*, Bull. LMS 7 (1975), 39–40. A particularly direct accessible primary treatment is Habegger–Lin, *On link concordance and Milnor's μ-invariants*, Bull. LMS 30 (1998), 419–428, introduction and §1 p.420: it identifies the Milnor information with the nilpotent Artin representations and applies Stallings to a concordance to prove their invariance. This is stronger source support than silently substituting nonrepeating link-homotopy invariants. [Habegger–Lin published PDF](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0024609398004494/resource/name/S002460939800449Xa.pdf); [Meilhan–Yasuhara versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1527v1). Casson's original [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/7.1.39) returned 403 here; its original proof was not inspected.

3. **Free exterior with a trefoil strand:** the complete inspected source is João Miguel Nogueira, **Knot complements with meridional essential surfaces of arbitrarily high genus**, Coimbra preprint **14–09**, received 27 February 2014, Appendix §4, pp.14–15, Figures 9–10. Its title lacks the word “Prime.” The appendix starts with a trefoil arc and an unknotting tunnel, slides the tunnel endpoints to the ball boundary, and states the resulting two-string tangle has handlebody exterior. The complete appendix text and Figure 9–10 image were inspected. This source, rather than a newly invented example, should receive the geometric credit. It works in the PL category (p.2); any transition to the smooth category is a standard smoothing step that should remain explicit in a fully scoped proof. [Coimbra PDF](https://www.mat.uc.pt/preprints/ps/p1409.pdf); [departmental 2014 index](https://www.mat.uc.pt/preprints/2014.html). The later title is *Prime knot complements…*, Topology and its Applications 194 (2015), 427–439, posted as arXiv:1706.03719 in 2017. These versions have different pagination/figure numbering; byte-identical artwork is not claimed.

4. **No-local-knot distinction:** Nogueira, *Tunnel number degeneration under the connected sum of prime knots*, Definitions 1–2 and §2 pp.3–4, separately defines free, trivial, unknotted, and product tangles. It states that free tangles have no local knots while permitting individually knotted strings. This agrees with the original question's motivation and does not contradict the proposed witness. The work also places free-tangle constructions in an older tunnel-number literature, including Morimoto. Earliest priority for the general free-tangle technique was not established. [Versioned source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.7275v2)

5. **Other standard inputs:** K#(-K) being smoothly null-concordant is ordinary knot-concordance background, recalled by Cochran–Harvey's introduction; connected-sum multiplicativity of Conway is recalled by Conant §1; Meilhan §2.1 gives the trefoil coefficient 1. The braid-complement retraction is explicit in Bar-Natan §4.1. None should be claimed as an original contribution of the candidate. The candidate's particular null-concordant test construction, coefficient elimination, boundary-marking argument, and four-evaluation application are its own proof assembly and require the independent mathematical audit.

## Nearby theorems that do not contradict the candidate

- Habegger–Masbaum, *The Kontsevich integral and Milnor's invariants*, Corollary 6.4 and its proof in §15 concern rational finite-type **concordance** invariants. Theorem 16.1 concerns **pure braids**. Neither says arbitrary finite-type restrictions to all free-exterior string links are Milnor-generated. Its tangles are framed; ordinary unframed specialization must be stated. The relevant statements/proofs were checked in the author preprint of 7 April 1999. [Author source](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gregor.masbaum/K39.ps.gz)
- Meilhan–Yasuhara Theorem 5.9 includes component Arf invariants and mod-2 Milnor data in a C_3-concordance classification. These torsion-valued invariants are not contrary examples to a claim about rational ordinary finite-type concordance invariants. The characteristic-zero qualification matters.
- Free nilpotent quotients, four-dimensional free slice-disc complements, welded/virtual “free tangles,” and string links inside handlebodies are different hypotheses or categories. None was treated as a result on the actual three-dimensional free-exterior locus in Stanford's question.
