## Controlling question and degree seven source

1. **Stanford's question.** T. Stanford's contribution to T. Ohtsuki (ed.), *Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), §2.6, printed p.408 (PDF p.36), uses rational-valued invariants of order at most n for a fixed strand count. Its condition is freeness of the actual complement group. The nontriviality and spanning clauses are distinct. The inspected passage contains no reversal argument. [Published PDF](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf).

2. **Duzhin–Karev, controlling version.** *Detecting the orientation of long links by finite type invariants*, arXiv:math/0507015v5, 25 July 2005. Definitions on p.1 preserve numbered components; pp.2–3 specify the symbol involution by reversing vertical order without exchanging support lines. Section 2, p.2, permits Q and states surjectivity onto ordinary weight systems satisfying framing independence. Lemma 4, p.6, gives the leg-parity rule; Proposition 2, p.8, gives the nonzero seven-wheel. Section 6 and Lemma 8, p.9, explicitly establish ordinary, unframed survival. Thus citing only the earlier framed calculation would be inadequate, but the candidate's v5 citation supplies the omitted step. Section 7, pp.9–10, discusses other orientation questions, not Stanford's free-exterior subspace. [Versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0507015v5).

   This work was subsequently published under the title *Detecting the Orientation of String Links by Finite Type Invariants*, Funct. Anal. Appl. 41(3) (2007), 208–216; Russian original, 48–59. The journal bibliographic record and abstract were checked; the publisher-version proof was not independently compared page for page. [Journal record](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=faa&option_lang=eng&paperid=2865&wshow=paper), [English DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10688-007-0018-7).

3. **Later published use and minimum orientation degree.** Meilhan–Yasuhara, *Abelian quotients of the string link monoid*, Algebraic & Geometric Topology 14 (2014), 1461–1488, Remark 1.4, p.1463, reports that seven is the smallest orientation-detecting degree, crediting Bar-Natan computations and Duzhin–Karev for existence. Section 3.2, pp.1474–1475, uses the rational ordinary diagram algebra and degree-seven noncommutativity. This is a prior application to C_k-equivalence, not an identified application to free exteriors. I did not rerun the computations underlying the minimum-degree statement. It does not establish N_n(2)=0 for n below seven: a reversal-even invariant could still vanish on all free exteriors. [Published PDF](https://msp.org/agt/2014/14-3/agt-v14-n3-p09-s.pdf).

## Geometric attribution and the marking issue

4. **An older direct handlebody reference.** Rubinstein–Scharlemann, *Genus two Heegaard splittings of orientable three-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs 2 (1999), 489–553, §2, pp.491–492, constructs the standard involution of a genus-two handlebody and states both preservation of every simple closed boundary curve up to isotopy and commutation, up to isotopy, with every boundary homeomorphism. This is a useful earlier citation for the exact classical ingredient recalled by Bruno–Mecchia (2014), p.272. It does not itself supply the candidate's endpoint-marked tangle application. Neither reference should be presented as establishing earliest historical priority. [Rubinstein–Scharlemann PDF](https://msp.org/gtm/1999/02/gtm-1999-02-024p.pdf), [Bruno–Mecchia PDF](https://rendiconti.dmi.units.it/volumi/46/014.pdf).

5. **Simple curves.** Haas–Susskind, *The geometry of the hyperelliptic involution in genus two*, Proc. AMS 105 (1989), 159–165, Theorem 1, p.159, proof pp.160–162, establishes preservation of simple closed geodesics, with orientation reversed on nonseparating curves. This is a surface theorem, not the exact string-link claim. The indexed author-uploaded primary-paper text was inspected; no new publisher-PDF retrieval or byte hash is certified. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1989-0930247-2).

6. **Close prior inversion context.** Futer, *Involutions of knots that fix unknotting tunnels*, arXiv:math/0401421v1, 29 January 2004, §2, p.2, describes gluing the genus-two involutions across a Heegaard surface. The resulting symmetry preserves the meridians of the two knot edges, exchanges the graph vertices, and reverses the tunnel. The introduction also treats tunnel-number-one two-component links. Futer credits Bleiler–Moriah, *Heegaard splittings and branched coverings of B^3*, Math. Ann. 281 (1988), 531–543. Futer's Theorem 1.1 concerns a different requirement: fixing a knot's tunnel pointwise. Neither inspected passage specifies the candidate's prescribed outer four-holed-sphere map and fixed endpoint labels. [Futer version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401421v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401421).

   Accordingly, unmarked strong invertibility is close prior context but is not, by itself, the required citation for equality under this particular ordered string-link reversal. The candidate's exact boundary identification, collar correction, tube filling, and boundary-relative ball isotopy remain essential obligations for the independent audit. The extension need not remain an involution after a collar correction; the stated conclusion only needs a boundary-matched diffeomorphism and relative isotopy.

## Integration and category safeguards

7. **Integration attribution.** The universal integration input belongs to Kontsevich and the established rational-associator framework, not to the candidate's matrix certificate. Le–Murakami, *The universal Vassiliev–Kontsevich invariant for framed oriented links*, Compositio Math. 102 (1996), 41–64, §4 p.48, §5 pp.48–50, and §8 p.54, provides the rational-associator and framed q-tangle construction, crediting Drinfeld's rational associator. Its associator-independence statement there is expressly about closed links; do not silently extend that formulation to a canonical string-link integration. The candidate needs existence, already stated over Q in Duzhin–Karev §2, and ordinary deframing separately. [Published PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1996__102_1_41_0.pdf).

- Preserve the distinction between reversing all component orientations with the ambient half-turn that restores the same labeled ends, exchanging the two labels, mirroring space, and taking a concordance inverse. These are not interchangeable.
- The ordinary conclusion does not assert preservation of a chosen ribbon framing. Dividing by two in antisymmetrization uses characteristic zero; the cited rational statements do not settle torsion-valued variants.
- Genus-two mutation is adjacent literature, not a blanket isotopy theorem: Morton–Ryder explicitly study distinguishable mutant knots in arbitrary embedded genus-two handlebodies. Their §2 pp.2–6 also distinguishes horizontal and other half-turns according to tangle connectivity. [Author PDF](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~su14/papers/genus2mutation.pdf).
- Neither the geometric sources nor the degree-seven detector license the free-exterior conclusion for three or more strands. The candidate correctly does not infer freeness after arbitrary strand deletion.
