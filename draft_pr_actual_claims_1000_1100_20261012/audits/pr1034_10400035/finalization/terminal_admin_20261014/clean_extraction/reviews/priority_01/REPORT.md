## The Milnor-algebra formula is prior mathematics

The fresh author preprint of N. Habegger and G. Masbaum, *The Kontsevich integral and Milnor's invariants*, dated 7 April 1999, was obtained as the original compressed PostScript and converted locally. The mathematical body pp.1–32 was read. Section 4 p.10 defines the tree quotient; Corollary 6.4 p.13 identifies rational finite-type concordance invariants with the Milnor algebra. Theorem 15.1 p.28 and its complete proof pp.29–30 are explicitly filtered, not merely an unfiltered algebra-generation assertion. Framing is explicit on p.5. Its §§14–16 distinguish concordance and pure braids. The publication is Topology 39 (2000), 1253–1289. The author site records a first version on 2 April 1997, but that first version's contents were not recovered, so the exact theorem is certified here at the 1999 edition. [Author source](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gregor.masbaum/K39.ps.gz), [author bibliography and version dates](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gregor.masbaum/).

Here is the checkable low-degree specialization. Pull an ordinary rational finite-type concordance invariant back to framed string links by forgetting the framing. Habegger–Masbaum identifies its degree-at-most-two filtered symbols with tree diagrams. In degree two, every connected tree has three legs. With only two colors, two leg colors coincide; swapping them changes the vertex orientation, so antisymmetry makes the tree zero over Q. Framing independence removes the two self-struts. Thus the ordinary tree quotient through degree two has basis the empty diagram, the mixed strut and its square. These are integrated by 1, ell and ell², which are independent on the integer-linking family of two-string pure braids. Hence all ordinary rational concordance invariants of order at most two are their span.

Every polynomial in every based Milnor invariant, including repeated indices and arbitrary lengths, is concordance invariant. Applying this filtered result to the resulting FUNCTION handles any cancellation in a high-length polynomial presentation. Conversely 1, ell and ell² are in the Milnor algebra. This gives exactly the submitted entire-intersection formula. The three null-concordant square-knot tests are an elementary alternative proof, useful for category clarity; they do not convert the formula into a novel theorem. A preprint should present it as a recalled corollary, with its elementary proof if desired. The source's framed convention is a reason to explain the ordinary specialization, not a reason to overlook the already filtered theorem.

Habegger–Masbaum's pure-braid Theorem 16.1 does not assert that every actual-free-exterior string link is a braid, or that its arbitrary finite-type restrictions are Milnor-generated. Importing either assertion would invalidate the historical comparison. Their mirrored concordance inverse also differs from the candidate's orientation-preserving simultaneous reversal.
