## Verdict and precise scope

**PASS for mathematical family A.** The group action and both fixed rings are correct, the full invariant derivation module is exactly `y k[[y]] d/dy`, its quotient first cohomology vanishes, and restriction on the actual deformation tangent space is injective. No mathematical gap in these claims was found. The permitted nilpotent constant coordinate changes do not alter this calculation and are allowed by the source category.

This verdict does not independently certify the universal-recipient argument, the final publication wording, or priority/novelty. Those are separate acceptance questions. The strongest claim independently established here is

\[
\ker\bigl(TD_G\longrightarrow TD_N\bigr)=0,
\qquad
\Theta^N\simeq yk[[y]]\partial_y,
\qquad
H^1(G/N,\Theta^N)=0.
\]

There is no remaining formal-series gap for these statements. The statement is for the source's unframed local deformation functors, on local Artinian `W(k)`-algebras, with equivalence by all coordinate automorphisms reducing to the identity.

## 1. Exact action and exact fixed rings

Put `k=F_4=F_2[u]/(u^2+u+1)`, `G=(k,+)`, and `N={0,1}`. Define `F_a(t)=t/(1+a t)` and let `rho(a)` substitute `F_a(t)` for `t`.

The denominator is a unit in `k[[t]]`. Exact rational substitution gives

\[
F_b(F_a(t))=\frac{t}{1+(a+b)t}=F_{a+b}(t).
\]

Thus these are continuous automorphisms, each nonidentity element has order two, and the action is faithful because the coefficient of `t^2` in `F_a(t)` is `a`. For `a!=0`, the difference `F_a(t)-t=a t^2/(1+a t)` has order exactly two. Hence every nonidentity element has conductor one in the source convention.

Let

\[
y=\frac{t^2}{1+t}=tF_1(t).
\]

It is `N`-invariant, and `F_1(t)=t+y`. The following explicit basis argument proves the fixed ring for all formal series, avoiding any implicit finite-degree or jet assumption.

Since `y=t^2+O(t^3)`, the series `y^n` and `t y^n` have respective leading terms `t^{2n}` and `t^{2n+1}`. For arbitrary `h(t) in k[[t]]`, repeatedly subtract the appropriate multiple of the basis series with the same lowest remaining power of `t`. The remainder's order strictly increases, so this gives convergent formal series `A(y),B(y)` with

\[
h(t)=A(y)+tB(y).
\]

Uniqueness follows because the leading orders of nonzero `A(y)` and `tB(y)` have opposite parity and cannot cancel. Thus `k[[t]]` is freely generated by `1,t` over `k[[y]]`. Under the nontrivial element of `N`, the displayed expression changes by `y B(y)`. The ring is a domain and `y!=0`, so invariance is equivalent to `B=0`. Therefore

\[
k[[t]]^N=k[[y]].
\]

Multiplying an arbitrary Laurent series by a large power of `y` puts it in `k[[t]]`; the same basis argument then proves

\[
k((t))^N=k((y)).
\]

This also proves, rather than assumes, the degree-two field statement. Its separable polynomial is `t^2+yt+y=0`, whose derivative with respect to `t` is the nonzero field element `y`.

The generator `u+N` of `Q=G/N` acts on `y` by

\[
\rho(u)(y)
=\frac{t^2}{(1+ut)(1+(u+1)t)}
=\frac{t^2}{1+t+t^2}
=\frac{y}{1+y}=:s(y).
\]

The middle equality uses `u(u+1)=1`. Let `z=y^2/(1+y)`. Repeating the basis proof with `y,z` gives

\[
k[[y]]^Q=k[[z]],\qquad k((y))^Q=k((z)),
\qquad s(y)=y+z.
\]

For nonzero `H(z)` one has `ord_y H(z)=2 ord_z H(z)`, including Laurent series. In particular, invariant Laurent series have even `y`-valuation. As a consistency check, the two successive quotient parameters give `z=t^4/(1+t^3)`.

## 2. All derivations, not a finite or smaller tangent module

For this perfect characteristic-two field, even a purely algebraic `k`-derivation of `k[[t]]` is determined by its value on `t`. Indeed every series has a decomposition

\[
h(t)=a(t)^2+t b(t)^2
\]

by extracting square roots of the even and odd coefficients in `k`. A derivation kills squares, hence

\[
d(h)=d(t)b(t)^2=d(t)h'(t).
\]

Thus the full module `Der_k k[[t]]` equals `k[[t]] partial_t`; there are no additional discontinuous derivations in this example. The same argument applies to the Laurent field and to the `y` coordinate. The displayed derivations are continuous: differentiation lowers valuation by at most one and multiplication by a fixed integral coefficient preserves continuity.

For `d=a(t) partial_t`, one calculates exactly

\[
d(y)=a(t)\frac{t^2}{(1+t)^2},\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt}=\frac{t^2}{(1+t)^2}\ne0.
\]

If `d` is `N`-invariant, then `d(y)` belongs to `k[[t]]^N=k[[y]]`. Write it as `b(y)`. The derivative formula forces

\[
a(t)=b(y)\frac{(1+t)^2}{t^2}.
\]

Conversely, for arbitrary `b(y) in k((y))`, this formula defines the unique field derivation extending `b(y) partial_y`: differentiating `t^2+yt+y=0` gives `y a(t)+(t+1)b(y)=0`, which is the same formula. Uniqueness shows that this extension commutes with `N`. It is integral exactly when

\[
\operatorname{ord}_t a(t)=2\operatorname{ord}_y b(y)-2\ge0.
\]

Since the valuation is integral, this is exactly `b in y k[[y]]`. The zero derivation is included separately and satisfies the same ideal statement. Therefore restriction of derivations is injective and identifies

\[
\Theta^N\cong M:=yk[[y]]\partial_y.
\]

The valuation threshold is sharp. `b=y` extends to `(1+t) partial_t`, which is integral; `b=1` extends to `(1+t)^2/t^2 partial_t`, which has a pole of order two and is excluded. This is one key boundary case: using the entire quotient derivation module in place of `M` would change the answer.

## 3. Quotient module action and conventions

Use left conjugation `g.d=rho(g)d rho(g)^{-1}`. The source writes inverse conjugation; these agree here because every group element is its own inverse. All subsequent cocycle formulae consistently use the stated left action. Restriction to the fixed ring is equivariant, so this identifies the actual quotient action on `Theta^N`.

Since `s=s^{-1}` and `s'(y)=1/(1+y)^2`, conjugating `f(y) partial_y` gives the coefficient operator

\[
T f(y)=\frac{f(s(y))}{s'(y)}=(1+y)^2 f\left(\frac{y}{1+y}\right).
\]

This is an exact operator on all of `k((y))`; it preserves `M`, and `T^2=1`. There is no omitted sign in characteristic two and no division by a nonunit within the formula for `T` on integral series.

Let `f in y k[[y]]`. Because `s(y)^2=y^2/(1+y)^2`, the equation `T f=f` is equivalent to

\[
\left(\frac{f}{y^2}\right)\circ s=\frac{f}{y^2}.
\]

The fixed field calculation then gives `f/y^2=H(z)` for a Laurent series. Its `y`-valuation is at least `-1`. By evenness of invariant valuations it is actually at least zero. Therefore `H in k[[z]]`, and conversely every such `H` gives an invariant vector field. Exactly

\[
M^Q=y^2 k[[z]]\partial_y.
\]

For every unrestricted formal series `H(z) in k[[z]]`, the series `yH(z)` belongs to `M` and satisfies

\[
(T-1)(yH(z))
=\left((1+y)^2\frac{y}{1+y}-y\right)H(z)
=y^2H(z).
\]

This supplies an exact preimage for every invariant, with no passage through inverse limits and no convergence of a corrective iteration left to prove. The reverse containment follows from `(T-1)^2=0`. Thus

\[
(T-1)M=M^Q.
\]

For a group of order two, a one-cocycle is specified by its value at the generator, and the relation on that value is `(1+T)f=0`; coboundaries are `(T-1)m`. In characteristic two `ker(1+T)=M^Q`, so

\[
H^1(Q,M)=\frac{\ker(1+T)}{(T-1)M}=0.
\]

No continuous-cohomology distinction is concealed: the group is finite, and the displayed cocycle computation applies to the entire module. The formal topology is used to define substitutions, not to replace the module by jets.

## 4. Restriction injectivity, with a direct proof

Let `c:G -> Theta` be a one-cocycle with zero restricted cohomology class. Subtract a coboundary so that `c(n)=0` for every `n in N`. For the lift `r=u` of the quotient generator, commutativity gives

\[
c(nr)=n.c(r),\qquad c(rn)=c(r),
\]

so `c(r) in Theta^N`. The relation `r^2=1` gives `(1+r)c(r)=0`. By the preceding calculation, `c(r)=(r-1)m` for some `m in Theta^N`. Subtract this additional coboundary. It leaves `c|_N=0` and kills `c(r)`, hence kills the cocycle on all of `G`. This proves

\[
H^1(G,\Theta)\longrightarrow H^1(N,\Theta)
\quad\text{is injective}.
\]

It also checks the usual inflation–restriction argument without depending on a spectral-sequence convention.

For clarity, the same proof can be performed on actual lifts over `A=k[e]/e^2`. Every identity-reducing coordinate change is `id+e d` for a full integral derivation `d`; it has inverse `id-e d`. Write a lift as `(id+e c(g))rho(g)`. Expanding the group relation gives the above cocycle law; coordinate conjugation changes `c` by a coboundary. No framing or choice of a hull enters.

More directly, if restriction of a lift is trivial, first conjugate so its `N`-action is exactly residual. Write its `r`-action as `(id+e d)rho(r)`. Commuting with `N` forces `d in Theta^N`, and being an involution forces `(1+r)d=0`. Pick `m in Theta^N` with `d=(r-1)m`. Conjugation by `id+e m` kills the perturbation of `r` and fixes `N`. Thus the whole lift is trivial. This verifies injectivity for the actual set-valued deformation functors independently of a tangent-space label.

The fixed functor `D_N^Q(A)` is literally a subset of `D_N(A)`. Hence the tangent restriction into that fixed functor is injective as well. The residual action base-changed from `k` to `A` gives its basepoint.

## 5. Category, nilpotent constants, and the nonzero quotient direction

The source category is local Artinian `W(k)`-algebras with fixed residue `k`. The equicharacteristic rings `A=k[e]/e^2` and `B=k[e]/e^3` are objects by the residue map `W(k)->k`. The map fixing `k` and sending `e` to `e+e^2` is a local `W(k)`-algebra automorphism of `B`: its square fixes `e` because `e^3=0`.

For an Artinian base `C` with `m_C^r=0`, the maximal-ideal topology on `C[[y]]` is equivalent to its `y`-adic topology: `(m_C,y)^n` is contained in `(y^{n-r+1})` for `n>=r-1`, and `(y^n)` is contained in `(m_C,y)^n`. A substitution with constant term in `m_C` is continuous in these topologies. The term of fixed `y`-degree in substituting an infinite series receives contributions from only finitely many powers of the nilpotent constant.

In particular,

\[
s_e(y)=\frac{y+e}{1+y}
\]

is an allowed `B`-algebra automorphism. Its matrix has determinant `1+e`, a unit, and its matrix square is `(1+e)I`; hence its inverse is itself. The identity-reducing coordinate change `(1+e)y+e` is allowed as well. The source does not require all lifts or equivalences to fix the section `y=0`; imposing that extra condition would change the problem. This point is also directly confirmed by the translated coordinate matrix in AIF 2009, Proposition 4.1.

Over `A`, every identity-reducing coordinate change has the form `y+e F(y)` with unrestricted `F in k[[y]]`, including an arbitrary constant. Its inverse is `y-eF(y)`. Conjugating the residual involution changes its first-order perturbation by either sign of

\[
F(s(y))-s'(y)F(y).
\]

The two signs coincide here. Its constant coefficient is `F(0)-F(0)=0`. But `s_e-s=e/(1+y)` has constant perturbation coefficient one. Thus the quotient direction represented by `s_e` is nontrivial even with all permissible nilpotent translations included.

Equivalently, under the first-order cocycle normalization `(id+e d)s`, this direction has `d=(1+y) partial_y`. It is invariant under `T`, and it cannot be a coboundary in the full quotient derivation module because every `(T-1)F` has zero constant coefficient. This is a consistency check, not an assumption used to prove restriction injectivity.

Finally, the ambient quotient derivation module is `Theta^sharp=k[[y]] partial_y`, and

\[
\Theta^\sharp/\Theta^N\cong k\partial_y.
\]

The quotient action is trivial because `T` preserves the constant coefficient. Therefore `H^1(Q,Theta^sharp/Theta^N)=k`, as claimed. A related exact check is that ambient invariants are `(1+y)k[[z]] partial_y`, ambient coboundaries are `y^2k[[z]] partial_y`, and `H^1(Q,Theta^sharp)` is one-dimensional. The vector field `1+y` belongs to the ambient invariants and not to `M`; that sharp boundary explains how the quotient tangent direction can coexist with vanishing `H^1(Q,M)`.

No falsification succeeded in this assigned family. There is no claim here that the corresponding statement holds for a category with fixed origin, for imperfect coefficient fields, for arbitrary cyclic groups, or after adding the prorepresentability assumptions on the subgroup and quotient deformation functors.
