## Exact theorem, independent proof and falsification attempts

The target quantifier order is fixed closed oriented genus g>=2, then one finite c_g>=0, then every center, every finite R>=0 and every x,y in the original **closed** half-log Teichmüller ball. The required conclusion is an actual continuous rectifiable path inside that same ball with ordinary metric length <=d_T(x,y)+4c_g. The resulting N_g(k)=k+4c_g includes both distance-at-most-k endpoints and Farb's sphere-pair distance2 condition. No uniform-in-genus, numerical or optimal bound is asserted.

`INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION.md` contains the full checkable argument. Here is its mechanism in compact form. The exact uniform extremal-length inequality supplies one K_g>=1 for every essential curve, every geodesic and every intermediate point. Divide by positive Ext_o(curve), apply half logarithms and then take curve suprema. Each endpoint logarithmic ratio is bounded by its Kerckhoff distance, giving

`d_T(o,z)<=max(d_T(o,a),d_T(o,b))+(log K_g)/2`.

The constant is finite and topology dependent only. There is no chosen maximizing curve or foliation. The ratio direction and factor1/2 agree with both Kerckhoff's original Theorem4 and FR equation2.2. The statement uses genuine minimizing Teichmüller segments, not quasi-geodesics or a wall metric.

For R>=c, retreat endpoints on selected radial minimizing segments by alpha=min(c,d(o,x)) and beta=min(c,d(o,y)). The new radial distances are max(d(o,x)-c,0) and max(d(o,y)-c,0), each <=R-c. Apply the radial estimate with the original center to the new pair; its middle segment stays in radiusR. Both radial tails stay in the same ball because subsegments of a minimizing radial segment minimize. Triangle inequality along a-x-y-b gives total length alpha+d(a,b)+beta<=d(x,y)+2alpha+2beta<=d(x,y)+4c. For R<c, the two radial pieces through the center have length<=2R<=d(x,y)+4c.

Finite arclength parametrizations paste continuously. Metric length is additive across finitely many pieces by partition sums; retracing does not invalidate that statement. Constant pieces cover coincident/collapsed cases. The derivation checks R=c, c=0, R=0, x=y, a=b, center endpoints, asymmetric short radial distances, interior/boundary endpoints and nonunique geodesics. It needs neither properness, completeness, intrinsic-distance attainment nor simultaneous/continuous geodesic selection. Thus there is no hidden central difficulty transferred to an unsupported projection or retraction estimate.
