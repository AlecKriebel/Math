                          Original-ball paths in Teichmüller space:
                          an elementary corollary of Lenzhen–Rafi
                                                      Alec Kriebel∗
                       13 October 2026          Version 1.0        Unrefereed expository note


                                                         Abstract
           We record an elementary path corollary of Lenzhen–Rafi’s established uniform quasiconvexity theorem.
       For each fixed closed oriented surface of genus at least two, every pair of points in any closed Teichmüller ball
       can be joined inside that same ball by a continuous rectifiable path whose length is at most their ambient
       distance plus a topology-dependent constant. Radially moving the endpoints inward before joining them
       gives the deduction. This supplies an aﬀirmative derivation of Farb’s Question 3.5 from prior Teichmüller
       geometry. We make no claim to historical priority or a new substantive Teichmüller theorem.


1      The original-ball conclusion
Fix a closed oriented surface S of genus g ≥ 2. Its marked Teichmüller space T (S) has the metric dT (X, Y ) =
1
2 log K(X, Y ), where K(X, Y ) is the least quasiconformal dilatation in the marking class. Balls below are
closed balls for this metric, and lengths are ordinary metric lengths of paths.
Theorem 1. There is a finite constant cg ≥ 0 such that, for every center o, every finite radius R ≥ 0, and
every x, y ∈ BdT (o, R), there is a continuous rectifiable path in BdT (o, R) joining x to y with length at most
dT (x, y) + 4cg .
    The same cg works for all centers, radii and endpoints at the fixed genus. In particular, ambient distance
at most k gives a path of length at most Ng (k) = k + 4cg . Taking k = 2 satisfies Farb’s definition of almost
convexity and Question 3.5, which asks for bounded-length paths inside the original radius ball [1, pp. 24–25].
No numerical value, genus-uniform bound or optimal constant is asserted.
    The substantive geometric input is Lenzhen–Rafi’s published uniform ball theorem [2, Theorems C and 17].
It allows a minimizing segment to leave the endpoint-radius ball by a bounded additive amount. The short
general deduction below converts that allowance into a path in the original ball. It requires no thickness
condition and no replacement of the Teichmüller metric.


2      The elementary metric deduction
Lemma 2. Let (M, d) be a geodesic metric space. Suppose one finite c ≥ 0 has the following property: for
every o, a, b ∈ M , some minimizing segment from a to b satisfies

                       d(o, z) ≤ max{d(o, a), d(o, b)} + c        at every point z on that segment.                        (1)

Then every x, y ∈ B(o, R) can be joined by a continuous rectifiable path in B(o, R) of length at most d(x, y)+4c.
Proof. If R < c, join x to o and o to y by radial minimizing segments. Their concatenation lies in B(o, R) and
has length at most 2R, which is at most d(x, y) + 4c.
   Suppose R ≥ c. On radial minimizing segments from x and y toward o, move inward by

                                     α = min{c, d(o, x)},          β = min{c, d(o, y)},

Arclength parametrizations of the three pieces paste to give an actual continuous rectifiable path. Constant
pieces cover a = b, coincident endpoints and endpoints at the center. The case R = c is included; so are c = 0
and R = 0. No attainment of an intrinsic-distance infimum, properness, or continuous choice of segments is
needed.


3    Application of the established theorem
Lenzhen–Rafi’s extremal-length result [2, Theorem 15] gives a finite Kg ≥ 1, depending only on the topology,
such that
                                   Extz (γ) ≤ Kg max{Exta (γ), Extb (γ)}                                 (2)
for every essential simple closed curve γ and every point z on the minimizing Teichmüller segment from a
to b. The constant is uniform in the curve, endpoints and intermediate point. Their Theorem 17 proves the
corresponding radial ball statement. For clarity, it can also be obtained directly from Kerckhoff’s formula [3],
in the normalization recorded in [4, Section 2, equation (2.2)]:

                                                      1         Extw (γ)

Divide (2) by the positive Exto (γ) and take the supremum of half logarithms. This gives

There is no need to select a curve or foliation attaining the supremum. Minimizing Teichmüller segments exist
between every pair of points; see also [4, Section 2]. Lemma 2, with cg = 12 log Kg , proves Theorem 1.
   The original published input and these geodesic and distance-formula facts are established external results.
This note does not reprove their foundational Teichmüller theory. The journal’s extremal-length and ball
theorem numbers are respectively 15 and 17; the earlier arXiv:1002.3939v1 uses 13 and 15 for those body
theorems.


4    Attribution, scope and disclosure
All substantive Teichmüller estimates here are due to Lenzhen–Rafi. The contribution of this exposition is
to write out the radial-retreat implication with the exact original-ball and path-length quantifiers. Historical
priority for that elementary implication or this corollary has not been established. The bounded source audit
located no explicit full-scope predecessor in the inspected bodies; it does not establish worldwide absence or
that Farb’s question remained open in 2026.
    Fortier Bourque–Rafi’s nonconvexity result [4] concerns the direct minimizing segment and is compatible
with the concatenated path above. Petyt–Zalloum’s Theorem I [5], present in their 2024 preprint and 2026
published version, gives almost convexity with N (k) = k+6 in a dual wall space. Its map from the original space
is quasiisometric; it does not identify original Teichmüller balls and path lengths. We make no comparison of
numerical constants across those different metrics. Neither the lemma nor this note asserts optimality of 4cg
in Teichmüller space.
    AI tools were used extensively in deriving the corollary, verifying the proof, adversarial review, source
auditing and preparation. This is an unrefereed expository preprint. Its AI adversarial audits do not consti-
tute conventional human peer review, journal acceptance or formal proof-assistant verification. Supporting
authored audit records and source identities are provided separately; third-party paper bodies are not redis-
tributed.
