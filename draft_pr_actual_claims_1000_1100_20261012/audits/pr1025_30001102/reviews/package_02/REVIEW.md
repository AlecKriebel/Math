## Independent universal derivation

### 1. Feasibility, sign change, boundedness and ray bits

Choose an integer `m >= max(0, max_e b_e)`; for empty E take m=0. Setting every original coordinate to m satisfies all cover rows, because `2m >= b_e`, and satisfies every selected integrality restriction. Its encoding length is polynomial in the demand input. Thus infeasibility is not a hidden branch.

The diagonal map with entries +1 on U and -1 on V is integral, rational, invertible and preserves integrality. It changes `x_u+x_v >= b_e` into `y_u-y_v >= b_e` and changes the objective by the same signs. The recession cone of this continuous relaxation is exactly `R={r:r_u-r_v >= 0}`.

If a rational objective is unbounded below on P, standard rational LP produces a rational r with the homogeneous row constraints and `a^T r <= -1`. LP witnesses have polynomial encoding length. The lcm L of its coordinate denominators has logarithm at most their total bit length; multiplying r by L therefore gives a polynomial-bit integral direction. From the integral feasible point, every nonnegative integer step remains mixed feasible and decreases the objective. Conversely T is a subset of P, so boundedness of P implies boundedness of T. No assumption that the unanchored P has vertices is needed. In the finite branch an exact rational LP optimizer y* exists and has polynomial encoding length.

An improving ray is a direction of the relaxation and of the actual hull, as established below. Selected-integer feasibility of the nonconvex mixed set is required at integer steps, not at every noninteger step. That is sufficient for the unboundedness certificate and the strong hull-optimization interface.

### 2. Signed layers and noncircular attainment

For an arbitrary d, write its positive coordinates as successive levels of nested positive indicator sets, and its negative coordinates using successive absolute-value levels and negative indicators. There are at most n nonzero distinct signed levels. All weights are strictly positive, all layer entries are 0/1 or 0/-1, and their weighted sum equals d.

For each ordered coordinate pair i,j, a layer difference has the sign of `d_i-d_j` or is zero. If both coordinates have the same positive sign, nested level sets give this directly; for two negative coordinates, magnitude order and the negative indicator reverse together. For opposite signs, every nonzero difference points from the larger d coordinate to the smaller. Zero coordinates obey the same cases. Because all nonzero contributions to a fixed coordinate or difference have one sign, each individual weighted contribution lies between zero and the total contribution. This verifies conformality on all row differences, including the negative layers that a one-sided decomposition would miss.

Take any mixed z and set d=z-y*. Each `y*+lambda r` lies row by row between y* and z, so is feasible in P. Exact optimality of y* gives `a^T r >= 0`. When lambda is at least one, `z-r` also lies row by row between these endpoints: `r_i-r_j` has the same sign as the difference, and its magnitude does not exceed the corresponding lambda-weighted contribution. The integer step preserves selected integrality. Its objective is no larger, and its distance to y* decreases strictly, since all nonzero layer coordinates have magnitude at least lambda in d and move one unit toward zero.

Crucially, this step is not first applied to an assumed mixed optimum. For any feasible z0, the objective sublevel through z0 is closed and nonempty. Intersect it with the closed l1 ball of radius `||z0-y*||_1`; this compact nonempty set has a nearest point. A closer point anywhere in the sublevel would also be inside that ball, so the minimizer is globally nearest in the sublevel. A layer of weight at least one would contradict nearestness by the removal step. All weights are therefore below one; at most n layers give strict sup-distance below n.

Repeating this reasoning for the sublevel of any z shows that every mixed point has a no-more-expensive representative in the fixed compact box `y*+[-n,n]^n`. Its mixed subset is closed and nonempty, so the global mixed minimum is attained there. Finally choose a closest mixed optimum; the same removal argument yields an optimum at strict distance below n. The conclusion is existence of at least one nearby optimum, not a bound on every optimizer.

### 3. Short labels, completion and exact partial values

The integer intervals from ceiling/floor around y* have width at most 2n, and endpoint encoding lengths are polynomial. An integer assignment to I can be completed if and only if the rows whose two endpoints are in I satisfy `p_u-p_v >= ceil(b_e)`. Necessity follows from an integer left side. For sufficiency, undo the sign change on fixed coordinates and assign every other original coordinate a common sufficiently large integer. An edge with one free endpoint is then satisfied by increasing that endpoint; an edge with two free endpoints is satisfied by increasing their common value. Bipartiteness and unrestricted signs are exactly what makes this completion argument valid. No continuous endpoint row is rounded.

Every admissible fixed slice is nonempty, is a rational polyhedron, and has a finite attained minimum because its recession directions are a subset of R and the objective is bounded on P. Thus exact LP computes its value and an optimizing rational completion. Both the difference-constraint system and the integer assignment domain are closed under componentwise min and max: monotonicity of min/max applied to `y_u >= y_v+b` preserves the inequality. Integer rounded rows and box bounds have the same property.

If y,z optimize two slices, their min and max are feasible in the slices indexed by the corresponding min and max assignments. Coordinatewise `y+z=min(y,z)+max(y,z)` makes a linear objective modular. Consequently the sum of the two slice values is at least the sum of the min/max slice values. This is exact submodularity. The same min closure fails in original cover coordinates, e.g. (1,0) and (0,1) for demand 1; their meet is infeasible. The proof uses the correct transformed coordinates.

### 4. Uniform oracle-bit bound

Let B be the repaired maximum of the union of {1} with all `ceil(abs(b_e))`; this is exactly one for empty E. Let H* and A0 be as in the note, and let k,q be the demand and objective denominator lcms, with empty lcm one. Their logarithms are bounded by sums of denominator input lengths.

In every slice graph component with no fixed coordinate, translation in both directions is feasible. Boundedness forces the sum of objective coefficients on that component to vanish; anchoring one coordinate at zero leaves the optimum unchanged. Other components already have fixed coordinates. The anchored slice has no lineality and has an optimal vertex. If a connected component of its active tight-edge graph had no fixed/zero anchor, translating that active component would preserve all active constraints and contradict full active rank. Tight forests therefore express every coordinate as an integer anchor plus at most n-1 signed demands. Each denominator divides k, and each magnitude is at most `H*+nB`.

Hence a chosen exact slice optimum has objective denominator dividing kq and absolute value at most `n A0(H*+nB)`. A numerator bound follows by multiplying this magnitude bound by kq. This supplies a known uniform polynomial encoding bound for all oracle outputs, not merely an arithmetic operation count. All LP inputs at an oracle call have polynomial-size labels and rational coefficients. The lcm operations are bit-polynomial; no numerical denominator residues are enumerated.

### 5. Explicit ring interface and exact rational minimization

Create one threshold node for every integer coordinate level above its lower bound. Prefix arcs guarantee that a selected-node set corresponds to a unique assignment. For a rounded integer edge with offset d, the lower value of its V endpoint already requires `p_u >= l_v+d`. This produces the essential forced base node, an automatic condition, or infeasibility. Every higher V node implies its offset U node; a target below the U lower bound is automatic, and a target above its upper bound forbids the V node. These three rules are equivalent to every admissible assignment, including zero-width intervals and out-of-range offsets of either sign.

There are at most `2n|I|` nodes and O(n²+n|E|) arcs/conditions; the numerical labels are stored in binary. Forced-node reachability detects infeasibility if it meets forbidden nodes, and otherwise gives the smallest member. Adding a requested node before the same closure gives the smallest member containing it or proves none exists. This is the actual Schrijver §6 interface, not a ring asserted solely from a value oracle. After removing forced and impossible nodes, the union/intersection bounds and closure digraph also give the GLS §10.3 encoding.

The repaired citation correctly names GLS §10.2 and **problem 10.3.8 with its reduction**, rather than calling 10.2.7 a theorem. The primary book makes the rational output-encoding bound part of the oracle model and reduces lattice minimization to ordinary submodular minimization. The supplied closure digraph and the uniform value bound meet those hypotheses. Schrijver's primary §6 independently supplies a ring reduction. The composition uses polynomially many polynomial-bit LP oracle calls and exact rational arithmetic. A minimizing assignment and its optimizing completion contain a global mixed optimizer by the proximity conclusion. The argument is polynomial in total binary length; it does not make rational LP strongly polynomial.

### 6. Core uniform over bounded real objectives

For the structural hull argument, consider any real objective bounded below on P. It annihilates every component translation direction because both signs are in the recession cone. Anchor one coordinate in every graph component at zero. This gives a nonempty pointed relaxation with the same objective optimum. A bounded-below real objective on a pointed polyhedron has an optimal vertex; the active-rank forest argument now connects each component to its zero anchor. The coordinates are sums of at most n-1 signed demands, so their sup-norm is at most nB. The proximity proof used only exact optimality, linearity and compactness, not rationality of the objective. It applies here as well, putting a mixed optimizer inside the objective-independent integer box with H=nB+n.

Thus *every bounded real objective* has its mixed optimum in the same core. Restricting this statement to rational objectives without justification would leave a gap in strict separation; the proof really establishes the stronger real-objective statement needed for that step.

### 7. Actual convex hull equality and closedness

The bounded mixed set in the core is a finite union of bounded rational slice polytopes, since only finitely many integer assignments lie in the box. Their finite convex hull K is therefore a rational polytope. R is a rational polyhedral cone, so K+R is a closed rational polyhedron.

For any r in R, its signed layers from step 2 individually satisfy every homogeneous difference row: the original difference is nonnegative, so every conformal layer difference is nonnegative. This expresses R using finitely many integral 0/1 or 0/-1 directions. For such a direction d and a mixed z, `z+md` is mixed feasible for every nonnegative integer m. Writing a real t between its floor and ceiling expresses `z+td` as a convex combination of two such points. This is an actual finite convex combination. Applying it to each point in a finite convex combination proves the same shift property on conv(T); successively adding finitely many layer directions gives `K+R subset conv(T)`.

If a mixed z lay outside the closed set K+R, a strict real separator would have finite lower support on K+R. It must be nonnegative on R; hence it is bounded below on P. Step 6 puts its mixed minimum in K. The separator would then say z is cheaper than that mixed minimum, a contradiction. Thus T is contained in K+R, and convexity gives `conv(T)=K+R`. No closure substitution or infinite limiting mixture is used. This simultaneously identifies the recession cone of the actual hull as R.

### 8. TU grid, homogeneous facets and lineality

The bounded slice matrices consist of directed incidence rows and signed unit rows for fixings/box bounds; these are TU. Scaling variables by k changes all slice right sides to integers, so every slice vertex lies in `(1/k)Z^n`. Its norm is at most H. K consequently has finite point generators with that denominator and norm bound, while R has integral signed-layer generators of norm at most one.

The actual hull is full-dimensional: in original coordinates, all sufficiently large integer vectors are feasible, and their convex hull contains a translated positive orthant. Homogenize the finite point generators as (kv,k) and the rays as (r,0); coordinates have integer magnitude at most `k(H+1)`. The homogeneous face of each facet has dimension n, hence includes n independent homogeneous generators. Cofactors of those n rows give a nonzero integral facet normal with constant term, bounded in absolute value by `n![k(H+1)]^n`. If there is lineality, both signs of its generators can span the face; independence is a dimension statement and does not require a pointed cone.

The displayed generous phi bound dominates signed coefficient lengths and is polynomial in input encoding, including log k and log H. In the whole-space case it also exceeds n+1, as required by GLS's convention. Generator/facet counts may be exponential; none are enumerated by the algorithm. Only their encoding bound is used.

### 9. Exact strong optimization, separation and boundary membership

Minimizing on T and on its actual hull has the same value; in the finite branch the supplied T point is an exact hull optimizer. In the unbounded branch the integral direction belongs to R, now proved to equal the hull recession cone. For a maximum query use the negative minimization objective and rescale the direction, for example by a further factor two, to meet the GLS normalization even if interpreted strictly. The resulting output still has polynomial bit length. The primary GLS definition does not demand a vertex optimizer.

The actual hull and phi are consequently a well-described rational polyhedron. GLS Theorem 6.4.9 expressly handles general well-described polyhedra, including unbounded and nonpointed ones, and converts this exact strong optimizer into a strong separator. A rational query on the boundary is classified exactly by that interface. The diagonal sign map transforms any returned valid rational inequality back to original coordinates without changing encoding length materially. Dimension zero is the immediate singleton in R^0. This completes the theorem with all advertised quantifiers.
