# Independent analytic nongraph control

Consider n=3, Ω=H\overline{B_a((0,0,c))}, c>a>0, β>0. This is connected with smooth boundary, compact symmetric difference from H, and an extra compact boundary component. On the obstacle boundary the outward normal for Ω is ν=−(z−(0,0,c))/a. On its lower hemisphere ν_y≥0 and y≤c, so

Bβ(Ω)≥2πa²e^(−2βc)>0.

The unchanged plane contributes zero. For R₀>a, choose the tangential logarithmic cutoff equal to one on |x|≤R₀ and taper to zero at R=R₀e^ell. Its exact kinetic integral is 2π/ell, and the ground-state trial satisfies

Q[e^(−βy)ψ]≤π/(β ell)−2πβa²e^(−2βc).

Every ell>e^(2βc)/(2β²a²) makes this strictly negative. The essential spectrum argument in INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION then makes this a discrete eigenvalue certificate. The radius R is large but finite. No coordinate graph of the entire boundary exists: vertical fibers under the ball are two disjoint intervals. This directly probes the forbidden graph-only reduction and the normal orientation on an obstacle.

In n=2, replacing the ball by a disk gives Bβ≥πae^(−2βc). A linear tangential taper with energy 2/L gives Q≤1/(βL)−πβae^(−2βc), negative for L>e^(2βc)/(πβ²a). Both are analytic controls, not new claims of novelty.

For contrast, equality σ=σess does not exclude embedded eigenvalues: the orthogonal direct sum of multiplication by t on L²([−1,∞),dt) and a one-dimensional operator of value zero has σ=σess=[−1,∞) but an eigenvalue at zero. This is a logical control for interpreting Theorem B; it makes no claim that the reviewed Robin examples have embedded eigenvalues.
