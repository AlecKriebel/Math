# Independent derivation, frozen before earlier review reasoning

The proposed statement is for every finite n >= 1 and every nonnegative row-stochastic A. Put S=sum a_ij^2 and h(a)=a^2 log(e/a), with h(0)=0. The target is per(A) <= exp(-n+e^4 sum h(a)) <= exp(-n+e^4 S(1+log n)). Success requires an analytic proof including sparse support, n=1, singular support, and fixed unnormalized S. Finite floating computations are only diagnostics.

Independent verification of the analytic mechanism: For any permutation law X with marginal row b_i, reveal rows in a uniformly random independent order. On a positive history, the conditional law c lies within the unused positive marginal coordinates. Comparing c with marginal weights normalized by their unused total Z gives H(c)+sum c_j log b_ij <= log Z by ordinary probability KL. Chain rule and averaging give H(X)+sum b log b <= sum E log Z. Condition instead on the full supported permutation and row priority t. The unused marginal mass has expectation b_iX_i+(1-t)(1-b_iX_i). Conditional Jensen bounds the expected logarithm by the integral of this expression. The integral is -1-b log b/(1-b); averaging over X_i adds the extra marginal b and gives q(b)=-b^2 log b/(1-b). The continuous endpoint is q(1)=1; zero coordinates are unsampled. Finite support bounds all logs at positive sampled coordinates. Therefore H(X)<=-sum b log b-n+sum q(b), including deterministic X.

For positive permanent define the Gibbs law proportional to the permutation product of entries. Its marginal B is doubly stochastic even if A has only row sums one. Gibbs entropy gives log per=H+sum b log a. Thus log per<=-n+sum q(b)-Dmat. Here Dmat=sum b log(b/a)=sum d(b,a), where d=b log(b/a)-b+a. The last equality uses total masses n and retains d(0,a)=a. If per=0 the desired positive upper bound is automatic. Positive b implies positive a.

For the scalar bound, let L=log(1/b). The elementary integral/log inequality bL<=1-b yields q(b)<=b and q(b)<=b^2(1+L)=h(b). The latter is equivalent to L <= (1-b)(1+L), hence bL<=1-b. Also h'(b)=b(1+2 log(1/b))>0 on (0,1]. If b>=e^2 a, d>=b+a>=q. Otherwise if b<=a then q-d<=h(a), and if a<b<e^2a then h(b)<=e^4h(a), by independently bounding b^2 and log(e/b). Handle b=0 directly and b=1 by continuity. Thus the entrywise inequality holds on the full closed permitted domain, including its switch line.

Sum the scalar inequality. Cauchy per row gives S>=1; row stochasticity also gives S<=n. With p=a^2/S, the exact identity sum a^2 log(1/a)=S/2[H(p)-log S] and H(p)<=log(n^2), log S>=0 give <=S log n. This proves the second bound without column constraints and with zeros by continuity. If S<=gamma, monotonicity of exp gives exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma)e^-n for all n. For n>=2, 1+log n<=3log n; n=1 cannot admit any prefactor-free n^C e^-n bound since A=[1]. Nonempty fixed-S classes need gamma>=1. Fixed gamma is essential. The uniform k-block example has S=k and per=(m!/m^m)^k ~ (2 pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)e^-n, requiring exponent >=k/2 at integer gamma=k.
