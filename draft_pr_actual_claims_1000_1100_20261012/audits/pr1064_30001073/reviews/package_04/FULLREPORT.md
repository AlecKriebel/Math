## Independent mathematical review

The precise hypothesis is finite positive integer n, nonnegative row-stochastic n-by-n A, S=sum_ij a_ij^2. The success claim is

per(A) <= exp[-n+e^4 sum_ij a_ij^2 log(e/a_ij)] <= exp[-n+e^4 S(1+log n)],

with continuous zero terms. Fixed S<=gamma gives exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma)e^-n for every n>=1. Only n>=2 admits the stated prefactor-free n^(3e^4 gamma)e^-n consequence. The original doubly stochastic fixed-gamma class is a subclass; no lower bound is claimed for arbitrary row-stochastic matrices.

INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION.md was written and hash-frozen after reading paper.tex and before earlier review reasoning. The assignment itself disclosed the claim and known issues; this is operational derivation independence, not a blind test. The full five-page proof, not merely the abstract or numerical evidence, was checked:

1. On each positive conditional history, c is supported in positive unused marginal coordinates and Z>0. Comparing c against b restricted and normalized on the unused columns yields the correct conditional KL inequality. Averaging over independent exposure orders and using the entropy chain rule gives H(X)+sum b log b <= sum E log Z. Impossible histories are excluded explicitly.
2. Condition on the supported full permutation and a row's independent uniform priority t. The expected available marginal mass is b_iX_i+(1-t)(1-b_iX_i). Conditional Jensen has the correct direction. The integral equals -1-b log b/(1-b), and averaging the selected coordinate supplies the second factor b in q(b). Finite positive support bounds sampled logs. q(0)=0 and q(1)=1 agree with limits; deterministic permutation entropy attains equality.
3. Zero permanent is handled before defining Gibbs probabilities. For positive permanent, Gibbs marginals B are doubly stochastic even when A lacks column stochasticity, and positive b implies positive a. The entropy identity gives the matrix relative-entropy correction with the right sign. Dmat=n KL(B/n||A/n), not the sequential permutation divergence.
4. Equal total masses cancel linear terms only after summation. The scalar decomposition d=b log(b/a)-b+a retains d(0,a)=a and d(0,0)=0. Positive-input, zero-marginal edges therefore cannot silently disappear.
5. The scalar estimates q(b)<=b and q(b)<=h(b) both follow from b log(1/b)<=1-b. h'(b)=b[1+2log(1/b)]>0 only on the needed domain [0,1]. If b>=e^2 a, d>=b+a>=q. Below the switch, monotonicity or the two independent factors b^2<=e^4a^2 and log(e/b)<=log(e/a) give q-d<=e^4h(a). The closed endpoints, zero input, zero marginal, unit marginal and switch line are covered. No monotonicity outside the domain is used.
6. Row Cauchy gives S>=1 (and S<=n). p=a^2/S has total mass one and at most n^2 points. The exact entropy identity sum a^2 log(1/a)=S/2[H(p)-log S] bounds it by S log n. This covers arbitrary density and zeros without column constraints. For n>=2, 1+log n<=3log n. At n=1, permanent[1]=1 disproves a prefactor-free n^C e^-n formula. Nonempty fixed-S classes require gamma>=1.
7. The fixed k uniform-block construction has S=k and permanent (m!/m^m)^k ~ (2pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)e^-n, so exponent >=k/2 is a legitimate asymptotic necessity at integer gamma=k. It is not a sharpness claim for 3e^4gamma. Replacing unnormalized S by S/n would admit permutation matrices and would not prove a fixed-degree polynomial result.

These deductions establish the theorem analytically. They do not establish novelty or historical priority.
