## Exact theorem and analytic attacks

For every positive integer n and nonnegative row-stochastic square matrix A, with unnormalized S=sum a_ij², the theorem is

    per(A) <= exp(-n + e^4 sum a_ij² log(e/a_ij))
           <= exp(-n + e^4 S(1+log n)).

The zero summands are continuously extended. Permanent zero is included. The exact Barvinok–Samorodnitsky question in OWR 44/2008, Problem 9, printed pp.2549–2550/PDF pp.73–74, fixes gamma>=1, assumes doubly stochastic A and S<=gamma, and asks for polynomial-factor e^-n scale. The all-n upper consequence retains exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma). Only n>=2 receives the prefactor-free n^(3e^4 gamma) form; at n=1 it would be false. The prior van der Waerden lower bound supplies the doubly stochastic lower direction. The paper does not substitute the stronger entrywise gamma/n condition, normalize S by n, allow growing gamma while asserting a fixed polynomial degree, or claim a lower bound for arbitrary row-stochastic A.

The independent derivation checks every entropy step. An arbitrary permutation law is exposed in independent priority order. At each positive-probability history, its conditional support lies within unused positive marginal entries, so the restricted marginal normalizer is positive. Relative entropy gives the local comparison and the entropy chain rule gives H(X)+sum b log b<=sum E log Z. The proof first averages this conditional-law inequality; it does not improperly replace that conditional law by conditioning on the full permutation. Only its scalar right side is subsequently reconditioned.

Given a supported full permutation and T_i=t, bijectivity and independent priorities give the mean suffix mass b+(1-t)(1-b). Jensen has the correct upper direction. Direct integration gives -1-b log b/(1-b); averaging the selected value supplies the second factor b in q(b)=-b² log b/(1-b). Zero marginal entries are never sampled; q(0)=0 and q(1)=1 are the correct continuous endpoints. Finite supported laws bound sampled positive marginals away from zero. Deterministic permutations give equality and test the unit endpoint. No conditioning on impossible greedy histories is required.

Positive permanent permits the Gibbs law; its assignment marginals B are doubly stochastic but generally differ from A. The exact entropy expansion gives log per(A)=H(mu)+sum b log a. Positive b implies positive a. Matrix KL equals the sum of nonnegative scalar d(b,a)=b log(b/a)-b+a only because both matrices have total entry mass n. Input-positive/marginal-zero entries retain d(0,a)=a; deleting them would break the cancellation. The a=b=0 convention is valid, and a=0<b cannot arise. Column sums of A are never used for the upper bound.

For scalar absorption, b log(1/b)<=1-b proves q<=b and q<=h(b), h(b)=b²log(e/b). If b>=e²a, then d>=b+a>=q. Otherwise discard d>=0. Monotonicity of h is used only between points in [0,1] when b<=a. When a<b<e²a, compare square factors and logarithms separately, obtaining h(b)<=e⁴h(a) without evaluating a monotonicity bound beyond one. This covers zero/unit endpoints and the switching line. Summing proves the entrywise bound.

Row Cauchy–Schwarz gives S>=1. The probability law p=a²/S has at most n² atoms and exactly sum a²log(1/a)=(S/2)(H(p)-log S)<=S log n. This proves the Frobenius conversion and all-n prefactor. For n>=2, 1+log n<=3log n gives the pure-power form. Equal fixed k uniform blocks have S=k and permanent asymptotic to (2pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)e^-n. Thus some polynomial loss is necessary, but the displayed exponent is not sharp. No mathematical gap or unsupported transfer of the central difficulty was found.
