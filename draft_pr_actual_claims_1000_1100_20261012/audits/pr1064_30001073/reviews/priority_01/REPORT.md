## Exact original question and accepted scope

Fresh full retrieval of the [official EMS PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46191), SHA-256 `3fcc907de0d7003b3d2cdfe3e7151e18309e926a4b6471e818e70f1ab085af35`, confirms problem 9 on printed pp. 2549-2550, PDF pp. 73-74. Those complete pages were rendered and visually inspected. For every fixed gamma>=1, the question concerns nonnegative doubly stochastic matrices with **unnormalized** S=sum a_ij^2<=gamma and polynomial-factor exp(-n) permanent order. The lower van der Waerden estimate is already known. The source explicitly calls a_ij<=gamma/n a stronger assumption. The [publisher metadata](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/2090) identifies the 2008 report volume, submission 21 September 2008 and publication 30 September 2009; these dates must not be conflated.

The submission proves for every nonnegative row-stochastic matrix of positive integer order

    per(A)<=exp(-n+e^4 sum a_ij^2 log(e/a_ij))
           <=exp(-n+e^4 S(1+log n)).

For S<=gamma, the all-n prefactor is exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma); a prefactor-free n^(3e^4 gamma) estimate is asserted only for n>=2. The n=1 restriction is necessary. No row-stochastic lower bound is claimed. Equal uniform blocks have S=k and permanent asymptotic to (2pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)exp(-n), requiring a polynomial exponent at least k/2 when gamma=k. This is a non-sharpness obstruction, not an optimality theorem.

Normalization and regime checks matter. S/n<=gamma would include permutation matrices for fixed gamma>=1 and destroy the desired exponential upper order. For growing gamma_n, the submitted bound gives exp(-n+O(gamma_n log n)), which generally is not a polynomial factor of fixed degree. At gamma=1, row Cauchy-Schwarz forces A=J/n. The original norm condition does not imply max entries O(1/n): with delta^2=(gamma-1)/(n-1), A_delta=(1-delta)J/n+delta I has S=gamma but diagonal entries of order n^(-1/2) for fixed gamma>1.
