## Exact result and deductive attack

For every positive integer n and nonnegative row-stochastic A, including zeros and permanent zero, with unnormalized S=sum a_ij², the paper proves

    per(A) <= exp(-n+e^4 sum a_ij² log(e/a_ij))
           <= exp(-n+e^4 S(1+log n)).

For S<=gamma the all-n bound is exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma)e^-n. The pure-power n^(3e^4 gamma)e^-n conclusion is only for n>=2; at n=1 it would be false. The precise OWR44/2008 Problem9 pp2549–2550 condition is fixed gamma>=1, doubly stochastic A, unnormalized S<=gamma. Its already-known van der Waerden lower bound supplies the lower direction. No lower bound is asserted for arbitrary row-stochastic inputs, and the stronger entrywise gamma/n hypothesis is not substituted.

The direct analytic reconstruction in INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION.md checks every central step. An arbitrary permutation law, rather than independent row samples, is exposed in independent priority order. Every conditional law is restricted to a positive-probability history, with support within unused positive marginal mass; this guarantees its reference normalizer is positive. Local KL yields the chain comparison against B, not A. Given full supported X and T_i=t, bijectivity gives expected suffix mass b_iX_i+(1-t)(1-b_iX_i). Finite positive sampled support bounds the logarithms. Jensen and the direct integral yield -1-b log b/(1-b); averaging over X_i supplies the second b in q. q(0)=0 and q(1)=1 have the correct limits, and deterministic laws give entropy equality.

The permanent-zero case is disposed of before Gibbs. Positive permanent gives doubly stochastic marginals B, with positive b implying positive a; B need not equal A. Expanding entropy gives the exact Gibbs identity. Matrix KL equals the sum of scalar d only because A and B have equal total mass. The term d(0,a)=a is retained over all entries; deleting it destroys cancellation. Column sums of A never enter the upper proof.

For the scalar estimate q-d<=e^4 h(a), q<=b and q<=h(b) follow from b log(1/b)<=1-b. At b>=e²a, d>=b+a>=q. Otherwise discard nonnegative d, use monotonicity only between points in [0,1] if b<=a, or separately bound b²<e⁴a² and log(e/b)<=log(e/a) if a<b. All zero/unit/switch-line cases are covered; h(e²a) is never illegally used outside its domain. Row Cauchy–Schwarz gives S>=1, and p=a²/S has entropy<=2log n. The exact square-entropy identity therefore proves the norm reduction. Fixed k uniform blocks have S=k and permanent asymptotic to (2pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)e^-n, requiring polynomial loss without making the displayed exponent sharp.
