# Independent core derivation, before prior reports

This derivation was written after reading the candidate TeX but before any earlier mathematical/source review reports. It verifies the proof rather than inventing an independent historical mechanism. The parent task already identified the relevant approach and sources, so this is not blind rediscovery.

Let X have an arbitrary law on permutations, with marginal matrix B. For a fixed independent exposure order and each positive-probability history, write U for the unused columns and c for the conditional law. Its support lies within U intersect {j:b_ij>0}. Z=sum_U b_ij is positive, and relative entropy to r_j=b_ij/Z gives H(c)+sum c_j log b_ij <= log Z. Summing the entropy chain rule and averaging independent orders gives H(X)+sum b_ij log b_ij <= sum_i E log Z_i.

Represent the random order by independent uniform priorities. Conditional on the sampled full X and T_i=t, the surviving mass has expectation b_iX_i+(1-t)(1-b_iX_i), because X is bijective and each other row survives with probability 1-t. Jensen can be conditioned on this pair. All sampled b_iX_i are positive; finite support yields a positive minimum for this fixed distribution, so log Z is bounded and the conditioning/integration is valid. Direct integration with u=b+(1-t)(1-b) gives integral log u dt = -1-b log b/(1-b), with limit zero at b=1. Averaging over X_i introduces the second factor b, hence q(b)=-b^2 log b/(1-b) and H(X)<=-sum b log b-n+sum q(b). The endpoint q(0)=0 is continuous and unsampled; q(1)=1 recovers deterministic equality.

For positive permanent, Gibbs law mu(sigma)=prod_i a_i,sigma(i)/per(A) has B doubly stochastic, even when A is only row stochastic. Positive b implies positive a. Expanding log mu gives log per(A)=H(mu)+sum b log a. The entropy inequality gives -n+sum q-D_mat, where D_mat=sum b log(b/a). Since A and B both have total mass n, D_mat=sum d(b,a), d=b log(b/a)-b+a. At b=0<a the term is a; deleting such terms breaks the equality. A=0<b is excluded by Gibbs support. If per(A)=0 the positive exponential upper bound is immediate.

For scalar b in (0,1), b log(1/b)<=1-b gives q<=b and q<=h(b)=b^2(1+log(1/b)). Continuity extends those to 0 and 1. d>=0 follows from x log x-x+1>=0. If b>=e^2 a, d>=b+a>=q. Otherwise discard d; for b<=a, h is increasing on [0,1] because h'(b)=b(1+2 log(1/b))>0. For a<b<e^2 a, b^2<e^4 a^2 and 1+log(1/b)<=1+log(1/a), giving h(b)<=e^4 h(a). This never applies h to a point outside [0,1]. At (a,b)=(0,0) both sides vanish; at b=0<a the left side is -a. The b=1 endpoint and exact threshold are covered. Summation proves per(A)<=exp(-n+e^4 sum h(a)).

Row Cauchy-Schwarz gives sum_j a_ij^2>=1/n, so S>=1. For p_ij=a_ij^2/S on at most n^2 sites, H(p)<=2 log n and sum a^2 log(1/a)=S/2[H(p)-log S]<=S log n. Thus the exponent is -n+e^4 S(1+log n). If S<=gamma, exp(e^4 gamma)n^(e^4 gamma)e^-n works for every n>=1. For n>=2, 1+log n<=3 log n gives n^(3e^4 gamma)e^-n. At n=1 the latter pure-power statement is false because per([1])=1>e^-1. No row-stochastic lower bound follows; repeated-column row stochastic matrices have permanent zero.

For k fixed uniform m-by-m diagonal blocks, n=km, S=k, and per=(m!/m^m)^k~(2pi/k)^(k/2)n^(k/2)e^-n. Thus at integer gamma=k an asymptotic polynomial exponent below k/2 fails. This proves nontrivial polynomial loss, without sharpness of the displayed e^4 bound. An S/n hypothesis permits identity matrices, so it is not the source question.

Verdict on core: all stated deductions pass the direct analytic check. Quantitative computations still needed as fault-oriented corroboration; they do not establish universal correctness. Historical credit and kit assertions remain separate checks.
