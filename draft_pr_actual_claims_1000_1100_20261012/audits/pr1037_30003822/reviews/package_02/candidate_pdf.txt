                                               Abstract
         For a generic n × n matrix A, the Teuﬂ–Wagner forest transfer matrix T is indexed by
     all set partitions. Each transition uses one ﬁxed unweighted tree per input block and counts
     each admissible interlayer edge subset once. We give a self-contained exposition of the divisor
     (det A)Cn | det T in Z[aij ] for every n ≥ 1, where Cn is the Catalan number. The proof applies
     established paired Grassmann forest algebra, constructs its constant Cn -dimensional invariant
     image, and computes the induced transfer determinant through ordered degree-triangular Gaus-
     sian factors. Alper Ferudun’s presently public Theorem 1.2 states the exact same conclusion
     (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23298482). The candidate repository PR is dated 9 October 2026, and
     that DOI was registered 11 October; historical public visibility, independent discovery and in-
     tellectual or worldwide priority are unestablished. This is a credited proof exposition, with no
     claim of a new theorem or method, exact multiplicity or complete factorization.

Introduction and comparison
Elmar Teuﬂ and Stephan Wagner posed the Catalan determinant-factor question in [1, Problem 1,
pp. 1457–1458]. The state space is the full Bell set of partitions, even though the useful quotient
has Catalan dimension. The present exposition explains the exact coeﬃcient-extraction convention
and the constant-kernel argument needed to pass from that quotient to an integral polynomial
divisor of the full matrix.
   The forest Grassmann representation belongs to Caracciolo, Jacobsen, Saleur, Sokal and
Sportiello [3]; forest multiplication, exponential expansion and partial elimination are developed by
Caracciolo, Sokal and Sportiello [4]. Sportiello’s Chapter 10 [5] proves the Catalan basis statement
that [4] announces with a deferred proof. Kenyon and Wilson [6] give the equivalent partition
projection and tree pairing. These inherited ingredients retain their attribution when they are
rederived below.
    Ferudun [2, Theorem 1.2(a)–(c)] gives the same all-n integral divisor for exactly this full-Bell
normalization, rather than a planar specialization or a merely related grove identity. Its Sec-
tions 2–5 use forest intertwining, a constant Catalan image, an inverse Gaussian identity and

3.1   A weight-space calculation

Consider a tensor power of the standard complex representation, or an exterior power of a direct
sum of copies of it. With the natural Hermitian inner product, let E, F, H be the usual inﬁnitesimal
operators, normalized by
                                      [E, F ] = H,    E ∗ = F,
with weights +1 and −1 on the two standard basis vectors. The identities extend to tensor and
exterior powers by the induced derivation actions. Write Mk for the weight-k subspace of any such
module.
   Lemma 3. Its invariant dimension is

                                    dim M SL2 = dim M0 − dim M2 .                                  (9)

   Proof. If v ∈ M0 and Ev = 0, then

                             kF vk2 = hv, EF vi = hv, (F E + H)vi = 0.

Thus v is killed by E, F, H. Being killed by E and F is equivalent to being ﬁxed by their expo-
nentials, the upper and lower unipotent one-parameter subgroups; those subgroups generate SL2 .
Conversely, an invariant vector has weight zero and is killed by E. Hence the invariant subspace is
ker(E : M0 → M2 ).
   This map is surjective. A vector w ∈ M2 orthogonal to its image has F w = 0, and hence

                       2kwk2 = hw, [E, F ]wi = kF wk2 − kEwk2 = −kEwk2 .

It follows that w = 0. Rank-nullity proves (9). The operators deﬁning the kernels have rational
matrices, so the dimensions are unchanged over Q. □

3.4   The determinant character

Each g ∈ GL(U ) acts by substitution on both copies of U , commuting with SL2 , and hence acts on
W . Denote the action by ρ(g).
    Lemma 5. For m > 0,
                                                    det ρ(g) = (det g)d .                                                  (17)
                                                                                                                       ∏
   Proof. On g = diag(t1 , . . . , tm ), the determinant of the action on W is a monomial i tαi i .
Indeed, the exterior algebra has a monomial weight basis, and the invariant subspace is stable
under the diagonal torus and is the direct sum of its weight spaces. Conjugation by permutation
matrices shows that all exponents are equal, say to α.
    The scalar tI acts on W2r as t2r , so (16) makes its determinant tmd . The diagonal formula
gives tmα . Thus α = d, since m > 0. Equation (17) holds on diagonal matrices and hence on
diagonalizable matrices. Over C, invertible matrices with distinct eigenvalues are Zariski dense.
Both sides are polynomial functions of the entries of g, so the identity holds on all of GL(U ), over
Q and every characteristic-zero extension. □

   Kenyon and Wilson [6], Section 1.2, Theorem 1.2 and Sections 2.1–2.3, Theorem 2.6, give the
partition projection, tree pairing and integral reduction from arbitrary partitions to a noncrossing
quotient. The associated meander nonsingularity is due to Di Francesco, Golinelli and Guitter [7],
rather than to the present exposition. The proof above uses its own invariant argument and does
not invoke that nonsingularity. Noncrossing matching tensors in Lemma 4 are a basis device within
the invariant calculation; they do not restrict the partitions or forests in (1).
