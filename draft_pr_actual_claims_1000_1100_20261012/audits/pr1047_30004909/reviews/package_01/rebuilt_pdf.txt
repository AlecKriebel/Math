1    The precise formulation
For a ﬁnite set V , let F(V ) consist of maps f : 2V → R≥0 such that f (∅) = 0, f ({i}) = 1 for all i ∈ V ,
f is monotone, and
                          f (A) + f (C) ≥ f (A ∪ C) + f (A ∩ C)   (A, C ⊆ V ).
An ordinary matroid M = (V, I) has rank rM (S) = max{|I| : I ⊆ S, I ∈ I}. The uniform interpretation
of the question is the assertion

            ∃α > 0 ∀ ﬁnite V ∀f ∈ F(V ) ∃M on V ∀S ⊆ V :                   ≤ f (S) ≤ αrM (S).              (1)

The approximant in (1) is an ordinary unscaled rank on the original V . No additional restriction on
the input function is imposed.
    Végh’s complete contribution [1], printed p. 2946, displays the denominator α on the left and the
same-V rank class. Its word “constant” does not separately quantify over V ; (1) makes the conventional
dimension-independent interpretation explicit. The sentence naming the rank uses f while the display
uses r; we use r consistently. This note addresses that displayed interpretation, without asserting an
unprinted alternative intention or current worldwide open status.
    For ﬁxed |V | = n ≥ 1, the hypotheses imply 1 ≤ f (S) ≤ |S| for nonempty S: monotonicity gives the
lower bound, and diminishing returns bounds each marginal by its singleton value. Thus the free rank
r(S) = |S| with α = n works for every input in ﬁxed dimension. This supports the uniform reading,
but does not add a printed source quantiﬁer. For V = ∅, both functions are zero and every positive
factor works.

    Goemans–Harvey–Iwata–Mirrokni [4,     5] study approximation with a polynomial query budget. Their
                                         √∑
actual structural output is root-linear,     i∈S ai with nonnegative coordinates, which includes square-
root cardinality. Their oracle lower bound uses matroid ranks as inputs. For any such input f =
rM , choosing that same rank gives an exact existential approximant; query indistinguishability alone
therefore cannot imply the obstruction proved here. Their approximant is not required to be an ordinary
matroid rank. This distinction acknowledges both their structural output and the mechanism of their
lower bound.
    An expanded copied-element representation is also a diﬀerent domain. Schrijver [6, Section 44.6b]
presents the integer-polymatroid block construction and credits Helgason. For this rational family, take
m − 1 disjoint rank-one uniform layers and one free layer, with m copies of each original element in total.
The rank of all copies of a subset S is (m − 1) min{|S|, 1} + |S| = mgm (S). Dividing by m represents
gm exactly on unions of copy blocks. That expanded matroid is not one ordinary rank on the original
V . Helgason’s original body was not independently read in the source review.
    The bounded primary-body review dated 13 October 2026 does not determine historical priority of
the exact one-rank argument or of the generic concave-cardinality one-rank envelope. No new-function,
new-decomposition, ﬁrstness, independent-discovery, intellectual-priority, or exhaustive literature claim
is made. The original Lovász and Helgason bodies, an unpublished GHIM citation, and some further
citation-chain consequences remain unveriﬁed. These source limits are not premises of the elementary
proofs.
