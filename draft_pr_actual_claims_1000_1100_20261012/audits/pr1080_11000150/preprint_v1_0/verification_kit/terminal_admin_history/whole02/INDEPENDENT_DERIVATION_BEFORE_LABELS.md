## Exact claim and scope

For each fixed integer g >= 1, on the oriented compact connected genus-g surface with one boundary component, mapping classes fix that boundary pointwise and isotopies do so as well. The target is ONE positive boundary twist. Factor curves are unoriented, simple, essential in the non-null-homotopic sense, possibly peripheral; each PAIR of distinct classes has geometric intersection <= 1. Ordered words can repeat letters. The asserted conclusion is finite simultaneous-conjugacy classes, hence a finite attained length maximum, with the even-chain lower bound 4g(2g+1), and credited exact values 12 and 40 in genera one and two. This resolves only the independently printed arbitrary-length clause. Computing the higher-genus maximum and classifications is not accomplished.

## Independent proof chain

1. Right-veering proper endpoint-relative arc actions form a monoid: if a <= h(a) and all arcs b <= f(b), equivariance gives a <= h(a) <= f(h(a)). A positive essential twist is nontrivial and right-veering, including the separating/peripheral cases. Faithfulness of the full proper-arc action follows by successively fixing a cutting arc system and using the disk isotopy. A nontrivial such twist has a strictly moved arc, so its inverse is not right-veering. Thus a nonempty all-positive essential product cannot be the identity. This requires the surface to have boundary; an essential positive identity on a closed surface would defeat this step. No null-homotopic twist may be included.

2. In ANY group with a conjugation-invariant set P admitting no positive identity, fix a finite alphabet in P. If u=s1...sm is retained as a scattered subword of v=b0 s1 b1 ... sm bm, then cancellation, in precisely this multiplication order, gives v u^-1 = product_j (Uj bj Uj^-1), where Uj=s1...sj and U0=1. Expand each block into its letters; a proper insertion produces a nonempty positive product of conjugates. Consequently distinct equal-valued words cannot be comparable by subword. Higman's finite-alphabet lemma then forbids an infinite evaluation fiber. Its minimal-bad-sequence proof shortens a subsequence with a common LAST letter; all three possible locations of a new good pair contradict original badness. It also covers an empty alphabet and an identity target. Neither commutativity nor centrality of the target nor normality of the chosen finitely generated submonoid is needed.

3. A separate prior-corollary route uses the ambient normal cone Q=<P>. The no-positive-identity assumption gives Q intersect Q^-1={1}. The relation x<=y iff yx^-1 in Q is a two-sided invariant PARTIAL order. The finitely generated alphabet submonoid H is strongly positive with no order-units except 1. Every divisor of a target has an expression using only generators dividing that target; the germ is generated by the finite subset of such generators. The claimed CT Corollary 4.13 interface must be checked directly. If it gives FF-atomicity here, it implies bounded atomic lengths: its factorization equivalence preserves length, even though it may permute letters. Fix one NONEMPTY atomic expansion per alphabet value. Replacing a raw word by these expansions bounds its raw length by a bound on atomic lengths. This avoids the false premise that the alphabet itself consists of atoms and avoids identifying CT equivalence with equality of ordered words. The self-contained Higman route independently supports the theorem if the bibliographic interface needs correction.

4. A finite support is bounded independently of word repetitions. Remove the one possible peripheral class. Map curves to the 2^(2g) classes in H1 over F2. Two with the same class have mod-two intersection zero because the form is alternating; since their minimal intersection is <=1 it is zero. A fiber can be simultaneously realized disjointly, is nonisotopic/nonperipheral, and has size <=3g-2 by a pants decomposition. Thus distinct support size <=2^(2g)(3g-2)+1. This is not a length bound. Zero-homology/separating classes cause no exception.

5. Finite bounded-size supports have finitely many boundary-fixing homeomorphism orbits. Use simultaneous simple geodesics in a fixed hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary. Separate finite higher concurrences by a generic perturbation of branches with the same alternating endpoint pattern. The pair count is unchanged. Record the union as an oriented ribbon graph, including opposite half-edge pairings at crossings, labeled constituent cycles, and one artificial bivalent vertex on isolated circles. Crossing and edge counts are bounded. For its potentially disconnected regular neighborhood record complementary boundary partitions, genera, and the component meeting the original boundary. There are finitely many records. Surface classification extends matching ribbon maps over complements. Collar extensions match actual circle boundary maps, including in annuli, so gluing twist integers do not give invariants of ambient-homeomorphism orbits. Adjust along the outer boundary, away from all curves, to fix it pointwise. An intersection graph alone would miss this information.

6. Normalize supports by one of these finitely many boundary-fixing maps. The product remains the same boundary twist because that twist is central. For each representative support the finite-alphabet result bounds all ordered words with this target. A finite union proves finite simultaneous-conjugacy classes. The 2g-curve even chain repeated 4g+2 times gives an admissible word of length 4g(2g+1), so the finite set of lengths is nonempty and attains its maximum.

7. Peripheral factors are central. If there are m>=1 of them, t_delta^m W=t_delta implies t_delta^(m-1)W=1; positivity forces m=1 and W empty. For a nonperipheral separating factor c, every factor has even intersection with c, so the <=1 condition forces disjointness. After capping the single boundary all factor curves remain essential: a newly bounding disk would give either a pre-existing disk or a pre-capping annulus. The capped positive identity gives a nontrivial relatively minimal positive Lefschetz fibration with the boundary lift supplying a (-1)-section. The section pairs once with the fiber, guaranteeing the nonzero fiber condition for the symplectic realization. Smith's common-invariant-essential-curve obstruction is the additional prior theorem to be checked. It rules out c. Relative minimality excludes fiber (-1)-spheres, not the (-1)-section.

8. In genus one, all remaining curves are nonseparating and the integer abelianization assigns them weight 1 and the boundary twist 12. In genus two the nonseparating-factor bound in BMVHM, after the preceding exclusion, gives 40. The chains attain these values under the pairwise restriction. These are prior deductions, not classifications. The invariant-single-curve argument, not a count of circle-action fixed points, is the relevant Smith mechanism; a claim of only countably many fixed points must not be used when a complementary subsurface can have a Cantor limit set.
