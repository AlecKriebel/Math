## Exact group lemma as a corollary of Cossu--Tringali

Let G be a group and P a conjugation-invariant subset of G for which no nonempty product of members equals 1. Let Q be the submonoid generated by P, including 1. Then Q is normal under conjugation. Also Q intersects its inverse only in {1}: if q and q^-1 belonged to Q with q != 1, their positive P-word expressions would concatenate to a forbidden nonempty identity.

Define an order on G by x <= y iff y x^-1 belongs to Q. Reflexivity and transitivity follow from the monoid property. Antisymmetry follows from Q intersect Q^-1 = {1}. Right multiplication preserves the order since (y v)(x v)^-1 = y x^-1. Left multiplication preserves it since (u y)(u x)^-1 = u (y x^-1) u^-1 and Q is normal. Strict inequality is preserved by left and right multiplication because group translations are injective. Thus this is a two-sided invariant partial order, not a claimed total ordering of a mapping class group.

Fix a finite alphabet A whose letters have values in P; values may repeat. Let H be the submonoid of G generated by these finitely many values, endowed with the restriction of this order. Every element of H is >= 1, and the only order-unit (element equivalent to 1) is 1. Therefore (H, <=) is a strongly positive monoid in [CT, Definition 2.3, PDF5]. H is finitely generated. By [CT, Definition 4.1, Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3, PDF13--15], it is weakly locally finitely generated up to order-units.

[CT, Corollary 4.13, PDF20--21] now says that this premonoid is FF-atomic. The relevant full proof invokes Theorem 4.12, Theorem 4.11 and Higman's lemma; those proof bodies were read. In particular it is atomic and BF-atomic: [CT, Definition 3.2 and Remark 3.3(2), PDF9--10] make the nonempty set of atomic factorization lengths of each nonunit finite. Order-atoms here are ordinary indecomposable nonunits, since the only order-unit is 1. No claim that the prescribed P-letters are atoms is needed.

If h is outside H, its A-word fiber is empty. If h=1, its fiber contains only the empty word, by the no-positive-identity hypothesis. Suppose h belongs to H and h != 1. For each value of an A-letter choose one nonempty atomic factorization in H. Replace each occurrence of a letter in an A-word by this chosen expansion. The resulting ordered atomic word has the same product h. If B(h) is the largest atomic factorization length of h, the expansion has length at most B(h), and the original A-word has no greater length because no chosen expansion is empty. Only finitely many words in the finite alphabet A have length at most B(h). Hence the evaluation fiber over h is finite.

CT's FF equivalence is defined by arbitrary injective matching of equivalent letters (Definition 3.1), and can identify permutations. It must not be silently read as equality of ordered words. The preceding argument uses the weaker BF consequence and finite alphabet, so it proves actual ordered-word finiteness without that mistake. Nor must H itself be normal or left duo: the order uses the ambient Q. Applying CT Theorem 5.8 to H by assuming left-duo closure would be unjustified.

This proves exactly the generic fixed-alphabet statement of submitted Lemma 3.1 as a corollary of prior factorization theory. It covers arbitrary h, including noncentral targets. The submitted deletion/Higman proof is a shorter direct proof of this same result. Its accuracy does not make the generic mechanism historically new.

## From prior surface components to the target application

On an oriented compact S(g,1), g>=1, with pointwise boundary fixing, let P be all positive twists about non-null-homotopic curves. Orientation and essentiality give conjugation invariance. HKM Lemma 2.5 (arXiv math/0510639v1, PDF4) supplies right-veering positivity for all homotopically nontrivial curves. The proper-arc faithfulness argument supplies no nonempty positive identity, as already credited by Wajnryb to Smith (printed124/PDF131). These are prior premises.

The support of an admissible word consists of distinct curve isotopy classes. Juvan--Malnic--Mohar, Systems of Curves on Surfaces, JCTB68 (1996), Theorem 3.3 (printed12--13/PDF6--7), gives a finite cardinality bound for a fixed compact bordered surface and fixed intersection threshold. Their theorem concerns actual representatives. Use a hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary to choose simultaneously minimal simple representatives; perturb multiple crossings locally and put the optional peripheral curve in a collar. Homotopy equals isotopy for the nontrivial simple curve classes here, so their general-position convention matches the distinct support. Repeated occurrences are discarded only in the support, never in the word.

For the particular threshold 1, the submitted coarse bound 2^(2g)(3g-2)+1 is a direct mod-two homology/disjointness argument. Malestein--Rivin--Theran, Topological Designs, arXiv1008.3710v5 (2013), Proposition 4.1 and its proof (PDF8), already use this mechanism and prove sharper bounds in their closed-surface setting; Theorem 1.2 and Section6 address boundary surfaces. The current coarse constant is not claimed identical to their sharper constant.

To pass from cardinality to support orbits, a bounded marked union has at most N(N-1)/2 transverse double crossings, and adding a bivalent vertex on every isolated circle gives a bounded finite ribbon graph. Keep cyclic orders, the opposite pairing at crossings, and marked curve cycles. The oriented regular neighborhood is determined by that graph. Its embedding is specified up to homeomorphism by the partition of its boundary circles among complementary components and their genera, together with which component carries the original boundary. Each datum has only finitely many possibilities on the fixed surface.

This is the established graph/complement mechanism of Cahn--Fanoni--Petri, Mapping class group orbits of curves with self-intersections, arXiv1603.00846v2 (2016), Lemma10 and proof (PDF8), and Section3.2 (PDF9--11). Their literal lemma is stated for a single curve on a punctured surface, so it is not cited as if it already states the exact disconnected, marked, bordered 1-system theorem. The needed adaptation is explicit: allow disconnected ribbon neighborhoods; label all constituent simple cycles and isolated circles; record the sole original boundary. The same surface-classification gluing proof extends prescribed orientation-compatible circle maps by collar adjustments. A complementary annulus does not add an integer invariant of the ambient-homeomorphism orbit. A final original-boundary collar correction fixes that boundary pointwise while preserving curve classes. This supplies finite support orbits without any fixed-length assumption about the factorization.

Choose one representative support C_r in each of the finitely many orbits. An admissible ordered word can be simultaneously conjugated to a word in the finite alphabet of twists about C_r. Its product remains the same single boundary twist because that twist is central under pointwise boundary fixing. Apply the already verified CT corollary above separately in each alphabet. The finite union of its finite fibers contains one representative of every ordered simultaneous-conjugacy class. The even-chain relation, already displayed by Wajnryb on printed125/PDF132, makes the set nonempty and gives 4g(2g+1)<=M(g)<infinity.

## Falsifiable interfaces and finite controls

The chain is invalid if Q is not normal, if Q has nontrivial units, if identity-valued letters are allowed, if atomic expansions can erase letters, if finite support cardinality is mistaken for length, if the original boundary is capped before applying positivity, if the target is moved by support normalization, or if CT's scrambled equivalence is mistaken for ordered equality. check_prior_corollary.py tests exact finite examples at those interfaces and rehashes all frozen inputs. It is a fault-control program, not a universal proof of CT, HKM, or surface classification.

One negative control is an all-length counterexample to omitting normality. In GL_2(Q(i)), put X=diag(2i,2), Y=[[1,1],[0,1]]. Any positive word containing a>0 copies of X has determinant of absolute value 4^a>1; a word containing only Y is a nontrivial positive power of a unipotent. Thus no nonempty positive X,Y word is the identity. Yet Y^m X^2 Y^m=X^2 for every m>=0. The alphabet {X,Y} has an infinite fiber. Normality fails because X^2 conjugates Y to Y^-1. This prevents interpreting ordinary finite generation, cancellation, or reducedness as sufficient alone.

In the positive control, the integer Heisenberg group has a normal lexicographic positive cone, and the three letters A=(1,0,0), B=(0,1,0), C=AB=(1,1,1) have fixed nonempty expansions A,B,AB. The substitution is not injective: C and AB expand to the same atomic word. Nevertheless every original length is at most its expanded length, and the expanded length for h=(a,b,c) is a+b. The target (2,2,1) is noncentral and its fiber is finite, explicitly showing that centrality is not part of the generic lemma. The central boundary condition is used only in the separate support-normalization step.
