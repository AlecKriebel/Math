## Exact claim and source boundary

Fix an integer g >= 1. S is compact, connected, oriented, genus g, with one boundary circle delta. The mapping class group consists of orientation-preserving maps fixing the boundary pointwise, with isotopy relative to that boundary. Consider ordered positive twist factorizations of t_delta along non-null-homotopic simple closed curves, with every pair of curve isotopy classes having geometric intersection at most one. Repeated factors are permitted. A peripheral curve is permitted, but the argument below will force it to occur only in the singleton factorization.

The claim to verify is: there are finitely many such ordered factorizations modulo simultaneous conjugation by this group. Their lengths therefore have a finite attained maximum M(g), and 4g(2g+1) <= M(g). The original restricted Smith question asks separately for the maximum and whether lengths can be arbitrarily large. The finite-maximum assertion settles the latter negatively; it does not give the exact maximum for every g. The nearby Artin/A5 positive-word and Hurwitz classification questions are additional unresolved targets. The original wording describes actual 0/1 intersections. A theorem under the geometric-intersection condition applies to that original subset. The original discussion explicitly allows repetitions. No null-homotopic identity twist may be silently inserted.

## Positivity obstruction, with boundary and faithfulness

For isotopy classes relative to endpoints of oriented properly embedded arcs beginning at x on the boundary, lift the arcs to the universal cover from the same lift of x. The right-side relation compares their lifted terminal points (or, after efficient positioning, their initial tangent order). Write a <= b to mean that b is weakly right of a; this reverses HKM's displayed inequality convention but not its geometric meaning. Distinct arc classes with the same prescribed endpoints are strictly ordered in one direction. A boundary-fixing orientation-preserving map preserves the order.

Right-veering means a <= f(a) for every such arc. If f and h are right-veering then a <= h(a) <= f(h(a)), so their composition is right-veering. The same endpoint set is preserved because the maps fix the boundary pointwise. HKM Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.5 supply this definition and the inclusion of every homotopically nontrivial positive Dehn twist. Their lemma's proof starts with the annulus, extends through 1-handles, and handles twists separating off a component without outer boundary using a positive nonseparating factorization. Thus this input is not restricted to nonseparating twists.

The action on all proper arc classes is faithful. Choose finitely many mutually disjoint arcs cutting S into a disk, with distinct endpoints. If a map fixes all their relative-endpoint classes, isotope it relative to the boundary to preserve them, then to fix them pointwise. On the cut disk, the remaining map fixes the entire boundary and is isotopic to the identity. This standard disk argument detects separating and peripheral twists too. A twist along any non-null-homotopic curve is nontrivial: a crossing arc records the annular winding of the twist; if the curve separates off a region away from the boundary, choose an arc that enters and returns from that region. The disk and annulus are the elementary exceptional surfaces. No nontrivial twist is present on the disk; the annular group is Z.

A nontrivial right-veering map must strictly move at least one arc, by faithfulness. If a < t_c(a), then t_c^{-1} sends the arc t_c(a) strictly to its left. Hence t_c^{-1} is not right-veering. If a nonempty product t_1 ... t_n equaled the identity, t_1^{-1}=t_2 ... t_n would be right-veering, a contradiction. For n=1 the right-hand side is the identity, still right-veering. Therefore no nonempty product of positive twists along non-null-homotopic curves equals the identity. Boundary fixing is indispensable: after capping, positive relations can be identities.

## Exact noncommutative insertion calculation

Let G be any group and P a conjugation-invariant subset with no nonempty product equal to 1. Give each letter of a finite alphabet a value in P. For a word u=s_1 ... s_m embedded as a subsequence of v, split v as b_0 s_1 b_1 ... s_m b_m. Put P_0=1 and P_j=s_1 ... s_j, using the retained-letter prefixes of u, not prefixes of v. Then

    v u^{-1} = product_{j=0}^{m} P_j b_j P_j^{-1}.

For example, the first three terms telescope to b_0 s_1 b_1 s_2 b_2 s_2^{-1}s_1^{-1}; induction gives the full expression. A conjugated block is a product of the conjugates of its letters. If v properly contains u, at least one inserted letter exists. The expression is consequently a nonempty product of elements of P and cannot be 1. Thus words evaluating to the same h are an antichain under the subsequence order. For m=0 the same calculation is simply v, so the empty-word boundary case is included. The element h need not be central. Neither commutativity, an Artin embedding, nor injectivity of a positive presentation is used.

## Finite-alphabet Higman proof without a numerical bound

Assume an infinite bad sequence of finite words exists, with no earlier word a subsequence of a later one. Build one successively so that each chosen word has least possible length among the words permitting an infinite bad continuation of its chosen prefix; resolve any ties arbitrarily. No chosen word is empty. Write w_i=v_i a_i. Finiteness of the alphabet gives indices i_0<i_1<... having the same terminal letter a. Replace the tail beginning at i_0 by v_{i_0},v_{i_1},.... If this new sequence were bad, the shorter choice at position i_0 would contradict minimality. It therefore has a good pair. A pair among unchanged initial words contradicts the original badness. A pair from an initial word into a v-tail contradicts original badness since v_i is a subsequence of w_i. A pair v_{i_j} <= v_{i_k} extends by the common last letter to w_{i_j} <= w_{i_k}, again contradicting original badness. These are all possibilities, including an empty v_i. The contradiction proves the finite-alphabet lemma.

If a fiber over h were infinite, enumerate distinct words in it. Higman gives a comparable pair. Equal-length comparable words over the same alphabet are identical, so distinctness makes the comparison proper. The insertion calculation contradicts their equal values. Each fiber is therefore finite, including the empty-alphabet case. This proof supplies no effective upper length or exhaustive-search stopping certificate. In fact no bound in alphabet size alone can hold for arbitrary h: in G=Z, P={1}, one letter has a unique word of every target length.

## Downstream interfaces needed for the surface theorem

Twist sign and non-null-homotopy are preserved by orientation-preserving conjugation, so P consisting of all admissible positive twists is normal as a subset. The preceding positivity obstruction applies. For each fixed finite curve support, only finitely many ordered positive words can equal any prescribed mapping class, in particular t_delta.

Support cardinality is bounded independently of repetitions. Remove the sole possible peripheral class. Partition remaining classes by mod-2 homology, of which there are 2^(2g). Equal classes have mod-2 intersection zero by alternation. Under geometric intersection <=1 their geometric intersection is zero. Each such fiber is pairwise disjoint and nonisotopic and extends to a pants decomposition, with at most 3g-2 members. Therefore support size is at most 2^(2g)(3g-2)+1. Zero homology and separating curves are included. This is not a factorization-length estimate.

There are finitely many support orbits: use simple geodesic representatives on a hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary and a separate peripheral collar. These simultaneously achieve minimal intersections. Split multiple crossings into ordinary double crossings within small disks, preserving the endpoint orders, with at most one crossing per pair. The resulting union has bounded vertices and edges; add one auxiliary vertex to each isolated circle. Label its constituent cycles, crossing pairings, and orientation cyclic orders. There are finitely many ribbon-graph records. Complementary components are compact oriented surfaces of bounded genus, with finitely many boundary circles labeled by the graph, and a single distinguished boundary delta. Their genus and attachment partition yield finitely many records. The oriented surface classification and collar adjustments extend any matching neighborhood map to the complementary surfaces. Circle gluing does not create an unbounded invariant under ambient homeomorphism: boundary maps extend by collars. An adjustment at delta gives pointwise boundary fixing. No graph edge winding survives as an invariant of this homeomorphism orbit.

Normalize an arbitrary support to one of these finitely many representatives by f. Conjugated twists are a word in that representative's finite alphabet. Here the target must survive normalization: boundary fixing gives f t_delta f^{-1}=t_delta. This centrality step is needed here, although the generic word-fiber theorem never required h central. The finite union of finite fibers contains a representative for every simultaneous-conjugacy orbit of factorizations. The 2g-chain relation has length 2g(4g+2)=4g(2g+1) and uses pairwise 0/1 intersections, so the finite set of lengths is nonempty and its maximum is attained.

A peripheral factor commutes with all others. If m>=1 peripheral factors occur, the relation reduces to t_delta^(m-1)W=1 with W positive; positivity forces m=1 and W empty.
