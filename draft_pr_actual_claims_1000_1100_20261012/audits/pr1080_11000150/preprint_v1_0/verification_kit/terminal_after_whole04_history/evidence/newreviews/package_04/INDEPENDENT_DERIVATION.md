# Independent derivation before historical review reasoning

This derivation was written after reading only the candidate TeX and the PDF skill, before opening any earlier review report or reasoning.

## Claim and assumptions
For fixed integer g >= 1, compact connected oriented S(g,1), maps and isotopies fix the sole boundary pointwise. An ordered word of positive twists about essential (non-null-homotopic) unoriented simple curve classes equals one positive boundary twist; each pair has geometric intersection <= 1. Peripheral classes and repeated occurrences are allowed. The goal is finite simultaneous-conjugacy classes and finite attained length maximum. The higher-genus exact maximum and classification are separate and not derived.

## Independent proof reconstruction
1. Positive identity obstruction: right-veering supplies a monoid of maps weakly moving every based proper arc rightward. Its order is preserved by boundary-fixing maps. An essential positive twist is right-veering and nontrivial on some proper arc. If its inverse were right-veering, applying the two inequalities to that moved arc contradicts antisymmetry. Therefore a nonempty positive product cannot be identity: isolate its first factor and express its inverse by the remaining right-veering factors. Arc-action faithfulness follows from a disk-cutting system with individually fixed endpoints and the Alexander disk isotopy. Peripheral and separating twists remain nontrivial, detected by crossing arcs. The external premise needed here is positivity of all essential twists, including separating twists.

2. Finite-alphabet fibers: an infinite fiber over a finite alphabet has comparable distinct words u<v under subsequence (finite-alphabet Higman). Express v=b0 s1 b1 ... sm bm when u=s1...sm. Direct telescoping gives v u^-1 = product over j=0..m of Uj bj Uj^-1 for retained prefixes Uj=s1...sj. For a proper insertion this is a nonempty product of conjugate positive twists, contradicting step 1 if values equal. This uses no central target and allows repeated-valued letters, empty u, and empty alphabet. Higman's minimum-bad-sequence proof: strip final letters on an infinite common-final-letter subsequence, replace first selected word by its shorter prefix, use minimality, and lift any resulting good pair back. No numerical bound or stopping algorithm follows from this argument alone. CT provides a second credited derivation subject to exact source-definition checks, but is unnecessary to logical completeness.

3. Bounded support: delete at most one peripheral class. Partition remaining distinct classes by mod-two homology (2^(2g) classes). Alternation forces even pairing for equal classes; geometric count <=1 forces disjointness. Each disjoint nonperipheral essential class collection has size <=3g-2, by pants decomposition. Thus support <=2^(2g)(3g-2)+1. At g=1, this coarse formula gives 5, deliberately not an asserted sharp bound. Separating classes lie in the zero fiber.

4. Finite support orbits: geodesic representatives realize simultaneous minimal pairwise intersections on a hyperbolic bordered surface. A generic local perturbation of a multiple intersection resolves all pair crossings without changing pairwise counts. The union is a ribbon graph of bounded size; isolated circles require auxiliary vertices, curve cycles need marks, and cyclic orders plus opposite pairing must be retained. The ribbon thickening and complementary-component boundary partition, genus, and distinguished outer boundary form finitely many records. Oriented classification extends an isomorphism of marked neighborhoods over matched complements. Boundary-map isotopies in collars remove gluing choices, including annuli. A final outer collar isotopy makes the map pointwise boundary fixed. Disconnected, non-filling, disk, and annular complements are admitted. No standalone intersection-graph or filling-system assertion is sufficient.

5. Normalize each support by simultaneous conjugation. The boundary twist is central for this mapping class convention, so each word lands in the same fixed target fiber over a representative finite support. A finite union of finite fibers covers all admissible simultaneous-conjugacy classes. The even-chain relation gives a word of length 2g(4g+2) and intersection pattern a chain, so maximum exists and is >=4g(2g+1).

6. Peripheral occurrences commute to the front; after canceling the target, remaining positive product equals identity unless there was exactly one peripheral and no other factors. Therefore the peripheral case is precisely the singleton.

7. Separating exclusion and low genus are logically additional to finiteness. A separating factor is disjoint from every factor by even geometric crossing parity. Capping the original boundary preserves essentiality of nonperipheral curves; a nonperipheral separating curve has positive genus on both capped sides. The capped relation yields a nontrivial relatively minimal positive Lefschetz fibration, and the one-boundary relation supplies a (-1)-section. Its fiber intersects the section once, so fiber homology is nonzero. Smith's invariant-essential-curve obstruction must apply under these premises. If it does, the separating curve gives a contradiction. For g=1 exclusion is elementary. Abelianization then gives length12 in genus1; the BMVHM nonseparating bound for genus2 with one boundary gives <=40; the four-chain gives equality40. These external interfaces require body verification; no gauge-theoretic reproof is claimed.

## Boundary checks and potential falsifiers
Null-homotopic factors would erase and destroy finiteness. Closed surfaces admit positive identity relations and invalidate step1. Fixed genus, positivity, sole boundary target and pointwise boundary convention are essential. Support cardinality alone does not bound occurrences. Right-veering closure alone does not prove strictness. CT atomic equivalence need not be ordered-word equality; a length bound and fixed nonerasing expansions are needed. Smith's fixed-point-count wording is not required; a false universal fixed-cardinality assertion would not be accepted. If source bodies fail the all-essential HKM positivity or Smith/BMVHM hypotheses, low-genus bridge claims fail even though the independent finiteness proof remains valid.
