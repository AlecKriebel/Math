# Independent derivation frozen before reading prior reviews

Exact hypothesis: fixed integer g >= 1, compact oriented surface S(g,1), boundary fixed pointwise, ordered positive Dehn twist factorization of its one positive boundary twist, essential non-nullhomotopic curves (peripheral allowed), repetitions allowed, geometric pairwise intersection <= 1. The intended conclusion is finitely many simultaneous-conjugacy classes of such ordered factorizations, hence a finite attained maximum M(g), with chain lower bound 4g(2g+1). This does not give an effective upper bound or exact higher-genus maximum/classification.

Approach derived independently from the supplied claim, without consulting earlier review reasoning:
1. Discard multiplicities only to enumerate the finite support. For each mod-2 homology class, two support curves have even mod-2 intersection, so their geometric intersection (0 or 1) is 0. Distinct isotopy classes in one homology fiber are therefore disjoint. An Euler-characteristic bound on disjoint nonparallel essential curves (including a possible boundary-parallel component) bounds each fiber; the coarse bound (3g-1) times 2^(2g) suffices. The peripheral class needs its separate collar representative.
2. Choose geodesics for nonperipheral curves in one hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary; minimal pairwise intersections are then attained simultaneously. Perturb multiple crossings in small disks while preserving every pairwise count. A bounded number of simple curves and crossings produces only finitely many abstract ribbon graphs, with isolated circular components retained separately. Their regular-neighborhood complements have bounded genus and boundary counts. Finite component and boundary-attachment data determine finitely many oriented ambient-homeomorphism types. This step must retain disconnected components, isolated circles, the distinguished original boundary, and complement gluing; a bare intersection graph would not suffice.
3. Fix one representative support and its finite alphabet of twists. If infinitely many distinct positive words represented the boundary twist, Higman's subsequence theorem yields two distinct words u properly embedded as a subsequence of v. Equality of their group values forces a nonempty product of conjugates of the inserted positive twists to equal the identity. Every such conjugate is a positive twist about an essential curve. Right-veering positivity must exclude identity for a nonempty such product; establishing this strict/nonidentity point, rather than merely closure of right-veering maps, is essential. A normal invariant positive cone, or an arc moved strictly right with monotonicity, supplies it. Thus only finitely many words per support.
4. Boundary centrality permits identifying simultaneous conjugation with ambient homeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise. Even if a chosen ambient map initially has a boundary rotation, a collar isotopy can correct that rotation without changing the curve classes. A quotient of finitely many representative words is finite.
5. The chain relation on 2g curves gives (t1 ... t(2g))^(4g+2) = t_boundary, length 4g(2g+1). The chain curves satisfy pairwise intersection <= 1. Since the set is nonempty and finite, a maximum is attained.
6. If a peripheral positive twist occurs, central cancellation leaves a positive word equal to identity unless the factorization is the singleton boundary twist. Thus any longer admissible word has no peripheral curves. The stronger conclusion that all remaining curves are nonseparating requires a genuine additional theorem (e.g. the exceptional-section/Lefschetz-fibration argument); it does not follow from the finite-word mechanism alone. The symplectic capping and (-1)-section convention must be checked directly, especially g=1,2.

Falsification targets: (a) a same-mod-2 fiber with geometric intersection one; (b) infinitely many relative twists in the complement missed by ribbon data; (c) a positive identity relation in the boundary-fixed group; (d) a failure of strictness when passing from right-veering generators to normal products; (e) peripheral cancellation or genus-one convention errors; (f) scope expansion from fixed-g finiteness to an effective formula, uniform genus bound, or full Hurwitz/classification answer. A finite computation only checks implementations and examples; it cannot certify these universal statements.
