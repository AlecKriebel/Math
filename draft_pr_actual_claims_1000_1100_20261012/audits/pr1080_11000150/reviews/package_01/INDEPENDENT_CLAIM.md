Fix g>=1, one pointwise fixed boundary, positive non-null-homotopic simple closed curves and ordered words equal to one positive boundary twist. Repetitions count; every pair has geometric intersection <=1. The theorem asserts finitely many simultaneous-conjugacy classes, hence finite attained maximum M(g)>=4g(2g+1), peripheral singleton only, no separating factors otherwise, M(1)=12 and M(2)=40. The exact maximum/classification bundle in genus >=3 remains partial. No historical novelty or current-openness theorem is asserted.

Independent deduction: (1) all essential positive twists lie in the right-veering monoid; any nontrivial such twist strictly moves an arc, so its inverse is not right-veering and there is no positive identity. (2) For a fixed finite alphabet, proper insertion changes the value because vu^-1 is a nonempty positive product conjugated by retained prefixes. Higman therefore gives finite fibers; the CT atomic-length route must independently satisfy strongly positive and weak local finiteness hypotheses. (3) Equal F2 homology classes are disjoint, bounding distinct support size by 2^(2g)(3g-2)+1. Hyperbolic representatives and marked ribbon-neighborhood/complement records give finitely many support orbits. (4) A support-normalizing map preserves the central boundary twist, so a finite union of finite fibers gives the full finiteness assertion. (5) Chain relation supplies the lower bound and nonempty length set. (6) Peripheral factors commute and cannot leave a positive identity. Any separating nonperipheral factor is disjoint from all factors and after capping becomes an invariant essential curve in a nontrivial relatively minimal positive Lefschetz fibration with a (-1) section, contradicting Smith's invariant-curve theorem. BMVHM's conventions then supply the low-genus values.
