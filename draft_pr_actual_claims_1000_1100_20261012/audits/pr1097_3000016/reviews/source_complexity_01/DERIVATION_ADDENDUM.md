# Source-language totality and period-removal addendum

The frozen pre-source derivation intentionally listed interface questions. Its malformed-string sentence is completed here rather than silently changing that historical file. There are two common source encodings. If NONHALTING means valid nonhalting instances only, reject invalid encodings and map them to the fixed NO reachability instance consisting of the directed two-cycle, X=(0,0), Y=(1,0). This target NO instance is nonnegative and strongly connected; mass conservation makes it unreachable. If NONHALTING literally means the set-theoretic complement of HALTING including invalid strings, instead map invalid encodings to the same two-cycle with X=Y=(0,0), a fixed YES reachability instance. Either convention yields a total polynomial map.

The valid-source language is coNP-hard, so neither convention weakens hardness. Given a reduction from an NP language to valid HALTING with malformed strings rejected, replace any invalid output by the fixed valid nonhalting two-cycle (1,0). Its HALTING answer is NO, preserving that reduction. Complementing gives a reduction from the complementary coNP language into valid nonhalting inputs. Parsing binary dimensions, integer signs and adjacency nonnegativity, and checking strong connectivity, all take polynomial time. The submitted report uses the usual valid-instance convention and is not claiming that invalid strings simultaneously belong and do not belong to a literal complement.

The source lattice construction can output the signed vector degree-minus-one-minus-sigma. The report explicitly supplies the necessary threshold-preserving signed-to-nonnegative normalization by loops; therefore hardness does not hinge on an unstated positivity of this intermediate vector. The auxiliary one-vertex comparisons and relay removal are exact before the main gate map.

Here is an independent proof of the period-removal theorem used in the certificate, authenticated in HKT Lemma 4. Let p be a nonnegative kernel period. Delete the first p_v occurrences of v from a legal word. At a retained firing of v all p_v deletions at v have happened, while deletions at u total r_u<=p_u. Thus (Lr)_v=-d_v p_v+sum_u a_uv r_u <= -d_v p_v+sum_u a_uv p_u=0. The retained execution has x+L(h-r) at that step rather than x+Lh; at its firing coordinate it has at least as many chips. Hence the retained word remains legal. When a word has firing count >=p, all prescribed deletions occur and leave its endpoint unchanged. Iterating proves legal realizability of the unique nonnegative reduced solution g whenever the target is reachable.

The first-exceedance count certificate in the frozen derivation is therefore complete. A certificate does not need a legal realization of its partial count u: legality of the hypothesized reduced full word would contradict the certificate at its first firing exceeding u. The reduced count g and period p have polynomial bit length, not necessarily polynomial numerical size. This follows from polynomial integer linear algebra for a solution h and cofactor or matrix-tree bounds for p, followed by g=h-min_v floor(h_v/p_v)*p. No exponential-length word is part of the certificate.
