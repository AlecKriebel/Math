## 1. Farrell–Levine supplies hardness, not the claimed reachability map

The inspected [Farrell–Levine v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04690v3), PDF2 and10–13, defines the nonnegative strongly connected adjacency-matrix HALTING problem, proves its NP membership, constructs a total Laplacian from a full-rank zero-sum lattice in polynomial bit time, and explicitly states its Karp hardness in Corollary3.2. Its introduction allows loops. This entire interface is prior work and must be credited.

Its conversion of a lattice question to stabilizability does not by itself convert the complementary question to a specified legal target. Net Laplacian equality only gives linear equivalence. For example, with adjacency `[[0,1],[2,0]]`, x=(0,1) is stable while y=(1,0) is distinct and hence unreachable. Nevertheless y−x is in the integer Laplacian image: firing-count (1,1) has net change (1,−1). Thus a lattice solution alone does not settle ordinary reachability.

The signed intermediate representative in the FL argument is handled by the submitted threshold-preserving chip-plus-loop offset, followed by relay removal. This is an explicit bridge; equality of the net Laplacian alone would not prove legality. The current work does not claim the prior lattice-hardness theorem, total-Laplacian realization or source NP membership as new. The older Amini–Manjunath v1 theorem was inspected as background to FL's citation; its printed polarity/notation is not silently substituted for the independently matched FL Corollary3.2 interface or certified afresh here.

## 2. Björner–Lovász and HKT already supply the membership mechanism

[Björner–Lovász's author manuscript](https://www.cs.elte.hu/~lovasz/morepapers/abacus.pdf), revised July1992, Lemma4.3 and §5, proves period deletion and uniqueness of a reduced firing score and gives a period-dependent reachability algorithm. The factors D² and per(G) can be exponentially large in the binary encoding; the polynomial logarithmic dependence on chip totals does not remove them. Their conjectural hardness remark is historical evidence of the question, not proof of its state today.

[Hujter–Kiss–Tóthmérész v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.03209v4), Theorem12, already proves coNP membership for the exact nonnegative, loopless, binary-adjacency model. A reduced nonnegative count f satisfying y=x+Lf and a proper partial count g≤f, stuck at every still-required coordinate, form the negative certificate. Period deletion ensures that a reachable target admits the reduced count. At the first firing exceeding g, nonnegative incoming entries give a contradiction with the stuck inequality. The partial count g need not itself have a legal word. These facts and their certificate interpretation belong to prior work. The new paper may explain them, with attribution, but cannot present them as a new membership proof.

HKT Theorem14 also supplies the prior implication `y recurrent and x linearly equivalent to y => x legally reaches y` on strongly connected digraphs. Its recurrence premise is material. The two-vertex example above shows that deleting it fails. The candidate does not give a uniform promise that every constructed target is recurrent regardless of the source answer; nor would such a promise be free to use. No earlier polynomial algorithm for promised recurrent targets thereby decides every constructed instance.

## 3. Tóthmérész's earlier barrier does not already print the requested Karp reduction

The full chip-firing proof in [Tóthmérész v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11970v2), PDF4–7, has two different interfaces.

First, Claim2.4 is a genuine deterministic recurrence-to-reachability conversion. If x has a legal move at v, it asks whether x+Le_v reaches x. Period deletion and the bounded-game abelian property show that this is equivalent to x being recurrent. A stable branch can be sent to a fixed unreachable pair. This useful prior many-one conversion should be described accurately; it is stronger than merely an informal relationship between the questions.

Second, Proposition2.8 treats NONHALTING by the *existence* of a recurrent representative y linearly equivalent to x. It supplies a certificate argument conditional on a polynomial recurrence decision algorithm. It does not deterministically compute such a y, nor specify one fixed reachability pair from x for which equivalence to NONHALTING is proved. That is the missing step in trying to derive the submitted classification directly from this prior proof. A nondeterministically guessed recurrent representative cannot be relabeled a deterministic Karp output.

The distinction is concrete. On the three-vertex bidirected path with adjacency `[[0,0,1],[0,0,1],[1,1,0]]`, x=(0,2,0) is nonhalting and is transient: five states are reachable, but no nonempty legal word returns to x. Its first move is forced at vertex1 and never reverses. Thus NONHALTING is not the same predicate as RECURRENT at the original configuration, even for a simple undirected graph. The fresh checker verifies this example and all first-move recurrence conversions in its stated finite universe.

The prior Theorem2.3 hierarchy-collapse consequence must therefore remain credited and distinguished from the submitted deterministic NONHALTING-to-REACHABLE map. This scope comparison does not prove that no other derivation or earlier publication exists.

## 4. A close prior method: bounded simulation with large reserves

[Kiss–Tóthmérész v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.6958v3), §4, is a genuine method antecedent, not merely a different problem title. Definition4.1 splits every directed edge of an Eulerian source into an undirected two-edge path, using M parallel edges on the tail segment and one on the head segment. The base configuration puts M times the outgoing degree on old vertices and M/2 on the new edge vertices. Claim4.4 proves a legal simulation for up to M/2 source moves, with exact old-coordinate identity and an edge-vertex reserve varying by at most the prefix length. The reverse argument uses a finite-game bound to infer nontermination. This is an explicit earlier use of a large reserve and a controlled finite horizon to preserve firing legality.

The submitted construction uses another invariant: before its first gate, old coordinates are `M z_v + N − t_v`, with M=N+1 and total prefix length below N. The integral remainder separates source-legal and source-illegal thresholds. A gate counts N old moves; the marker forces a first gate in **every** target-reaching word; one gate firing then funds completion to a specified target. The inspected KT construction proves a distance-to-nontermination/rank hardness statement through an Eulerian-to-undirected simulation. It supplies no such gate/marker target equivalence in the read proof. Conversely the candidate does not establish KT's undirected conclusions, and its generic source need not be Eulerian. The common finite-horizon reserve technique should be expressly credited; neither its general idea nor scaling to suppress bounded drift should be advertised as novel.

The fresh checker implements the actual KT construction on six simple Eulerian three-vertex graphs, 162 initial configurations, 1,634 prefix count states and 2,208 legal bundles, to horizon4. It checks the published identities rather than a keyword comparison. This finite reproduction is supporting evidence for the method comparison; the written source proof is the universal basis.

## 5. Recent close controls and model changes

The inspected 2026 [Auger–Coucheney–Etse journal paper](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v33i2p5/pdf/), Theorem21 and complete construction/proof PDF39–43, proves NP-completeness for generalized rotor mechanisms. Its source variables choose between arc-state paths, the full state includes an arc/rotor configuration, and the construction has absorbing sink destinations. A single routing operation moves one particle along one arc and changes the arc mechanism. Ordinary chip-firing instead emits along every outgoing arc at the full-degree threshold, with no rotor state. For example, a vertex with two outgoing edges and one chip can make a routing move but cannot fire. There is no ordinary, conservative, strongly connected chip-firing compilation established by the inspected NP-hardness proof. Hardness for the broader model does not transfer downward by containment. Its journal first-page dates are submission15Nov2024, acceptance3Mar2026 and publication14Apr2026; arXiv's first public posting is27Nov2024, v2 is21Nov2025. No equality of those editions' PDF bytes is asserted.

[Dubussy–Michalski v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13949v2), posted28May2026, addresses the minimum initial **total** allowing some infinite game on a given graph. Its main statements minimize over strategies and a primitive-period extension. Optimizing over initial configurations is different from deciding whether a specified one reaches a specified target. The minimum value does not encode every prescribed distribution of the same total. The read statements and comparison pages do not provide a Karp map to ordinary reachability. The full technical proof was not independently certified, and no exclusion of every possible corollary is inferred.

Chan–Levine's [Abelian networks IV v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.03322v2), Theorem5.6/Algorithm1, extends the burning test to critical networks. Its running time is explicitly the sum of the period vector, which may be exponential. Its period for a sandpile processor includes an outgoing-degree factor, whereas a graph firing period does not; PDF45 explicitly warns about this normalization. Hence neither the existence of a burning test nor the group description supplies a polynomial classifier or the submitted many-one hardness map. These are credited structural antecedents, not a priority clearance.

The primary Petri controls make another common overclaim visible. Alimonti et al.'s2011 published body, PDF3, explicitly uses unit-weight arcs and no parallel arcs; its conflict-free statements cannot be silently applied with arbitrary binary thresholds. Tredup–Wallner's2021 acyclic join-free NP-hardness allows choices among transitions and does not impose conservative strongly connected graph firings. Hujsa et al.'s2020 weighted marked-graph reachability statements require extra input-incidence restrictions and/or liveness/reversibility hypotheses. A graph chip-firing net may have many predecessor transitions at a place and need not have those promises. Its liveness result and a potential state equation do not alone produce the target classification. Howell–Rosier1988 and Jones–Landweber–Lien1977 original bodies were not successfully inspected; their exact printed conventions and any additional applicable consequences remain UNKNOWN. The checked primary follow-up bodies establish only their own explicit model boundaries.

General VAS/Petri decidability or hardness, parallel updates, infinite-lattice universality, divisor rank, minimum recurrent totals, and rotor-routing algorithms are not interchangeable with this exact language. A one-way reduction or inclusion into a larger model is not a hardness reduction back into ordinary chip-firing. No universal claim about all those literatures follows from this audit.
