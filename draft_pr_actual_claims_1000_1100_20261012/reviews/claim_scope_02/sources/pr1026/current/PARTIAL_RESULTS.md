# Odd two-torsion theta singularities: partial results and remaining gap

9 October 2026. **The all-genus conjecture is not proved or disproved here.**
This document gives reductions and a fully local dimension argument. It makes
no novelty claim. In particular the diagonal dimension statement overlaps
known work; the purpose of the proof below is to justify the local conclusion
without an algebraic-independence test for truncated equations.

## 1. Exact target and conventions

Work over the complex numbers. Write \(N_g=g(g+1)/2\), and let
\(\mathcal A_g\) be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties,
including decomposable varieties. The target is the reduced support

\[
 I_g=\{(A,\Theta):\text{some odd two-torsion point of the symmetric theta
 divisor has multiplicity at least }3\}.
\]

The question is whether, for every integer \(g\geq1\), **each nonempty
irreducible component** of \(I_g\) has codimension \(g\). If genus zero
is admitted as a convention, there are no odd characteristics and its
locus is empty as well. The empty cases \(g=1,2\) are vacuous, not
counterexamples. Neither multiplicity exactly three, reducedness of the
gradient scheme, nor equality with a compactified zero scheme is part of
the target.

For local calculations take the smooth level cover
\(M_g=\mathcal A_g(8)\). The subgroup inclusion \(\Gamma(8)\subset\Gamma(4,8)\) gives a finite
cover \(M_g\to\mathcal A_g(4,8)\). Equivalently, work on Siegel space
\(\mathfrak H_g\). For a characteristic
\(m=(\epsilon,\delta)\in(\mathbb Z/2)^{2g}\), its parity is
\(\epsilon\cdot\delta\). For odd \(m\), put

\[
 F_m(\tau)=\nabla_z\theta_m(\tau,0),\qquad Z_m=V(F_m).
\]

Theta parity implies \(\theta_m(\tau,-z)=-\theta_m(\tau,z)\);
therefore \(F_m=0\) is exactly the order-at-least-three condition. Locally
\(Z_m\) has the ideal generated by its \(g\) gradient coordinates.
Globally these coordinates are a section of a rank-\(g\) bundle, with
the usual theta multiplier. Trivializations change the ideal by units
and invertible matrices. The finite union of the \(Z_m\) maps onto
\(I_g\). Finite maps preserve dimensions, even though the map to the
coarse space need not be unramified at points with automorphisms.
The same codimension statements consequently apply to the moduli stack.

**Elementary upper bound.** Every component of \(I_g\) has codimension
at most \(g\). Indeed, every component of each \(Z_m\) has height at
most \(g\) in the regular local ring of the level cover. A maximal
component of the finite union of their finite images is the image of
one such component. Thus a possible counterexample must have **excess
dimension**, namely codimension strictly less than \(g\).

## 2. Decomposable components cannot have excess dimension

### Proposition 2.1

Every irreducible component of \(I_g\) that is contained in the
decomposable locus has codimension exactly \(g\).

**Proof.** The decomposable locus is the finite union of product images
\(P_a=\operatorname{im}(\mathcal A_a\times\mathcal A_{g-a})\),
\(1\leq a\leq\lfloor g/2\rfloor\). It suffices to bound
\(\dim(I_g\cap P_a)\leq N_g-g\) for each \(a\), because the
dimension of a finite union of constructible sets is the maximum of
their dimensions. The argument uses only that these are algebraic
product morphisms and that the dimension of an image is at most the
dimension of its domain. It does not require injectivity, uniqueness
of a chosen factorization, or a finite-product-morphism theorem.

If both factors have dimension at least two, then

\[
 \dim P_a\leq N_a+N_{g-a}=N_g-a(g-a).
\]

For \(g\geq4\) and \(2\leq a\leq g-2\),
\(a(g-a)\geq2(g-2)\geq g\). Hence
\(\dim(I_g\cap P_a)\leq N_g-g\).

It remains to handle an elliptic factor. Put \(h=g-1\). Theta
factorization at a block diagonal period matrix gives

\[
 \theta_{(m_E,m_B)}(z_E,z_B)
       =\theta_{m_E}(z_E)\theta_{m_B}(z_B).
\]

An elliptic odd theta function has a simple zero at zero. An elliptic
even theta constant is nonzero: an even zero would have order at least
two, impossible for a divisor of degree one. Total odd parity therefore
gives precisely two cases. An odd elliptic factor requires an even
theta-null on \(B\), while an even elliptic factor requires
\(B\in I_h\). Consequently the preimage of \(I_g\) under the product
map is exactly

\[
 \mathcal A_1\times(\theta_{\mathrm{null},h}\cup I_h).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The set in parentheses is a proper closed subset of \(\mathcal A_h\).
To see properness without a generic-smooth-theta assertion, each even
theta constant is not identically zero, and each odd gradient is not
identically zero. These assertions follow by a symplectic change of
characteristic and evaluation on products of elliptic curves: use all
even factors for the first assertion and exactly one odd factor for
the second. A finite union of proper analytic zero sets cannot cover
the irreducible space \(\mathfrak H_h\). It follows that
\(\dim(\theta_{\mathrm{null},h}\cup I_h)\leq N_h-1\) whenever the
union is nonempty. By (2.1),

\[
 \dim(I_g\cap P_1)\leq1+(N_h-1)=N_h=N_g-g.
\]

This also treats \(g=3\). In genus two the right side of (2.1) is empty.
Thus \(\operatorname{codim}C\geq g\) in every nonempty case. The
upper bound from Section 1 proves equality. \(\square\)

This argument concerns components actually contained in the decomposable
locus. It does not infer that an arbitrary product family is a component.
In particular a counterexample must have a dense open subset of
indecomposable ppav.

## 3. The cubic tangent term controls first-order transversality

At \(\tau\in Z_m\), let

\[
 P_\tau(z)=\frac16\sum_{i,j,k}
       \partial_{z_i}\partial_{z_j}\partial_{z_k}
          \theta_m(\tau,0)z_i z_j z_k
\]

be the degree-three term of the theta function. It is zero if the
multiplicity is at least five. In independent symmetric coordinates
\(\tau_{ab}\), the heat equation is

\[
 \partial_{\tau_{ab}}F_{m,i}
   =\frac{\partial_{z_i}\partial_{z_a}\partial_{z_b}
                 \theta_m(\tau,0)}{2\pi i(1+\delta_{ab})}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Proposition 3.1

If no nonzero constant vector \(v\) satisfies
\(\partial_vP_\tau=0\), then \(Z_m\) is smooth of codimension
\(g\) at \(\tau\). Conversely, if a reduced-support component
\(C\subset Z_m\) has codimension \(c<g\), its cubic tangent term
at a general point depends, after a linear change of variables, on at
most \(c\) variables.

**Proof.** Up to nonzero column scalings, the Jacobian in (3.1) is the
cubic contraction map \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\to V^*\). Its dual
is \(V\to\operatorname{Sym}^2 V^*\), \(v\mapsto\partial_vP_\tau\).
The stated absence of an annihilator is equivalent to Jacobian rank
\(g\), so the holomorphic implicit function theorem applies.

At a smooth point of the reduced component \(C\), the differential
of each equation annihilates \(T_\tau C\), so its rank is at most
\(c\), regardless of whether the gradient scheme is reduced.
The annihilator of the cubic has dimension at least \(g-c\).
Choose coordinates adapted to it. In characteristic zero, zero
derivatives in these directions mean that the cubic does not depend on
those coordinates. \(\square\)

Thus a nonconical cubic suffices for the expected codimension, but the
converse is false as a method: first-order rank deficiency need not
imply excess dimension. Section 4 gives points of Jacobian rank only
three where the full zero locus nevertheless has codimension \(g\).
Multiplicity-at-least-five points are not excluded by Proposition 3.1.

As a check, the product cubic \(z_1Q(z_2,\ldots,z_g)\), for a
nondegenerate quadratic form \(Q\), has no constant-direction
annihilator. The coefficient of \(Q\) forces the \(z_1\)-component
of the direction to vanish, and nondegeneracy then forces the remaining
components to vanish. This recovers the transversality expected at an
elliptic factor times an ordinary theta-null. It is not a classification
of all components.

## 4. A rigorous local three-odd-factor slice

The following recovers a restricted local dimension conclusion compatible
with Grushevsky–Salvati Manni's diagonal calculation. It does not assert
their global component statement, all-diagonal-points statement, or
smoothness statement.

### Proposition 4.1

Let \(g\geq3\), let \(m_0\) have characteristic \([1;1]\) in the
first three elliptic factors and \([0;0]\) in the others, and consider
a diagonal period matrix \(\tau_0=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_g)\).
For \(j\geq4\), write

\[
 \beta_j=\frac{\vartheta_j''(0)}{\vartheta_j(0)},\qquad
 \gamma_j=\frac{\vartheta_j''''(0)}{\vartheta_j(0)},\qquad
 c_j=\gamma_j-3\beta_j^2,
\]

where \(\vartheta_j(z)=\theta[0;0](t_j,z)\). If all \(c_j\ne0\),
then every local irreducible component of \(Z_{m_0}\) through
\(\tau_0\) has codimension \(g\). These nonvanishing conditions hold
on a nonempty open subset of the diagonal.

**Proof.** Put \(x_{ab}=\tau_{ab}/(2\pi i)\) for \(a<b\). The
off-diagonal heat equation gives the convergent local Taylor expansion
by applying

\[
 \exp\left(\sum_{a<b}x_{ab}\partial_{z_a}\partial_{z_b}\right)
               \prod_{i=1}^g\vartheta_i(z_i).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Here the first three \(\vartheta_i\) are odd elliptic theta functions.
Normalize all gradient coordinates by the common nonzero factor
\(A=\prod_{i=1}^3\vartheta_i'(0)
        \prod_{j=4}^g\vartheta_j(0)\), and call them \(f_i\).
For \(i\leq3\), set \(\alpha_i=\vartheta_i'''(0)/\vartheta_i'(0)\).

Take the linear slice \(Y\) defined by

\[
 x_{jk}=0\ (4\leq j<k\leq g),\qquad
 x_{1j}=x_{2j}=x_{3j}=y_j\ (j\geq4).
\]

Let \(a=x_{12},b=x_{13},c=x_{23}\) and
\(S=\sum_{j\geq4}\beta_j y_j^2\). Parity in (4.1), through degree
two, gives

\[
 f_1=c+\alpha_1ab+S+O(3),\quad
 f_2=b+\alpha_2ac+S+O(3),\quad
 f_3=a+\alpha_3bc+S+O(3).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The Jacobian of these equations in \((a,b,c)\) is invertible. Thus
the equations \(f_1=f_2=f_3=0\) solve uniquely for these variables
as convergent functions of \(y\) and the diagonal parameters, with
\(a=b=c=-S+O(3)\).

For completeness, the terms of \(f_j\), \(j\geq4\), that can
contribute through degree three after this elimination are

\[
 \beta_j y_j(a+b+c)
  +3\beta_j y_j\sum_{k\geq4,\ k\ne j}\beta_k y_k^2
  +\gamma_jy_j^3.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Terms involving \(\alpha_i ab y_j\), \(\alpha_i ac y_j\), or
\(\alpha_i bc y_j\) have degree at least five after elimination.
Formula (4.3) follows directly by enumerating the three-edge terms of
the exponential: all four vertices \(1,2,3,j\) must have odd
derivative parity; an additional even vertex \(k\) contributes the
three choices of paired first-three vertices. Edges between two
indices at least four are zero on the slice. Repeated-edge factorials
are those of the exponential in (4.1).

Substitution of (4.2) into (4.3) leaves

\[
 f_j^{\mathrm{elim}}=c_jy_j^3+O(\|y\|^4),\qquad j=4,\ldots,g.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

This is a statement about convergent functions, not just a formal
test of polynomial independence. In a small neighborhood of the fixed
diagonal parameters there are constants \(\eta>0,K<\infty\) such
that \(|c_j|\geq\eta\) and every remainder in (4.4) has absolute
value at most \(K r^4\), where \(r=\max_j|y_j|\). If \(r>0\)
and all equations vanish, take \(j\) with \(|y_j|=r\). Then
\(\eta r^3\leq K r^4\), impossible for sufficiently small \(r\).
Hence \(y=0\), and then \(a=b=c=0\) by uniqueness. Thus the
set-theoretic intersection \(Z_{m_0}\cap Y\) is exactly the diagonal
as a germ at \(\tau_0\).

The slice has codimension
\(r_Y=(g-3)(g-4)/2+2(g-3)=g(g-3)/2\) in the ambient
\(N_g\)-dimensional manifold. For every component \(C\) through
\(\tau_0\), the local intersection inequality gives

\[
 \dim C-r_Y\leq\dim(C\cap Y)\leq g.
\]

Therefore \(\dim C\leq g+r_Y=N_g-g\). The \(g\) defining
equations give the reverse bound, proving pure local codimension \(g\).
For \(g=3\), there are no \(y\)-variables and (4.2) alone proves
the assertion.

Finally, put \(q=e^{\pi i t_j}\). The convergent elliptic Fourier
series yields
\(\vartheta_j(0)=1+2q+O(q^4)\),
\(\vartheta_j''(0)=-8\pi^2q+O(q^4)\), and
\(\vartheta_j''''(0)=32\pi^4q+O(q^4)\). Consequently
\(c_j=32\pi^4q+O(q^2)\), so it is not identically zero. This
establishes the required nonempty open set. \(\square\)

The equation Jacobian at the diagonal in Proposition 4.1 has rank
exactly three. For \(g>3\) it is therefore inappropriate to infer
smoothness of the entire fixed-characteristic gradient scheme at that
point from the dimension argument. No reducedness claim is made.

## 5. Two other approaches and their stopping points

### 5.1 Rank-one degeneration

There is a useful conditional induction, already present in
Grushevsky–Salvati Manni. Suppose the lower-genus locus \(I_{g-1}\)
has pure codimension \(g-1\), and an irreducible component \(C\) of
\(I_g\) has closure meeting the rank-one boundary of a partial
toroidal compactification. The boundary gradient formulas bound that
intersection by the union of the doubled relative theta singular locus
and a relative theta divisor over \(I_{g-1}\). The relative singular locus has dimension \(N_{g-1}-1\) by
Ciliberto–van der Geer, arXiv:math/9911127v2, Theorem (2.4). Together
with the inductive hypothesis, this bounds both sets by \(N_{g-1}-1=N_g-g-1\). Work on a sufficiently fine level cover of the partial compactification,
where the boundary is Cartier. Its nonempty intersection with a lifted
component closure has codimension one, and finite descent preserves
dimension. This forces
\(\operatorname{codim}C\geq g\), and hence equality.

The missing implication is crucial: noncompleteness of \(C\) only
forces its closure to meet **some** toroidal boundary. It does not
force meeting the open rank-one boundary. Higher-rank strata can
contain the entire boundary intersection. The small-genus boundary
classification cannot simply be reused in arbitrary genus. Thus a
least-genus counterexample, if it exists, has \(g\geq6\) and cannot
be excluded by rank-one induction without an additional boundary
accessibility theorem. The displayed conditional argument uses the
published boundary-dimension interface; it is not a new proof of that
interface.

### 5.2 Spin Jacobians as a counterexample search

On smooth curves, Riemann's singularity theorem identifies the relevant
odd theta characteristics with those having at least three sections.
Teixidor i Bigas, *Half-canonical series on algebraic curves*,
Theorem (2.17), applied to projective dimension \(r=2\), gives their
image in \(\mathcal M_g\) pure dimension \(3g-6\) for \(g\geq6\).
For \(g=5\), Clifford equality identifies the locus with the
hyperelliptic locus, of dimension nine. For \(g\leq4\) it is empty
by Clifford's bound. Finite spin marking and the quasi-finite Torelli
map preserve this dimension. Its codimension in \(\mathcal A_g\), for \(g\geq5\), is

\[
 N_g-(3g-6)=g+\frac{(g-3)(g-4)}2>g.
\]

Thus this natural family supplies neither an excess-dimensional
component nor the desired full classification. Its codimension-three
description *inside curve moduli* cannot be transferred to ppav moduli.
Conversely, its ambient codimension greater than \(g\) shows that it
must lie in a larger component of the gradient locus, rather than
contradicting the upper bound from Section 1. No similar unproved
dimension or component assertion about all Prym varieties is used.

## 6. What is still missing

Any genuine global counterexample would have to be a component with
codimension \(c<g\), generically indecomposable, whose marked cubic
tangent term generically depends on at most \(c\) variables. A
least-genus counterexample would evade the rank-one induction. The
local slice theorem only controls a specified characteristic near
specified generic diagonal points; it does not show that an arbitrary
component passes through such a point. The integral symplectic group
changes markings, not the ppav's moduli point, so it cannot supply this
missing specialization. Rational symplectic transformations change
isogeny/polarization data and have not been shown to preserve the
gradient-zero condition.

These are genuine global and higher-order gaps. Neither a nonzero
virtual top Chern class nor finite theta expansions resolve them.

## 7. Exact checks

`check_formal_jets.py` enumerates the heat-operator monomials exactly,
with rational coefficients, on the slice for \(g=3,\ldots,8\).
It verifies (4.2) and (4.4), basic dimension identities, and product
cubic ranks. `CHECK_RESULTS.json` records all passes. The checks are
finite sanity checks; the all-\(g\) local argument is the proof above.
They do not test arbitrary period matrices or prove the global
conjecture.
