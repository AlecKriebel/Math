# Planar valuation covariograms: scoped results and the remaining obstruction

**Target:** 30000630 / OWR-1394-006, rank 1095.  
**Disposition:** partial results; the arbitrary-planar-body determination question is not resolved here.  
**Bounded proof program:** five distinct approaches used, starting from zero inherited substantive approaches located by the supplied gate. No claim of novelty or worldwide openness is made.

## 1. Exact question and conventions

Let \(\mathcal K^2\) consist of compact convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^2\), including the empty set, and let \(\mathcal K^2_0\) consist of those with nonempty interior. A real-valued valuation \(\varphi\) has \(\varphi(\varnothing)=0\) and satisfies
\[
\varphi(C\cup E)+\varphi(C\cap E)=\varphi(C)+\varphi(E)
\]
whenever \(C,E,C\cup E\) are convex. Translation invariance has its usual meaning. Monotonicity here includes the empty set. We distinguish:

* **Body-strict monotonicity:** \(C\subsetneq E\), with both sets full dimensional, implies \(\varphi(C)<\varphi(E)\).
* **Strong strict monotonicity:** the same implication for every nonempty compact convex \(C\subsetneq E\), including segments and points.

The original report does not specify this distinction, continuity, homogeneity, or evenness. The intersection functional requires values on lower-dimensional sets and on the empty set. We use the standard compact-convex extension, including \(P([a,b])=2|b-a|\) for perimeter. We do not silently impose homogeneity or replace perimeter by area.

For a fixed **known** valuation, set
\[
g_{K,\varphi}(x)=\varphi(K\cap(K+x)).
\]
The determination assertion under investigation is
\[
K,L\in\mathcal K^2_0,\qquad g_{K,\varphi}=g_{L,\varphi}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L=K+t\ \text{or}\ L=-K+t. \tag{Q}
\]
Reflections mean reflections in a point, not arbitrary line reflections or rotations. The competitor in (Q) is any planar convex body. A theorem whose competitors are polygons, smooth bodies, or analytic bodies is not a solution of (Q).

### Source reconciliation

Bianchi's contribution in OWR 56/2006, printed pp. 3343–3344, asks about replacing area by perimeter or a strictly monotone translation-invariant valuation. The proposed positive polygon result is stated separately. It is not an arbitrary-body theorem and does not explicitly add evenness. The source: [EMS report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46086).

Averkov–Bianchi, *Covariograms generated by valuations*, IMRN 2015, 9277–9329, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnu219), [author manuscript, arXiv:1307.1529v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1529), works principally with even, monotone, translation-invariant valuations vanishing on singletons. Its Theorems 1.1–1.3 give, respectively:

1. determination of a centrally symmetric body among **all** planar convex bodies;
2. determination of a polygon among **all** planar convex bodies for the stated strictly monotone even class;
3. a polygon theorem for a fixed directional-width valuation.

The manuscript's Section 7(2) explicitly asks for arbitrary planar bodies in the strictly monotone class. Section 7(1) separately raises valuations outside the even monotone class. The ordinary area-covariogram theorem cannot be substituted for these questions.

The inspected proof of Theorem 1.2 uses support recovery, boundary edge/normal-cone recovery, the radial derivative identity of Theorem 4.2, and a finite polygonal arc-gluing argument. It does not supply a passage to arbitrary curved boundaries. If the common difference body is polygonal then both summands are polygons, which explains why its theorem really allows arbitrary convex competitors when the original body is a polygon. The degree-one/area decomposition and the proof of Theorem 1.1 were also checked directly. The width theorem is recorded by its exact statement; this report does not claim an independent line-by-line reproof of its separate long argument.

There are normalization points in the inspected v2 manuscript that require explicit guards:

* Its p. 2 definition uses strong strictness, but the p. 3 assertion that a positive area coefficient suffices only holds for body-strictness. Nested segments disprove the unqualified strong version. This does not obstruct the Euclidean-perimeter case.
* Removing a singleton value \(c_0\) means subtracting \(c_0\chi\), where \(\chi(C)=1\) for nonempty \(C\), and \(0\) for the empty set. At covariogram level the subtraction is \(c_0\mathbf1_{K-K}\), not a constant on the whole translation plane.
* Under the manuscript's segment convention, the boundary-segment formula in Claim 5.2.1 has a factor of two: at the midpoint of a difference-body edge the perimeter contribution is twice the smaller seminorm edge length. A rectangle gives an immediate check. The local recovery still works because this is a known nonzero factor. This observation concerns the inspected manuscript, not an uninspected publisher version.

These points are not presented as a disproof of the credited polygon theorem.

## 2. The valuation class without an evenness assumption

We use the standard classification ingredients identified in the proof of Averkov–Bianchi Theorem 2.2: continuity of monotone translation-invariant valuations; homogeneous decomposition; the degree-two volume classification; and the degree-one mixed-area classification. None requires evenness until the final symmetrization of the mixed-area body.

Consequently, after subtracting \(c_0\chi\), a monotone translation-invariant planar valuation has the form
\[
\psi(C)=V(C,H)+\alpha A(C),\qquad \alpha\ge0, \tag{2.1}
\]
where \(H\) is a nonempty compact convex set, determined up to translation, and
\[
A(C+H)=A(C)+2V(C,H)+A(H).
\]
Here \(A\) is area. The two terms in (2.1) have degrees one and two, respectively. Thus the original assumptions imply continuity in this standard domain, but do not impose a single homogeneity degree. If \(\psi\) is even then \(H\) may be chosen centrally symmetric. The proofs below also apply directly to any valuation (2.1), so their geometric content does not depend on re-establishing the general classification from scratch.

For clarity, the classification argument is as follows. Translation invariance makes the singleton value \(c_0\) independent of the point; monotonicity including the empty set gives \(c_0\ge0\). The function \(\psi=\varphi-c_0\chi\) remains a valuation and remains monotone: subtract the same constant for an inclusion between nonempty sets, and use \(\varphi(C)\ge c_0\) when comparing a nonempty set with the empty set. The continuity used here is Hausdorff continuity on the nonempty compact convex sets, with the empty-set value treated separately. After applying the cited monotone-continuity and continuous homogeneous-decomposition theorems, the degree-zero component vanishes: its value on any nonempty \(C\) is the limit of \(\psi(sC)\) as \(s\downarrow0\), namely the singleton value zero. No evenness hypothesis is used in these steps. Homogeneous decomposition after normalization gives \(\psi=\psi_1+\psi_2\). The identities
\[
\psi_1(C)=\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{\psi(sC)}s,
\qquad
\psi_2(C)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\psi(sC)}{s^2}
\]
show both components are monotone. The volume classification gives \(\psi_2=\alpha A\), with \(\alpha\ge0\), and the degree-one classification gives \(\psi_1=V(\cdot,H)\). This is the argument of the cited paper before its use of evenness to replace \(H\) by \((H-H)/2\).

The exceptional zero valuation is excluded below: either \(\alpha>0\), or \(H\) is not a singleton. A singleton \(H\) gives the zero mixed-area term.

### Proposition 2.1. Reflection need not preserve the data

Every translation-invariant valuation gives \(g_{K,\varphi}(-x)=g_{K,\varphi}(x)\), since the two intersections are translates. Nevertheless, reflection of the **body** preserves all such covariograms if and only if the valuation is even on the bodies in question. In general
\[
g_{-K,\varphi}=g_{K,\varphi^-},\qquad \varphi^-(C)=\varphi(-C).
\]
In particular, equality at \(x=0\) for all \(K\) would already imply \(\varphi(K)=\varphi(-K)\).

For an explicit strongly strictly monotone non-even example, let
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
\varphi(C)=P(C)+V(C,T).
\]
Perimeter is strongly strictly monotone and mixed area is monotone, so their sum is strongly strictly monotone. But
\[
V(T,T)=\tfrac12,\qquad V(-T,T)=1,
\]
since \(A(T)=1/2\) and \(A(T-T)=3\). Hence
\[
g_{T,\varphi}(0)=P(T)+\tfrac12\ne P(T)+1=g_{-T,\varphi}(0).
\]
This is **not** a counterexample to (Q): it demonstrates that reflection is not an unavoidable data symmetry in the literal non-even class. The weaker uniqueness implication (Q) remains meaningful.

For completeness, strong strictness of Euclidean perimeter follows from the Cauchy formula \(P(C)=\int_0^\pi w_C(\theta)\,d\theta\), valid with the segment convention. Proper inclusion makes the support function strictly smaller on an open set of directions, and hence makes the nonnegative width difference positive on an open set.

## 3. Area recovery and central symmetry without evenness

Write \(m(C)=V(C,H)\), and fix the nonzero valuation \(\psi=m+\alpha A\) from (2.1). Put \(D K=K-K\).

### Lemma 3.1. Support and an integral identity

For every \(K\in\mathcal K^2_0\),
\[
\operatorname{supp}g_{K,\psi}=D K,
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R^2}g_{K,\psi}(x)\,dx
=2A(K)m(K)+\alpha A(K)^2. \tag{3.1}
\]
Here support means the closure of the nonzero set. Global continuity across \(\partial DK\) is not assumed; perimeter values on boundary segments can be nonzero.

**Proof.** The intersection has nonempty interior for \(x\in\operatorname{int}DK\), and is empty off \(DK\). A nonzero monotone degree-one mixed-area functional is positive on every full-dimensional body; this follows by putting a positive-radius disk inside it and using monotonicity. The area term is also positive there if \(\alpha>0\). Thus support is \(DK\).

Translate \(H\) to contain the origin, so its support function is nonnegative. On \(\partial K\), at almost every point with outer normal \(n_K(y)\), define
\[
d\mu_K(y)=\tfrac12 h_H(n_K(y))\,d\mathcal H^1(y).
\]
The mixed-area boundary formula gives \(\mu_K(\partial K)=m(K)\). For almost every translation \(x\), the boundaries \(\partial K\) and \(\partial(K+x)\) have no common arc of positive length. Indeed, integrating the length of their common part over \(x\) and using Fubini gives zero, because \(\partial K\) has area zero. The boundary of their intersection then consists, up to length-zero sets, of one portion from each translated boundary. Its mixed area is consequently the sum of the two integrals of the corresponding indicators against \(\mu_K\). Integrating in \(x\) makes each term equal to \(A(K)m(K)\), again by Fubini. The usual indicator-convolution identity gives \(\int g_{K,A}=A(K)^2\). All measures are finite, and the integrands have compact support. This proves (3.1). ∎

The even special case of (3.1) is Averkov–Bianchi Theorem 3.1. The proof above explains why evenness is unnecessary for this identity.

### Theorem 3.2. Explicit area reconstruction

Let
\[
c=g_{K,\psi}(0)>0,\qquad I=\int g_{K,\psi}>0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle A(K)=\frac{I}{c+\sqrt{c^2-\alpha I}}.} \tag{3.2}
\]
The nonnegative root is used. This formula also includes \(\alpha=0\), when it becomes \(A(K)=I/(2c)\).

**Proof.** Write \(a=A(K)>0\) and \(p=m(K)\ge0\). Then \(c=p+\alpha a\) and \(I=2pa+\alpha a^2\). Thus \(c^2-\alpha I=p^2\). Substitution into (3.2) gives \(a\). Its denominator is positive. In the pure-area case \(p=0\), the discriminant is zero, which is valid. The other quadratic root, when present, is excluded by \(p=c-\alpha a\ge0\). ∎

If the original valuation is \(\varphi=c_0\chi+\psi\), then \(c_0\ge0\), and \(\operatorname{supp}g_{K,\varphi}=DK\). Hence the known \(c_0\) and the observed support give the normalization
\[
g_{K,\psi}=g_{K,\varphi}-c_0\mathbf1_{DK}.
\]
A strictly monotone valuation cannot have \(\psi=0\). This completes the normalization without requiring additional measurements.

### Corollary 3.3. A centrally symmetric body is determined among all bodies

If \(K\) is centrally symmetric and \(g_{K,\psi}=g_{L,\psi}\), with \(K,L\in\mathcal K^2_0\), then \(L\) is a translate of \(K\). No evenness or strict monotonicity beyond the stated nonzero monotone mixed-area class is needed.

**Proof.** The data give \(DK=DL\) and, by (3.2), \(A(K)=A(L)\). Since \(K\) is centrally symmetric, \(A(DK)=4A(K)\). Brunn–Minkowski gives
\[
A(L-L)^{1/2}\ge 2A(L)^{1/2},
\]
and equality holds here. The equality characterization for full-dimensional convex bodies makes \(L\) and \(-L\) homothetic; equal areas make the ratio one. Thus \(L\) is centrally symmetric. Each centered body is half its difference body, so they differ by translation. ∎

This follows the established central-symmetry method, with (3.2) replacing the evenness-based perimeter calculation. It is a scoped extension, not a solution for nonsymmetric bodies.

## 4. Smooth lenses recover antipodal curvature pairs

Suppose \(K\) has a \(C^2\) support function \(h_K(\theta)\) with strictly positive curvature radius
\[
r_K(\theta)=h_K(\theta)+h_K''(\theta)>0.
\]
Write \(u=u(\theta)\), \(p_+=p_K(u)\), \(p_-=p_K(-u)\), and
\[
x_0=p_+-p_-\in\partial DK,\quad x_\varepsilon=x_0-\varepsilon u,
\quad \kappa_+=1/r_K(\theta),\quad \kappa_-=1/r_K(\theta+\pi).
\]
Let \(w_H(u)=h_H(u)+h_H(-u)\).

### Lemma 4.1. Leading lens coefficient

For every direction with \(w_H(u)>0\),
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{g_{K,\psi}(x_\varepsilon)}{\sqrt\varepsilon}
=w_H(u)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa_++\kappa_-}}. \tag{4.1}
\]
The area term does not enter the leading coefficient.

**Proof.** Use tangent-normal coordinates \((s,y)\) centered at \(p_+\), with \(u\) the upward normal. Near the unique contact, the upper boundary of \(K\) and the lower boundary of \(K+x_\varepsilon\) have equations
\[
y=-\tfrac12\kappa_+s^2+o(s^2),\qquad
 y=-\varepsilon+\tfrac12\kappa_-s^2+o(s^2).
\]
The lens is entirely in these neighborhoods for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\): otherwise compactness would give a second point in the limiting support intersection. Its two intersection endpoints have tangent coordinates
\[
s_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon}{\kappa_++\kappa_-}}\,(1+o(1)).
\]
Each of the two boundary arcs has Euclidean length
\(2\sqrt{2\varepsilon/(\kappa_++\kappa_-)}(1+o(1))\), and their outer normals converge uniformly to \(u\) and \(-u\). The mixed-area boundary formula therefore gives (4.1). The lens height is \(O(\varepsilon)\) and width \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon)\), so its area is \(O(\varepsilon^{3/2})\). ∎

In fact the same graph calculation gives
\[
A(K\cap(K+x_\varepsilon))
=\frac43\sqrt{\frac2{\kappa_++\kappa_-}}\,\varepsilon^{3/2}
+o(\varepsilon^{3/2}).
\]
For Euclidean perimeter, \(H=2B_2\), so \(w_H=4\). A disk of radius \(R\) gives the coefficient \(4\sqrt R\), in agreement with its exact lens perimeter.

### Corollary 4.2. Unordered opposite radii are determined in the smooth class

If \(H\) is not a singleton and \(K,L\) are both of the above \(C^2\) positive-curvature class, equality of their \(\psi\)-covariograms implies
\[
\{r_K(\theta),r_K(\theta+\pi)\}
=\{r_L(\theta),r_L(\theta+\pi)\}
\quad\text{for every }\theta. \tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** The common difference body gives the sum
\(s=r_K(\theta)+r_K(\theta+\pi)\). Formula (4.1) gives
\(q=\kappa_++\kappa_-\). Thus the product of the two radii is \(s/q\), and the radii are the two roots of
\[
z^2-sz+s/q=0.
\]
If \(H\) has interior then its width is positive in all directions. If it is a nondegenerate segment the width vanishes in just one opposite pair of directions, which is filled in by continuity. ∎

### Theorem 4.3. Determination within the real-analytic positive-curvature class

Fix a nonzero valuation of the form (2.1). Among bodies with real-analytic support functions and positive curvature radii, its covariogram determines the body up to translation and point reflection.

**Proof.** If the mixed-area term vanishes, \(\alpha>0\), and the established planar ordinary-area covariogram theorem applies. Otherwise use (4.2). Put \(a=r_K\), \(\widetilde a(\theta)=a(\theta+\pi)\), and \(b=r_L\). The analytic functions satisfy
\[
(b-a)(b-\widetilde a)=0
\]
everywhere on the connected circle. If \(b-a\) is not identically zero it is nonzero on some open interval, so \(b-\widetilde a\) vanishes there and therefore everywhere by analytic continuation. Thus either \(b=a\) globally or \(b=\widetilde a\) globally. In the first case \((h_L-h_K)''+(h_L-h_K)=0\), so their difference is a first harmonic and the bodies are translates. In the second case the same argument applies to \(L\) and \(-K\). ∎

**Essential scope limit:** both competitors in this theorem are assumed real analytic with positive curvature. The data have not been shown to force an arbitrary competing convex body into that class. In particular, this is not a theorem that an analytic body is determined among all convex bodies.

## 5. A rigorous smooth switching obstruction to local reconstruction

The previous curvature information cannot be glued globally using continuity alone. More strongly, the support, area, perimeter, and the complete perimeter covariogram on a collar of the difference-body boundary can all coincide for two inequivalent smooth bodies.

### Proposition 5.1. Boundary-collar data and the recovered global scalars do not suffice

There are \(C^\infty\), strictly positively curved planar bodies \(K_+,K_-\), neither a translation nor a point reflection of the other, such that:

1. \(DK_+=DK_-\) is a disk;
2. \(P(K_+)=P(K_-)\) and \(A(K_+)=A(K_-)\);
3. the unordered opposite-curvature pairs agree in every direction;
4. for some \(\delta>0\),
\[
g_{K_+,P}(x)=g_{K_-,P}(x)
\quad\text{whenever }\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial DK_+)<\delta.
\]

**Construction and proof.** Choose nonzero smooth functions \(f,g\) on the circle with
\[
f(\theta+\pi)=-f(\theta),\qquad g(\theta+\pi)=-g(\theta),
\]
and disjoint closed supports separated by a positive angular distance. Such functions are obtained from a smooth compactly supported bump on a sufficiently short arc, subtracting its antipodal translate, and using two disjoint opposite pairs of arcs. Choose \(R\) so large that
\[
R>\|f\|_\infty+\|g\|_\infty+
\|f+f''\|_\infty+\|g+g''\|_\infty.
\]
Then
\[
h_\pm(\theta)=R+f(\theta)\pm g(\theta)
\]
are positive support functions with positive radii \(h_\pm+h_\pm''\), and define smooth strictly positively curved bodies.

Their widths are both \(2R\), so both difference bodies are the radius-\(2R\) disk, and both perimeters are \(2\pi R\). The support-function area identity gives
\[
A(K_\pm)=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(h_\pm^2-(h_\pm')^2)\,d\theta.
\]
All terms linear in \(f,g\) integrate to zero by antipodal oddness. All mixed \(f,g\) terms vanish because the supports, including derivative supports, are disjoint. Therefore the two areas agree. The opposite radii are \(R\pm(f+f''+g+g'')\) for one body and \(R\pm(f+f''-g-g'')\) for the other. At every point one of the two bump components vanishes in a neighborhood, so the unordered pairs agree.

The support-function difference \(h_+-h_-=2g\) is not a first harmonic: it is nonzero and vanishes on an open interval. Hence the bodies are not translates. Likewise
\(h_-(\theta)-h_+(\theta+\pi)=2f(\theta)\) is not a first harmonic, excluding a point reflection followed by a translation.

It remains to prove the **exact** collar assertion, not merely an asymptotic equality. Here are the required local and compactness details.

**Local body-germ lemma.** Suppose two smooth strictly convex bodies \(C,E\) have equal support functions on an open normal interval \(J\) around \(u\). Their support points at \(u\) coincide, since \(p_C(\theta)=h_C(\theta)u(\theta)+h_C'(\theta)u'(\theta)\). Denote this point by \(p\). Choose a smaller interval \(J_0\) whose closure lies in \(J\). For every normal \(v\notin J_0\), smoothness at \(p\) implies
\[
h_C(v)-\langle p,v\rangle>0,
\qquad h_E(v)-\langle p,v\rangle>0.
\]
Compactness of the complementary normal set makes both gaps uniformly positive. On a sufficiently small ball \(B(p,\rho)\), all these complementary support inequalities are therefore strict. Membership in either body on that ball is decided entirely by the support inequalities for \(v\in J_0\), which coincide. Consequently
\[
C\cap B(p,\rho)=E\cap B(p,\rho).
\]
The same conclusion holds independently at the opposite support point.

Fix \(\theta\). Antipodal oddness makes each bump's support antipodally invariant. Positive separation of the two closed supports gives paired normal intervals about \(\theta,\theta+\pi\) on which either \(g\) vanishes identically or \(f\) vanishes identically. In the first case set \(C=K_+\); in the second set \(C=-K_+\). Then \(E=K_-\) and \(C\) have equal support functions on both chosen intervals: in the second case
\(h_{-K_+}(\eta)=h_+(\eta+\pi)=R-f(\eta)-g(\eta)=R-g(\eta)=h_-(\eta)\).
Their support points \(p,q\) at \(u,-u\) agree. Since both have constant width \(2R\),
\[
p-q=2Ru=:x_0.
\]
The local body-germ lemma supplies equal membership near \(p\) and near \(q\).

For either \(F=C\) or \(F=E\), the contact intersection \(F\cap(F+x_0)\) consists of the single point \(p\). Indeed its points must lie on the support line at \(u\), and strict convexity makes that face a singleton. For \(x\to x_0\), every nonempty lens \(F\cap(F+x)\) lies in an arbitrarily small prescribed neighborhood of \(p\), and every translated-back lens lies in the corresponding neighborhood of \(q\). To prove this, a contrary sequence \(x_j\to x_0\), \(z_j\in F\cap(F+x_j)\) has a subsequence with \(z_j\to z\), because \(F\) is compact. Closedness implies \(z\in F\) and \(z-x_0\in F\); hence \(z=p\) and \(z-x_0=q\), a contradiction. The assertion is vacuous for empty lenses.

Choose a single neighborhood \(U_\theta\) of \(x_0\) that has this localization property for both \(C,E\). The equal local membership statements at \(p\) and \(q\) then give the exact set equality
\[
E\cap(E+x)=C\cap(C+x)\qquad(x\in U_\theta).
\]
When \(C=-K_+\), the map \(z\mapsto x-z\) carries \(K_+\cap(K_++x)\) onto \((-K_+)\cap((-K_++x)\); it is a point reflection and preserves perimeter. Thus the two perimeter covariograms agree throughout \(U_\theta\) in both cases, including empty and singleton intersections.

Finally the union of these open neighborhoods contains the compact circle \(\partial DK_+\). A finite subcover exists, and its union has strictly positive distance from that circle to its closed complement (if the complement is empty, choose any \(\delta>0\)). Taking \(\delta\) smaller than this distance gives the asserted uniform collar, on both sides of the circle. Outside the difference body both functions vanish. This argument asserts no equality for translations farther into its interior. ∎

The integral of the full perimeter covariogram also agrees, by (3.1). Nevertheless Proposition 5.1 makes **no claim that the full covariograms agree in the interior**. It is an exact obstruction to a proposed proof from the recovered local and scalar data, not a counterexample to (Q).

The construction makes the obstruction explicit: smooth antipodal curvature patches can be switched across intervals on which the perturbations are flat. Analytic continuation rules out this construction in Theorem 4.3, but not in the arbitrary-body problem.

## 6. Homogeneity separates the components only with additional data

A final attempted reduction was to extract the ordinary area covariogram from a single mixed valuation measurement. The two homogeneous degrees give a precise two-measurement identity, but not such a reduction for the stated input.

### Proposition 6.1. Two-scale separation

Suppose \(\alpha>0\), and suppose, in addition to \(g_{K,\psi}\), the covariogram of the known homothetic copy \(tK\), for some \(t>0\), \(t\ne1\), is supplied for the same valuation. Then
\[
g_{K,A}(x)
=\frac{g_{tK,\psi}(tx)-t\,g_{K,\psi}(x)}{\alpha t(t-1)}. \tag{6.1}
\]
Consequently the two data sets determine \(K\) up to translation and point reflection by the ordinary planar area theorem.

**Proof.** The intersection defining the first term is \(t(K\cap(K+x))\). Its degree-one contribution scales by \(t\), its degree-two contribution by \(t^2\). Subtraction gives (6.1). ∎

This does not solve (Q). Evaluating the observed function at \(tx\) gives \(\psi(K\cap(K+tx))\), not \(\psi(tK\cap(tK+tx))\). The missing second measurement cannot be synthesized by rescaling the translation variable. When \(\alpha=0\), even the extra scaling measurement is redundant, so this route offers no solution for perimeter itself.

Equivalently, two independent linear combinations of the same degree-one valuation and area permit a two-by-two linear inversion. The original question supplies only one combination. The integral identity recovers total area, not the pointwise area-covariogram summand.

## 7. Outcome and precise remaining task

The bounded program has not produced either a proof of (Q) for all planar bodies or an unequal nontrivial pair with equal full covariograms for an admissible strictly monotone valuation.

What is established here:

* precise separation of the literal non-even question from the even published class, with an explicit example showing reflection need not preserve the data;
* area recovery and centrally symmetric determination for the normalized non-even mixed-area class;
* antipodal curvature-pair recovery in the \(C^2\) positive-curvature class;
* determination **within** the real-analytic positive-curvature class;
* a smooth, exact boundary-collar obstruction showing why those local data, support, area, and perimeter do not settle the global problem;
* a two-scale reduction that explicitly identifies the extra data it requires.

The outstanding geometric implication, already visible in the source's Section 7(2), is global consistency of local antipodal identifications using the full interior covariogram. In the smooth switching construction, the full interior data have not been proved equal or unequal. In a positive approach, one would need an additional global propagation theorem and a treatment of arbitrary nonsmooth competitors. For non-even valuations, one must also retain the oriented mixed-area contribution instead of presuming reflection symmetry. Neither polygon approximation nor the ordinary area theorem supplies these missing steps.

No formal proof-assistant verification is claimed. The accompanying read-only checker checks exact rational consequences, constants, degenerate cases, and meaningful negative inputs; it is not evidence of a general geometric theorem. Independent mathematical review is appropriate for the scoped lemmas and their hypotheses.
