# Finite-subgroup colimits for Out(F_n) and extended mapping class groups

**Problem:** 11000298 / AMR-109-0298, rank 1257.
**Attempt:** first substantive proof attempt, 10 October 2026 UTC.
**Outcome:** partial only. The general higher-rank question is not resolved. No novelty certification is claimed.

## 1. Exact target and source boundary

For each n≥2, let F_n be a free group of rank n. For each g≥1, let S_g be a closed, unmarked, connected orientable surface and let Mod±(S_g) include orientation-reversing mapping classes. The question is whether each specified group G can be the group-theoretic colimit of a finite diagram of its **actual finite subgroups**, whose arrows are inclusions, with the canonical map from that colimit to G an isomorphism.

This is Bridson–Vogtmann Question 6.2 in the retained author-hosted volume, printed331 / PDF338. Its chapter introduction, printed319 / PDF326, supplies n≥2 and g≥1 and the common GL(2,Z) endpoint. Both pages were visually inspected. The equivalent geometric formulation requires a **developable simple** complex of finite groups on a simply connected base. A proper action, a presentation generated by torsion, a non-simple complex, or extra relators imposed on an amalgam does not meet this requirement. In particular, the adjacent Aut(F_n) seven-stabilizer construction has additional gluing relations; the source explicitly distinguishes it from a simple complex. [Original source](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf).

Throughout, a colimit means a categorical colimit in groups, not a directed union, an arbitrary quotient, a homotopy colimit, or a diagram with conjugation maps in place of inclusions.

The results below establish:

- The classical affirmative endpoint Out(F_2)=Mod±(S_1)=GL(2,Z), with explicit finite subgroups of orders 8,12,4.
- An explicit infinite-order kernel element for the natural signed-permutation/theta-stabilizer inclusion amalgam, for every n≥2.
- A general rational degree-two obstruction and its exact limitation here.
- A dimension-one obstruction for n≥3 and g≥2.
- A counterexample to replacing the chosen finite subsystem by the system of all finite subgroups.

None of the last four items excludes an arbitrary finite higher-dimensional inclusion diagram for the two target families.

## 2. Classical positive endpoint, with actual inclusions

Let

S = [[0,-1],[1,0]], U = [[0,-1],[1,1]], R = [[0,1],[1,0]], Z = -I.

Set A=〈S,R〉, B=〈U,R〉 and C=〈Z,R〉, all as subgroups of GL(2,Z). Then

|A|=8, |B|=12, |C|=4, A∩B=C, and GL(2,Z) ≅ A *_C B.

Here the subscript on a dihedral group denotes its **order**: A≅D_8, B≅D_12, C≅C_2×C_2≅D_4.

### Proof

Direct multiplication gives S²=U³=Z, S⁴=U⁶=R²=I, RSR=S⁻¹, and RUR=U⁻¹. Thus A consists of S^i and S^iR (0≤i<4), while B consists of U^j and U^jR (0≤j<6). Their determinant-one parts intersect in {I,Z}; multiplying the determinant-minus-one parts by the common R shows A∩B={I,Z,R,ZR}=C. These are actual intersections, not merely abstract isomorphic edge groups.

The classical modular-group presentation is

SL(2,Z)=〈S,U | S⁴=1, S²=U³〉 = C_4 *_{C_2} C_6.

One way to recognize it is to quotient by the central order-two matrix Z, giving the classical PSL(2,Z)=C_2*C_3 presentation, and then retain the common lift S²=U³=Z. In the displayed abstract presentation S² is central and has order at most two; its matrix image is nontrivial, so the lift introduces no extra kernel.

Conjugation by R inverts S and U. It preserves their presentation, defines an involution, and GL(2,Z)=SL(2,Z)⋊〈R〉 because determinant gives the split quotient C_2. Consequently the complete presentation is

〈S,U,R | S⁴=1, U⁶=1, R²=1, RSR=S⁻¹, RUR=U⁻¹, S²=U³〉.

This is exactly the presentation of A *_C B. The interval with vertex groups A,B and edge group C is a developable simple graph of finite groups, with a tree as development. Its base is simply connected. The standard identifications of GL(2,Z) with Out(F_2) and the full mapping class group of the closed unmarked torus yield the claimed endpoint. ∎

This is credited classical mathematics, not new progress on n≥3 or g≥2. An explicit modern source is Debray–Dierigl–Heckman–Montero, §3.1, equations(3.2)–(3.13), especially(3.12), in [their author-hosted paper](https://adebray.github.io/papers/type_2b_duality_anomaly.pdf). The retained PDF was visually inspected at PDF11 / printed10. Its equation(3.11) omits R²=1 from the displayed presentation; R²=1 follows from its specified matrix and is present in its dihedral-group definitions. Our complete abstract presentation above explicitly includes it.

## 3. The standard two-stabilizer diagram has a nontrivial kernel

Fix a free basis x_1,…,x_n and compose automorphisms right to left. Let W_n be all signed basis permutations, of order 2^n n!. Let τ_i invert x_i and fix the other basis elements, and let σ=σ_12 interchange x_1,x_2. Define

η(x_1)=x_2⁻¹x_1, η(x_2)=x_2⁻¹, η(x_k)=x_k for k>2.

Let W_{n−2} act by signed permutations on x_3,…,x_n, with W_0 trivial. Set

H_n=〈σ,η〉×W_{n−2},   C_n=〈σ〉×W_{n−2}.

The images of these groups in Out(F_n) will carry the same notation.

### Theorem 3.1

For every n≥2:

1. H_n≅S_3×W_{n−2}, and W_n∩H_n=C_n, both in Aut(F_n) and in Out(F_n).
2. The canonical map q: P_n=W_n *_{C_n} H_n → Out(F_n) is surjective.
3. The element

r=((ητ_1)²τ_2)²

is in ker(q), but has infinite order in P_n.

In particular, this actual finite-subgroup diagram does not answer Question6.2. Adding local groups that are subgroups of W_n or H_n, with only their actual inclusion maps and retaining the displayed diagram, cannot repair it.

### Proof of(1)

η²=σ²=(ση)³=1 by free-word substitution. On the first two abelianized basis vectors their matrices, with images as columns, are

E=[[1,0],[-1,-1]], J=[[0,1],[1,0]].

These generate six distinct matrices, so 〈σ,η〉 is exactly S_3, not a smaller quotient. It commutes with W_{n−2}, and the actions have disjoint support, proving the direct product claim.

The six first-block matrices are

I, J, E, JE=[[-1,-1],[1,0]], EJ=[[0,1],[-1,-1]], JEJ=[[-1,-1],[0,1]].

Only I and J are signed permutation matrices. An element of H_n that is also a signed permutation therefore lies in C_n. The reverse inclusion is immediate. The homology actions are faithful on both W_n and H_n. If their outer classes coincide, their homology matrices coincide, so the same matrix intersection calculation identifies an element of C_n and faithfulness identifies the original automorphisms. This proves the intersection assertion also in Out(F_n). The local groups embed in Out(F_n): a finite-order inner automorphism of the centerless free group is trivial (Inn(F_n)≅F_n is torsion-free).

### Proof of(2)

The signed permutations together with η generate Aut(F_n): ητ_2 sends x_1 to x_2⁻¹x_1 and fixes all other basis elements. Signed permutations conjugate this to the elementary Nielsen moves, which together with signed permutations generate Aut(F_n). This is also the generating statement in Armstrong–Forrest–Vogtmann's complete presentation, valid with its low-rank corollaries. Passing to Out(F_n) proves surjectivity.

### Proof of(3)

Write a=x_1,b=x_2 and f=ητ_1. Then

f(a)=a⁻¹b, f(b)=b⁻¹;
f²(a)=b⁻¹ab⁻¹, f²(b)=b.

Hence v=f²τ_2 sends a to b⁻¹ab⁻¹, b to b⁻¹, and fixes x_k for k>2. Applying v twice fixes a,b and every other generator. Thus r=v²=1 already in Aut(F_n).

In P_n, however, the same word has the alternating form

η · τ_1 · η · (τ_1τ_2) · η · τ_1 · η · (τ_1τ_2).

Each η syllable belongs to H_n\C_n. Each τ_1 or τ_1τ_2 syllable belongs to W_n\C_n, since its first block is neither I nor J. The normal-form theorem for an amalgamated free product makes this a reduced word of length eight. Its first and last syllables lie in different factors, so every positive power is again reduced, of length eight times that power. Therefore r is nontrivial and has infinite order in P_n. ∎

### Corollary 3.2: subordinate groups do not fix this diagram

Suppose a finite diagram D contains W_n,H_n,C_n with the two displayed inclusions, and every further local group is a subgroup of W_n or H_n. Map each local group into P_n by inclusion into either containing factor. If both factors contain it, it is a subgroup of C_n, so the two choices coincide. These maps respect every actual inclusion arrow: when source and target are assigned to different factors, the source lies in their intersection C_n. They therefore induce p:colim(D)→P_n. The retained base diagram induces j:P_n→colim(D), and p∘j is the identity on the two generating factors, hence on P_n. Thus P_n is a retract of the enlarged colimit, and j(r) still has infinite order while its canonical image in Out(F_n) is trivial.

If each additional group has an inclusion arrow to a containing factor (in particular, for the full inclusion poset), its image is already determined by those factors and j is an isomorphism. Without those arrows, equality of colimits need not hold; the retraction is all that is required for the obstruction.

The theorem therefore rules out an entire support-restricted family, not just one omitted edge. It still permits any genuinely different finite subgroup in a larger diagram.

### Attribution and scope

The finite groups, η, and the relation r are taken from the established framework of Armstrong–Forrest–Vogtmann, [A presentation for Aut(F_n)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701937), J. Group Theory11(2008),267–276, DOI10.1515/JGT.2008.015. Their Theorem1, Corollaries1–2 and §3.3–3.7 supply the presentation and stabilizer interpretation. The normal-form kernel proof above is an explicit deduction from that prior structure; its novelty has not been established. It is not a proof that their full seven-stabilizer presentation has precisely this kernel, and it is not a proof that no other finite diagram exists.

## 4. A general degree-two obstruction

### Proposition 4.1

Suppose G is the colimit of a diagram of finite groups. Then H²(G;Q)=0, where Q has trivial G-action. The diagram need not be finite or consist of subgroups for this assertion.

More generally, if A is a torsion-free abelian group with trivial G-action, restriction to the local groups is injective:

H²(G;A) → ∏_i H²(F_i;A).

### Proof

Consider a central extension 1→A→E→G→1 whose restrictions to all F_i split. A splitting over a finite F_i is unique: two splittings differ by a homomorphism F_i→A, and such a homomorphism is zero because A is torsion-free. The splittings are therefore compatible with every arrow of the diagram. Its universal property produces s:G→E whose composite with E→G is the identity, since that composite is the identity on every local group and these generate the colimit. The extension splits. The usual classification of central extensions by H² proves injectivity.

For A=Q every central extension of a finite group splits. This can be checked without invoking a vanishing theorem: for a normalized cocycle c:F×F→Q put b(g)=|F|⁻¹∑_{k∈F}c(g,k). Summing the cocycle equation

c(g,h)+c(gh,k)=c(h,k)+c(g,hk)

over k gives c(g,h)=b(g)+b(h)−b(gh). Thus c is a coboundary, H²(F;Q)=0, and the injection has zero target. ∎

### Why this does not settle the specified families

The proposition is a necessary condition, not a sufficient condition. This attempt has not produced a nonzero rational H² class of either named group. A nonzero class of the orientation-preserving mapping class subgroup cannot simply be substituted. For example, if κ_1 is defined by integrating the square of the vertical Euler class over an oriented surface fiber, reversing fiber orientation changes the Euler class's sign and also reverses fiber integration; consequently κ_1 changes sign. Restriction from the extended group must be invariant under orientation-reversing conjugation, so an anti-invariant rational class cannot be its restriction. This diagnoses the failure of that proposed obstruction without making an unverified all-genus cohomology computation.

Likewise, stable cohomology or high-degree classes do not give a degree-two obstruction. No inference that all rational cohomology vanishes follows from Proposition4.1.

## 5. Why higher-dimensional bases are essential

### Proposition 5.1

For n≥3 and g≥2, neither Out(F_n) nor Mod±(S_g) is the fundamental group of a developable graph of finite groups. Hence an affirmative solution in these ranges needs a complex of dimension at least two.

### Proof

In Aut(F_n), let α send x_1 to x_1x_2 and let β send x_3 to x_2x_3, each fixing all other basis elements. They commute because they modify distinct generators and both fix x_2. The homology action of α^pβ^q has independently visible entries p and q, so their outer classes generate Z².

For S_g with g≥2, take nonseparating curves on two distinct handles. Their twists commute, and their powers have independent transvections on the corresponding two homology summands. Thus they also generate Z² in the full mapping class group.

A developable graph of finite groups acts on its Bass–Serre tree with finite vertex stabilizers. The displayed torsion-free Z² subgroup would act freely on that tree (after removing inversions by subdivision), so it would be a free group. Since Z² is not free, this is impossible. ∎

This elementary obstruction is consistent with established fixed-point results; it does not prohibit a simply connected base of dimension two or higher. The term “pushout” in the original question cannot be restricted to a single span of finite groups.

## 6. Enlarging to all finite subgroups is not an equivalent test

Let G=〈a,b | a²=b³=1〉=C_2*C_3. The finite subsystem {1,〈a〉,〈b〉}, with its two inclusions, already has colimit G. Put C=〈bab⁻¹〉, a distinct order-two subgroup.

Every finite subgroup of G fixes a vertex of the free-product Bass–Serre tree, and is therefore conjugate into one of the two factors. The two factors have prime order. Consequently all nontrivial finite subgroups have order2 or3; distinct such subgroups have trivial intersection and there are no inclusions between them. The colimit of **all** finite subgroups under inclusions is their free product.

In that colimit let c denote the generator of the factor C corresponding to bab⁻¹ in G. The word b a b⁻¹ c⁻¹ is reduced, since its factor sequence is 〈b〉,〈a〉,〈b〉,C. It is nontrivial there but maps to1 in G. Thus even a group with a successful finite subsystem need not equal the colimit of all its finite subgroups. No conjugation morphisms are included in this example.

This closes a tempting but invalid reduction. A chosen subsystem and the universal all-finite-subgroups diagram have different universal properties.

## 7. Remaining gap and stopping statement

For n≥3 and g≥2, this attempt supplies neither:

- a finite diagram with a proof that its canonical map to the target group is injective; nor
- an obstruction that applies to every possible finite inclusion diagram.

The standard two-stabilizer amalgam is explicitly too large, but its failure does not persist automatically after adding genuinely different finite subgroups. The degree-two obstruction has not been activated for the named extended groups. Existing proper-action spines and the general theory of simple complexes do not supply the missing strict fundamental domain or remove twisting. In particular, Petrosyan–Prytula's [later theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02161) assumes strict/simple structures in its applicable results; the mention of Outer space and Teichmüller spines in its final section is not a construction answering Question6.2.

The original bundled target therefore remains unresolved in this packet. The outcome is a source-grounded, checkable partial, with the known low-rank solution credited.
