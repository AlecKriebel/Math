# Rank-four Torelli restriction: first substantive attempt

Target: **11000312 / AMR-109-0312**, rank 1258. 10 October 2026 UTC.

**Outcome: unresolved after one substantive attempt.** This report proves exact reductions and an obstruction to detecting the desired class on a familiar infinite family of cycles. It does not prove or disprove Morita's restriction isomorphism, does not establish nonvanishing of the higher torsion, and makes no novelty claim for its elementary deductions. This is substantive attempt 1 of 5.

## 1. Exact question and conventions

Put

\[
G=\operatorname{Out}(F_4),\quad \Gamma=\operatorname{GL}(4,\mathbb Z),\quad
N=\operatorname{IOut}_4=\ker(G\xrightarrow{p}\Gamma).
\]

Nielsen surjectivity gives the exact sequence \(1\to N\xrightarrow{i}G\xrightarrow{p}\Gamma\to1\). Conjugation gives the \(\Gamma\)-action on \(H^*(N;\mathbb Q)\), since inner automorphisms of \(N\) act trivially on its cohomology. The map in question is restriction

\[
r=i^*:H^4(G;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\longrightarrow
V:=H^4(N;\mathbb Q)^\Gamma.
\]

Morita asks whether this is an isomorphism and whether its target is generated by a nonzero scalar normalization of the degree-four higher Franz–Reidemeister torsion \(\tau_2\). The original statement was visually inspected at printed p.359, one-based PDF p.366 in the [Farb volume](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf). The following degree-eight discussion introduces another problem and is excluded. Superscripts are cohomology; the first Morita **homology** class \(\mu_1\in H_4(G;\mathbb Q)\) is not itself the cohomology class \(\tau_2\).

The torsion is initially real. A scalar-normalized rational generator means that, after \(\mathbb Q\to\mathbb R\), some rational generator becomes a nonzero real multiple of \(\tau_2\); it does not mean an arbitrary real-valued class is automatically rational. No normalization constant is computed here.

## 2. Credited inputs and their scope

The deductions below use the following prior results, not new computations.

1. \(H^4(G;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\). This is part of the original question's baseline, corroborated by [Conant–Vogtmann, *Morita classes in the homology of automorphism groups of free groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406389), abstract/first page. We have not independently replayed that graph-homology computation.
2. \(H^4(\Gamma;\mathbb Q)=0\). [Brück–Miller–Patzt–Sroka–Wilson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11967), Theorem B and its immediately following transfer statement, give \(H^{\binom n2-2}(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb Z);\mathbb Q)=0\) for \(n\ge3\). At \(n=4\), the degree is four. The theorem statement was visually inspected on PDF p.5; its computational connectivity proof was not replayed.
3. \(H^5(\Gamma;\mathbb Q)=0\). [Church–Putman, *The codimension-one cohomology of SL_n Z*](https://msp.org/gt/2017/21-2/gt-v21-n2-p07-s.pdf), Theorem A, printed p.1000 / PDF p.2, gives \(H^{\binom n2-1}(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb Z);\mathbb Q)=0\) for \(n\ge3\). This directly supplies degree five at rank four. The theorem statement was visually inspected. The earlier Lee–Szczarba rank-four result is also credited by [Morita–Sakasai–Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06365), PDF p.17; the original 1978 proof was not inspected.
4. The central element \(-I\in\Gamma\) acts by \(-1\) on \(H^1(N;\mathbb Q)\). Here the quotient by inner automorphisms matters. [Kawazumi, *Cohomological aspects of Magnus expansions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505497), Theorems 6.1–6.2 and formula (6.5), identifies, equivariantly,

   \[
   H_1(N;\mathbb Q)\cong(H^*\otimes\Lambda^2H)/j(H),\qquad j(v)(h)=v\wedge h,
   \quad H=\mathbb Q^4.
   \]

   These formulas were visually inspected on PDF pp.25–26 (the proof and outer theorem). They follow from the IA abelianization theorem and right exactness of abelianization for \(F_4=\operatorname{Inn}(F_4)\to\operatorname{IA}_4\to N\). Explicitly \(j(v)=\sum_i e_i^*\otimes(v\wedge e_i)\). The contraction \(C(f\otimes a\wedge b)=f(a)b-f(b)a\) has \(Cj(v)=-(n-1)v\), so \(j\) is injective over \(\mathbb Q\). In rank four the quotient has dimension \(24-4=20\). The element \(-I\) acts by \(-1\) on \(H^*\otimes\Lambda^2H\), on its quotient, and on that quotient's dual \(H^1(N;\mathbb Q)\). This also agrees with the outer representation \(V_{1,1^2}\) recalled by [Habiro–Katada](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13458), p.12. No rank-four stable-range hypothesis is needed for this abelianization theorem.

The familiar Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre (LHS) cohomological spectral sequence for a group extension is used with its standard first-quadrant convergence and edge map. No splitting of this extension, finite-dimensionality of \(H^q(N;\mathbb Q)\), exactness of the invariant functor, or stable-range algebraicity is assumed.

## 3. Theorem A: exact degree-four edge obstruction

Write

\[
E_2^{p,q}=H^p(\Gamma;H^q(N;\mathbb Q))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(G;\mathbb Q),
\qquad d_r:E_r^{p,q}\to E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}.
\]

Introduce the actual differentials

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=d_2^{0,4}:V\longrightarrow H^2(\Gamma;H^3(N;\mathbb Q)),\\
b&=d_2^{1,3}:H^1(\Gamma;H^3(N;\mathbb Q))
 \longrightarrow H^3(\Gamma;H^2(N;\mathbb Q)),\\
c&=d_2^{0,3}:H^3(N;\mathbb Q)^\Gamma
 \longrightarrow H^2(\Gamma;H^2(N;\mathbb Q)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(E_3^{0,4}=\ker a\) and

\[
E_3^{3,2}=\operatorname{coker}b.
\]

Let

\[
e=d_3^{0,4}:\ker a\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}b.
\]

**Theorem A.** Under the credited inputs in §2,

\[
\operatorname{im}r=\ker e\subseteq\ker a\subseteq V, \tag{A1}
\]

and there is a natural short exact sequence of vector spaces

\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}c\longrightarrow\ker r
\longrightarrow\ker b\longrightarrow0. \tag{A2}
\]

Consequently \(r\) is an isomorphism if and only if all four conditions hold:

- \(a=0\);
- \(e=0\) on \(\ker a=V\);
- \(b\) is injective;
- \(c\) is surjective.

These are conditions on the actual unstable LHS differentials, not on unspecified stable approximations. The theorem neither verifies nor negates those conditions.

### Proof

First, if a central element \(z\) of a group acts as \(-1\) on a characteristic-zero coefficient module \(M\) and has order two, then \(H^p(\Gamma;M)=0\) for all \(p\). Indeed, for the extension \(1\to\langle z\rangle\to\Gamma\to\Gamma/\langle z\rangle\to1\), averaging makes higher cohomology of the finite kernel vanish. Its degree-zero invariants also vanish because \(m=-m\) implies \(m=0\). The LHS sequence has zero \(E_2\)-page. This argument works for infinite-dimensional modules too.

Apply this with \(z=-I\) and \(M=H^1(N;\mathbb Q)\). Thus the entire row \(E_2^{p,1}\), and hence that row on every later page, is zero.

There are no incoming differentials at \((0,4)\). The only possible outgoing ones are

\[
(0,4)\xrightarrow{d_2}(2,3),\quad
(0,4)\xrightarrow{d_3}(3,2),\quad
(0,4)\xrightarrow{d_4}(4,1),\quad
(0,4)\xrightarrow{d_5}(5,0).
\]

The last two targets vanish: the first by the zero row, the second because it is a subquotient of \(H^5(\Gamma;\mathbb Q)=0\). For \(r\ge6\) the target lies outside the first quadrant. Therefore \(E_\infty^{0,4}=\ker e\), viewed inside \(E_2^{0,4}=V\). The cohomological edge map is restriction, so its image is precisely this subspace, proving (A1).

At \((3,2)\), the \(d_2\) target \((5,1)\) vanishes and the only \(d_2\) source is \((1,3)\). This proves \(E_3^{3,2}=\operatorname{coker}b\).

At \((1,3)\), there are no incoming differentials. After \(d_2=b\), the possible targets are \((4,1)\) and \((5,0)\), both zero, followed by out-of-quadrant targets. Thus \(E_\infty^{1,3}=\ker b\).

At \((2,2)\), the only incoming differential is \(d_2=c\), because any later source has negative first coordinate. The \(d_2\) target \((4,1)\) and \(d_3\) target \((5,0)\) vanish; later targets have negative second coordinate. Hence \(E_\infty^{2,2}=\operatorname{coker}c\).

The diagonal terms \(E_\infty^{3,1}\) and \(E_\infty^{4,0}\) are zero, the latter using \(H^4(\Gamma;\mathbb Q)=0\). Let \(F^pH^4(G;\mathbb Q)\) be the decreasing LHS filtration. Then \(\ker r=F^1\), \(F^3=F^4=0\), \(F^2\cong E_\infty^{2,2}\), and \(F^1/F^2\cong E_\infty^{1,3}\). This gives (A2). The four-condition criterion follows immediately. ∎

**Numerical consequence.** All three summands in

\[
1=\dim\ker e+\dim\ker b+\dim\operatorname{coker}c \tag{A3}
\]

are finite, because they are associated-graded pieces of the one-dimensional \(H^4(G;\mathbb Q)\). This does not imply finite-dimensionality of \(V\), \(\ker a\), or the \(E_2\)-terms. In particular, knowing only that the source is one-dimensional cannot prove target dimension one or restriction nonzero.

## 4. Theorem B: the correct homological dual and coefficients

Let

\[
W=H_4(N;\mathbb Q)_\Gamma
=H_4(N;\mathbb Q)/\langle\gamma x-x\rangle,
\]

and let \(\bar i_*:W\to H_4(G;\mathbb Q)\) be induced by inclusion. This factors through coinvariants because conjugation in \(G\) acts trivially on \(H_4(G;\mathbb Q)\).

**Theorem B.** There are natural identifications

\[
H^4(N;\mathbb Q)^\Gamma=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(W,\mathbb Q),\qquad
H^4(N;\mathbb R)^\Gamma=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(W,\mathbb R).
\]

Under these identifications, rational restriction is the linear dual of \(\bar i_*\). Hence

\[
r\text{ is an isomorphism}\iff\bar i_*\text{ is an isomorphism}. \tag{B1}
\]

In particular, if \(r\) is an isomorphism, then \(W\cong\mathbb Q\), real invariant cohomology is one-dimensional, and every nonzero invariant real class is a real multiple of the image of a rational generator.

### Proof

Universal coefficients over a field give \(H^4(N;\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(H_4(N;\mathbb Q),\mathbb Q)\). The analogous real statement can be written \(H^4(N;\mathbb R)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(H_4(N;\mathbb Q),\mathbb R)\). Taking invariants means exactly annihilating all \(\gamma x-x\), giving the displayed formulas. Naturality of evaluation identifies the dual map with restriction.

For any linear map \(f:U\to U'\), its algebraic dual is an isomorphism exactly when \(f\) is. If \(f\) is not onto, a nonzero functional on its cokernel makes the dual noninjective. If \(f\) is not injective, a functional nonzero on a kernel vector, extended to \(U\), cannot be in the dual image. The converse is immediate. No finite-dimensional hypothesis is needed. Apply this to \(\bar i_*\). Finally, \(H_4(G;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\), so (B1) gives \(W\cong\mathbb Q\) when \(r\) is an isomorphism, and the real assertion follows. ∎

Thus the homological target that matters is a **coinvariant quotient**, not \(H_4(N;\mathbb Q)^\Gamma\). Also, the scalar-normalization clause becomes automatic from an isomorphism and nonvanishing of the specified invariant torsion class; it is not a third independent unknown under those hypotheses. It remains essential to prove that torsion is nonzero.

## 5. Theorem C: orientation reversal kills the standard top abelian cycles

This section gives a direct obstruction to an otherwise tempting detection argument.

For \(n\ge3\), fix the basis \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) of \(F_n\) and put \(w=[x_1,x_2]\). For each \(j\ge3\), let

\[
L_j(x_j)=wx_j,\qquad R_j(x_j)=x_jw,
\]

with every other basis element fixed. All these automorphisms commute. They lie in \(\operatorname{IA}_n\), and their outer classes generate a subgroup

\[
A=\langle L_3,R_3,\ldots,L_n,R_n\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^{2n-4}
\subseteq\operatorname{IOut}_n.
\]

This is the subgroup used in [Bestvina–Bux–Margalit, *Dimension of the Torelli group for Out(F_n)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0603177), p.2. Their top-homology theorem provides infinitely many independent cycles from specified conjugates of this construction. The theorem is credited; the elementary symmetry argument below is independently proved.

**Theorem C.** The image of the fundamental class \([A]\) is zero in both

\[
H_{2n-4}(\operatorname{IOut}_n;\mathbb Q)_{\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb Z)}
\quad\text{and}\quad H_{2n-4}(\operatorname{Out}(F_n);\mathbb Q).
\]

The same holds for every \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\)-conjugate of \(A\). Every \(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb Z)\)-invariant cohomology class of degree \(2n-4\) restricts to zero on each such torus.

### Proof

For completeness, the claimed embedding of \(A\) is elementary. A product of the generators fixes \(x_1,x_2\) and sends \(x_j\) to \(w^{p_j}x_jw^{q_j}\). If it is inner, its conjugating word commutes with both \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). Their centralizers in a free group are \(\langle x_1\rangle\) and \(\langle x_2\rangle\), with trivial intersection, so the conjugating word is trivial. Reduced free-product normal form in \(F(x_1,x_2)*\langle x_j\rangle\) then forces \(p_j=q_j=0\). This proves injectivity in the outer group. Commutativity follows because each generator fixes \(w\), different indices have disjoint affected basis elements, and left and right multiplication commute.

Let \(s\) invert \(x_3\) and fix all other basis elements. In particular, \(s(w)=w\) and \(s^2=1\). A direct calculation gives

\[
sL_3s^{-1}=R_3^{-1},\quad sR_3s^{-1}=L_3^{-1},\qquad
sL_js^{-1}=L_j,\quad sR_js^{-1}=R_j\quad(j>3).
\]

For example, \(sL_3s(x_3)=s(L_3(x_3^{-1}))=s(x_3^{-1}w^{-1})=x_3w^{-1}\). Thus \(s\) normalizes \(A\). On its ordered exponent lattice the first two coordinates transform by

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

and the others are fixed. Its determinant is \(-1\). On top homology of the torus \(BA\), conjugation by \(s\) therefore sends \([A]\) to \(-[A]\).

In the \(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb Z)\)-coinvariant quotient, the two classes are identified, so twice the image of \([A]\) is zero. Over \(\mathbb Q\) the image is zero. In \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\) homology, conjugation by \(s\) is inner and acts as the identity, giving the same conclusion. An invariant cohomology class pairs equally with \([A]\) and \(-[A]\), so its evaluation is zero. Since the top cohomology of \(A\) is one-dimensional, its restriction is zero. Conjugating the entire argument by any element of \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\) proves the remaining assertion. ∎

At \(n=4\), these are four-dimensional Torelli tori. Their infinite independent homology family does **not** imply a large invariant-cohomology target: its image in the relevant coinvariants vanishes. In particular it cannot establish nonvanishing of invariant \(\tau_2\). This does not say that these cycles generate all of \(H_4(N;\mathbb Q)\); Bestvina–Bux–Margalit's injection of their toy model is not a surjectivity theorem. Claiming total generation would introduce a new unsupported premise.

## 6. Theorem D: unstable primitive ambiguity is exactly restriction image

Work with normalized inhomogeneous real group cochains. Let \(b\in Z^5(\Gamma;\mathbb R)\) represent the rank-four restriction of the first Borel regulator. Because \(H^5(\Gamma;\mathbb R)=0\), choose \(x\in C^4(\Gamma;\mathbb R)\) with \(\delta x=b\). The real vanishing follows from the rational one by universal coefficients, without a finiteness assumption.

**Theorem D.** As \(z\in C^4(G;\mathbb R)\) varies over all solutions of \(\delta z=p^*b\), the set of cohomology classes \([i^*z]\) is exactly \(\operatorname{im}(r_{\mathbb R})\).

### Proof

The cochain \(u=z-p^*x\) is closed. Since \(p\circ i\) is the trivial homomorphism and \(x\) is normalized in positive degree, \(i^*p^*x=0\). Thus \([i^*z]=r_{\mathbb R}([u])\). Conversely, for every closed four-cochain \(u\) of \(G\), the cochain \(z=p^*x+u\) solves the equation and realizes \(r_{\mathbb R}([u])\). ∎

Therefore an arbitrary primitive of the pulled-back Borel cocycle cannot prove canonical nonzero torsion at this rank. One allowed primitive gives zero; adding a cocycle changes the restriction by precisely the unknown restriction image. If restriction is nonzero, choice-independence fails for this unrestricted primitive recipe. This does not invalidate Igusa's canonical higher torsion, which uses additional geometric/compatible data.

Morita–Sakasai–Suzuki Proposition 3.1 uses \(n\ge2k+4\) to eliminate exactly this ambiguity; for degree four, \(k=1\) requires \(n\ge6\). Their §5 supplies unstable secondary constructions but leaves the relevant nonzero pairings conjectural. Their \(k=1\) Borel-vanishing input is established, while the subsequent detection assertion is not. These are distinct logical steps.

## 7. Separate conditional torsion-permanence reduction

Here is a useful formal implication, explicitly separated from the unconditional deductions above to avoid silently extending a stable identification.

**Fibration input FI.** Suppose the canonical rank-four torsion is the fiber pullback of a universal degree-four class \(t\) under a map of fibrations

\[
(BN\to BG\to B\Gamma)\longrightarrow(F\to E\to B),
\]

where \(t\) survives to the fifth Serre page, its earlier differentials vanish, and \(d_5t=\beta_3\), with the pullback of \(\beta_3\) to \(B\Gamma\) equal to zero.

**Conditional proposition.** Under FI, \(\tau_2\in\operatorname{im}r_{\mathbb R}\). If additionally \(\tau_2\ne0\), rational restriction is injective and \(\tau_2\) is a real nonzero scalar multiple of the restriction of a rational generator. Surjectivity still requires the absence of other invariant classes, equivalently the conditions in Theorem A.

**Proof.** Naturality makes \(\tau_2\) survive \(d_2,d_3,d_4\), with fifth differential zero. There are no incoming differentials at \((0,4)\) and no outgoing ones after \(d_5\). It is a permanent cycle, hence an edge-image class. Since \(H^4(G;\mathbb R)\) is the real line obtained from \(H^4(G;\mathbb Q)\), a nonzero real restriction implies nonzero rational restriction. A nonzero map from a rational line is injective, and its real image is a line containing \(\tau_2\). ∎

The homotopy-commutative Whitehead-space diagram in Morita–Sakasai–Suzuki's proof of Theorem 3.5, PDF p.8, is the intended source of FI. It attributes the underlying geometric construction to Igusa's book, Proposition 8.5.6. This attempt inspected that reproduced diagram but did **not** independently inspect the book or verify the complete low-rank fibration-level identification. Accordingly this proposition is retained as a precise conditional reduction, not as a newly certified unconditional extension theorem. It is not used to assert nonzero torsion or to remove any unresolved condition from the outcome.
