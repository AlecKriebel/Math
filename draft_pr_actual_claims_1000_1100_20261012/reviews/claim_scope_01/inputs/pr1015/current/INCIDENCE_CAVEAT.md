# A covering family need not have generically finite incidence

Let V be a five-dimensional complex vector space, let W⊂V have dimension two, and set Z=P(V)=P⁴ and Y=P(W)=P¹. Then dim Z=n+p with n=p=2, and dim Y=p−1. The normal bundle is O_{P¹}(1)^{⊕3}, which is Griffiths-positive with its direct-sum Fubini–Study metric.

Let S=P(V/W)=P². A point [ℓ]∈S is a one-dimensional subspace of V/W. Its inverse image V_ℓ⊂V has dimension three. Put X_[ℓ]=P(V_ℓ). The universal linear-subspace construction gives an analytic family of compact two-cycles with smooth compact normal parameter space S. Every point of Z lies in at least one X_[ℓ]: a vector outside W determines [ℓ], and every vector in W lies in all V_ℓ.

But X_[ℓ]∩Y=Y for every parameter. Therefore the incidence locus Σ equals S, and the incidence projection {(s,y): y∈X_s∩Y}→S has one-dimensional fibres everywhere. No member meets Y in a finite set.

Consequently compactness, covering, analytic variation and positivity of N_{Y/Z} do not by themselves imply that this particular family has generically finite incidence with Y. In this example a(Z)=4≥2, so it is not a counterexample to the algebraic-dimension question. A successful general argument must handle this case or legitimately replace the family; it cannot silently apply a finite-incidence theorem to the supplied family.
