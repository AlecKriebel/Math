## Global model and order of limits

- Graphs are finite, connected, loopless, undirected, and have symmetric nonnegative weights with positive weighted degree at every vertex (LaTeX 143–147; PDF p. 2).
- Mutant fitness is fixed at `r>1`; residents have fitness one. Fixation is averaged over a uniformly random single-mutant start (LaTeX 148–176; PDF pp. 2–3).
- The complete-graph comparators have the same order `n=|V(G)|` as the candidate graph (LaTeX 179–185; PDF p. 3).
- Central quantifier order (LaTeX 102–110, 191–199, 324–336, 1348–1356):
  `exists one family {G_t}; for every fixed r in (1,R); there exists t_0(r); for every t>=t_0(r)` both strict inequalities hold. The graph and every weight may depend on `t`, never on the subsequently fixed `r`.
- Population scale: `C=t^4`, `q=t`, `m=floor(lambda_* t)`, `n=C+m+2q`, so `eta=q/C=t^-3=Theta(n^-3/4)` and `m/q -> lambda_*` (LaTeX 222–225, 1251–1270, 1460–1464).
- Limit order used in the proof: first obtain the exact finite-module trace as `epsilon -> 0` for fixed `(C,m,q)`; obtain compact-uniform population asymptotics as `t -> infinity`; then use an exact least-dyadic diagonal `epsilon_t` to transfer the separated result to one connected family (LaTeX 276–321, 368–440, 1348–1356).

## Certificate boundary

- The nine-vertex program exhausts 512 labelled configurations and 108 orbit fibres at one rational parameter point. It checks the orientation and declared transition aggregation at that point; it cannot prove arbitrary-size lumpability, the weak-cut limit, or any population asymptotic.
- The symbolic programs check algebraic identities, exact endpoint values, root counts, and marker integration. They do not certify stochastic estimates or any infinite quantifier.
- `verify_paper_claims.py` is a marker/integration regression audit, not a proof checker.
- The analytic proof and software claim the same finite certificate boundary. A successful replay is supporting reproducibility evidence only.
