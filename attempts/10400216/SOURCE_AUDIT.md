# Exact source and literature gate: 10400216

The original source is Ohtsuki's problem collection, printed p.536, Problem 12.11 by Dylan Thurston. Surrounding Section 12.4 is material. The request concerns a condition on Turaev shadow diagrams covering the shadows from alternating knots and yielding a hyperbolic-volume lower bound. Imported text after the question belongs to separate exposition. The pinned title is truncated, so the complete source takes precedence.

The problem is motivated by Lackenby's existing alternating-diagram theorem and by the potentially much greater efficiency of general shadow presentations. A restatement of that theorem on a marked planar subcase should not be presented as a new general solution. Hyperbolic volume means the knot/link exterior when it is hyperbolic; a general Gromov-norm formulation also needs the geometric-decomposition convention. Nonhyperbolic alternating torus knots cannot be silently included in a positive hyperbolic-volume claim.

## Full primary inputs read

- [Ohtsuki, publisher version](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf), Section 12.4, pp.533–536. Both screen and print versions are pinned. The question page was visually checked.
- [D. Thurston, arXiv:math/0311458v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311458v2), corresponding to Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), 337–362. Printed p.351 gives the canonical shadow cylinder and the signed half-corner gleam rule; pp.354–356 give the definitions and reconstruction. The corner diagram was visually checked. The construction retains an auxiliary boundary component. The prose complexity sentence on p.361 reverses the directions of the immediately preceding bounds; those directions are not used as a theorem here.
- [Costantino–Thurston, arXiv:math/0506577v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0506577v3), published Journal of Topology 1 (2008), 703–745, DOI 10.1112/jtopol/jtn017. Theorem 3.37 gives a lower bound for minimal shadow complexity in terms of Gromov norm, hence an upper volume bound from a presented shadow. Theorem 5.5 gives a quadratic upper bound for the minimum. Proposition 3.33 constructs the octahedral drilled manifold. None directly gives a volume lower bound from the vertex count of an arbitrary shadow.
- [Ishikawa–Koda, arXiv:1403.0596v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0596v1), full primary preprint. Section 5, Proposition 5.1 and Lemma 5.3, use branched special shadows of closed orientable manifolds and a minimum filling-slope hypothesis. Theorem 5.2 is the stronger complexity-equality statement. The published article is Math. Ann. 367 (2017), 1819–1863, DOI 10.1007/s00208-016-1403-4; later citations use Section 6. The attempted publisher PDF endpoint returned HTML, so all exact numbering and mathematical reliance here are bound to the complete arXiv edition, not an uninspected final PDF.
- [Lackenby, arXiv:math/0012185v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0012185v2), Theorem 1. Its lower bound is v_3(t(D)-2)/2 for a prime alternating hyperbolic link diagram; the stated twist-reduced improvement is stronger. The Agol–Thurston appendix improves the upper bound. These directions and the primeness/hyperbolicity assumptions are not interchangeable.

These sources provide known methods and exact hypotheses. No located current primary source has yet been verified to furnish the requested general shadow condition with the required alternating coverage. A negative search is not proof of present openness or novelty.
