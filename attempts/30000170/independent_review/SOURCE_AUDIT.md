## The exact question and geometric convention

[OWR 2/2005, Lewis contribution](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45976?nt=1), printed pp.104–106, contains the real-polynomial control example, the variational discussion, and Question 1. The final question concerns unique nearest stable polynomials near the repeated-root polynomial. The immediately preceding prose describes uniqueness of nearest points in the entire epigraph. The imported “equivalently” is absent. The question page was visually checked.

The same contribution is available as the author's [standalone primary PDF](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/aslewis/publications/oberwolfach.pdf). It confirms the wording and the separation between the motivating regularity discussion and the numbered nearest-stable question.

[Burke–Overton 2001](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~burke/papers/reprints/37-va-abs-poly.pdf), opening definitions, explicitly uses complex monic polynomials. [Burke–Lewis–Overton 2005](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~burke/papers/reprints/53-va-polys-GL.pdf), p.266, defines the ordinary real Euclidean coefficient inner product over the complex polynomial space; its exact-degree domain is not restricted to monics.

Lewis's [Eigenvalues and nonsmooth optimization](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/aslewis/publications/focm-paper.pdf), Section 1.5, defines the stable monic set in C^n. Its Section 1.6 extends the set/epigraph geometric regularity convention to functions. The [2007 survey](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/aslewis/publications/07-nonsmooth.pdf), Theorem 7.1 and the subsequent discussion on printed p.172, expressly states the complex-monic setting and again describes local unique nearest points in the whole epigraph.

These primary passages support the complex/geometric reading used in the candidate. They do not make the short OWR question's omitted field into an explicit quotation. The field inference and its real-only limitation should remain disclosed.

## Projection and normals

[Poliquin–Rockafellar–Thibault, Local differentiability of distance functions](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~rtr/papers/rtr170-LocalDiffDistance.pdf), Definition 1.1 and Proposition 1.2, printed p.5233, supply the uniform support condition in nearby points and nearby normals. Theorem 1.3, printed pp.5234–5235, includes equivalence of set prox-regularity and locally single-valued projection in finite dimension, clause (k). Closedness is necessary for that invocation and is separately checked for the monic set and the nonmonic compact localization.

[Poliquin–Rockafellar, A Calculus of Prox-Regularity](https://www.heldermann-verlag.de/jca/jca17/jca0849_b.pdf), Definition 1.1, p.203, is the finite-subgradient function definition. The discussion on p.205 identifies its epigraph normal as (v,-1). It does not identify it with regularity relative to every horizontal normal. This qualification is decisive for the source-target boundary.

The concluding function conjecture in Burke–Lewis–Overton 2005, p.291, cites Rockafellar–Wets Definition 13.27. The present horizontal-normal construction does not refute that formulation. The later Burke–Eaton source concerns subdifferential regularity, rather than supplying the missing finite-subgradient conclusion.
