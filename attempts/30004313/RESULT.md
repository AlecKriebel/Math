# Generalized left semi-braces: five-turn scoped partial results

**Original target: unsolved, 5/5 substantive author turns. Independent review pending.**

The exact target is Catino's Question 2, OWR 51/2019, printed p. 3234: characterize generalized left semi-braces with arbitrary associative addition and completely regular multiplication whose associated map satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation. This packet does not complete that characterization.

## Retained results

The complete proofs are in the five turn files, indexed by PROOF_COLLECTION.md.

1. For meet multiplication and addition a+b=f(b), compatibility is exactly retraction onto a lower subset; Yang–Baxter holds exactly when f is monotone and deflationary. With a top element, f(x)=x∧e.
2. For left-zero or right-zero multiplication, arbitrary compatible additions are classified and all their associated maps solve Yang–Baxter. A four-element rectangular-band example shows that combining these directions admits failures. For coinciding laws on a Rees-matrix presentation, compatibility is exactly row-column factorization of the sandwich matrix; the resulting map solves Yang–Baxter.
3. For arbitrary completely regular multiplication and addition a+b=f(b), compatibility is exactly retraction onto a left ideal. If f is also an endomorphism, Yang–Baxter is equivalent to the right-cryptogroup identity on its image.
4. Every retracted addition on an arbitrary Rees-matrix presentation is classified. The necessary and sufficient conditions are row preservation and f(f(b)^-b)=f(b)^0. The full parametrization uses idempotent group-valued residual set maps and column labels constant on residual fibers. Endomorphism is not necessary here.
5. Across arbitrary Clifford components, the retraction onto a fixed nonempty left ideal J is unique if it exists: f(b)=τ(b^0)b, where τ(e) is the greatest idempotent of J below e. Existence of all such greatest elements is necessary and sufficient. In this subclass, endomorphism is equivalent to Yang–Baxter.
6. The rectangular-band failure extends to genuinely two-argument additions on rectangular groups with any prescribed maximal group. On the same multiplicative structure, a different compatible addition gives a solution.

All statements retain their stated hypotheses. The group inverse is the commuting inverse in an element's maximal subgroup; inverse anti-multiplicativity is used only after it is justified in the Clifford case or inside the explicit group factors. The maps may be degenerate and non-bijective.

## Exact remaining gap

General two-argument associative additions on arbitrary completely regular multiplicative semigroups remain unclassified. The Rees retraction parametrization does not classify arbitrary additions even on one completely simple component. The Clifford theorem does not cover general non-Clifford component coupling. Published strong-semilattice constructions do not imply that every source structure admits an addition-compatible decomposition of that kind.

The finite examples show the necessity of retaining the addition and all three braid coordinates. They do not refute a classification request. No product-preservation criterion or ordinary/skew/weak-brace theorem is promoted to a universal source criterion.
