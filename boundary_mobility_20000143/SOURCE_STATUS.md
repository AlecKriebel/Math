## Objects and conventions

Work over C, as in Lehmann's Iitaka paper and the generated attempt. For an integral numerical k-cycle class α, ordinary mobility count is the largest number of general points contained in an effective cycle of that class. Formally it is the maximum over effective cycle families of the number of factors for which the incidence map to X^b is dominant. Reducible cycles and positive component multiplicities are allowed. This is neither a count of cycles nor the dimension of one chosen Hilbert component, and is not restricted to rational equivalence or integral representatives. Rational classes require divisible integral multiples; top-dimensional positive classes have infinite point capacity and are excluded from the finite-count discussion.

Write ρ(α) for the raw power-growth exponent of mc(mα). Modern Iitaka dimension is κ(α)=(n−k)ρ(α), when positive multiples are effective. In particular κ=2ρ for surfaces on these fourfolds. Mobility volume is limsup mc(mα)/(m^{n/(n−k)}/n!). Do not identify any of these three quantities.

For X=P²_x×P²_y, set A=p_x^*O(1), H=p_y^*O(1). The surface effective cone is generated by H², AH, A², all coefficients nonnegative. H² is a first-factor plane over a point of the second factor; A² is the opposite fiber; AH is the product of two lines. Boundary means at least one coefficient vanishes. The mixed open faces are aH²+bAH or aA²+bAH with a,b>0, not the H²,A² face.

For Q=Gr(2,4), the Plücker embedding identifies Q with a smooth quadric fourfold. Its surface cone has generators σ₂ and σ₁,₁, and P²=σ₂+σ₁,₁ for P the Plücker hyperplane. The two boundary rays are plane rulings: lines through a fixed point of P³ and lines lying in a fixed plane of P³. Both positive coefficients mean a big surface class.

## Initial claims examined in the mathematical audit

The following initial claims were targets of the separate mathematical audit. REPORT.md contains the accepted refinements and their precise scope:

- For nonnegative integral a,c with a+c>0: mc(m(aH²+cA²))=m(a+c).
- For positive integer m: mc(mAH)=2m. Beware that restricting to irreducible products would change the optimization; sums of line products are permitted by ordinary mobility.
- For positive integral a,b,d and α=aH²+bAH: mc(4ad²α) ≥ (bd+1) binom(2ad+2,2)−2. The claimed square-subsequence consequence is limsup mc(Mα)/M^{3/2} ≥ b√a/4. Factor interchange supplies the other face. It is a lower bound, not an exact leading constant or all-m formula.
- On Q: mc(mσ₂)=mc(mσ₁,₁)=m; and mc(d²P²)≥h_Q(d)−2 with h_Q(d)=(d+1)(d+2)²(d+3)/12.
- For β=uσ₂+vσ₁,₁, positive integral u,v and t=min(u,v), the attempt claims mc(td²β)≥h_Q(td)−2+td²(u+v−2t), hence mob(β)≥2t², without equality.

The attempt also contains other-dimensional scale checks, not the source problem's exclusive focus. Its proposed exact asymptotic behavior for interior classes remains a suggested direction, not an established conclusion.
