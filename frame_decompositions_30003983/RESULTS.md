# Decomposition of equiangular tight frames: scoped results, not a general solution

## Source and scope
The primary source is Alex Fink, Emily J. King, Cynthia Vinzant and Shayne Waldron, *Decomposing (equiangular) tight frames into (equiangular) tight frames for their spans*, Oberwolfach 2018, author PDF https://www.math.colostate.edu/~king/publications/OWR_2018_46.pdf. Its Question 0.2 asks about nontrivial disjoint decompositions, excluding the whole frame and all singletons. Its Proposition 0.3 already handles two blocks and Theorem 0.4 already handles three blocks. The final paragraph explicitly identifies higher-order and nested decompositions, dualities, and infinite families as ongoing topics. The intended classification question is NOT settled by saying some frames decompose and others do not. The printed two-block ETF assertion needs the nondegenerate dimension assumption d>1: collinear unit vectors in dimension one are an exception.

All deductions below assume a unit-norm ETF with n>d>1 and squared coherence q=(n-d)/(d(n-1)), so 0<q<1. Novelty of these elementary deductions is not asserted. The commuting-simplex characterization is a rigorously derived scoped structural statement, not a claim of previously unpublished mathematics.

## Approach 1: Gram and projection algebra
Let S contain m vectors and H be its principal Gram matrix. Write a=1+(m-1)q. It is tight for its rank-r span if and only if H²=aH. Indeed its trace and squared trace are m and m+m(m-1)q; a positive semidefinite matrix satisfying this equation has only eigenvalues 0,a, and conversely tightness gives this spectrum. Necessarily r=m/a.

For m<n, monotonicity of m/[1+(m-1)q] gives r<d and a<n/d=A. The complement's frame operator is AI-aP, strictly positive definite. Consequently n-m>=d. This is a useful additional necessary size bound, not just an integrality test.

For a partition into tight blocks of sizes m_i, ranks r_i and projections P_i, a_i=m_i/r_i, one has sum_i a_i P_i=A I and, for distinct blocks,
tr(P_i P_j)=r_i r_j q.
This follows by expanding the squared Frobenius norm of the cross-Gram matrix: it is m_i m_j q, and also a_i a_j tr(P_iP_j). Equal-size blocks therefore give an equichordal tight fusion frame; arbitrary ranks give weighted projection constraints. These conditions do not reconstruct the vector phases and are not sufficient for the general classification.

For two blocks the spectrum of A I-a_1P_1=a_2P_2 forces either full spans or orthogonal complementary spans. In the present nondegenerate ETF setting neither is possible for a proper nonempty partition. This recovers an EXISTING primary-source result, not new progress.

## Approach 2: integer and dimension obstructions
For a proper nonsingleton tight block, m>r, and hence m>=r+1. The Welch identity q=(m-r)/(r(m-1)) then implies q>=1/r², equivalently r>=1/sqrt(q), and m>=1+1/sqrt(q). Also m<=n-d as above.

Write q=a/b in lowest terms and D=b-a+am. The integer rank identity ar=b-b(b-a)/D implies D divides b(b-a). This divisibility is necessary, not sufficient: candidates must still have integer r=bm/D and satisfy the exact Gram equation. A finite divisor sieve is materially faster than blindly considering all subset sizes, but is not a conceptual solution of the open classification.

Every regular simplex is indecomposable: each proper subset is independent and nonorthogonal, whereas an independent unit-norm tight frame must be orthonormal. More generally, every full-spark nonorthogonal ETF is indecomposable, since a proper tight subset has rank r<d and contains a dependent r+1 subset of size at most d. These are scoped negative families, not disproof of the intended 'when' question.

Additional restrictions follow from elementary Gerzon bounds: rank-one projectors of m equiangular vectors with q<1 are linearly independent (their Hilbert–Schmidt Gram is (1-q)I+qJ). Thus m<=r(r+1)/2 over the reals and m<=r² over the complexes. Combined with the Welch identity, r<=1/q-2 in the real case and r<=1/q-1 in the complex case.

## Approach 3: exact examples and exhaustive small-instance certificates
The primary source's 9-vector complex 3-dimensional Hesse SIC was reconstructed exactly over Q(sqrt(-3)). `exact_check.py` verifies its ETF Gram identity and coherence and enumerates every possible proper tight subset (integrality first leaves size 3 only). `exact_results.json` contains all twelve tight triples, in zero-based indexing. Their disjoint collections give 12 decompositions with one triple and six singletons, 12 with two triples and three singletons, and 4 with three triples: 28 nontrivial unordered decompositions total. This is exact symbolic computation, with no tolerance or floating-point rank inference. Its incidence is the known Hesse/affine-plane configuration; no novelty is claimed.

For the source's harmonic ETF(n=15,d=8), q=1/16 and m<=7. Combining that size bound with the integer-rank test leaves only m=5, r=4 among nonsingleton blocks. Integrality alone also allows m=9, r=6; the bound m<=7 is what excludes that candidate. Its displayed three blocks therefore comprise regular 4-simplices and cannot themselves be further nontrivially decomposed. The source supplies existence, and `harmonic_exact_check.py` independently verifies the difference-set certificate (every nonzero difference occurs four times), block tightness via residues modulo 5, and all tight subsets by exact cyclotomic reduction modulo Phi_15. It finds exactly the three displayed residue-class simplices, hence 3 one-simplex-plus-singletons partitions, 3 two-simplex-plus-singletons partitions, and 1 complete three-simplex partition: 7 nontrivial partitions total. Details are in `harmonic_exact_results.json`.

The exact simplex test uses the following elementary equivalence: a size-(s+1) equiangular block of coherence 1/s is a regular simplex if and only if every three-cycle Gram product is -1/s³. To see sufficiency, fix one vector and switch the other phases so its inner products are -1/s; the triangle identities force every remaining off-diagonal Gram entry to be -1/s. The resulting Gram has eigenvalues (s+1)/s with multiplicity s and zero once. Necessity follows by reversing this phase switching for a unit-norm regular simplex.

## Approach 4: structural characterization in the commuting-simplex case
**Scoped theorem.** Suppose an ETF is partitioned into k regular simplices, each spanning dimension s, and their span projections commute pairwise. Up to a unitary change of coordinates, permutations, and the usual vector phase changes, it is a Steiner ETF built from a 2-(k,L,1) design and flat local simplices, where L=1+(k-1)/s is an integer at least 2. Conversely the usual Steiner construction has such a commuting simplex decomposition.

**Proof.** Each block has s+1 vectors and q=1/s². Simultaneously diagonalize the commuting projections. Each support has s coordinates. The cross-trace identity gives intersection size s²q=1 for every pair of distinct supports. Tightness gives sum_i P_i=(ks/d)I. Thus every coordinate belongs to exactly L=ks/d supports. Counting intersections of one support with all others gives s(L-1)=k-1, so L=1+(k-1)/s>=2. Regard supports as points and coordinates as subsets of points. Each coordinate subset has L points; each pair of points is in exactly one coordinate subset. This is the asserted design, with d=ks/L coordinates.

At a coordinate shared by two supports, every cross-inner-product is a product of the two vector entries at this unique shared coordinate and has modulus 1/s. Fixing a vector in one block shows all entries at that coordinate in the other block have a common nonzero modulus. Local tightness gives the sum of their s+1 squared moduli as (s+1)/s, so the common modulus is 1/sqrt(s). Every coordinate is shared (L>=2), hence each local simplex is flat on its support. If its local matrix is B, set C=sqrt(s)B. Its rows are orthogonal and have squared norm s+1. A unit kernel vector u has |u_j|=1/sqrt(s+1), since C*C=(s+1)(I-uu*) and its diagonal is s. Appending sqrt(s+1)u* to C therefore gives a complex Hadamard matrix. This recovers precisely the Steiner construction. Conversely flat local simplices on a Steiner incidence design give equal cross moduli 1/s and a tight sum because every coordinate occurs L times. QED.

This gives an actual higher-order classification under an additional hypothesis, but does NOT handle noncommuting projections or non-simplex blocks. The source's (15,8) example with k=3,s=4 has L=3/2, so its projections cannot commute: an explicit witness that the hypothesis misses legitimate decompositions. The (9,3) displayed coordinate decomposition has k=3,s=2,L=2 and belongs to the commuting class.

The standard Steiner existence family itself is established literature: Fickus–Mixon–Tremain, https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.5730. The simplex/binder and fusion-frame theory is developed in Fickus–Jasper–King–Mixon, https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07081, and harmonic refinements in https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09177. These publications are not evidence of a general arbitrary-block classification.

For a general commuting tight-block partition, tr(P_iP_j)=r_i r_j q must be an integer. No singleton block is possible: a commuting rank-one projection with positive cross trace is included in the other span, giving 1=r_j q, contradicting r_j<1/q. More generally no two distinct commuting block spans can be nested. Every coordinate belongs to at least two supports because a_i<A, whence sum_i r_i>=2d. If one block is a rank-s regular simplex, every other rank r_j is divisible by s, since tr(P_iP_j)=r_j/s is an integer. These are additional obstructions, not sufficiency criteria.

## Approach 5: duality, nested structure, and exact decision limits
For a normalized Naimark complement, G'=(nI-dG)/(n-d). A proper nonsingleton tight subset with H-spectrum a (multiplicity r), 0 (multiplicity m-r) yields H'-spectrum (n-da)/(n-d) (multiplicity r), n/(n-d) (multiplicity m-r). Both are positive and distinct. Thus the SAME index subset in the Naimark complement is independent and not tight. A subframe decomposition does not simply pass through Naimark complementation unchanged. This is a precise duality obstruction, not a classification of all decompositions of the complement.

For nested proper tight subframes S inside T, applying the complement-spans argument in span(T) gives |S|<=|T|-rank(T). Thus a regular simplex is terminal under nontrivial tight refinement. Candidate nested ranks/sizes obey the same coherence identity at every level and this additional redundancy bound. These constrain possible trees but do not establish their existence.

Because singleton blocks are allowed, existence of ANY nontrivial decomposition is equivalent to existence of ONE proper nonsingleton tight subset (fill remaining indices with singletons). For a fixed exact algebraic Gram matrix, enumerate admissible subsets and test H²=aH; exact-cover enumeration then finds all partitions and nested trees. This is a finite decision procedure, not a resolution of the intended structural classification, and no uniform complexity claim is made for arbitrary real input data.

Later paired-difference-set work, https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06639, constructs non-simplex subETFs in infinite families, often overlapping. Such overlap gives a nonsingleton-block-plus-singletons decomposition under the literal definition but does NOT supply an all-nonsingleton disjoint partition. The paper's Theorem 3.3 and quadratic examples must not be misreported as a general partition theorem. The binder study https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08879 addresses further simplex structure, again not arbitrary-block classification.

## Outcome
Five approaches yield exact obstructions, independently checkable small examples, a scoped commuting-simplex/Steiner characterization, and duality/nesting constraints. They do not resolve arbitrary higher-order or nested decompositions of all ETFs, nor the broader non-equiangular tight-frame case. General question remains UNSOLVED IN THIS INVESTIGATION.
