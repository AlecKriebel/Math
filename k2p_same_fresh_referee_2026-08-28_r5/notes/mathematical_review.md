### K2P domain, subdivision, rerooting, and restrictions

Inverse Fourier transformation of `(1,s,g,s)` gives exactly

`(1+2s+g, 1-g, 1-2s+g, 1-g)/4`,

so strict stochasticity on the positive-eigenvalue component is exactly `0<s<1`, `0<g<1`, and `g>2s-1`. The near-identity subdivision proof at `main.tex:377-394` is correct and avoids the false claim that arbitrary stochastic K2P square roots are physical.

The rerooting proof at `main.tex:396-413` uses reversibility only on ordinary edges and separately treats an old root adjacent to an incoming reticulation edge switching by switching. In the unchosen-parent switching the remaining stem subtends all leaves, carries total character zero, and contributes one. I found no missed inheritance-weight or root-adjacent multiplier.

For marginal restrictions, the product of two `D_plus` pairs satisfies the lower inequality because

`g1*g2 > (2s1-1)(2s2-1) = 2s1*s2-1 + 2(1-s1)(1-s2)`

when both `s_i>1/2`, and the lower condition is automatic otherwise. The section condition `max(S,G,2S-G,0)<r^(m-1)<1` is exactly what is needed. The complete visible-signature definition at `main.tex:728-778` correctly keeps paired `(s,g)` products, forgets only tensor-invisible coordinates, and complements inheritance only when a certified graph transport reverses the stored parent order.
