# A Weyl--Bell-diagonal divisibility theorem

**Status:** exact theorem for a precisely defined symmetry class.

**Scope:** this note treats arbitrary balanced reflections diagonal in one
fixed generalized Bell basis.  Equivalently, it treats arbitrary standard
exceptional reflections commuting with the two Bell stabilizers associated
with a primitive \(d\)-dimensional Weyl pair.  It does **not** assert that an
arbitrary exceptional reflection has this symmetry.

## 1. Statement

Let \(V=\mathbb C^d\), let
\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/d},\qquad
X|j\rangle=|j+1\rangle,\qquad
Z|j\rangle=\zeta^j|j\rangle,
\tag{1}
\]
and put
\[
|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle
=
\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_d}
\zeta^{bj}|j,j+a\rangle ,
\qquad (a,b)\in\mathbb Z_d^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Choose arbitrary signs \(h_{a,b}\in\{+1,-1\}\) with
\[
\sum_{a,b}h_{a,b}=0,
\tag{3}
\]
and define
\[
H=\sum_{a,b}h_{a,b}
|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle\langle\Phi_{a,b}|.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(P=(I-H)/2\) is a rank-\(d^2/2\) projection.

> **Theorem.**  If
> \[
> H_{12}H_{23}H_{12}-H_{23}H_{12}H_{23}
> =\frac13(H_{12}-H_{23}),
> \tag{5}
> \]
> then \(4\mid d\).

In particular, the complete rank-\(18\) generalized-Bell-diagonal ansatz is
empty at \(d=6\).  The proof is uniform in all \(2\bmod4\) dimensions and
does not enumerate the \(\binom{36}{18}\) sign tables.

The arithmetic obstruction is compatible with \(d=4\), but this note does
not claim that the published \(d=4\) matrix is Bell diagonal in the
conventions (1)--(2).  In fact, Section 5 gives a separate exhaustive exact
check that this fixed Bell-diagonal ansatz is empty at \(d=4\).

## 2. Bell stabilizers and automatic standardness

The vectors (2) obey
\[
(X\otimes X)|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle
=\zeta^{-b}|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle,
\qquad
(Z^{-1}\otimes Z)|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle
=\zeta^a|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle.
\tag{6}
\]
The two commuting stabilizers have simple joint spectrum, so (4) is
equivalent to
\[
[H,X\otimes X]=[H,Z^{-1}\otimes Z]=0
\tag{7}
\]
together with the Hermitian-involution condition.

Every Bell line is maximally entangled:
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_1
|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle\langle\Phi_{a,b}|
=
\operatorname{Tr}_2
|\Phi_{a,b}\rangle\langle\Phi_{a,b}|
=\frac1d I_d.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently, the balance condition (3) gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_1H=\operatorname{Tr}_2H=0.
\tag{9}
\]
No additional standardness assumption has been inserted.

## 3. The common Weyl algebra on three sites

Write
\[
S=H_{12},\qquad T=H_{23},\qquad U=ST.
\tag{10}
\]
Both \(S\) and \(T\), hence \(U\), commute with
\[
\mathsf X=X_1X_2X_3,\qquad
\mathsf Z=Z_1^{-1}Z_2Z_3^{-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, for \(S\) the non-spectator factors in (11) are respectively
\(X_1X_2\) and \(Z_1^{-1}Z_2\).  For \(T\), they are \(X_2X_3\) and the
inverse \(Z_2Z_3^{-1}\) of its second Bell stabilizer.

The convention \(ZX=\zeta XZ\) gives
\[
\mathsf X\mathsf Z=\zeta\,\mathsf Z\mathsf X,
\qquad
\mathsf X^d=\mathsf Z^d=I.
\tag{12}
\]
Because \(\zeta\) is primitive, the algebra generated by
\(\mathsf X,\mathsf Z\) is \(M_d(\mathbb C)\).  Here is a direct
multiplicity proof, avoiding an appeal to Stone--von Neumann.

The \(d^2\) monomials
\[
\mathsf X^r\mathsf Z^s,\qquad r,s\in\mathbb Z_d,
\tag{13}
\]
are trace orthogonal.  If \(r\ne0\), the simultaneous shift
\(\mathsf X^r\) has zero diagonal.  If \(r=0\) and \(s\ne0\), already the
first factor \(Z^{-s}\) has trace zero.  Relation (12) then makes distinct
monomials orthogonal after taking adjoints and products.  Hence the
generated algebra has dimension \(d^2\), and its standard matrix units can
also be recovered by Fourier projection onto the \(\mathsf Z\)-eigenspaces
and shifts by \(\mathsf X\).  It is therefore \(M_d(\mathbb C)\).

Its representation on the \(d^3\)-dimensional three-site space is a direct
sum of exactly \(d^2\) copies of its unique \(d\)-dimensional defining
module.  Since \(U\) commutes with this algebra, every eigenvalue
multiplicity of \(U\) is divisible by \(d\):
\[
d\mid\operatorname{mult}_U(\lambda)
\quad\text{for every eigenvalue }\lambda.
\tag{14}
\]

## 4. Spectrum forced by the cubic relation

Right-multiplying (5) by \(T\), using \(S^2=T^2=I\), and then multiplying
by \(U=ST\) gives
\[
U^3-I=\frac13(U^2-U).
\tag{15}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
(U-I)\left(U^2+\frac23U+I\right)=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Let
\[
\lambda_\pm=-\frac13\pm\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}i.
\tag{17}
\]
The three roots \(1,\lambda_+,\lambda_-\) are distinct.  Also
\[
SUS=TS=U^{-1},
\tag{18}
\]
so the \(\lambda_+\)- and \(\lambda_-\)-multiplicities agree.  Denote the
three multiplicities by \(m_1,m,m\).

The zero marginals (9) give the exact trace contraction
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{123}(H_{12}H_{23})
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{2}\!\left[
(\operatorname{Tr}_{1}H)
(\operatorname{Tr}_{2}H)
\right]
=0.
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(\lambda_++\lambda_-=-2/3\),
\[
m_1-\frac23m=0,
\qquad
m_1+2m=d^3.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore
\[
m_1=\frac{d^3}{4},
\qquad
m=\frac{3d^3}{8}.
\tag{21}
\]

Now combine (14) and (21).  Write \(m=dk\).  Then
\[
8k=3d^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(8\mid 3d^2\).  Since \(3\) is odd, \(v_2(d)\ge2\), proving
\[
\boxed{4\mid d.}
\tag{23}
\]

For \(d=6\), the forced multiplicities are
\[
(m_1,m_+,m_-)=(54,81,81).
\tag{24}
\]
The nonreal multiplicities \(81\) are not divisible by \(6\), which is the
explicit contradiction.

For comparison, at \(d=4\) the forced triple is
\[
(16,24,24),
\tag{25}
\]
and all entries are divisible by \(4\).  This calibrates the obstruction:
it detects precisely the missing factor of two in dimensions
\(d\equiv2\pmod4\).  It makes no positive existence claim inside the
Bell-diagonal ansatz at \(d=4\).

## 5. Exact \(d=4\) calibration

The divisibility theorem is silent at \(d=4\).  This smallest remaining
Bell-diagonal case is finite enough to settle exactly.

For a sign table \(h_{a,b}\), put
\[
F_a(t)=\sum_{b=0}^3h_{a,b}i^{bt}.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the pair coefficient of \(H\) along the preserved difference \(a\)
is \(F_a(t)/4\).  Applied to the input
\[
|0,a,a+c\rangle
\]
and the output
\[
|r,r+a+p,a+c+p\rangle,
\]
the cubic residual, multiplied by \(192\), is the Gaussian integer
\[
\begin{aligned}
&3\sum_{t=0}^3
F_a(t)F_{c-t}(p)F_{a+p}(r-t)\\
&\quad
-3\sum_{u=0}^3
F_c(u)F_{a+u}(r)F_{c-r}(p-u)\\
&\quad
-16\left(\delta_{p,0}F_a(r)-\delta_{r,0}F_c(p)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
All subscripts and arguments are modulo four.  Global simultaneous
translation of the three computational coordinates shows that (27), for
all \(a,c,p,r\), covers every possibly nonzero matrix coefficient.

The independent verifier
`verifiers/verify_d4_bell_diagonal_exhaustive.py` first compares (27)
against every entry of a direct \(64\times64\) tensor-matrix residual for
one calibration table.  It then evaluates (27) over the Gaussian integers
for all
\[
\binom{16}{8}=12{,}870
\tag{28}
\]
balanced sign tables, without quotienting by complement or any other
symmetry.  The exact survivor count is zero.

> **Finite corollary.**  No balanced reflection diagonal in the fixed
> \(d=4\) Bell basis (2) satisfies the exceptional cubic relation.

This corollary does not conflict with the published \(d=4\) witness: it
proves that the witness is outside this specific Bell-diagonal symmetry
class.

## 6. Relation to earlier project tracks

This theorem is not C18: the diagonal-regular group-relative ansatz in that
claim is controlled by one group coordinate and was excluded by its leg
commutant.  A generic Bell-diagonal reflection can have scalar left and
right leg commutants.

It is also not C20: that claim constructs a Weyl-diagonal **channel**
countermodel which deliberately need not arise from one tensor-local
projection.  The present proof uses the spatially shifted copies of one
actual \(H\), through the common three-site Weyl algebra (11).

The result remains an ansatz theorem.  Extending it to every exceptional
solution would require proving that an arbitrary solution carries a
primitive common Weyl action of the form (11), and no such universal
symmetry statement is proved here.
