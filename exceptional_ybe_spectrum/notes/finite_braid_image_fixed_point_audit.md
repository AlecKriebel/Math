# Finite braid image does not force an algebraic fixed point

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Status:** PROVED assumption audit; no unrestricted \(d=6\) existence or
nonexistence theorem
**Scope:** arbitrary exceptional solutions for the finite-image and
nonergodicity statements; an exact non-exceptional Yang--Baxter family for
the logical counterexample

## 1. Conclusion

There are two correct universal facts about an exceptional solution
\[
R=-P+q(I-P),\qquad q=e^{i\pi/3},\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}_1P=\operatorname{Tr}_2P=\frac d2I_d.
\tag{1}
\]

1. Every finite-strand braid image \(\rho_R(B_n)\) is finite.
2. For \(d>2\), the associated Yang--Baxter endomorphism is not ergodic on
   the hyperfinite type-\(\mathrm{II}_1\) factor.

These facts, even together with the exact exceptional scalar partial
trace, do **not** imply a nontrivial finite-stage fixed point or a
nontrivial one-leg commutant.  The implication already fails for a
particularly elementary family of unitary Yang--Baxter matrices.  That
family has, simultaneously:

- finite braid image at every strand number;
- finite-order local braid generators;
- exactly the same scalar normalized partial trace as (1);
- scalar left and right one-leg commutants;
- no nontrivial algebraic fixed points;
- a nontrivial von Neumann fixed algebra.

Thus finite image cannot by itself upgrade Conti--Lechner nonergodicity to
the leg projection needed by the divisibility argument.  The countermodel
does have an opposite eigenvalue pair, whereas the exceptional spectrum
does not.  It therefore does not refute a stronger theorem using
no-opposite-spectrum and its consequence, irreducibility of the shifted
braid subfactor.  No such bridge appears in the audited sources.

## 2. The two universal facts

Put
\[
\tau_1=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}
\]
on \(M_d\).  Automatic standardness gives
\[
\phi_R(R)
=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}_2R
=qI-\frac{q+1}{d}\operatorname{Tr}_2P
=\frac{q-1}{2}I.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(q-1=e^{2\pi i/3}\),
\[
\boxed{\|\phi_R(R)\|_{2,\tau_1}^2=\frac14.}
\tag{3}
\]
Conti--Lechner, Proposition 7.12, proves the necessary ergodicity identity
\[
\|\phi_R(R)\|_{2,\tau_1}^2=\frac1{d^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently every exceptional solution with \(d>2\) is nonergodic:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N^{\lambda_R}
=\mathcal L_R'\cap\mathcal N\ne\mathbb C.}
\tag{5}
\]

The finite-image statement is also genuine, but it does not follow merely
from finite dimensionality of \(H_n(3,6)\).  A finite-dimensional complex
algebra can contain infinite finitely generated subgroups of its unit
group.  The needed extra theorem is Rowell's main theorem in *A
quaternionic braid representation (after Goldschmidt and Jones)*:
the subgroup generated by the canonical Hecke generators in
\(H_n(3,6)\) is finite for every \(n\).

Every exceptional tensor representation factors through \(H_n(3,6)\).
Therefore its braid image is a homomorphic image of Rowell's finite
canonical group:
\[
\boxed{|\rho_R(B_n)|<\infty\quad\text{for every fixed }n.}
\tag{6}
\]
Faithfulness of the tensor representation makes this image isomorphic to
the canonical group, but faithfulness is not needed for finiteness.

## 3. An exact countermodel matching the partial trace

Let \(m\ge2\), let
\[
V=A\oplus B,\qquad \dim A=\dim B=m,\qquad d=2m,
\]
and define
\[
U_m=I_m\boxplus I_m.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(U_m\) acts as the identity on \(A\otimes A\) and \(B\otimes B\),
and as the tensor flip on
\[
(A\otimes B)\oplus(B\otimes A).
\]
The box-sum construction gives a unitary involutive Yang--Baxter matrix:
\[
U_m^*=U_m,\qquad U_m^2=I,\qquad
(U_m)_{12}(U_m)_{23}(U_m)_{12}
=(U_m)_{23}(U_m)_{12}(U_m)_{23}.
\tag{8}
\]

Set
\[
\omega=q-1=e^{2\pi i/3},\qquad S_m=\omega U_m.
\tag{9}
\]
Scalar multiplication preserves the braid equation, and
\[
S_m^6=I.
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}_2U_m
=\frac12I_d
=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}_1U_m,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}_2S_m
=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}_1S_m
=\frac{q-1}{2}I_d.
}
\tag{11}
\]
This is exactly the exceptional normalized partial trace (2), not just
the same norm.

### 3.1 Finite braid image

The representation generated by the \((U_m)_i\) factors through the
symmetric group because the generators satisfy the braid relations and
\((U_m)_i^2=I\).  For a braid word \(\beta\), let
\(\operatorname{exp}(\beta)\) be its exponent sum and let
\(\overline\beta\in S_n\) be its permutation.  Then
\[
\rho_{S_m}(\beta)
=\omega^{\operatorname{exp}(\beta)}
 \rho_{U_m}(\overline\beta).
\tag{12}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{|\rho_{S_m}(B_n)|\le 3n!}
\tag{13}
\]
for every \(n\).

### 3.2 Scalar one-leg commutants

Suppose
\[
[X\otimes I,U_m]=0,\qquad
X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{AA}&X_{AB}\\X_{BA}&X_{BB}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Apply the commutation relation to \(a\otimes b\), with \(a\in A\),
\(b\in B\), and compare the four orthogonal color sectors.  The
\(A\otimes A\) and \(B\otimes B\) components force
\[
X_{AB}=X_{BA}=0.
\]
The remaining mixed component gives
\[
b\otimes X_{AA}a=X_{BB}b\otimes a
\qquad(a\in A,\ b\in B),
\]
and therefore
\[
X_{AA}=cI_A,\qquad X_{BB}=cI_B.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\{X:[X\otimes I,S_m]=0\}
=\{X:[I\otimes X,S_m]=0\}
=\mathbb CI_d.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Conti--Lechner, Proposition 7.10, identifies this one-site condition with
the absence of all nontrivial algebraic fixed points (and proves that
algebraic fixed points of a non-scalar Yang--Baxter matrix lie in the UHF
algebra).  Hence
\[
{}^0\mathcal O_d^{\lambda_{S_m}}=\mathbb C.
\tag{15}
\]

On the other hand, (11) gives
\[
\|\phi_{S_m}(S_m)\|_2^2=\frac14\ne\frac1{d^2}
\qquad(m\ge2).
\]
Proposition 7.12 therefore makes \(S_m\) nonergodic:
\[
\mathcal N^{\lambda_{S_m}}\ne\mathbb C.
\tag{16}
\]
All nontrivial fixed points in (16) are necessarily nonalgebraic.  Taking
\(m=3\) gives this countermodel in the unresolved local dimension \(d=6\).
It is deliberately **not** an exceptional witness: its two eigenvalues
are the antipodal pair \(\{\omega,-\omega\}\), not
\(\{q,-1\}\).  In particular, it is not covered by Lechner's
no-opposite-spectrum theorem for irreducibility of
\(\varphi(\mathcal L_R)\subset\mathcal L_R\).

## 4. Why finite group averaging does not repair the argument

At each fixed \(n\), finiteness of \(\rho_R(B_n)\) supplies a group-average
conditional expectation onto its finite-stage commutant.  The global
fixed algebra is instead
\[
\mathcal N^{\lambda_R}
=\left(\bigvee_{n\ge2}\rho_R(B_n)''\right)'\cap\mathcal N,
\tag{17}
\]
an infinite decreasing intersection of commutants.  The finite groups
grow with \(n\); there is no single finite group whose average is the
global expectation.

To turn (5) into an algebraic fixed point one would need, for example,
invariance under the tensor-prefix martingale expectations
\[
E_k:\mathcal N\longrightarrow M_d^{\otimes k}:
\qquad
E_k(\mathcal N^{\lambda_R})
\subseteq\mathcal N^{\lambda_R}.
\tag{18}
\]
Then martingale convergence would make the von Neumann fixed algebra the
weak closure of its algebraic fixed points.  But (18) is not implied by
finite braid images.  The crossing generator \(R_{k,k+1}\) straddles the
traced boundary, and group averaging at level \(n\) does not commute with
\(E_k\).  The family \(S_m\) proves that finite image, finite generator
order, Yang--Baxter locality, scalar one-leg partial traces, and the exact
value of the exceptional partial trace do not by themselves supply (18).

Likewise, finite depth of the horizontal inclusion
\[
\varphi(\mathcal L_R)\subset\mathcal L_R
\]
does not identify the vertical relative commutant
\(\mathcal L_R'\cap\mathcal N\) with the union of its finite tensor-stage
intersections.

## 5. Consequence for the spectrum program

The proposed route is closed in its finite-image-only form:
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite braid images}+\text{nonergodicity}
\;\not\Longrightarrow\;
\text{nontrivial one-leg commutant}.
}
\tag{19}
\]
The sharp remaining route is to use the hypothesis absent from the
countermodel:
\[
\sigma(R)\cap-\sigma(R)=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi(\mathcal L_R)'\cap\mathcal L_R=\mathbb C.
\tag{20}
\]
This is horizontal braid-subfactor irreducibility.  The desired conclusion
concerns the vertical relative commutant
\[
\mathcal L_R'\cap\mathcal N=\mathcal N^{\lambda_R}.
\tag{21}
\]
Conti--Lechner carefully distinguish these relative commutants, and the
audited sources contain no theorem that turns (20), finite braid image,
finite depth, or equality of the two horizontal Jones indices into
algebraicity of (21).  Their \(d=2\) finite-image matrix \(R_4\) is
nonergodic and has nontrivial algebraic fixed points, so it is compatible
with such a theorem but does not prove it.

It therefore remains legitimate to seek an exceptional-specific proof of
(18), perhaps using (20) and the detailed quaternionic embedding of
\(H_n(3,6)\) together with tensor locality.  Such a theorem would be new
input; it is not a consequence of Rowell's finite-image theorem or of the
currently audited Conti--Lechner fixed-point results.
