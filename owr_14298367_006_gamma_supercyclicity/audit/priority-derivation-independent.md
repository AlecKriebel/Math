## Verified source

Arafat Abbar and Yulia Kuznetsova, *Γ-supercyclicity of families of translates in weighted Lp-spaces on locally compact groups*, [arXiv:2005.11230v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11230v2). The arXiv record dates v1 to 22 May 2020 and v2 to 28 October 2020, and lists publication in *Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications* 495(1) (2021), DOI [10.1016/j.jmaa.2020.124709](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2020.124709). Theorem B appears on PDF page 3 and is restated/proved on pp. 21–23. I independently read its hypotheses, the convention for translation, and the weight convention in the locally preserved PDF/text.

For second-countable locally compact noncompact G, the theorem concerns the space with norm (∫|f|^p ω^p)^(1/p). Translation is (T_s f)(t)=f(s^(-1)t). The weight must be positive, locally p-integrable, and make all translations indexed by S bounded; Γ must contain a nonzero element. If the subgroup generated by S is abelian, Γ-supercyclicity of this translation family is equivalent to the theorem's simultaneous two-sided small-weight test on almost all of each compact set.

## 1. Scalar specialization

Let c_j>0, 1≤p<∞, and let B be bounded bilateral translation on ℓp(Z,c), (Bx)_j=x_(j+1). Set

\[
G_1=\mathbb Z,\qquad S_1=\{-n:n\ge0\},\qquad
\omega_1(j)=c_j^{1/p}.
\]

Use counting Haar measure. All group hypotheses hold. Compact subsets are finite, so local p-integrability is automatic. The weight is S1-admissible because T_(-n)=B^n is bounded. The sign is important: the paper's convention gives T_(-n)x(j)=x(j+n), as required.

Assume B is Γ-supercyclic. Let F⊂Z be finite and nonempty and let δ>0. Apply Theorem B with a tolerance ε<min(1,δ). Its compact exceptional-set conclusion supplies E⊂F with card(F\E)<ε. Consequently E=F. There exist n≥0 and λ∈Γ\{0} such that

\[
|\lambda|\max_{j\in F} c_{j-n}^{1/p}<\delta,
\qquad
|\lambda|^{-1}\max_{j\in F} c_{j+n}^{1/p}<\delta.\tag{1}
\]

Thus Theorem B supplies the scalar tail property needed below without the candidate's tail extraction argument. Arbitrarily large n are not required for this derivation: Theorem B already supplies its own sufficiency implication. If desired, large n follow because, for bounded n in a finite set, the product of the two positive maxima in (1) has a positive lower bound.

## 2. A second discrete coordinate supplies ℓp-valued amplification

Now set

\[
G_2=\mathbb Z^2,\quad S_2=\{(-n,0):n\ge0\},\quad
\omega_2(j,k)=c_j^{1/p}.
\]

This is a second-countable locally compact noncompact abelian group. Positivity and local p-integrability are immediate. Translation by (-n,0) has exactly the same norm as B^n: the second coordinate contributes an additional sum without changing the weight ratios. Hence S2-admissibility also holds.

Let K⊂Z² be compact, hence finite, and let ε>0. If K is empty the test is immediate. Otherwise its first-coordinate projection F is finite and nonempty. Choose n and λ from (1) with δ=ε. Take E=K in Theorem B. The deleted measure is zero, while

\[
\max_{(j,k)\in K}\omega_2((-n,0)+(j,k))
=\max_{j\in F}c_{j-n}^{1/p},
\]

and the inverse-translation maximum equals max_(j∈F)c_(j+n)^(1/p). Thus both tests in Theorem B hold. Its sufficiency implication produces a Γ-supercyclic vector for translation in the first coordinate on Lp(Z²,ω2).

Rearranging the nonnegative double sum identifies this space isometrically with

\[
\ell^p\bigl(\mathbb Z,c;\ell^p(\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]

The first-coordinate translation is precisely the ℓp(Z)-valued amplification of B.

## 3. Dense-range factors supply every separable Banach coefficient space

Let E be any nonzero separable complex Banach space. Choose unit vectors e1,e2,… whose complex linear span is dense in E. Repetition is allowed, including when E is finite dimensional. Define

\[
R:\ell^p(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow E,\qquad
R(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}a_k e_k.
\]

The series converges absolutely in E, since

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}|a_k|\le
\Big(\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}\Big)\sup_k|a_k|
\le\|a\|_p.
\]

So R is a bounded complex-linear map of norm at most 1, valid also for p=1. Its range contains each e_k because R(2^k δ_k)=e_k. Its range is consequently dense in E. Surjectivity is neither asserted nor needed.

Define the coordinatewise map

\[
\mathcal R:\ell^p(\mathbb Z,c;\ell^p(\mathbb Z))
\longrightarrow\ell^p(\mathbb Z,c;E),\qquad
(\mathcal Ru)_j=R(u_j).
\]

It is bounded by the coordinate norm estimate. It has dense range: every finitely supported sequence with values in the span of the e_k has an exact preimage with only finitely many nonzero coordinates, and such sequences are dense in the target. It commutes with translation.

A continuous linear intertwiner with dense range transfers Γ-supercyclicity. Indeed, if D is a dense Γ-orbit in the source, continuity puts the entire image of the source in the closure of the image of D, and density of the range then makes that image orbit dense in the target. This argument preserves the same scalars λ∈Γ and does not require Γ to be measurable, closed, multiplicatively stable, or closed under division.

Therefore B Γ-supercyclic implies its E-valued amplification is Γ-supercyclic. The converse follows from any nonzero bounded complex-linear functional E→C, extended coordinatewise, which is an onto intertwiner. If Γ\{0} is empty, both operators fail to be Γ-supercyclic and the equivalence remains true.

## 4. Consequence for the candidate and the priority conclusion

Steps 1–3 recover the candidate's full amplification statement from a theorem available in 2020. The proof even needs only bounded forward bilateral translation; the source problem supplies bounded invertibility as well. Applying the composition-space conjugacy verified separately in `source-match.md` gives the exact source-setting equivalence of Γ-supercyclicity for Tf and Bw.

No circular use of the candidate's amplification lemma or construction occurs in this derivation. No equality of arbitrary separable Banach spaces with ℓp is assumed; the bounded dense-range map is what permits passage to E. The discrete group enlargement preserves the same exponent p, the same weight c_j, and the same allowed scalars Γ.

The alternative G=Z×T/Fourier-coefficient argument is also valid with normalized Haar measure on T, but the discrete proof avoids integration and is easier to verify.
