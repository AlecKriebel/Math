\begin{abstract}
We record an affirmative answer to the question on arbitrary-$\Gamma$ supercyclicity posed in Oberwolfach Report 19/2024. In the report's separable $L^p$ setting, a dissipative composition operator of bounded distortion is $\gs$ if and only if its associated bilateral weighted shift is $\gs$, for every subset $\Gamma\subseteq\C$. The observation follows from existing results of Abbar and of Abbar--Kuznetsova, together with a sequence-space representation of the composition operator. We give a short direct proof to make the application explicit. Neither the scalar criterion nor the amplification mechanism is claimed as new.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the statement}

For a bounded operator $T$ on a complex Banach space and a set $\Gamma\subseteq\C$, call $T$ \emph{$\gs$} if some vector $x$ has dense orbit
\[
\{\lambda T^n x: n\ge0,\ \lambda\in\Gamma\}.
\]
This terminology follows Charpentier, Ernst, and Menet~\cite{CEM}. In her contribution to Oberwolfach Report 19/2024, D'Aniello, reporting joint work with Maiuriello, asks whether the equivalence of this property for a dissipative composition operator of bounded distortion and its associated shift holds for arbitrary $\Gamma$~\cite[p.~1083]{OWR}. The cases of ordinary hypercyclicity and real or complex supercyclicity are part of the preceding work~\cite{DDM,DM}.

We use the standing separability convention in that report. Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$ be a $\sigma$-finite measure space with separable complex $L^p(X,\mu)$, where $1\le p<\infty$. Let $f:X\to X$ be a bijective bimeasurable transformation such that, for finite constants $A,B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded}
\mu(f^{-1}C)\le A\mu(C),\qquad \mu(fC)\le B\mu(C)
\quad(C\in\mathcal B).
\end{equation}
Thus $T_f\varphi=\varphi\circ f$ is bounded and invertible. Suppose a measurable wandering set $W$ satisfies
\[
X=\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j\in\Z} f^jW,\qquad 0<\mu(W)<\infty,
\]
where a null remainder can be discarded. Write $m_j=\mu(f^jW)$. Bounded distortion means that some $K\ge1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bd}
K^{-1}m_j\mu(C)\le \mu(f^jC)m_0\le Km_j\mu(C)
\quad(j\in\Z,\ C\subseteq W\text{ measurable}).
\end{equation}
These are the dissipative hypotheses recalled in~\cite[pp.~1079--1080]{OWR}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under these assumptions, let $B_w$ on $\ell^p(\Z)$ be given by
\[
(B_wa)_j=w_{j+1}a_{j+1},\qquad
w_j=\left(\frac{m_{j-1}}{m_j}\right)^{1/p}.
\]
For every $\Gamma\subseteq\C$, $T_f$ is $\gs$ if and only if $B_w$ is $\gs$.
\end{theorem}

The contribution of this note is the explicit application to the reported question. Abbar's scalar criterion~\cite[Theorem A]{Abbar} and his $\Gamma$-supercyclicity criterion~\cite[Theorem 2]{Abbar} already imply the amplification step below. Theorem B of Abbar--Kuznetsova~\cite{AK} gives a second route, described in Section~\ref{sec:prior}. We reproduce the elementary estimates for ease of verification, not as additional novelty claims.

\section{The scalar criterion and its amplification}

Let $c_j>0$ and suppose the translation $(Sa)_j=a_{j+1}$ is bounded on
\[
Y=\ell^p(\Z,c),\qquad \norm{a}_c^p=\sum_{j\in\Z}c_j\abs{a_j}^p.
\]
For a nonzero separable complex Banach space $E$, use the same translation $S_E$ on $Y_E=\ell^p(\Z,c;E)$, with norm $\norm{u}_c^p=\sum_j c_j\norm{u_j}_E^p$. Boundedness follows from $\sup_j c_{j-1}/c_j<\infty$. Put $\Gamma^*=\Gamma\setminus\{0\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-block form of Abbar's criterion]\label{lem:tails}
The following condition is necessary for $S$ to be $\gs$, and sufficient for $S_E$ to be $\gs$: for every finite nonempty $F\subset\Z$, $\eta>0$, and $L\ge0$, there are $n>L$ and $\lambda\in\Gamma^*$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
\frac{\bigl(\sum_{j\in F}c_{j+n}\bigr)^{1/p}}{\abs{\lambda}}<\eta,
\qquad
\abs{\lambda}\bigl(\sum_{j\in F}c_{j-n}\bigr)^{1/p}<\eta.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

For scalar coefficients, this is the finite-sum formulation of~\cite[Theorem A]{Abbar}, with weight $\omega_j=c_j^{1/p}$; finite sums and finite maxima give equivalent conditions. The sufficiency argument below is the usual universality-criterion argument underlying~\cite[Theorem 2]{Abbar}.

\begin{proof}
Suppose $x\in Y$ has a dense $\Gamma$-orbit. Every nonempty open set is reached at arbitrarily large times: for each $N$, the closed set $\bigcup_{n=0}^N\C S^n x$ has empty interior in the infinite-dimensional space $Y$. Also the support of $x$ is unbounded above, since otherwise every forward iterate would vanish above a common index.

Fix $F$ and choose $k\ge\max F$ with $x_k\ne0$. With $e_j$ denoting the coordinate vectors, set $v=\sum_{j\in F}e_j$. There are $n_r\to\infty$ and $\lambda_r\in\Gamma^*$ such that $\delta_r=\norm{\lambda_r S^{n_r}x-v}_c\to0$. For large $r$, each $j\in F$ satisfies $\abs{\lambda_r x_{j+n_r}}>1/2$, whence
\[
\frac{\bigl(\sum_{j\in F}c_{j+n_r}\bigr)^{1/p}}{\abs{\lambda_r}}
\le 2\Bigl(\sum_{j\in F}c_{j+n_r}\abs{x_{j+n_r}}^p\Bigr)^{1/p}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The limit follows because $F+n_r$ escapes every finite interval and $x\in Y$. For large $r$, $k-n_r\notin F$, so
\[
\abs{\lambda_r}c_{k-n_r}^{1/p}\abs{x_k}\le\delta_r.
\]
Writing $M=\norm{S}$, the coordinate estimate $c_{t-d}^{1/p}\le M^d c_t^{1/p}$ for $d\ge0$ yields
\[
\abs{\lambda_r}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in F}c_{j-n_r}\Bigr)^{1/p}
\le \Bigl(\sum_{j\in F}M^{p(k-j)}\Bigr)^{1/p}\frac{\delta_r}{\abs{x_k}}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves necessity, with the same allowed scalar in both estimates.

Conversely, assume~\eqref{eq:tails}. Let $U,V\subset Y_E$ be nonempty open sets. Choose finitely supported $a\in U$ and $b\in V$. Let a finite nonempty $F$ contain both supports, and bound their coordinate norms by $H\ge1$. For $n\ge0$, define the right translation of a finite-support sequence by $(R_n b)_j=b_{j-n}$, so $S_E^nR_n b=b$. The condition supplies $n,\lambda$ for which
\[
z=a+\lambda^{-1}R_n b\in U,\qquad
\lambda S_E^nz=\lambda S_E^na+b\in V:
\]
indeed, the two errors are bounded by $H$ times the first and second quantities in~\eqref{eq:tails}, respectively, and can be arbitrarily small.

Take a countable base $(V_q)$ of $Y_E$. For each $q$, the set
\[
G_q=\bigcup_{n\ge0,\,\lambda\in\Gamma}\{z:\lambda S_E^nz\in V_q\}
\]
is open by continuity and dense by the preceding argument. Baire's theorem makes $\bigcap_qG_q$ dense and nonempty; its elements are $\Gamma$-supercyclic vectors. No countability or regularity assumption on $\Gamma$ is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Amplification]\label{prop:amp}
The operators $S$ and $S_E$ are $\gs$ for exactly the same subsets $\Gamma\subseteq\C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The forward implication is Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}. For the reverse, choose $e\in E$ and $\ell\in E^*$ with $\ell(e)=1$. The bounded surjection $Q:Y_E\to Y$, $(Qu)_j=\ell(u_j)$, satisfies $QS_E=SQ$ and sends a dense $\Gamma$-orbit to a dense $\Gamma$-orbit. If $\Gamma^*=\varnothing$, neither operator has such an orbit, so this case also satisfies the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\section{The composition model and the answer}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Set $c_j=m_j/m_0$ and $E=L^p(W,\mu|_W)$. The measure bounds~\eqref{eq:bounded} ensure $0<m_j<\infty$ and boundedness of both scalar translation directions. The space $E$ is nonzero and separable: extension by zero embeds it as a closed subspace of $L^p(X,\mu)$.

For measurable $C\subseteq W$, define $\nu_j(C)=\mu(f^jC)$. Bimeasurability and injectivity make this a measure, and bounded distortion says
\[
K^{-1}c_j\mu|_W\le\nu_j\le Kc_j\mu|_W.
\]
Define $J\varphi=(\varphi\circ f^j|_W)_{j\in\Z}$. The measure-class bounds ensure that $J$ is well-defined on equivalence classes. Decomposing the integral over the wandering partition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
\norm{\varphi}_p^p
=\sum_j\int_W\abs{\varphi\circ f^j}^p\,d\nu_j,
\qquad
K^{-1}\norm{J\varphi}_c^p\le\norm{\varphi}_p^p\le K\norm{J\varphi}_c^p.
\end{equation}
For $u\in Y_E$, choose measurable representatives and define $\varphi$ on $f^jW$ by $\varphi=u_j\circ f^{-j}$. This countable gluing is measurable and~\eqref{eq:J} makes it integrable. Thus $J$ is a bounded linear isomorphism onto $Y_E$, and direct substitution gives $JT_f=S_EJ$.

Finally, $D:\ell^p(\Z)\to Y$, $(Da)_j=c_j^{-1/p}a_j$, is an onto isometry, with
\[
(DB_wa)_j=c_j^{-1/p}w_{j+1}a_{j+1}
=c_{j+1}^{-1/p}a_{j+1}=(SDa)_j.
\]
Complex-linear conjugacies preserve $\Gamma$-orbits for every fixed $\Gamma$. Proposition~\ref{prop:amp} therefore gives $T_f\ \gs\iff S_E\ \gs\iff S\ \gs\iff B_w\ \gs$.
\end{proof}

Separability cannot be dropped from this application merely because the measure is $\sigma$-finite. Every projective orbit is contained in the separable closed span of a countable orbit. For example, on $X=\Z\times\Omega$, let $\Omega=\{0,1\}^I$, with $I$ uncountable, carry the product of fair Bernoulli probability measures, give layer $j$ mass $2^{-|j|}$ times that measure, and set $f(j,t)=(j+1,t)$. Bounded distortion holds with $K=1$ and $T_f$ is bounded and invertible. Its $L^p$ space is nonseparable, while the scalar shift is hypercyclic by~\eqref{eq:tails}. This is a scope clarification, not an additional novelty claim.

\section{A direct deduction from the translation theorem}\label{sec:prior}

For completeness, the following deduction identifies the relation to Abbar--Kuznetsova~\cite[Theorem B]{AK} without using the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}. On the discrete group $\Z$, take the translation parameters $\{-n:n\ge0\}$ and weight $\omega(j)=c_j^{1/p}$. Their translation convention gives $T_{-n}a(j)=a(j+n)$. The weight is admissible because $S$ is bounded. Counting measure and an exceptional-set tolerance smaller than one force the retained set in their criterion to be the whole finite set. Scalar $\Gamma$-supercyclicity therefore supplies the two maximum versions of~\eqref{eq:tails}. The times can exceed any fixed $L$: over finitely many times $n\le L$, the product of the two positive maxima has a positive minimum, independently of $\lambda$.

Now use $\Z^2$, parameters $\{(-n,0):n\ge0\}$, and weight $\omega(j,k)=c_j^{1/p}$. A finite set has a finite first-coordinate projection, so the same estimates verify their criterion. This gives $\Gamma$-supercyclicity on $\ell^p(\Z,c;\ell^p(\Z))$. For arbitrary separable $E$, choose unit vectors $e_k$ with dense linear span. The map
\[
R:\ell^p(\Z)\to E,\qquad R(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}a_k e_k
\]
is bounded, since $\norm{R(a)}\le\norm{a}_\infty\le\norm{a}_p$, and has dense range. Its coordinatewise extension has dense range and commutes with translation, hence transfers a dense $\Gamma$-orbit. The functional quotient gives the converse. This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:amp} as a consequence of the earlier theorem. The case $\Gamma^*=\varnothing$ is immediate separately.
