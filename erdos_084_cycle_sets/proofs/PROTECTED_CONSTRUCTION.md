# The protected central-signature construction

This note freezes the definitions and elementary deductions. It does not prove
the remaining averaged-shadow estimate.

## 1. Difference supports and fans

For \(X\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N\}\), define the positive difference support

\[
\Delta(X)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in X,\ x\ne y\}.
\]

Take the path \(v_0v_1\cdots v_N\), add an apex \(z\), and join \(z\) to
\(v_x\) for each \(x\in X\). Every cycle uses the apex, two spokes, and the
unique path segment between their endpoints. Consequently its length is
\(|x-y|+2\), and the cycle-length set is exactly \(\Delta(X)+2\).

Let \(h(N)\) count the possible \(\Delta(X)\) with \(0,N\in X\). Then

\[
f(N+2)\ge h(N).
\]

## 2. Central signatures

Fix \(k\ge1\), and write

\[
J_k=\{-k,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,k\}.
\]

For \(P\subseteq[2k]\) and \(b\in J_k\), define

\[
G_{k,P}(b)
=
\{b,-b\}\cup\bigl((b-P)\cap[-k,k]\bigr),
\]

where \(b-P=\{b-p:p\in P\}\). For \(B\subseteq J_k\), put

\[
\Phi_{k,P}(B)=\bigcup_{b\in B}G_{k,P}(b).
\]

The central signature family and its aggregate count are

\[
\mathcal F_k(P)
=
\{\Phi_{k,P}(B):B\subseteq J_k\},
\qquad
q_k(P)=|\mathcal F_k(P)|,
\qquad
S_k=\sum_{P\subseteq[2k]}q_k(P).
\]

The signature may contain \(0\); it records a centered interval of ordinary
positive differences after translation by \(M\).

## 3. Protected geometric realization

Let \(M>3k+1\). Choose

\[
P\subseteq[2k],\qquad
A\subseteq\{2k+1,\ldots,M-k-1\},\qquad
B\subseteq J_k,
\]

and set

\[
X=\{0,2M\}\cup P\cup A\cup(M+B).
\]

The high band

\[
\Delta(X)\cap[M+k+1,2M-1]
=
\{2M-x:x\in P\cup A\}
\]

recovers \(P\cup A\), hence recovers \(P\) and \(A\) separately.

The central band is

\[
(\Delta(X)-M)\cap[-k,k]
=
\Phi_{k,P}(B).
\]

Indeed, the two endpoint distances from \(M+b\) contribute \(b\) and \(-b\).
The distances from \(M+b\) to \(p\in P\) contribute \(b-p\) when that value
lies in the central interval. The separation inequalities exclude all other
points and pair types from this band.

There are \(M-3k-1\) freely chosen positions in \(A\). Distinct triples
\((P,A,\Phi)\) give distinct difference supports, so

\[
h(2M)\ge2^{M-3k-1}S_k.
\]

Thus, if \(S_k/8^k\to\infty\), choosing, for example,
\(k=\lfloor(M-2)/3\rfloor\) proves the target lower bound for \(f(n)\).
Explicitly, writing \(T_k=S_k/8^k\),

\[
\frac{f(2M+2)}{2^{(2M+2)/2}}
\ge
\frac{h(2M)}{2^{M+1}}
\ge
\frac14T_k.
\]

Adding an isolated vertex does not change a graph's cycle-length set, so
\(f(n+1)\ge f(n)\). Divergence on the even subsequence therefore gives the
full limit: for \(n=2M+3\), the same choice of \(k\) gives
\[
\frac{f(n)}{2^{n/2}}\ge\frac{T_k}{4\sqrt2}.
\]

## 4. Eight-way extension

Fix \(P\subseteq[2m]\), \(C\in\mathcal F_m(P)\), and a representative
\(B\subseteq J_m\) with \(\Phi_{m,P}(B)=C\). Extend

\[
P'=P\cup Q,\qquad Q\subseteq\{2m+1,2m+2\}.
\]

The two choices

\[
B,\qquad B\cup\{-(m+1)\}
\]

inside \(J_{m+1}\) have the same restriction \(C\) on \([-m,m]\). They are
distinct because \(m+1\) is absent from the first signature and present in
the second. Hence

\[
q_{m+1}(P')\ge2q_m(P).
\]

Summing over the four choices of \(Q\) gives

\[
S_{m+1}\ge8S_m.
\]

## 5. Union-shadow gain

For \(P\subseteq[2m]\), define

\[
V(P)=m+1-P,
\qquad
V_\alpha(P)=V(P)\cup
\begin{cases}
\varnothing,&\alpha=0,\\
\{-m\},&\alpha=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Let

\[
e_\alpha(P)
=
\left|
\{C\cup V_\alpha(P):C\in\mathcal F_m(P)\}
\setminus\mathcal F_m(P)
\right|
\]

and

\[
E_m=\sum_{P\subseteq[2m]}\bigl(e_0(P)+e_1(P)\bigr).
\]

Adding \(+(m+1)\) to \(B\) produces the old-window restriction
\(C\cup V_\alpha(P)\), where \(\alpha\) records whether \(2m+1\in P'\).
Every resulting full signature is exactly
\[
\bigl(C\cup V_\alpha(P)\bigr)\cup\{-(m+1),m+1\}.
\]
Thus distinct new old-window sets remain distinct, and because they are not
in \(\mathcal F_m(P)\), they cannot collide with either baseline lift.
Membership of \(2m+2\) does not affect the old-window restriction and doubles
the new outputs. Therefore

\[
S_{m+1}\ge8S_m+2E_m.
\]

Writing \(T_m=S_m/8^m\) yields

\[
T_{m+1}\ge T_m+\frac{E_m}{4\cdot8^m}.
\]

Any proof that \(\sum_mE_m/8^m=\infty\) completes this route.

## 6. Trace statistic and restricted witnesses

Define

\[
R_m(P)=|\{C\in\mathcal F_m(P):-m\notin C\}|.
\]

A generator \(b\in J_m\) is safe exactly when

\[
|b|<m\quad\text{and}\quad m+b\notin P.
\]

Thus \(R_m(P)\) is the number of distinct unions generated by the safe
generators.

For \(P\) containing \(1\), restrict further by choosing at most one of
\(-t,+t\) for each \(1\le t<m\). Across all such \(P\), the number of these
restricted witnesses is

\[
12\cdot8^{m-2}\qquad(m\ge2).
\]

If the average collision energy of the witness-to-signature map is \(O(m)\),
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_{\substack{P\subseteq[2m]\\1\in P}}R_m(P)
\gg\frac{8^m}{m}.
\]

This is one current theorem-strength gap. Pointwise fiber bounds are false;
the proof must exploit averaging over \(P\).

## 7. The trace-to-excess bridge is separate

When \(1\in P\), the set \(V(P)\) contains \(m\) and omits \(-m\). Hence
every \(C\) avoiding \(-m\) produces a union \(C\cup V(P)\) outside
\(\mathcal F_m(P)\): every signature in \(\mathcal F_m(P)\) that contains
\(m\) also contains \(-m\).

However, different trace signatures \(C\) can have the same union with
\(V(P)\). Thus the pointwise inequality

\[
e_0(P)\ge R_m(P)
\]

is false. Computation suggests the aggregate inequality

\[
\sum_{\substack{P\subseteq[2m]\\1\in P}}e_0(P)
\ge
\sum_{\substack{P\subseteq[2m]\\1\in P}}R_m(P),
\]

but no proof is presently known within this program. The collision estimate
and this aggregate trace-to-excess inequality are independent missing lemmas.
