# Aperiodic orbit reduction

This note investigates a conditional reduction based on cyclic defects. The
exceptional-set estimate and several additive orbit bounds are rigorous; the
required mass-scaled orbit-boundary lemma is conjectural and is now under
serious empirical threat from an explicit four-run family.

## 1. Cyclic words and defects

Represent \(P\subseteq[2m]\) by its cyclic binary word

\[
w(P)=w_1\cdots w_{2m},
\qquad
w_i=\mathbf 1_{\{i\in P\}},
\]

with \(w_{2m+1}=w_1\). Define its alternating defect count by

\[
a(P)=|\{i\in[2m]:w_i=w_{i+1}\}|.
\]

Let the group generated by cyclic rotation and bitwise complement act on
these words, and write \(\mathcal O(P)\) for the resulting orbit. The value
\(a(P)\) is constant on every orbit.

Early computation suggested that alternating or nearly alternating words
needed separate treatment. Larger exact and targeted computations instead
find the smallest normalized ratios among high-defect four-run words. The
following quantitative statement records the conditional route that these
later examples may ultimately refute.

## 2. Orbit-boundary conjecture

There are absolute constants \(c>0\) and \(m_0\) such that, for every
\(m\geq m_0\), every orbit satisfies

\[
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}
\bigl(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q)\bigr)
\ge
\frac{c\,a(P)}{m^2}
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}q_m(Q).
\tag{O}
\]

Using group averages instead of distinct orbit elements gives an equivalent
statement, because every orbit element has the same stabilizer multiplicity.

The factor \(a(P)\) reflects the number of possible nonalternating cuts. The
factor \(m^2\) permits one factor \(m\) for choosing a cut and one factor \(m\)
for congestion in a proposed map from signatures to new boundary shadows.

Statement (O) is unproved. The intended mechanism is to reinterpret
\(V(P)\) as the lower neighborhood of a hypothetical top generator. Each
equal-adjacency defect should supply a candidate cut at which an essential
generator becomes a genuinely new top shadow; a charging map may use both
\(\alpha=0,1\) and both word complements.

### An unconditional but exponentially weak orbit bound

There is a simple rigorous baseline. If \(1\in Q\), then \(V(Q)\) contains
\(m\) and omits \(-m\). No member of \(\mathcal F_m(Q)\) can contain \(m\)
without also containing \(-m\). Since the empty signature belongs to
\(\mathcal F_m(Q)\), it follows that

\[
e_0(Q)\geq1.
\]

Complementation is a fixed-point-free involution of every distinct
rotation/complement orbit and flips membership of \(1\). Exactly half of the
orbit elements therefore contain \(1\), so

\[
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q))
\geq\frac{|\mathcal O(P)|}{2}.
\tag{W}
\]

Since \(q_m(Q)\leq2^{2m}\) and \(a(P)\leq2m\), this implies

\[
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q))
\geq
2^{-2m-2}\frac{a(P)}{m^2}
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}q_m(Q).
\]

Thus an orbit inequality is unconditional with an exponentially decaying
constant. The entire difficulty in (O) is replacing it by a constant
independent of \(m\); (W) is far too weak to drive the normalized recurrence.

There is also a stronger unconditional bound in terms of defects alone. Let
\(z_{00}(P)\) and \(z_{11}(P)\) count the cyclic \(00\)- and \(11\)-edges,
and set \(z_*=\max(z_{00},z_{11})\). Then \(z_*\geq a(P)/2\).

First suppose that \(Q\) contains \(1\), and let \(z_{\mathrm{left}}(Q)\)
count its
\(00\)-edges \(r,r+1\) with \(1\leq r<m\). For every such edge choose the
row \(b=-(m-r)\). It is safe because \(r\notin Q\). Its positive marker
\(m-r\) is absent from \(V(Q)\) because \(r+1\notin Q\), and no other
chosen negative row can create that positive coordinate except as its own
distinct marker. Arbitrary subsets of these rows therefore give distinct
trace signatures and remain distinct after union with \(V(Q)\). Each
resulting shadow contains \(m\) but not \(-m\), so it is new. Hence

\[
e_0(Q)\geq2^{z_{\mathrm{left}}(Q)}.
\tag{W+}
\]

For a nonconstant cyclic word of length \(2m\) with \(z\) \(00\)-edges,
average the number of \(00\)-edges over all windows of \(m-1\) consecutive
edges. Some window contains at least
\(\lceil(m-1)z/(2m)\rceil\) of them. Moving its start backward to the
preceding \(1\) loses at most one \(00\)-edge: if the move crosses \(d\)
edges, the entering segment contains \(d-1\) forced \(00\)-edges while the
departing segment contains at most \(d\). After rotation, (W+) applies.
Apply this either to \(P\) or to its complement, according as
\(z_{00}\) or \(z_{11}\) is larger. Thus every nonconstant orbit satisfies

\[
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O(P)}(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q))
\geq
2^{\max\{0,\lceil(m-1)z_*/(2m)\rceil-1\}}
\geq
2^{(m-1)a(P)/(4m)-1}.
\tag{AD}
\]

For the constant empty/full orbit, direct use of \(P=\varnothing\) gives
\(e_1(P)=2^{m-1}\), so the same coarse defect bound also holds. Bound (AD)
is exponentially stronger than (W) when \(a(P)\) is large, but it is
additive rather than scaled by \(\sum q_m(Q)\), and remains insufficient for
the harmonic recurrence.

The constant orbit itself is not an asymptotic obstruction to (O). For
\(P=\varnothing\), the union family consists of arbitrary unions of the
\(m\) symmetric pairs, so \(q_m(P)=2^m\). For \(P=[2m]\), positive row \(t\)
is \([-m,t]\), while negative row \(-s\) is
\([-m,-s]\cup\{s\}\). Unions using no positive row contribute at most
\(2^m\) sets. If \(t\) is the largest selected positive row, the remaining
positive spikes form an arbitrary subset of \(\{t+1,\ldots,m\}\), giving at
most

\[
\sum_{t=1}^m2^{m-t}=2^m-1
\]

further sets. Hence the orbit has total \(q\)-mass below \(3\cdot2^m\), while
its total excess is at least \(2^{m-1}\). Since \(a(P)=2m\), its normalized
constant satisfies

\[
\Lambda(\mathcal O)>\frac{m}{12}.
\]

Thus the finite \(c=1/2\) failures on this orbit for \(m=2,3,4\) disappear
with room to spare; the difficult candidates are nonconstant.

The normalized orbit constant

\[
\Lambda(\mathcal O)
=
\frac{m^2\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O}(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q))}
{a(P)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O}q_m(Q)}
\]

has been computed exhaustively for every nonalternating orbit through
\(m=10\). The concrete choice \(c=1/2\) holds in every exact case
\(5\le m\le9\), but is false at \(m=10\). (For \(m=2,3,4\), the only
failures are the finite empty/full orbit.) At \(m=8\), there are
\(2{,}068\) total orbits:
\(2{,}067\) with \(a(P)>0\) and one alternating orbit. The minimum over the
nonalternating orbits is \(0.585695352914\). It is represented by
\(0000001111001111\), has \(a(P)=12\), orbit size \(32\),
\(\sum q=31{,}188\), and \(\sum e=3{,}425\). Thus testing only
near-alternating words would be inadequate.

At \(m=9\), the minimum is \(0.519819418576\), represented by
\(000000011110011111\), with \(a(P)=14\). This is still above \(1/2\), but
the decline from \(m=8\) makes continued falsification essential. At
\(m=10\), the minimum drops to

\[
0.428738331457,
\]

represented by \(00000001111100011111\), with \(a(P)=16\), orbit size \(40\),
\(\sum q=170{,}544\), and \(\sum e=11{,}699\). Even the \(a=14\) class
slightly violates \(1/2\), with minimum \(0.499928817150\). Thus \(c=1/2\)
is refuted, while the theorem-strength assertion that some fixed \(c>0\)
works remains open.

Sparse exact evaluation of natural four-run continuations gives a more
serious warning:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
m&\text{cyclic run lengths}&\Lambda&m\Lambda\\ \hline
11&(8,5,3,6)&0.380167815381&4.181845969\\
12&(8,6,4,6)&0.353540606477&4.242487278\\
13&(9,6,4,7)&0.293504662562&3.815560613\\
14&(9,7,5,7)&0.295934680041&4.143085521
\end{array}
\]

The sparse evaluator exactly reproduces the exhaustive minima at
\(m=8,9,10\). Across \(8\leq m\leq14\), the product \(m\Lambda\) remains
between roughly \(3.8\) and \(4.7\). This is strong evidence that this
four-run family may have \(\Lambda\asymp1/m\), which would refute (O), but
finite computation does not prove that asymptotic. Deriving formulas or
asymptotic bounds for this explicit family is now a prerequisite to relying
on the orbit route.

For \(m=8\), the minima stratified by defect count are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}

\min\Lambda&
3.465&1.796&1.108&0.892&0.761&0.586&0.787&0.806.
\end{array}
\]

### The obstruction exposed by the first proof attempt

Cyclic rotation is not a symmetry of \(\mathcal F_m(P)\). It preserves
differences \(b-p\) away from the cut, but the fixed markers
\(\{b,-b\}\) move differently and the window truncation loses wrapped
coordinates. Consequently there is no automatic map
\(\mathcal F_m(P)\to\mathcal F_m(\rho P)\).

Choosing a minimal representation by generators does not immediately repair
this: representation fibers and shadow fibers can be exponentially large.
The missing statement is an explicit contraction of a shadow-saturated
signature across one defect cut, with total congestion \(O(m^2)\). This is
the substantive part of (O); the exceptional-set count is already complete.

There is an exact reindexing that exposes the obstruction. Put \(L=2m\),
replace an output coordinate \(c\) by \(j=m+1-c\in[L+1]\), and replace a row
index \(b\) by \(r=m+1-b\). Then

\[
R_r(P)
=
\{r,L+2-r\}
\cup
\{r+p:p\in P,\ r+p\leq L+1\},
\tag{C1}
\]

while the two shadow rows become

\[
W_0(P)=P,\qquad W_1(P)=P\cup\{L+1\}.
\tag{C2}
\]

If \(\sigma\) is a one-step left cyclic shift of the \(P\)-word, then for
\[
r\in\{2,\ldots,L+1\}\setminus\{m+1,m+2\}
\]
the Toeplitz part of \(R_r(\sigma P)\) agrees exactly with that of
\(R_{r-1}(P)\). The two excluded values correspond to the missing row
\(b=0\). The marker pairs do not align:

\[
\{r,L+2-r\}
\quad\hbox{versus}\quad
\{r-1,L+3-r\}.
\]

For a union of selected rows, this discrepancy is controlled by the run
boundary of the selected row set, not by \(a(P)\), and can involve
\(\Theta(m)\) coordinates. For example, when \(m=2\), \(P=1100\), and
\(\sigma P=1001\),

\[
R_1(P)=\{1,2,3,5\},
\qquad
R_2(\sigma P)=\{2,3,4\}.
\]

Thus even cutting at an equal-adjacency defect does not transport a single
generator. Any proof of (O) must average or contract the marker boundary as
well as the Toeplitz part; the direct row-by-row rotation map fails.

## 3. Rigorous exceptional-set estimate

Fix \(0<\delta<1\), and call \(P\) exceptional when
\(a(P)\le\delta m\). A cyclic word is determined by its first bit and the set
of edges at which consecutive bits agree. Therefore

\[
\#\{P:a(P)\le\delta m\}
\le
2\sum_{j\le\delta m}\binom{2m}{j}.
\]

For \(\delta<1\), the binary entropy bound gives

\[
\#\{P:a(P)\le\delta m\}
\le
2^{1+2mH_2(\delta/2)}.
\]

Since \(\mathcal F_m(P)\) is generated by \(2m\) sets,

\[
q_m(P)\le2^{2m}.
\]

Consequently the total \(S_m\)-mass of the exceptional parameters is at most

\[
2^{1+(2+2H_2(\delta/2))m}.
\]

For example, \(\delta=0.1\) gives

\[
2+2H_2(0.05)<2.573<3,
\]

so the exceptional mass is \(o(8^m)\).

## 4. Conditional completion of Erdős Problem 84

The eight-way lift and \(S_1=10\) give the proved lower bound

\[
S_m\ge10\cdot8^{m-1}=\frac54\,8^m.
\]

Fix \(\delta=0.1\). By the exceptional estimate, all nonexceptional orbits
together have \(S\)-mass at least \(8^m\), say, for all sufficiently large
\(m\). If (O) holds, then summing it over the nonexceptional orbits gives

\[
E_m
\ge
\frac{c\delta}{m}
\sum_{\substack{P\subseteq[2m]\\a(P)>\delta m}}q_m(P)
\gg\frac{8^m}{m}.
\]

The normalized recurrence then has a harmonic nonsummable gain, so
\(S_m/8^m\to\infty\). The protected fan construction converts this into

\[
\frac{f(n)}{2^{n/2}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Thus (O) is a precise standalone combinatorial lemma whose proof would
complete this route.

## 5. Falsification protocol

The continuing falsification statistic is

\[
\Lambda(\mathcal O)
=
\frac{m^2\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O}(e_0(Q)+e_1(Q))}
{a(P)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O}q_m(Q)}
\]

for all orbits with \(a(P)>0\). A sequence of aperiodic orbits with
\(a(P)\ge\delta m\) and \(\Lambda(\mathcal O)\to0\) would refute (O).
Alternating words have \(a(P)=0\) and are deliberately handled by the
exceptional estimate. The displayed four-run family is the leading candidate
for such a refuting sequence.
