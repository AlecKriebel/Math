### Glassey, The Cauchy Problem in Kinetic Theory (1996)

Printed p.140, system (5.3), uses physical momentum (p), velocities

\[
\widehat p_a=p/\sqrt{m_a^2+|p|^2/c^2},\qquad
\dot p=e_a(E+c^{-1}\widehat p_a\times B),
\]

and signed sources (\rho=4\pi\sum_a e_a\int f_a\,dp), (j=4\pi\sum_a e_a\int\widehat p_af_a\,dp). Theorem 5.2.1 assumes nonnegative (C_c^1) distributions, (C^2) fields, Gauss constraints **and** (\int\rho_0dx=0), and a continuous common momentum-support bound. It then gives unique global (C^1) solutions. Printed p.159 adapts the representation proof by species-dependent transport operators, signed source sums and separate iterate-difference estimates. Chapter 6 gives a small-data theorem for several species. These are conditional or perturbative inputs, not the new unrestricted a priori estimate. [Publisher chapter](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611971477.ch5).

### Luk–Strain 2014 continuation

[arXiv:1406.0165v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0165v1), submitted June 1, 2014; published as *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **331** (2014), 1005–1027, DOI [10.1007/s00220-014-2108-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-014-2108-8).

Their Theorem 1.1 states bounded-full-momentum continuation for nonnegative phase-compact (f_0\in H^5), (E_0,B_0\in H^5) and Gauss constraints. A footnote distinguishes the original Glassey–Strauss iterate-support hypothesis and lower regularity. Theorem 1.2 needs bounded momentum projection onto a fixed two-plane. Theorem 1.3 replaces that bound by a time/angular integrability condition. Remark 1.2 explicitly states an analogous multispecies continuation criterion follows, with details omitted. These remarks support attribution and a continuation strategy, not automatic satisfaction of their (H^5) field hypothesis by (C_b^\infty\cap L^2) data. No neutrality assumption appears in the displayed statements.

### Luk–Strain 2014/2016 moment continuation

[arXiv:1406.0169v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0169v1), submitted June 1, 2014; combined published version *Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis* **219** (2016), 445–552, DOI [10.1007/s00205-015-0899-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-015-0899-1).

Theorem 1.1 explicitly includes bounded support for every approximation, and Theorem 1.2 is the corresponding continuation statement. Later results permit noncompact momentum support subject to weighted regularity and additional bounds, including a field integral along characteristics. These do not supply an unconditional estimate for our compact large-data target. This distinction matters when citing a continuation theorem: the exact version and its iterate, regularity and solution hypotheses must be stated.

### Bigorgne small-distribution theorem

[arXiv:2208.08360v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08360v2), first submitted August 17, 2022, revised August 31, 2023; published *Analysis & PDE* **18** (2025), 629–714, DOI [10.2140/apde.2025.18.629](https://doi.org/10.2140/apde.2025.18.629).

Theorem 2.10 assumes (N\ge3), weighted (C^N) distribution derivatives bounded by a small parameter and weighted field derivatives bounded by (\Lambda); the decisive smallness has an exponential dependence on (\Lambda). Its exponents include (N_v\ge15), (N_x>7). It permits a large Maxwell field, but the distribution still must be small. The introduction explicitly states extension to different charges and positive masses. Thus it is established perturbative multispecies scope, not duplicate unrestricted large-data scope. The 2020 sharp-asymptotic paper also has a small-data hypothesis and permits nonneutral data.

### Wang 2026 cylindrical symmetry

[arXiv:2203.01199v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01199v2), first submitted March 2, 2022, revised July 16, 2026, pairs with [arXiv:2607.14685v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14685v1), submitted July 16, 2026. Part I Theorem 1.1 is for one-species data invariant under simultaneous rotations around an axis, (s\ge6), Sobolev fields and a strong weighted distribution condition (the displayed (N_0=10^{10})). Part I expressly states cylindrical symmetry is crucial and discusses the unrestricted problem separately. Part II provides pointwise estimates for that mechanism. This is a substantial large-data result under symmetry; it does not resolve arbitrary nonsymmetric data or the new signed species-pair estimate.
