## Accepted prior construction, with ancillary corrections

This authored edition independently reconstructs the core of Theorem 1 in
M. Czech's pinned July 2026 manuscript. Every positive integral solution of
z²−56x²=65 with x≥37 gives five explicit distinct divisors of
n=(x²−1)(x²−4)(x²−16)(x²−49) in the strictly open right interval
(√n,√n+31 n^(1/4)). A positive Pell recurrence from (37,277) yields
infinitely many distinct n. Any K in the original one-sided uniform-bound
formulation therefore satisfies K≥5. The general EP887 question remains
unresolved by this work.

This is an audit of a prior construction, not a newly claimed theorem.
The complete elementary factorization, positivity, distinctness, strict
window inequalities, finite base cases and infinitude proof are retained.
No external upper-bound theorem, general Pell existence theorem, complete
divisor census, or author-supplied verifier is a proof dependency.

## Corrections and limits

- 31 is the least integer constant for all five displayed divisors at every
  admissible parameter. It is not least among real constants: 30.5 works
  throughout the family. Every C>30 works eventually; C≤30 never contains
  all five displayed divisors. The shifted-polynomial proof and all 26
  small-parameter square exclusions are public.
- Pell necessity applies only to the fifth pair with its prescribed midpoint.
  An explicit off-Pell additional-divisor witness is retained without making
  a complete-census claim.
- The original question is one-sided; a symmetric version changes the
  numerical normalization and is not a general same-window count equality.
- The balanced-split method is shared with the consecutive-factor construction;
  the weight sets are not literal affine recenterings. Exhaustive maximality
  is not asserted from an omitted enumeration.

The nonsquareness argument is retained. Complete sample counts, eventual
exact-five behavior, the incomplete secondary family, speculative sixth-pair
and geometric exclusions, and historical priority or record claims are
outside this edition's accepted conclusions.
