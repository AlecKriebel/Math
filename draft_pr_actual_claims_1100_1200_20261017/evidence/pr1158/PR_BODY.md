## Accepted partial results; exact conjecture unresolved

Problem 30000879 / OWR-1738-004 asks whether a torsion-free nilpotent group G of finite integral cohomological dimension n, with a class α in H²(G,C×), has a subgroup H on which α vanishes and for which gldim(CH) = gldim(C^αG) = r. Finite generation is not assumed. Global dimension here is left global dimension; restriction concerns the cohomology class.

This unrefereed AI-assisted exposition and independent internal AI mathematical/source/computational audit preserve the complete accepted proofs and substantive audit:

- Every twist splits on γ_m(G), m = ceiling((c + 2)/2), for nilpotency class at most c. No torsion-freeness or finite generation is needed for this splitting. Explicit finitely generated torsion-free examples prove the universal cutoff sharp for every positive class.
- Under the original hypotheses, G is countable. The countable telescope gives d ≤ r ≤ d + 1, where d is the supremum over finitely generated subgroups. If r = d + 1, any subgroup witnessing the original conclusion must be infinitely generated.
- The example G = Q with trivial twist has d = 1 and gldim(CQ) = cd_Z(Q) = 2. It is not a counterexample: H = Q is a witness.
- Subgroup freeness and bimodule splitting establish the dimension inequality. The twisted diagonal argument gives r ≤ n for arbitrary modules. A separate class-two example prevents replacing the splitting subgroup by its isolator.
