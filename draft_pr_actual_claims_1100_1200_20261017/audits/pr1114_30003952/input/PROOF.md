# Exact smooth braid lifts of two prescribed Dehn twists

Problem 30003952 / OWR-16415-015.

Complete smooth construction accepted by the accompanying [mathematical audit](MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md). This AI-assisted manuscript and audit are unrefereed; acceptance is not external human peer review, journal acceptance, or formal proof-assistant certification. No novelty or priority is claimed.

## Result and conventions

**Theorem.** Let S be an oriented smooth surface and let α, β be essential simple closed curves with geometric representatives meeting transversely at exactly one point. There are orientation-preserving C∞ diffeomorphisms A, B of S with

[A] = tα,  [B] = tβ,  and  ABA = BAB

as an equality of actual maps. Here tα, tβ are the prescribed right Dehn-twist mapping classes, and composition is from right to left. The diffeomorphisms can moreover be chosen supported in the interior of any sufficiently small compact regular neighborhood of α ∪ β.

In particular this answers all three questions in A’Campo's Problem 9 affirmatively on the usual oriented-surface interpretation: homeomorphisms, C1 homeomorphisms, and Cr homeomorphisms for every finite integer r, as well as C∞. The constructed inverses are C∞ too. Thus the conclusion holds whether “Cr homeomorphism” requires a Cr inverse or merely requires that the forward map be Cr.

The negative-Euler-characteristic hypothesis causes no difficulty: the construction is made on a one-holed torus and then extended inside the given surface. It does not use a closed torus as the final answer. Fixing a boundary collar and compact support are additional properties of the constructed solution, not hypotheses silently added to the problem. As usual, curves on a marked or punctured surface lie in the complement of the marked points or punctures. Their compact regular neighborhood can therefore be chosen to avoid them. Those points, punctures and any existing boundary can be left untouched. “Right” is taken with respect to the orientation, as customary in the report's mapping-class-group context. More generally the same construction applies whenever the regular neighborhood of the two curves is an oriented one-holed torus. No assertion about unspecified conventions for twists on a nonorientable surface is needed.

The proof below is direct: it contains no branched-cover smoothness assumption. Mann–Tshishiku [MT, Theorem 4.1] already proves a smooth B3 realization on the three-marked disk. This directly relevant positive prior result is credited prominently; no novelty or priority is claimed for the construction here.

## 1. The model and the standard mapping-class facts used

Let T = R²/Z², oriented by dx ∧ dy. Put

U = [[1,1],[0,1]],  V = [[1,0],[-1,1]],

Q = UVU = [[0,1],[-1,0]],  R = UV = [[0,1],[-1,1]].

Matrices act on column vectors. Q is clockwise rotation by π/2. Direct multiplication gives

Q² = R³ = −I,  Q⁴ = R⁶ = I,
R⁻¹Q = U,  Q⁻¹R² = V.

Choose model curves α0 = {y = 1/2} and β0 = {x = 1/2}; these are disjoint from a sufficiently small disk around 0 and meet once. Let u and v denote the same-handed twist classes about α0 and β0 whose homology actions are U and V. This specifies their signs without relying on terminology. There is one common s ∈ {−1,+1} such that u=tα0^s and v=tβ0^s for the prescribed right-twist convention. We will construct maps in u,v and then raise both to the power s. The Farb–Margalit draft calls its annular map (θ,t)↦(θ+2πt,t) a left twist, and explicitly lists the homology matrices of its left twists as U⁻¹ and V⁻¹ (printed p.68). Thus U,V are right-twist matrices in that convention, so s=+1. We nevertheless state the final simultaneous-inversion step to make the argument robust to any opposite handedness convention; the words “positive shear” by themselves are not a reliable orientation convention.

We use three standard facts about a one-holed torus N, with boundary fixed pointwise in its mapping class group:

1. Capping the boundary by a disk with a marked center gives a surjection
   Mod(N,∂N) → Mod(T,0) ≅ SL(2,Z).
   Its kernel is the infinite cyclic central subgroup generated by the boundary twist δ with the same handedness as u and v.
2. The two classes u and v satisfy the braid relation.
3. The two-chain relation is (uv)^6 = δ.

Both relations hold with either simultaneous choice of handedness. For the chain relation with inverse twists, use (tα0⁻¹tβ0⁻¹)^6=(tβ0tα0)⁻⁶ and conjugacy of tβ0tα0 to tα0tβ0.

These concern mapping classes only; they will identify the classes of maps after, independently, an exact map-level relation has been built. For references see Farb–Margalit [FM], the discussion of the marked torus in §2.2, the capping homomorphism in §3.6.2 (Proposition 3.19), and the chain relation in §4.4 (Proposition 4.12). To recall the content of the kernel statement: an isotopy after capping can be arranged to preserve the marked center; its only relative-boundary ambiguity is the integral winding of the boundary circle, represented by δ. The annular winding number also proves that δ has infinite order. The two-chain relation is the genus-one, one-boundary instance of the chain relation. Neither fact assumes a splitting into actual homeomorphisms.

## 2. A smooth equivariant local change of coordinates

Let q and r be the linear torus diffeomorphisms induced by Q and R. Let ι(z) = −z, so q² = r³ = ι.

We arrange for q and a conjugate of r to be literal rotations in one common Euclidean disk, while preserving the common square/cube globally. Define

P = [[1,−1/2],[0,√3/2]].

Its determinant is positive, and

PRP⁻¹ = [[1/2,√3/2],[−√3/2,1/2]],

which is clockwise rotation by π/3. The path

P_t = [[1,−t/2],[0,1+t(√3/2−1)]],  0 ≤ t ≤ 1,

runs through GL⁺(2,R) from I to P. In the standard embedded coordinate disk of radius 1/4 about 0, take a smooth radial cutoff κ that is 1 on radius ≤ 1/32 and 0 on radius ≥ 1/16. Extend the time-dependent vector field

W_t(z) = κ(|z|²) (dP_t/dt) P_t⁻¹ z

by zero to the rest of T. Here, more precisely, κ(s) is 1 for s ≤ (1/32)² and 0 for s ≥ (1/16)².

An explicit cutoff is available: let E(u)=exp(−1/u) for u>0 and E(u)=0 for u≤0, and put

K_{a,b}(s) = E(b−s) / (E(b−s)+E(s−a)),  a<b.

Then κ = K_{(1/32)²,(1/16)²}. The denominator is everywhere positive. This defines a C∞ function, constant on the stated intervals.

Let h_t be the flow from time 0 to time t of W_t and put h=h_1. The field is smooth on the compact torus, so the full time-dependent flow exists and each h_t is a C∞ diffeomorphism. It fixes 0 and is identity outside the disk of radius 1/16. Because W_t(−z)=−W_t(z), uniqueness of the flow gives h_t ι = ι h_t everywhere.

For every t, the Frobenius norm of P_t is less than 2: its square is 1+t²/4+[1+t(√3/2−1)]² ≤ 9/4 < 4. Consequently, if |z| ≤ 1/64 then |P_t z| < 1/32 for the whole time interval. The curve t ↦ P_t z therefore solves the cutoff differential equation exactly, and uniqueness shows

h(z) = Pz for |z| ≤ 1/64.

Set y = h r h⁻¹ and x = q. These are global smooth torus diffeomorphisms. Since h commutes with ι,

x² = y³ = ι

as global map equalities, not merely local identities.

Also, y agrees with PRP⁻¹ on a disk of radius 1/512. Indeed ||P⁻¹|| ≤ ||P⁻¹||F = √(8/3) < 2 and ||R|| ≤ ||R||F = √3 < 2. For |z| ≤ 1/512, both P⁻¹z and RP⁻¹z lie in the radius-1/64 disk where h=P; the same calculation identifies h⁻¹z=P⁻¹z. Thus x and y are the respective clockwise rotations by π/2 and π/3 throughout the common disk D of radius 1/512.

In particular D and every smaller concentric disk are invariant under both x and y. This invariance will be essential for global commutation below.

## 3. A common commuting collar flow and the exact relation

Choose the second radial cutoff

λ(s) = K_{(1/2048)²,(1/1024)²}(s).

For every real angle θ define a smooth torus diffeomorphism fθ by

fθ(z) = rotation by angle θ λ(|z|²) applied to z

on the radius-1/512 coordinate disk D, and fθ=id outside D. The definitions agree on an open collar because λ is zero for radius ≥ 1/1024. At 0 the formula is an ordinary linear rotation because λ=1 near 0. In polar coordinates it preserves r and adds θ λ(r²) to the angle. Hence fθ fφ = fθ+φ and fθ⁻¹=f−θ; all these maps and inverses are C∞.

**Global commutation.** Each fθ commutes with both x and y. On D all maps are radial rotations, so they commute there. Outside D, fθ is identity; moreover x and y carry the complement of D to itself because they are bijections preserving D. Hence the two compositions agree outside D too. This verifies commutation globally and is stronger than checking it only where the cutoff varies.

Define

X = fπ/2 x,  Y = fπ/3 y.

The commutation just proved and x²=y³=ι give

X² = fπ ι = Y³.                                      (1)

Near 0, the clockwise rotation of x or y is exactly canceled by the respective positive-angle f. Therefore X and Y are identity on the entire disk of radius 1/2048.

Remove the open disk of radius ρ=1/4096 and call the resulting compact one-holed torus N. X and Y restrict to smooth diffeomorphisms of N, equal to identity on a collar of ∂N. Define on N

A0 = Y⁻¹ X,  B0 = X⁻¹ Y².

Now there is a short exact group calculation:

A0 B0 = Y,
A0 B0 A0 = X,
B0 A0 B0 = X⁻¹ Y³ = X⁻¹ X² = X.

Thus A0 B0 A0 = B0 A0 B0 as actual smooth maps on all of N. All four maps X,Y,A0,B0 and their inverses are identity on the same boundary collar.

The sixth power of the product is also known exactly:

(A0 B0)^6 = Y^6 = f2π y^6 = f2π.                    (2)

Since y³=ι, indeed y^6=id. The map f2π restricts on N to a single full annular twist: it is identity near ∂N, its continuous angular lift changes by precisely 2π between the inner and outer edges of the annulus, and it is identity off that annulus. Therefore its relative-boundary mapping class is δ^ε for ε ∈ {−1,+1}. Keeping this sign temporarily undetermined makes the subsequent argument independent of the angular-coordinate convention for a right boundary twist.

## 4. Verification of the exact twist classes and zero central offset

Capping N back to T with its center marked, the maps X and Y have homology actions Q and R. The maps fθ are isotopic to identity through maps fixing 0, and h is isotopic to identity through maps fixing 0; thus the preceding statement also holds in Mod(T,0), not just at an unmarked level. Consequently A0 and B0 cap to U and V, respectively.

By the exact capping sequence there are integers k,l such that, in Mod(N,∂N),

[A0] = u δ^k,  [B0] = v δ^l.                   (3)

Both the constructed maps and the classes u,v satisfy their braid relation. Since δ is central, substituting (3), canceling the common braid word, and using its infinite order gives

2k+l = k+2l, hence k=l.                             (4)

The two-chain relation then gives

[(A0 B0)^6] = (uv)^6 δ^{12k} = δ^{1+12k}.

Equation (2) identifies the same class as δ^ε. Thus

1+12k = ε,  ε ∈ {−1,+1}.

The only integral solution is k=0 and ε=+1. Equation (4) gives l=0. Therefore

[A0]=u,  [B0]=v

with the exact shear-handed signs specified in §1 and no central boundary-twist offset. This also resolves the convention-independent sign left temporarily open in §3. An abstract B3 action without this computation would not suffice.

## 5. Right-handed normalization and transfer to the prescribed pair

First put A1=A0^s and B1=B0^s, where s is the common sign fixed in §1. These maps have [A1]=tα0 and [B1]=tβ0. If s=−1, inverting the already established relation gives A0⁻¹B0⁻¹A0⁻¹=B0⁻¹A0⁻¹B0⁻¹, so A1B1A1=B1A1B1 holds in either case. Thus this is an explicit normalization to the requested right-handed classes, without a naming convention being assumed.

A regular neighborhood M of two oriented-surface curves α,β meeting once is a one-holed torus. The labeled model pair (α0,β0) and (α,β) have orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic regular neighborhoods. One way to see this is to thicken their respective four-valent one-vertex graphs, using the cyclic order from the surface orientation; the unique transverse intersection gives the same ribbon graph. Extend across their boundary collars. Thus there is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism j:N→M sending the isotopy classes of α0,β0 to those of α,β, in that order. If the initial curves were only topological, smooth their pair by an ambient isotopy first; their twist mapping classes are unchanged.

On M take j A1 j⁻¹ and j B1 j⁻¹; on S minus the interior of M take identity. Because both maps already equal identity on an open collar of ∂M, these formulas glue to C∞ diffeomorphisms A,B of S, with C∞ inverses. No derivative matching or singular-point smoothing is left implicit. The equality ABA=BAB holds separately on M and on its complement and agrees on the overlap. Extension of a right twist from an oriented subsurface is precisely the right twist about the same curve in the ambient surface, so the resulting mapping classes are [A]=tα and [B]=tβ.

This proves the theorem. In particular it proves the requested result for every allowed negative-Euler-characteristic oriented surface, including closed surfaces of genus at least two. The closed torus served only as an auxiliary construction before removing a disk.

## 6. Prior work and what the proof does not infer

Mann–Tshishiku [MT, Theorem 4.1] proves a smooth section for the three-strand braid group acting on a disk with three marked points. Its proof arranges rigid translations near the three marked points and identity near the external boundary. That additional local information is relevant to lifting through a double branched cover, but the proof above does not need such a lift. It gives the one-holed-torus model and its exact central normalization directly. The positive smooth realization is therefore closely connected to existing published work; it is not a consequence of the high-strand non-realization statements alone.

Salter–Tshishiku's n≥5/n≥6 obstruction and Chen's high-strand homeomorphism results do not apply to this two-generator construction. Likewise, this theorem lifts the particular two-generator braid homomorphism B3→Mod(S); it does not assert that every additional relation in its ambient mapping-class image holds for the chosen maps. For example, a boundary twist may become trivial after including the model in a once-punctured torus. The construction does not provide a section for the full mapping class group of a genus≥2 surface and does not contradict Markovic's non-realization theorem.
