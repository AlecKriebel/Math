## 1. Exact claim and original target

The independently retrieved [publisher report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46762?nt=1), Problem 9, printed p.2527 / physical p.53, asks whether two essential curves meeting transversely once admit representatives of their respective right-twist mapping classes satisfying the braid relation as actual maps, and asks this successively for homeomorphisms, C1 maps, and maps differentiable r times. The requested relation is precisely `ABA=BAB`. It does not request a section for the whole ambient mapping class group or the full subgroup image including every relation that the image acquires on a special ambient surface.

The manuscript proves the right statement for oriented smooth surfaces: actual C-infinity diffeomorphisms with smooth inverses, with the specified twist mapping classes and exactly this map equality. This implies all three asked regularities. The report's local construction works for negative Euler characteristic, including closed genus at least two; its use of a closed torus as an auxiliary model is harmless. Its stronger extension to other oriented surfaces is also justified locally. No assertion for arbitrary nonorientable surfaces has been verified or is needed under the manuscript's explicit oriented-surface interpretation. The original report does not separately spell out an orientation convention in Problem 9; the manuscript makes that convention explicit instead of silently resolving twists on nonorientable surfaces.

Success criterion used in this audit: for every permitted prescribed pair, obtain actual smooth maps whose relative twist classes are correct before extension, and preserve the exact braid relation after extension. A homological realization alone, an abstract B3 action with unknown generator classes, or a section only after capping would fail this criterion.

## 2. Marked capping is the correct quotient

Let N be a compact oriented torus with one boundary component, fixed pointwise. Let `Cap: Mod(N, boundary N) -> Mod(T,0)` extend by the identity over a disk, retaining its center as a marked point. The exact sequence used in the candidate is

`1 -> <delta> -> Mod(N, boundary N) -> Mod(T,0) -> 1`,

where delta is the boundary-parallel twist and `<delta>` is infinite cyclic and central. This is precisely the marked version of the capping result in Farb-Margalit Proposition 3.19, printed pp.89-90; their printed p.107 expressly records centrality. The one-holed-torus calculation on printed p.92 independently identifies the kernel as generated by `(uv)^6`, rather than `(uv)^3`. Source: [primary-authored university-hosted draft](https://pagine.dm.unipi.it/~a019210/Farb%20Magalit_Primer%20on%20Teichmuller%20theory.pdf). The corresponding statement for an unmarked cap is a different general construction and is not substituted here.

The homology action identifies `Mod(T,0)` with `SL(2,Z)`; the marked-torus case is verified directly on FM printed p.57. There is also a useful independent check specific to the torus: given an unmarked isotopy F_t between maps fixing 0, the formula `G_t(z)=F_t(z)-F_t(0)` corrects it to an isotopy fixing 0, with the same endpoints. Thus retaining this one marked point creates no invisible point-pushing ambiguity in the homology classification. The proof's h_t and f_theta fix 0 throughout their isotopies, so the capped X,Y really have marked classes Q,R, and the capped A0,B0 have marked classes U,V.

Infinite order of delta need not be inferred merely from winding inside an annulus whose inclusion has not been checked. Here is a direct independent control: choose a base point on boundary N and identify `pi_1(N)=F(a,b)`. The boundary loop is `c=[a,b]` up to inversion. A boundary twist acts on this based fundamental group by conjugation by c or c inverse. If delta to the n were isotopic to the identity relative to the boundary, conjugation by c to the n would be the identity. The free group F(a,b) has trivial center, while the cyclically reduced commutator has nontrivial powers of reduced length `4|n|` for n nonzero. Hence n=0. Centrality follows geometrically because any boundary-fixing mapping class sends the boundary-parallel twist curve to itself with its surface-induced handedness.

## 3. The full center and capping kernel are different

For the braid generators u,v on N, set `z=(uv)^3=(uvu)^2`. The element z is central, but caps to `-I`, not to the identity. Its square is `delta=(uv)^6`, which generates the marked capping kernel. This is the possible factor-of-two trap, and the candidate avoids it.

The candidate's matrices satisfy `Q^2=R^3=-I`, `Q^4=R^6=I`, `R^-1 Q=U`, and `Q^-1 R^2=V`. They therefore match the square/cube central presentation and the marked torus, with the full boundary twist measured by the sixth power of the product. The exact matrix control in `verify_algebra.py` independently verifies these equalities and rejects the false claim that the braid center caps trivially to SL(2,Z).

## 4. Exact braid equality and zero offsets

In an arbitrary group assume only `X^2=Y^3`, and put `A=Y^-1 X`, `B=X^-1 Y^2`. Direct cancellation gives

`AB=Y`,

`ABA=X`,

`BAB=X^-1 Y^3=X`.

No commutation assumption or mapping-class quotient is used in those three cancellations. Simultaneous inversion of the resulting palindromic braid equation yields the same braid equation for A inverse and B inverse. Thus handedness correction by simultaneous inversion is valid.

The candidate separately supplies the global commutation needed to obtain `X^2=Y^3`: the disk D is invariant under both base maps, all relevant maps are radial rotations on D, and the cutoff maps are the identity on its complement. This is a global argument, not merely a commutation check on the transition annulus. It also gives the exact map equality `(A0 B0)^6=Y^6=f_(2pi)` since `y^6=id`.

Capping identifies the classes as `Abar=u delta^k` and `Bbar=v delta^l`. Centrality and the two braid relations give

`uvu delta^(2k+l)=vuv delta^(k+2l)`,

so infinite order of delta gives `k=l`. The same-handed two-chain relation `(uv)^6=delta` is FM Proposition 4.12 at printed pp.112-113, specialized to a chain of length two. Consequently

`[(A0 B0)^6]=delta^(1+12k)`.

The explicit annular map has class `delta^epsilon`, epsilon equal to +1 or -1. Equality of these classes gives `1+12k=epsilon`. For epsilon=-1 the unique rational candidate is k=-1/6 and is not integral. For epsilon=+1 it is k=0. Hence both offsets vanish. This determines the sign of the annular class at the same time; it does not assume that sign. All prerequisites of this arithmetic, especially primitiveness of the annular twist and infinite order in N, were separately checked. There is no circular appeal to a realization or to a section to establish the mapping-class facts.

## 5. Unit winding, including the normalization of the lift

On the annulus from inner radius rho to any outer radius beyond the cutoff, the actual map is `(r,theta) -> (r,theta+2pi lambda(r^2))`, with lambda exactly 1 near the inner boundary and 0 near the outer boundary. It fixes both boundary circles pointwise. In the universal cover choose the angular lift fixing the inner edge: it adds `2pi(lambda-1)`. That lift adds 0 at the inner edge and `-2pi` at the outer edge. Thus its annular winding is one unit in absolute value. The class is a primitive annular twist, not zero, a half twist, or a higher power. A homotopy of the radial profile relative to its constant edge values identifies it with the standard annular twist representative. Extension along this boundary-parallel annulus makes its class delta or delta inverse in N. FM's annulus classification on printed pp.53-54 supplies an additional primary-source check of this normalized-lift invariant.

The parameter path f_(t theta) is an isotopy fixing the capped center, but, when theta=2pi, it does not fix boundary N pointwise at intermediate times. It therefore proves triviality after marked capping without invalidating the nontrivial relative-boundary winding. This distinction is essential and is respected by the proof.

## 6. Handedness and prescribed curve labels

FM printed p.68 calls its displayed annular map a left twist and lists the homology matrices `[[1,-1],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[1,1]]`. These are U inverse and V inverse, as stated in the candidate. U,V therefore correspond to the same, opposite handed choice, which is right in those conventions. Regardless of naming convention, a single common sign changes both to prescribed right twists. The two-chain relation for the inverse pair follows by inversion and the conjugacy of vu to uv. The algebra above validates simultaneous inversion of the exact map relation.

The model pair is un-oriented as curves; assigning temporary orientations to match the surface's cyclic order causes no restriction. Orientation-preserving conjugacy of subsurfaces preserves the right-twist convention and the order of the two labels. The argument does not accidentally exchange alpha and beta or reverse only one twist.

## 7. Neighborhood and gluing, including ambient extra relations

The union of two smooth curves meeting transversely once is a ribbon graph with one vertex, two edges, and alternating half-edges. Its boundary permutation has a single cycle, so its oriented regular neighborhood has one boundary component; Euler characteristic -1 then forces genus one. The small independent ribbon calculation in `verify_algebra.py` reproduces this. A pair with tangent contact or with a nonorientable neighborhood would not meet these hypotheses and is not silently admitted.

The labeled ribbon structures determine an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of their regular neighborhoods, taking the model curve classes to the prescribed classes. A compact neighborhood can be taken arbitrarily small around the compact pair, disjoint from existing boundary and from the usual marked points or punctures. On a noncompact surface the same compact neighborhood suffices. This uses the standard surface meaning of marked points or punctures, disjoint from the curves; no assertion for a non-discrete marking accumulating onto the curves is intended.

Because A0,B0 are exactly the identity on an open boundary collar, conjugation to the neighborhood and extension by the identity yield global smooth diffeomorphisms with smooth inverses. The support is a compact subset of the neighborhood's interior. The map relation survives extension pointwise, and extension of the twist classes is precisely the ambient twist class. Injectivity of the ambient inclusion map on mapping classes is unnecessary.

In particular, in a once-punctured torus the boundary twist becomes trivial as an ambient mapping class, although the annular map f_(2pi) remains an actual nonidentity map. Therefore the result realizes the prescribed B3 homomorphism and need not factor through its SL(2,Z) image. This is a concrete control separating the target from an unjustified section claim. No realization of the full genus-at-least-two mapping class group is inferred.
