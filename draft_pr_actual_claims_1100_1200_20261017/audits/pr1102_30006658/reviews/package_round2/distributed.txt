                      Vanishing of the cellularization map for
                        non-p-nilpotent component groups
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                         Independent researcher
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                Abstract
          Let G be a compact Lie group whose component group is not p-nilpotent, and let k be any
      ﬁeld of characteristic p. For R = C∗ (Ω((BG)∧
                                                  p ); k), we prove that the canonical k-cellularization
      map Γk R → R induces zero in every homotopy degree. This answers Greenlees’s Question 2.2 in
      Oberwolfach Report 9/2026. No adjoint-orientability, component-action, or spectral-sequence-
      collapse assumption is needed. The proof combines classical proxy-smallness and the ﬁnite-ideal
      theorem for Hopf algebras: each homotopy class in a cellular source factors through a module
      with ﬁnite total homology, whose image in an inﬁnite-dimensional graded Hopf algebra must
      vanish.


1    The question and its answer
Fix a prime p and a ﬁeld k of characteristic p. Write
                                    X = (BG)∧
                                            p,            R = C∗ (ΩX; k),
where completion is Bousﬁeld–Kan completion and is taken before looping. We regard R as an aug-
mented diﬀerential graded k-algebra, equivalently an Hk-algebra; π∗ denotes its homology. Modules
are left modules. The k-cellular objects form LocR (k), the smallest localizing subcategory contain-
ing the augmentation module k. The canonical cellular approximation is denoted ν : Γk R → R. A
ﬁnite group is p-nilpotent if it has a normal subgroup of order prime to p and p-power index.
Theorem 1. For every compact Lie group G with non-p-nilpotent component group π0 G, the map
                                        πn (ν) : πn (Γk R) −→ πn R
is zero for every integer n.
    Greenlees asks precisely this vanishing question for positive-dimensional G in [1, Question 2.2,
p. 594]. The theorem also includes ﬁnite groups. We do not assert that ν is a null morphism in
the derived category. The proof avoids the untwisted Gorenstein-duality description of its source,
so orientability does not enter.
    The Hopf-algebra ﬁniteness input is classical, credited to Sweedler in [4, §2.4]; compact proxies
and the Morita machinery are due to Dwyer–Greenlees–Iyengar [3]. Filtered approximation by
ﬁnite constructions is also explicitly developed in [6]. Our use of these ingredients is to answer the
stated question. Greenlees’s ﬁnite-group result and restricted compact-Lie conclusions remain prior
work: see [2, Lemmas 6.2 and 6.5, Remark 6.6]. In particular, the latter compact-Lie statements
use a nilpotent-action hypothesis, a restricted image, or an additional tensor-collapse hypothesis.
No novelty is asserted for the constituent ﬁniteness and approximation results.

Lemma 2. An inﬁnite-dimensional graded Hopf algebra over a ﬁeld, with the Koszul sign symmetry,
has no nonzero ﬁnite-dimensional graded left or right ideal.

Proof. We ﬁrst recall the ordinary Hopf-algebra argument. Let B be an ordinary Hopf algebra, S
its antipode, and I 6= 0 a ﬁnite-dimensional right ideal. Set

                                 J = span{(1 ⊗ f )∆(i) : i ∈ I, f ∈ B ∗ }.

it. The counit shows I ⊆ J, and coassociativity shows ∆(J) ⊆ J ⊗ B. It is a right ideal, since

Thus J = B, forcing B to be ﬁnite-dimensional. Applying the argument to B op,cop , with the same

    Now let H be graded. In characteristic two, forgetting the grading yields an ordinary Hopf
algebra. Otherwise reduce the grading modulo two and use the standard parity bosonization
B = H#kC2 , where g 2 = 1. For homogeneous h, a and i, j ∈ {0, 1}, its formulas are

                            (h#g i )(a#g j ) = (−1)i|a| ha#g i+j ,
                                                 ∑

                                 εB (h#g i ) = εH (h),

Here parity exponents are modulo two. These give an ordinary Hopf algebra: for example the
multiplicativity sign is (|h(2) | + i)|a(1) | + i|a(2) | = |h(2) ||a(1) | + i|a|. The graded antipode identities
give both ordinary antipode identities. If I is a graded one-sided ideal of H, then I#kC2 is the
corresponding one-sided ideal of B. Its dimension is 2 dim I, whereas dim B = 2 dim H. The
ordinary result proves the assertion.


3    A cellular vanishing criterion
For an augmented diﬀerential graded k-algebra A, write thickA (K) for ﬁnite construction from K
using shifts, ﬁnite sums, cones and retracts. The augmentation module k is proxy-small if some K
satisﬁes
                           K ∈ thickA (A) ∩ thickA (k),   k ∈ LocA (K).
Then K is compact and LocA (K) = LocA (k); see [3, Deﬁnition 4.6, Remark 4.8].

Lemma 3. If k is proxy-small over A, every class in the homology of a k-cellular A-module M is
the image of a class in some P ∈ thickA (K). Each such P has ﬁnite-dimensional total homology
over k.

                                                         2
Proof. Put E = REndA (K). Compact Morita theory identiﬁes right E-modules with LocA (K),
with inverse N 7→ N ⊗L E K; this is the compact-generator case of [3, Theorem 4.9]. Every diﬀerential
graded E-module is a ﬁltered homotopy colimit of perfect modules. For completeness, choose a semi-
free resolution with a well-ordered basis whose diﬀerentials use ﬁnitely many earlier generators. The
dependency closure of a ﬁnite set of generators is ﬁnite: a ﬁnitely branching inﬁnite dependency tree
would have an inﬁnite branch, contradicting strict descent in a well-order. The ﬁnite dependency-
closed submodules are ﬁnite semi-free, form a ﬁltered system, and exhaust the resolution. Their
union computes its homotopy colimit.
    Derived tensor with K preserves these colimits and carries these stages to thickA (K). Homology
commutes with ﬁltered homotopy colimits, so every class comes from a stage. Finally K ∈ thickA (k),
and ﬁnite total homology is preserved under shifts, ﬁnite sums, cones and retracts. Thus all stages
have ﬁnite total homology. See also [6, Lemma 4.1 and its proof].

Proposition 4. Suppose k is proxy-small over A, and the multiplication on H∗ (A) admits a graded
Hopf-algebra structure over k of inﬁnite total dimension. Then every A-linear map f : M → A
with M ∈ LocA (k) induces zero on homology.
Proof. Given x ∈ Hn (M ), Lemma 3 provides P ∈ thickA (K), a map u : P → M , and y ∈ Hn (P )
with u∗ (y) = x. The image of H∗ (f u) is a graded left ideal of H∗ (A), by A-linearity, and has ﬁnite
total dimension. Lemma 2 makes this ideal zero. Hence f∗ (x) = 0, in every degree.

    The approximation is class by class. It does not say that a cellular module itself has ﬁnite
homology, or that k is compact. The inﬁnite-dimensional hypothesis is essential: when A = k, its
cellularization is the identity.


4    Application to completed loop chains
Proof of Theorem 1. Set R0 = C∗ (ΩX; Fp ). Dwyer–Greenlees–Iyengar [3, §5.7, p. 382] prove that
Fp is proxy-small over R0 for every compact Lie group G. Their argument embeds G in SU (n), uses
the ﬁnite homogeneous space SU (n)/G to construct a cochain proxy, and transfers it to completed
loop chains by their double-centralizer and Morita results. It does not require orientability or a
nilpotent action of π0 G on the identity component.
    Chains commute with scalar extension:
                                           R ≃ R0 ⊗Fp k.
Extending a compact proxy K0 gives K = K0 ⊗Fp k. Scalar extension preserves ﬁnite constructions
and arbitrary homotopy colimits, so K is compact, ﬁnitely built from k, and builds k. Thus k is
proxy-small over R. This uses chains, and does not interchange an inﬁnite cochain dual with scalar
extension.
   Pontryagin multiplication, diagonal and inversion in a loop-group model give H∗ (ΩX; k) its
graded Hopf structure. Künneth over a ﬁeld identiﬁes homology of a product with the graded
tensor product. Neither connectedness of ΩX nor ﬁnite total homology is needed.
   Ishiguro [5, Proposition 1.3(a), p. 199] proves the necessary implication
                             BG is p-compact =⇒ π0 G is p-nilpotent.
Here p-compactness of BG means ﬁnite total mod-p homology of Ω((BG)∧      p ). Taking the contra-
positive under our hypothesis gives dimFp H∗ (ΩX; Fp ) = ∞. Flat ﬁeld extension preserves inﬁnite
dimension, so dimk H∗ (R) = ∞. Proposition 4 applies to the canonical R-linear map Γk R → R
and proves the theorem.
