            A negative-curvature normal barrier on an algebraic cone
                        that is not a hyperbolicity cone

                                                         Abstract
              We give an explicit aﬃrmative answer to Tunçel’s 2017 existential question about alge-
          braic convex cones with negative-curvature normal barriers. (     The one-sided

          {(x, y, z) ≥ 0 : xy 2 ≥ z 3 } admits the barrier F = −25 log z(xy 2 − z 3 ) with parameter 100 and

1        The problem and the example
Tunçel [1, p. 799, Open Problem 1] asks whether an algebraic convex cone can admit a normal
barrier with negative curvature without being a hyperbolicity cone. Here algebraic means deﬁned by
homogeneous polynomial inequalities. For a regular cone (closed, pointed, convex, with nonempty
interior), negative curvature means
                               D3 F (a)[h, u, u] ≤ 0 (a ∈ int K, h ∈ K, u ∈ R3 ).                              (1)
This is the third-derivative convention of [1, 2], equivalently the concavity of a 7→ DF (a)[h] for
ﬁxed h ∈ K [3, Deﬁnition 3.1]. A normal barrier is a logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant
barrier; we also verify its gradient bound directly.
Theorem 1. Let
                         K = {(x, y, z) ∈ R3 : x, y, z ≥ 0, p(x, y, z) := xy 2 − z 3 ≥ 0}.
Then K is a regular algebraic convex cone. On int K, the function
                                        f = − log p − log z,       F = 25f
is a normal barrier with parameter 100 satisfying (1). No homogeneous polynomial in three variables
has K as its hyperbolicity cone.
   The underlying cone appears in Hildebrand [4, Theorem 3.2, family 5]: take the conjugate
exponents 3, 3/2 and coordinates (x1 , x2 , x3 ) = (x, z, y). We claim no new cone. The theorem
concerns this one-sided cone and the original existential question. It does not resolve the broader
question whether every regular cone admits such a barrier [3, Section 6]. All arguments below are
analytic; the accompanying exact computations are independently checkable supplements.

4    Excluding every hyperbolicity polynomial
Recall that a homogeneous polynomial H is hyperbolic in e if H(e) > 0 and H(v + te) has only real
roots for every v. Its closed hyperbolicity cone is the closure of the positive-eigenvalue region in the
convention of [1]. The direction e lies in the interior of that cone. The boundary lies in {H = 0}:
if all eigenvalues at a boundary point were positive, continuity of roots (with ﬁxed nonzero leading
coeﬃcient) would give an interior neighborhood.
     Suppose that this cone were K. Then e ∈ int K, and the curved boundary patch implies

                                   H(z 3 /y 2 , y, z) = 0   (y, z > 0).

Clearing denominators gives a polynomial identity on an open set in R2 , hence an identity every-
where. In R(y, z)[x] this says x − z 3 /y 2 divides H. The polynomial p = y 2 x − z 3 is primitive over
R[y, z], since gcd(y 2 , z 3 ) = 1, and is irreducible over the fraction ﬁeld, being linear in x. Gauss’s
lemma gives p | H in R[x, y, z].
   Write a = (ex e2y )1/3 and b = ez , so a > b > 0. For v = (0, 0, 1),

                     p(v + te) = a3 t3 − (1 + bt)3
                                                 (                                  )
                               = ((a − b)t − 1) (a2 + ab + b2 )t2 + (a + 2b)t + 1 .

The quadratic discriminant is (a + 2b)2 − 4(a2 + ab + b2 ) = −3a2 < 0. Therefore p(v + te) has
nonreal roots. Since p | H and H(e) > 0, the restricted product is nonzero and these roots cannot
cancel. This contradicts hyperbolicity in e, proving the ﬁnal assertion of Theorem 1. The argument
treats arbitrary homogeneous H, without an assumed degree or a particular deﬁning polynomial.
