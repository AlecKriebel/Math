The mixed paper's Definition 1.3 and Lemma 3.1 already generate the submitted mixed class: choose the fourth-root extension, differential degree one, and the element 1 + beta^2 z in its square subfield. The coefficient after squaring is b z^2/(1+b z^2). Choosing the Artin–Schreier representative a=s^(-4) is legitimate since it differs from s^(-1) by an Artin–Schreier element.

Thus the class's vanishing in the two-field compositum and its presentation as an extra mixed generator are directly antecedent. This is mandatory credit, not a new theorem of the submission.

The original target requires failure after adding the separate bilinear Pfister function-field kernel while preserving anisotropy over L. The 2016 predecessor proves the positive result with exponent-one L only. The mixed paper computes the KL kernel for higher exponents, but its theorems do not state a failure modulo a third independent Pfister kernel. Its Section 6 counterexample has two purely inseparable factors, not the target's separable quadratic factor. It is not valid to infer a strict three-kernel inequality merely by noticing that the displayed mixed summand is written separately.
