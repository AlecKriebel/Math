# Independent norm-obstruction core

Written after the authorized candidate and before opening external theorem bodies.

The critical implication uses no surjectivity of residue norms: if f/c is a norm, remove any integer valuation r by multiplying the norm of theta^{-r}; N(theta^{-1})=s. The resulting unit norm has square residue in kprime because the extension is ramified quadratic, residue degree one, and Galois acts trivially on residue. A complete discrete valuation field with the explicitly Eisenstein degree-two polynomial has e=2 and f=1, so the degree is consumed by e f and no defect can be hidden here. Normalizations give v_D(N(a))=v_T(a), including negative valuations.

The residue contradiction is a universal vector-space argument. Let x=beta^2, y=z^2, tau=gamma^2. Then S=F2(x,y,tau), R=F2(x^2,y,tau), k=R(z), kprime^2=S. Any c0 in k uniquely equals u+v z for u,v in R, including rational c0 with arbitrary denominators. Since z is nonsquare over S (its square y has odd y-valuation), {1,z} is S-linearly independent. If (1+x z)/c0=q belongs to S*, then q u=1 and q v=x, forcing x=v/u in R. The x-valuation forbids this. This is an exclusion of every c0, not a finite generator/sample check.

A cyclic separable quadratic extension has Br(D/E)=E*/N(D*) even in characteristic two and over imperfect E. Under the characteristic-two Kato H_2^2 to Br[2] identification this identifies the entire kernel, not only single quaternion classes known a priori. The full three-summand inclusion reduces by restricting to M: the L-kernel and F(B)-kernel each vanish, leaving only [s^{-1},c). Thus there is no assumption that every global Brauer class is a single symbol.
