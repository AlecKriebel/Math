## Exact original question and admissibility

The original source is Laghribi's contribution, joint with Aravire and O'Ryan, to Oberwolfach Report 5/2016, printed pp.248-251, Question 2 on p.251. The entire contribution, including definitions, preceding results, both questions, and references, was read. The question permits an arbitrary finite purely inseparable L/F, an anisotropic bilinear Pfister form B that remains anisotropic over L, and a separable quadratic K/F, in characteristic two. It asks for equality of the composite Kato kernel and the sum of the three separate kernels. No exponent-one, minimum dimension four, positive-dimensional, smoothness, or geometrically integral condition is imposed. A counterexample in one allowed degree settles this universal question negatively.

The talk defines the form's field by the PROJECTIVE quadric of B(v,v). Its exact predecessor defines the AFFINE quadric field instead; its Section 2.2 explicitly allows every positive fold count, including one. Thus one must distinguish the conventions, but cannot exclude a binary bilinear Pfister form merely because its projective quadric is zero-dimensional.

For B=<1,t> over E with t nonsquare, the associated homogeneous equation is X^2+tY^2. Its polynomial is irreducible. Its projective zero-locus is entirely in the Y-chart, since the scheme-theoretic intersection with Y=0 has no projective point. The chart ring E[x]/(x^2+t) is a field. Hence the projective quadric is the integral scheme Spec E(sqrt(t)), of dimension zero; it is not geometrically reduced. Geometric reducedness is neither true for this quasilinear quadric nor required by the source. The candidate uses a two-dimensional regular symmetric bilinear form, not a one-dimensional form or a nonsingular quadratic Pfister form.

In the affine convention, the function field is E(sqrt(t))(Y), with Y transcendental. At n=1 this changes none of the relevant kernels: Br(E') -> Br(E'(Y)) is injective for every field E'. One elementary verification is that a finite-dimensional central division algebra over E' remains a domain after adjoining a central indeterminate and central rational functions, hence remains a division algebra. The differential/Brauer identification then gives the assertion for H_2^2. Thus this counterexample also survives the predecessor's affine convention.

## Independent verification of all construction hypotheses

Write F=F2(b,z,s,t), with independent variables, beta^4=b, theta^2+theta=s^-1, and gamma^2=t. The independent-variable valuations prove the needed nonsquare statements without perfection assumptions.

1. The b-valuation of b is one, so b is not a square in F. Consequently X^4-b is irreducible and L=F(beta) has degree four, is purely inseparable, and has exponent exactly two: beta^2 is not in F, while the fourth power of every element of L lies in F. L is the rational field F2(beta,z,s,t).
2. If an element x has a pole for the s-valuation, x^2+x has even pole order; if x has no pole, neither does x^2+x. Thus s^-1 is not Artin-Schreier over F. The equation has derivative one, so K/F is genuinely separable quadratic. The same argument applies over L and later over k'((s)).
3. t has odd t-valuation in L and so is not a square there. Therefore X^2+tY^2 is anisotropic over L and over F. Its underlying diagonal bilinear matrix diag(1,t) is nondegenerate, and it is precisely a one-fold bilinear Pfister form.
4. A separable algebraic extension of a field cannot contain a new purely inseparable square root over that field. Since K/F and LK/L are separable, t stays nonsquare over K and LK. The notation K(B) therefore denotes a legitimate field, not a product or a chosen component.
5. M=L F(B)=F2(beta,z,s,gamma), and the full composite is M(theta). This rational description follows by the displayed algebraic substitutions, not by presuming linear disjointness of arbitrary inseparable extensions.

These arguments establish all stated hypotheses. In particular the exponent-two L is outside the predecessor's exponent-one multiquadratic theorem, so that theorem does not contradict the construction.

## Independent verification that the class dies

Set f=1+beta^2 z and h=beta^2 z/f in L. Since d(beta^2)=0, dlog(f)=h dlog(z), and h^2=bz^2/(1+bz^2). All these nonzero denominators are guaranteed by algebraic independence. In H_2^2(L), the difference of s^-4 h^2 dlog(z) and s^-2 h dlog(z) is the Artin-Schreier differential of s^-2 h dlog(z). It therefore vanishes. The latter form is s^-2 dlog(f); replacing s^-2 by s^-1 changes it by an Artin-Schreier multiple of the logarithmic differential dlog(f). Thus the candidate class restricts to [s^-1,f) over L. It vanishes after adjoining theta. This proves membership in the full composite kernel already over LK.

No implication here uses the desired nonmembership, finite searches, or an old audit result.

## Independent verification of full three-summand nonmembership

Suppose the original class were x+y+w, with the three summands in the L, K, and F(B) kernels respectively. The characteristic-two differential/Brauer identification is natural under field extension. The cyclic relative Brauer theorem for the separable quadratic K/F says y=[s^-1,c) for some c in F*. This holds over an imperfect field: relative classes are cyclic crossed products, and the associated norm quotient is F*/N(K*).

Restrict to M=L F(B). This kills BOTH x and w, so it forces [s^-1,f/c)=0 over M. This step is essential: merely excluding the first two kernels would not disprove the question. The norm criterion is applicable because M(theta)/M is a separable quadratic field extension. The candidate's use of multiplicativity in the second symbol slot is valid.

For completeness I checked the local obstruction directly. Let k=F2(b,z,t), k'=F2(beta,z,gamma), so M=k'(s). Embed M in D=k'((s)). In T=D(theta), pi=theta^-1 satisfies pi^2+s pi+s=0. This is Eisenstein, with e=2 and residue degree one, over the complete DVR k'[[s]], regardless of imperfection of k'. The conjugate is pi/(1+pi), and N(pi)=s. Thus every power s^r, including negative powers, is a norm.

Write c=s^r c0(1+O(s)), where c0 belongs to k*. If f/c is a norm, then u=s^r f/c is a norm and a unit. For normalized valuations, v_D(N(a))=v_T(a). A norm that is a unit therefore comes from a unit. Conjugation acts trivially on the residue field, so the residue of such a norm is a square in k'. Consequently f/c0 must be a square in k'. No sufficiency of this residue condition is being assumed; necessity suffices.

Now S=k'^2=F2(beta^2,z^2,gamma^2) and R=F2(beta^4,z^2,gamma^2). We have k=R(z), of degree two over R. Every c0 in k has a UNIQUE expression u0+v0 z with u0,v0 in R. Also z is not in S, so 1,z are independent over S. If f=q c0 with q in S*, comparison gives q u0=1 and q v0=beta^2. Hence beta^2=v0/u0 belongs to R. This is impossible: its valuation as the independent variable beta^2 has value one, whereas nonzero elements of R have even valuation at that variable. This excludes every possible c in F*, with all rational functions and all positive or negative s-orders covered.

The contradiction proves full three-summand nonmembership. It also independently forces the class itself to be nonzero. The argument is universal, not a conclusion from a finite enumeration of possible cyclic representatives.

## Boundary cases and degree conventions

The obstruction is genuinely at n=1: H_2^2 is the two-torsion Brauer group, whereas n=0 gives H_2^1=E/wp(E). The talk explicitly sets Omega^0=F and allows 0<=n in its preceding theorem; this convention is not manufactured to shift the claimed degree. The modern primary reference below states both identifications explicitly.

The candidate's n=0 equality is sound for every positive-fold B in the original setting. Here is a separate proof. An Artin-Schreier root is separable algebraic over its constant field. It cannot newly occur in a purely inseparable extension. It also cannot newly occur in a rational function field because the constant field is relatively algebraically closed there. For an anisotropic quasilinear form of dimension d>=2, a projective chart expresses its field as a purely inseparable quadratic extension of a rational field in d-2 variables. Anisotropy ensures the defining polynomial remains irreducible.

Separable extension preserves anisotropy of a quasilinear form: coefficients independent over F^2 remain independent after separable base change, by linear disjointness of the separable extension E^2/F^2 and the purely inseparable extension F/F^2. In the present application B_L is anisotropic and KL/L is separable, so B_KL is anisotropic. Over KL the same chart description therefore applies. An Artin-Schreier root over F lying in LK(B) first descends through that purely inseparable chart extension to its rational constant field KL, then through the purely inseparable KL/K to K. This proves precisely the n=0 equality, while the separate L and F(B) kernels are zero.

A harmless convention clarification is available for a paper: state this universal n=0 observation for positive-fold B, or add that if zero-fold forms are admitted with the predecessor's conventional F(B)=F then the zero-fold case is immediate. The projective quadric of <1> is empty and has no literal function field, so the phrase 'all stated data' should not be read as defining that empty scheme's field. This does not affect the actual positive-fold counterexample or any degree claim.

### Auxiliary repair identified and verified: the exponent-one shortcut

The parent relayed a sibling's observation after my independent core review and initial unsealed report. I checked it directly. The first bullet of candidate Section 6 asserts that replacing b by a square u^2 in the base makes the same differential calculation prove xi=[s^-1,1+uz) over the BASE field, and hence membership in the separable quadratic kernel alone. This calculation drops a term: dlog(1+uz)=(u dz+z du)/(1+uz), and du need not be zero in the base. Its vanishing in L does not justify vanishing in F. The main proof does not make this error: its calculation occurs over L, where beta^2 is a square and d(beta^2)=0.

A minimal, fully proved replacement preserves the intended compatibility with the exponent-one theorem. Set b=u^2 and retain beta^4=b, so beta^2=u and L=F(beta) has exponent at most one. The calculation IN L gives xi_L=[s^-1,1+uz)_L. Since 1+uz is now in F, the difference xi-[s^-1,1+uz) belongs to H_2^2(L/F), while the symbol belongs to H_2^2(K/F). Thus xi belongs to the SUM of the L and K kernels. This is sufficient to explain that the modified data do not contradict the exponent-one theorem. The repaired proof uses exactly this replacement and explicitly identifies the omitted du term. I verified its complete diff against the immutable input: no other statement changes. Its correction is adequate. No unsupported claim of membership in the K-kernel alone remains in that bullet. I have not asserted that stronger membership is always false; the original displayed proof simply did not establish it.

The second Section 6 diagnostic is sound: replacing the Pfister slot by z makes adjoining sqrt(z) kill dz and hence the original class. The genuine original example's independent t slot avoids that collapse. These checks are exact deductions, not numerical controls.
