## 3. Mathematical assessment and falsification

The exact claim is a negative instance of the displayed equality in OWR5/2016 Question2 at original index n=1, under its original finite purely inseparable, separable quadratic, and bilinear-Pfister hypotheses. The source on printed248 defines the projective quasilinear-quadric field. Printed251 imposes neither exponent one nor a minimum bilinear dimension of four. The binary positive-fold case is admissible.

1. **Fields and form.** With independent b,z,s,t, the b-valuation proves b nonsquare and X^4-b irreducible. Thus L=F(beta), beta^4=b, has degree4 and exponent2. The odd s-pole proves theta^2+theta=s^-1 defines a genuine separable quadratic field, including over the later completion. The t-valuation proves t nonsquare in L. A separable algebraic extension cannot create a new purely inseparable square root. Consequently B=<1,t> is anisotropic over L and remains so over LK. The projective binary quadric is the integral zero-dimensional scheme with chart field F(sqrt(t)); it need not be smooth or geometrically reduced. M=L F(B)=F2(beta,z,s,gamma), gamma^2=t, and LK(B)=M(theta). In particular the full composite has degree16 over F, rather than the total-degree-four scope of some adjacent Brauer theorems.

2. **Kato/Brauer convention.** The natural H_2^2(E) to Br(E)[2] identification sends u dlog(v) to precisely the displayed characteristic-two quaternion [u,v), with i^2+i=u, j^2=v and ji=(i+1)j. It is compatible with scalar extension. No slot reversal, reciprocal convention, or perfection assumption is hidden. For a genuine separable quadratic field E(eta)/E, the entire relative Brauer group is E*/N(E(eta)*), through cyclic crossed products. Thus every class in that kernel is a single fixed-first-slot symbol [u,c), even though arbitrary Brauer classes need not be single symbols. Milne's checked result is for finite Galois extensions of arbitrary fields, not merely local/global fields. The splitting/norm equivalence is also confirmed by the official characteristic-two Voight statement. These are established inputs; I do not claim a foundational reproof of Kato's theorem or all central-simple-algebra theory.

3. **Vanishing.** In L, f=1+beta^2 z and h=beta^2 z/f satisfy dlog(f)=h dlog(z) and h^2=bz^2/(1+bz^2). The Artin–Schreier relation applied first to s^-2 h dlog(z), then using dlog(f), gives xi_L=[s^-2,f)=[s^-1,f). It dies over LK. The latter identity is in the quotient by exact forms; treating wp as scalar-linear would be invalid. 5. **Universal norm condition.** In D=k'((s)), pi=theta^-1 satisfies pi^2+s pi+s=0, conjugate pi+s, norm s. Every element of T=D(theta) is A+B pi. Its norm is A^2+sAB+sB^2. 6. **Every rational scalar, including zero subcoefficients.** For every nonzero c in k(s), including arbitrary rational denominators and positive, zero or negative s-order, write c=s^r c0(1+O(s)), c0 in k*. Multiplication by N(pi^r)=s^r makes f/c a unit norm and forces f/c0 in k'^2. Put alpha=beta^2, S=k'^2=F2(alpha,z^2,t), R=F2(alpha^2,z^2,t).
