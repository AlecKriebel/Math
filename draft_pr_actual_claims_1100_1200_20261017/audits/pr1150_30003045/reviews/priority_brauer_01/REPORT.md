## Closest overlap: the mixed class is already an ALO generator

The author manuscript of Aravire–Laghribi–O'Ryan, [*Cohomological kernels of mixed extensions in characteristic 2*](https://laghribi.perso.math.cnrs.fr/cohom-kernels.pdf), is dated 15 August 2019; the author catalogue lists Journal of Algebra 542 (2020), 249–276, DOI [10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.09.012). Definition 1.3 and Theorem 1.4 explicitly supply higher-exponent mixed generators. Their introductory discussion already attributes failure of two-field splitting to higher exponent. Section 6's worked Example 6.2 concerns a different decomposition into purely inseparable subfields.

The exact specialization is important. In their generator notation take `n=m=1`, `l=2`, `a=s^-4`, and `f=1+β²z`, with `f0=1`, `f1=z`. The zero term vanishes, and the other term is `(β²z/f)² dlog z`. Multiplication by `a` gives precisely the candidate `ξ`. Their separable field is the same: if `θ²+θ=s^-1`, then `η=θ⁴=θ+s^-1+s^-2` satisfies `η²+η=s^-4` and generates the same field.

Consequently the candidate must not present its mixed generator or its killing over the compositum as a newly discovered mechanism. What still needs the candidate's argument is exclusion of **all three** original kernels, including after the independent Pfister slot. ## Independent norm comparison and remaining target-specific step

The candidate's use of local norms does not require a perfect or finite residue field. Complete `M=k'(s)` to `D=k'((s))`. The polynomial `π²+sπ+s` is Eisenstein, so its quadratic extension has residue degree one and ramification degree two; its uniformizer norm is `s`. A norm that is a unit comes from a unit, whose residue norm is its square. Thus an arbitrary base-field multiplier with leading coefficient `c0∈k*` would force `1+β²z∈k* k'^*²`.

This last failure is an exact rational-function-field statement. Set `S=k'^2` and `R=F2(β⁴,z²,γ²)`. Then `k=R(z)` has basis `1,z`, while `z∉S`. Writing `c0=u+vz`, `u,v∈R`, and comparing coefficients in `1+β²z=q(u+vz)`, `q∈S*`, forces `β²=v/u∈R`, impossible by its odd valuation at `β²=0`. This accounts for the Pfister summand after restriction, rather than merely exhibiting a nonsplit two-field class.

These deductions are my comparison of the corrected candidate with standard norm machinery, not a claim that the norm criterion, total-ramification residue fact, or square-class method is new. A generic old tool plus this target-specific argument is not by itself evidence that the original target was previously recorded as solved. Conversely this reasoning does not certify that the target-specific bridge was historically unknown.
