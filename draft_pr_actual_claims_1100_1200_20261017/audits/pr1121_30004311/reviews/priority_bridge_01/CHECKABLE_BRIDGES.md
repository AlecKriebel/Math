# Checkable prior-implication bridges and obstructions

These are **new audit derivations made on 17 October 2026** from the published formulas identified below. They are not assertions that the sources stated these derived lemmas or answered Cedó's Problem 9. Parent review independently checked the three principal eliminations before this note was finalized.

## 1. A group-action lemma

Let a finite group have the form G=N⋊S, where N is abelian. Assume G is metabelian and every Sylow subgroup of G is abelian. Then every element of S′ acts trivially on N.

Proof. Regard S as the displayed complement in G. For a∈S′, its image in G lies in G′. The subgroup [a,N] is also contained in G′. Since G′ is abelian, a centralizes [a,N]. Writing the automorphism of N induced by a as 1+D gives D²N=0 (changing the commutator convention only multiplies D by an invertible map).

It suffices to treat an element a∈S′ of order p^e: S′ is finite abelian, so it is generated by such elements. Write N=⊕q Nq. The subgroup Np⋊⟨a⟩ is a p-subgroup of G, hence abelian; therefore D(Np)=0. For q≠p, the identity (1+D)^(p^e)=1 and D²=0 give p^e D=0 on Nq. Multiplication by p^e is invertible on the finite q-group Nq, so D(Nq)=0. Thus D=0. ∎

No claim that a general metabelian group has an abelian normal Sylow subgroup is involved. Finiteness and the abelian-Sylow hypothesis are both used.

## 2. Elimination of the nontrivial-factor family in arXiv:2401.12904v2, Theorem 4.5

Source: [Cedó–Okniński, current arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12904v2), §4, especially its opening definition of A1 and Theorem 4.5 (PDF pp. 12–19). The 2025 journal DOI is 10.1016/j.jpaa.2025.108025. This audit read the arXiv body; access to the final journal body failed, so no final-body identity is asserted.

The source defines A1 as the semidirect product of trivial braces on A and T=⟨t⟩. In coordinates, its addition and multiplication are

    (a,t^i)+(c,t^j)=(a+c,t^(i+j)),
    (a,t^i)∘(c,t^j)=(a+t^i(c),t^(i+j)).

The construction assumes t−id is an automorphism of A. Theorem 4.5 additionally assumes that A has finite exponent n>1 and ord(t) is coprime to n. The resulting brace has the form

    B=N⋊°A1,  N=H′/H1 a trivial brace,
    (B,∘)=N⋊α(A⋊T),

and N is an abelian group of exponent dividing n. The cocycle b takes values in A×{id}. Its exact values are unnecessary for this elimination.

**Claim.** No finite nontrivial simple brace in this family can have both a metabelian multiplicative group and abelian multiplicative Sylow subgroups.

Proof. If B is finite, A and N are finite. Since t−id is onto A, the embedded subgroup A equals [T,A] inside A⋊T; therefore A⊆G′, where G=(B,∘). The proof of Lemma 1 applies to each p-element a∈A directly: metabelianity gives (αa−1)²=0 on N; abelian Sylows give αa=1 on Np; coprime order gives αa=1 on each Nq for q≠p. Thus α(A)=1.

For s=(a,id)∈A1, the displayed operations give λs=id on A1. The lambda formula for an asymmetric product with trivial N is

    λ_(f,s)(g,u)=(αs(g), λs(u)−b(αs(g),f)).

Taking f=0 and using the normalized cocycle gives λ_(0,(a,id))=id on B. Hence

    { (0,(a,id)) : a∈A } ⊆ Soc(B).

A≠0, because exp(A)=n>1. This is a nonzero proper socle: A1 includes nontrivial T because t−id invertible and A≠0 force t≠id. Thus simplicity is impossible. ∎

This handles the generally nontrivial second factor in Theorem 4.5 without silently replacing it by a trivial brace. It is an elimination of this particular published construction, not a classification of all simple braces.

## 3. Flattening the general simple extension of arXiv:1705.08493v1, Theorem 3.3

Source: [Bachiller–Cedó–Jespers–Okniński, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08493v1), Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 (PDF pp. 6–8). Theorem 3.3 constructs a simple brace N⋊°S from a trivial brace N, a simple nontrivial brace S, a nondegenerate admissible cocycle, and a suitable action. We show that starting with a simple two-trivial-factor S cannot supply the requested counterexample through this theorem if the resulting group has the target group properties.

Suppose S=U⋊°V is simple and nontrivial, with U,V trivial. Write its action as ρ:V→Aut(U) and its cocycle as b0:U×U→V. Both factors are nonzero. They have operations

    (u,v)+(u′,v′)=(u+u′,v+v′+b0(u,u′)),
    (u,v)∘(u′,v′)=(u+ρv(u′),v+v′).

The general admissibility forces b0 to be biadditive; equivalently this follows by comparing λ_(u,0)λ_(u′,0) with λ_(u+u′,0). Simplicity implies rad(b0)=0, because (u,0) for u in the radical would lie in the socle.

Let W=Σv Im(ρv−1). Then W×V is an ideal of S: it is an additive subgroup and lambda-invariant by the displayed formulas, and multiplicatively normal because conjugation changes the U-coordinate by W and preserves W. It is nonzero, so simplicity gives W=U. Hence S′=U.

Now let B=N⋊°S be any finite admissible asymmetric product with N trivial, and assume G=(B,∘) is a metabelian group with abelian Sylow subgroups. Let its action be α:S→Aut(N), and cocycle b:N×N→S. The group is N⋊αS. Lemma 1 gives α(S′)=α(U)=1.

Again b is biadditive: on arbitrary (x,s), the maps λ_(n,0) and λ_(m,0) subtract b(x,n) and b(x,m) from the S-coordinate, while their product is λ_(n+m,0). The symmetric cocycle then is additive in both variables. The source's compatibility is

    λs(b(n,m))=b(αs(n),αs(m)).

For s∈U, its right side is b(n,m). In S the elements fixed by every λ_(u,0), u∈U, are exactly {0}×V: the first coordinate must be in rad(b0), which is zero. Consequently

    b(n,m)=(0,β(n,m)) with β:N×N→V biadditive.

All λs of S fix {0}×V pointwise, so the same compatibility implies β(αs(n),αs(m))=β(n,m). The action α factors through V because α(U)=1.

In the coordinates (n,u,v), the operations in B are therefore

    (n,u,v)+(m,u′,v′)
      =(n+m,u+u′,v+v′+β(n,m)+b0(u,u′)),
    (n,u,v)∘(m,u′,v′)
      =(n+αv(m),u+ρv(u′),v+v′).

This is the asymmetric product of the two trivial braces N⊕U and V, with cocycle β⊕b0 and action αv⊕ρv. Thus B remains in the two-trivial-factor class.

This argument also blocks iteration of Theorem 3.3 from simple two-trivial-factor seeds, whenever the final group satisfies the target group hypotheses: a quotient of a finite metabelian A-group is again a finite metabelian A-group, so the hypotheses descend to each earlier seed/quotient. Starting with a seed already outside the two-trivial-factor class would transfer the target difficulty to an unsupported input, not establish priority.

## 4. What the general matched-product theorem does recover

Sources: [Bachiller, arXiv:1511.08477v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08477v3), Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 (PDF pp. 11–13), and [arXiv:1610.00477v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00477v1), Proposition 2.7 and Theorem 2.6.

Use the primary braces R=xF5[x]/(x5) and H=V×F2 from the submitted PROOF.md. In H,

    (v,z)∘(w,Z)=(v+w,z+Z+β(v,w)),
    e(v,z)=z+Q(v),   e(h∘k)=e(h)+e(k).

Define cross-actions

    α_h(r)=σ^e(h)(r),
    γ_r(v,z)=(M^η(r)v,z).

They are group homomorphisms to brace automorphisms. The identities η(σr)=η(r), Q(Mv)=Q(v), and preservation of β give

    α_(γ_r h)=α_h,   γ_(α_h r)=γ_r.

These satisfy the sufficient matched-product conditions. Theorem 4.2 yields lambda maps

    α_h λ^R_(α_h^−1 r)(u)=(1+r)σ^e(h)(u),
    γ_r λ^H_(γ_r^−1 h)(w,Z)
      =(M^η(r)w, Z+β(v,M^η(r)w)),

exactly the submitted operations. Thus this is indeed an instance of the established general brace-construction formalism.

**Exact remaining gap:** this theorem asserts a brace structure, not simplicity. The general simplicity theorem in arXiv:1610.00477v1, Theorem 2.6, assumes each factor is simple. Here R has the proper nonzero ideal F5x4, and H has the proper nonzero ideal {0}×F2. Theorem 2.6 therefore cannot certify simplicity. Supplying the submitted propagation proof is new reasoning in this audit context, not locating an earlier theorem that already asserts the full target.

## 5. Other named-source families and boundary checks

- In arXiv:1705.08493v1 §5.2, the factor V=(F_p)^n and the final F_p produce the p-subgroup V⋊⟨f⟩. The assumptions require ord(f)=p, so this subgroup is nonabelian: choose v with f(v)≠v, and (0,1) and (v,0) fail to commute. Every Sylow containing this p-subgroup is nonabelian. This rules out Theorem 5.7's entire stated family under the abelian-Sylow target. Setting f=id abandons a theorem hypothesis.
- The wreath family of the same paper, Theorem 5.3, has multiplicative derived length n+1 with n≥2, so it is not metabelian.
- arXiv:2401.12904v2 Theorem 5.3 explicitly reuses the previous §5.2 construction. Its ring R=F_p[x]/(x^(n−1)+⋯+1) is used as a **trivial brace** in the first semidirect step. The variable name R does not make this a higher-nilpotency radical-ring primary brace. Its same-prime subgroup is nonabelian as above.
- For H(p^r,n,Q,f) from arXiv:1610.00477v1 Theorem 3.1, the coordinate q=μ−Q(x) converts the multiplicative group to (Z/p^r)^n⋊⟨q⟩, with q acting by f^q. A nonidentity f gives a nonabelian primary multiplicative group. If f=id, its star product has only the final scalar coordinate and every primary triple star is zero. The submitted R has (x*x)*x=x3≠0 and an abelian circle group, so it cannot be one of these H factors. The published Theorem 6.2 in arXiv:1705.08493v1 explicitly rewrites their iterated matched products as products of two trivial braces.
- The extra nested Example 4.3 of arXiv:1610.00477v1 also flattens: all f_i=id, all cross-actions depend only on q_i and preserve Q_i, and the added actions are coordinate permutations. In coordinates s_i=μ_i−Q_i(x_i), its addition is (x,s)+(y,t)=(x+y,s+t−⊕b_i(x_i,y_i)) and multiplication is (x,s)∘(y,t)=(x+A_s y,s+t). Both x- and s-factors are trivial. This last flattening is a direct audit calculation for that explicit example, not a universal classification of arbitrary nested matched products.


## 6. Current kernel–wreath construction: an additional elimination

Source: [Damele, arXiv:2609.20401v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20401v1), §3, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 (PDF pp. 5–6), and §4 Theorem 4.4 (PDF pp. 10–11). This source was located by a final search for brace embeddings; its body was then read rather than classifying it from the abstract.

Let C be any finite seed skew brace with multiplicative group R and additive group A. Let χ:R→T be onto, where T is nontrivial finite abelian. The construction has additive group A^Ω, Ω=T, and multiplicative group

    G=ker ε ≤ W=R^Ω⋊T,
    ε((r_x),u)=(∏_x χ(r_x))u.

The top group acts regularly by permutation of the coordinates. The source proves that simple nontrivial seeds give simple outputs. We do **not** infer preservation of the abelian-Sylow property.

**Claim (new audit derivation).** No output of this kernel–wreath construction can be a finite nontrivial simple classical left brace with abelian multiplicative Sylow subgroups, even without requiring metabelianity and even if the seed is arbitrary.

Proof. Suppose first |T|>2. Choose a prime p dividing |T|, an element u∈T of order p, and a Sylow p-subgroup P of R. Its image under χ is T_p, so choose r∈P with χ(r)=u^−1. Fix a coordinate ω. The element g with top component u and base component r at ω, identity elsewhere, belongs to G.

Choose distinct coordinates ω,ν such that {ω,ν} is not invariant under multiplication by u. This is possible: if p>2, a two-point set cannot be a union of regular u-orbits; if p=2, |T|>2 permits ν outside {ω,uω}. Choose a≠1 in P. The base element h with entries a at ω and a^−1 at ν, top component 1, lies in G because its χ-product is 1. Both g,h belong to the p-group P^Ω⋊⟨u⟩. Conjugation by g moves the support of h from {ω,ν} to u{ω,ν}; base conjugations preserve which entries are nonidentity. Thus ghg^−1≠h, and G contains a nonabelian p-subgroup. Its Sylow p-subgroups cannot be abelian.

Now let T=C2. If a Sylow 2-subgroup P of R has |P|>2, the kernel of χ restricted to P is nonzero. Choose k≠1 there. The base element h supported only at ω with entry k lies in G. The same lift g has the nontrivial top component and conjugates this support to the other coordinate. Again g,h lie in a common 2-group and do not commute.

Consequently an abelian-Sylow output must have T=C2 and |P|=2. Put K=kerχ; it has odd order |R|/2. The subgroup K² of the base R² is normal in the full wreath product W, lies in G, and has exactly the odd part of |G|=|R|². It is therefore a normal Hall odd subgroup of G.

If the output is a classical left brace, A² is abelian, so A is abelian. Every normal Hall subgroup N of the multiplicative group of a finite left brace is an ideal; this is also [Bachiller, arXiv:1511.08477v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08477v3), Proposition 6.1. Directly, let π be the primes dividing |N| and let L be the unique additive Hall π-subgroup. It is lambda-invariant, hence also a multiplicative Hall π-subgroup. Its image in G/N has order dividing two coprime numbers, so L≤N; equal cardinalities give L=N. Thus N is lambda-invariant as well as multiplicatively normal. If K≠1, K² is a nonzero proper ideal, contradicting simplicity. If K=1, then R=C2, the seed is the trivial brace of order 2, and the output has order 4; it is not simple. Explicitly, its lambda action is the top coordinate swap, with a nonzero proper kernel (the diagonal base element), so its socle is nonzero. ∎

The support arguments use the source's actual multiplicative group and regular top action; the result does not rely on the title, the solvability assertion, or an assumption that derived length stays bounded. It is an elimination made now, not a published priority bridge.
