## Exact priority target and falsification criterion

The source is Ferran Cedó, “Finite simple left braces: constructions and problems,” OWR 51/2019, printed 3226–3228, with Problem 9 on printed 3228 / PDF page 22. The [official report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46831) was retrieved and the contribution, question and references inspected.

The priority target is the **full negative answer**: a finite nontrivial simple left brace, with abelian additive group, whose multiplicative group is metabelian and has abelian Sylow subgroups, together with a proof that it is not isomorphic to *any* asymmetric product of two trivial braces. A smaller or differently presented earlier counterexample would defeat priority just as decisively as the identical 20,000-element construction. An earlier general theorem plus a fully checked specialization meeting all these conditions would also defeat priority.

The manuscript's example has additive group C5^4 × C2^5 and multiplicative group (C5^3 ⋊ C2) × (C2^4 ⋊ C5). Its decisive invariant is a nonzero triple star product in its additive Sylow 5-subbrace, whereas every two-trivial-factor asymmetric product with abelian multiplicative Sylows has zero primary triple star products. Nontrivial multiplicative center supplies a supplementary route. Searches therefore included construction mechanisms, group restrictions, primary nilpotence and center, not just this order or its coordinates.

The full-cocycle scope matters historically as well as mathematically: the obstruction must cover every admissible two-trivial-factor presentation. The reviewed manuscript addresses this by deriving biadditivity from admissibility. This review did not locate an earlier full-answer theorem from the existence of more general asymmetric products.

Returning by the Baer transform returns that seed, not the desired counterexample.
- Damele, [2609.20401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20401), 17 September 2026: full PDF retrieved; Theorems 3.1, 3.7, 4.4 and the group mechanism inspected. This is a serious near candidate because simplicity and abelian addition can persist. However its conclusion preserves **solvability**, not metabelianity or abelian Sylows. In prime-top towers from the familiar A-group seeds, a nonzero p-element in the derived p-part of the seed lies in ker χ. Put it at one base coordinate; a p-element lifting the top C_p moves its support. They do not commute, so the new p-Sylow is nonabelian. This is an inference from the displayed kernel–wreath law, not a theorem quoted from that paper. No other checked specialization supplied a prior full answer.
- Verbeken, [2608.01884, current v2 of 8 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01884): full PDF retrieved; Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.1 inspected. The invariant-homomorphism/radical-algebra/regular-affine mechanism is relevant prior methodology and should not be claimed as a new general idea. Its examples have a proper three-dimensional ideal and a nonabelian p-group as multiplicative group, so they fail both simplicity and the abelian-Sylow restriction.
- Damele, [2607.17125, current v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17125): full PDF retrieved; introduction and Theorems A–C inspected. It concerns cyclic Sylows and splitting/obstruction results, not the noncyclic abelian Sylows here.
- Ercan–Güloğlu, [2609.06994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06994): full PDF retrieved; main results, residual/supplement statements and examples of orders 18 and 36 inspected. It does not provide the target simple counterexample.
