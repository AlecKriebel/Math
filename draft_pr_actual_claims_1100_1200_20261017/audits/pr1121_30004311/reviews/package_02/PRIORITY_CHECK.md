## Independently checked audit bridges

The following checks reconstruct the supplied bridge arguments; they are deductions in this audit, **not claims that the old papers published these eliminations**.

1. **Finite metabelian A-group action lemma.** If G=N semidirect S with N abelian, take a p-element a in S'. Write its action 1+D on N. Both a and [a,N] lie in G', so abelianness of G' gives D^2=0. On N_p the p-subgroup N_p semidirect <a> is abelian, giving D=0. On N_q, q different from p, the identity (1+D)^(p^e)=1 gives p^e D=0, and p^e is invertible on N_q. Since S' is finite abelian and generated by primary elements, alpha(S')=1. This uses the split group law actually present in the candidate constructions; it is not a classification of all metabelian A-groups.

2. **The generally nontrivial second factor in [2401.12904v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12904v2), Theorem 4.5.** I read the A1 setup, quotient action/cocycle statement and relevant proof. Here A1=A semidirect <t>, with t-id onto A, and the final multiplicative group is N semidirect A1 for trivial N. Hence A is contained in G'. Under both target group hypotheses the preceding p-element argument kills its action on N. In A1, lambda_(a,id) is itself the identity. The general asymmetric lambda formula then puts every (0,(a,id)) in the final socle. A is nonzero and this socle is proper, excluding simplicity. The argument does not silently declare A1 trivial. The later finite family has a nontrivial same-prime action, excluding abelian Sylows. Its polynomial ring is used as a trivial brace in that step.

3. **Iterating [1705.08493v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08493v1), Theorem 3.3.** For a simple two-trivial seed S=U asymmetric-product V, radical zero and the ideal generated by action differences give S'=U. For B=N asymmetric-product S with trivial N and target group properties, alpha(U)=1 by item 1. The new cocycle is forced biadditive by lambda composition, and equivariance forces its values into the fixed set of lambda_U, namely {0} x V. It follows directly in coordinates that B is the asymmetric product of trivial N direct-sum U by trivial V: the cocycle is the sum of the new V-valued form and old form. Thus the route stays in the excluded class. The group hypotheses descend to quotients, so iteration does not evade this check. Assuming a seed already outside that class would transfer the central problem to an unsupported input.

4. **Kernel–wreath construction [2609.20401v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20401v1).** I read the actual kernel formula in §3 and the simplicity-permanence statement in §4. With Omega=T, its group is ker(epsilon) in R^Omega semidirect T, epsilon((r_x),u)=(product chi(r_x))*u. For |T|>2 choose a p-element u in T and a lift r in a Sylow p-subgroup P of R. A kernel element with top u moves a two-coordinate-support kernel base element with entries a,a^-1, for a nonidentity a in P. A support not invariant under u exists, and both elements are in one p-group, hence provide noncommutativity in a p-subgroup. For T=C2 and |P|>2, a nontrivial kernel element of chi|P supported on one coordinate supplies the same obstruction. In the remaining T=C2, |P|=2 case, K=ker chi has odd order and K^2 is a normal Hall odd subgroup of the output group. In a finite classical left brace a normal multiplicative Hall subgroup equals the unique lambda-invariant additive Hall subgroup (its image in the coprime quotient is trivial), hence is an ideal. If K is nontrivial this contradicts simplicity. If K=1 the seed is the trivial brace of order two and the output of order four has a nonzero diagonal socle. This independently validates the arbitrary-seed elimination, not merely failure of the advertised solvable seeds.

## Matched-product formalism versus the full result

I read [1511.08477v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08477v3), Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2. Its lambda formula recovers the operative law from R and H=V x F2 using cross-actions sigma^e and (v,z) mapped to (M^eta v,z). The identities eta(sigma r)=eta(r) and Q(Mv)=Q(v), together with invariance of beta, verify the compatibility. Thus existence of the brace is an established general construction method and is correctly credited.

The direct simplicity proof is still needed. Theorem 2.6 of [1610.00477v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00477v1) assumes simple factor braces; R has the nonzero proper ideal F5*x4 and H has {0} x F2. Those hypotheses fail. The special quadratic factors have nonabelian multiplicative primary groups when their f action is nonidentity, while f=id has star images only in the scalar coordinate and zero primary triples. The old two-trivial reinterpretation in 1705.08493v1, Theorem 6.2, was also checked. A generic matched-product existence theorem is therefore not a previously published proof of all the required target conditions.
