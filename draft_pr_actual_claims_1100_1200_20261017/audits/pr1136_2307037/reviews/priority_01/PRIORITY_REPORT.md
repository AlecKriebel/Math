## Exact comparison target

For every finite sum g(z)=sum_j lambda_j/(z-z_j)^2 with positive real lambda_j summing to one and arbitrary complex, possibly repeated poles, one compact set S(g) has area exactly pi and, for every real R>1, the ordinary nonnegative area integral over D_R minus S(g) is at most 2 pi log R + pi(log 2+1/e). The logarithmic coefficient is optimal with a finite absolute additive constant. The candidate does not claim its additive constant is optimal.

An older result would count as full overlap if it explicitly establishes these quantifiers (with any absolute additive constant for the original problem), or gives a genuinely covering result from which they follow by a checkable direct specialization. A result only for poles in a fixed disk, a radius-dependent exceptional set, first-order Cauchy sums, a nonsharp logarithmic coefficient, or a constant depending on the configuration does not suffice. A construction of a quadrature set and an indicator estimate remain distinct inputs until their target-specific bridge has been supplied.

## Independently checked bridge and its attribution boundary

The candidate's bridge was checked analytically to decide what would have to be present in an older source. The following are deductions of this audit from the cited inputs, not statements attributed wholesale to any one source.

1. Old positive quadrature construction with masses pi lambda_j gives a bounded open Omega of area pi and null boundary. For z outside its closure, the harmonic test (w-z)^(-2) gives T1_Omega(z)=-g(z). This uses the quadrature identity on the whole open set, including when its complement has bounded components.
2. Merely enclosing Omega in D_L and applying the indicator estimate gives a bound depending on its enclosing radius. Since the poles are arbitrary, that does not establish a universal additive constant for every R>1.
3. For each R, split the already fixed Omega into E=Omega intersect D_(sqrt(2)R) and F=Omega minus that disk. If a=area(E), the near estimate is a log(2 pi R^2/a). The far kernel has exact disk integral pi log(rho^2/(rho^2-R^2)), at most pi log 2 when rho>=sqrt(2)R. Thus its contribution is at most (pi-a)log 2.
4. Their sum equals 2a log R + pi log 2 + a log(pi/a), at most 2 pi log R + pi(log 2+1/e). The same Omega was fixed before R. These localization and entropy steps close the arbitrary-location, universal-constant gap; they are not supplied by an appeal to the title of the indicator theorem.

Consequently the availability of ingredients by 2010 does not, by itself, date publication of this complete target-specific deduction to 2010. This audit found no source in its read scope that already supplies the whole bridge. It makes no claim that no such source exists.

A weak-type inequality alone also does not close the gap: integrating a distribution bound with constant K>1 generically retains a nonsharp leading factor, and threshold sets vary with the threshold. No illegitimate differentiation of first-order inequalities, reversal of the implication recorded in the problem collection, or unrestricted replacement of atomic measures by indicators was accepted as priority evidence.
