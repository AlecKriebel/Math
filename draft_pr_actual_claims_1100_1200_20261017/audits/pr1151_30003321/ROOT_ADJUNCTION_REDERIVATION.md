# Root direct proof of the additive cut-adjunction principle

Written at 2026-10-18T00:40:24.952191+00:00, before reading the fourth dependency reviewer's proof. Root has received its mechanism summary (initial sums and cut adjunction), so this is independently checked deduction from that summary, not blind discovery. It avoids reliance on the unavailable2001 predecessor proof. Only the defining surreal addition cut and sign-prefix model are imported here.

## Prefix covering for any defining cut

Suppose x={L|R} is the simplest surreal between sets L<R. Every proper sign prefix v of x is a prefix of or equal to some member of L union R. Indeed, if v<x and every l in L were strictly below v, then L<v<R, contradicting that x is simplest, since v is simpler. Thus some l satisfies v<=l<x. Because v is a prefix of x, every surreal in the interval [v,x) has v as a prefix (including equality); a first difference before the end of v would put it on the same strict side of both v and x, so cannot lie in that interval. Thus v is a prefix of or equal to l. If v>x the argument is symmetric using a member r of R with x<r<=v. This is valid for ordinal-length sequences; no numerical density or set enumeration is involved.

## Sums of initial subsets are initial

Let A,B be set-sized initial subsets of No and C={a+b:a in A,b in B}. For each pair(a,b), induct on the natural sum of the ordinal sign lengths of a and b. Each defining addition option aL+b, a+bL, aR+b or a+bR has a strictly smaller induction measure, and it lies in C because A,B contain all prefixes. By induction every prefix of each option lies in C. The defining addition formula makes a+b the simplest cut between these options. The prefix-covering fact therefore puts every proper prefix of a+b in C. Hence C is initial. The zero-length base has no options and no proper prefixes. Natural sum is used solely as a well-founded ordinal measure strictly increasing in each operand, not as an arithmetic operation on surreal exponents.

For a set group all classes in this proof can be sets: A,B,C and the relevant option sets are sets. No class-length recursion is needed. Arbitrary unions of initial subsets are initial immediately from the definition.

## The exact group extension

Let M be a set-sized initial ordered additive subgroup of No. Suppose b is the simplest surreal realizing a partition (L_M,R_M) of M and b is not in M. Applying prefix covering to this partition gives every proper prefix of b in M: the relevant option belongs to M, and M is initial. Thus M union{b} is initial. Negation flips signs and preserves prefixes, and M=-M, so C=M union{b,-b} is initial as well. Its n-fold sum C+...+C is initial by the preceding result. Since0 belongs to C, these sums increase with n. Their union is precisely the group generated by M and b: a finite sum equals m+kb, and every m+kb is such a sum. The union is therefore initial. If b is already in M, no extension is needed.

## From small options to a whole partition

Suppose b={L0|R0} with L0,R0 subsets of M and b not in M. Set L_M={m in M:m<b} and R_M={m in M:b<m}. They partition M, with L0 subset L_M and R0 subset R_M. The element b realizes the whole partition. Any realization of the whole partition realizes the smaller cut, so cannot be simpler than b; indeed the simplest b of the smaller interval is a prefix of every member of that interval. Consequently b is the simplest element realizing the partition. The group extension above applies.

This proves exactly the additive Z-module adjunction needed in the repaired characterization. It does not assert the more general K-module lemma or a novel theorem. The separate monomial and terminal-limit-generator construction must still supply its legal small cut options and actual existing elements; this lemma alone does not replace those checks.
