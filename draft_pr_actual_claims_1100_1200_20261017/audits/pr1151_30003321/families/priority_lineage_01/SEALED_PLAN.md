## Original target reconstructed from primary sources

Oberwolfach Report 60/2016, printed pp.3354-3356, Kaplan (joint work Ehrlich), Question 1 on p.3356, asks: “Is every discrete initial subgroup of No isomorphic to an initial subgroup of Oz?” The author-hosted sequel by Ehrlich and Kaplan asks exactly this as Question 9.1 on p.15. Its p.2 fixes NBG with global choice and explains that class includes set and proper class. Initial means downward closed under the simplicity/predecessor relation of No. Isomorphism is as ordered additive groups; the map need not preserve simplicity or multiplication. The target image must itself be initial and be contained in Oz, and the isomorphism must be onto that image. Discrete means nontrivial group with least positive member (sequel p.10); zero can be handled separately without changing the substantive target.

The adjoining established result is about *subdomains*, not all additive subgroups: discrete initial subdomains are already contained in Oz. The sequel example D + Z/omega is discrete initial but not contained in Oz; its displayed isomorphism onto omega D + Z is one example, not a general theorem. The generic group characterization (sequel Theorem 5.1) requires an initial exponent class, initial coefficient groups, truncation closure/cross-section and a dyadic condition when a right predecessor occurs; this is useful machinery, not by itself a recorded full solution. Question 8.1 (and report Question 2) concerns set-only countermodels for densely ordered theories and is a different problem.
