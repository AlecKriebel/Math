# The imported characterization: literal proof cautions and a class-safe repair

This note distinguishes two issues in the pinned author's proof of Theorem 5.1 from the truth of its stated characterization. Neither issue is a counterexample to the theorem. Both can be repaired using the same primary paper's normal-form facts, Lemma 3.1, and initial-module extension Lemma 4.1. The repair below proves the full converse needed by the candidate, first for a set exponent class and then for arbitrary classes. It introduces no theorem equivalent to the target question.

## A. Precise counterexample to the displayed exhaustion

In the pinned text, Theorem 5.1's proof, PDF pp.8–9, enumerates Gamma=(a_i)_{i<beta} by nondecreasing birthdays, puts A_i={x in A: supp(x) subset {a_j:j<=i}}, and displays A=union_{i<beta} A_i.

Let q_n be the finite sign sequence + followed by n minuses; q_n=2^{-n}. Put

    Gamma={0} union {q_n:n<omega},
    R_0=Z,  R_{q_n}=D,

and let A be all reverse-well-ordered series over Gamma with the indicated coordinate coefficient groups. This is a set group, is truncation closed and cross sectional, and satisfies the characterization's hypotheses. Gamma is initial; all its required right-predecessor coefficient groups are D. Its birthday-respecting enumeration is 0,q_0,q_1,... and has type beta=omega.

The series b=sum_{n<omega} omega^{q_n} is an actual element of A. Its support is strictly decreasing but is not contained in any finite initial segment of the exponent enumeration. Thus b is in no A_i for i<omega. The displayed exhaustion is literally false for this legal set example. Finite-support groups have the displayed exhaustion; arbitrary supported groups need a final stage handling series cofinal in the enumeration.

## B. Precise counterexample to automatic membership of the dyadic cut options

Define q_n=+ -^n as above and p_n=+ -^n +. Then (p_n) is strictly decreasing: at the first position after + -^n, p_n has plus and p_{n+1} has minus. The p_n are pairwise prefix-incomparable. Let

    Gamma={0} union {q_n:n<omega} union {p_n:n<omega},
    R_0=Z,  R_{q_n}=D,  R_{p_n}=Z,

and again take A to be the full set-supported series group with these coordinate groups. All prefixes of the leaves p_n are 0 and some q_j. A leaf is never a proper prefix of any other exponent in Gamma. Hence only the q_j can be nonzero right simplicity predecessors, and their coefficient groups are D. All hypotheses of Theorem 5.1 are met as algebraic and tree conditions, independently of whether its conclusion is invoked.

The series b=sum_{n<omega} omega^{p_n} belongs to A and has infinite limit support and leading coefficient 1. Its first exponent p_0 is ++, namely 2. The source's displayed limit-series cut with mu=0 and n=1 includes

    (1-1/2) omega^{p_0} = (1/2) omega^2.

This number is not in A because R_{p_0}=Z. Therefore the assertion that all such dyadic cut options automatically belong to the previously generated subgroup of A is not valid from the stated hypotheses. Such cut options need not be simplicity predecessors, so their absence is not a counterexample to initiality.

## C. Exact cut-extension principle used in the repair

The primary source's Lemma 4.1 says that if M is an initial submodule of an s-hierarchical ordered module M', and b is the simplest element of M' realizing a partition cut of M, then the submodule generated by M and b remains initial. We apply it only with M'=No as an ordered Z-module and with set-sized M. Section 4 explicitly supplies No's s-hierarchical module structure over an initial subdomain; Z is such a subdomain.

Here is the check that converts a small normal-form cut into the required partition. Suppose b is not in M and b={L_0|R_0} in No, with L_0 and R_0 subsets of M. Define

    L_M={u in M:u<b},  R_M={u in M:b<u}.

Then (L_M,R_M) partitions M, since b is not in M. It contains the small options on the correct sides. Every surreal realizing this whole partition also lies strictly between L_0 and R_0. Since b is the simplest surreal in the latter interval and itself realizes the partition, it remains the simplest surreal realizing the partition. Thus the hypothesis b={L_M|R_M} of Lemma 4.1 is satisfied. If b already lies in M, skip the extension.

At a limit stage, the union of the preceding increasing initial subgroups is itself an initial subgroup: finitely many elements have a common earlier stage, and each prefix belongs to the stage containing its extension. All recursions below have set-length and states contained in the fixed set A.

## D. Set theorem: first build the finite-support subgroup

Assume Gamma is a nonempty initial set and A is a truncation-closed cross-sectional subgroup of its Hahn group, satisfying the coefficient and right-predecessor conditions. The Hahn group over a set exponent domain is a set, so A is a set. Every coordinate appearing in a member of A lies in its one-term coefficient group, by subtracting adjacent truncations.

Well order Gamma by nondecreasing sign length, using choice within set-sized levels. Process the exponents in this order. The first exponent is 0. At exponent y, all proper simplicity predecessors of y have already been processed.

Start by adjoining the monomial omega^y. Its usual defining cut has options 0 and integer multiples of omega^{yL} on the left and dyadic multiples 2^{-n}omega^{yR} on the right. Here yL and yR range over the left and right simplicity predecessors of y. The left options belong to earlier coefficient blocks by cross sectionality and integer closure; the right options do so by the assumed D subset R_{yR}. Thus these small cut options are in the current initial subgroup. The new monomial cannot belong to the subgroup of finite sums of monomials at earlier exponents, by uniqueness of normal form. Section C and Lemma 4.1 make its adjunction initial. All integral multiples at y are then present.

Process the remaining r omega^y, r in R_y, in order of the real coefficient's sign length, with choice within each level, skipping elements already present. The group R_y is initial in R, so every proper real simplicity predecessor rL,rR is also in R_y and was processed earlier. The source's Lemma 3.1(ii) gives the required small cut using these coefficient predecessors when r is nondyadic, or when r is a nonintegral dyadic and y has no left simplicity predecessor. Lemma 3.1(iii) gives it in the other nonintegral dyadic case, adding or subtracting integer multiples of omega^{yL}. These latter terms lie in earlier exponent blocks. Thus every small cut option is in the current initial subgroup, and Section C again applies. Integers were already present. The union of these coefficient steps is initial.

Proceed through all exponents. Every finite sum uses finitely many exponent and coefficient stages, so the final union is exactly

    F = {x in A: supp(x) is finite}.

It is initial. This phase does not assert that F=A; hence it does not lose cofinal infinite supports at a terminal limit.

## E. Set theorem: terminal stage for all limit-support generators

Let Z consist of the elements z in A whose normal-form support length lambda is an infinite limit ordinal and whose leading coefficient is 1. This is a set. Well order Z by nondecreasing lambda, using choice within each length block. All length blocks form a set because Z is a set.

The group generated by F and Z is A. To verify this directly, an arbitrary support ordinal nu can be written nu=lambda+k with k finite and lambda=0 or an infinite limit ordinal. The final k terms are in F, and the length-lambda prefix is in A by truncation closure. If lambda is nonzero and its leading coefficient is r, subtract the one-term element (r-1)omega^{y_0} to make that prefix a member of Z. Both r and 1 are in R_{y_0}, so this subtraction stays in A. This also proves the generator claim without relying on its abbreviated published proof.

When processing b in Z of support length lambda, every proper truncation b_{<mu}, mu<lambda, belongs to the subgroup M generated earlier. Indeed, if mu is an infinite limit, that prefix has leading coefficient 1 and shorter support, so is an earlier member of Z. Otherwise split mu into a limit prefix and a finite tail as above; all tail monomials lie in F. The empty prefix is 0.

For each mu<lambda use *unit* perturbations

    L_mu = b_{<mu} + (r_mu-1)omega^{y_mu},
    R_mu = b_{<mu} + (r_mu+1)omega^{y_mu}.

They belong to M: the prefix is present, and r_mu and 1 belong to the coefficient group R_{y_mu}, so the perturbed monomial is in F. The tail after index mu is infinitesimal relative to omega^{y_mu}; therefore L_mu<b<R_mu.

To verify that these options define b, compare with the correct normal-form cut in primary Proposition 3.2(iii), which uses dyadic perturbations at every index. Given any index mu<lambda and any positive dyadic epsilon, choose nu with mu<nu<lambda; this is possible because lambda is limit. Both L_nu and R_nu agree with b at every exponent through y_mu. Therefore, by the leading-coefficient comparison,

    b_{<mu}+(r_mu-epsilon)omega^{y_mu}
       < L_nu < b < R_nu
       < b_{<mu}+(r_mu+epsilon)omega^{y_mu}.

Conversely, for a given unit option at mu, a dyadic option at the same index with epsilon<1 is closer to b on its side. Thus the unit left family and the dyadic left family are mutually cofinal, and the unit and dyadic right families are mutually coinitial. The two cuts define the same simplest surreal, b. No dyadic perturbation is asserted to belong to A.

All unit options lie in M. If b is not in M, Section C supplies the exact partition of M and Lemma 4.1 applies; otherwise skip b. At the final union, the result is an initial group containing F and Z, contained in A, and generating all of A. Thus A is initial.

This is a terminal stage after all monomials, so it includes every existing infinite series whose support was cofinal in any earlier exponent enumeration. It does not insert any missing series or pass to a completion.

## F. Proper-class theorem by actual set restrictions

Let Gamma or A be a proper class satisfying the characterization conditions. For any x in A, let S=supp(x), which is a set. Form

    I = union_{y in S} ( {y} union Prefix(y) ).

For each set sign sequence y, Prefix(y) is a set, and class replacement and set union make I a set. Because Gamma is initial and S subset Gamma, I subset Gamma. It is an initial exponent set containing S.

Let A_I consist of the *existing* members of A supported in I. It is a set: it is a subclass of the set-sized Hahn group over I. It is an additive subgroup because sums have support contained in the union of the two input supports, and negation does not change support. It is truncation closed. It is cross sectional because each omega^y with y in I was already in A. For y in I its one-term coefficient group is exactly the original R_y: restricting support to I neither deletes nor adds a one-term series at y. Hence all those groups remain initial, and every right-predecessor pair x,y in I inherits D subset R_y from Gamma.

Sections D and E apply to this set group A_I. It is initial and contains x. Every simplicity predecessor of x therefore lies in A_I and consequently in A. As x was arbitrary, A is initial. The empty support case x=0 has no predecessors and requires no nonempty exponent set.

This proof uses ordinary set recursions and elementary class comprehension/replacement. It does not assume that an initial subtree generated by a set support is a subgroup; only its being an initial *exponent set* is needed. It never assumes that A equals the full Hahn group over that set.

## Assessment

The displayed exhaustion and the automatic dyadic-option membership need correction if the primary proof is read literally. The characterization theorem itself is supported by the complete repair above, using strictly earlier primary normal-form and initial-module results. The candidate's use of that characterization therefore leaves no unsupported theorem or class-size gap in this audit. These dependency repairs do not modify the candidate's exponent surgery or bottom normalization and are not a weakening of the target.
