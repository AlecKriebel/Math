## Accepted full negative answer and published preprint

The complexity-comparison question of Gishboliner and Shapira (OWR19/2019, p1139, Problem 5; later Problem 5.6) has a negative answer. The single fixed hereditary, extendable property consisting of K4-free graphs that become 3-colorable after deletion of at most one vertex admits a polynomial classical canonical tester on every nonempty finite input size. Every adaptive two-sided vertex-distribution-free tester has superpolynomial worst-case total sample-plus-adjacency cost, even with the vertex set, its size, the full positive rational distribution and the heavy vertex supplied. Distances permit arbitrary edge additions and deletions. This concerns total oracle cost, rather than sample cost with unlimited free queries to arbitrary named vertices; it makes no polynomial running-time claim.

Published preprint: **A polynomial versus superpolynomial gap in vertex-distribution-free graph testing**, Alec Kriebel, v1.0.0 (2026-10-17), [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23441523](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23441523). The record contains the five-page note and source/verification archive. The short proof gives an exact heavy-apex distance identity and an elementary factor-36 tripartite-cover reduction from the credited Alon–Shapira (2004) two-sided triangle-testing lower bound.
