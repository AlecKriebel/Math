## 1. Exact claim and success criterion

The candidate fixes `P = K4-free ∩ {delete at most one vertex → 3-colorable}`. It proves heredity, extendability, a polynomial classical canonical tester for every finite size, and an `exp(Ω(log²(1/ε)))` worst-case VDF **total sample-plus-adjacency** lower bound along a rational sequence, against adaptive two-sided testers. Its separate sample lower bound permits free induced adjacency only on sampled names. Neither a hard property outside heredity/extendability nor a sample-only bound against a weaker oracle answers this exact target.

A priority defeat would require an earlier complete answer to the comparison question, perhaps for a different fixed hereditary extendable property; or a checkable bridge from earlier results preserving the hypotheses, proximity scale, and oracle strengths. An earlier ingredient, a finite blowup obstruction, or an unsupported reduction does not meet that criterion.

The original question is Gishboliner's joint contribution with Asaf Shapira to Oberwolfach Report 19/2019: Problem 5, physical PDF page 27 / printed 1139. The model on physical page 26 / printed 1138 counts both kinds of calls. The later manuscript retains the question as Problem 5.6, §5.3, physical page 22. The attribution is Gishboliner–Shapira; Goldreich introduced the model and related preceding questions. [Original publisher report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46798), [latest arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09903v5).

## 3. Strongest possible prior implications

### Gishboliner–Shapira classification and blowup transfer

The classification proves testability, not a polynomial quantitative comparison. Proposition 5.7 gives the classical canonical sample bound for blowup-avoidable properties, with proximity relaxation for arbitrary irrational weights. Its proof and hypotheses were inspected. The heavy-apex construction is outside that sufficient condition, as its own doubled-wheel proof establishes. This removes the positive theorem's applicability; it does not turn the proposition into a lower bound. [§5.3 and proof](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.09903v5#S5.SS3).

There is an explicit check on the invalid converse: ordinary `P3`-freeness (three vertices, two edges) is maximum degree at most one. It is hereditary and extendable. Any two-blowup of the member `K2` contains `K2,2`, regardless of internal edges, so it is not blowup-avoidable. Yet Goldreich's Theorem 2.12 gives a polynomial VDF tester for this bounded-degree property. Thus the pre-existing non-blowup-avoidable examples, or the candidate's finite wheel witness alone, cannot answer the complexity question.

### Goldreich's separations and conversions

Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 transfer two-sided VDF testing to one-sided testing and yield lower bounds using difficult **classical one-sided** properties. The explicit hard examples include connectivity, relative-size clique/cut properties, and the two-star property. They do not give a hereditary extendable property with an easy classical canonical tester and a hard VDF tester. The two-star example is not hereditary. Its heavy-center construction is a clear antecedent for the importance of high-mass vertices, not the requested full theorem. Theorem 2.12/2.13 instead prove positive polynomial results for bounded-degree/minor-free properties. [Inspected current body, §§2.1–2.4](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/R3/vdf.pdf).

Applying Corollary 2.4 to the candidate itself cannot supply its hard side: its classical one-sided complexity is already polynomial. A source upper bound for 3-colorability likewise cannot be combined with K4-freeness by assuming arbitrary intersections preserve the easier complexity. Such an argument would require the very repair/sampling mechanism being proved.

Shapira's ICM 2022 publisher chapter, §4.4, independently retains the qualitative weighted-removal result as Theorem 4.10. Its universal quantifier over distributions and its existence/extendability equivalence give no quantitative comparison with the classical removal function. The prescribed-growth theorem in §4.3 concerns the classical function alone; no relation of the two functions follows from assigning a growth rate to one of them. [Inspected publisher VoR](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/33311?nt=1).

### Fei: explicit oracle and distance obstruction to a direct implication

Fei's Definitions 1.2–1.3 concern arbitrary distributions on unordered vertex pairs; Remark 1 makes the default oracle sample-only. Theorem 1.5 and §7.1 give the triangle sample lower bound using Ruzsa–Szemerédi hosts and a two-fold parity lift. Section 8 identifies the power of extra queries as unresolved. These theorem/proof bodies, not merely the abstract, were inspected. [Current body](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22702v2).

The following is this reviewer's direct analysis of those definitions and constructions:

1. In Fei's Lemma 7.4, the host edge set `E` is fixed. If arbitrary named adjacency queries are granted on that very hard distribution, choose any known host triangle `abc`. Query `(a,0)(b,0)`, `(b,0)(c,0)`, `(c,0)(a,0)`. Each answer reveals whether the associated bit `y` is zero; the xor of the three bits deterministically distinguishes the two parity classes. Thus these *specific* sample-hard worlds are distinguishable in three adjacency queries. The lemma cannot directly certify the candidate's unrestricted total-oracle lower bound. Hiding host identities and proving a new exposure coupling is substantive extra work; random relabeling itself is an old technique.
2. For VDF weights, tail pair costs have the form `u_ij=p_i p_j` (up to a common normalization). On four distinct vertices they obey `u_12 u_34 = u_13 u_24 = u_14 u_23`. Arbitrary pair distributions need not obey this: positive mass on two disjoint pairs and zero mass on the cross pairs is already a counterexample. Adding one heavy apex leaves this factorization on tail pairs intact. A direct weight identification therefore does not turn Fei's arbitrary edge distribution into the required vertex-product distance.
3. In the RS edge-support example, deleting one edge per triangle has constant mass under the uniform distribution on existing edges, but cost proportional to `T/n²` under uniform tail-product weights. That quantity tends to zero. Proximity parameters cannot be identified without a scale calculation. Moreover, simulating all adjacency queries from an edge sampler would require additional work.

These observations rule out the straightforward claimed implications, not every conceivable reduction between models. A new incidence encoding or oracle simulation with proved farness and complexity preservation could change the audit. No such earlier complete reduction was found.

### Earlier triangle lower bounds and local masking

Alon (2002) establishes the polynomial/nonpolynomial distinction for ordinary subgraph-freeness. Alon–Shapira's directed-subgraph paper, §7, extends the superpolynomial lower bound to two-sided testers; physical author-PDF pages 26–27 use randomized local deletions, random permutations, and the probability of exposing three vertices of a designated cycle. Alon–Kaufman–Krivelevich–Ron, §6, uses the explicit two-fold parallel/cross parity worlds and an adaptive query simulation. Fei credits this older constructional lineage. [Alon primary paper copy](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr04/cos598B/bib/AlonTestGraphs.pdf), [Alon–Shapira author body](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~asafico/subdir.pdf), [AKKR full author body](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~krivelev/testtria.pdf), [AKKR journal record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/07067917X).

Those results supply much of the hard-triangle technology. They do not by themselves provide an *easy different classical property* whose weighted testing preserves that hardness. Taking triangle-freeness as the property retains a hard classical side, so it does not yield the target separation. The candidate's local even/odd masking must not be advertised as a new parity principle.

### Colorability, partition closure, graphons and other near hits

Polynomial colorability sampling predates the target: GGR and Alon–Krivelevich supply the original mechanisms. GGR §10.1.3 already discusses product vertex distributions; §10.1.4 gives an arbitrary-edge-distribution separation. Its hard pair distribution has correlated supports, so the product-weight obstruction above applies. [Original JACM copy, printed 737–738](https://www.diochnos.com/teaching/CS5970/2020F/project-papers/PropertyTesting.pdf), [Alon–Krivelevich](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr04/cos598B/bib/AlonKrivelevich.pdf).

CFP's general birthday lemma is a prior quantitative sampling tool and its decision-list/threshold-graph connections concern arbitrary pair distributions, not a proof of the present product-weight theorem. CSY's Theorem 1.4 characterizes standard uniform one-sided complexity; its partition and large-substructure applications also use uniform farness/sampling. Their actual mechanisms do not prove a VDF comparison or a counterexample to one. The 2025 survey discusses improved classical colorability and triangle bounds, with no occurrences of “distribution” in the retrieved full text. Absence from a survey is not evidence of novelty.

Lovász–Szegedy's graphon sampling uses independently sampled continuum points, which remain distinct sampling positions even when they lie in the same step-function class. It therefore allows multiple representatives of a heavy atom after converting an atomic graph to a step function. The candidate's oracle observes one vertex label with repetitions identified. Applying that older graphon theorem requires a completeness argument under blowup/repeated-atom effects; the candidate's doubled wheel is precisely an obstruction to assuming it. No quantitative separation is supplied by those graphon statements. [Primary definitions, §§2.4, 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.1248v2).

Search hits for almost 3-colorability included approximation/NP-hardness work where an arbitrary small *fraction* of vertices can be removed. Such runtime statements do not establish information-theoretic sample/query lower bounds, do not impose the candidate's fixed one-vertex exception, and do not give the comparison theorem. No primary theorem encountered supplied the missing bridge.
