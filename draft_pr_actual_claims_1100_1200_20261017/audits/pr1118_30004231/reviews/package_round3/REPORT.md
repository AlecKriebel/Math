## Independent mathematical reconstruction

Let the simplex have five affinely independent vertices. Any nonzero functional determines disjoint, nonempty index sets (A,B) at its largest and smallest vertex heights. Conversely, arbitrary disjoint (A,B) are realized by assigning three levels to the vertices and subtracting the level of the vertex at the origin. This proves exhaustiveness, not merely that the proposed faces exist.

The corresponding face of the simplex difference is the sum of the (A)-simplex and the negative (B)-simplex. If their direction spaces intersected nontrivially, writing an intersection vector in both spaces would give an affine dependence with coefficient sum zero separately on the two disjoint index sets. Affine independence forces all coefficients to vanish. The sum-face dimension is therefore (|A|+|B|-2), and the summand dimensions are (|A|-1,|B|-1). An exposing functional for a containing face proves that containment is precisely separate inclusion of the two index sets. Equality of faces consequently cannot identify distinct pairs.

The positive-dimension filter leaves cardinality types (2,2), (2,3), (3,2). The first type supplies 30 minimal elements, while the latter two types supply 20 maximal elements. Each maximum covers three minima, each minimum lies below two maxima, and no longer chain exists. Thus the order complex has 50 vertices, 60 edges and no isolated vertices.

The connectivity argument in the manuscript is valid. After suppressing the degree-two minima, the graph has signed two-subsets of a five-element set; opposite signs are adjacent on disjoint underlying subsets. Two distinct intersecting same-sign subsets have a common opposite-sign neighbor, obtained as the complement of their union. For disjoint same-sign subsets, an intermediate two-subset meeting both reduces to two such paths. Every vertex has an opposite-sign neighbor. This connects the whole graph. A spanning tree leaves eleven edges, giving eleven circle generators.

For the product summands, a nonzero interval coefficient exposes an endpoint, preserving the base dimensions. Zero interval coefficient exposes the entire shared interval in **each** summand. If the base dimensions are (a,b,c), the two summand dimensions add to (a+b+2), while the sum-face dimension is (c+1\le a+b+1). Every vertical face fails equality, including faces whose base includes vertices. The horizontal entire-base faces have dimensions (sum, first, second) = (4,4,4) and fail; the entire sum has (5,5,5) and fails. Empty faces fail positivity. These cases exhaust all supporting functionals.

Exactly two endpoint copies of the base graph survive, with no cross-comparisons. Consequently the ordinary Betti numbers are (2,22), with no homology in degree at least two. Every facet is an edge. In any ordering the first edge from the second component meets all preceding facets in the empty complex, whose dimension is -1 instead of the required zero. This obstructs both pure and standard nonpure shellability. A wedge of positive-dimensional spheres is connected, so the printed three-sphere homology assertion fails as well; replacing that dimension by two does not help.

Canonical decomposition is not a hidden assumption. Common exposure gives containment of any putative smaller summand faces in the canonical summands; their support-function deficits are nonnegative and sum to zero in every direction, so each vanishes. This agrees with [Fukuda–Weibel, Lemma 4 of v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0510470v4). The theorem is a complete exact-target counterexample without a computational hypothesis.
