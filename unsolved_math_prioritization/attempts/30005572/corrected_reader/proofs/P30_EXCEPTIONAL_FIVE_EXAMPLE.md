# A concrete occurrence of the surviving five-line

Final shared PASS5/5, row1777 /30005572. This is an exact example of the ambient smoothing quotient and common kernel, not a cocycle counterexample.

Take F=Q(sqrt(−5)), O=Z[sqrt(−5)], and q=(11). The reduced forms of discriminant −20 are (1,0,5) and (2,2,3), so the class group is C2. Equivalently the familiar nonprincipal prime over 2 generates it. The Hilbert quadratic extension is F(sqrt(5)).

The polynomial X²+5 has no root modulo 11: −5=6 is not among 0,1,3,4,5,9, the quadratic residues modulo 11. Thus (11) is a prime of norm Q=121. It is principal, so its class has order f=1 and it splits completely in H/F. For every n>=1 the accepted prime-power residue theorem gives

  T_q(F(q^n)) = (F_121^×)^2,
  M=T_q(F(q^n)) tensor Z[1/6] = F_5².

The nontrivial class g interchanges the two summands. This is exactly the exceptional situation 5 ramified and Q=1 modulo 5.

For a concrete auxiliary, c=(3,1+sqrt(−5)) has norm 3 and is nonprincipal, since a²+5b² cannot equal 3. It is prime to 5q. Its smoothing operator is

  Norm(c)²−g = 9−g = −(1+g) on M.

Hence its image is the diagonal line and its kernel is the anti-diagonal line. The general theorem proves that every other auxiliary smoothing operator has image contained in this diagonal line and kills the anti-diagonal line. Therefore

  M[J]={(x,−x):x in F_5},
  M/JM=F_5²/{(x,x):x in F_5}.

Both are the nontrivial character of C2. The example verifies that the one-dimensional smoothing quotient and common kernel in the general theorem do occur in the actual arithmetic residue module. It does not show that an actual normalized arithmetic cocycle residue hits either line, that an unaugmented arithmetic class fails to exist, or that an integral absolute map is impossible.
