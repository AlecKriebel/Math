# Actual cusp-loop vanishing by global reflections

14 October 2026. Final shared PASS 5/5, row 1777 / 30005572. New authored partial result; no novelty claim. This does not solve the frozen construction contract.

## 1. Results first

Write R=Z[1/6], let F be imaginary quadratic with maximal order O, and let N be a nonzero proper ideal. Put n_q=v_q(N) and e_q=v_q((2)). All ideal-class components are included.

1. There is an exact integral criterion for a specified cusp to admit a global reflection in its strict level stabilizer. When O has only the units +1 and -1, it is also an exact criterion for the cusp's R-valued first boundary homology to vanish. It gives an actual zero value of every existing K2-valued cocycle on that cusp's unipotent loops, not just a spectral-support assertion.
2. Every boundary component has zero first homology if

   at most one q has n_q>e_q, and at that q n_q<=2e_q+1.       (V)

   Consequently H1(Y1(N),R)->H1(X1(N),R) is an isomorphism. This proves unconditional compactification, with unchanged target and all existing Hecke relations, for every already constructed open map at these levels. For O^times={+1,-1}, (V) is necessary as well as sufficient for the *ambient boundary H1* to vanish. A failure of (V) does not prove a nonzero value of the actual K2 map.
3. At the more general levels

   n_q<=2e_q+1 for every q,                                (L)

   every cusp admits a reflection in Gamma0(N), possibly with a nontrivial diamond. Let W_N be the subgroup of the ray group generated by all local sign classes (Definition 6.1). It is an elementary abelian 2-group. Every existing Gamma0 cocycle's W_N-trivial target summand has zero value on every cusp loop. Thus it factors through the compactification. This includes arbitrary squarefree odd support, the wild p-primary part of the ray group, and p=5; the assertion still requires an already constructed cocycle or cohomology class.

These results do not construct missing exceptional smoothing blocks or small-level arithmetic maps, remove prime-power arithmetic residues, prove unrestricted bad-U, or identify the full prescribed relative theta formula. Their zero-cusp statements are unaffected by the inverse-tame sign conversion, but the existing source calibration is retained rather than replaced.

## 2. Precise lattice and marking setup

For a standard Bianchi component let Lambda be its rank-two projective O-lattice, for example a_r^{-1} direct-sum O in the source's column-action matrix convention. Its strict level structure is the surjective O-linear marking

   lambda: Lambda -> O/N,

which defines the strict Gamma1 level structure. In the standard row convention lambda is the row (0,1) modulo N. Thus

   Gamma1 = {g in Aut_O(Lambda): lambda g=lambda},
   Gamma0 = {g in Aut_O(Lambda): lambda g=a lambda
                                      for some a in (O/N)^times}.

The source may enlarge the strict Gamma1 group by scalar global units, equivalently allowing the lower-right residue to be a global unit. Multiplying by that scalar unit inverse returns an element of the strict group. Since scalar units act trivially on the symmetric space and by trivial conjugation, this enlargement gives the same coarse quotient and the same cusp-unipotent coinvariants. The finite scalar difference is harmless over R.

The coefficient action of Gamma0 in the accepted arithmetic construction is R_N(a). This is the direct Artin convention of PASS4, not an inverse convention.

Let ell in P^1(F) be a cusp line and I=ell intersect Lambda its saturated rank-one submodule. Saturation makes Lambda/I torsion-free. Over the Dedekind ring O, this quotient is a projective rank-one module J. The exact sequence splits, so choose

   Lambda=I direct-sum J.

This is a projective direct sum; neither I nor J is asserted principal. An automorphism preserving ell then has the unique upper-triangular form

   g = [[epsilon,b],[0,delta]],
   epsilon,delta in O^times, b in Hom_O(J,I).             (2.1)

The restrictions u=lambda|I and v=lambda|J define a primitive row modulo N. After trivializing I/NI and J/NJ, write this row as (u,v) in (O/N)^2. Such trivializations exist since a finite product of local Artinian rings has trivial Picard group. Primitivity means (u,v)=O/N.

The criterion derived below is independent of these trivializations and of the split complement. Define the ideal D with N subset D subset O by the image

   lambda(I)=D/N.

Then r_q=v_q(D) satisfies 0<=r_q<=n_q, and is exactly the truncated valuation of u in any local trivialization. Changing J leaves I and lambda(I) unchanged.

The reduction Hom_O(J,I)->Hom_{O/N}(J/NJ,I/NI) is surjective. Indeed Hom_O(J,I)=I tensor J^{-1} is projective rank one, and reduction is its ordinary quotient modulo N. Therefore every compatible finite system of residues prescribed for b lifts to an actual global b in (2.1). This is the global lifting step needed for the reflection; merely writing a local matrix would not suffice.

The strict cusp unipotent subgroup is

   U={n_z=[[1,z],[0,1]]: z in T},
   T={z in Hom_O(J,I): uz=0 modulo N}.                    (2.2)

T is an integral rank-two Z-lattice. The quotient of the cusp stabilizer by U embeds in O^times x O^times, so its order has only the prime factors 2 and 3. All its higher group homology vanishes over R.

## 3. Exact reflection criterion and actual evaluation

Consider first

   s_+=[[-1,0],[0,1]].

It lies in Gamma1 precisely when 2u=0 modulo N, equivalently

   r_q>=n_q-e_q for every q|N.                            (3.1)

Negative lower bounds are harmless. Next consider

   s_b=[[1,b],[0,-1]].

It lies in Gamma1 exactly when ub=2v modulo N. This equation is solvable precisely when

   r_q<=e_q for every q|N.                                (3.2)

To verify necessity and sufficiency locally, if r_q=0, u is a unit and b can be solved freely. If r_q>0, primitivity forces v to be a unit, and 2v is divisible by u modulo q^{n_q} exactly when r_q<=e_q. The global lift of b follows from Section 2. If e_q>=n_q, the equation imposes no restriction, as it should.

Both s_+ and s_b are genuine involutions of Lambda: the off-diagonal entry of s_b^2 is b-b=0. They conjugate every n_z to n_{-z}. No equality modulo N is being mistaken for equality in O; the matrices themselves square to the identity globally.

### Theorem 3.1 (actual zero cusp values)

Let A be any R-module and let f:Gamma1->A be a group homomorphism. If (3.1) or (3.2) holds, then

   f(n_z)=0 for every z in T.                              (3.3)

Indeed conjugation by the strict reflection preserves f, while it sends n_z to its inverse. Thus f(n_z)=-f(n_z), and 2 is invertible in R.

The entire cusp restriction is zero. The finite quotient of the cusp stabilizer contributes no positive-degree R-homology, and H1 of that stabilizer is a quotient of T tensor R. In particular this is an *actual evaluation* of every accepted normalized auxiliary homomorphism z_{c,N}, and of every existing unaugmented homomorphism, on the two cusp-lattice generators. It is not a conclusion inferred from possible support of boundary cohomology.

If O^times={+1,-1}, the criterion is exact. Every stabilizer element with unequal diagonal entries has one of the two forms above (with a possibly nonzero harmless b in the first case); its level equations imply (3.1) or (3.2). If neither holds, every stabilizer element has equal diagonal entries, so its conjugation action on T is trivial. Hochschild-Serre then gives

   H1(B_cusp,R)=T tensor R,                               (3.4)

which has R-rank two. If a reflection exists, its action is -1 and the same coinvariant description gives H1(B_cusp,R)=0.

For fields with more units, (3.1) or (3.2) is still sufficient. Section 8 gives an exact general-unit criterion; no converse using only plus/minus one is asserted there.

## 4. Exact all-cusp zero criterion for ordinary unit groups

Every possible valuation vector r_q in the box 0<=r_q<=n_q is covered by (3.1) or (3.2) if and only if (V) holds.

Proof. Call q active when n_q>e_q. At inactive primes both inequalities hold for every r_q. If no prime is active, every vector is covered. If exactly one prime is active, failure of both conditions requires

   e_q<r_q<n_q-e_q.

There is an integer in this interval if and only if n_q>=2e_q+2. Hence the required coverage is equivalent to n_q<=2e_q+1. If two distinct primes q and l are active, choose r_q=n_q and r_l=0 (other entries arbitrary). The first value violates (3.2), and the second violates (3.1). Thus coverage fails. This proves the combinatorial statement.

The converse is genuinely realized by a cusp, not only a formal divisor. In the principal component Lambda=O^2, choose c in O by the Chinese remainder theorem so that gcd((c),N)=D for the desired divisor D. The primitive vector (1,c)^t spans a saturated cusp line I, and its complement (0,1)^t gives the marking (u,v)=(c,1). Hence every valuation vector occurs in this one component. Therefore if O^times={+1,-1} and (V) fails, the actual boundary has a rank-two first-homology summand. We make no assertion that its image in H1(Y) is nonzero, or that the K2 homomorphism is nonzero on that image.

### Concrete sufficient families, for every imaginary quadratic F

- Every prime ideal level, recovering the source's prime-level observation.
- A prime q above 2 with N=q^n and 1<=n<=2e_q+1: through q^3 if 2 is split or inert, and through q^5 if 2 ramifies.
- Any level dividing (2).
- A level obtained from a divisor of (2) by adjoining one odd prime to exponent one.
- More generally exactly the ideals satisfying (V), including compatible factors at the other primes above two.

These are statements about topology at all levels, including levels where unit reduction is not injective. An arithmetic-map theorem still needs that map to have been constructed at the level under consideration.

## 5. Compactification of the actual accepted open maps

Let M=X1^BS(N), D=boundary M, Y=Y1(N), and X=X1(N). Collapsing each boundary component to its own cusp gives the exact sequence

   H1(D,R) -> H1(Y,R) -> H1(X,R) -> 0.                   (5.1)

This is the accepted PASS1 pushout criterion, valid without a field or character restriction. Geometric finite stabilizers have only 2,3-primary order, so the group/coarse comparison and finite-index Hecke correspondences are valid over R.

Under (V), H1(D,R)=0. Thus the middle arrow in (5.1) is an isomorphism, naturally compatible with every Hecke correspondence defined on these spaces. An existing map on H1(Y,R) consequently has a unique compactified map with exactly the same target. In particular, for every unit-injective proper N satisfying (V), the accepted PASS4 auxiliary maps give

   zbar_{c,N}:H1(X,R)->K2(O'(N)) tensor R,

for auxiliaries c prime to 5N. Their source-calibrated normalization is unchanged. Every existing unaugmented block or full unaugmented map compactifies in the same way. The good-prime Hecke relations, including primes above five, descend from the accepted open theorem. Any already-proved bad-U relation also descends, but no new unrestricted bad-U relation is inferred.

At prime-power levels the target remains K2(O_L[1/N]) tensor R. The topological argument cannot remove its vertical tame residues. At levels not supported at one prime the accepted target is already K2(O_L) tensor R.

The argument does not manufacture an unaugmented map on an unresolved squared-cyclotomic smoothing block. It applies to the auxiliary map there, and to an unaugmented map only if its existence is independently established.

## 6. A diamond reflection relation without a Hecke or norm hypothesis

### Definition 6.1

For every choice of signs at the prime-power factors of N, use CRT to obtain a in (O/N)^times with a congruent to +1 or -1 at each q^{n_q}. Let W_N be the image of all these sign choices under the principal ray map

   (O/N)^times -> Cl_N(F)=Gal(F(N)/F).

This is a subgroup of the principal ray kernel, with all global-unit identifications already quotiented out. The subgroup W_N is elementary abelian of exponent two. Its order is a power of two, so its averaging projector

   e_W=|W_N|^{-1} sum_{w in W_N} w

belongs to R[Cl_N(F)]. This is a projection in the target coefficient module; no additional source-character action is assumed. It retains the entire ray class group, including all class-group p-parts for p>=5. A local sign class can be trivial because of global units; no injectivity is assumed.

Suppose a cusp valuation vector satisfies, at every q separately,

   r_q<=e_q OR r_q>=n_q-e_q.                              (6.1)

Choose a=-1 at primes of the first type, and a=+1 at primes of the second type; at overlap either choice is allowed. Fix the *global* reflection form

   s=[[-1,b],[0,1]].                                      (6.2)

At a=-1 primes, the first marking equation (-1-a)u=0 is automatic and the second equation ub=(a-1)v is solved as ub=-2v. At a=+1 primes, the first equation is 2u=0 and the second can be solved by b=0. The projective reduction map in Section 2 lifts this prescribed b globally. Therefore

   lambda s=a lambda,

so s lies in Gamma0, has lower-right residue a in standard coordinates, and normalizes Gamma1. It squares to the identity and conjugates n_z to n_{-z}. This does not require one global sign choice across all level primes; the required *single global matrix* has been constructed using b and CRT.

Let z:Gamma0->A be any crossed homomorphism with action through R_N(a), as in the actual arithmetic construction. Since n_z belongs to Gamma1 its coefficient action is trivial. The cocycle identity gives

   z(s n_z s^{-1})=R_N(a)z(n_z).

The left side is z(n_z^{-1})=-z(n_z). Hence the actual cusp value satisfies

   (1+R_N(a)) z(n_z)=0.                                  (6.3)

The terms involving z(s) cancel exactly; no evaluation of a singular geometric comparison cochain or unverified source-sign convention is involved. Projecting by e_W turns (6.3) into 2 e_W z(n_z)=0. Thus

   e_W z(n_z)=0.                                         (6.4)

The same statement is well-defined for a Gamma0 cohomology class: a coboundary restricts to zero on Gamma1 because that subgroup acts trivially on A.

### Theorem 6.2 (locally shallow, sign-trivial compactification)

Assume (L). Every cusp satisfies (6.1), since the two integer intervals [0,e_q] and [n_q-e_q,n_q] cover [0,n_q]. Consequently e_W z restricts to zero on every cusp and gives a unique map on H1(X,R).

For the accepted unit-injective arithmetic family this proves compactification of e_W z_{c,N} for all auxiliaries prime to 5N, with unchanged K2(O'(N)) tensor R target. It also applies to every existing unaugmented Gamma0 class after applying e_W. At a rational p>=5 and a character chi of the prime-to-p full ray group, the hypothesis is simply

   chi|W_N=1.

There is no condition on the p-primary ray subgroup, no use of norm injectivity, and no residual-character exclusion beyond this explicit local-sign condition. The conclusion is valid at p=5 as well. It is a distinct direct parabolicity criterion; it is not claimed to contain every character covered by the source's good-Hecke boundary theorem.

The source-calibration and inverse-tame sign are retained. Equation (6.3) is homogeneous in z, so replacing all standard-sign representatives by their source-sign negatives changes no vanishing assertion. We do not use this sign insensitivity to identify actual nonzero relative edge values.

## 7. What happens outside these proved regions

When (V) fails with only the units +/-1, the rank-two boundary summand from Section 4 is an ambient topological fact. It is not a nonzero K2 cusp value. Even its image in the interior homology may have a kernel, and the actual normalized map may vanish there.

For a general cusp at which (6.1) fails, no matrix of the specific form (6.2) with local signs +/-1 can supply the required conjugation identity. A middle cusp at an odd prime power q^n, 0<r_q<n, is the basic example. At an odd q the inequalities reduce to r_q=0 or r_q=n. Thus q^2 already leaves a middle-cusp obstruction to this argument. For units +/-1 the strict cusp stabilizer there acts trivially on its unipotent lattice.

On sign-odd target summands even when a reflection in Gamma0 exists, equation (6.3) becomes zero times the cusp value and imposes no vanishing. No division by 1+chi(a) is made when chi(a)=-1.

The prior unrestricted bad-U obstruction is also retained. The accepted norm theorem gives Cor(f(U^vee-1))=0 for proper lower M, but noninflated characters can lie entirely in that norm kernel and wild relative degrees cannot be canceled. Neither the cusp-reflection proof nor the existence of boundary tori makes this defect vanish. At pure prime-power level the lower origin remains outside the accepted norm-specialization argument.

No actual nonzero cusp value contradicting the source-calibrated construction has been found. The unresolved part of compactification is therefore a *specific uncomputed restriction of the actual normalized cocycle* on the remaining cusp lattices, not a proof of failure of the construction.

## 8. Optional exact criterion with all roots of unity

This section records the exact topology criterion for the exceptional unit fields without substituting it for the simpler theorem.

In (2.1), an element of the strict cusp stabilizer with epsilon!=delta exists precisely when some distinct units epsilon,delta satisfy, at every q,

   r_q>=n_q-v_q(epsilon-1),
   r_q<=v_q(delta-1),                                    (8.1)

with v_q(0)=infinity and bounds truncated to [0,n_q]. These are just the two marking equations

   (epsilon-1)u=0,   ub=(1-delta)v,

using the same primitive-row and global b-lifting argument as before. Such a stabilizer element acts on T by multiplication by epsilon/delta. The number 1-epsilon/delta is a unit in O tensor R: differences of distinct roots of unity in an imaginary quadratic field have norm supported at 2 and 3. Thus its coinvariants on T tensor R vanish. If no distinct pair satisfies (8.1), every stabilizer acts trivially and H1(B_cusp,R)=T tensor R.

This proves an exact, finite criterion at every cusp for every imaginary quadratic F. As before, all divisor vectors are realized in the principal component, so checking this finite union of boxes for every divisor of N is equivalent to vanishing of the entire boundary H1.

For F=Q(i), every nontrivial unit epsilon has (epsilon-1) dividing (2), and the plus/minus-one criterion already contains every box (8.1). Thus (V) is also necessary and sufficient in this field.

For F=Q(sqrt(-3)), write q2=(2), q3=(1-omega), with omega a primitive cube root of unity. Nonzero valuations of epsilon-1 are: one at q2 for epsilon=-1; one at q3 for epsilon=omega or omega^2; and none for epsilon=-omega or -omega^2. Formula (8.1) is the exact criterion. In particular every q3^n level for n<=3 has zero first boundary homology, using the rotations with ratio omega, while q3^4 has a rank-two middle cusp. The general mixed-level decision is made by (8.1), not by treating these two exceptional primes independently when a single global pair of units is required.
