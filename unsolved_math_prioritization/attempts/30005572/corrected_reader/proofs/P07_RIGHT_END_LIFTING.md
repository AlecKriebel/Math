# Trace-fixed residue primitives over Z[1/6]

Authored proof, 14 October 2026. Shared mathematical PASS 2/5, row 1777 / 30005572. This note is a proof candidate for independent audit. It changes no accepted PASS 1 file. No claim of novelty or of resolution of the original Eisenstein-map problem is made.

## 1. Statement and precise scope

Put R = Z[1/6]. All tensors in this note are over Z with R, unless another ring is displayed. Let L be a number field, F an imaginary quadratic field with maximal order O, and A = E1 ×_L E2. The O-actions are defined over L. For the general right-end theorem assume the two O-actions have the same CM type, equivalently

Hom_O(E1_barL,E2_barL) is a nonzero rank-one projective O-module.

This hypothesis holds for every one of the h² products in the CM model family of Lecouturier–Sharifi–Shih–Wang (LSSW), §3.1: the curves have compatible analytic descriptions C/a_r and are L-isogenous, and the displayed isogenies are defined over L. Neither class number one nor a principal auxiliary ideal is assumed.

Use the Gersten complex

C2 = K2(L(A)) ⊗ R,

C1 = (direct sum over prime divisors D of A of L(D)^×) ⊗ R,

C0 = (direct sum over closed points x of A of Z[x]) ⊗ R.

Its differentials are the tame residues and divisors. A trace T_alpha means pushforward by simultaneous multiplication by alpha. For a module M with these traces, M^tf is the union of the submodules fixed by all alpha prime to some nonzero exceptional element of O. Enlarge exceptional elements whenever necessary; this is the same eventual-invariance convention as in the frozen PASS 1 proofs.

**Right-end theorem.** Under the same-type hypothesis,

C1^tf -> C0^tf -> R -> 0

is exact, where the last map is degree. For the source's h² products the proof uses no extension-degree division. For arbitrary same-type twists it only divides by the order of a subgroup of O^×, hence by factors of 2 and 3.

For any product of two O-CM elliptic curves, even without the same-type hypothesis, the particular auxiliary zero-cycle

e_c = (Nc)^2[(0,0)] - [E1[c] × E2[c]]

has the explicit full-CM-trace-fixed primitive in §6. The same-type hypothesis is needed by our proof of the general right-end theorem, not by that particular construction.

The proof does not infer that diagonal multiplication orbits on a product are Cartesian products. For example, over a field where the relevant torsion is rational, the orbit of (P,Q) under simultaneous units is generally smaller than the product of the two individual orbits. The precise meaning of the source's “components” would have to be settled before importing its matrix argument literally. The argument below bypasses that issue, without alleging a defect in the published theorem.

## 2. The one-curve normalization lemma

Let E/L be an elliptic curve with its origin rational over L. Suppose E has O-action over L. The scalar-integer version also works without CM. Let D be a degree-zero divisor with R coefficients which is eventually trace-fixed.

**Lemma.** There is a unique eventually trace-fixed element

u_D in L(E)^× ⊗ R

whose divisor is D. If D is supported on E[n], then u_D belongs to O(E - E[n])^× ⊗ R. This is an element of a tensor product; it does not assert the existence of literal 2-power or 3-power roots of functions.

### 2.1 Integral trace weights

The divisor sequence gives exact sequences

0 -> L^× ⊗ R -> L(E)^× ⊗ R -> Prin(E) ⊗ R -> 0,

0 -> Prin(E) ⊗ R -> Div^0(E) ⊗ R -> Pic^0(E)(L) ⊗ R -> 0.

The rational origin identifies the degree-zero Picard group here with Pic^0(E)(L); no Brauer obstruction is being discarded. Flatness of R preserves the sequences. Norm by [m] acts as m² on L^× and as m on Pic^0(E)(L), for every positive integer m. The latter identity is integral: on a divisor class represented by [P]-[0] over an algebraic closure, pushforward is [mP]-[0], and the Abel–Jacobi identification is compatible with descent. Equivalently it follows from the pointed integral curve projectors in the accepted PASS 1 proof.

Thus

P_m(T_m) = (T_m-m)(T_m-m²)

annihilates both the constants and Pic^0(E)(L) ⊗ R. These are literal integral actions, not rational eigenvalue arguments followed by cancellation in torsion groups.

### 2.2 An augmentation-one annihilator

Fix an exceptional element beta and a positive integer n divisible by all rational primes under beta. If support on E[n0] is prescribed, enlarge n also by the rational primes under n0. For positive integers m prime to n, set

r(m) = (1-m)(1-m²).

The ideal generated by these r(m) in R is R. Indeed, for every prime q >= 5 there is a nonzero residue a modulo q different from 1 and -1. CRT produces an m congruent to a modulo q and prime to n. This excludes q from the common divisor. At least one r(m) is nonzero, and 2 and 3 are units in R. Therefore a finite Bezout identity exists:

sum_j a_j r(m_j) = 1, with a_j in R.

Put Phi_E = sum_j a_j P_(m_j)(T_(m_j)). It annihilates constants and Pic^0, commutes with every full-CM trace, and has augmentation one. It therefore acts as the identity on every fixed divisor for this exceptional monoid. No coefficient other than a denominator supported at 2 and 3 occurs.

### 2.3 Existence and uniqueness

The class of D in Pic^0 is both fixed by Phi_E and annihilated by it, so is zero. Choose an ordinary tensor-valued function f with divisor D. For every allowed alpha,

(T_alpha-1)f belongs to L^× ⊗ R.

Consequently u_D = Phi_E f is fixed by all these alpha and has divisor D. If D is supported on E[n0], so is the divisor of f, and all scalar traces used in Phi_E preserve E[n0]. Thus u_D is a unit off E[n0].

If two fixed functions have divisor D, their difference is an eventually fixed constant. The same augmentation-one annihilator kills that difference and also fixes it. The difference is zero. This proves uniqueness, including when the two initial exceptional elements differ.

This also proves that u_D is independent of f, Phi_E, and the exceptional element. If some additional trace T_alpha fixes D, then T_alpha u_D is eventually fixed and has the same divisor, so T_alpha u_D = u_D. In particular, when D is invariant under every alpha prime to n0, the normalized function has that exact invariance, without extra exceptional primes.

All these assertions commute with extension of the base field: pullback of u_D is the unique normalized function for the pulled-back divisor. Constants are fixed by no nonzero eventual invariant over R, in every characteristic-zero extension field as well.

## 3. Fixed zero-cycles are sums of torsion orbits

Choose a positive integer m >= 2 prime to an exceptional element for z in C0^tf. After geometric base change, z is a finite signed R-valued measure on A(barL), invariant under pushforward by [m]. Such a measure is supported on periodic points. Here is a direct finite-support argument that also handles cancellation: every point having nonzero coefficient must have a preimage with nonzero coefficient in the finite support; follow these preimages backwards. A repeated point gives a periodic cycle, and its unique forward path contains the original point. Therefore the original point is periodic. In an abelian variety, [m]^k P=P implies (m^k-1)P=0. Every support point is torsion.

Choose a common annihilating ideal n. The reductions modulo n of the alpha prime to the original exceptional element and to n surject onto (O/n)^×: prescribe the desired unit modulo n and use CRT to avoid the remaining exceptional prime ideals. It follows that z is invariant under (O/n)^× on A[n](barL), as well as under Galois.

Let Omega run over orbits of the group generated by Galois and (O/n)^× on that finite set. Write [Omega] for the corresponding reduced L-zero-cycle and d_Omega = #Omega for its degree. Then

z = sum_Omega r_Omega [Omega], with r_Omega in R.

If deg z=0, this becomes

z = sum_Omega r_Omega ([Omega] - d_Omega[0]).

This identity introduces no division by an orbit size, by Nn, or by a class number.

## 4. An elliptic subgroup containing an entire orbit

First suppose all O-linear isogenies between E1 and E2 are defined over L. For every nonzero ideal n there is such an isogeny phi:E1 -> E2 with kernel prime to n.

To check the prime-to-n assertion, use compatible CM lattices E_i(C)=C/a_i. The Hom module is the invertible fractional ideal J=a2 a1^(-1). For each prime p dividing n, choose a nonzero class in J/pJ. CRT for the module J produces a nonzero alpha in J with those classes. Its kernel ideal is

k = alpha a1 a2^(-1) = alpha J^(-1),

which is integral and prime to n. The associated isogeny is defined over L by the hypothesis. This lattice computation is only an existence proof for an algebraic isogeny; it introduces no extension or analytic function into a residue primitive.

The module E1[n](barL) is free of rank one over O/n. Indeed, the CM lattice description gives an invertible rank-one O/n-module, and an invertible module over the finite semilocal ring O/n is free. Choose a generator U. As phi is prime to n, it identifies E1[n] with E2[n]. For any P=(P1,P2) in A[n](barL), write

P1=aU,  P2=phi(bU), with a,b in O/n,

and choose lifts a,b to O. If P is not zero, the morphism

E1 -> A,  T |-> ([a]T, phi([b]T))

has elliptic image B over L. It is O-linear, so B is O-stable. It contains P; since it is defined over L and O-stable, it contains the full Galois-and-unit orbit Omega of P. The induced origin and O-action make B another same-type CM elliptic curve. For the orbit of zero use either coordinate elliptic curve, although its degree-zero difference is already zero.

On B the divisor [Omega]-d_Omega[0] is degree zero and invariant under every alpha prime to n. Section 2 supplies its unique trace-fixed function u_Omega. The closed immersion i_B:B -> A pushes u_Omega into the B-component of C1. Proper pushforward commutes with residues and CM traces, so

partial(i_B,* u_Omega) = [Omega]-d_Omega[0],

and i_B,*u_Omega is trace-fixed. Summing these primitives with coefficients r_Omega proves the right-end lifting theorem when all isogenies are defined over L. Degree is onto because [0] has degree one, and every principal divisor has degree zero.

### 4.1 Same-type twists: why only 2 and 3 occur

For the general same-type hypothesis, let J=Hom_O(E1_barL,E2_barL). Galois acts O-linearly on J because both CM actions are defined over L. Since J is an invertible O-module,

Aut_O(J)=O^×.

The action is continuous with finite image; for example, a Z-basis of the Hom module is defined over a finite extension. Let L'/L be its kernel extension. It is finite Galois and its degree divides #O^×, which is 2, 4, or 6. All the required isogenies are defined over L'.

For p:A_L' -> A, apply the preceding proof to p^*z, obtaining eta'. Then

eta = [L':L]^(-1) p_* eta'

is defined with coefficients in R, is full-CM-trace-fixed, and has residue z because p_*p^*z=[L':L]z. This is a fully specified transfer argument, not an unlicensed division by a general field-extension degree. No factors beyond 2 and 3 are inverted.

For the LSSW model family, this paragraph is unnecessary: §3.1 already supplies L-defined isogenies between every pair, and the proof in the preceding paragraphs applies directly to all h² products. Opposite CM types, nonmaximal orders, and an arbitrary large extension degree are not included in this argument.

## 5. Canonical auxiliary one-curve functions and exact distribution laws

For a nonzero ideal c of O, write q=Nc and define

Z_c(E)=q[0]-[E[c]].

This has degree zero and is fixed by all alpha prime to c. Section 2 gives a unique normalized element

theta_c,E in O(E-E[c])^× ⊗ R,

div(theta_c,E)=Z_c(E),  T_alpha theta_c,E=theta_c,E if (alpha,c)=1.

For c=O it is zero in additive notation. We will use additive notation for tensor-valued multiplicative groups throughout the formulas below. There is no undetermined multiplicative constant, including no residual fifth-root ambiguity.

Let f:E -> E' be an O-linear isogeny over L with kernel E[a]. Put g=a+c, the ideal gcd. Scheme-theoretic torsion calculations in characteristic zero give

f^*E'[c]=E[ac],

f_*E[c]=Ng · E'[c g^(-1)],

f^*[0]=E[a],  f_*[0]=[0].

For example the kernel on E[c] is E[g], of size Ng, and the image is exactly E'[c g^(-1)]; this can be checked prime by prime on the free rank-one torsion modules. Hence uniqueness gives the exact identities

f_*theta_c,E = Ng · theta_(c g^(-1)),E',                                      (5.1)

f^*theta_c,E' = theta_ac,E - Nc · theta_a,E.                                 (5.2)

For (a,c)=1, (5.1) is simply f_*theta_c,E=theta_c,E'. Pushforward preserves eventual invariance because all endomorphisms commute. Pullback preserves it after excluding primes of a: the square with f and [alpha] is cartesian when their kernel ideals are coprime, so finite-flat base change commutes with the trace. Thus the uniqueness lemma really applies in (5.2); the equality is not merely an equality modulo constants.

These formulas, their compositions, and the base-field compatibility in §2 give a canonical norm-compatible system on every curve in the h-component CM family. They involve no choice of generators of nonprincipal ideals and no averaging over the class group.

## 6. An explicit primitive on every product

For A=E1×E2 define, with q=Nc,

eta_c = theta_c,E1 exterior [E2[c]] + q[0] exterior theta_c,E2.               (6.1)

Exterior products here put functions on the horizontal or vertical prime divisors of A, with the usual finite-étale component decomposition if torsion is not rational. They are defined over L without splitting a torsion field. Their boundaries are

partial eta_c = (q[0]-E1[c]) exterior E2[c]
                 + q[0] exterior (q[0]-E2[c])
               = q²[(0,0)] - [E1[c]×E2[c]] = e_c.

For alpha prime to c, finite pushforward respects exterior products, preserves the reduced torsion cycles, fixes [0], and fixes the two theta functions. Therefore T_alpha eta_c=eta_c. Formula (6.1) works for both CM-type choices and for ramified as well as unramified 5; no left-end injectivity is needed to construct it.

### 6.1 Exact product norms, including non-coprime ideals

Let f_i:E_i -> E_i' have kernel E_i[a_i], and put g_i=a_i+c and c_i=c g_i^(-1). For f=f1×f2, (5.1) and exterior-product compatibility give

f_*eta_c = Ng1 Ng2 · theta_c1,E1' exterior E2'[c2]
              + q Ng2 · [0] exterior theta_c2,E2'.                         (6.2)

Its boundary is exactly

q²[0] - Ng1 Ng2 · [E1'[c1]×E2'[c2]],

as can also be seen by pushing e_c. In particular, if both a_i are prime to c,

f_*eta_c = eta_c on the target product.                                    (6.3)

For pullback, the exact formula is

f^*eta_c' = (theta_a1c,E1-q theta_a1,E1) exterior E2[a2c]
                 + q E1[a1] exterior (theta_a2c,E2-q theta_a2,E2).          (6.4)

Thus there is no hidden “up to a constant” in any norm or pullback formula. In contrast, identifying different two-dimensional primitives can require a K2 boundary; it need not be literal equality of C1-elements.

For clarity, when a1=a2=a, let r=Na. Then

f^*e_c = e_ac-q²e_a.                                                       (6.5)

With the tame-symbol convention partial{u,v} = u exterior div(v) - div(u) exterior v for a separated-variable symbol, (6.4) also gives the explicit chain correction

f^*eta_c' - eta_ac + q²eta_a
  = q partial{theta_a,E1, f2^*theta_c,E2'}.                                 (6.6)

The brace is the bilinear Steinberg product in K2(L(A)) ⊗ R. The displayed ordinary K2 symbol is not asserted trace-fixed; the left-exact repair, when applicable, supplies its unique trace-fixed replacement with this residue. Formula (6.6) specifies the chain correction rather than suppressing it as a normalization issue.

0 -> Q -> C1^tf -> C0^tf -> R -> 0.                                        (7.1)
