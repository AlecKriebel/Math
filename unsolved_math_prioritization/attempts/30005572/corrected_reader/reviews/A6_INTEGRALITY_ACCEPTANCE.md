## 1. Arithmetic localization

The finite-set localization argument is correct. For A the ring of integers of L and S the primes above q, finite-field K2 vanishes, so the left map K2(A) to K2(A_S) is injective. The next obstruction is the direct sum of residue-field multiplicative groups. The map K1(A) to K1(A_S) is injective because SK1 vanishes for these number rings and K1 identifies with the unit group. Thus the displayed sequence is genuinely short exact, not just exact in the middle.

Tensoring with Z[1/6], Z[1/30], or Z_p preserves exactness. A homomorphism into the localized K2 group therefore has a unique integer-ring lift exactly when its residue composite vanishes. No splitting of the short exact sequence is needed. Passing to a prime-to-p character component also preserves exactness: it is the idempotent localization of the finite prime-to-p group ring used in the source, with its unramified coefficient ring. This last fact is implicit in the application and supplies the required justification for the character-component lift.

The sign convention for the tame symbol is consistent throughout. Reversing all tame symbols would change neither support nor integrality conclusions.

## 2. Ray inertia and depth-independent residue module

For modulus q^n, L/F is abelian and unramified away from q. In an abelian extension, all the inertia groups above q agree. Killing that common group I therefore kills every finite ramification group of L/F. Since F is imaginary quadratic, there is no real-place issue. The fixed field L^I is an everywhere-unramified abelian extension of F and hence lies in the Hilbert class field H. Conversely, H is a subfield of the ray field and inertia acts trivially on H. Thus L^I=H and I=Gal(L/H).

Consequently each H-prime above q is totally ramified in L/H, with a unique prime above it and identical residue field. This proves the canonical inflation statement, including its Galois action. This is stronger than an order calculation for the ray kernel and avoids the invalid inference that a ramified extension must be totally ramified merely because its ramification is supported at one rational prime.

Let C=Cl(F), kappa=[q] under arithmetic Artin reciprocity, and f=ord_C(kappa). The decomposition group in H/F is the cyclic group generated by kappa. Hence there are h/f residue fields, each of size Q^f, where Q=Nq. The exponent is exactly Q^f−1 and not merely a divisor of that integer. The induced-module formula with kappa acting by multiplication by Q on the additive presentation of the residue units follows directly. The noncanonical presentation Z[C]/(kappa−Q) has the correct index and exponent: the relation kappa^f=1 forces Q^f−1=0, leaving h/f independent cyclic generators.

The construction uses the ordinary ideal class of q, never a nonexistent ray class of a prime dividing the modulus. No principality hypothesis is hidden here. Its action through C and its exponent are independent of n.

## 3. Exact prime-to-p character support

The character theorem is correct in the precise source convention. In the author manuscript, §4.3 defines the component by tensoring with the quotient imposing g=chi(g) on G', not g=chi(g)^{-1}; the full p-Sylow action remains. The same section identifies the cyclotomic character composed with the Artin map with the norm character modulo p. Proposition 5.1.2 uses direct Artin action on K2. Thus the surviving residue condition is chi(kappa)=Q modulo p, with no character inversion.

Here is an independent module calculation. Write C=C_p×C', J=<kappa>=J_p×J', f=p^b f_0, and a=v_p(Q^f−1). If a=0 there is no p-primary residue. Otherwise the p-primary residue at one prime is V=Z/p^a with the generator kappa acting by Q. Over O_chi it is O_chi/p^a. The restriction of the action to J' has prime-to-p order and is its unique Teichmuller lift from reduction modulo p. A character chi that does not descend through G/I kills the component, since a nontrivial prime-to-p character value differs from 1 by a unit. For a descending chi, its restriction to J' either matches the indicated action exactly modulo p^a or differs by a unit on some element. The former condition is precisely bar_chi(kappa)=Q. In that case induction leaves

(O_chi/p^a) raised to the power |C_p|/|J_p|,

with the actual induced C_p action, so it is nonzero. This proves both directions and the stated multiplicity.

No p-power character was cancelled by dividing by zeta_p−1. The report explicitly retains the possible nontrivial C_p action. Its warning example with a trivial F_p inertia module and a faithful p-power character has nonzero tensor quotient O/(p,zeta_p−1), as claimed. Ramification through a p-group is therefore not being used as an integral vanishing criterion.

At p equal to the residue characteristic, all residue groups vanish because their orders are prime to that characteristic. At p=5 the same group calculation remains valid, but the existing Z[1/30] construction provides no 5-primary map. These two issues are correctly separated.

## 4. Integral targets of actual maps and diamond coinvariants

The unique integral lifts off the exact support follow from the short exact sequence and exact character projection. All the original source hypotheses remain necessary for existence of the maps. No compactification is inferred from arithmetic integrality. Conversely, a separately known compactification does not by itself kill arithmetic tame symbols.

The diamond calculation is valid: the source relation is f(<d>x)=R_N(d)f(x), while every principal ray class maps trivially to the ordinary class group. Hence it lies in I and acts trivially on the residue module. The residue map factors through diamond coinvariants. This conclusion does not identify those coinvariants with lower-level homology when the relevant index has p-part, and it does not supply a factorization through cusp boundaries.

Any pre-existing Hecke, Artin, or smoothing relation for the localized map survives uniquely in the lifted integral target, because the target inclusion is injective and equivariant. This is preservation of existing relations, not a new bad-prime theorem.

## 5. Auxiliary reduction

For p>=7 different from ell, choose a power d of ell lying in q^n. The conditions t=1 mod d and t=u mod p, where u^4!=1, are compatible. Such a nonzero u exists because p−1>4. Increasing t by dp makes it a proper positive auxiliary integer. The principal auxiliary ideal (t) is ray-trivial, has norm t^2, and has smoothing factor t^4−1. It is a p-adic unit. Thus the equivalence between vanishing of the unaugmented residue and of this one auxiliary residue is legitimate coefficient-ring inversion, not division in an arbitrary finite K2 group.

For the finitely many p>=7 dividing Q^f−1, the Chinese remainder conditions can be imposed simultaneously. The resulting smoothing factor is an automorphism of the whole Z[1/30] residue module; it need not be an automorphism of the entire K2 group. That distinction in the report is essential and correct. This argument cannot resolve the 5-primary issue because every 5-adic unit has fourth power 1 modulo 5.

## 7. Cusp-potential and symbol criterion

The relative homology sequence is correct because the finite cusp set has H1=0. Thus H1(X) injects into H1(X,C), and its cokernel is ker[H0(C) to H0(X)]. Given an already constructed relative map, its absolute restriction is integral if and only if its residue factors through this boundary quotient. The factorization is unique. Assigning one base cusp in each connected component converts it to the difference-of-potentials formula.

For a proposed endpoint graph, zero residue sum on every finite endpoint-balanced combination is exactly the graph-incidence factorization condition. A spanning forest constructs the potential without division in the target, so finite torsion poses no problem. A disconnected edge graph is handled componentwise. This graph test does not manufacture a relative homology map or account for absolute cycles not represented by these edge combinations.

The three-term smoothing identity checks by bilinearity. With a=u^m/(sigma u) and b=v^m/(sigma v), expanding {a,b}+{a,sigma v}+{sigma u,b} cancels the two mixed terms and leaves m^2{u,v}−{sigma u,sigma v}. Applying the equivariant residue map preserves that identity. Keeping only {a,b} would be wrong. The valuation/angular-component tame-symbol formula is also correct.

The surviving obligation (7.6) is an actual cocycle-residue calculation on every class-group component, after the legitimate smoothing reduction. It is expressly unproved. Neither nonzero ambient residue groups, diamond coinvariants, nor the generic theta identities replace it.
