## 1. Accepted normalization claims

### 1.1 One-dimensional auxiliary normalization

For a characteristic-zero elliptic curve and a nonzero rational endomorphism alpha, the divisor

`deg(alpha)(0) - E[alpha]`

has Picard class killed by two. The rational zero section and Hilbert 90 justify obtaining a function after inverting two. The scalar norm defects are constants. Their commutation relation and a finite Bezout combination of `r^2-1`, with r coprime to `deg(alpha)`, produce a unique scalar-trace-fixed function in `k(E)^x tensor Z[1/6]`.

The asserted coefficient ideal is indeed the unit ideal: for each prime at least five, the specified CRT choices give an allowed r whose square is not one modulo that prime. This argument works even when the auxiliary degree has factors two or three. It neither invokes nor resolves the distinct constant obstruction on a product of elliptic curves.

The same uniqueness proves isomorphism functoriality, evenness, and invariance under CM units. Commuting isogeny norm/pullback squares are used only with an additional scalar degree coprime to the degrees concerned. The full-CM norm claim is restricted to endomorphisms prime to the auxiliary kernel.

### 1.2 Exact auxiliary exchange

The two sides of equation (2.1) have the displayed common divisor. Their difference is a constant fixed by all scalar traces of suitable coprime degree, hence is zero over R. Evaluation at a nonzero point fixed by both endomorphisms is defined and gives (2.2) exactly.

Crucially, this does **not** by itself give a divided exchange relation when every degree-minus-one coefficient has a common nonunit factor. The corrected proof respects that limitation.

- Use the point's **exact order e**, not an assumed exact order n.

## 2. Accepted restricted CM root construction

Let I_N be the ideal generated by `Norm(alpha)-1` for actual elements `alpha=1 mod N`. The claimed criterion

`I_N=R` if and only if no rational prime p at least five has `(p)|N^2`

is correct. At a split prime, both conjugate primes must divide N for the obstruction to occur; an arbitrarily deep one-sided split level is not excluded. At an inert prime, any occurrence in N forces the obstruction. At a ramified odd prime, the first prime-ideal power already does so.

An independent formulation is useful: in the quadratic algebra `O/pO`, all `Norm(1+x)-1`, for x in the image of N, vanish exactly when the square of that ideal image is zero. This is the same ideal-divisibility condition. Direct exact checks in split, inert, and ramified quadratic algebras at seven primes are retained in `INDEPENDENT_ALGEBRA_CHECKS.json`; the written case argument supplies the general proof.

When I_N is the unit ideal, equation (2.2) supports the Bezout definition without canceling a nonunit. Thus the unpowered theta value is well-defined in the actual marked-point field's multiplicative group tensored with R, including its retained torsion. Its agreement `U_n=n theta` follows by the unit ideal of scalar coefficients, again without dividing by n in that group.

When `I_N=dR` with d a nonunit, the initial proposed canonical V_N is withdrawn. The corrected proof claims only the failure of this particular unit-coefficient method and the separately valid scalar-powered U_n. ### Cyclic order seven

`(-A+3B-2C) wedge (2A+B-3C) = -7(A wedge B + B wedge C + C wedge A)`.
