# Source scope acceptance for the five free blowup counterexample

## Verdict

Accept the five-blowup result as a counterexample to the primary-source Nash-intersection assertion under the explicit natural indexing readings mapped in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md). This supports a qualified claimed-solved designation. It is stronger than a counterexample restricted to the originally disclosed last-shared normalization. No source-stated hypothesis excludes the example, and the identified index repairs do not save the assertion.

The printed bare relation remains undefined. This verdict does not authenticate an erratum or claim a result about every conceivable intended replacement. It does not concern the catalogue's union transcription, Conjecture 2, the wider generalized Nash problem, or global novelty.

## Source hypotheses and definitions

The inspected primary sources are [the full paper, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.06898v3), §2 pp.5–7, §4.1 pp.24–25, and §4.2 pp.30–32; and [the workshop contribution](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46651), printed pp.2678–2680. The relevant definitions and conjecture passages were read and visually checked during source review.

A prime divisor includes any irreducible exceptional component of a point-blowup model. The source expressly discusses nonsatellite divisors and additional free blowups after resolving the associated branch. Combinatorial type records the full exceptional configuration, including these additional blowups. There is no singular-curvetta, satellite-only, or minimal-embedded-resolution restriction on the asserted quantifier. In the example the selected center c3 is free; no convention declaring the origin itself free is required.

Contact counts common blown-up centers. Closed Nash sets are Zariski closures, and Lemma 4.3 gives the same closure from transverse loci. Thus an arc whose limit meets a satellite point may still belong to every closed Nash set in a free family. The positive integer q is an intersection/contact multiplicity. The exact-q formulation in the paper and the at-least-q workshop formulation yield the same closed cylinders in the free-chain calculation.

## Exhaustive mapping of the identified natural readings

For E=D5 along y=0, reference Dj has weights (1,j). The full F2 intersection has thresholds (3,5), and the full F3 intersection has thresholds (2,5). Every proposed multiple cylinder has thresholds (q,jq).

- Last-shared normalization: F2/D2, requiring q=3 and 2q=5.
- Literal zero-based created-divisor reading p2=c3: F2/D3, requiring q=3 and 3q=5.
- Fix the selected point as well: F3/D3, requiring q=2 and 3q=5.
- One-based local labels p3=c3 and contact at least 3: again F3/D3.

All fail. The stronger arithmetic statement excludes every reference Dj because neither 3j=5 nor 2j=5 has an integer solution. A separately identified conditional exact-contact interpretation also fails: first moving coefficients 1 and 2 force exactly the same reduced intersections. This conditional calculation is not attributed as a definition supplied by the source.

The three-blowup example would become a singleton if its final selected center were fixed. The five-blowup example eliminates that endpoint loophole. Restricting the question to singular curvettas, satellite final divisors or the first reference divisor would change the stated quantifier. An unspecified stronger replacement is not established or refuted here.

The requested computation of q is conditional on the equality being true. Its failure completes that conditional question for the asserted general statement.
