# Credited source resolution: essential finite generation of valuation rings

**Proposed disposition: already_solved, 0/5 substantive author turns.** This packet checks a published classification against the exact original question. It makes no new-proof or novelty claim. Independent source review and the parent publication gate are pending.

## 1. Exact source question and notation

Novacoski's contribution, joint with Kuhlmann, in OWR49/2014 printed pp.2798–2800 (PDF42–44), first discusses finite inertial extensions and finite generation by henselian elements. Its **second** question, at the end of printed2799, explicitly removes the inertial restriction and asks when a finite valued-field extension has an essentially finitely generated extension of valuation rings. The following page supplies a necessary condition and special-case sufficiency. Thus the surrounding target is a characterization, not an unconditional assertion that every finite extension works.

Use the source's upper/lower fields F/L and a specified valuation v on F. Put V=O_(v|L), W=O_v, and let m_W be W's maximal ideal. The inclusion V⊂W is local: m_W∩V=m_V. Both rings may be non-Noetherian. Define

 e=[vF:vL], f=[Fv:Lv],
 ε=|{γ∈vF: γ≥0 and γ<δ for every δ∈vL with δ>0}|.

Take the compatible henselizations induced by V⊂W and write L^h=Frac(V^h), F^h=Frac(W^h). The local defect is

 d=[F^h:L^h]/(ef).

All degrees and the initial index are finite here. No separability, normality, rank-one, discreteness, henselian-base, residue-perfectness, function-field or characteristic-zero hypothesis is added.

## 2. Full published classification

**Datta, Theorem1.2:** W is essentially of finite type over V exactly when d=1 and ε=e. Equivalently W is essentially of finite presentation, or W^h is finite over V^h. The theorem covers arbitrary finite F/L with the chosen extending valuation. Its proof also produces a finite V-subalgebra B of the integral closure in F with W=B_(B∩m_W).

Reference: Rankeya Datta, *Essential finite generation of extensions of valuation rings*, Math. Nachr.296(2023),1041–1055, DOI10.1002/mana.202100190. The complete arXiv2101.08337v2 (24 September2024) was read; arXiv explicitly describes it as updated to match the journal version. The publisher verifies publication on5January2023. No downloaded typeset journal PDF is claimed.

Datta's notation L/K corresponds to F/L here. Theorem1.2 is on PDF2 and11, the defect conventions on PDF10–11, and the decisive descent proof on PDF11–12. These are the actual source inputs, not an inference from the abstract.

## 3. Why the conclusion has exactly the requested localization

The original asks for elements a_i∈W and localization at p=V[a_i]∩m_W, not an arbitrary unnamed multiplicative set. The two formulations agree for this local domain extension. If W=S^(-1)A with A=V[a_i]⊂W, then S avoids p. Every a∈A\p is a unit of W because W is local. Therefore A_p⊂W, while S⊂A\p gives W⊂A_p. Hence W=A_p. Datta's stronger finite-subalgebra conclusion already supplies precisely this form.

This elementary convention check is not a new research attempt. In particular, “essentially finite type” does not mean that W itself is a finite V-module, or even a finite type V-algebra without localization.

## 4. Necessary correction to the report's displayed defect

The actual report image on printed2800 displays [F:L]/(e_i f_i) for d_i while also allowing multiple extensions of v|L. That total degree cannot be used as the local defect in the general statement. The compatible henselian degree [F_i^h:L^h] is required. This is the convention used in Cutkosky–Novacoski, PDF3, and explicitly in Datta Definition4.8, PDF10. If the extending valuation is unique, the two degrees agree; the distinction matters in split extensions.

A classical split-prime check illustrates the issue: let V=Z_(5), F=Q(√6), and select the prime with √6≡1 modulo5. The polynomial X²−6 has distinct roots ±1 modulo5, so this branch has e=f=1 and henselian local degree1. Its valuation ring is a localization of the finite algebra V[√6], hence is essentially finite type. Indeed, (√6−1)(√6+1)=5 and √6+1 is a unit at the selected prime, so that localization is a one-dimensional Noetherian local domain with maximal ideal generated by5, hence a DVR. The total degree2 would incorrectly give a putative defect2. This is a routine convention diagnostic, not a new counterexample to the classification.

The extracted bare yes/no question has answer “not always.” Published counterexamples were already available: Cutkosky–Novacoski Example2.5 has a branch ω2 with e=2, ε=1 and d=1. By their Knaf necessity theorem (Theorem4.1), that branch is not essentially finite type. We cite their stated finite field extension; we do not claim their displayed cubic is irreducible over every valued field with the same value group. The complete source-context problem is answered by the if-and-only-if theorem above.

## 5. Scope boundaries and credit

- The condition is local at the **chosen** extending valuation. Finiteness of the entire integral closure requires the corresponding conditions at **all** extensions. One cannot demand all branches for the local question or infer their conditions from one branch.
- The earlier finite-inertial theorem and its non-finite-generation examples concern finite generation without localization. They do not contradict essential finite generation in the inertial case.
- ε=e is stronger than merely having nonnegative representatives of value-group cosets. Each representative in the defining initial segment must lie below every strictly positive base value.
- Henselization, not completion or strict henselization, appears in d. Residue extension degree f includes any inseparable part.
- The trivial valuation is included: both value groups are zero, ε=e=1, and f is the field degree. The localization convention remains meaningful.
- No local-uniformization or resolution-of-singularities problem is declared solved by this result. Those are motivations, not equivalent tasks.
