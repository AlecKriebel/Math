# Author turn 3: all-order relative intersection data and the remaining realization gap

**Outcome:** an all-order integral, based cochain construction with proved uniqueness and exact Milnor extraction; a precise obstruction to discarding its lower holonomy. This remains a scoped partial result for Polyak's requested derived-link geometry, not a claimed full solution. Three substantive author turns are used.

The construction below is a concrete form of the classical nilpotent representation viewpoint. It is not claimed novel. Its purpose is to make the prospective geometric correction system exact, integral, and based, rather than to assume that a formal Massey expression is automatically a well-defined derived link.

## 1. The relative complex has no integral cohomology obstruction

Let $M$ be the exterior of an arbitrary based $n$-component string link and $P$ its bottom punctured disk. Then

$$
H_q(M,P;\mathbb Z)=0\quad\hbox{for every }q,
\qquad H^q(M,P;\mathbb Z)=0\quad\hbox{for every }q. \tag{1}
$$

Here is the elementary homology calculation behind (1). Write the ambient ball as the union of $M$ and the disjoint tubular neighborhoods of the strands. Their intersections are $n$ annuli. Mayer–Vietoris gives $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^n$, generated by the meridians, and $H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0$. The exterior is connected and has no absolute third homology. The punctured disk has the same homology and its meridians map to these generators. The long exact sequence of the pair proves the homology assertion; the universal coefficient theorem proves the cohomology assertion. In particular all subsequent filling equations can be solved over the integers, not merely over the rationals.

Fix a finite ordered triangulation of the pair $(M,P)$, with base vertex $*$ in $P$. Subdivision can be used as necessary. Fix the canonical meridian basing throughout.

## 2. Prescribed unipotent boundary transport

Fix a target index word $I=(i_1,\ldots,i_r)$ of distinct labels, excluding the distinguished component $k$. Let $U=U_{r+1}(\mathbb Z)$ be the upper unitriangular matrix group. Prescribe on the free meridians of $P$

$$
x_{i_s}\longmapsto I_{r+1}+E_{s,s+1}\quad(1\le s\le r),
\qquad x_j\longmapsto I_{r+1}\quad(j\notin I). \tag{2}
$$

The symbol $I_{r+1}$ denotes the identity matrix, unlike the index word $I$. A representation of the free group is obtained by the universal property, with no commutativity imposed on adjacent matrix units.

Choose a spanning tree in the one-skeleton of $P$. Use the representation (2) to assign a matrix $g^0_{uv}$ to each oriented boundary edge, by transporting from the base vertex to that edge along the spanning tree and back. Then

$$
g^0_{uw}=g^0_{uv}g^0_{vw}
$$

on every ordered two-simplex of $P$, and the edge products around the based meridians give (2). The opposite edge receives the inverse matrix. This constructs a fixed boundary flat cocycle; no extension over $M$ has yet been assumed.

## 3. Integral extension by diagonal distance

For every ascending edge $e$, write its prospective label as

$$
g_e=I_{r+1}+\sum_{p<q}a_{pq}(e)E_{pq},
$$

where $a_{pq}$ is an integral simplicial one-cochain on the chosen ascending orientations. On a triangle with ordered vertices $u<v<w$, the condition $g_{uw}=g_{uv}g_{vw}$ is exactly

$$
\delta a_{pq}=-\sum_{p<s<q}a_{ps}\smile a_{sq}. \tag{3}
$$

The cup product is the ordinary ordered Alexander–Whitney product:
$(a\smile b)(u,v,w)=a(u,v)b(v,w)$.

Proceed by the diagonal distance $d=q-p$. Suppose all smaller distances have been constructed and agree with the fixed boundary cochains. For the pair $(p,q)$ define

$$
b_{pq}=-\sum_{p<s<q}a_{ps}\smile a_{sq}.
$$

This is a two-cocycle. Indeed, the cup-product differential rule and the already satisfied equations (3) give

$$
\delta b_{pq}
=\sum_{p<u<v<q}a_{pu}\smile a_{uv}\smile a_{vq}
-\sum_{p<u<v<q}a_{pu}\smile a_{uv}\smile a_{vq}=0.
$$

Associativity of the ordered cup product is used here, not commutativity.

Extend the prescribed boundary cochain $a^0_{pq}$ arbitrarily to a cochain $\widetilde a_{pq}$ on $M$. Because the boundary labels are flat, $b_{pq}-\delta\widetilde a_{pq}$ is a relative two-cocycle in $C^2(M,P;\mathbb Z)$. By $H^2(M,P;\mathbb Z)=0$, there is an integral relative one-cochain $c$ with

$$
\delta c=b_{pq}-\delta\widetilde a_{pq}.
$$

Set $a_{pq}=\widetilde a_{pq}+c$. This solves (3) and preserves the boundary normalization. The same argument starts the first superdiagonal, where the sum defining $b$ is empty. Induction constructs all the entries.

Thus (2) extends to a flat $U$-valued edge cocycle on $M$. This is also a finite procedure: at each diagonal distance one solves a finite integral linear system for the coboundary map of the pair. Equation (1) proves solvability. No polynomial-time complexity bound is asserted.

**Orientation caution.** The opposite edge is labeled by $g_e^{-1}$. Its higher entries are not generally the negatives of the entries of $g_e$. The one-cochain notation is used on ascending edges to formulate (3); it is not a license to treat all matrix entries as independent additive intersection counts under path reversal.

## 4. Independence of filling choices, with the basing fixed

Two flat cocycles extending the same prescribed boundary cocycle are related by vertex gauge transformations that equal the identity on $P$.

To prove this, suppose their entries of distances below $d$ have already been matched by such a gauge. At distance $d$, equations (3) show that the entrywise difference is a closed relative one-cochain. Since $H^1(M,P;\mathbb Z)=0$, it equals $\delta f_{pq}$ for an integral zero-cochain vanishing on $P$. The vertex matrices

$$
 u_v=I_{r+1}+\sum_{q-p=d}f_{pq}(v)E_{pq}
$$

act by $g_{vw}\mapsto u_v^{-1}g_{vw}u_w$. At distance $d$ this changes an entry by $f_{pq}(w)-f_{pq}(v)$, while leaving every smaller distance unchanged. Choosing the sign of $f$ to match the difference aligns the distance-$d$ entries. Induction finishes the argument.

For a based loop in $M$, a vertex gauge conjugates its holonomy by the matrix at its base vertex. That matrix is the identity here, so the holonomy itself is unchanged. This proves independence of all integral filling choices. A different triangulation can be compared on a common subdivision. Two boundary spanning-tree realizations of the same based representation are related by a boundary vertex gauge fixing the base vertex; extend those vertex values arbitrarily to the other vertices, align the boundary cocycles, and then use the relative uniqueness just proved. Thus both changes preserve based holonomy. The canonical nilpotent identification below gives the same conclusion.

## 5. Exact extraction of every ordered nonrepeating invariant

Let $\lambda_k$ be the canonical zero-framed longitude of the distinguished strand, closed through the fixed boundary basing. Compute its edge holonomy $G(\lambda_k)\in U$ from the cocycle constructed above.

The standard string-link nilpotent isomorphism $F/F_{r+1}\cong\pi_1(M)/\pi_1(M)_{r+1}$ identifies this representation with the unique representation induced by (2). The group $U_{r+1}$ has nilpotency class at most $r$, so it factors through this quotient. This is the classical cut-open/Stallings input already identified in Turn 2; the integral extension and uniqueness above also give a direct construction of the required representation.

In a Magnus expansion substitute $X_{i_s}=E_{s,s+1}$ and all other variables zero. A product of these matrix units has a nonzero $(1,r+1)$ entry exactly when it is the ordered product

$$
E_{12}E_{23}\cdots E_{r,r+1}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\quad G(\lambda_k)_{1,r+1}=\mu_{i_1\cdots i_r,k}(S).\quad} \tag{4}
$$

Every entry $G(\lambda_k)_{p,q}$ similarly extracts the coefficient of the contiguous subword $(i_p,\ldots,i_{q-1})$. Formula (4) is integral, is valid for arbitrary string links, and has no vanishing-lower-invariant assumption. It is an all-order topological/cochain presentation of the canonical nilpotent transport. It is **not yet** identified with the source's iterated-derived-link expression.

## 6. What this says about an intersection-surface construction

Equation (3) supplies an exact all-order correction system. In a dual-cell interpretation, its one-cochains correspond to weighted oriented two-dimensional dual cells. The coboundary equation prescribes a one-dimensional boundary expression assembled from all consecutive splits of the index interval. For instance,

$$
\delta a_{13}=-a_{12}\smile a_{23},\qquad
\delta a_{14}=-a_{12}\smile a_{24}-a_{13}\smile a_{34}.
$$

These are rigorously defined **cellular** boundary/cup identities. Turning them into transverse embedded surfaces, closed derived curves, and the specific iterated linking required by Polyak entails an additional realization and comparison theorem. It is not proved merely by writing Poincaré-dual symbols for the cochains. In particular, multiplicities, relative boundary pieces, ordered push-offs, and all split terms must be controlled.

Even after choosing cochains, the top entry is not just their unweighted sum on the longitude. For a loop with edge labels $g_1,\ldots,g_m$ in $U_3$, it is

$$
(g_1\cdots g_m)_{13}
=\sum_s(g_s)_{13}
+\sum_{s<t}(g_s)_{12}(g_t)_{23}. \tag{5}
$$

The second term is essential. A bare intersection with the dual of $a_{13}$ would retain only the first term. At every higher order the same product contains further ordered lower-entry terms. Thus the filling system alone cannot be renamed an ordinary linking number without proving that these boundary/transport corrections have the claimed geometric realization.

## 7. A precise gauge obstruction along the longitude

Let $Z\subset U_{r+1}$ be the central subgroup consisting of matrices $I_{r+1}+zE_{1,r+1}$. Pull the flat cocycle back to the abstract based circle tracing $\lambda_k$. There is a vertex gauge, identity at the base point, for which **every edge label lies in $Z$** if and only if its holonomy lies in $Z$.

Necessity follows by multiplying the central edge labels; based gauge does not change the product. For sufficiency, let $P_j=g_1\cdots g_j$. On the successive vertices before the last edge choose $u_j=P_j^{-1}$, and take the identified initial/final vertex gauge to be the identity. All but the last edge then have label identity, and the last has the original holonomy. It is central exactly under the stated hypothesis.

By (4), the criterion is the vanishing of all **proper contiguous-subword** coefficients for this particular index flag. Vanishing of all lower Milnor invariants is a stronger sufficient condition. For arbitrary nonzero lower coefficients the central reduction can fail; the elementary word $x_{i_1}x_{i_2}$ already exhibits this in $U_3$.

The sufficiency construction is a gauge on the pulled-back abstract circle. It is not asserted to extend to a global gauge on the exterior with the whole boundary normalization fixed.

This is an obstruction to that specific attempt to discard lower transport and leave a single additive central quantity. It does not rule out a different based, relative or corrected derived-link interpretation. The needed correction system is now explicit rather than hidden in an unsupported ordinary-Massey-product claim.

## 8. Exact remaining target

We have a complete all-order integral relative algorithm and a proved comparison with the desired Magnus coefficients. What is missing is the genuine geometric theorem converting these cellular/holonomy data into Polyak's recursively constructed $L_{12\cdots n-1}$ and its appropriately defined linking with $L_n$, with all choices and boundary corrections handled.

The next route must address that realization/comparison. Merely declaring the holonomy entry itself to be the requested linking number, or encoding its already computed integer with meridian circles, would evade the original geometric demand. The original problem remains active after three turns, with no final unresolved disposition or result PR.
