# Exact target: Polyak's Problem 2.14

Numeric ID **10400036**, code **AMR-103-0036**, queue rank 284.

The original is M. Polyak's **Problem 2.14**, in T. Ohtsuki (ed.), *Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), printed p. 409, PDF page 37. The publisher PDF currently served at [the source URL](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf) has front matter dated 1 June 2004, a July 2004 PDF production date, and contains the same numbered question. The exact source page was rendered and visually inspected.

The question asks for a **topological presentation of string-link Milnor invariants without assuming that lower invariants vanish**. Its two explicit requirements are:

1. make the iterated-intersection linking expression $\operatorname{lk}(L_{12\cdots n-1},L_n)$ well-defined in an appropriate sense;
2. identify it with the ordered invariant $\mu_{12\cdots n-1,n}(L)$.

The preceding paragraph describes the classical vanishing-lower-invariant construction: start with Seifert surfaces of components, intersect them to form a derived link, choose a Seifert surface for that link, and continue. Reference [89] is Cochran's 1990 *Derivatives of links: Milnor's concordance invariants and Massey's products*. Reference [329] is Polyak's 1999 preprint on skein/Gauss-diagram formulas, followed by the published 2005 skein paper.

## Scope corrections

- The supplied record includes the following Section 2.7 on virtual knots. That material is outside Problem 2.14.
- The source uses a barred mu symbol even in the question about string links. The preceding discussion on p. 408 explicitly says that the string-link invariants are well-defined, unlike the indeterminate invariants of closed links. We write unbarred $\mu$ for these integer-valued, boundary-based string-link invariants.
- The target is ordered, and the displayed multi-index has no repeated indices. A result only for closed links modulo lower linking numbers is not an integer answer for string links.
- The words “in an appropriate sense” leave room for based, relative, or corrected intersection data. A failure of the unmodified closed-surface recipe is therefore not a counterexample to the original request.
- General algebraic, Gauss-diagram, or configuration-space formulas are relevant established inputs. Merely restating one, or defining an artificial curve using the already computed value of $\mu$, does not by itself establish the particular iterated-Seifert linking interpretation requested here.

The research target remains the full original two-part geometric interpretation. Restricted-family formulas and necessary correction identities must be labeled as partial results.
