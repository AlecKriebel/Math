# Author turn 2: canonical lifts and the degree-two transport correction

**Outcome:** scoped partial results for arbitrary string links, with a precise obstruction to a closed-link shortcut. The original all-order iterated-derived-link construction is still unresolved. These are credited consequences of classical Magnus/Wirtinger theory, not novelty claims.

## 1. Keep the cut-open exterior

Let $S$ be an ordered, oriented $n$-component string link in a cylinder, with its fixed boundary basing. Let $M$ be its exterior. The inclusion of the bottom punctured disk induces the standard isomorphisms

$$
F/F_q\cong\pi_1(M)/\pi_1(M)_q,
$$

where $F$ is free on the based meridians $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and subscripts denote the lower central series. This is the usual homology-cobordism/Stallings description of string links. The corresponding cut-open argument is recalled explicitly in Davis–Nagel–Orson–Powell (2020), proof of Theorem 6.1, pp. 2534–2535. In particular the canonical longitude of each strand has a unique class in $F/F_3$.

Closing the string link adds peripheral relations. For the closed-link exterior group $G$, the standard presentation gives

$$
G/G_3\cong F/\langle F_3,[x_a,\ell_a]\ (1\le a\le n)\rangle,
\tag{1}
$$

where $\ell_a$ is a longitude word. Thus equality in the closed group does not in general identify the required lift in $F/F_3$.

Write $b_{ab}=\operatorname{lk}(\widehat S_a,\widehat S_b)$ for $a\ne b$ and set $b_{aa}=0$. In $F/F_3$, the commutator subgroup is central and free abelian on $[x_a,x_b]$, $a<b$. The degree-two central relation associated to strand $a$ is

$$
R_a=\sum_b b_{ab}[x_a,x_b]. \tag{2}
$$

Here additive notation is used for the central subgroup. This follows because the commutator with $x_a$ depends only on the abelianization of $\ell_a$ at this order. Therefore the kernel of $F/F_3\to G/G_3$ is the central lattice generated by the vectors $R_a$.

For fixed distinct $i,j$, the $X_iX_j$ coefficient of $R_i$ is $b_{ij}$, that of $R_j$ is $-b_{ij}$, and all other $R_a$ have coefficient zero. Multiplying a representative by a central kernel element changes this coefficient by an arbitrary multiple of $b_{ij}$, and only by such a multiple. Consequently that coefficient of a **closed-group representative** is generally defined only modulo $|b_{ij}|$, even with the meridian basing fixed. If $b_{ij}=0$, this particular representative ambiguity disappears; additional changes of basing are a separate issue.

This is not indeterminacy of the string-link invariant. The string link already selects its unique cut-open lift before the quotient (1).

## 2. An explicit warning against a source transfer

Take a positive Hopf link on components 1 and 2, split from an unknot on component 3. Its group is $(\mathbb Z^2)*\mathbb Z$, with $[x_1,x_2]=1$ and with the third preferred longitude equal to 1. Both words

$$
1,\qquad [x_1,x_2]=x_1x_2x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}
$$

therefore represent that longitude in the closed group, and also modulo $G_3$. But their degree-two Magnus coefficients are respectively 0 and 1 at $X_1X_2$, since

$$
E([x_1,x_2])=1+X_1X_2-X_2X_1+O(3).
$$

The Hopf pair has an ordinary one-clasp C-complex and the split third component has a disjoint disk, so a basing subordinate to this surface system exists. Subordinating a basing does not remove the above group relation.

This example is relevant to the wording of Proposition 6.11 in the retrieved published-format Davis–Nagel–Orson–Powell PDF, p. 2542, which speaks of “any word” sent to a closed-link longitude. We cannot use that literal wording to infer a lift-independent integer formula: the elementary control above prevents that inference. The proof on that page constructs a **particular** product of conjugated meridians, whose coefficients can be calculated. Transferring it to the canonical string-link longitude requires verifying the lift in the cut-open exterior, not just equality after closure.

This is a narrowly specified source-transfer caveat. It is not a claim that the paper's quotient-valued main theorem is false, nor a claim to have supplied a replacement theorem for all of its assertions. No external contact or accusation is made. The source's closed-link indeterminacy framework and its useful geometric calculations are retained with their actual categories.

## 3. An unconditional degree-two correction formula

The following calculation applies directly in the cut-open exterior and is therefore available for **arbitrary** string links, not just the point-pushing family of Turn 1.

Fix three distinct labels $i,j,k$. Use a regular diagram with the fixed boundary basing and its standard Wirtinger longitude construction. After discarding the framing factor supported on the meridian of strand $k$, the $k$-th longitude has an ordered product representation

$$
\lambda_k=\prod_{r=1}^N h_r x_{a_r}^{\epsilon_r}h_r^{-1},
\qquad \epsilon_r\in\{+1,-1\}, \tag{3}
$$

at least modulo the third lower-central term, which is all that is needed. The factors are the signed meridians encountered along the preferred parallel of strand $k$. Each $h_r$ is the based transport loop comparing the encountered meridian's whisker with the standard whisker of its strand. Such a loop exists because meridians on the same strand are conjugate. Equivalently one may keep the self/framing factors in (3): every contribution involving $X_k$ disappears from the coefficient considered here. The order and signs are those of the actual Wirtinger longitude word, rather than an independently chosen commutator convention.

For a based loop $h$ in $M$, put

$$
a_s(h)=\text{its meridian-}s\text{ exponent sum}
       =\operatorname{lk}(h,\widehat S_s).
$$

The last equality regards a pushed-off representative of the loop as an integral cycle in the complement of the closed component. It also equals the algebraic intersection of $h$ with any properly embedded oriented surface representing the cohomology class dual to meridian $s$. Such surfaces exist by representing the integral cohomology class by a map to the circle and taking a regular level. These values are ordinary homology/intersection data and do not use a higher Milnor invariant.

Define

$$
O_{ij}=\sum_{r<s}\epsilon_r\epsilon_s
             \mathbf1_{a_r=i}\mathbf1_{a_s=j}
$$

and

$$
C_{ij}=\sum_r\epsilon_r\left(
\mathbf1_{a_r=j}\,a_i(h_r)
-\mathbf1_{a_r=i}\,a_j(h_r)\right).
$$

Then the exact integer formula is

$$
\boxed{\mu_{ij,k}(S)=O_{ij}+C_{ij}.} \tag{4}
$$

There is no vanishing-lower-invariant assumption in (4). It uses ordered local incidence and **based transport** linking numbers, rather than an arbitrary word for the longitude in a closed-link quotient.

### Proof of the formula and its choice independence

Write the linear Magnus part of $h$ as $A=\sum_s a_s(h)X_s$. For $i\ne j$, the mixed degree-two coefficient of a conjugated signed meridian is

$$
[X_iX_j]\,E(hx_a^\epsilon h^{-1})
=\epsilon\bigl(a_i(h)\mathbf1_{a=j}
-\mathbf1_{a=i}a_j(h)\bigr). \tag{5}
$$

Indeed, to degree two the conjugation contributes $\epsilon(AX_a-X_aA)$. The pure $X_a^2$ term that can occur when $\epsilon=-1$ is irrelevant for distinct $i,j$. The linear part of the same factor is $\epsilon X_a$.

For an ordered product, its degree-two mixed coefficient is the sum of the individual degree-two coefficients plus the products of the relevant linear coefficients from earlier and later factors. Substituting (5) gives exactly (4). This proves the formula in $F/F_3$, whose identification with the cut-open group was fixed in §1.

Any alternative diagram, whisker choices or valid decomposition (3) that still represents the same canonical cut-open longitude yields the same result, because the result is its uniquely defined Magnus coefficient. Likewise the choice of dual surface in the definition of $a_s(h)$ does not matter: the loop is closed and the represented integral cohomology class is fixed. This reasoning does **not** declare arbitrary closure-equivalent words to be admissible decompositions.

## 4. What the formula adds to the ordered-family model

In the point-pushing example with word $x_ix_j$, all chosen transport loops can be trivial, and (4) is the ordered term 1. In the conjugated-meridian word $x_jx_ix_j^{-1}$, one may use a single factor with $a=i$, $\epsilon=1$ and $h=x_j$. Then $O_{ij}=0$ and $C_{ij}=-1$, so $\mu_{ij,k}=-1$ while $\mu_{ji,k}=1$. This shows why simply retaining ordered crossings of strand $k$ with an untransported set of meridians misses genuine contributions on general diagrams.

Formula (4) is a small explicit part of established Magnus/Wirtinger and Polyak skein/Gauss-diagram theory. The role of the surface realization here is to identify the transport exponents with actual signed intersections of loops and dual surfaces. It is not advertised as a newly discovered triple invariant.

## 5. Remaining geometric problem

This turn supplies an arbitrary-string-link, degree-two integer correction algorithm and a precise central-lattice description of what a closed-link shortcut loses. It does not yet turn (4) into a canonical ordinary linking number of a recursively constructed derived link, and it does not supply all higher orders.

At higher orders the transport loops' higher Magnus data and the topology of the spanning surfaces must themselves be resolved geometrically. A compact C-complex clasp-word expression cannot simply be assumed to select the canonical cut-open lifts. The next task is to construct and control that additional data, while preserving the source's intended iterated-intersection meaning. The full original target remains active after two of five substantive author turns.
