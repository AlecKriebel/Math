# Independent audit of the fast-bump prior-resolution candidate

Date: 8 October 2026. Record: 30003846. Credit: Gili Golan, arXiv:2607.10961v1, submitted 12 July 2026. No new proof-search attempt or novelty claim is credited by this audit.

## Decision

**The connected-crossing-graph classification passes this mathematical review, subject to the expressly imported representation/core results. Unconditional acceptance of the exact original Questions 71–72 is withheld because the exclusion-to-irreducibility bridge has not been independently justified.**

This is an audit-completeness limitation, not a counterexample or a finding that Golan's theorem is false. The original candidate remains preserved and unmodified. Its reconstruction has SHA-256 `2e1d34c9d252f3c88f0bfa1d9487ae37522e41aca9c1506b1aa0b9feabf3880d`.

The verified connected statement is: for each fixed n >= 2, a group generated by a geometrically fast set of n one-bump interval homeomorphisms with connected crossing graph is isomorphic to the interval PL group F_n and is of type F_infinity. Signs can be normalized by inversion using Belk–Stott's feet convention. This is a credited verification of an existing preprint, not a new result or certification of journal acceptance.

## 1. Exact original scope and the remaining obligation

The [original report, printed page 1610](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46748?nt=1), defines its class by excluding nontrivial direct sums and wreath products. Fact 70 says membership can be recognized from generating graphs. It does not specify the crossing-graph criterion or prove the needed decomposition statement in that passage.

[Golan's introduction, page 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10961v1), explicitly asserts that a disconnected crossing graph gives a nontrivial direct-product or permutational-wreath decomposition involving smaller fast sets, and attributes this to the report. Thus the candidate's import I0 has a verified location, but the introduction is part of the very manuscript being audited and supplies no proof of that assertion. A reference to graph-detectability alone does not establish its exact meaning or identify the wreath-product action.

Belk–Stott, Section 4, independently defines irreducibility by connectedness and gives the disjoint-support direct-product example; it does not provide the missing general disconnected-case argument there. A bounded additional check of Bleak–Brin–Moore, [arXiv:1711.10998v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10998v2), found an applicable-form wreath-product criterion in Lemma 3.2, but its consistency and faithfulness hypotheses still need to be established for all diagrams in I0. Its Proposition 7.1 has additional hypotheses and is not an automatic replacement. No new decomposition proof is supplied by this audit.

To close this gate, provide either a proof of the required implication for arbitrary finite fast one-bump sets or an independently applicable primary theorem with hypotheses and the product convention matched explicitly. Only the direction from the original excluded-decomposition class to connectedness is needed. Neither an abstract indecomposability classification of F_n nor the converse is required.

Question 109 has different literal scope, including all finitely presented fast one-bump subgroups. This audit does not accept that whole statement or any larger record that bundles it with an embedding question. In particular, disconnected examples cannot simply be called one F_n.

## 2. Gap swap and combinatorial induction

The metric gap-swap argument is valid with the half-open endpoint conventions. The first moved foot has infimum q in the old fundamental interval, and the new marker b^(-1)(q) lies in the support. Moved feet are contained in the interval from that new marker to the old marker; unmoved gap feet occur between the old marker and q. The two new feet occupy the remaining left and right portions. Closedness ensures no owner of a moved foot also owns an unmoved gap foot. Its other foot, if outside the support, is fixed by the conjugator. This proves disjointness without assuming all support intervals move together. Keeping the conjugating bump among the generators proves equality of subgroups.

The nested-component argument was checked as an all-n argument. After deletion of the rightmost bump, every component has a crossing neighbor of it. Crossers in different components are nested because their spans meet and they cannot cross. Nesting propagates along crossing paths: a span containing a neighbor cannot also be inside its crossing successor without contradicting that crossing. This yields the component order and the innermost dichotomy. The initial-block argument uses a foot strictly inside the innermost reach to contradict both possible locations of an outside component's endpoints.

The merge swaps only feet owned by different old components, so no old crossing is lost. The order reversal of the two selected right feet adds an edge from the innermost component to each other component. A recursive swap lifts because only the removed bump's left foot can be inserted in a smaller gap. Its owner cannot violate closedness. The last right foot stays last; either all previous neighbors remain or the bump at which the swap occurs remains a crossing neighbor. No finite enumeration is used in place of this induction.

## 3. Directed-complex and GS checks

The forced vertex identification for each bump is exactly between the vertex following its left foot and the vertex preceding its right foot. Ordering these identifications by right-foot index proves each merges previously distinct classes. The source and sink remain distinct. The inner-strong-connectivity proof was checked, including nonemptiness of both sides of the proposed separation: the owners of the first and last feet witness it.

GS edge addition and its inverse require nonempty defining paths. Rewriting requires distinct witness and target cells; ordinary semigroup Tietze equivalence is insufficient. Guba–Sapir Theorem 4.1 and its proof were inspected at this precise boundary. The base is substituted before each deletion and is related to the old base by a diagram; it is not silently retained after its edge disappears.

The two-bump zipper uses its fresh cell to rewrite two different old cells. Both removed edges occur nowhere else when deleted. All dummy words may be empty without making the defining zipper path empty.

In the induction, the discarded terminal dummy word D may be empty. In that case the old terminal vertex is identified with the vertex following the reclassified left foot; treating the smaller complex as an injective subcomplex would be wrong. The lifting statement permits this quotient map. Before a lifted deletion, all occurrences in extra cells are rewritten with the defining cell as a distinct witness. Fresh labels avoid collision with additional edges.

The image of the remaining gap is considered first in the smaller core, where source and sink restrictions force it onto the inner cycle. This order matters when the larger complex has identified the old sink with an inner vertex. Its additional full turns can then be absorbed using the old right-boundary cell. The new edge's defining path always contains two old feet, even when the auxiliary cycle pieces are empty. The two subsequent deletions remove original feet, not original dummies or original outer boundaries.

At the end, source/sink and directed reachability have survived the GS moves. Equality of inner edge and vertex counts is used only after inner strong connectivity is established. Every substituted cycle uses each final inner edge once. Thus the displayed final rules really are the model-core rules, with the transported base still a source-to-sink path.

## 4. Model core, imports and finiteness

Attachment sliding uses a fresh boundary edge and a different old boundary cell; after substitution the discarded boundary occurs only in its defining cell. A source-to-sink path contains an active boundary edge, which inserts full cycle traversals; deterministic cyclic order identifies paths differing by such traversals. The base edge of the standard core is likewise deleted only after transport.

The final canonical realization has no isolated bump because the crossing graph is connected with at least two vertices. Its partition therefore consists only of feet, so final k=0. The stronger induction with dummies is a structural reduction statement. It does not require proving that a general dummy-containing core has diagram group F_n. Accordingly Golan's separate dummy-contraction Lemma 4.9 is outside this proof's dependency chain and is not certified here.

Explicit imports retained: the fast-diagram marked-isomorphism theorem and no-isolated canonical realization of Bleak–Brin–Kassabov–Moore–Zaremsky; Belk–Stott Theorem 3.7 with its canonical base; diagram reduction and base conjugacy; and the standard tree-pair/core interpretation. Their relevant statements and constructions were inspected, rather than every proof in all supporting papers. The presentation in Golan–Eytan Sapir Remark 5.2 matches the n-1 cyclic edges and two boundary edges, with the extra base edge removed as above.

Brown's Proposition 4.4, printed page 56, identifies F_(n,1) with the required interval PL group. Theorem 4.17, pages 65–67, supplies both finite presentation and FP_infinity; those scan pages were visually inspected. Finite presentation is essential to the geometric conclusion. For completeness: start with a finite presentation complex. If its universal cover is (m-1)-connected and the complex has finite m-skeleton, FP_(m+1), comparison of finite partial resolutions, and Schanuel's lemma give a finitely generated m-th cellular kernel. Hurewicz identifies it with pi_m for m >= 2. Attaching finitely many (m+1)-cells kills that module. Inducting yields an aspherical complex with finite skeleton in every degree, proving F_infinity. No implication from FP_infinity alone is used.

## 5. Code finding, correction and finite controls

The original programs contain 41 assertions and write JSON beside themselves. Under Python -O/-OO the assertions disappear. A deliberately nonclosed swap is rejected normally but is accepted under both optimized modes, even reversing one bump's feet. All original read-only executions fail on the final sidecar write. Those are reproducibility weaknesses, not mathematical counterexamples.

The separately identified derivatives replace assertions by explicit conditional raises and remove only the two sidecar writes. They retain arithmetic, enumeration, outputs and nondeterministic elapsed-time reporting. `HARDENING.patch` records every edit. `verify_hardening.py` proves AST equivalence after exactly those transformations against the original hashes. The original packet was not changed.

All hardened scripts pass normally, with -O, and with -OO under UID 1000 in 0555 directories with 0444 files. Create and append attempts are denied, and the input files are unchanged. Every published JSON field matches the original results; only elapsed time is excluded from equality. Full stdout/stderr is retained in execution records, with fixture paths normalized.

Independent controls separately enumerate matchings, construct crossing graphs, replay closed swaps, build vertex equivalence classes, and replay GS certificates without using the producer's guard methods. They independently check distinct cells, occurrences, path endpoints, legal deletions, dummy/boundary survival, exact base substitution, final strong connectivity and model rules. The certificate-producing peel/reduce routines remain shared and this dependency is explicit.

Coverage: 146,598 diagrams, including 41,342 irreducible ones; 48,150 independently replayed swaps; 7,405 independently replayed GS instances containing 608,241 elementary moves, with maximum length 135. Eight deliberately invalid controls are rejected in every independent execution. The independent suite also passes normal/-O/-OO read-only UID-1000 execution with equal complete outputs apart from elapsed time.

These are finite falsification and certificate checks. They are not an all-n proof, a test of arbitrary real homeomorphisms, a machine verification of imported theorems, or proof-assistant formalization. The source-scope gate in Section 1 remains open despite all successful computations.
