# Fast one-bump groups: the excluded-decomposition scope bridge

Date: 8 October 2026. Record: 30003846.

## Decision

**The mathematical implication I0 passes for restricted permutational wreath products, with their indexing action specified below.** Every disconnected finite fast one-bump diagram has a nontrivial direct-product decomposition or the explicit restricted permutational-wreath decomposition proved here. Both constituent groups are generated by proper subsets of the original bumps.

This supplies the missing mathematical argument for applying the already reviewed connected-crossing-graph classification to the original Questions 71–72 under the restricted permutational convention explicitly defined in Bleak–Brin–Moore Section 3.5 and in this supplement. The original report says only “wreath product”; it does not separately define that term in the question passage. This report therefore makes the convention visible rather than treating regular, unrestricted, faithful-action-only, and general permutational wreath products as interchangeable. No regular-only or faithful-action-only reinterpretation of the original exclusions is certified. Acceptance of the exact original target is therefore conditional on the stated restricted permutational convention; the later INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md has now accepted this authored supplement without a proof correction.

This is an elementary reconstruction of the existing decomposition observation from the established fast-set ping-pong argument. It is not a new classification or a novelty claim. It neither modifies the earlier candidate and audit nor re-audits their connected classification. The prior attribution remains Gili Golan, arXiv:2607.10961v1, a publicly available preprint; journal acceptance is not established. Question 109 is outside this report.

## 1. Primary-source boundary

1. The [Oberwolfach report, printed page 1610, Section 6](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46748?nt=1), defines the original class using n fast one-bump generators and exclusion of nontrivial direct sums and wreath products. Fact 70 supplies graph detectability, finite presentation, and membership of F_n. Questions 71 and 72 ask geometric finiteness and uniqueness of the isomorphism type for each fixed n. The passage does not define a wreath indexing action and does not prove the crossing-graph criterion.
2. [Golan, introduction, page 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10961v1), identifies disconnected crossing graphs with nontrivial direct-product or permutational-wreath decompositions into smaller fast groups. We verify that observation below instead of using the assertion as its own proof.
3. [Bleak–Brin–Kassabov–Moore–Zaremsky, arXiv:1701.08321v1, Section 5, Lemmas 5.1–5.2 and 5.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08321v1), give local reduction, fast ping-pong, and the disjointness of distinct trivial-history point orbits. These are the elementary dynamical ingredients reconstructed in Section 4.
4. [Bleak–Brin–Moore, arXiv:1711.10998v2, Section 3.5, pages 15–16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10998v2), define the finite-support permutational wreath product as a semidirect product for a group acting on an indexing set. The algebraic definition itself does not impose faithfulness on that action. Their Lemma 3.2 adds consistency and faithfulness to obtain a faithful permutation realization. Section 7, Proposition 7.1, has additional hypotheses on their special generating sets; it is not invoked here. Its Lemma 7.2 uses the same trivial-history argument for consistency.
5. [Belk–Stott, arXiv:2303.16868v1, Section 2.1, page 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16868v1), specify negative-bump feet by interchanging the source and destination of the inverse and normalize bump signs by inversion. Section 4 defines irreducibility by connectedness of the crossing graph and discusses the direct-product example.

The original report and the exact wreath definition were also visually checked in their PDFs. Public source hashes and inspection locations accompany this report; source PDFs and source-text extracts do not.

## 2. Exact decomposition statement and convention

Let B be a finite geometrically fast set of n nonidentity, orientation-preserving one-bump homeomorphisms of an interval, with n at least 2. Let G be the generated group. Invert every negative bump, preserving the group, supports, feet as unlabelled intervals, fastness, and crossing graph. A positive bump b has support I_b = (a_b,c_b), marker m_b, and feet (a_b,m_b) and [b(m_b),c_b).

For an arbitrary right action of a group H on a set Omega, write

    K wr_Omega H = (direct sum over Omega of K) semidirect H,

where H acts solely by permuting the factors. The direct sum consists of finitely supported tuples. This is the algebraic restricted permutational convention of Bleak–Brin–Moore, Section 3.5. The action on Omega may have a kernel; H itself, not its permutation image, remains the top factor. This point is necessary in the construction below.

**Decomposition theorem.** If the crossing graph of B is disconnected, there is a nonempty proper subset C of B, with K = <C> and H = <B minus C>, such that either:

- G = K times H as an internal direct product; or
- G is isomorphic to K wr_Omega H, for an explicitly defined transitive infinite H-set Omega of intervals.

K and H are nontrivial groups generated by fewer than n fast one-bump generators. In the second case the indexing action is nontrivial. There is no claim that it is regular or faithful.

## 3. Choosing a component with a foot-free support interval

The union J_C of the support intervals of a crossing component C is an open interval. Indeed, each crossing pair has overlapping supports, and a crossing path joins every member to every other member. This includes singleton components.

We first record containment propagation. Let b be outside a crossing component C and suppose some I_c is contained in I_b. Since b crosses no member of C, every member of C has support contained in I_b. To see the inductive step, suppose c and d cross and I_c is contained in I_b. I_d cannot be disjoint from I_b, since it meets I_c. It cannot contain I_b, since then it would contain I_c, contrary to c and d crossing. It cannot cross I_b, since b and d lie in different crossing components. Thus I_d is contained in I_b. Propagate along a crossing path.

Choose C so that J_C is minimal under proper inclusion among the finitely many component-support intervals, and put J = J_C. Every b outside C either contains J in I_b or is disjoint from J. Otherwise some I_b meets some I_c; the two cannot cross, so one contains the other. If I_b contains I_c, containment propagation gives J contained in I_b. If I_b is contained in I_c, apply propagation to the component D containing b with the outside bump c. This gives J_D contained in I_c, properly inside J_C, contrary to minimality.

Nested fast bumps cannot share a support endpoint: their feet at that endpoint would overlap. Thus the preceding nested inclusions have strictly separated support endpoints. If b contains J, each support I_c is wholly in the gap [m_b,b(m_b)): its two feet are inside I_b but disjoint from both feet of b, and the whole interval between them is still in that gap. Taking the union over c gives the same assertion for J. If b is disjoint from J, so are its feet.

Consequently J is disjoint from every foot of the fast set B minus C. No no-isolated-bump assumption was used. In particular a minimal component is allowed to be a single isolated bump.

## 4. Consistency of the H-action on translates of J

For a word in signed bumps and a point x, remove letters that fix the current point, and cancel adjacent inverse letters, until the word is freely reduced and every retained letter moves the current point. These operations preserve the word's value at x. Termination follows because each operation shortens the word.

If x is outside all feet and such a locally reduced word is nonempty, its first letter sends x into its destination foot. Inductively, suppose the preceding letter was a and the next is b. Since b is not a inverse, the destination foot of a is disjoint from the source foot of b. The next letter moves the current point, which is therefore in b's support but outside its source foot, and sends it into b's destination foot. Thus the word ends in a foot. This is the fast ping-pong argument of the cited Lemma 5.2, with inverse feet treated as in Section 2.

It follows that if x and xh both lie in J for h in H, then xh = x: a nonempty locally reduced word would end in a foot, whereas J contains none.

Suppose Jh meets J. Some x in J has xh in J, so xh = x. Let F be the fixed-point set of h relative to J. F is nonempty and relatively closed by continuity. It is also relatively open: near any fixed point x in J, continuity gives a neighborhood U contained in J with Uh contained in J, and the preceding paragraph fixes every point of U. Since J is connected, F = J. We have proved

    Jh meets J  implies  h fixes J pointwise, hence Jh = J.

Applying this to hk inverse shows that any two intervals Jh and Jk are equal or disjoint. Moreover the setwise stabilizer of each translated interval fixes it pointwise. This conclusion holds for all finite fast sets here, without the special-generating-set hypotheses of Bleak–Brin–Moore Proposition 7.1.

## 5. Identifying the base and proving the splitting

Use right actions and conjugation K^h = h inverse K h, so the support of K^h is Jh. Let Omega = {Jh : h in H}.

For each interval Jh in Omega, the homomorphism K -> K^h given by conjugation depends only on that interval, not on the representative h. Indeed, Jh = Jk implies hk inverse fixes J pointwise, so it commutes with every homeomorphism supported in J. Thus conjugation by h and k agrees on K.

Distinct factors K^h have disjoint supports, commute, and are independent: in any finite product over distinct supporting intervals, restriction to one interval recovers its one factor. Hence their generated subgroup is exactly the restricted direct sum

    N = direct sum over Omega of K.

N is normalized by H, and conjugation by a member h of H carries the factor indexed by Jt to the factor indexed by Jth with the same K-coordinate. Therefore H acts only by permuting the coordinates. Since K and H together generate G, one has G = NH.

The intersection N intersect H is trivial; this does not require faithful action on Omega. If h lies in that intersection, its membership in N shows it preserves every interval in Omega and fixes everything outside their union. Its membership in H and the consistency proved in Section 4 show it fixes every such interval pointwise. Therefore it fixes the entire ambient interval and is the identity.

This proves the asserted abstract isomorphism

    G = N semidirect H = K wr_Omega H.

All base elements have finite support because they are finite products of conjugates. No unrestricted direct product has entered the argument, and no quotient of H has silently replaced H.

If every remaining bump is disjoint from J, then H fixes J and Omega is a singleton; the result is the nontrivial direct product K times H. Otherwise some remaining positive bump b contains J in its fundamental gap. The intervals Jb^k, for integer k, lie in successive, pairwise disjoint fundamental domains of b. Thus Omega is infinite, and its action is nontrivial. K and H are nontrivial because both of their generating subsets are nonempty and consist of genuine bumps. This finishes the theorem.

## 6. Why the indexing convention cannot be suppressed

Nonfaithfulness is possible even in a three-bump example. Put two disjoint bumps b and c inside one fundamental gap of an outer bump a, with the whole set fast. Choose K = <b>, H = <a,c>, and J = support(b). The orbit intervals are J a^k. The nonidentity element c fixes all of them, so H -> Sym(Omega) has nontrivial kernel. The decomposition above still holds because it retains all of H. Replacing H by its permutation image would discard genuine group elements. This example also has the alternative familiar decomposition (<b> times <c>) wr Z; the example is used only to show that our particular H-action need not be faithful.

Nor must a point stabilizer be trivial. With a fast crossing pair generating H, put an additional nested bump in a foot-free interval inside one member's support and outside the other's. The latter member fixes the initial interval, so this orbit action is not regular. Thus identifying Omega with H without proof would change the claimed wreath product.

The unrestricted wreath convention is definitely different: in the infinite-orbit case, the full product of nontrivial K over Omega is uncountable, while G is finitely generated and therefore countable.

## 7. Consequence for the original questions

Under the explicit restricted permutational convention of Section 2, a group in the original excluded-decomposition class cannot admit a disconnected crossing graph: the theorem would give one of its forbidden nontrivial decompositions. This is the only direction required. No converse or abstract indecomposability classification is needed.

The previously reviewed connected classification now applies at the original number n of generators and identifies the group with F_n. The already audited Brown finiteness implication then gives type F_infinity. Thus Questions 71 and 72 follow at this convention, retaining the earlier audit's explicit representation/core and finiteness imports.

The 2018 question passage itself does not resolve a reader's possible insistence on a narrower regular-only or faithful-action-only use of “wreath product.” Under either such additional restriction, the present proof is not sufficient to certify the exact excluded class; a decomposition satisfying that restriction, or authoritative clarification that it was not intended, would be the remaining obligation. That is a terminology/scope boundary, not a counterexample to Golan's connected theorem or to the explicit decomposition proved here.
