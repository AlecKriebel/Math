# Final symmetry frontier for three-coloured tournaments

Row 1754 / problem 3276 / OPG-1808. 12 October 2026 UTC.

Status: unsolved. This is the fifth and final substantive pass in the present program, with at least five confirmed passes and lifetime count unknown. Editing, audit and integration are not extra mathematical passes. No sixth pass is authorized by this edition.

The accepted structural theorem excludes every induced-minimal counterexample whose direction-preserving global-color-permutation automorphism group is vertex-transitive. Such a hypothetical minimal counterexample has a proper odd-order cyclic rotation group; at prime order that group is trivial. The all-order argument uses the failed-predecessor cycle, internal-fiber strong colors, and forced offmatching arcs. It does not rely on a computer bound.

The independently accepted finite lane searches only exact fully twisted rotational encodings. Candidate zero scopes are orders 3,9,15,21,27,45,81. The independent audit covers those seven same orders with its bounded solver; complete literal enumeration remains orders 3 and 9. These are not general tournament orders and not arbitrary periodic-cycle scopes. The general bound remains ten; periodic lengths 3,6,9 remain accepted, and the periodic-twelve feasibility run was incomplete.

A minimal induced subtournament can lose the symmetry of its parent. The new theorem therefore does not settle all symmetric tournaments, arbitrary strict three-periodic obstruction cycles, or the unrestricted conjecture. No counterexample, novelty, priority, exhaustive-literature, current-open-status, or proof-assistant certification is claimed.
