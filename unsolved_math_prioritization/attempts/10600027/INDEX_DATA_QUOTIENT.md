# The universal additive index-data obstruction for ordinary virtual passes

10 October 2026 · Supplementary partial result for 10600027

## Scope

This theorem classifies additive obstructions that depend only on the existing n-writhe data. It does **not** classify knots up to pass-equivalence and does not assert that any algebraically allowed sequence of index changes can be realized on a specified knot. The n-writhes and their crossing-change behavior are credited to Satoh–Taniguchi [1]. Originality of the present quotient calculation has not been certified.

Throughout, E is the free abelian group on symbols e_n for nonzero integers n. Put e_0=0, and w_n=e_n+e_{−n}, so w_0=0 and w_n=w_{−n}. All sums below have finite support. The n-writhe spectrum of a closed virtual knot is

j(K) = sum over n≠0 of J_n(K)e_n.

Let R be the subgroup of E generated by j(K′)−j(K), as (K,K′) ranges over **all** one-ordinary-pass pairs of closed virtual knots. This is a universal subgroup of differences across all knots; membership in R does not imply pass-equivalence of any given pair.

## 1. Definitions and established index facts

For a signed arrow c from t to h with sign ε, give its tail weight −ε and its head weight +ε. Its index I(c) is the sum of endpoint weights strictly in the oriented circle arc from t to h. Define J_n as the signed count of arrows with index n. For n≠0 these are Reidemeister invariants [1, Lemma 2.3]. The elementary move verification is: an R1 arrow has index zero; R2 arrows have equal indices and opposite signs; R3 preserves each crossing's index; outside indices stay unchanged. Virtual moves leave the Gauss diagram fixed.

Changing a crossing reverses both its arrow and its sign. Its endpoint weights consequently stay fixed, its index changes from n to −n, and all other indices stay fixed. Thus the spectrum change is −εw_n. The same assertion includes n=0 because e_0=0.

There is an identity

sum over n≠0 of n J_n(K) = 0.

For completeness, each contribution from an ordered linked arrow pair to ε(c)I(c) is the negative of the contribution from the reversed ordered pair: their signs multiply symmetrically and the endpoint in the oriented interval is opposite in kind. Unlinked pairs contribute zero. Summing cancels pairs. Set A=ker(φ), where φ(e_n)=n; hence j(K) lies in A.

## 2. Exact local pass change

**Lemma 1.** The subgroup R is generated by

r(u,v,w,z) = w_(v−u) − w_(z−u) − w_(v−w) + w_(z−w),

for arbitrary integers u,v,w,z.

**Proof.** Use a square pass disk with vertical strands V0 oriented up, V1 down, horizontal strands H0 right, H1 left, and the vertical strands over before the move. Name the crossings a=V0∩H0, b=V0∩H1, c=V1∩H0, d=V1∩H1. Choose the global planar crossing convention so their signs are respectively +,−,−,+; using the opposite convention merely negates the resulting generator. Along the oriented four strands their two-endpoint blocks are

V0: (b,a); H0: (a,c); V1: (c,d); H1: (d,b).

The endpoint weights along each of these directed blocks are (+1,−1). Let u,v,w,z be the cumulative endpoint weights immediately before the blocks V0,H0,V1,H1 respectively, with any fixed origin on the circle. Each block has total weight zero. For an arrow with sign ε, tail potential P and head potential Q, the index is Q−P+ε. Direct substitution gives

I(a)=v−u; I(b)=z−u; I(c)=v−w; I(d)=z−w.

Changing all four crossings therefore changes the spectrum by −r(u,v,w,z). This computation is independent of the cyclic order of the four blocks: cumulative weights on the circle are well-defined up to a constant because the total endpoint weight is zero. For the second displayed oriented version, put V0↑ on the right, V1↓ on the left, H0→ above H1←. The directed blocks become (b,a),(c,a),(c,d),(b,d). The vertical endpoint weights are (+1,−1), while the horizontal weights are (−1,+1). The four indices are now v−u−1, z−u−1, v−w−1, z−w−1. Substituting v′=v−1 and z′=z−1 gives precisely the same generator family. Inverse moves negate the change; rotating the disk merely relabels strands. Thus both ordinary oriented versions and all their inverses give changes in the asserted subgroup.

Conversely, fix integers u,v,w,z and arrange the four blocks cyclically in the order V0,H0,V1,H1. A common shift of all four parameters changes no index, so take u=0. Fill the four gaps after the blocks with outside endpoints whose total weights are v−u, w−v, z−w, u−z respectively. For a positive desired weight put that many +1 endpoints in the gap; for a negative weight put that many −1 endpoints. The total is zero, so pair the positive endpoints bijectively with the negative endpoints. Each pair becomes an outside arrow of sign +1 directed from its negative endpoint to its positive endpoint. This gives exactly the prescribed block potentials.

Every such finite signed Gauss diagram with its designated square tangle is realized by a virtual diagram: draw the square disk and the outside classical crossing disks, join the prescribed oriented half-edges, and mark all other transverse intersections virtual. The square remains a legitimate ordinary pass disk. Its pass change is precisely −r(u,v,w,z). Thus every displayed generator is realized by an actual one-pass pair. ∎

## 3. The quotient calculation

Let S be the free subgroup generated by w_n for n>0. Define θ:S→Z/2 by θ(w_n)=n modulo two.

**Lemma 2.** R=ker θ.

**Proof.** The integer combination

(v−u)−(z−u)−(v−w)+(z−w)

is zero, so θ kills every generator in Lemma 1, giving R⊆ker θ. Conversely put u=0, w=1, v=n, z=0 in that lemma. It gives

w_n − w_(n−1) + w_1 in R.

At n=1 this says 2w_1 lies in R. Induction for n>0 then gives w_n congruent to n w_1 modulo R. Thus S/R is generated by the class of w_1 and that class has order at most two. Since θ(w_1)=1 and θ kills R, its order is exactly two and R=ker θ. ∎

Let B denote the group of finitely supported integer sequences (a_n)_(n>0) satisfying sum n a_n=0.

**Theorem 3.** There is an explicit isomorphism

A/R ≅ B ⊕ Z/2,

sending a spectrum j to

(a_n = J_n−J_(−n) for n>0,  τ = sum over positive odd n of J_(−n) modulo 2).

**Proof.** Antisymmetric coefficients a_n satisfy the defining equation of B because φ(j)=0. Every symmetric generator w_n is killed by the first coordinate, and the second coordinate on S is θ. Lemma 2 proves that R is in the kernel.

If both coordinates vanish, then j has equal coefficients at n and −n, hence belongs to S, and θ(j)=0. Lemma 2 gives j∈R. The induced map is injective.

For any a∈B, the spectrum sum_(n>0) a_n e_n lies in A and maps to (a,0). Adding w_1 changes only the binary coordinate. The map is surjective. ∎

Consequently any homomorphism from A to any abelian group whose value on j(K) is unchanged by every ordinary pass factors uniquely through these two coordinates, provided it kills the actual one-pass difference subgroup R. Conversely any homomorphism out of B⊕Z/2 gives such an additive pass invariant.

## 4. Relation to flat projection and odd writhe

The antisymmetric differences J_n−J_(−n) are unchanged by an arbitrary single crossing change and by Reidemeister moves. They therefore depend only on the flat virtual-knot class. These are credited prior flat index invariants, rather than fresh knot invariants.

Write J_odd=J(K). Then

J_odd = sum over positive odd n of (J_n−J_(−n)) + 2 sum over positive odd n of J_(−n).

Thus the coordinate pair in Theorem 3 is equivalently the full antisymmetric index sequence together with J(K) modulo four. The weighted equation defining B implies that the first sum on the right is even, consistent with the established fact that J(K) is even.

The theorem identifies exactly what can be extracted from additive n-writhe data for ordinary passes: the flat antisymmetric data and one extra binary choice. It imposes no limit on invariants outside this framework. In particular it cannot establish that flat type plus the binary choice is a complete classification.
