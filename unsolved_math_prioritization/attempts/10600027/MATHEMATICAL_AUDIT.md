## 1. Exact move and source alignment

Kauffman's *Virtual Knot Cobordism*, Section 3.4 and Figure 32, and Chrisman's *Band-Passes and Long Virtual Knot Concordance*, Figure 1, show the ordinary pass used here: two antiparallel strands cross another antiparallel pair, and all four classical over/under assignments switch. No endpoints move, no crossings become virtual, and no outside crossing changes. The checkerboard sign pattern has two positive and two negative entries.

Both figures were visually inspected. Chrisman's Figure 9 was also inspected: the proof does not assume a particular one of its five illustrated connection patterns. The cyclic order of the four directed intervals around a closed component may be arbitrary, and the exterior may contain classical crossings. The four-block argument handles that freedom directly.

Nakamura–Nakanishi–Satoh–Wada, Figure 3.1, was separately inspected. Its virtualized pass replaces the four classical crossings by virtual crossings; it is a different relation. Its unknotting theorem is not imported into either accepted note.

## 2. Main note: proof checks

### Reidemeister invariance and parity

An R1 chord has adjacent endpoints and links nothing. In R2 the two endpoint pairs lie in consecutive two-point blocks; any old chord links both new chords or neither. The new chords have equal Gaussian parity and opposite signs, including the case in which they link each other. Thus their contributions cancel and every old parity is fixed.

An R3 move swaps three adjacent endpoint pairs. Each pair of participating chords has exactly one such swap, so every mutual linking status toggles. Each participating degree changes by an even integer, exterior incidences are unchanged, and signs are preserved. Virtual moves do not change the Gauss diagram. This verifies the stated integer-valued odd writhe without a basepoint. The handshaking argument correctly implies that its value is even.

### Four-block lemma and exact variation

An exterior chord's endpoints cannot cut a switched-endpoint block. Either of its complementary arcs therefore contains an even number of switched endpoints. Counting these endpoints modulo two counts its incidences with the switched set. The boundary-edge count of the switched set is even, while its internal edges count twice. Hence an even number of switched chords have odd parity.

A pass reverses signs and arrows while fixing all endpoints, so all Gaussian parities, including exterior parities, remain fixed. Its odd-writhe change is minus twice the signed sum of its odd switched chords. An even subset of the checkerboard signs has sum only −2, 0, or 2. Therefore the exact universal range is {−4,0,4}. This proof is valid for both orientations, both directions of the move, all oriented closed connections, and arbitrary exterior crossings.

The distance bound follows by summing absolute changes along a sequence, since Reidemeister moves change the odd writhe by zero. The modular obstruction is consequently valid for sequences of any length.

### Two-arrow same-flat witness

The exact signed code O_a(+), O_b(−), U_a(+), U_b(−) is realizable by a removable oriented RII pair. The accepted text gives an explicit local bigon: the over-arc y=sin(πx) and under-arc y=0, both directed right, have opposite-sign intersections at x=0 and x=1 and no interior crossing in the bigon. With the sign convention used for the square, the first crossing is positive. The exterior connects the end of the over-arc to the start of the under-arc and then back to the over-arc; any incidental intersections are virtual. This is one closed component with the displayed code.

Switching b reverses its arrow and sign. The endpoint word remains abab, with two positive linked chords and odd writhe 2. Flattening ignores precisely the changed crossing information, so the flat RII cancellation still applies. The original RII lift has odd writhe 0 and represents the unknot. Thus the claimed same-flat separation is sound.

### Insertion into every flat fiber

One may cut the two-arrow representative at an exterior regular point immediately before the first endpoint. The RII disk is disjoint from that cut. Insert this chosen long diagram along any regular arc of a chosen lift of an arbitrary oriented flat diagram. This is an operation on representatives and does not require a well-defined connected-sum operation on closed virtual knot types.

Both inserted endpoints of each chord lie in one new consecutive interval of the Gauss circle. No inserted chord links an old chord. The odd writhe therefore rises by exactly 2, and after flattening the same local RII cancellation, followed by virtual detours, restores the original diagram. Both binary values occur in every flat fiber.

### Six-arrow sharpness witness

The specified arrows at positions 0 through 11 are compatible with a genuine designated square disk. Its four intervals are precisely (b,a), (a,c), (c,d), and (d,b), with the vertical pair over the horizontal pair. The remaining endpoints belong to exterior arrows x and y. Direct independent calculation gives only the linked pairs ax, bx, by, and dy. Thus only a and d are odd. Their signs change from two positives to two negatives, changing the odd writhe from 2 to −2.

The exterior arrows can be realized in separate crossing disks and joined outside the protected square; all incidental intersections are virtual. The pair is therefore related by one actual ordinary pass, rather than merely by a formal arrow operation. Different odd writhes exclude distance zero. The bound is sharp, and the exclusions of larger odd-writhe moduli are correct.

## 3. Supplement: independent audit

### Index convention and crossing change

The endpoint weights −ε at the tail and +ε at the head agree with Satoh–Taniguchi, Definition 2.1. Their Lemma 2.3 supplies Reidemeister invariance for n≠0. Under a crossing change both the direction and sign reverse, leaving the weight at every geometric endpoint fixed. The changed arrow's index negates and every other index remains fixed. This gives the stated spectrum change −ε(e_n+e_−n), with zero contribution for n=0.

The weighted-sum identity follows by pairwise cancellation of ordered linked-chord contributions. It is also Proposition 4.2 of the cited source. No unproved assumption about possible spectra is required for the quotient argument.

### Local formulas, orientations, and realization

For the first square orientation, each directed two-endpoint block has weights (+1,−1). Cumulative potentials immediately before the blocks give the indices v−u, z−u, v−w, z−w. This remains true if an arrow interval wraps across the chosen origin: total endpoint weight is zero.

For the second orientation, the two horizontal blocks have weights (−1,+1). Its four indices are v−u−1, z−u−1, v−w−1, z−w−1. Replacing v and z by v−1 and z−1 gives the same relation family.

Conversely, the four requested gap totals telescope to zero. Pairing positive exterior endpoints with negative exterior endpoints creates valid positive outside arrows and exactly the requested potentials. The realization keeps the designated pass disk: place it and finitely many disjoint exterior crossing disks first; connect the prescribed directed half-edges in their connected planar complement; perturb to generic transverse arcs and mark all incidental intersections virtual. Connections follow one cyclic word, so there is exactly one closed component. This works for arbitrary integer potentials, with no bound on exterior-arrow count. Hence every generator is realized by an actual one-pass pair.

### Quotient algebra

Every generator lies in the free symmetric subgroup S and is killed by θ(w_n)=n modulo two. Choosing the stated parameters gives w_n−w_(n−1)+w_1 in R. At n=1 this gives 2w_1; the remaining recurrences give w_n≡n w_1 modulo R. Since θ(w_1) is nonzero, S/R is exactly Z/2 and R=ker θ.

For A=ker(φ), the map to the antisymmetric sequence a_n=J_n−J_−n and the negative-odd coefficient sum modulo two has kernel exactly R. Its surjectivity follows algebraically from the positive-index representative of any sequence in B and the addition of w_1. Thus A/R≅B⊕Z/2 is correct. The relation with the odd writhe modulo four is a direct identity; the weighted constraint makes the odd antisymmetric coefficient sum even.

The universal claim concerns homomorphisms on A that kill the subgroup generated by differences across all actual one-pass pairs. It does not say that an element of R is attainable from a specified knot, nor that equality of quotient coordinates implies pass-equivalence.

## 4. Attribution and remaining scope

The original odd writhe is Kauffman's established invariant; his 2004 paper, PDF page 2, was visually inspected. Flat projection as an ordinary-pass obstruction, including a Kishino example, is already present in Kauffman's 2014 cobordism paper. The source problem and the 2019 problem collection ask for a classification or corresponding invariants; they do not propose the false sufficiency statement used to formulate the new counterexample. The notes correctly avoid attributing that statement to them.

Chrisman's long-knot mod-two invariant is prior work. The notes do not assume it descends to closed knots. Satoh–Taniguchi supply the established n-writhe and flat antisymmetric index invariants. These attributions are appropriate.

A bounded additional search found no directly matching ordinary-pass theorem. One apparent modulo-four lead, Nikonov's *Local transformations and functorial maps*, concerns different surface smoothing relations in the inspected appendix, and does not certify prior or new status here. A negative search result is not an originality proof.

Full ordinary-pass classification, sufficiency of flat type plus the binary obstruction, the classical trefoil/unknot question through virtual intermediates, and the broad concordance/cobordism problem remain unresolved by these notes.
