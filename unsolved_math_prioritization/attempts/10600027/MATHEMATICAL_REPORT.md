# A closed-virtual-knot obstruction to ordinary pass-equivalence

10 October 2026 · Problem 10600027 / AMR-105-0027

## Status and exact scope

This is a proved partial result for oriented **closed** virtual knots. It does not classify ordinary pass-equivalence. The invariant used is Kauffman's existing odd writhe, not a newly defined knot invariant. The deductions below concern its reduction modulo four, same-flat fibers, and the ordinary pass distance. A bounded literature search did not establish originality of these deductions; no novelty certification is asserted.

The equivalence relation is generated by classical and virtual Reidemeister moves and the ordinary oriented pass move: two oppositely directed parallel strands pass through another pair of oppositely directed parallel strands, switching all four classical crossings. The endpoints and underlying immersed arcs stay fixed. The four crossings remain classical. In particular, the virtualized pass move that replaces the four crossings by virtual crossings is excluded.

## 1. Gauss diagrams and the established odd writhe

A Gauss diagram consists of an oriented circle, finitely many chords directed from the overpassing preimage to the underpassing preimage, and crossing signs in {+1,−1}. Chords are linked if their endpoints alternate on the circle. Write p(c) for the number of chords linked with c, reduced modulo two, and define

J(D) = sum over chords c with p(c)=1 of sign(c).

This is Kauffman's odd writhe. For completeness its elementary invariance is recalled. An R1 chord is isolated in the intersection graph. In R2 the two new chords have equal parity and opposite signs: every old chord links both of them or neither, and their mutual linking contributes equally to both parities. Thus they contribute zero to J, while old parities do not change. In R3 each of the three participating chords reverses its linking status with each of the other two, hence its degree changes by an even number; links to outside chords are unchanged. Signs are unchanged. Virtual moves and detours do not change the Gauss diagram. Consequently J is an integer-valued closed-virtual-knot invariant.

The intersection graph has an even number of vertices of odd degree by the handshaking lemma. Therefore J is always even. We may equivalently use the binary invariant

b(K) = J(K)/2 modulo 2.

No basepoint or long-knot closure is used in this definition.

## 2. The four-block lemma

**Lemma.** Suppose S is a set of chords such that their endpoints occupy disjoint consecutive blocks, every block containing an even number of endpoints and no endpoint of a chord outside S. Then an even number of chords in S have odd Gaussian parity in the full diagram.

**Proof.** Fix a chord x outside S. Either open arc between its endpoints contains whole S-blocks and no partial S-block. The number of S-endpoints in that arc is even. Modulo two this number equals the number of S-chords linked with x, because an S-chord with both endpoints in the arc contributes two and one with neither contributes zero. Thus x links an even number of S-chords. Summing the degrees of vertices of S in the full intersection graph gives twice the number of edges internal to S plus the number of edges between S and its complement. Both terms are even. The number of odd degrees in S is consequently even. ∎

## 3. The ordinary pass invariant and sharp variation bound

**Theorem 1.** If closed virtual knots K and K′ differ by one ordinary oriented pass move, then

J(K′) − J(K) belongs to {−4, 0, 4}.

In particular b(K)=b(K′), and b is an invariant of ordinary pass-equivalence.

**Proof.** The pass disk contains four strand intervals and four crossings. Each interval meets exactly two of these crossings and no others. Thus the eight endpoints of the four corresponding chords S occur in four consecutive two-endpoint blocks. Arbitrary connecting arcs outside the disk can contain virtual or classical crossings; they create no endpoint inside these four blocks. A pass reverses the direction and sign of each chord in S without moving any chord endpoint. In particular, every Gaussian parity remains unchanged, including outside S.

The four-block lemma gives an even number of odd chords in S. Only their signs change, so

J(K′) − J(K) = −2 sum over c in S with p(c)=1 of sign(c).

This is divisible by four. The four local signs comprise two +1 and two −1, because reversing the direction of either of the two parallel strands reverses its crossing signs with the other pair. The sum in this formula is therefore one of −2, 0, 2: zero selected chords give zero; two give −2,0,2; and all four give zero. This proves the asserted bound. Together with Reidemeister invariance from Section 1 it proves pass-invariance. ∎

This proof applies to every oriented closure of the local four-strand tangle, both displayed versions of the ordinary move, their inverses, and global orientation reversal. It needs neither a chosen one of Chrisman's five connection patterns nor an implicit restriction to classical connecting arcs.

**Corollary 2.** If d_pass(K,L) is the least number of ordinary pass moves in a sequence with free Reidemeister moves, then whenever this number is finite,

d_pass(K,L) ≥ |J(K)−J(L)|/4.

If J(K) and J(L) differ modulo four, no such sequence exists.

## 4. A same-flat counterexample

In a signed Gauss code, O and U mean the overpassing and underpassing occurrences. Consider the following complete closed diagram D0:

O_a(+), O_b(−), U_a(+), U_b(−).

It is a Reidemeister-II pair with one arc passing over the other at both crossings. An explicit local planar realization takes the over-arc y=sin(πx) and under-arc y=0 for −1/2≤x≤3/2, both directed toward increasing x; their crossings at x=0,1 have opposite signs, named a+,b− after choosing the plane convention. The region 0<x<1 is the empty RII bigon. The two connecting arcs may have virtual crossings. One RII move removes both classical crossings, leaving a virtual-only circle, which a detour makes the unknot.

Now change crossing b, reversing its arrow and sign. The resulting complete closed diagram D1 is

O_a(+), U_b(+), U_a(+), O_b(+).

In D1 the two chords are linked, so both are odd and J(D1)=2. In D0 their signs cancel and J(D0)=0. Crossing change does not change the flat diagram, so D1 has trivial underlying flat virtual-knot type, exactly as D0 does. Theorem 1 nevertheless forbids an ordinary pass-equivalence from D1 to the unknot.

**Corollary 3.** Underlying flat virtual-knot type is not a complete invariant of ordinary pass-equivalence. In particular, even the fiber over the flat unknot contains at least two distinct pass classes.

No conjecture attributed to the original authors is being refuted here. The precise false statement disproved is: “Two closed virtual knots with the same underlying flat virtual-knot type are ordinary pass-equivalent.”

## 5. Every flat fiber has at least two pass classes

**Theorem 4.** For every oriented flat virtual-knot class F, there are at least two distinct ordinary pass classes of closed virtual knots whose flat projection is F.

**Proof.** Choose a flat diagram of F and assign crossing information to obtain a virtual-knot diagram K. On a chosen arc insert the long version of D1, cut immediately before its first displayed endpoint. This is a diagrammatic operation on chosen representatives; it does not assume that connected sum is well-defined on closed virtual knots.

The inserted chords occupy a consecutive block disjoint from all old chord endpoints. They link one another and no old chord. Therefore the new diagram K1 satisfies J(K1)=J(K)+2. After flattening, the inserted two-crossing tangle cancels by the same flat RII move as D0, wholly away from the cut, so K and K1 have the same flat virtual-knot type F. Their values of b differ. Theorem 1 separates their ordinary pass classes. ∎

## 6. Sharpness, with a complete one-pass pair

Here a Gauss diagram is specified by its cyclic endpoint word and by each arrow's tail, head, and sign; endpoint positions start at zero. Take the word

x b a x a c c d y d b y.

Take arrows and signs

a: 2→4, +1; b: 1→10, −1; c: 6→5, −1;
d: 7→9, +1; x: 0→3, +1; y: 8→11, +1.

The four consecutive strand blocks of the pass are (b,a), (a,c), (c,d), (d,b). They are the directed crossings of two antiparallel vertical strands with two antiparallel horizontal strands, with the vertical strands over before the pass. The outside arrows x and y may be realized on the connecting arcs; an arbitrary signed directed Gauss diagram is realizable by a virtual diagram, drawing the prescribed classical crossing disks first and declaring all remaining transverse intersections virtual. This realization also keeps the designated pass disk intact.

The linked pairs are {x,a}, {x,b}, {y,b}, {y,d}. Thus exactly a and d are odd, and J=2. Reverse directions and signs of a,b,c,d, keeping x,y and all endpoints fixed. This is one ordinary pass move. The new odd writhe is −2. Hence the bound four is attained and this pair has pass distance exactly one. In particular J modulo eight is not a pass invariant, and no modulus m not dividing four can make J modulo m an invariant of all ordinary passes.

## 7. What remains

The results give a surjective two-valued obstruction within every flat fiber and a sharp distance lower bound. They do not show that the two values exhaust any fiber, that equality of flat type and b is sufficient for pass-equivalence, or whether a classical trefoil can become pass-equivalent to the unknot through virtual intermediate diagrams. The broad concordance/cobordism request in Problem 27 also remains unresolved. This is a partial first attempt, not a completed classification.
