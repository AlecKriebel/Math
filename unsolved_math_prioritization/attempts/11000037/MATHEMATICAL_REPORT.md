# Torelli density in ambient word balls: first substantive attempt

Target: 11000037 / AMR-109-0037, normal rank 1254. Date: 2026-10-10 UTC.

## Result and exact unresolved target

The arbitrary-generating-set conjecture is **not solved** here. A rigorous elementary partial result is proved: every prescribed finite subset of the mapping class group of a closed oriented surface of genus at least two is contained in a finite symmetric generating set for which Torelli elements are exponentially negligible in **distinct-element ambient word balls**. Explicit constants are given. This is an application of a general free-subgroup/spectral counting argument. It is not claimed to be a new theorem or to supersede prior genericity results.

Let G = Mod(S_g), let rho be its action on H_1(S_g; Z), and let I_g = ker(rho). For a finite symmetric generating set S not containing the identity, write

b_S(n) = |B_S(n)|,   a_S(n) = |I_g intersection B_S(n)|.

The source-faithful unresolved assertion studied here is:

For every integer g >= 2 and every such S, the limit lim_(n->infinity) a_S(n)/b_S(n) exists and equals zero.

The original source fixes generators in defining density and then discusses their choice; this report makes the all-generating-set interpretation explicit. Integer radii suffice because the word metric is integer-valued. The genus-one Torelli group is trivial and its density is zero in the infinite torus mapping class group. Genus zero is not included: the unmarked oriented sphere mapping class group is trivial, so a blanket genus-zero extension would be false. No punctured, bordered, extended, random-walk, intrinsic-Torelli-ball, or dilatation substitution is made.

The original statement and the definition immediately above it were visually inspected in Farb's chapter, printed p.29 / one-based PDF page 36, equation (9) and Conjecture 3.16. The logarithmic ratio uses the logs of numerator and denominator; it is an additional question, not equivalent to density zero. The original page fixes this interpretation and rules out a dilatation meaning of d.

## 1. An elementary counting theorem

### Theorem 1 (kernel negligibility after a specified enlargement)

Let pi: G -> Q be a homomorphism of a finitely generated group. Suppose pi(G) contains a nonabelian free subgroup. Let T be any finite symmetric generating set of G, with d = |T|, and choose k >= 2 elements x_1,...,x_k of G whose images freely generate a rank-k free subgroup. Put

S = T union {x_i, x_i^(-1): 1 <= i <= k},
q = 2k - 1,   c = d + 2 sqrt(q).

Then for every integer n >= 0,

|ker(pi) intersection B_S(n)| <= sum_(j=0)^n c^j,
|B_S(n)| >= q^n.

Consequently, whenever c < q,

|ker(pi) intersection B_S(n)| / |B_S(n)|
 <= [c/(c-1)] (c/q)^n.

In particular the limit exists and is zero. (For the prescribed finite-subset formulation, an identity element in the subset may be retained in S without changing any ball.) For any fixed d one can choose k large enough that c < q. One sufficient explicit condition is q > (1 + sqrt(d+1))^2. If a finite subset F was prescribed, first choose symmetric T containing F and a finite generating set; remove any identity letter or retain it as harmless padding, so S also contains F.

#### Proof: free-group adjacency bound, with no unproved spectral input

Let F_k have its standard 2k-regular Cayley tree. Its adjacency operator A on square-summable vertex functions has norm at most 2 sqrt(2k-1). To see this, orient the tree toward any one end. Every vertex has one parent and q children. For each parent-child edge and finitely supported f, use

2 |f(parent) f(child)|
 <= q^(-1/2)|f(parent)|^2 + q^(1/2)|f(child)|^2.

Summing over edges gives |<Af,f>| <= 2 sqrt(q) ||f||_2^2, since every vertex occurs q times as a parent and once as a child. A is bounded and self-adjoint; density of finitely supported functions proves the operator-norm bound. The familiar equality is not needed.

Use the left regular representation lambda of pi(G) on ell^2(pi(G)). Let

A_X = sum_i [lambda(pi(x_i)) + lambda(pi(x_i)^(-1))],
A_T = sum_(t in T) lambda(pi(t)),   A = A_T + A_X.

The action of the free subgroup generated by pi(x_i) partitions pi(G) into its right cosets. Each coset, with the left action, is a copy of the free group's regular representation. Thus ||A_X|| <= 2 sqrt(q). Also ||A_T|| <= d by the triangle inequality for unitary operators. Therefore ||A|| <= c. This uses a free subgroup of the quotient image, not a surjection of that image onto a free group.

For every j, the nonnegative integer <A^j delta_e, delta_e> counts length-j words in the labelled alphabet consisting of T and the 2k additional labels whose image under pi is the identity. If a new element is already in T, keep both labels for this count. Identity images and coinciding images are harmless. The number is at most ||A||^j <= c^j.

Every element of ker(pi) intersection B_S(n) has at least one word of length at most n in this labelled alphabet. Counting all such words is an upper bound on the number of distinct elements, so the first inequality follows. No assertion that endpoint multiplicities are uniform is used.

Every reduced word in x_1,...,x_k remains nontrivial and distinct in G because its image is a reduced word in a free basis. There are 2k q^(n-1) distinct words of length n for n >= 1, all in B_S(n); together with the identity this gives the weaker bound b_S(n) >= q^n used above. Summing the geometric series and dividing proves the stated estimate. Finally q > d + 2 sqrt(q) is equivalent to sqrt(q) > 1 + sqrt(d+1). QED.

### Lemma 2 (arbitrarily large free bases in the quotient image)

If u and v freely generate a subgroup of pi(G), then

v, u v u^(-1), ..., u^(k-1) v u^(-(k-1))

freely generate a rank-k subgroup, for every k >= 1.

Proof. In a freely reduced word in these generators and their inverses, combine successive occurrences having the same conjugating exponent into nonzero powers of v. Between two resulting blocks the conjugating exponents differ, leaving a nonzero power of u. The result has the form

u^(i_1) v^(m_1) u^(i_2-i_1) v^(m_2) ... v^(m_l) u^(-i_l),

with m_j nonzero and all interior u exponents nonzero. It is nontrivial in the free group on u,v. QED.

### Application to the Torelli kernel

Choose simple closed curves a,b intersecting once in a one-holed torus subsurface of S_g. With a suitable oriented homology basis, the actions of appropriate cubes of their Dehn twists, taking an inverse if necessary, restrict to

U = [[1,3],[0,1]],    V = [[1,0],[3,1]]

on the rank-two symplectic summand, and are the identity on its complement. The twist formula on homology is the elementary transvection formula, so these matrices lie in rho(G). They freely generate a group of rank two:

On the real projective line, set X = {x: |x|>1} including infinity and Y = {x: |x|<1}. For nonzero m, U^m maps Y into X, and V^m maps X into Y. The latter follows from |3mx+1| > |x| for |x|>1; infinity maps to 1/(3m). The usual ping-pong conclusion can be verified directly: a mixed cyclically reduced word starting with a power of U and ending with a power of V moves 0 from Y to X, while the opposite pattern moves infinity from X to Y; pure nonzero powers also are nonidentity. Every nonempty reduced free-product word is conjugate to one of these. Thus no nontrivial free relation holds.

Choose lifts of the rank-k basis from Lemma 2 and apply Theorem 1 with pi=rho. For every g>=2 and every prescribed finite subset F of G, this proves the existence of a finite symmetric S containing F and constants C>0 and 0<theta<1 such that a_S(n)/b_S(n) <= C theta^n for all n. The same estimate immediately holds for every subgroup of I_g, including the deeper Johnson-filtration terms contained in it, but only for these S. No comparison of their individual growth exponents is proved.

## 2. What prevents the arbitrary-generator conclusion

For a fixed S, the same word-counting argument works if the quotient adjacency norm ||sum_(s in S) lambda(rho(s))|| is strictly smaller than the ambient ball growth rate omega_S = lim b_S(n)^(1/n). The existence of omega_S follows from b_S(n+m) <= b_S(n)b_S(m) and subadditivity of logarithms. This is a sufficient condition, not an established fact for arbitrary S in Mod(S_g).

Nonamenability alone makes the normalized quotient return operator smaller than one; it does not compare its unnormalized norm with omega_S. The enlargement in Theorem 1 forces that comparison by providing a large, genuinely free collection of distinct elements downstairs and upstairs. Applying that conclusion back to the old word metric without a new estimate would discard the central difficulty.

### Explicit obstruction to the tempting general transfer

Let G = F_3 x F_2, H = F_3 x {e}, and use the union S_1 of the standard symmetric factor bases. Then the length is the sum of the two factor lengths, the quotient G/H is the nonamenable free group F_2, and H has infinite index and is normal. Nevertheless

|H intersection B_(S_1)(n)| = (3*5^n - 1)/2,
|B_(S_1)(n)| = (9*5^n - 8*3^n + 1)/2,

so H has density 1/3, not zero. These formulas follow by summing the products of the F_2 sphere sizes (1 at radius 0, and 4*3^(j-1) at radius j>=1) and F_3 ball sizes.

Now use the finite symmetric generating set

S_2 = ((A union {e}) x (B union {e})) minus {(e,e)},

where A and B are the same standard factor bases. Its word length is the maximum of the two factor lengths: coordinate projections give the lower bound, and padding the shorter reduced word by identity coordinates gives equality. Thus B_(S_2)(n) is the product of the factor balls and the density of H equals 1/|B_(F_2)(n)|, which tends exponentially to zero.

This is not a counterexample in the mapping class group. It proves that normality, infinite index, a nonamenable quotient, and changing among finite generating sets do not by themselves justify the desired argument. The original page's surrounding assertion about nonpositivity and generators is not used here as an unproved all-groups transfer theorem.

Likewise, if r distinct quotient cosets have representatives of length at most R, coset packing only yields

r * |H intersection B_S(n)| <= |B_S(n+R)|.

The factor |B_S(n+R)|/|B_S(n)| cannot be dropped. For exponentially growing balls it can offset the number of representatives.

## 3. Literature boundary and status

Choi, arXiv:2110.06678v3, Theorem A and Corollary 1.1, proves exponential pseudo-Anosov genericity for suitable enlarged generating sets. It does not state the arbitrary-generator Torelli assertion. Pseudo-Anosov elements exist in Torelli, so membership in Torelli is not included in the exceptional non-pseudo-Anosov set. Choi's Question 1.4 also retains the arbitrary-generator distinction for pseudo-Anosov genericity. The theorem above instead uses the homology quotient and elementary word counting. It is not presented as a corollary of Choi's curve-complex theorem.

The bounded present-day source search did not identify an exact arbitrary-generator resolution. This is not a certification of continuing openness or novelty. The enlargement argument is a standard elementary spectral/free-subgroup mechanism and receives no priority claim. The original conjecture, including the limit for an arbitrary finite generating set, remains the precise gap.
