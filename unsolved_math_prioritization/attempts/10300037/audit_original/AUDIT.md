## 2. Mathematical review of all five routes

### Route 1: parity, cyclic monodromy, and descent

Accepted for its stated combinatorial model. Adjacent sign reversal forces an even cyclic length and gives exactly two assignments. An orientation-preserving homeomorphism of a marked disk induces a cyclic shift of sides; an even shift preserves either assignment and an odd shift exchanges them. Passing to a degree-d base cover multiplies the shift by d, while descent still requires invariance under the original deck action. Thus an even-sided fiber does not by itself suffice for cooriented descent. The six-side, three-step shift is a valid obstruction to the proposed argument, not an example of an alternating-knot counterexample.

Orientation reversal of the circular ordering changes the shift's sign, not its parity. The proof does not assert that arbitrary unmarked polygon-bundle holonomy is conjugate to the chosen local model. No global essentiality follows from this combinatorics.

### Route 2: cusped solid torus and exact surgery coordinates

Accepted. For m parallel primitive (a,b) curves with b nonzero, a meridian disk has m|b| marked intersections. Independently tracing their positions in rational torus coordinates gives a shift of `sign(b) m a` in the fixed increasing angular order. This is the same as the packet's `m a` up to the stated reversal of circular orientation. There are m cycles, each of length |b|. The sign convention is therefore harmless for the claimed parity and orbit data; it must not be read as a fixed signed-shift identity for both signs of b in the same orientation.

An invariant alternating prescription requires both m|b| and m a to be even. Coprimality makes this equivalent to m being even: for odd m, an odd |b| fails the side-count condition, while an even |b| forces odd a and fails the shift condition.

For the determinant-one basis `(r,l)`, with `r=p mu+q lambda` and `l=c mu+d lambda`, direct inversion gives `mu=d r-q l`. Thus 2k meridional annuli produce `N=2k|q|` and the even shift represented by `2k d`. Changing l by a multiple of r changes d by a multiple of q and leaves the cyclic permutation unchanged. Reversing a representative of the unoriented filling slope does not change the parity conclusion.

The q=0 meridian is correctly excluded. The p=0 longitude is included when primitive. The case k=1 and |q|=1 is a two-sided strip/product model, not genuine polygon guts. The arithmetic concerns the marked compact cusped solid-torus model. Identifying the complete lamination complement also needs the interstitial/noncompact-extension data; the packet explicitly retains that gap.

### Route 3: logarithmic saddle model and smooth sides

Accepted for a regular even polygon with even rotational marking, and for the separately defined digon product. The affine side distances are positive in the interior. Even rotation preserves the alternating logarithmic sum. The one-form has nonzero t coefficient and is closed, giving an invariant nonsingular cooriented foliation. In the covering product, leaves are graphs. The deck action changes the graph constant by one and never fixes a graph point, so the quotient leaf has disk topology.

At an open side, multiplying by that side's positive distance gives a smooth nonzero defining form whose boundary value is a nonzero conormal to the side. Its kernel is exactly the tangent plane to that side; Frobenius extends by continuity. The other affine distances remain positive near an interior point of that side. This is a genuine side-extension argument, not merely divergence of a function. At polygon vertices the square's rescaled form vanishes, which is why deleting the ideal vertices is essential. The argument does not fill ordinary corners.

The rotational center is fixed and the core circle meets every interior graph leaf transversely. That proves the local interior-transversal assertion only. It does not prove tautness for previously existing boundary leaves or for a globally glued foliation. Coorientation, holonomy matching, essentiality, and the actual compactification remain separate global requirements. The digon uses identity side monodromy, as required in the two-cusp integer-filling case.

### Route 4: double-diamond sufficient subclass

Accepted as a credited conditional theorem. The statement preserves the necessary tight decomposition, the source-sector condition, the exclusion of a product disk, and the no-sink-disk condition on the associated Gabai branched surface. These are genuine geometric hypotheses and cannot be replaced by a sign assignment.

The dependency chain in the inspected [double-diamond preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01899v2) is correct: Theorem 5.10 supplies a taut cooriented branched surface with the two-annulus torus collar; Corollary 5.11 uses the no-sink condition to obtain laminarity and a fully carried lamination, and verifies the noncompact-extension step needed for Proposition 4.10. A taut branched surface alone has not automatically supplied a carried lamination. The proof appropriately distinguishes these statements.

The exceptional distinguished slope really is the knot meridian in S^3. Otherwise the actual meridian would be strongly realized, and disk capping would yield a cooriented taut foliation of S^3, contrary to the classical obstruction. This is consistent with the independently inspected composite paper's Remark 1.3. The argument uses strong boundary realization and the standard taut filling theorem; it does not infer tautness from an arbitrary boundary lamination.

The plumbed unknotted even-band subclass matches Corollary 6.3, with the minimum half-twist count retained. Reflection provides the mirrored construction with the corresponding sign changes. No source-inspected argument here converts every alternating diagram into a double-diamond/no-sink decomposition. The route is therefore legitimately partial.

### Route 5: annular gluing, boundary tautness, and geometry

Accepted after making the boundary-tautness argument and source scope precise. The [composite preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04838v3), Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 4.2, supports strongly realized slope preservation when a summand is glued to a longitude-foliated knot exterior. A finite slope is transverse to the meridional summing annulus. Matching the interval foliations and coorientations permits nonsingular gluing. With the standard connected-sum longitude, meridional displacement is the first displacement plus zero, hence the original slope. This calculation does not authorize arbitrary twists of the gluing map.

The original explanatory paragraph covered leaves with interior closed transversals and leaves meeting the outer boundary. Under a definition allowing proper transverse arcs, that leaves an omitted case: a leaf might meet such an arc without itself meeting the boundary. The correction closes every such arc by a positively transverse boundary path. For a linear nonmeridional foliation on the connected boundary torus, sufficiently many positive meridional turns make any endpoint displacement positively transverse. Smoothing and moving that boundary segment into a product collar gives a closed interior transversal. These old interior transversals survive gluing, and every new leaf contains a piece of an old leaf. This completes the boundary-tautness explanation without requiring the omitted leaf to meet the boundary.

Generic foliation gluing still does not specify a common essential sublamination or its polygon-bundle complementary component. The packet correctly stops short of that implication.

For the stronger fibered-connected-sum geometry, Sections 6.1–6.6 of the composite paper first adopt the same-veering setup. Citing those sections alone for all nontrivial fibered summands was incomplete. The correction cites and explains Section 7's summand-by-summand continuation, immediately after Corollary 7.2. It retains one Type C meridional cusp from each summand; opposite-sign transitions use Type A at the other smoothing, and Figures 27–29 preserve the Gabai product sutures. Theorem 5.12 provides the corresponding end-effective laminar splitting. Hence this is a geometric continuation of the collar/product construction, not merely a bare assertion of persistent foliarness.

There is also a distinct construction in Theorem 7.3. Its proof explicitly constructs a collar with two meridional annuli and a product complementary component and invokes the splitting argument. Its tight opposite-sign-arc hypothesis is retained; it is not silently inferred from a generic foliation-existence statement. Corollary 3.3 identifies the distinguished meridian with the actual knot meridian in S^3. PDF pages 32, 33 and 35, including the relevant continuation and figures, were visually checked. The source's Proposition 6.10 has abbreviated all-slope language in its statement, but its proof explicitly excludes the meridian; the corrected packet follows that necessary exception.
