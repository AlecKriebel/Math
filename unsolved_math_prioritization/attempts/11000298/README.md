# Finite-subgroup colimits for Out(F_n) and extended mapping class groups

Problem 11000298 / AMR-109-0298, rank 1257. Accepted partial result, substantive attempt 1 of 5.

## Established results

The target asks whether Out(F_n), n >= 2, and the full mapping class group Mod±(S_g) of a closed, unmarked, connected orientable surface, g >= 1, are colimits of finite diagrams of their actual finite subgroups under inclusions. The canonical map must be an isomorphism. The geometric formulation requires a developable simple complex of finite groups over a simply connected base.

The [complete mathematical report](MATHEMATICAL_REPORT.md) and [independent mathematical audit](MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md) retain all symbolic arguments, source hypotheses, credited inputs and authored mathematical examples:

- The classical endpoint Out(F_2)=Mod±(S_1)=GL(2,Z), with actual finite matrix subgroups of orders 8, 12 and 4 and their amalgam. The complete presentation explicitly includes the relation R^2=1 missing from one cited display.
- For every n >= 2, the actual outer-group intersection W_n intersect H_n=C_n, surjectivity of W_n *_(C_n) H_n onto Out(F_n), and an explicit eight-syllable kernel element with infinite order in the amalgam.
- The corrected subordinate-diagram retraction: adding only groups contained in the original factors cannot repair this kernel. Equality of colimits additionally needs inclusion arrows into the containing factors. The isolated-object counterexample to omitting this condition is retained.
- H^2(G;Q)=0 as a necessary condition for any group colimit of finite groups, with a torsion-free trivial-coefficient restriction-injectivity generalization and full central-extension/averaging proof. The condition has not been activated for the named higher target groups.
- Explicit Z^2 subgroups exclude graph-of-finite-groups descriptions for n >= 3 and g >= 2. This obstruction stops at dimension one.
- A C2*C3 counterexample shows that the colimit of all finite subgroups under inclusions need not equal the group even when a smaller finite subsystem succeeds.

## Remaining gap and credit

Arbitrary finite higher-dimensional inclusion diagrams for n >= 3 and g >= 2 remain unresolved by this work. Failure of the standard amalgam does not rule out adding genuinely different finite subgroups. Torsion generation, proper actions, higher cohomology or an orientation-preserving subgroup's anti-invariant class do not close that gap.

The original question is credited to Bridson–Vogtmann, Question 6.2 in [Problems on Mapping Class Groups and Related Topics](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf), printed 331 / PDF 338. The modular amalgam is classical. Armstrong–Forrest–Vogtmann supply the finite-stabilizer framework, generator and relation. Petrosyan–Prytula's later results retain strict/simple hypotheses and are not asserted to solve this target.
