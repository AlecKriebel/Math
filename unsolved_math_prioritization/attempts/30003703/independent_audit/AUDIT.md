## 2. Universal matrix identity

Use the associative bracket [U,V]=UV-VU. For a traceless 2-by-2 matrix X over any commutative ring, Cayley–Hamilton gives X^2 = -det(X)I. Put U=[X,Y] and V=[X,Z]. Then

    XU+UX = X^2Y-YX^2 = 0,
    XV+VX = X^2Z-ZX^2 = 0.

Consequently XUV=UVX and XVU=VUX, hence

    P(X,Y,Z) = [[[X,Y],[X,Z]],X] = 0.

The proof is an identity over arbitrary commutative rings; it neither divides by 2 nor uses generic diagonalization, eigenvalue assumptions, a nondegenerate trace pairing, or characteristic-zero density. In the target graded ring every input required to be traceless is traceless. Substitution Z=[X,Y] is legitimate: the commutator has trace zero over a commutative coefficient ring. This yields

    Q(X,Y) = [[[X,Y],[X,[X,Y]]],X] = 0.

P is homogeneous of free degree 5; Q has free degree 6. Substituting Z=[X,Y] has weight 2, not weight 1. This is why the rank-three result is at filtration degree 6.

Independent controls multiply completely generic traceless matrices with independent commuting indeterminates. P and Q vanish entry by entry. The truncation bound lies strictly above their maximum possible degrees, so the zero output is not created by truncation.

## 3. The representation ideal bridge is valid

Let B be an associative ring filtered by two-sided ideals I^r. For invertible U=1+u and V=1+v with u in I^r and v in I^s, the exact algebraic identity is

    UVU^(-1)V^(-1)-1 = (uv-vu)U^(-1)V^(-1).

Both inverses differ from 1 by elements in their corresponding ideals, because U^(-1)-1=-uU^(-1). The degree r+s term is therefore the bracket of the leading terms of u and v, and all omitted contributions lie in I^(r+s+1). This is an ideal calculation in the original ring. It does not require convergence, a completed local ring, injectivity into a power-series ring, or separation of the ideal filtration. The same argument works with I^r = Mat_2(J^r).

For the tautological SL2 generator matrices M_i=I+B_i, every entry of B_i lies in J. Expanding the determinant gives

    0 = det(I+B_i)-1 = tr(B_i)+det(B_i).

Hence tr(B_i) lies in J^2. Only the class X_i in Mat_2(J/J^2) is declared traceless. The proof never sets tr(B_i)=0 in the original ring.

For the word

    w = [[[x_1,x_2],[x_1,x_3]],x_1],

the exact filtered-commutator calculation puts M(w)-I in Mat_2(J^5), with degree-five class P(X_1,X_2,X_3). P vanishes in the commutative ring gr_J R. By the definition of a quotient J^5/J^6, zero class means membership in J^6, entry by entry. Thus all four entries, including both off-diagonal entries, satisfy the necessary ideal-power bound.

For

    v = [[[x_1,x_2],[x_1,[x_1,x_2]]],x_1],

the intermediate commutators have weights 2 and 3; their commutator has weight 5; the final bracket with x_1 has weight 6. Its leading class is Q(X_1,X_2), so every entry of M(v)-I lies in J^7.

This implication remains true in a nonreduced or singular commutative coefficient algebra, and does not require the claim that gr_J R is freely polynomial. Determinant relations or any additional function-algebra relations cannot invalidate a polynomial identity already true over every commutative ring.

### Independent finite-quotient verification

A separate implementation also evaluates the full group words, not just their Lie leading terms, in universal determinant quotients. Take

    S = Z[a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i]/(a_i+d_i+a_i*d_i-b_i*c_i),
    m = (all a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i).

In S/m^N, 1+a_i is a unit, with inverse sum from r=0 to N-1 of (-a_i)^r. Thus

    d_i = (b_i*c_i-a_i) * sum((-a_i)^r, r=0,...,N-1).

Substitution induces an isomorphism from S/m^N to the polynomial ring in the 3n variables a_i,b_i,c_i truncated by all monomials of total degree at least N. To see both directions, send a,b,c to their names and d to the displayed polynomial. The determinant relation vanishes; conversely the determinant relation solves for d using the finite geometric inverse. All generators of m are in the ideal of a,b,c, and the assignments are inverse. This uses finite rings, not analytic arcs. There is a surjective algebra map S tensor Q -> R sending universal entries to tautological entries; the augmentation ideal maps onto J because all group-word entries are polynomial in generator entries and inverse entries are adjugates. Ideal membership in S therefore gives membership in R, whether or not that surjection is assumed injective.

The independent program verifies det(M_i)=1 and M_i*adj(M_i)=I in each finite quotient before evaluating the words. It finds:

- w evaluates to I modulo m^6; the degree-six displacement is nonzero, with 28 monomials in each matrix entry.
- v evaluates to I modulo m^7; the degree-seven displacement is nonzero, with entry counts 15,18,18,15.

These are symbolic exact integer calculations with 9 and 6 independent variables, respectively. They check all coordinates of the relevant universal finite quotients. The next-degree nonzero results are negative controls against overclaiming stronger membership; the accepted theorem does not need or rely on those extra nonmembership assertions in R.

## 4. Integral free-group nonmembership

The Magnus homomorphism sends x_i to 1+X_i in the group of noncommutative integral formal series with constant coefficient 1. Its existence follows directly from the universal property of the free group and the geometric-series inverse of 1+X_i. A filtered group-commutator calculation proves E(Gamma_r F_n) subset 1+T_(>=r): the relevant unit groups are closed under products, inverses and conjugation, and taking a commutator with an element of positive order raises order by at least one. No injectivity theorem, dimension-subgroup converse, or rational lower-central-series theorem is needed.

The leading terms of the same nested group words are P and Q, now interpreted in the free associative algebra, whose variables do not commute. They do not vanish there:

- In P, the coefficient of X_1 X_1 X_2 X_1 X_3 is -1. The positive final-X term cannot contribute because the target ends with X_3. In the negative initial-X term the only contribution is -X_1*(X_1 X_2)*(X_1 X_3).
- In Q, the coefficient of X_1 X_1 X_1 X_2 X_1 X_2 is +1. Writing U=XY-YX and V=X^2Y-2XYX+YX^2, only X*V*U contributes to this target, from X*(X^2Y)*(XY).

A member of Gamma_6 would have no Magnus terms of degree 5; a member of Gamma_7 would have no degree-6 terms. Therefore w is not in Gamma_6 and v is not in Gamma_7 over the integers. This is a contrapositive of the elementary inclusion, not its converse. Rational coefficients in R do not weaken or replace these integral nonmembership assertions.

The independent Magnus program uses a dense coefficient recurrence on the full finite set of words, rather than the author's sparse polynomial-product implementation. Right multiplication by 1+X adds the coefficient of the appropriate prefix; right multiplication by its inverse solves new[word] = old[word] - new[prefix] when the final letter is X. It recovers every retained coefficient, not merely the two certificates. There are 12 nonconstant terms for w and 10 for v, all in their stated degrees. The reduced word lengths are 32 and 30.

## 5. Genuine automorphisms and action on the entire component algebra

If u avoids x_t, the endomorphism fixing other generators and sending x_t to x_t*u fixes u itself. The assignment x_t -> x_t*u^(-1), fixing the same other generators, is an inverse in both orders. Consequently these assignments are automorphisms, not merely endomorphisms inducing some truncated action.

For n >= 4 use u=w, t=4. For n >= 3 use u=v, t=3. They avoid the moving generator as required. In larger ranks all additional generators are fixed. The inverse argument is identical for every rank, without a stability assumption. Independent free reduction checks both compositions in the minimal rank, the next rank, and rank 9; the written generator argument proves all remaining ranks.

The component ring is generated by the finitely many entries of the free generator matrices: products are polynomial and inverse entries are polynomial because det(M_i)=1 and M_i^(-1)=adj(M_i). Hence equality modulo J^(k+1) on those entries implies equality on every element of R/J^(k+1), and in particular on J/J^(k+1). The ideal is invariant because the trivial representation is fixed by precomposition with a free-group automorphism.

For the moving generator, the difference is M_t*(M(u)-I); multiplication by entries of M_t preserves J^(k+1). All other generators are fixed exactly. This establishes D_n(5) membership for the first automorphism and D_n(6) membership for the second. If the chosen action is expressed using the inverse automorphism, the kernel is unchanged; an automorphism and its inverse act trivially simultaneously.

On the free-group side, x_t^(-1)*phi(x_t)=u. The nonmembership proved above gives phi_w outside A_n(5) and phi_v outside A_n(6). Their words remain nonzero in the larger-rank Magnus algebra with the same coefficients. Equivalently, a retraction onto the smaller free factor would send any purported higher lower-central membership back to the disproved one. No passage from a free-group element to an automorphism is missing.

## 6. Adversarial controls and what they rule out

The following controls pass in independent_controls.py. PASS means the correct result was recovered or the deliberately incorrect variant was detected.

1. Reversing only the outer commutator changes the decisive coefficient from -1 to +1. This catches an incorrectly transcribed bracket order.
2. The inverse-first convention u^(-1)v^(-1)uv has the same leading Lie bracket, not its negative. Both nested words retain their correct leading polynomials, but their higher-order Magnus terms differ. This guards against a misleading convention test.
3. Omitting the off-diagonal product b*c when solving the determinant equation produces determinant defect -b*c. It is rejected.
4. Treating the whole perturbation B as exactly traceless, rather than only its degree-one class, produces determinant defect -a^2-b*c. Actual tr(B) has nonzero degree-two terms. This invalid simplification is detected.
5. Replacing the final repeated X in P by a different matrix can give -8E, not zero; the repeated-variable structure matters.
6. Removing the final commutator leaves the nonzero diagonal matrix diag(-4,4). Thus vanishing has not been incorrectly lowered to degree 4.
7. In size 3 the same proposed identity gives 2E_13 for an explicit traceless example. The argument cannot be generalized to SL_m, m > 2, on this basis.
8. An ordinary group commutator has leading term XY-YX. Commuting the Magnus variables would erase the valid nonmembership evidence.
9. Moving a generator that appears in u makes the proposed inverse fail for both words. Generator disjointness is essential and explicitly checked.
10. Calculating one degree past each accepted ideal bound finds nonzero universal jets. The program is not returning identity simply because its truncation occurs too early.
11. Concrete SL2(Z) substitutions give the nonidentity matrix with rows (-101,390),(-36,139) for each chosen word. The Lie identity is not a group law and no exact word identity is asserted.
12. The rank-three word's Magnus expansion through degree 5 is exactly 1. It supplies no certificate of failure at A_3(5), and that stronger claim is deliberately excluded.

The proof is short because a universal low-degree Lie identity for 2-by-2 matrices creates a gap between matrix-entry augmentation order and integral free-group lower-central order.
