Checked 7 October 2026. The mathematical disposition is **unsolved by this packet**, after five substantive author approaches. A bounded literature search is not an exhaustive certification of world-wide open status or historical novelty.

## Exact target normalization

The source is Alexander Plakhov's contribution, “Billiards, optimization of resistance and invisibility,” in Calculus of Variations, Oberwolfach Report 31/2010, pp. 1855–1857, specifically p. 1856, second part. The workshop ran 18–24 July 2010; the journal volume is 7(3) (2010), pp. 1827–1884, published in March 2011. Thus the corpus's 2011 bibliographic date and 2010 problem year describe different dates, not different problems.

The report uses informal all-ray language. Its cited mathematical treatment [P] specifies a connected body with piecewise-smooth boundary, exterior specular billiards, and reversal almost everywhere in incident phase space. Singular trajectories are excluded in the formal definition. The meaningful unresolved setting is bounded bodies in optical dimensions at least two; [P] explicitly supplies unbounded examples. The report does not impose a fixed reflection count, convexity, simple connectivity, or purely polygonal boundary. No narrower boundary class is silently substituted here.

For incidence measure, use Euclidean flux on oriented rays hitting the body. On an enclosing transverse section its density is (−v·n)_+ dS dv, restricted to rays interacting with the body; normalization gives a probability measure. For a planar connected body one can instead use its convex hull boundary, with density cosφ ds dφ and total mass twice the hull perimeter. “Almost every” refers to this joint position-and-angle measure, not just one chosen illumination direction. Reversal means outgoing velocity −v; equality of entrance and exit position or retracing the same line is not demanded. Refractive lenses and electromagnetic metasurfaces are outside this specular billiard model.

The authored geometric exclusions and branch results in Turns 2–5 are explicitly planar. They are not a proof of nonexistence in every dimension or every boundary class admitted by [P].
