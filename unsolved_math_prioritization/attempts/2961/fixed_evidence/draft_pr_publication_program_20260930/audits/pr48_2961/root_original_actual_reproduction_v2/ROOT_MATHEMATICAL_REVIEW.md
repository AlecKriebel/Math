## Compression and its geometric hypotheses

Use [a,b]=aba^-1b^-1 and increasing-index products. With pairwise commuting displaced copies T^i(H), put c_i=[a_i,b_i], h=c_1...c_m, s_i=c_(i+1)...c_m, A=product T^i(a_i), B=product T^i(b_i), C=product T^i(s_i). At copy0 the product C T(C^-1) is s_0=h. At copy i it is s_i s_(i-1)^-1=c_i^-1 because s_(i-1)=c_i s_i; at the last copy it is c_m^-1. Different copies commute; factors within one copy need not commute. Thus [C,F]=h product T^i(c_i^-1) and [A,B]=product T^i(c_i), giving h=[C,F][A,B]. No injective direct-product embedding is required; the same word identity holds in its quotient image. The identity itself needs zero commutators.

For D(M×R²), specify endpoints of smooth isotopies with one compact total support. Imported smooth Mather–Thurston perfectness gives a finite commutator decomposition. Its finitely many factor map supports fit in M×B_R. Select L>2R and a compactly supported vector field equal to L∂x on a neighborhood of every translated disk B_R+(Lt,0), 0≤t≤m. Uniqueness for the ODE makes the time-j images exactly those disks. They are pairwise disjoint. Because M is compact, id_M×u and its isotopy have compact total support, as do the finite products, inverses and conjugates used in compression. This is the portable product mechanism of Burago–Ivanov–Polterovich; it does not assert that a closed product is portable or that the classical perfectness theorem was reproved here.

## Stabilization, all powers, and the scope of the bound

This refutes the naive universal cutoff map being a diffeomorphism. It does not refute every factorization scheme.

## Imported results and other boundary cases

The unrestricted even-dimensional Theorem1.2 requires dimension at least six and cannot settle this target. S¹×S³ has a product Morse function of indices0,1,3,4; standard connected sums of k copies have counts(1,k,0,k,1). These supply the required decomposition. The K3 remark limiting understood examples to S⁴ is too broad; Webb's original OWR report already names the no-2-handle class.

Compact manifolds have finitely many components. The identity component cannot permute them. For a finite product of perfect groups, projection gives a lower bound by the maximum component length, and padding shorter decompositions with identity commutators gives the reverse inequality. Thus the product length is the maximum. For a central extension, a homogeneous quasimorphism descends exactly when it vanishes on the central kernel, by commuting-element additivity. Subgroup inclusion decreases ambient length; the fragmentation inequality is an upper bound and supplies no requested lower bound.
