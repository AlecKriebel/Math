# Attempt 3: restriction to a direct factor

## Target and outcome

The original assertion reduces to infinite irreducible finite-rank Coxeter groups. This removes reducible cases but leaves the irreducible case open. The reduction uses an actual surjective restriction homomorphism, not an unjustified passage to an arbitrary finite-index subgroup. No novelty claim is made.

## Lemma: finite-index factor stabilizer with full restriction image

Let W=W_I×W_J be a standard direct-product decomposition of a finite-rank Coxeter group. Then

G_I={f∈Aut(W): f(W_I)=W_I}

has finite index in Aut(W), and restriction G_I→Aut(W_I) is surjective.

Proof. Let Spe(W) be the kernel of the action of Aut(W) on the finitely many conjugacy classes of involutions. This subgroup has finite index. For each standard generator s∈I and f∈Spe(W), there is g∈W with f(s)=gsg⁻¹. The factor W_I is normal in W, so f(s)∈W_I. Hence f(W_I)⊆W_I. Applying the same argument to f⁻¹ gives equality. Thus Spe(W)⊆G_I, proving that G_I has finite index.

Every automorphism α of W_I extends to α×id_{W_J}∈Aut(W), which belongs to G_I. Restriction is consequently onto the entire Aut(W_I). ∎

## Proposition: transfer of the exact quotient conclusion

Assume a finite-index subgroup K≤Aut(W_I) surjects onto an infinite Coxeter group Q. Then a finite-index subgroup of Aut(W) surjects onto Q.

Proof. Let r:G_I↠Aut(W_I) be the preceding restriction. The subgroup r⁻¹(K) has finite index in G_I and hence in Aut(W). Its restriction map onto K is surjective, so composition with K↠Q gives the result. ∎

Every finite-rank Coxeter system decomposes into finitely many irreducible standard factors, and an infinite W has an infinite factor. Therefore a proof for every infinite irreducible factor would prove the full original assertion.

## Essential finite-index warning

It would be invalid to replace G_I by Spe(W), observe a map onto Spe(W_I), and then merely intersect a successful subgroup K with Spe(W_I). An epimorphism K↠Q need not map K∩Spe(W_I) onto a Coxeter group. Its image is only known to have finite index in Q.

The property “virtually surjects onto an infinite Coxeter group” is not preserved by taking arbitrary finite-index subgroups. The infinite dihedral Coxeter group D∞ satisfies the property, but its index-two subgroup Z does not. Every finite-index subgroup and every quotient of Z is cyclic; an infinite cyclic group is not a Coxeter group, since an abelian group generated by involutions has exponent 2. The use of the full factor stabilizer and its surjective restriction eliminates this defect.

## Exact gap

The argument says nothing new about a single infinite irreducible factor with infinite outer automorphism group. In particular, a standard special subgroup that is merely an odd factor need not be a direct factor, and an arbitrary automorphism of it need not extend by the identity to W. The extension step above depends on the direct product and must not be reused for a general parabolic subgroup.
