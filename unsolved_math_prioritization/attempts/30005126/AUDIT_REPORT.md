## Definitions and direction checks

1. The base field in the inspected formulation is C. No characteristic-independent claim is licensed by this audit.
2. Symmetry reverses arrows and preserves the vertex and arrow sets. An admissible ideal yields a finite-dimensional quotient; the source requires σ-stability.
3. The bilinear form is fixed, nondegenerate, ε-symmetric, and pairs only opposite graded pieces. Self-duality by itself is not a substitute for specifying this compatible form and the ε-representation variety.
4. For α:i→j the correct arrow equation is B(Mαv,w) = -B(v,Mσ(α)w). An unqualified “M = -M*” must be understood arrowwise, especially when parallel arrows occur.
5. The ordinary group is ∏ᵢ GL(Vᵢ), not all ungraded changes of basis on V. The subgroup is the full group preserving the fixed form; replacing O by SO may change the orbit problem.
6. The convention is that M ≤deg N means M degenerates to N, so N is the point in the closure of M's orbit. The candidate uses this direction correctly.
7. H ⊆ G proves ≤degε ⇒ ≤deg. Indeed H·M ⊆ G·M, hence closure(H·M) ⊆ closure(G·M). As the symmetric variety is closed and contains H·M, that closure is also contained in the symmetric variety. This proves the automatic inclusion in the equivalent orbit-closure equality.
8. The reverse inclusion is the conjectural content. Equality of ordinary and symmetric isomorphism classes on the symmetric locus does not by itself make taking closure commute with intersection with that locus.

A convention safeguard: the source defines the twisted dual by (∇M)α = -Mσ(α)*. For general relation presentations this requires invariance under the signed path anti-involution α ↦ -σ(α), or an equivalent hypothesis ensuring that ∇ preserves the module category. Bare unsigned σ-stability does not, for arbitrary mixed-parity relations, imply signed stability. The direct arrowwise orbit formulation remains well-defined without making that additional inference. CORRECTED_TARGET.md therefore explicitly assumes signed stability for the self-dual module formulation, and separately records the source-literal arrowwise reading. We do not assert that those two classes of relation presentations coincide. This is a compatibility safeguard, not a claimed counterexample to the conjecture or an error in the zero-relation type-A theorem.

## What the ordinary theorem does and does not say

Write [X,Y] = dim HomA(X,Y). The cited convention for Hom-order is M ≤Hom N if [M,E] ≤ [N,E] for every indecomposable E. The ordinary Ext-order is generated by replacing the middle term of a short exact sequence 0→U→M→W→0 by U⊕W. These satisfy

≤Ext ⇒ ≤deg ⇒ ≤Hom.

The published 2022 Boos–Cerulli Irelli paper, pp.138–139, explicitly attributes equality of all three orders for representation-directed algebras to Bongartz, Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 4.2. It also recalls Zwara's stronger ordinary degeneration/Hom equivalence for representation-finite algebras. The Bongartz publisher metadata/abstract independently supports the author, title, journal, pages, and the representation-directed extension result. [Published 2022 PDF](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e383532e-02dd-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Boos_SymmetricDegenerations_2022.pdf), [Bongartz publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001870896900535).

Correct citation: Klaus Bongartz, “On Degenerations and Extensions of Finite Dimensional Modules,” Advances in Mathematics 121(2) (1996), 245–287, DOI 10.1006/aima.1996.0053. The candidate's DOI is not the DOI established by the publisher record.

Reliance boundary: this audit relies on the published theorem and its explicit scholarly attribution. It did not retrieve or independently reconstruct every step of the 43-page Bongartz proof. It does not present the ordinary theorem as a new result.

An ordinary extension chain need not preserve a fixed form or use isotropic submodules. Consequently ordinary Ext/Hom equivalence supplies no missing proof that a GL degeneration can be realized through the isometry subgroup.

## Exact scope of the 2025 positive theorem

The actual published 55-page PDF was retrieved, rather than identifying the arXiv preprint with the final version. The relevant setup, main theorem, conjecture, and proof opening were inspected; pp.64–65, 67, 69, 78, and 107 were also checked visually.

Theorem 7.1 on p.107 assumes a symmetric quiver Q of type Aₙ, A = C Q, and a compatible ε-form. It proves equivalence of ordinary degeneration, ε-degeneration, and ε-Ext order. The introduction on p.67 explains the finite-type language in terms of symmetric hereditary quiver algebras; it must not be read as all finite-representation-type bound quiver algebras. Page 69 explicitly retains the representation-directed extension as a conjecture. [Published PDF](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/7e049881-5a31-4c23-b75f-3ea79f4d19e4/Cerulli_degenerations_2025.pdf).

The ε-Ext order uses isotropic L⊆M and a replacement isomorphic to L⊕∇L⊕(L⊥/L), with the natural symmetric structure. The general implication ε-Ext ⇒ ε-degeneration is Corollary 3.3. The hard implication of Theorem 7.1 uses type-A-specific generic isotropic embeddings and generic ε-subquotients, not simply the ordinary Bongartz theorem.

There is a harmless additional scope consequence worth making explicit. If Q is one of the symmetric type-A quivers covered by the theorem, its conclusion about the two orbit-closure orders also restricts to any closed, invariant relation locus R(C Q/I,V). Proof: that locus contains the entire G-orbit of any of its points and is closed, hence contains its ambient G-orbit closure. It likewise contains the H-orbit closure. Thus the two closures computed in the relation locus equal their ambient closures, and the theorem applies to M,N in that locus. This elementary restriction argument is a complete consequence of the cited theorem; it does not address arbitrary representation-directed algebras whose ambient quivers are outside its type-A hypothesis.

## Why finite representation type alone is insufficient

The published 2022 counterexample article has Proposition 4.2 and Corollary 4.3 on p.141: ordinary and symmetric degenerations differ for its orthogonal type-D seesaw example, despite finite representation type. “Type D” here is the orthogonal Lie-group case in that construction; it is not a license to replace the 2025 theorem's quiver hypothesis by all Dynkin diagrams. The seesaw counterexample does not settle the representation-directed target. The 2021 preprint explicitly notes that the seesaw algebra is not representation-directed.

This also explains why the finite-type theorem and the finite-type counterexample are compatible: the theorem concerns the stated symmetric quiver/path-algebra setting, while the counterexample concerns a bound quiver algebra outside it.
