# Five approaches, their outputs, and stopping point

1. **Direct near-minimal hyperbolic filling.** An Ahlfors-regular gauge metric can be filled by a hyperbolic space, but quasi-isometry to G does not supply an isometric geometric G-action preserving that marked boundary metric. PROOF.md, Theorem 2, makes this gap explicit even for an optimal surface-group metric. This is the strongest partial result.
2. **Equivariant supremum metric.** Lemma 3 proves that uniformly bounded additive failure of translation invariance can be corrected exactly without unbounded metric error. The hypothesis is not supplied by an arbitrary quasi-isometric filling, and a proper geodesic realization is still an additional requirement. No unconditional reduction is claimed.
3. **Entropy and rescaling.** The growth/visibility quotient is unchanged when both parameters are transformed consistently under a homothety. Rescaling is not a way to force the quotient to the conformal dimension. Trees do allow arbitrary visual parameter; general connected boundaries do not.
4. **Explicit geometric models.** Free groups and real-hyperbolic uniform lattices satisfy the equality by the complete standard arguments in Section 6. The free-group infimum is not attained. These controls distinguish numerical equality from existence of a minimum and from realizing a particular marked metric. They do not cover all hyperbolic groups.
5. **Poincare-profile comparison.** The 2020 paper defines E(G) and names the exact conjecture. The 2022 improvement obtains profile upper bounds directly from A(G), bypassing E(G). The 2025 paper studies equality of a different critical exponent with A(G). Even equality of those profile invariants would not by itself construct a geometric action attaining or approximating A(G); no reverse realization implication is established in the inspected sources.

The general problem remains unresolved in this work. Stop after these five routes rather than treating the explicit marked obstruction as a disproof of Kleiner's existential statement. The deliverable is an authored partial-result proof and reproducible algebra certificate, with credited standard cases and a precise construction gap. No new-literature-resolution or originality claim is made.
