## 2. Exact cone and dual certificate

The five normalized rays, in (L^2,LM,M^2,beta_2) pairing coordinates, are

`A=(1,0,0,72), F=(0,1,0,-12), T=(0,1,2,-6), C0=(0,0,1,-3), K=(0,0,1,1)`.

The scaling factors (1152,96,48,48,4) agree directly with the inspected primary table. The sole circuit is F+3K=T+C0. Four of the five four-ray subsets are linearly independent.

Set p=-72x+12y+3z+w, q=72x-8y+z-w, and g=72x-12y+3z-w. Solving a general ray decomposition independently gives, with t the coefficient of T and k0=p/4,

`(a,b,t,c,k) = (x, y-t, t, z-k0+t, k0-3t)`.

Thus the feasible interval is

`max(0,k0-z) <= t <= min(y,k0/3)`, together with x>=0.

Its feasibility is equivalent to x,y,p,q,g>=0. The useful exact identities are q=4y+4z-p, p+g=6z, and p-12y+3q=2g. Choosing t=max(0,y-q/4) recovers precisely the author's formula. All five coefficients are nonnegative and reconstruct every real point satisfying the inequalities. This is a universal algebraic argument, not an inference from finite tests.

Independent enumeration of all supporting rank-three triples yields exactly the five facet normals. Each has a three-dimensional zero face, so none is redundant. Hence

`C* = cone(L^2, LM, F1, F2, G)`

with G=72L^2-12LM+3M^2-beta_2. The dual here uses the nonnegative pairing convention.

## 4. The two-nef-class criterion is correctly repaired

The audited argument explicitly uses the credited containment statement from GH Remark 9.2 and the effective cones of the exceptional loci. For an irreducible S with y>=0, the condition 3z-8y<0 also gives z-3y<0. Both effective-divisor restrictions therefore have negative pairing with the nef class M and force containment in their common support. Restricting an effective Cartier divisor to an integral surface not contained in it gives an effective curve; this justifies the negativity step with nef M, without pretending M is ample. Positive multiples handle rational Cartier classes on the stack.

On the exceptional support, the credited cone result puts [S] in C. Elsewhere z>=8y/3, and the independently checked identity gives

`G·S = F2·S + 2z - 4y >= F2·S + (4/3)y`.

Thus **F2 nef implies G nef**, with the localization input retained. Together with the correct polar description and nefness of L^2 and LM, simultaneous nefness of F1,F2 is equivalent to the proposed cone equality. This does not prove either unknown nefness assertion. The correction of a printed polar formula is therefore compatible with the author's repaired two-class criterion.
