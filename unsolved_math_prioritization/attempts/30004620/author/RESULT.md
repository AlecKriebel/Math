# Effective surfaces on the genus-three toroidal compactification

**Problem 30004620 / OWR-4990375-013, rank 782. Disposition: unsolved, five scoped approaches.**

The five-ray generation statement has neither been proved nor disproved here. The package gives an exact primal/dual certificate, a version-specific discrepancy in a printed dual-cone formula, a positive result for every product of four nef divisor classes, a careful separation of effective and closed faces, and a repaired two-class nefness reduction using a credited geometric localization result. ## 1. Exact target and conventions

Let X be the complex, six-dimensional smooth Deligne–Mumford stack compactifying the moduli stack of principally polarized abelian threefolds by the perfect-cone fan. In genus three this is also the second-Voronoi and central-cone compactification. Use real cycle classes and the stack intersection normalization. The coarse space has finite quotient singularities; its cycle rays correspond under the rational coarse-space comparison, but its multiplicity conventions must not be substituted silently into the table below.

The target is the closed cone of **two-dimensional** effective cycles, overline(Eff_2(X)), inside N_2(X)_R, identified here with H_4(X,R). These surfaces have codimension four, whereas a class testing them by degree has codimension two and belongs to H^4(X,R). The four algebraic classes L^2, LM, M^2, beta_2 span H^4, and the full-rank pairing below shows that numerical equivalence does not further identify surface classes. Here L is the Hodge divisor, D the boundary divisor, M=12L-D, and beta_2 is the codimension-two torus-rank-at-least-two boundary class.

Write Z=N(Abar_1 x Abar_2) for the decomposable locus. The proposed surfaces are:

- S_A: the image of products of three elliptic curves, with one factor fixed;
- S_F: the image of Abar_1 times a fiber curve of the genus-two Satake contraction;
- S_D: the image of a fixed elliptic curve times the genus-two boundary;
- sigma_K3+1 and sigma_C4: closures of the two toroidal surface strata. Their fan cones are generated respectively by x1^2,x2^2,x3^2,(x1-x2)^2 and x1^2,x2^2,(x1-x3)^2,(x2-x3)^2.

Let C be their nonnegative real span. The question is overline(Eff_2(X))=C. Because C is finitely generated, closed, and generated by effective classes, this is equivalent to Eff_2(X)=C. Grushevsky–Hulek's theorem gives extremality in Eff_2; it does not automatically give extremality in its closure or generation.

The controlling OWR statement is Conjecture 2.2, printed p.352. The supplied original wording incorrectly said codimension two; the corrected dataset wording still suppresses closure and compactification bars. Its statement hash matches the descriptor exactly. The live problem page returned HTTP 403, so no claim is made that its current displayed bytes were inspected. The OWR bibliography's arXiv 2011.06785 points to an unrelated syzygies paper; the matching Grushevsky–Hulek paper is 2007.02995. See SOURCES.md and provenance.json.

## 2. Approach 1: exact cone duality and constructive membership

Use coordinates s=(x,y,z,w)=(L^2.s,LM.s,M^2.s,beta_2.s). Positive rescaling of the primary intersection table yields

| normalized ray | x | y | z | w |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A=1152 S_A | 1 | 0 | 0 | 72 |
| F=96 S_F | 0 | 1 | 0 | -12 |
| T=48 S_D | 0 | 1 | 2 | -6 |
| C0=48 sigma_C4 | 0 | 0 | 1 | -3 |
| K=4 sigma_K3+1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |

The letter T here denotes the normalized S_D ray, not a theta divisor. There is one relation, F+3K=T+C0. The rank is four.

Define linear forms

p=-72x+12y+3z+w, q=72x-8y+z-w, g=72x-12y+3z-w.

**Proposition 1.** Membership in C is equivalent to the five inequalities

x>=0, y>=0, p>=0, q>=0, g>=0.

For a point satisfying these inequalities, put d=max(0,y-q/4). Then its coefficients on (A,F,T,C0,K) are

(x, y-d, d, d+q/4-y, p/4-3d).

**Proof.** Evaluation on the five rays gives the matrix with rows A,F,T,C0,K and columns x,y,p,q,g:

(1,0,0,0,0), (0,1,0,4,0), (0,1,12,0,0), (0,0,0,4,6), (0,0,4,0,2).

Thus membership implies the inequalities. Conversely all displayed coefficients are nonnegative: d<=y follows from q>=0, while d<=p/12 follows from p>=0 and p-12y+3q=2g>=0. Direct substitution reconstructs (x,y,z,w). The identities p+g=6z and q-g=4y-2z supply the remaining coordinate check. This proves the statement for arbitrary real coordinates, not just rational test points. Each zero set contains three independent primal rays, so all five inequalities are irredundant facets. The polar cone is therefore generated by their five normals. An independent enumeration of rank-three triples verifies exactly the same facets. QED.

In cohomology the fifth normal is

G=72L^2-12LM+3M^2-beta_2,

and the polar is cone(L^2,LM,F1,F2,G), with F1 and F2 the classes corresponding to p and q.

**Version-specific discrepancy.** ArXiv v2, p.3, displays cone(L^2,LM,M^2,F1,F2) as the dual of its primal conjecture. With its own p.12 table this is not the polar: M^2=(F1+G)/6 is redundant, while G is missing. The vector u=(0,1,0,-10) pairs as (0,1,0,2,2) with the printed list but G.u=-2. It is therefore outside C although it passes those five printed tests. This is a numerical-class witness, not an effective surface. The discrepancy claim is confined to the inspected v2; the publisher's PDF endpoint returned institutional-login HTML, not full article bytes. It does not refute the primal conjecture. Section 5 below repairs the separate two-class-nefness reduction by a geometric argument.

**Gap after this approach:** a facet certificate for the candidate cone does not establish nonnegativity of its facets on all actual surfaces.

## 3. Approach 2: all nef complete-intersection surface classes

Using corrected van der Geer v3, put t_j=degree(L^(6-j) M^j). Direct binomial expansion of M=12L-D gives

(t_0,...,t_6)=(1/181440,1/15120,1/1260,41/5040,1/21,73/336,871/1008).

Similarly, b_j=degree(beta_2 L^(4-j) M^j) for j=0,...,4 is

(0,0,0,11/48,83/48).

The source inputs are the degree arrays for L^(6-j)D^j and beta_2 L^(4-j)D^j, specified and attributed in verify.py. They use D=sigma_1 and beta_2=sigma_2. The relevant corrected tables were visually inspected.

**Proposition 2.** Every product H1.H2.H3.H4 of four nef real divisor classes belongs to C. Every nonzero such product lies in the interior of C.

**Proof.** Shepherd–Barron's nef-divisor theorem, as stated in the controlling primary sources, writes H_i=a_i L+b_i M with a_i,b_i>=0. It suffices to treat the five monomials Q_j=L^(4-j)M^j. Their coordinates are (t_j,t_(j+1),t_(j+2),b_j). The five facet values are all strictly positive, as certified exactly in results.json. For clarity the p,q,g values in order j=0,...,4 are:

| j | p | q | g |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1/360 | 1/1512 | 1/504 |
| 1 | 7/240 | 11/1680 | 11/560 |
| 2 | 11/60 | 5/126 | 43/420 |
| 3 | 13/15 | 27/140 | 367/840 |
| 4 | 7/2 | 52/63 | 283/168 |

The first two facets are positive t_j,t_(j+1). Proposition 1 supplies explicit nonnegative decompositions. Expanding the four factors yields a nonnegative linear combination of Q_j. If no H_i is zero the product expansion has at least one positive coefficient, so every facet remains strictly positive. Products with a zero factor vanish. This proves the assertion for arbitrary real nef divisors. No assertion that every effective surface is such a complete intersection is used. QED.

**Gap:** the cone generated by nef complete intersections need not exhaust the effective cone; the five proposed extremal surfaces themselves lie on its boundary and are not represented by the positive interior products above.

## 4. Approach 3: contracted effective faces and the closure obstruction

The proof of Grushevsky–Hulek Proposition 7.3 establishes that every effective surface class with x=y=0 belongs to cone(C0,K). Its geometric input classifies surfaces over points of the Satake compactification: genus-two fibers, rank-two boundary fibers with their multiplicative action, and the toric rank-three fiber. We use this as a credited theorem, not as a new classification.

For any such class the exact coefficients are

c=(z-w)/4, k=(3z+w)/4,

so -3z<=w<=z. Thus the closure of the **already contracted effective cone** is known. This does not identify overline(Eff_2(X)) intersect {x=y=0}, since taking the closure and then taking a face can add limits.

The following explicit convex model verifies the logical issue. Let v_n=(1/n^2,1/n,1,2), n>=6, and let E be the cone of finite nonnegative sums from C and the v_n. Since every v_n has x,y>0, E intersect {x=y=0}=cone(C0,K). The five old rays remain extreme in E: rays F,T,C0,K have x=0, precluding any v_n in their decompositions; A has y=z=0, also precluding a v_n. Yet v_n tends to v_infty=(0,0,1,2), outside cone(C0,K), and

K=(4/5)v_infty+(1/5)C0.

Consequently K is not extreme in the closure of E. Every v_n satisfies x,y,z>0 and y^2=xz, so even those familiar individual-surface numerical tests do not rule out this abstract limit mechanism.

**Gap:** this is an unrealized cone model. It demonstrates why the effective-face and extremality arguments alone cannot settle the closed cone; it proves no additional effective or pseudoeffective class exists on X.

## 5. Approach 4: theta localization repairs the two-nef-class criterion

Let V be the boundary locus over the decomposable genus-two locus, as in the primary paper. Grushevsky–Hulek determine the surface cones of Z and V; their images lie in C. Their Remark 9.2 gives the following geometric input: for an irreducible effective surface S, the inequality 3z-8y<0 forces S to lie in Z union V. This uses the theta-null divisor and a second modular-form divisor, whose common support is Z union V.

The elementary negativity step is valid with a nef divisor: if an irreducible surface is not contained in an effective divisor E, the restriction E|S is an effective curve, so its intersection with the nef class M is nonnegative. Thus a negative intersection forces containment. Applying this to the two effective divisors with classes proportional to M-3L and 3M-8L gives the stated common-support conclusion, using y>=0. We retain the primary source's modular-form/base-locus calculation as an explicit dependency.

**Proposition 3.** With that credited localization input, nefness of F2 implies nefness of G. Therefore the original five-ray conjecture is equivalent to simultaneous nefness of F1 and F2, even though the printed dual-cone formula is not the polar of the displayed ray table.

**Proof.** It suffices to test an irreducible effective S. If 3z<8y, the localization gives S in Z union V, hence [S] in C, and Proposition 1 gives G.S>=0. Otherwise z>=8y/3 and

G.S=F2.S+2z-4y >= F2.S+(4/3)y >=0.

Extend by addition and continuity. If F1,F2 are nef, G is therefore nef, as are L^2 and LM. Proposition 1 puts every effective class in C and closedness completes the equality. The converse follows by evaluating on C using the nonnegative matrix in Section 2. QED.

**Gap:** no proof of the universal nefness of F1 or F2 is obtained. In particular, the proposition does not establish G nef without its stated assumption. For a hypothetical irreducible counterexample outside C we have 3z>=8y; if F1 and F2 were both nonnegative on it, the proposition would already put it in C.

## 6. Approach 5: numerical searches for additional effective directions

A natural counterexample search asks for numerical classes outside C while preserving easy necessary tests. Two exact targets are

v_-=(1,3,9,0), v_+=(1,4,16,57).

They satisfy x,y,z,w>=0, the Hodge-index equality y^2=xz, and all the following divisor-intersection inequalities:

12x-y>=0, 12y-z>=0, y-3x>=0, z-3y>=0, 3y-8x>=0, 3z-8y>=0.

The first pair comes from D, the second from the theta-null class, and the third from the moving modular divisor, in the cases where the surface is not contained in the relevant support. We only test these inequalities; no sufficiency assertion is made. Nonnegativity of w is an extra test, not a universal effectivity criterion for all surfaces.

Their (p,q,g) values are respectively (-9,57,63) and (81,-1,15). Thus either would contradict the proposed cone if realized by an effective class. Adjoining either vector to C preserves the five original extreme rays: x=0 blocks its use in decompositions of F,T,C0,K, and y=0 blocks its use for A. Hence knowing those five rays are extreme does not eliminate either added direction.

No realization was found. The nef complete-intersection family is excluded by Section 3; the credited exceptional loci and exactly contracted effective face are excluded by Sections 4–5. These rational targets do not define algebraic surfaces, and their Hodge equality is not a construction.
