# Exact dependency scope

The universal proof is mathematical, not computational. It uses the usual finite-field classification of nondegenerate alternating and quadratic forms, nondegeneracy of finite-field trace pairings, Witt extension for nondegenerate subspaces, and the displayed full-isometry order formulas. These are standard results in D. E. Taylor, The Geometry of the Classical Groups, 1992, corrected author edition https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/don/papers/gcg.pdf. The original-priority report conventions use natural matrix groups and sufficiently-large-dimension, fixed-confidence sample complexity, as in Leedham-Green–O'Brien, J. Algebra 322 (2009),833–881, DOI10.1016/j.jalgebra.2009.04.028, Sections1.1–1.2,2.1.

The class-generation proof imports two precise quantitative results:

1. T. C. Burness, Fixed point ratios in actions of finite classical groups, I, J. Algebra309(2007),69–79, DOI10.1016/j.jalgebra.2006.05.024; Definitions1–2, Theorem1 and Corollary1. For a non-subspace maximal action of an almost simple classical group on a natural e-space and a prime-order x, fpr(x)<|x^S|^(-1/2+1/e+iota). In the active large symplectic/orthogonal cases, Table1 gives 0<=iota<=1/(e-2). The small-dimensional cases and the characteristic-two orthogonal subspace exception are outside the manuscript. Source https://seis.bristol.ac.uk/~tb13602/docs/fpr1.pdf. These statement bodies, definitions and correction table were inspected. This does not constitute an independent reproof of the four-part published fixed-point-ratio theory or CFSG.
2. M. W. Liebeck, L. Pyber and A. Shalev, On a conjecture of G. E. Wall, J. Algebra317(2007),184–197, DOI10.1016/j.jalgebra.2006.10.047, Theorem1.1. The number of maximal subgroups of a finite simple classical group of rank r is at most C*r^(-2/3)*|S| for an absolute C. Source https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~mwl/WALL.PDF. Introduction and exact theorem statement were inspected, not every step of its17-page proof. No numerical C is certified here. Consequently the generation threshold e0 depending on C is existential.

Taylor Theorems8.7–8.8 supply perfectness and simplicity for Sp and its scalar quotient in the large dimensions used. Theorems11.24,11.45–11.48,11.50–11.51 supply Siegel transformations in Omega, derived-group/perfectness/simple-quotient and determinant/spinor kernel descriptions in odd characteristic. Printed pages72,74,150,160–165 and the relevant statement/proof surroundings were inspected; the full237-page book was not reaudited. The natural representation centraliser and the kernel bound <=2 are proved in the manuscript, not assumed from a possibly inapplicable projective statement.

Praeger–Seress2011 DOI10.1515/JGT.2010.061 is historical attribution for class-random involution generation, not an active scope import. Its exact original full proof was not recovered here. The new 192-class argument is supplied to remove that dependency. DPS2018 and GPRD2020 are credited antecedents, not substitutes for the Sp/O marginals. GPRD Section11.1 supplies the earlier linear/unitary generation and independent diagonal-escape strategy; the actual graph bridge here treats full central covers and every simple quotient graph separately.

No installation, network or package is required to execute the exact finite diagnostics or frozen-PDF release reconstruction. LaTeX compilation is separate. No finite checker is offered as a proof of the published standard dependencies.
