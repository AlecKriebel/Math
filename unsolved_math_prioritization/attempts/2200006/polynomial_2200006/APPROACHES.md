# Five substantive approach families

All five concern the exact Problem 3; none determines its full extremal function.
The count records mathematical approaches, not tool calls or conversational
messages. Standard methods and credited prior constructions are not presented
as new discoveries.

1. **Common-zero ideals and degree-k Bézout.** Reducing an SOS zero to common
   zeros of its summands is exact. Generic linear combinations give the
   conjectured bound when the complex common-zero set is finite. The missing
   hypothesis is material: the credited quartic has a one-dimensional complex
   common-zero scheme and 1,152 isolated real zeros. Perturbing the original
   degree-k equations cannot be assumed to preserve each real zero.

2. **Morse perturbation and signed gradient degree.** Preserve one local minimum
   near each isolated zero while adding a small coercive degree-2k term and a
   generic linear perturbation. Bézout bounds the perturbed critical points;
   the degree-one gradient forces their positive/negative orientation balance.
   This proves (M(k,l)\le((2k-1)^l+1)/2), but leaves a large gap, including
   (1152\le M(2,10)\le29525).

3. **Real interval selectors.** Independently verify DannyExperiments' explicit
   1,152-point quartic and the full even-degree family. Exact sign partitioning,
   root counts by IVT and degree, and degenerate boundary cases are covered.
   This is an existing refutation of Conjecture 4, not a new answer to Problem 3.

4. **Cartesian products and restricted optimization.** Combine finite SOS zero
   sets in disjoint variables. A recurrence computes the optimal count within
   the available quartic selector/grid block library, with exhaustive partition
   cross-checks. The restriction to this library cannot certify the actual
   extremum. Positive multipliers handle exact-degree transfer separately.

5. **Projective reformulation and later low-dimensional claims.** Homogenize the
   explicit quartic and prove directly that it has no real points at infinity.
   Hence projectivization does not eliminate the obstruction. Search for later
   results locates a 30 September 2026 unrefereed note advertising low-dimensional
   theorems; its PDF is unavailable in this pass and those claims are withheld
   from the proved-result list. Its general-open wording is incompatible with
   the already verified higher-dimensional counterexample, but this alone says
   nothing about the correctness of its separately advertised small cases.
