# Turn 1: a credited full-scope smoothing argument

Problem 30002865 / OWR-13678-006. Author candidate, pending independent review. One substantive turn; no novelty or priority claim.

## 1. Exact claim and provenance

Let R=C[x_1,...,x_d], let P:R→R be a complex-linear idempotent of finite rank N>0, and suppose I=ker P is an ideal generated by d polynomials. Set G=ran P. We prove that there are Lagrange projectors P_n:R→G such that P_n f converges coefficientwise in the fixed finite-dimensional space G to Pf for every f∈R. Consequently (P_n f)(z)→(Pf)(z) for every f and z∈C^d. This is precisely the Hermite condition in the original source.

The original is Shekhtman's contribution to OWR21/2015, printed pp1182–1184: Definition5 and Conjecture1, with Definition2 and Theorem3 fixing the meaning of Hermite and smoothability. The later paper [S] Definition1.2 confirms pointwise convergence for every polynomial and evaluation point, always onto the same range. Its Theorem3.1 proves a restricted version under an additional condition at infinity.

The essential established input is [CJN], Section2D, p1533: every affine zero-dimensional complete intersection over C is smoothable in the **finite-flat** sense. This is an external theorem, not a discovery of this packet. That section explains the affine case by realizing the scheme as a union of components of a projective complete intersection, using projective smoothing and preservation of smoothability under direct factors. In particular it does not require a chosen affine generating system to avoid infinity. Its field convention includes C. We rely on this stated classical input, not on the paper's Gorenstein classification or its other long proofs.

The rest of this turn writes out the passage from an abstract smoothing algebra to a sequence of interpolation operators for the original embedding and arbitrary original range. It is also consistent with the already-stated equivalence in OWR Theorem3. The contribution here is an explicit, credited reconstruction of that bridge and an exact example, not a claim to have first proved the conjecture.

## 2. Fixed-range bridge lemma

**Lemma.** Let A=R/I have complex dimension N>0. Suppose A admits a finite flat algebraic smoothing: there is an irreducible finite-type complex base S with a point s_0 and a finite flat family of rank N whose fiber at s_0 is A and whose fibers are reduced on a nonempty Zariski open subset U⊆S. Then every linear complement G to I in R is the range of a Hermite projector with kernel I.

Here and below the family may be restricted to a neighborhood of s_0; taking the reduced base does not alter any fibers or destroy finite flatness. Finite-type algebraic families are the usual convention for smoothability. If a broader base convention is used, the finite-type reduction can be made explicit. A local frame sends the original base to the affine scheme of N-dimensional unital commutative algebra structures, defined by finitely many unit, symmetry and associativity equations in the structure constants and unit coordinates. Take the reduced irreducible closure of its image. The universal rank-N algebra on that closure has the prescribed special algebra. The nondegeneracy of its trace form is an open condition, equivalent over C to being reduced; this open set is nonempty because the original general algebra is reduced. This produces a finite-type smoothing of the same special algebra. Thus the finite-type and Euclidean sequence step does not hide an additional embedding hypothesis.

**Proof.** Choose a basis g_1,...,g_N of G. The classes [g_j] form a basis of A since R=I⊕G. After restricting to an affine neighborhood of s_0, write the smoothing as a finite locally free algebra B over its coordinate ring. Choose sections u_i∈B specializing to [x_i]∈A. Such lifts exist because B→B⊗κ(s_0) is the quotient map of modules.

Put v_j=g_j(u_1,...,u_d)∈B. On the special fiber these are precisely the basis [g_j]. In any local trivialization of B, their determinant is nonzero at s_0. Restrict to the open neighborhood where it is nonzero. Hence v_1,...,v_N are now a frame of B on this neighborhood, not merely a basis on a single fiber.

For each f∈R there are unique regular functions c_j(f,s) such that

    f(u_1,...,u_d) = Σ_j c_j(f,s) v_j.

For a complex point s in the neighborhood, define

    P_s f = Σ_j c_j(f,s) g_j ∈ G.

Polynomial evaluation in the commuting elements u_i gives a C-algebra map q_s:R→B_s. This map is surjective: every element of B_s is a linear combination of the v_j(s), and each v_j(s) lies in its image. In particular no assumption that a random lift remains a generating tuple has been omitted.

For f=g_k the coefficient vector is the k-th unit vector. Thus P_s is linear and fixes G, so P_s²=P_s and ran P_s=G. Moreover P_s f=0 exactly when q_s f=0, making its kernel an ideal. At s_0 we have q_(s_0)(f)=[f]. Therefore P_(s_0) is the unique projector with range G and kernel I, namely the given P.

For s∈U, the finite-dimensional reduced algebra B_s is isomorphic to C^N. Let χ_1,...,χ_N be its distinct characters and set

    z_r(s) = (χ_r(u_1(s)),...,χ_r(u_d(s))) ∈ C^d.

These N points are distinct. Indeed, equal coordinate vectors would make two characters agree on every polynomial in the u_i(s), hence on all of B_s by surjectivity, a contradiction. Applying χ_r to the defining coefficient equality gives

    (P_s f)(z_r(s)) = f(z_r(s)).

Evaluation G→C^N at these points is an isomorphism: it is the composition of the basis isomorphism G→B_s with the character isomorphism B_s→C^N. Thus P_s is exactly the unique Lagrange projector onto G at those N distinct points. No ordering of the points continuous in s is needed.

A nonempty Zariski open subset of an irreducible finite-type complex variety is Euclidean dense. In particular there are complex points s_n∈U in the chosen neighborhood with s_n→s_0. One may alternatively select an algebraic curve through s_0 meeting U and approach s_0 along its normalization; if the base is zero-dimensional then A is already reduced. Regular functions are continuous in this topology. For every fixed f and j,

    c_j(f,s_n) → c_j(f,s_0).

Consequently P_(s_n)f→Pf coefficientwise in G, hence pointwise at every z, and even uniformly on every fixed compact subset of C^d. The sequence s_n is chosen once, independently of f or z; separate pointwise continuity statements use that same sequence. This proves the required quantifiers. ∎

## 3. Application to the complete-intersection kernel

Since P is a projector, R=I⊕G and dim_C R/I=N. Thus I is a proper zero-dimensional ideal. Its generation by d elements is exactly the affine complete-intersection convention in the source. The theorem from [CJN] supplies the finite flat smoothing required by the lemma. Applying the lemma proves the full claim in Section1 for every d and every N, with arbitrary nonreduced structure, any finite number of support points and every original complement G.

The proof does not deform the chosen generators by subtracting arbitrary constants. Such a deformation can have a different total number of affine solutions. Finite flatness maintains rank N, and the frame determinant selects precisely a neighborhood on which the original range remains unisolvent.

For an already radical I, P is itself Lagrange and a constant sequence suffices. Rank zero can be treated by the usual empty-node convention, but the proper zero-dimensional complete-intersection formulation has N>0. The case d=0 is trivial. No assertion over the real field, no rationality of nodes, no convergence rate, and no operator norm on the entire infinite-dimensional polynomial ring is needed or claimed.

## 4. Exact example outside the no-infinity hypothesis

Work in C[x,y] and let

    q(y)=y²(y−1)²(y−2),   I=(x−y², q(y)).

Then R/I≅C[y]/(q) has dimension5. It has double structures at (0,0) and (1,1), and a simple point at (4,2). These three points are noncollinear. The leading forms of the displayed generators are −y² and y⁵, so they share the nonzero vector (1,0) at infinity.

In fact I has no two-generator presentation without a common root at infinity. For such a presentation (h_1,h_2), Bezout's length formula ([S], Theorem2.6) would give deg(h_1)deg(h_2)=5, so one generator would be linear. A nonzero linear polynomial cannot vanish at all three noncollinear support points. This optional observation concerns only this illustrative example; the main proof already covers all complete intersections.

For 0<t<1/2 define

    q_t(y)=y(y−t)(y−1)(y−1−t)(y−2),
    I_t=(x−y²,q_t(y)).

Its five nodes (r²,r), for r=0,t,1,1+t,2, are distinct. The monic degree of q_t is5 for every t, so these quotients have the constant free basis 1,y,y²,y³,y⁴, including at t=0. This is an explicit finite-flat smoothing.

To stress that the original range need not be that monomial span, take the fixed space

    G=span{1,y,y²,y³,y⁴+q(y)}.

At t=0 this is a complement to I. Modulo q_t, the first four basis vectors are unchanged, while y⁴+q(y) has fourth-degree coefficient 1+2t: both q and q_t are monic degree5, and the difference of their y⁴ coefficients is 2t. Thus the quotient frame determinant equals 1+2t.

For an arbitrary bivariate f, first replace x by y² and divide by the monic q_t. Express its remainder in the above quotient frame. These are exactly the coefficients of P_t f in the fixed G. They are rational functions of t with denominator a power of 1+2t (indeed only the first power in this explicit conversion) and regular at0, so P_t f→P_0f as t→0. At positive small t this is the five-node Lagrange projector.

Shrinking the neighborhood really matters: at t=−1/2 the nodes are still distinct, but the chosen range loses unisolvence because 1+2t=0. This is a useful negative control, not a failure of smoothability or of the lemma.

## 5. Scope of executable checks

`verify_turn1.py` uses exact rational polynomial arithmetic from Python's standard library. It checks the example's factorization, length frame, generator relations, fixed-range projection, interpolation, idempotence and the singular-range negative control over explicitly bounded rational parameters and monomials. It also verifies arbitrary deterministic linear combinations. The output records every bound and assertion count.

These finite checks do not prove the general smoothability theorem, do not certify a convergence rate and do not replace the all-polynomial lemma. The full argument is Sections2–3, using the explicitly credited external theorem.
