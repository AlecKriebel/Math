## Executable checks and limits

The [independent replay](INDEPENDENT_REPLAY.json) reran all 19 delivered
checkers/diagnostics in isolated, nonoptimized Python. Its parsed output is
identical to the shipped CHECK_REPLAY.json. Assertions were enabled; an
execution-flags probe verified that setting. The runner measured that its
source tree was unchanged. The finite four-time computation reproduces all
180 costs and divisor triples, including maximum 34777/50976 at (2,2,2).
The one-dimensional certificate replay checks all 729 six-move words, and the
separate adaptive-preload diagnostic checks all 729 three-move words.

[Sixty-seven control tests](TAMPER_REVIEW.json) passed. These cover valid
manifest verification in normal/-O/-OO modes; optimized-runner refusal;
file changes, truncation, deletion and extras; manifest hash, size, duplicate,
omission and unsafe-path changes; extra directories, live/dangling/directory
links and a FIFO; directly corrupted four-time and one-dimensional
certificates; and a broken relative link even after its file hash was updated.
The replay runner also rejected six malformed one-dimensional certificates.
A symlink-detection weakness found before freezing was corrected and retested.
No post-freeze public correction was needed.

The package hash checker is an integrity tool, not a signature or Markdown
link checker. A party replacing both a file and the manifest can make their
new pair internally consistent; the external manifest/archive pins above
are therefore essential. The separately tested link audit detects the
rehashed broken link. These selected negative controls do not certify every
possible malformed input.

Finite diagnostics do not replace the analytic proofs and their quantifier
checks. The 180-case computation and one-dimensional certificate are scoped
proof components; the remaining selected computations are supplementary.
The accepted package establishes neither an unrestricted winning strategy
nor a current-literature openness or originality theorem.
