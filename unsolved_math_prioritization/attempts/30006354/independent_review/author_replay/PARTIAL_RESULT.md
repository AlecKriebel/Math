# SU(3)₁ and Haah's net: exact Pauli diagnostics and the locality gap

**30006354 / OWR-14299511-003. Original target unresolved; proposed unsolved 2/5. Separate review pending.**

The original asks for an actual two-sided bounded-spread isomorphism between two two-dimensional nets. We verify the concrete Pauli presentation and its local complementary factorization, give a general finite-window block certificate, and distinguish these facts from the missing comparison map. The invertibility mechanism is Haah's prior result, not a new discovery. No human peer review or priority claim is made.

## 1. Exact source and current literature

Penneys's report in *Subfactors and Applications*, OWR34/2025, printed pp.1839–1842, states Conjecture2 on p.1840. It asks whether the SU(3)₁ braided fusion spin system, possibly with the reversed braiding, is bounded-spread isomorphic to the specific qutrit edge-lattice algebra generated by the two clock-and-shift patterns in Haah's Figure1(a,b). The generator diagrams and X,Z matrices were visually inspected. They are not commuting stabilizer generators, and the conjecture is not merely about matching anyon types.

The modern precise construction is Jones–Naaijkens–Penneys (JNP), arXiv:2506.19969v3, Section4 and Definition4.3. For a unitary braided fusion category B and site object X, the local algebra of a disk-like finite region R is End_B(X^{tensor R}); geometric inclusions are formed by braided tensoring with identities. Their Definition4.12 requires a single s independent of the region such that a quasi-local *-isomorphism alpha obeys

\[
\alpha(A(\Lambda))\subseteq B(\Lambda^{+s}),\qquad
\alpha^{-1}(B(\Lambda))\subseteq A(\Lambda^{+s})
\]

for every bounded contractible region. An abstract isomorphism of inductive limits, a correspondence of individual finite-dimensional algebras, a stable equivalence, and an equivalence of sector categories are not this conclusion.

The Walker–Wang boundary construction in JNP uses the sum of simple objects as its site generator. The report describes a strong generator without restating it in Conjecture2. The finite fusion-algebra diagnostic below explicitly uses the standard sum-of-simples choice; no statement for every different generator or site multiplicity is inferred.

A recent source update matters: Faurot–Li–Penneys–Sherman, arXiv:2609.20725v1, TheoremB, proves that the sector category of the braided net is braided equivalent to Hilb(B)^op, with the precise opposite-category convention in that paper. This addresses the report's adjacent Conjecture1 in its formulated conventions. It does not give the Conjecture2 isomorphism to Haah's particular net. JNP v3, dated September21, now points to this result. We inspected the full primary texts and the exact theorem, but do not independently recertify the Connes-fusion proof. No corresponding bounded-spread comparison theorem was recovered.

Haah's *Invertible subalgebras*, CMP403 (2023),661–698, arXiv:2211.02086, Sections4–5, already proves invertibility and a fourth-tensor-power trivialization for this example. The source's abstract algebra is the finitely supported operator algebra; one can take the norm completion for comparison with the C*-net. The local constructions below respect both versions. The JNP edge-restricted Levin–Wen comparison in Section4.2 concerns a Drinfeld-center/Lagrangian-object construction and must not be substituted for this SU(3)₁ example.

## 2. Source Laurent matrix and a fully explicit complement

Let R=F_3[x^{±1},y^{±1}], with bar sending x,y to their inverses. In the unit-cell convention of Haah equations(30)–(31), put

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
x^{-1}-x & x+xy-y+1\\
-x^{-1}+y^{-1}-x^{-1}y^{-1}-1 & y-y^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},\quad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here K is the source's Xi. Direct multiplication gives bar(K)^t=-K and det K=4=1 in F_3. The determinant is already identically4 over the integer Laurent ring. Therefore

\[
K^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}K_{22}&-K_{12}\\-K_{21}&K_{11}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{over }F_3.
\]

Both K and its inverse have Laurent exponents in [-1,1]^2. Write

\[
V_+=\begin{pmatrix}I_2\\K/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V_-=\begin{pmatrix}I_2\\-K/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The columns and all their translates give the Pauli generators of A and its conjugate complementary algebra A_-, respectively. This is the source's equation(30) prescription for Figure1, not a replacement lattice model inferred from the picture. Both have an identity X-block, so their generator monomials are linearly independent modulo phases.

For a Pauli vector (u,z) with upper X and lower Z exponents, use W(u,z)=X^u Z^z. Since ZX=zeta XZ, the commutator exponent of W(v),W(w) is minus the constant coefficient of bar(v)^t J w. This overall sign has no effect on orthogonality, centers, or ranks, but is recorded to avoid choosing a chirality from a sign convention.

We have
\[
\bar V_+^tJV_+=K,\quad \bar V_-^tJV_-=-K,\quad
\bar V_+^tJV_-=0.
\]

More explicitly, for every finitely supported physical vector (u,z), its unique decomposition is

\[
(u,z)=V_+a+V_-b,\qquad
 a=u/2+K^{-1}z,\quad b=u/2-K^{-1}z. \tag{1}
\]

Thus the two Pauli subalgebras commute, and multiplication identifies their algebraic tensor product with the full finitely supported qutrit operator algebra. Surjectivity follows from(1). For injectivity, distinct pairs(a,b) give distinct physical Pauli vectors; their monomials are linearly independent in the ambient tensor-product operator algebra. Every finite linear combination lies in finite-dimensional C*-algebras, so these mutually inverse *-maps are isometric there and extend to the norm completions, with the minimal tensor product.

The projection onto the first summand is especially simple in F_3:
\[
\Pi=V_+K^{-1}\bar V_+^tJ
=\begin{pmatrix}2I_2&K^{-1}\\K&2I_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \Pi^2=\Pi. \tag{2}
\]

These are local formulas. If the input is physically supported in a set of unit cells S, the coefficient vectors a,b in(1) have support in S^{+1}; applying V_± yields individual factors supported in S^{+2} in the l-infinity cell metric. An operator is a finite linear combination of such monomials, so the same support bound applies termwise. This reconstructs the source's invertible-subalgebra property with an explicit conservative support bound. It identifies A with a factor of a product A tensor A_-, not with the requested SU(3)₁ net. The fourth-power trivialization in Haah Proposition5.1 is credited, not derived here.

## 3. Exact finite-window certificate

Fix any finite list E of distinct generator labels (cell,type) with type in{1,2}, and let A_E be the subalgebra generated by those operators in the infinite lattice. All their physical supports lie in a finite set. Because V_+ has identity X-block, the 3^{|E|} monomials indexed by F_3^E are linearly independent, and form a basis of A_E. There are no unrecorded phase-only multiplicative relations.

Define the alternating matrix C_E over F_3 by
\[
(C_E)_{(r,i),(s,j)}=[x^{r_1-s_1}y^{r_2-s_2}]K_{ij}.
\]
This is the constant coefficient of the corresponding translated pairing; reversing all signs merely changes the commutator convention. Its rank is even, say2q, and put z=|E|-2q. Then

\[
 A_E\cong\bigoplus_{3^z}M_{3^q}(\mathbb C). \tag{3}
\]

Proof: a Pauli monomial with exponent v commutes with every generator exactly when v is in the radical of C_E. Hence the center has basis the 3^z radical monomials. An alternating form over F_3 admits a basis of q symplectic pairs and z radical vectors: choose a nonzero pairing, scale it to one, split off its two-dimensional span and proceed inductively. The operators of each symplectic pair generate M_3, different pairs commute, and the radical generates a commuting algebra of dimension3^z. Every monomial has order3 after an irrelevant cube-root phase normalization. Linear independence gives the asserted tensor-product/block decomposition. This is a standard finite Heisenberg-algebra argument, supplied here as a checkable diagnostic rather than a novelty claim.

The checker computes this matrix for open rectangles of generator *centers*, without periodic wraparound. It also checks periodic quotients separately. These are different finite algebras. Neither finite construction automatically equals the entire net algebra assigned to a physical-support rectangle: boundary-supported products can affect that identification.

For comparison, in a pointed three-simple fusion category with fusion group Z/3 and the sum-of-simples generator T=1+g+g², elementary fusion counting gives
\[
 T^{\otimes N}\cong\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb Z/3}g^r\otimes\mathbb C^{3^{N-1}},\qquad
\operatorname{End}(T^{\otimes N})\cong\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb Z/3}M_{3^{N-1}}(\mathbb C), \ N\ge1. \tag{4}
\]
Each charge occurs equally often, because after N-1 labels there is one last label giving any prescribed total charge. Equation(4) uses only the pointed fusion law; its conclusion is unchanged by reversing the braiding. It is presented as this explicit fusion-law diagnostic, not as a reconstruction of geometric inclusions or a proof of the net conjecture.

Even when finite-dimensional matrix blocks look compatible after adding a boundary collar, individual abstract isomorphisms need not commute with inclusions of regions or have uniformly bounded supports. In particular, finite symplectic elimination can send a local operator to one supported across the whole box. Equation(3) therefore cannot be promoted to a bounded-spread map.

## 4. Why the remaining step is genuinely missing

A solution must specify one compatible *-isomorphism between the two quasi-local nets and a fixed finite bound for both directions on every bounded contractible region. None of the following is a substitute:

- The prior anyon content of Haah's Hamiltonian, or the new sector theorem for the braided category.
- The explicit factorization A tensor A_- into the full qutrit algebra.
- The prior fourth-tensor-power trivialization, which changes the system.
- A finite-window Heisenberg algebra calculation or separate matrix-block identifications.
- A formal ordering of tensor factors without the geometric braid inclusions.

The first research route reconstructs the local Laurent decomposition but stops at the absent categorical comparison map. The second route computes finite algebra invariants and considers matching their blocks; it stops at compatibility and uniform support. No obstruction to the conjectured isomorphism has been proved either. These are two substantive approaches, with the original target unsolved.
