# A relation-incidence proof of low-exponent supersolvability

**Target:** 30004609 / OWR-4990374-015.  
**Status:** claimed_solved; complete candidate, awaiting independent review.  
**Date:** 2026-10-04.  
**Scope:** finite central hyperplane arrangements over a field of characteristic zero. Arrangements are sets of distinct hyperplanes, not multiarrangements. Degrees of the coefficients of derivations are the exponent convention. This note makes no claim of independent verification or established novelty.

## Theorem

Let A be a free central hyperplane arrangement over a field K of characteristic zero. Suppose its nonzero exponents are all 1 or 2, except for at most one exponent 3. Then A is supersolvable.

The formulation using nonzero exponents also permits nonessential arrangements. Essentialization does not change the intersection lattice and removes only zero exponents. Thus it suffices to prove the essential case. Supersolvability means that the intersection lattice, ordered by reverse inclusion, has a maximal chain of modular elements.

## 1. Standard inputs and elementary reductions

We use two standard theorems about free arrangements:

1. Terao factorization: if the exponents are d_1,...,d_r, then the characteristic polynomial is the product of (t-d_i). In particular, n=|A|=sum d_i and the rank-two coefficient is b_2=sum_{i<j}d_i d_j.
2. Yuzvinsky formality: the vector space of linear relations among defining forms of a free arrangement is generated by the relations supported on rank-two flats. Rank one and two are immediate cases.

A product arrangement is free exactly when its factors are free; its exponent multiset is the union of those of the factors. A product is supersolvable exactly when its factors are supersolvable. Therefore it suffices to handle an essential irreducible factor. These product facts also follow from the direct-sum decomposition of normal spaces and logarithmic derivation modules.

For completeness, an irreducible essential arrangement has exactly one exponent 1. Choose r independent defining forms as coordinates. Every degree-one logarithmic derivation is diagonal in these coordinates, and each further defining form is an eigenvector of its dual diagonal linear map. A nonscalar such derivation partitions the defining forms among distinct coordinate eigenspaces, giving a product decomposition. Conversely, each nonempty product factor contributes its Euler derivation. An essential arrangement has no degree-zero logarithmic derivation. Thus an irreducible factor of rank r>=2 has exponents either

- (1,2,...,2), with n=2r-1, or
- (1,2,...,2,3), with n=2r.

Rank-one factors are supersolvable.

Choose a nonzero defining form a_e for every hyperplane e. Let E be their index set, and let

R = ker(K^E -> V*, (c_e) -> sum_e c_e a_e).

Since the arrangement is essential, dim R=n-r. A line will mean the complete set of indices whose forms lie in a given two-dimensional span, with at least three indices. Distinct lines meet in at most one index. If a line L has m elements, its local relation space R_L, supported on L, has dimension m-2. Formality says R=sum_L R_L.

Construct a bipartite incidence graph G: one vertex for every element of E and one vertex for every line L; join e to L exactly when e belongs to L.

**Connectedness.** G is connected for an irreducible formal arrangement. Indeed, all local relations are supported within components. Formality therefore decomposes R as the direct sum of the relation spaces on the components. It follows that V* is the direct sum of the spans of their defining forms. More explicitly, any equality among vectors in different component spans induces a relation in R, and its restriction to each component is itself a relation, so every component vector is zero. Multiple components would therefore give a product decomposition. This also rules out isolated element vertices when r>=2.

## 2. Rank-two multiplicities and counts

If every exponent is at most d, then every rank-two flat has multiplicity at most d+1. Here is a proof sufficient for our use. Suppose m hyperplanes contain a rank-two flat, and choose coordinates so their forms are x,y,x+c_1 y,...,x+c_{m-2}y, with the nonzero c_i distinct. For a homogeneous logarithmic derivation theta of degree at most d, write theta(x)=xP and theta(y)=yQ. Tangency to x+c_i y implies that x+c_i y divides P-Q. If m-2>d-1, the product of these m-2 distinct linear forms has degree greater than P-Q, so P=Q. Applying this to a homogeneous free basis of D(A) makes its first two rows proportional over K(V). That contradicts independence of the basis over K(V). Thus m<=d+1.

We also use

binom(n,2)-b_2 = sum_L binom(|L|-1,2).

Every unordered pair of hyperplanes belongs to exactly one rank-two flat, and a flat of multiplicity m contributes m-1 to b_2. Subtracting m-1 from binom(m,2) proves the identity.

### Only exponents 1 and 2

All lines have size three. Write u for their number. Factorization and the identity give

n=2r-1,  b_2=2(r-1)^2,  u=r-1.

The connected graph G has n+u vertices and 3u edges, so its cycle-space dimension is

beta(G)=3u-(n+u)+1=0.

Hence G is a tree.

### Exactly one exponent 3

All lines have size three or four. Let u and v be their respective counts. Then

n=2r,  b_2=2r^2-2r-1,  u+3v=r+1.

Formality supplies the crucial additional inequality

r=dim R <= sum_L dim R_L = u+2v = r+1-v.

Consequently v<=1.

If v=1, then u=r-2. The connected incidence graph has 2r+u+1=3r-1 vertices and 3u+4=3r-2 edges. It is a tree, with one four-element line vertex.

If v=0, then u=r+1. The connected incidence graph has 3r+1 vertices and 3r+3 edges. Its cycle-space dimension is exactly 3. Choose one nonzero relation rho_L on each triple line. These r+1 vectors span an r-dimensional space, so they are dependent.

## 3. The only possible cyclic core

Assume v=0 in the one-3 case. Choose an inclusion-minimal dependent set T of the triple-relation vectors, of size t. The dependence has nonzero coefficient on every member of T. Since every coordinate of a relation on a triple is nonzero, cancellation implies that every element appearing in these triples occurs at least twice among them.

Let s be the number of these elements, and let G_T be the incidence subgraph consisting of the t triples and their s elements, with all their incidence edges. Minimality makes G_T connected: otherwise the dependence splits over disjoint supports into smaller dependences. Its 3t edges give

s<=3t/2,  beta(G_T)=3t-(s+t)+1=2t-s+1.

A subgraph cannot have greater cycle-space dimension than the whole graph, so beta(G_T)<=3. Therefore s>=2t-2, and combining the two bounds gives t<=4.

On the other hand, every one of the t triples must have each of its three elements covered by another triple. A different triple meets it in at most one element, so t-1>=3. Thus t>=4.

It follows that t=4, s=6, every element has incidence degree two in G_T, and each pair of triples meets in one distinct element. Label the four triples 1,2,3,4, and label their six elements e_ij for 1<=i<j<=4. Triple i is precisely {e_ij: j!=i}. Thus G_T is the incidence graph of the vertices and edges of K_4, with the triple vertices playing the role of graph vertices. It has cycle-space dimension 3, exhausting that of G.

Every connected component outside this core is consequently a tree attached to the core at exactly one vertex. One way to see this is to contract the connected core to one vertex: contraction preserves the difference beta(G)-beta(G_T)=0, so the resulting connected multigraph is a tree. Since each core triple already has all three of its incidence edges within the core, attachments occur only at its element vertices.

## 4. Removing a terminal pencil preserves supersolvability

We give the linear-algebra justification for pruning the trees, rather than assuming that arbitrary deletions remain free or formal.

Suppose L={a,p_1,...,p_{m-1}} is a line of size m>=3 in a formal configuration, and each p_i occurs in no other line. Put E'=E\{p_1,...,p_{m-1}}, so the attachment element a remains. Let R' be the relation space of E'.

The local relation spaces other than R_L are supported on E'. Moreover, R_L intersects K^{E'} trivially: a relation in that intersection would be supported only on the one nonzero form a. Formality therefore implies

R=R_L direct-sum R',  dim R'=dim R-(m-2),

and R' is generated by the remaining local relation spaces. In particular E' is formal and its rank is r-1.

Write W=span{a_e:e in E'} and U=span{a_e:e in L}. No removed form a_{p_i} lies in W. If it did, a relation supported on E' together with p_i would, after subtracting relations supported on E', yield a local relation supported on {a,p_i}; those two forms are independent. This is impossible. Thus W is a hyperplane of V*, U has dimension two, and U intersect W=K a_a.

Every normal outside W belongs to U. We claim W is a modular flat of the normal matroid. Let F be any subspace generated by a subset of the defining forms. If that subset contains at most one form outside W, then F intersect W is spanned by the forms of the subset inside W. If it contains at least two, those two distinct forms span U, so F intersect W is spanned by the forms of the subset inside W together with a_a. In either case F intersect W is spanned by defining forms. This is the linear-representation criterion for modularity. Also E' is exactly the set of normals lying in W, so it is indeed a flat.

If E' is supersolvable, choose a maximal modular chain within W. Each member C of this chain is modular in the whole matroid: for any flat F, first W intersect F is a flat in W, then C intersect F=C intersect(W intersect F) is a flat. Appending V* therefore gives a maximal modular chain for E. Under the normal-space/intersection-lattice identification this proves supersolvability of A.

## 5. Completion of the proof

For the all-2 case, root the incidence tree at any triple-line vertex. Repeatedly choose a triple-line vertex farthest from the root. Its two outward element vertices are leaves, so Section 4 removes the corresponding two forms. Eventually only the root triple remains, a rank-two configuration, which is supersolvable. Reversing the removals proves the claim.

For the one-3 case with v=1, root the tree at its unique four-element line. The same process removes only terminal triple lines and finishes at a four-element rank-two pencil. Again Section 4 proves supersolvability.

For the one-3 case with v=0, root all attached trees at the K_4 core obtained in Section 3. Every terminal triple outside the core again has two leaf element vertices. Prune all such triples using Section 4. The remaining six-element configuration is formal, with exactly the four indicated triple lines. Its relation space is spanned by their four vectors, whose rank is three because the chosen dependence was minimal. Thus its rank is 6-3=3.

This rank-three configuration is supersolvable. Fix one of its triple lines L_i. Every other triple line meets L_i in an element. A pair of elements not lying in a triple is a pair of disjoint edges of the indexing K_4; it contains exactly one element of L_i. Hence every rank-two flat intersects span(L_i) in a represented point. The triple line is modular. Together with any of its points and the zero and full spaces, it supplies a maximal modular chain. Reattaching the pruned terminal triples preserves supersolvability by Section 4.

All irreducible factors are now handled. Taking their product and undoing essentialization proves the theorem. QED.

## 6. Boundaries and source correction

The proof uses characteristic-zero formality of free arrangements; it is not a claim about arbitrary characteristic, multiarrangements, or free divisors. No inductive-freeness hypothesis is imposed or inferred as a premise. The theorem implies inductive freeness only through the standard implication from supersolvability. It does not assert that every deletion is free.

At most one exponent 3 is essential to this proof. With two exponents 3 the numerical defect changes, and a larger incidence-cycle core is possible; the free nonsupersolvable non-Fano arrangement with exponents (1,3,3) is a standard boundary example.

The target is stated in Tohaneanu's Oberwolfach contribution, printed pages 283-286 of Report 5/2021. The earlier manuscript arXiv:1707.07091v3 contains the same conjecture. Its v4 record was withdrawn on January 16, 2022, explicitly flagging incomplete case enumeration in Sections 4.2.1-4.2.2 and a failed inference in Proposition 4.8(a). None of those claims is a premise of the proof above. The rank-two bound was proved directly in Section 2; the global argument is the relation-incidence calculation.
