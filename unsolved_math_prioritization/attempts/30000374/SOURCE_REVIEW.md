# Source review for ordinal colored dense order preorders

Historical authored review date: 2026-10-10 UTC.

The retrieval, page-inspection and search statements below report the authored review on that date. Edition preparation rechecked frozen identities and verification records, without new scholarly-source retrieval, source-page inspection or literature searching.

## Exact problem

Riccardo Camerlo's *Universal analytic preorders* in Oberwolfach Report 55/2005, printed pages 3127–3129, defines Borel reducibility of relations and the dense order preserving witness maps. Problem 5 on printed page 3128 asks for classification for every countable ordinal with its usual ordinal order. The report does not limit the question to positive, finite, or infinite ordinals. The manuscript consequently treats the empty and singleton spaces explicitly. The formula and neighboring Theorems 3–4 were checked in the PDF and its rendered page.

Public source: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46028
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2005/55

## Dense image equivalence

Alberto Marcone and Christian Rosendal, *The complexity of continuous embeddability between dendrites*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 69 (2004), 663–673, Definition 4.1 and Proposition 4.2, specify density in the least interval containing the image. Their real extension theorem uses a continuous order preserving embedding of the whole real line, with rational points having rational images. The domain is not merely the rational subspace with its topology. These definitions and the proof of the extension proposition were inspected in PDF page 6.

Public author copy: https://users.dimi.uniud.it/~alberto.marcone/CED.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.2178/jsl/1096901760

## Why the known universality theorems do not supply the classification

Riccardo Camerlo, Alberto Marcone and Luca Motto Ros, *Invariantly universal analytic quasi-orders*, Section 5.3, Theorems 5.13–5.14, were inspected in PDF pages 15–18. The current arXiv PDF retrieved during this review has the same hash and size as the supplied source copy.

Theorem 5.13 handles equality colors and reversed natural number colors, and analytic preorders between those two witness relations. With usual natural number colors, constant zero lies below constant one; for reversed comparison it does not. Thus the required upper inclusion fails even on constants. Theorem 5.14 handles two incomparable colors and then color quasiorders with two incomparable elements. A usual ordinal has no such pair. Equality on two colors cannot be replaced by the two element chain merely because both use the same two symbols. For example, monotone comparison permits constant zero to embed below constant one, whereas equality comparison forbids it.

The proofs in the reviewed manuscript therefore do not transfer these universality conclusions to the ordinal case.

Public source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4932

## Analytic sets and trees

Matthew Foreman, Daniel J. Rudolph and Benjamin Weiss, *The conjugacy problem in ergodic theory*, Annals of Mathematics 173 (2011), 1529–1586, Theorem 4 on printed page 1538, records that ill founded trees form a complete analytic set and are not Borel. The surrounding text distinguishes this notion of reduction for sets from reduction for relations. The present proof gives the usual continuous tree section construction as well, so the continuity of the hardness reduction is explicit.

Public publisher PDF: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v173-n3-p07-p.pdf

## Other continuity theorems considered

Raphaël Carroy, Yann Pequignot and Zoltán Vidnyánszky, *Embeddability on functions: order and chaos*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 371 (2019), 6711–6738, Section 5.1, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.3, concern continuous order embeddings for the order topology or topological embeddings of the rationals. The arXiv version's PDF page 19 was inspected. Such an embedding can jump at an irrational cut. The explicit example in the proof shows why this does not establish a better quasiordering result for the stronger dense in convex hull witnesses.

Public source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08341
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/7739

Arnold Beckmann, Martin Goldstern and Norbert Preining, *Continuous Fraïssé Conjecture*, arXiv:math/0411117, concerns countable closed subsets of the real line. Definitions 11–12 on PDF page 3 were inspected, including the labeled version. An arbitrary rational coloring does not have a countable closed underlying real domain or countable closure of each color support. The fixed source compact set in the present proof is deliberately uncountable. No transfer to the full ordinal colored family is claimed.

Public source: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411117

## Search limits and attribution

Additional exact phrase searches combined dense order preserving, countable ordinal, ordinal colors, Camerlo, classification, and the title of Camerlo's earlier colored order paper. They recovered the original problem and the related results above, but no full theorem classifying the exact usual ordinal family was located. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no solution or earlier partial result exists. The partial theorem is presented with a proof and without a novelty claim.

All mathematical implications used in the manuscript are either proved there or tied to the exact primary source statement above. Search snippets and secondary bibliographic pages are not used to establish a mathematical transfer.
