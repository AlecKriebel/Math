# Source assessment and claim boundary

Checked 7 October 2026. No exhaustive priority claim.

## Exact target

Problem 30000717, OWR-1465-011, rank 963, asks for a four-way equivalence for all real polynomials after replacing the gradient ideal. Its catalogue ideal is explicitly generated by x_j f_i−x_i f_j. Item (iii) is an exact SOS representation modulo the ordinary radical; item (iv) requires f+ε to be SOS modulo the original ideal for every ε>0. The proposed replacement applies without an attainment assumption, not only to nonattaining polynomials.

## Primary report inspection

[Reelle Algebraische Geometrie, Oberwolfach Reports 14/2007](https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2007/14), [publisher PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46100): printed pp.798, 810, and 811 were rendered and inspected visually. Page 810's Theorem 1 has the ordinary radical and the exact/epsilon distinction. Page 811's Conjecture 2 displays separate cross-products with commas and an ellipsis, not a minus sign. The catalogue has changed this to differences. We do not silently identify them or assert an author-approved correction. The candidate treats both readings and refutes both.

Robinson's sextic is credited, with its formula already appearing in Reznick's contribution on p.798, equation (2). Its nonnegativity and ten-zero obstruction are established classical facts. The candidate supplies a self-contained proof, then applies the zero-line/axis obstruction to the proposed certificates. It does not claim discovery of the sextic.

Bounded searches for the exact report/conjecture, tangency-ideal counterexamples, and Robinson/tangency combinations did not locate a prior statement of this precise counterexample. That absence is not evidence of historical novelty or of exhaustive coverage through the check date. Classical ingredients remain credited.

## What the candidate settles

For the catalogue tangency-minor formulation, R≥0 and R≥0 on its real tangency variety, but R+ε is not SOS modulo even its real vanishing ideal for any ε≥0. Hence both proposed certificate implications fail. The proof also applies to the literal product ideal. A separate elementary Q example shows that the literal product reading additionally loses (ii)⇒(i), whereas the catalogue's (i)⇔(ii) holds by sphere minimization.

The main argument requires no radical computation, numerical optimization, degree cutoff, or nonattainment of infimum. Independent review is a separate step.
