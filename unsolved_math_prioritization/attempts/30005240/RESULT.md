# Result: boundary-density periodic asymptotics, 30005240

**Original status: unresolved after five substantive author turns.** The target is the actual-density, many-reflection high-frequency periodic rate described in Ecevit's OWR contribution, with its phase, illuminated-domain and remainder qualifications. It is not simply the already-established fixed-reflection boundary-layer expansion. No full source-specific theorem or counterexample is claimed.

## Proved scoped results

1. **A source simplification is algebraically inconsistent.** For the normal two-bounce orbit, the printed continued-fraction recurrence and the independently calculated linearized billiard matrix give q=sqrt(A)−sqrt(A−1), A=(1+d kappa_0)(1+d kappa_1). The positive shortcut printed in the OWR and the earlier manuscript differs. The recurrence-defined general optical product is retained. This corrects a simplification; it does not prove the actual-density rate.
2. **A uniform abstract dominant-mode criterion.** A weighted contracting return map can be conjugated to composition. A small same-space operator perturbation, together with explicitly quantified initial excitation, gives a uniform rank-one decomposition and O(1/k) quotient after logarithmically many returns. The proof is direct, with stated constants. The physical strong-norm approximation remains unproved.
3. **A complete analytic Gaussian model.** Holomorphic interior contraction and a normalized centered Gaussian average genuinely yield the required same-space estimate. The resulting iterate quotient and its first multiplier correction are proved. These extra analytic/model assumptions have not been transferred to general smooth obstacles.
4. **An exact physical fixed-frequency reduction.** For two smooth disjoint Dirichlet obstacles, the true two-return current is eta_j=B M^j f_0, where M is the compact physical boundary return. A nonzero eigenmode is observed by B on an open dense boundary set. A simple dominant eigenvalue plus excitation and a nonvanishing observed region gives a quantitative current quotient. Its needed high-frequency eigenvalue, projector and observation bounds are stated explicitly rather than assumed solved.
5. **A limited actual-density joint limit.** The source's fixed-iteration expansion, including its original hypotheses, gives some slowly growing reflection window on any fixed stabilized illuminated compact set in which actual density-modulus ratios tend to the optical product. The same is true after removing the exact ray-phase increment. The window has no effective lower growth rate. Replacing the phase by the limiting periodic phase is not justified by this argument. An exponential remainder-growth envelope would give an explicit power-rate criterion, but that envelope is unproved for the PDE.

The proofs also contain several carefully scoped operator countermodels: an all-orders hidden mode, identical resonance sets with different dominant iteration modes, and a finite-rank compact clock family. They expose invalid inference routes; none is a configuration of scattering obstacles.

## Sharp remaining gap

A resolution requires uniform high-frequency control of the physical periodic return: either relative one-period remainder control compatible with the required logarithmic reflection scale, or a justified dominant-mode statement with physical excitation and sufficiently controlled projectors/observations. Fixed-reflection expansions, compactness at each k, and resonance locations alone do not establish it. The strongest global pointwise quotient additionally requires shadow/transition analysis and a treatment of zeros. The limited two-dimensional/two-obstacle results do not settle the three-dimensional or general-period versions.
