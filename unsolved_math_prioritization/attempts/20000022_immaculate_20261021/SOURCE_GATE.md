## Identity and disposition

AIM-ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY-0022, *Immaculate line bundles as unit-orthogonal toric branes*, follows row 1894 (ID 158). Canonical target is AIM Syzygies and mirror symmetry, section 9, problem 9.1: understand the symplectic interpretation of fan criteria for infinitely many line bundles with every cohomology group zero, and formulate consequences/generalizations involving lower-dimensional fan geometry.

**Gate: partial-progress / no new complete solution.** The corpus's report itself says partial_result, proved, low novelty. Its categorical equivalence is a formal transport of RHom(O,L)=RΓ(L); its projective-product examples follow from the standard cohomology window and Künneth. Neither establishes a general geometric mirror criterion, nor necessity of the deficient-slope condition, nor resolves the ambiguous proposed subvariety generalization.

## Primary literature checked

Remark 4.5 distinguishes the slope-hull condition and leaves its necessity open even in dimension three. This is not a proof of the entire AIM mirror/generalization request. ## Mathematical acceptance boundaries

The independently elementary product calculation is consistent: for X=product P^{n_j}, O(a) is immaculate exactly when some coordinate satisfies -n_j <= a_j <= -1. For P²×P² the locus is a=-1,-2 or b=-1,-2, despite no collinear fan rays. This is an example in dimension four, not a counterexample to the two-dimensional theorem. The zero-degree cohomology condition is essential. Formal CCC orthogonality must preserve variance; under an opposite-category convention it reverses the order of wrapped morphisms. Vanishing is not geometric disjointness. A bare toric subvariety hypothesis is too weak; a relative vanishing hypothesis Rf_*L=0 is stronger and gives families via projection formula.
