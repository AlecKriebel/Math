## Short neighbors

For every successfully pressable symmetric binary matrix of rank r with more than one successful word, every successful word S has a distinct successful word T satisfying LCS(S,T)≥r−4. The argument includes arbitrary ambient dimension, singular matrices, omitted coordinates, equal quotient representatives, isotropic omitted vectors, and zero coordinates. Rank zero has only the empty word and cannot meet the nonunique hypothesis. The constant four is sharp.

The full corollary proofs in audit Section 11 establish connectivity with at most three successful words; nonsingular rank-at-most-six connectivity; and rank-six connectivity when the distinct nonzero quotient images form a basis with any number of duplicate and zero coordinates. The stronger assertion for nonsingular matrices of rank at least six concerns the auxiliary LCS≥2 graph only. No unrestricted rank-six coordinate-set exchange conclusion is accepted.

## Terminal coordinates

For an initially black coordinate in any successfully pressable matrix, universal terminal occurrence among containing successful words is equivalent to column equality Ae_v=diag(A) and to being the canonical quotient vector. A containing word always exists. The construction of a nonterminal occurrence when the equality fails is accepted, including the even-length C-word branch and odd-length final-pivot replacement branch.

The necessary component-separation signatures, boundary cases, and the constructive at-most-four-edge bridge when a signature fails are accepted for the auxiliary graph with LCS≥2 and rank r≥3. They do not prove that separated components are impossible. An exceptional signature is not sufficient for separation. Initial blackness and successful pressability are essential, and nonterminal cannot be strengthened to first.
