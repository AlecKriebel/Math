## Accepted findings

- **Both source Hardy displays, regular leading weight:** equivalent to the exact coefficient-one full-interval Bessel property for every \(Q\ge3\), \(V>0\), real \(W\), with no extra endpoint hypotheses. The radial-gradient display is still quantified over all source tests.
- **Proposed Hardy–Rellich core, current dimensions and nonnegative potential:** necessity holds for every \(Q\ge4\), \(V>0\), \(W\ge0\), \(S_Q[V]\ge0\), without extra endpoint hypotheses. In fact, the sign and side conditions need hold only near zero for this regular-weight argument.
- **Proposed Hardy–Rellich core, unrestricted real potential:** necessity fails, even with strict positive \(V\) and \(S_Q[V]\ge0\), by the fully all-test \(Q=5\) counterexample. This single example disproves the universal assertion but is not a separate classification of every dimension.
- **Proposed Hardy–Rellich core, historical dimension \(Q=3\):** necessity fails even with \(V>0\), \(W>0\), and \(S_3[V]>0\). The two-sheet angular basis and full original test class are established directly.
- **One fixed independent positive remainder function:** the proposed stronger necessity statement holds for every \(Q\ge3\), regular \(V>0\), and real \(W\), without requiring the side condition. The given function is forced to be a supersolution; an exact positive solution follows separately and may be different.
- **Literal nonnegative leading weights:** both Hardy necessity statements can fail even when \(W>0\), under the stated \(C^1\) flux-classical convention, using an interior zero of \(V\). For the classical global side condition, \(V\ge0\) implies either \(V>0\) everywhere or \(V\equiv0\). The zero case is classified exactly: each relevant inequality premise holds iff \(W\le0\), while the positive-solution property holds iff \(W\equiv0\).

As a direct corollary of the last dichotomy, the current-dimensional \(Q\ge4\), nonnegative-potential, globally side-conditioned core theorem extends from \(V>0\) to \(V\ge0\) in the classical \(C^2\) class: the nonzero case is regular, while the zero case and premise force \(W=0\) and admit the positive solution \(f=1\). The independently authored review of the degenerate addendum verifies this extension.
