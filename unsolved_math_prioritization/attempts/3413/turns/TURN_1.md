# Turn 1: a necessary restriction on signed cycles

**Unreviewed partial. The full realization classification is unresolved.**

The exact target is the representation of the full cyclic source symmetry group, not an arbitrary chosen subgroup or its image. The standard finite-action and signed-cycle facts below are credited background; the local reversal argument is included in full. No novelty is claimed.

## 1. Algebraic data, including the domain order

Let B_n=(Z/2)^n semidirect S_n be the signed symmetric group. For a representation rho:C_m->B_n, choose a generator g of the domain. Decompose its underlying permutation into cycles. To each cycle assign its length d and the product epsilon in{+1,-1} of its component-orientation signs.

Relabeling the components and changing their reference orientations conjugates rho in B_n. These conjugacy classes are exactly the multisets of pairs(d,epsilon). To verify the sign part, along a cycle choose a sign at the first basis vector and propagate it by the successive map signs. All transitions except the last become positive, and the last is their total product epsilon. Thus a cycle is conjugate to the usual signed cycle with only its final transition possibly negative.

Its order is d when epsilon=+1 and2d when epsilon=-1. Therefore the abstract representation exists exactly when

    r=lcm({d:positive cycles},{2d:negative cycles}) divides m. (1.1)

The image has order r and the kernel has order m/r. For n=0 take the empty lcm to be1. Replacing g by g^a with gcd(a,m)=1 does not change the signed cycle multiset: gcd(a,d)=1 preserves each cycle length, and its sign becomes epsilon^a. If epsilon is negative then2d divides m, so a is odd and the sign is unchanged. Hence the B_n conjugacy class is independent of which cyclic generator is used. Domain order m must nevertheless be retained.

These are algebraic classifications of homomorphisms, not their realization by links.

## 2. A periodic component reversal is an involution

**Lemma.** Let h be a finite-order orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of a connected oriented smooth3-manifold. Suppose it preserves a smoothly embedded circle K and reverses its orientation. Then h has order2.

Proof. Average a Riemannian metric over the finite cyclic group generated by h. Then h is an isometry, and its restriction to K is an orientation-reversing isometry for the induced circle metric. It has a fixed point p, and Dh acts by -1 on the tangent line T_p K.

The orthogonal normal two-plane is invariant under Dh. Because the ambient derivative is orientation preserving and the tangent eigenvalue is -1, its determinant on that plane is -1. An orthogonal map of a two-dimensional real space with determinant -1 is a reflection; its square is the identity. Thus (Dh_p)^2 is the identity on the full tangent space, and h² fixes p.

An isometry of a connected Riemannian manifold is determined by its value and derivative at one point. For completeness, if both agree with the identity, the exponential map makes it the identity in a normal neighborhood. The set of points where its value and derivative are the identity is then nonempty, open by the same argument, and closed by continuity. Connectedness makes it the entire manifold. Apply this to h² to get h²=id. Since h reverses K, h is not the identity. QED.

The ambient orientation hypothesis is essential. On S3 in R4, the orthogonal map diag(1,-1,R_(pi/2)) has order4 and reverses the circle in the first coordinate two-plane, but its ambient determinant is -1. It is therefore excluded from the lemma and from the source group. This is a calibration of the hypothesis, not a source counterexample.

## 3. Necessary signed-cycle theorem for the exact source problem

For the hyperbolic link in the source, Budney's isometry realization identifies the relevant mapping classes with a finite group B_L of extendable orientation-preserving hyperbolic isometries. The paragraph before Definition3.3 explicitly extends this finite group coherently to an action on(S3,L). This is the source-supported finite action used here; no Nielsen-realization assertion for arbitrary mapping class groups is assumed. Faithfulness on the complement makes the order of an ambient group element equal to its order in B_L. The OPG notation A_L corresponds to this full B_L, not to the smaller knot-dependent subgroup A_f.

**Theorem.** If a representation C_m->B_n is realized by the source group and has a negative signed cycle of length d, then

    2d=m.                                                   (3.1)

In particular, every negative cycle has the same length m/2.

Proof. Let g be a generator of the actual order-m finite action. A length-d component orbit has stabilizer generated by g^d, of order m/d. The product sign of that cycle is the orientation degree of g^d on one of its circles. For a negative cycle this restriction reverses that circle. The lemma applies to the actual ambient diffeomorphism g^d, forcing m/d=2. QED.

This proof does not require that the chosen circle be an unknot. Hyperbolicity is used here to supply the actual finite action, and the unlink condition will be relevant to further realization constraints. The theorem is only necessary, not sufficient.

## 4. Consequences and concrete exclusions

1. If any negative cycle occurs, its signed block already has order2d=m. Thus the signed representation is faithful. Consequently every realized nonfaithful representation is conjugate into the ordinary unsigned permutation group S_n. This follows because all its cycle signs are positive and the orientation relabeling in section1 removes its individual signs.
2. Signed realizations with at least one negative cycle satisfy m<=2n. More precisely, k negative cycles use exactly k*m/2 components, so k*m/2<=n. No such bound on m is asserted for arbitrary unsigned or trivial representations.
3. The abstract homomorphism C4->B_1 sending a generator to the negative fixed point is not realizable: its negative cycle has d1 instead of m/2=2. Similarly C8->B_2 with one negative2-cycle is excluded even though its image has order4 dividing8.
4. Faithfulness alone is insufficient. The signed permutation(12-)(345+) has order lcm(4,3)=12 and defines a faithful C12 representation on five components. Its negative cycle length2 violates(3.1), so it cannot arise from the source group.
5. Budney's already credited Borromean example C4 with a negative2-cycle, Whitehead C2 inversion, and Sakuma's C_(2n) negative n-cycle examples for odd n satisfy the restriction. The credited unsigned n-cycle examples also pass. These are existing source examples, not newly constructed links.

The invariant m and signed cycle data make these exclusions insensitive to relabeling, reference-orientation changes or a different cyclic generator. No assertion is made that every remaining multiset can be realized.
