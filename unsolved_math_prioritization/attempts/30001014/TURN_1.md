# Turn 1: every finite compact spectrum is excluded

Substantive author turn **1/5**. The literal universal assertion printed in OWR37/2008, Question4, has a negative answer: a one-point compact Hausdorff space cannot occur with the stated tensor-product pathology. More generally **no nonempty finite compact Hausdorff spectrum can occur**. This is an elementary finite-dimensional boundary obstruction, not a classification or counterexample within the substantive infinite-spectrum realization problem.

The proof below permits the ambient C*-algebra initially to be nonunital. Thus an unspecified ambient-unit convention cannot evade the obstruction. It excludes failure of maximal abelianness in **every** C*-completion of A⊙A, not merely the maximal completion.

## 1. A unital MASA forces the ambient algebra to share its unit

Let C be a nonzero unital MASA of a C*-algebra A, and let p be the identity of C. Work in the unitization of A when necessary. The projection p belongs to A. For any a in A,

 b=(1−p)a* a(1−p)

is a positive element of A, and cb=bc=0 for every c in C. The C*-algebra generated by C and b is abelian. Maximality therefore implies b in C. Since pb=0 and p is the identity of C, b=0. The C*-identity gives

                         a(1−p)=0.

Applying this to a* and taking adjoints also gives (1−p)a=0. Thus pa=ap=a for all a in A, so A is unital with identity p.

The argument uses actual maximality as an abelian C*-subalgebra, rather than assuming that a unital subalgebra was embedded unitally. For any MASA C, its relative commutant in A is C: if x commutes with C, both real and imaginary self-adjoint parts commute with C and hence belong to C by maximality.

## 2. Finite-dimensional MASAs force finite-dimensional ambient algebras

Suppose C has finite nonempty spectrum with n points. By the elementary Gelfand identification, C is isomorphic to the diagonal algebra of n-tuples of complex numbers. Let p_1,...,p_n be its minimal nonzero projections. They are orthogonal and sum to the common identity1 of A, by §1.

For each i, every element of p_i A p_i commutes with every p_j, hence with C. Therefore

                            p_i A p_i = C p_i.                    (1)

For i,j, put E_ij=p_i A p_j. If E_ij is nonzero, take x in E_ij nonzero. By (1), x*x is a positive scalar multiple of p_j and xx* is a positive scalar multiple of p_i. Both scalars equal ||x||² by the C*-identity. Thus v=x/||x|| satisfies v*v=p_j and vv*=p_i. For any y in E_ij, v*y is in p_j A p_j=C p_j, say v*y=αp_j. Hence

                           y=vv*y=αv.

Consequently dim_C E_ij is either0 or1. Since

                        a=Σ_(i,j=1)^n p_i a p_j

for every a in A, it follows that

                              dim_C A <= n².                     (2)

This is a full algebraic proof of the finite-dimensional conclusion; no separability or representation on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space was assumed. In the singleton case n=1 it simply says A=C=C1.

## 3. No tensor completion can create a larger relative commutant

Let β be any C*-norm on A⊙A and B=A⊗_β A its completion. Set q_ij=p_i⊗p_j. These are n² mutually orthogonal projections with sum1 in B, and their linear span is D=C⊗C. For every i,j,

                 q_ij (A⊙A) q_ij
                  =(p_i A p_i)⊙(p_j A p_j)=C q_ij.

Compression is norm-continuous in every C*-norm, and C q_ij is closed. Density of A⊙A therefore gives

                            q_ij B q_ij=C q_ij.                   (3)

If z in B commutes with D, it commutes with every q_ij. Expanding with the finite sum of these projections, its off-diagonal corners vanish:

 q_ij z q_kl=z q_ij q_kl=0   when (i,j)≠(k,l).

By (3), each remaining corner is a scalar multiple of q_ij. Thus z belongs to D, proving

                              D'∩B=D.                            (4)

The algebra D is abelian and self-adjoint, so (4) means it is a MASA in B. The proof does not need to invoke nuclearity or uniqueness of tensor norms; it works directly for every possible completion. In this finite-dimensional situation those classical facts give the same conclusion.

## 4. The literal counterexample and the precise remaining interpretation

Take X={*}, the one-point compact Hausdorff space. Every C*-algebra C with spectrum X is isomorphic to the scalars. If such a C is a MASA in A, §§1–2 force A=C. Then C⊗C=A⊗_β A is already the whole tensor product. It cannot fail to be maximal abelian. Thus X fails the existential requirement, and the literal assertion that **every compact Hausdorff X** can occur is false. Sections2–3 exclude all nonempty finite X, not just this smallest example.

This refutes the exact unrestricted wording but does **not** settle the likely intended infinite-space problem. The source's known pathological spectrum is the one-point compactification of a countably infinite discrete set, not a finite set. Its announced [0,1] example is also infinite. The source does not explicitly insert the word infinite, and we neither silently add it to the printed statement nor pretend that its omission makes the underlying research problem disappear.

No new priority is claimed for the finite-MASA lemma, standard C*-identities, Gelfand theory or the finite-corner argument. The result is submitted as a **source-qualified literal negative answer at turn1/5**, with the infinite-spectrum realization/classification gap prominent.
