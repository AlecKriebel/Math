# Discriminant-localized flattening and the standard tautological object

**Problem:** 20000088 / AIM-ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY-0088, original AIM Problem 4.3.
**Status:** rigorous localized partial result, accepted within the scope of the accompanying audit; the original problem remains unresolved by this result.
**Scope:** characteristic zero; the standard type-A realization specialized at the imaginary root δ=0; every n≥3; homogeneous localization away from collisions of the ordered X-eigenvalues. No 2-periodization. No global flattening or global flag-Hilbert equivalence is assumed.

## 1. What is proved

Write R=C[x₁,…,xₙ], with deg(xᵢ)=2, Δ=∏_{i<j}(xᵢ−xⱼ), S=R[Δ⁻¹], and U=Spec S. Let A be the graded, additive, idempotent-complete extended-affine diagrammatic Hecke category, with δ=0 and the central GLₙ variable included. Let P be the full additive idempotent-complete category generated by the graded graph S-bimodules S_w, w∈Sₙ. Here S_w has ordinary left action and right action a·f=a w(f). Its objects are direct summands of finite sums of shifts of these graph modules. They are projective as one-sided S-modules; no projectivity over S⊗C S is asserted.

There is an actual strong monoidal dg functor

    Flat_U : Chᵇ(A) → Chᵇ(P)

whose restriction to A, followed by passage to the homotopy category, is naturally monoidally isomorphic to homogeneous localization of the positive-rotation Mackaay–Miemietz–Vaz evaluation functor. It preserves the internal grading and cohomological grading separately. In particular, this is a construction of the higher coherence on this open set, not an assumption that a global extension exists.

For the standard Gaitsgory complex Vₙ and the filtered endomorphism χ of Hathaway, let M=Flat_U(Vₙ). Then, in Kᵇ(P),

    (M, Flat_U(χ), Flat_U(μ)) ≅ (⊕ᵢ S, diag(x₁,…,xₙ), 0).

The isomorphism may be chosen to preserve the indexed Wakimoto/Jucys–Murphy filtration. The eigensummands themselves are canonical; trivializations of each line are choices. The displayed right-hand side is the actual tautological bundle with its X,Y operators on the regular-semisimple open of the **semi-nilpotent slice FHilbₙ(C)**, interpreted as a degree-zero diagonal S-bimodule. The comparison preserves the two specified operator degrees, deg χ=(internal 2, cohomological 0) and deg μ=(internal −2, cohomological 2).

The statement does not compare a specified source highest-weight/cyclic arrow or a specified half-braiding. It does not extend over Δ=0. It does not identify a restriction of an already constructed global GNR functor, since such a functor is not assumed. It constructs and compares the localized objects directly.

## 2. Exact literature inputs and conventions

Only the following established inputs are used.

1. Mackaay–Miemietz–Vaz, arXiv:2207.02459v2, Theorem 5.4 and §5.1, supply a graded additive strong monoidal evaluation A_aff→Kᵇ(SBimₙ). It is not a functor on complexes. We pin the convention by actual complexes, rather than the decategorified labels ev and ev′: their equation (60) is Tᵢ=[Bᵢ→R⟨1⟩], Definition 4.14 is Tρ=T₁…Tₙ₋₁, and equation (117) sends Bρ to Tρ at r=s=0. These are the complexes Fᵢ used below, with rotation mapping to F₁…Fₙ₋₁ as in Elias §2.6. No homological or extra internal parameter shift is added to the rotation. Their p.6 separately explains the bar-dual evaluation; it is not silently substituted here.
2. Elias, arXiv:1811.06188v1, constructs Vₙ and its Wakimoto filtration (Theorem 8.40); each standard weight occurs once. On δ=0 the pseudocomplex is a complex. His §2.6 uses rightward Jucys–Murphy braids jᵢ=fᵢ…fₙ₋₁ fₙ₋₁…fᵢ, jₙ=1. Their positive writhe is 2(n−i). A simultaneous reversal of indices gives the customary leftward convention.
3. Hathaway, December 2023 dissertation, Theorem 1.8.1 / 5.1.6, constructs χ:Vₙ→Vₙ(2), preserving the Wakimoto filtration and inducing right multiplication by xᵢ on the i-th graded piece. The standard monodromy is μ:Vₙ→Vₙ(−2)[2]. The dissertation's unrelated affine GNR conjectures are not used as theorems.
4. Gorsky–Neguț–Rasmussen, arXiv:1608.07308v1, equations (2.15)–(2.17), define FHilbₙ(C) by lower-triangular X, strictly lower-triangular Y, [X,Y]=0, and a cyclic vector, modulo the Borel. This is not unrestricted FHilbₙ(C²). The tautological bundle carries X and Y and the ordered quotient flag. In their notation q scales X and t scales Y (§2.5). We make the grading conversion q→(−2)[0], t→(2)[−2], so qT→T becomes T→T(2), and tT→T becomes T→T(−2)[2]. Hathaway exchanges the letters q,t in his geometric discussion; we do not exchange the actual operators.

Adding the invariant GLₙ coordinate to the root-span realization is scalar extension: write xᵢ=c/n plus the prescribed linear combination of finite simple roots, with c=∑xᵢ. The finite simple roots and c map identically; α₀ maps to −∑_{i=1}^{n−1}αᵢ. Thus δ maps to zero and the scalar xᵢ on each Wakimoto piece maps to the same xᵢ in S. Equivalently, the composite evaluation followed by H⁰ below factors through the δ=0 additive quotient. This avoids claiming that a chain-level image of δ vanished before taking H⁰.

All uses of these results retain their exact stated category and grading. The subsequent lemmas are proved here.

## 3. Explicit local contraction

For a finite simple reflection s=(i,i+1), write α=xᵢ−xᵢ₊₁. It is a unit of S. There is an isomorphism of graded bimodules

    S⊗_{Sˢ}S → S ⊕ S_s,
    a⊗b ↦ (ab, a s(b)).                                    (3.1)

To prove surjectivity explicitly, put

    e_id = (1⊗1 + α⁻¹⊗α)/2,
    e_s  = (1⊗1 − α⁻¹⊗α)/2.

They map to (1,0) and (0,1). Since S is free of rank two over Sˢ, with basis 1,α, both sides are free of rank two as left S-modules; these formulas give the inverse. The right action on the second component is s-twisted. Localization from R is legitimate because Δ² is invariant and acts equally on both sides of every finite Soergel bimodule; inverting Δ² is the same as inverting Δ.

Use B_s=R⊗_{Rˢ}R(1), and positive Rouquier complex F_s=[B_s→R(1)], in degrees 0,1, with multiplication differential. Formula (3.1) sends this to

    [ S(1) ⊕ S_s(1) --(1,0)--> S(1) ].                     (3.2)

The first summand and its target are a split contractible pair. Projection p onto S_s(1), inclusion i of that summand, and h:S(1)→S(1)⊕S_s(1), h(a)=(a,0), satisfy

    pi=1,   1−ip=dh+hd.

Thus the contraction is an actual graded chain homotopy equivalence, not a quasi-isomorphism inferred from projectivity.

For F_s⁻¹=[R(−1)→B_s], in degrees −1,0, the usual startdot becomes (α,0) in (3.1), up to a nonzero normalization scalar. Since α is invertible, this is again a split pair, with contracting homotopy (a,b)↦α⁻¹a. The remaining graph summand S_s(1) is isomorphic to S_s(−1) by multiplication by α⁻¹, of degree −2. We use the normalization S_s(−1) when describing inverse crossings. This last choice does not erase the grading: multiplication by the stated homogeneous unit implements the shift isomorphism.

Tensoring split contractions preserves homotopy equivalences, by tensoring their explicit homotopies with identities and using the ordinary cochain signs. Consequently every localized Rouquier complex is homotopy equivalent to a degree-zero graph module. Positive F_β is S_{w(β)}(ℓ(β)); negative crossings have the corresponding inverse normalization. In particular,

    localization(F_{jᵢ}) ≃ S(2(n−i))[0].                    (3.3)

No assertion is made that every complex of graph bimodules is formal.

## 4. Coherent H⁰ strictification

### Lemma 4.1
Let P be an idempotent-complete additive monoidal full subcategory of an abelian category of graded bimodules. Let C₀⊂Kᵇ(P) be the full subcategory of complexes **already homotopy equivalent** to an object of P placed in degree zero. Then inclusion j:P→C₀ is a fully faithful strong monoidal equivalence. Ordinary H⁰ is a strong monoidal quasi-inverse.

Proof. Between two degree-zero complexes, a chain map is exactly a map of their objects, and no nonzero chain homotopy is possible. Thus j is fully faithful. Essential surjectivity is the definition of C₀. For C∈C₀, any homotopy equivalence C≃P[0] induces H⁰(C)≅P as a graded bimodule. Hence H⁰(C) belongs to P, after closing P under isomorphism.

There is a unique morphism η_C:jH⁰(C)→C in Kᵇ(P) which induces the identity of H⁰(C): existence follows from any chosen homotopy equivalence; uniqueness follows from full faithfulness after conjugating by such an equivalence. The same argument proves naturality. The Künneth map

    H⁰(C)⊗H⁰(D) → H⁰(C⊗D)

is defined by tensoring cycle representatives. It is an isomorphism because C,D are homotopy equivalent to degree-zero objects, not because graph modules are projective over the enveloping ring. Associativity and unit diagrams commute by the definition on cycles, or by uniqueness after H⁰. The maps η_C are compatible with these tensor and unit maps by the same uniqueness. This proves the strong monoidal equivalence, with its full coherence. ∎

### Lemma 4.2
The localized MMV evaluation of every object of A lies in C₀.

Proof. The finite generators Bᵢ localize to degree-zero sums of graph bimodules by (3.1). Each rotation Ω^{±1} is evaluated to a finite Rouquier complex, with no homological parameter shift, so §3 gives a degree-zero graph module. The affine generator B₀ is evaluated to a conjugate of a finite Bᵢ by rotation Rouquier complexes; it is therefore homotopy equivalent to a degree-zero sum of graph modules. Tensor products, sums, and internal shifts preserve C₀. Finally, a retract in Kᵇ(P) of an object homotopy equivalent to P[0] corresponds to an actual idempotent of P by full faithfulness; this idempotent splits in P. The affine category is generated from the specified objects by these operations. ∎

Let L be termwise homogeneous localization, and E⁺ the positive-rotation additive evaluation. Define

    F₀ = H⁰ ◦ L ◦ E⁺ : A → P.                              (4.1)

Lemmas 4.1–4.2 prove F₀ is a genuine graded additive strong monoidal functor. All morphisms are actual maps of degree-zero bimodules. In particular, if d₂d₁=0 in A, then F₀(d₂)F₀(d₁)=0 literally. Define Flat_U on a complex by terms F₀(Cⁱ) and differentials F₀(dⁱ); define it on the whole dg Hom complex by applying F₀ to every component. Additivity and preservation of composition imply that it commutes with the differential df=d_D f−(−1)^{|f|}f d_C. This is a dg functor. The tensorator of F₀, applied termwise, makes it strong monoidal on total tensor products; naturality ensures compatibility with the Koszul sign (−1)^i on the second differential, and associativity/unit follow from those of F₀.

This constructs the actual localized extension. There is no appeal to arbitrary choices of null-homotopies satisfying unknown equations.

The construction agrees on finite Rouquier complexes with L, because E⁺ is the identity on the finite additive subcategory. On an affine crossing, MMV equation (122) expresses the image of the affine enddot as the counit composed with the conjugated finite enddot (and expresses the startdot by the corresponding unit). Applying H⁰ and its tensorator turns these into actual maps of degree-zero objects. Thus the two-term complex for the affine crossing is the conjugated finite two-term complex, with its degree-zero target identified by the counit; the split contractions in §3 identify this with the localized conjugate finite Rouquier crossing. This is a cone calculation on a specified map, not an inference from agreement of object classes. Rotation maps to its localized positive braid. Tensoring these identifications gives

    Flat_U(Wᵢ) ≃ L(F_{jᵢ}),                                 (4.2)

with the braid convention of §2.6 of Elias. Formula (4.2) only concerns the explicit Rouquier words and does not presuppose an extension of E⁺ to arbitrary unlocalized complexes.

## 5. The standard object and both operators

Apply the exact functor Kᵇ(F₀) to the Wakimoto filtration of Vₙ. Its successive pieces, by (3.3) and (4.2), are S(2(n−i))[0]. A triangle whose outer objects are degree-zero objects P,Q splits: its connecting map lies in Hom_{Kᵇ(P)}(Q,P[1])=0, because no such nonzero chain map exists. Induction over the finite filtration therefore gives

    M ≃ ⊕ᵢ S(2(n−i))[0].                                  (5.1)

This conclusion uses actual exact triangles and zero chain-map groups, rather than a K-class equality. The proof does not infer splitness for arbitrary extensions of bimodules in a derived category: its category is Kᵇ(P), where the displayed groups vanish.

Choose any splitting respecting the filtration. The endomorphism χ becomes an upper- or lower-triangular degree-two matrix with diagonal entries xᵢ. Let p(z)=∏ᵢ(z−xᵢ). The filtration itself shows p(χ)=0: each factor χ−xᵢ lowers past the corresponding filtered quotient, and the factors commute. Equivalently, triangularity and Cayley–Hamilton prove the same identity over the commutative ring S.

All xᵢ−xⱼ are units, so define degree-zero endomorphisms

    eᵢ = ∏_{j≠i}(χ−xⱼ)/(xᵢ−xⱼ).                           (5.2)

Polynomial interpolation in S[z]/(p) gives eᵢ²=eᵢ, eᵢeⱼ=0 for i≠j, ∑eᵢ=1, and χeᵢ=xᵢeᵢ. Because P is idempotent-complete, M splits into these eigensummands. On the filtered quotient indexed by j, eᵢ acts as δᵢⱼ. The filtration on eᵢM therefore has exactly one nonzero piece, S(2(n−i)); hence

    eᵢM ≅ S(2(n−i)),   χ|_{eᵢM}=xᵢ.                       (5.3)

This also proves that the spectral splitting recovers the original indexed filtration. The canonical objects are eᵢM, not their chosen generators.

Let aᵢ=∏_{j>i}(xᵢ−xⱼ), aₙ=1. It is a homogeneous unit of degree 2(n−i). With shift convention N(k)_d=N_{d+k}, multiplication by aᵢ⁻¹ gives a degree-zero isomorphism S(2(n−i))→S. These explicit units turn (5.3) into the eigenvalue model Sⁿ without forgetting the grading.

Finally,

    Hom_{Kᵇ(P)}(M,M(−2)[2])=0,

since M is homotopy equivalent to a degree-zero object. Thus Flat_U(μ)=0 as a morphism in the homotopy category. A chosen chain representative on Flat_U(Vₙ) need only be null-homotopic, not literally the zero chain map; it becomes zero on the degree-zero model. ∎

## 6. Geometric identification on the exact semi-nilpotent slice

Let F be FHilbₙ(C), using GNR equation (2.16), and take the inverse image of U under the map recording the ordered diagonal entries xᵢ of X.

Over any S-algebra, the idempotents

    εᵢ(X)=∏_{j≠i}(X−xⱼ)/(xᵢ−xⱼ)

split the tautological rank-n bundle into eigenline bundles. This works over rings, not only at complex-valued points: the same polynomial identities hold universally. Since Y commutes with X, it preserves each eigenline. As a strictly triangular endomorphism relative to the ordered flag, its action on each rank-one filtered quotient is zero. Consequently its action on each eigenline is zero and Y=0. Without the strict-triangular/nilpotent hypothesis this deduction would be false: arbitrary diagonal Y would remain.

Write vᵢ=εᵢ(X)v. Cyclicity of v means the S-algebra generated by X,Y applied to v generates the tautological bundle locally. On the i-th eigenline, any polynomial in X,Y acts by its value at (xᵢ,0). Therefore cyclicity is equivalent to vᵢ generating that line. Each vᵢ is consequently a nowhere-vanishing section, and the ordered basis (vᵢ) identifies the bundle with O_Uⁿ. In this basis X=diag(xᵢ), Y=0, and v=(1,…,1). The ordered quotient flag is the corresponding coordinate flag. This construction is functorial over S-algebras and has no stabilizers, so it proves F|_U≅U, not just a bijection on closed points.

The derived enhancement introduces no hidden term here. GNR equations (2.9)–(2.10) use the strictly triangular commutator target, and equation (2.13) uses its Koszul complex; there are no additional diagonal commutator equations. In the matrix chart with diagonal entries of X fixed and pairwise distinct, ad_X on strictly triangular matrices is triangular with diagonal coefficients xᵢ−xⱼ, all units. Thus [X,Y]=0 uniquely and transversely eliminates every strictly triangular Y-coordinate. The Koszul complex is quasi-isomorphic to its degree-zero quotient. This is not a claim that its ambient-module resolution splits. Taking the free Borel quotient on the cyclic locus yields the same underived U.

For completeness, the iterated derived-projectivization definition in GNR §2.7 gives the same conclusion directly. In the next stage of (2.25), put A=X−xₙ₊₁. On the locus where all n+1 eigenvalues are distinct, A is invertible and Y=0. Equations (2.26)–(2.27) become Ψ(w)=(0,Aw,0) and Φ(w₁,w₂,f)=Aw₁+fv, with their indicated equivariant twists. The left term cancels with the second middle summand using A⁻¹. Changing the first middle coordinate to w₁+A⁻¹fv then cancels it with the right term. The remaining middle summand is exactly O, including its character. Thus Eₙ is homotopy equivalent to O, its derived symmetric algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the ordinary symmetric algebra on one generator, and its derived Proj is the base itself. This proves inductively that no derived directions survive on U in the precise tower model used by GNR.

The sections vᵢ have equivariant weight zero: the numerator and denominator in εᵢ are homogeneous of the same X-weight, while v has weight zero. Thus the trivialization retains the equivariance and the grading conversion fixed in §2. Under the diagonal-bimodule realization of vector bundles on the affine scheme U, its operator pair is exactly (Sⁿ,diag(xᵢ),0). Combining this with §5 proves the claimed localized comparison.

The geometric section has been computed. No unproved assertion is made that a particular source highest-weight arrow maps to this section. A comparison of those arrows would require nonvanishing and precise normalization of each source spectral component.

## 7. Why this cannot settle the original question

The proof depends essentially on Δ⁻¹ in three places: split contractions, Lagrange projectors, and trivializations of the eigenlines. These denominators cannot be removed formally.

A concrete collision test is X=[[x,0],[1,x]], Y=0, v=(1,0). This is a legitimate cyclic semi-nilpotent rank-two flag-Hilbert point. Here X−xI is nonzero nilpotent, so X is not a direct sum of its scalar diagonal pieces. The discriminant is zero and (5.2) is undefined. This does not contradict the known rank-two comparison; it proves that its extension data are invisible after our localization.

Also the nonzero cone [R --Δ²--> R] becomes contractible over S. Hence agreement after localization cannot prove global agreement, even for perfect complexes. Any genuine obstruction to the original comparison can remain supported on Δ=0 and disappear from this theorem.

What remains includes an unlocalized coherent extension at n≥3, the actual global GNR image and object comparison, source cyclic-arrow normalization, and compatibility of half-braidings. The present result neither proves nor disproves any center-generation question in neighboring problem 20000087.

## 8. Verification and novelty

The proof is the categorical and algebraic argument above. The accompanying mathematical audit reviews the literal MMV conventions, affine-dot/cone comparison, scalar specialization, coherent H⁰ strictification, graded spectral splitting, and the exact derived geometric model. The complete Soergel diagrammatic relations are supplied by the cited MMV theorem, not independently re-proved here. Supplemental computation is not part of this proof-only edition and is not a substitute for the written proof or the cited theorem.

Novelty confidence is low: discriminant localization, spectral splitting, and formality on the regular-semisimple locus are standard tools, and this combination may be familiar to experts. The contribution is a precise unconditional localized dg construction and operator comparison with all excluded global conclusions made explicit. It is stronger than a K-class comparison and weaker than the original all-rank problem.
