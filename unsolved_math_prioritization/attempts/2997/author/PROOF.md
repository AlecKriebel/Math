# Kirby Problem 4.121: binding constraints and the identity-monodromy case

## Status and scope

This is an authored, bounded partial analysis, not a solution of Kirby Problem 4.121 and not a novelty claim. The question asks for an existence criterion for smooth closed four-dimensional open books, especially in the simply connected, signature-zero case. Our results below do not resolve that last case with unrestricted monodromy or exotic smooth structures.

All manifolds in the propositions are smooth. The ambient manifold X is closed, connected and oriented; the page P is compact, connected and oriented, with nonempty boundary B. Its monodromy phi is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism equal to the identity near B. Write

X = OB(P,phi) = T_phi union_(B x S^1) (B x D^2),

where T_phi is the mapping torus. Betti numbers use rational coefficients. A boundary-relative isotopy is an isotopy through diffeomorphisms equal to the identity near the boundary.

## Proposition 1: exact binding identities

Suppose B has c connected components, of genera g_1,...,g_c. Put G = sum_i g_i, and let s be the number of components with genus zero. Then

chi(X) = chi(B) = 2c - 2G,

c - G = 1 - b_1(X) + b_2(X)/2,

s = 1 - b_1(X) + b_2(X)/2 + sum_(g_i >= 2)(g_i - 1).

In particular b_2(X) is even. If X is simply connected and b_2(X)=2k, then

c = k + 1 + G,

s = k + 1 + sum_(g_i >= 2)(g_i - 1) >= k + 1.

Thus a simply connected open book with positive second Betti number cannot have connected binding. Nor can any simply connected closed four-manifold have an open book whose binding is a union of positive-genus surfaces only.

### Proof

The mapping torus has Euler characteristic zero, by multiplicativity of Euler characteristic in a bundle over S^1 (or by its finite cellular mapping-cone chain complex). Also chi(B x S^1)=0 and chi(B x D^2)=chi(B). Euler characteristic additivity for the gluing gives chi(X)=chi(B). Classification of closed oriented surfaces gives the displayed genus expression.

Poincare duality for X gives chi(X)=2-2b_1(X)+b_2(X). Equating expressions proves the second identity and the parity assertion. Finally

c-G = sum_i(1-g_i) = s - sum_(g_i >= 2)(g_i-1),

which proves the last identity and its consequences. This proof does not infer that any individual spherical binding component is nonzero in H_2(X).

If one permits empty-binding circle bundles in the definition of an open book, their Euler characteristic is zero. They therefore cannot occur for simply connected closed oriented four-manifolds, whose Euler characteristic is 2+b_2. The nonempty-binding convention loses no case of the highlighted question. For a connected total open book with nonempty binding and monodromy fixed at the boundary, the page is connected: a page component meeting the boundary is preserved, so its orbit already gives a whole connected component of the total space.

## Proposition 2: a complete identity-monodromy subcase

The following statements about a closed, connected, simply connected, oriented smooth four-manifold X are equivalent:

1. X has an open book with monodromy boundary-relatively isotopic to the identity.
2. For some integer k>=0, X is diffeomorphic to the standard connected sum #_k(S^2 x S^2), with #_0 interpreted as S^4.

For such an open book, the page is diffeomorphic to S^3 with the interiors of k+1 disjoint standard balls removed. The binding has exactly k+1 spherical components, attaining the lower bound of Proposition 1.

### Proof

Replace the monodromy by the identity using its boundary-relative isotopy. This preserves the diffeomorphism type of the open book. Then

OB(P,id) = boundary(P x D^2),

with corners smoothed.

First, pi_1(OB(P,id)) is pi_1(P). Indeed, start with P x S^1, whose fundamental group is pi_1(P) x Z. Attach each connected component B_j x D^2 along B_j x S^1. The inclusion into B_j x D^2 is surjective on fundamental groups and its kernel is generated by the S^1 factor. Van Kampen therefore kills the central circle generator and imposes no other relations. This remains true for disconnected B, attaching the caps one at a time and choosing paths in the connected mapping torus. Consequently pi_1(P)=1.

Poincare-Lefschetz duality over Z gives H_2(P,B;Z)=H^1(P;Z)=0. The exact sequence of the pair, together with H_1(P;Z)=0, then gives H_1(B;Z)=0. Each oriented boundary component is therefore S^2. Let their number be c. Cap all c components with standard 3-balls. Van Kampen gives a closed simply connected smooth 3-manifold; the three-dimensional Poincare theorem and uniqueness of smooth structures in dimension three identify it with S^3. Thus P is a punctured S^3. The three-dimensional smooth Schoenflies theorem identifies its complement of finitely many disjoint balls with the standard c-holed S^3.

For c=1, P is D^3 and boundary(P x D^2)=S^4. For c>=2, a c-holed S^3 is a boundary connected sum of c-1 copies of S^2 x I. Product with D^2 preserves this boundary-connected-sum description after smoothing corners: its connecting 3-dimensional 1-handle becomes a 5-dimensional 1-handle. Since (S^2 x I) x D^2 is S^2 x D^3, the boundary is the connected sum of c-1 copies of S^2 x S^2. Put k=c-1.

Conversely, take precisely that punctured S^3 page with identity monodromy. The same explicit product and boundary-sum construction gives #_k(S^2 x S^2). Its binding consists of k+1 spheres and b_2=2k. This proves both the equivalence and sharpness. No four-dimensional Schoenflies statement is used.

## Why homologically trivial monodromy is insufficient

The condition in Proposition 2 is isotopy relative to the boundary, not identity on homology. Take P=S^2 x I. A boundary-parallel sphere twist fixes both boundary components and acts as the identity on H_*(P;Z), but its open book is the nontrivial oriented S^2-bundle over S^2, diffeomorphic to CP^2 # overline(CP^2). With identity monodromy the total manifold is S^2 x S^2. Hsueh explicitly verifies these Kirby diagrams in Section 5.2, pp. 332-333, and defines sphere twists in Section 6 [H]. The forms are respectively odd and even, so the two total manifolds are not diffeomorphic. This rules out extending Proposition 2 merely by replacing isotopy-triviality with homological triviality.

## Literature and remaining gap

K3, Problem 4.121, pp. 290-291 [K3], formulates the simply connected signature-zero question and asks about exotic versions of the familiar connected-sum examples. Its printed odd-form example is CP^2 # overline(CP^2), not CP^2 # CP^2; the overbar matters.

Kastenholz's Theorems A and B [K] prove a simplicial-volume obstruction and give products of two genus-at-least-two surfaces as signature-zero counterexamples, even after arbitrary connected sums. This is an obstruction in the general, nonsimply connected setting. It does not decide the simply connected question. Lawande and Saha [LS], Theorem 1.2, further construct infinitely many parallelizable counterexamples by connected sums of those products with copies of S^1 x S^3. Their construction also has nontrivial fundamental group. We use the positive simplicial volume of products of hyperbolic surfaces; we do not call such a product a real-hyperbolic four-manifold.

The binding identities control how complicated a proposed decomposition must be, not whether one exists. Proposition 2 settles one monodromy class, not all open books. None of these arguments excludes or constructs open books on arbitrary exotic simply connected signature-zero smooth four-manifolds. Search results are dated evidence, not a proof that no later theorem exists.
