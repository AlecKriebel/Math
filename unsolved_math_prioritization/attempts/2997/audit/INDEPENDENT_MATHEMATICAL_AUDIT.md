## 1. Scope and conventions

The construction is the smooth abstract open book

\[
X=T_\phi\cup_{B\times S^1}(B\times D^2),\qquad B=\partial P,
\]

with the standard binding gluing, oriented connected compact page P, and monodromy equal to the identity in a boundary collar. This collar condition controls the binding framing and the meaning of relative isotopy. Gluing by an arbitrary extra boundary map is not included.

All boundary components are closed oriented surfaces. Nonempty binding and connected total space force a connected page: any page component meeting the boundary is individually preserved because the monodromy fixes its boundary points. Its mapping torus and caps form a whole component of X. Remaining page components, including any closed components permuted by monodromy, would give separate components of X. Thus they cannot occur.

If the binding is empty, X is a mapping torus, with Euler characteristic zero. A simply connected closed oriented four-manifold has Euler characteristic 2+b2>0. Hence no case relevant to the highlighted simply connected question is lost by requiring nonempty binding. Its fundamental group also maps onto Z, giving a separate obstruction.

The Euler argument is topological whenever the open-book pieces satisfy the usual manifold/fibration hypotheses. The classification in Proposition 2 is explicitly smooth. The audit does not replace diffeomorphism by homeomorphism or claim a result about wild topological embeddings.

## 2. Proposition 1: exact identities and the spherical count

Euler characteristic of a mapping torus is zero; each gluing component B_j x S1 also has zero Euler characteristic. Additivity therefore gives chi(X)=chi(B). Let c be the number of boundary components, g_j their genera, G their total genus, and s the number of spheres. Then

\[
\chi(X)=2c-2G=2-2b_1(X)+b_2(X).
\]

The second equality uses Poincare duality on the closed oriented ambient manifold, not on the page. Therefore b2(X) is even and

\[
c-G=1-b_1(X)+\frac{b_2(X)}2,
\qquad
s=1-b_1(X)+\frac{b_2(X)}2+\sum_{g_j\ge2}(g_j-1).
\]

For pi1(X)=1 and b2(X)=2k this becomes

\[
c=k+1+G,\qquad
s=k+1+\sum_{g_j\ge2}(g_j-1)\ge k+1.
\]

These are component counts, not ranks of homology classes. The conclusion concerns **spherical** binding components, a strictly stronger statement than counting all binding components. Torus components make no contribution to c-G. A genus-g component with g>=2 requires g-1 additional spheres. Equality in the spherical lower bound permits any number of tori but forbids higher-genus components. Equality c=k+1 forces all components to be spheres.

The connected-binding conclusion is correctly restricted. If c=1, then k=G=0 and the binding is S2. This forces b2(X)=0, not by itself the standard smooth S4. The standard-S4 conclusion is proved only under the stronger hypothesis of Proposition 2. A nonempty all-positive-genus binding is impossible in the simply connected case, including k=0.

As a separate consistency check, doubling an oriented three-dimensional page gives chi(P)=chi(B)/2. Since b0(P)=1 and b3(P)=0,

\[
b_2(P)=b_1(P)+c-G-1=b_1(P)+k.
\]

The exact sequence of (P,B), with Poincare-Lefschetz duality, also gives rank(im(H1(B;Q)->H1(P;Q)))=G, hence b1(P)>=G. The map H2(B;Q)->H2(P;Q) has rank c-1: its kernel is generated by the entire oriented boundary. These facts do not prove the same rank after mapping into H2(X). Monodromy and gluing intervene. No inequality between the count s and the rank of the corresponding classes in H2(X) is silently substituted.

## 3. Proposition 2: boundary-relative identity monodromy

### Isotopy and fundamental group

An isotopy to the identity that remains fixed on a boundary collar induces a diffeomorphism of the mapping tori compatible with the standard binding caps. Thus one may replace the monodromy by id, and the resulting manifold is the smoothed boundary of P x D2.

For identity monodromy, start with P x S1, whose group is pi1(P) x Z. Attach B_j x D2 along the connected B_j x S1 one component at a time. The map on fundamental groups to the cap is surjective, with kernel generated by the circle. Van Kampen consequently quotients by that circle's normal closure and adds neither generators nor other relations. Paths chosen within P identify the circle factors for different boundary components because the mapping torus is a product. Thus pi1(OB(P,id))=pi1(P), even when the boundary is disconnected.

This calculation does **not** establish pi1(X)=pi1(P) for general monodromy. In that case the mapping-torus presentation includes t g t^{-1}=phi_*(g). After capping, t is killed, imposing g=phi_*(g). When the boundary has several components, choosing paths gamma_j between fixed boundary basepoints introduces further relations represented, up to inversion/conjugacy, by gamma_j phi(gamma_j)^{-1}. Relative arc motion matters. All these extra relations are trivial for id. Consequently the author is entitled to infer pi1(P)=1 exactly in the restricted case being classified.

### Boundary and page classification

For simply connected P, integral Poincare-Lefschetz duality gives

\[
H_2(P,B;Z)\cong H^1(P;Z)=0.
\]

The segment H2(P,B)->H1(B)->H1(P)=0 of the exact sequence then forces H1(B;Z)=0. Classification of connected oriented closed surfaces makes every boundary component S2. This is an integral argument with no torsion gap.

Capping these c spheres with ordinary three-balls does not change pi1. The closed resulting smooth three-manifold is simply connected, so the three-dimensional Poincare theorem, together with uniqueness of smoothing in dimension three, makes it diffeomorphic to S3. Smooth three-dimensional Schoenflies and the standard position of a finite collection of disjoint smoothly embedded balls identify P with the standard c-holed S3. No four-dimensional Poincare or Schoenflies assertion is invoked; in particular, no unknown statement about exotic four-balls is smuggled into the argument.

### Product handles and smooth type

For c=1 the page is D3; P x D2 is D5 after smoothing corners, and its boundary is the standard S4.

For c>=2 the c-holed S3 is the boundary connected sum of c-1 copies of S2 x I. A connecting three-dimensional 1-handle becomes an ordinary five-dimensional 1-handle after multiplication by D2. Each product summand is

\[
(S^2\times I)\times D^2\cong S^2\times D^3.
\]

The boundary of a five-dimensional boundary connected sum is the four-dimensional connected sum of the boundaries. Therefore

\[
\partial(P\times D^2)\cong\#_{c-1}(S^2\times S^2).
\]

This explicit product construction supplies the standard smooth type and its untwisted framing. It is not an inference from an even intersection form, a homotopy type, or Freedman's topological classification. Those weaker inputs would not justify the smooth conclusion.

Conversely, precisely these product pages give the stated standard connected sums, with c=k+1 spherical binding components and b2=2k. This proves the equivalence and sharpness, including k=0. The homology check gives H2(X;Z)=Z^(2k) and hyperbolic intersection form H^k, consistent with the geometry. No exotic smooth structure is implicitly identified with the standard one.

## 4. Sphere-twist countercheck and framing

Hsueh's published article, Section 5.2, identifies the two open books on S2 x I arising from the two relative mapping classes. The audit independently checked that portion, its diagrams, and the definition of sphere twist in Section 6.1. [H]

An explicit twist has the form (x,t)->(R_t x,t), where R_t is a loop representing the nonzero element of pi1(SO(3))=Z/2, constant near both endpoints. It fixes boundary collars. Its maps on H0 and H2 are identity; the other positive homology groups of S2 x I vanish. Thus it is homologically trivial.

The open book is the oriented S2-bundle over S2 whose clutching loop is R_t. The nontrivial loop gives the twisted bundle, diffeomorphic to CP2 # overline(CP2). In Hsueh's diagrams the Hopf-link framings are 0 and 1, whereas identity monodromy gives 0 and 0. The forms are respectively

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\cong\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle-1\rangle,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Oddness versus evenness distinguishes them even topologically. Both have signature zero and b2=2, but only the identity example belongs to Proposition 2. The nonidentity example directly refutes the proposed weakening to homologically trivial monodromy. It does not refute Kirby's existence question, since both manifolds actually admit the displayed open books.

## 5. Literature and remaining mathematical gap

The primary K3 PDF's printed pages 290-291 state the smooth existence problem and its simply connected signature-zero subquestion. Inspection of the rendered page confirms the overbar on the second CP2 in the odd-form example. Replacing that example by CP2 # CP2 would incorrectly change its signature to 2. The manuscript correctly restores the overbar. The title correction supplied with this audit is bibliographic only. [K3]

Kastenholz's Theorems A and B establish the vanishing-simplicial-volume obstruction and exhibit products of surfaces of genus at least two, retaining an obstruction after connected sum with any four-manifold. The surface-product factor has nontrivial fundamental group, and connected sum in dimension four produces a free product of fundamental groups. It remains nontrivial after these stabilizations. Hence these examples do not settle the simply connected subquestion. The arXiv record still displays only v1 in the bounded check; no journal publication was verified. [K]

Lawande-Saha's Theorem 1.2 uses connected sums of such products with copies of S1 x S3 to obtain parallelizable examples. For a product with Euler characteristic 4(g-1)(h-1), taking 2(g-1)(h-1) such summands makes Euler characteristic zero. The product group survives as a free factor, so these examples also are not simply connected. The source's informal claim that a product of hyperbolic surfaces is “hyperbolic” need not, and in the real-hyperbolic sense must not, be imported; positive simplicial volume is the required fact. Its record displays v1, with no journal publication verified here. [LS]

The manuscript's last attempted route, inferring a gauge-theoretic obstruction merely from the number of spherical binding components, is correctly stopped. Binding components have trivial normal bundles, but the genus identity alone does not establish a nonzero homology class for any of them. The entire oriented boundary of a page vanishes in ambient homology. The S4 example is a valid caution at b2=0; it is not evidence that all binding spheres must vanish when b2>0. No Seiberg-Witten conclusion is proved.

The accepted result classifies a relative monodromy class, not arbitrary open books. It neither proves nor disproves open-book existence on every simply connected signature-zero smooth four-manifold, including exotic smoothings. No exhaustive literature-absence or novelty claim is justified by a bounded search.
