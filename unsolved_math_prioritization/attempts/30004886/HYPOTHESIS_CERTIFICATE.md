# Prior resolution certificate for problem 30004886

Checked 9 October 2026. Source identifier OWR-8415355-007.

## Verdict

**PRIOR RESOLVED, with no remaining hypothesis mismatch found.** The complex-algebraic conjecture follows from Serganova–Sherman–Vaintrob's Sylow theory (2024). Its standard block subgroup is precisely the relevant Sylow subgroup. Entova-Aizenbud–Serganova–Sherman's subsequently published Green-correspondence paper explicitly uses this group identification.

This is a source-and-implication certificate for an existing theorem, not a new proof-search approach or an independent verification of every proof in the cited papers. The original source audit consumed zero new proof-search approaches.

## Original target

Vera Serganova, joint work with Inna Entova-Aizenbud and Alexander Sherman, “Green correspondence for supergroups,” Oberwolfach Report 52/2021, printed pp. 2840–2841, DOI 10.4171/OWR/2021/52. These are PDF pages 14–15 of the [official report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46930#page=14).

The conjecture immediately after Theorem 1 on printed p. 2841 asks for the following containment: for each splitting subgroup K of GL(m|n), there is a group element g such that gHg^{-1} is a subgroup of K.

The preceding definitions and theorem fix the data:

- The base field is C, and groups are algebraic supergroups.
- G = GL(m|n), with m ≥ n.
- H = SL(1|1)^n is embedded by disjoint standard (1|1) blocks.
- K is a quasireductive algebraic subsupergroup.
- Splitting means that the natural G-map C → Ind_K^G C has a G-equivariant retraction.

The report calls G quasireductive when its even underlying group G_0 is reductive. The induction module is allowed to be infinite-dimensional. The subsequent separate Green-correspondence conjecture on indecomposable modules is not the target here.

## Checking the standard embedding and the global form

The following calculation identifies the input to those results; it avoids inferring conjugacy merely from an abstract Lie-algebra isomorphism.

Write V = C^{m-n|0} ⊕ V_1 ⊕ ... ⊕ V_n with each V_i of dimension (1|1). Choose paired even and odd basis vectors e_i and f_i. Relative to the usual diagonal Cartan, let α_i = ε_i − δ_i. These n roots are linearly independent, isotropic and mutually orthogonal. They form a maximal such set because the defect of gl(m|n) is min(m,n) = n.

Put x_i = E_{e_i,f_i}, y_i = E_{f_i,e_i}, and z_i = E_{e_i,e_i} + E_{f_i,f_i}. The superbrackets give [x_i,y_i] = z_i; the other brackets within that factor vanish, and brackets between distinct factors vanish. Consequently the algebra generated by the opposite root spaces is

h = ⊕_{i=1}^n span_C{x_i,y_i,z_i} = sl(1|1)^n.

Its natural connected embedded group is exactly H. The even part H_0 is the n-dimensional torus acting by the same scalar t_i on e_i and f_i and by the identity on the remaining even subspace. Its Lie algebra is span{z_i}, while its odd space is span{x_i,y_i}. Thus there is no extra GL(m−n) factor, central quotient, or isogeny substituted for the required subgroup.

Both G and H are global forms of their respective Lie algebras in SSV's Definition 2.4: they are connected, and a closed subgroup containing the full Lie superalgebra cannot have a smaller even identity component or odd tangent space. Equivalently, the super Harish-Chandra pair for h has exactly the torus H_0 just described. SSV Lemma 6.19 therefore applies to Lemma 8.7 and makes this actual embedded H a Sylow subgroup of G. Lemma 6.21 records the conjugation-equivariant correspondence, rather than an unqualified rule that abstractly isomorphic Lie superalgebras determine equal groups.

A separate group-level check appears at the start of §4.1, printed/PDF p. 13 of [Entova-Aizenbud–Serganova–Sherman, arXiv:2507.09912v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09912v1#page=13): for GL(m|n) the Sylow subgroup is SL(1|1)^d, d = min(m,n). This independently corroborates the group identification; the embedded root calculation above supplies the precise match to the OWR block embedding.

## Why an arbitrary splitting subgroup contains that Sylow subgroup

Let K ⊆ G be any quasireductive splitting subgroup as in the original problem.

1. Apply Sylow existence to K itself, obtaining S ⊆ K minimal among the subgroups splitting in K.
2. Since S splits in K and K splits in G, transitivity gives that S splits in G.
3. To check minimality in G, suppose T ⊊ S were a quasireductive subgroup splitting in G. Applying the converse direction of transitivity to T ⊆ K ⊆ G shows that T splits in K. This contradicts the choice of S. Hence S is Sylow in G.
4. The verified block subgroup H is also Sylow in G. Conjugacy now gives an element g ∈ G_0(C) with S = gHg^{-1}.
5. Therefore gHg^{-1} ⊆ K, exactly the old conjecture.

The converse direction in step 3 is important: forward transitivity alone would not prove that a Sylow subgroup chosen inside K is minimal in G. Corollary 6.4 supplies precisely the missing direction.

One can alternatively choose a subgroup of K minimal among those splitting in G, using the same existence argument, then apply conjugacy directly. The argument above explicitly verifies the requested inside-K Sylow route.

## Connectedness and other qualifications

No connectedness assumption on K needs to be added. SSV's definition covers disconnected quasireductive supergroups, so every group used in the inside-K argument is within its scope. If the older word “reductive” is interpreted to demand connectedness, that merely makes the original scope narrower than the theorem being applied.

Moreover the Sylow subgroup itself is connected. For example, K^0 ⊆ K has the same odd Lie space, and the quotient by the identity component is purely even finite. Its constant functions split by averaging in characteristic zero. Applied to a Sylow subgroup S, this and transitivity would contradict minimality unless S = S^0. The same conclusion is recorded by the odd-generation results in SSV, Remark 6.9 and Remark 2.9(i). Thus Lie-to-group reconstruction only concerns connected Sylow groups, even when K is disconnected.

The opening sentence of Green §2.1 has a tautological wording error (it says the identity component is connected). Its introduction supplies reductivity, and the precise hypothesis used here is SSV Definition 2.1. The parenthetical discussion of disconnectedness in Green §4.1 does not impose a new assumption on GL(m|n).

The equality Ind_K^G C = C[G/K] identifies the OWR definition with SSV Definition 6.1(1); there is no finite-dimensionality restriction accidentally added. Conjugacy under G_0 is stronger than the unspecified group conjugacy in the original sentence. If n = 0, H is the trivial group and the containment claim is immediate. For m < n the analogous statement replaces n by min(m,n), but the OWR target already assumes m ≥ n.
