# Five completed approaches

All approaches concern the exact triangle sign set from AIM Problem 1.32. None resolves its general optimum. This is a partial-result investigation, 5/5 substantive approaches.

## 1. Symmetry and rebasing

Recovered the true six-element triangle action, checked determinants and edge permutations, and separated it from independent rotations. Passing from the independent reflection and one rebase to a shear proves that their common decaying invariant subspace is zero. Valid triangle averaging therefore produces non-independent competitors at every objective value. This strengthens the old set-symmetry obstruction but does not decide value equality.

## 2. Product duality and support

Rechecked the published product-value theorem and its scaling to the square sign set. Reproved the independent-subclass collapse by rotating vectors oppositely. Tested the natural missing lower-bound route: tensoring radial one-point duals. For n>=2, any nonzero radial support contains a full sphere and hence two distinct points less than one unit apart. Their product lies outside the required triangle support. Thus that route fails rigorously; correlated or nonradial dual measures remain possible.

## 3. Edge-restriction conversion

Derived all three Fourier slice formulas, including the diagonal slice rather than a pointwise Fourier restriction. Each slice normalization is strictly positive. A triangle-symmetric Gaussian family yields an exact all-dimensional counterexample to the sufficient condition that one edge mass be at least the square root of the original objective. This proves that all three naive restriction conversions may worsen the bound even after genuine symmetrization.

## 4. Direct primal tensor

Tested the function-level construction g(x)g(y). Fourier positivity and normalization survive, but any strictly negative admissible one-point value becomes positive at (u,-u). Explicit Gaussian one-point functions supply such examples. This excludes this universal formula, not arbitrary nonlinear conversion schemes.

## 5. Triangle-invariant Gaussian optimization

Computed the Fourier transform of (1-q)exp(-beta q) with q=|x|^2+|y|^2-<x,y>, proving global sign and Fourier certificates, then solved its one-parameter optimization exactly. The optimum beta=n+1 has the stated explicit objective. Every function in this family is square-feasible, proving that this promising angular family cannot yield a strict improvement on Cohn–Elkies.

## Scope and novelty

The symmetry-incompatibility theorem, all-edge-mass counterexample, and radial-dual support obstruction are the additions beyond the cached partial attempt. Their arguments are elementary; novelty confidence is low and no priority claim is made. No unverified floating-point certificate or numerical optimizer is used.

Equality follows from prior sharp lattice cases n=1,8,24. The general triangle value and a universal objective-preserving optimizer conversion remain unresolved. Full triangle symmetry and separate evenness must not be simultaneously imposed in any Schwartz search.
