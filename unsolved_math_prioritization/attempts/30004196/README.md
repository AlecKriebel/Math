## Accepted scope

The main arrival-time theorem concerns smooth closed embedded globally
mean-convex initial surfaces in R^3. After exact extinction recentering, an
oval limit forces distinct spherical extinction centers to approach a
cylindrical point asymptotically along its axis, with subquadratic time offsets
and oval scale smaller than spatial separation. The arrival-time Hessian
jumps by exactly 1/2 in operator norm. The separate fixed-flow moving-center
escape lemma retains its compactness and tangent-flow monotonicity assumptions.

The Hessian-continuity exclusion and nondegenerate-neck exclusion are
conditional. The latter retains both the applicable CHH neck/continuation
hypotheses and the Sun–Wang–Xue isolation hypotheses. No universal
nondegeneracy or continuous-Hessian assertion is made.

The full infinite C^{1,1}, twice-differentiable scalar bump construction matches
selected critical jets but is not an arrival-time PDE solution. The complete
audit includes an explicit smooth noncritical transition point where the
arrival operator equals 4229/18 rather than -1, with residual 4247/18.
This scalar example supplies no mean curvature flow.

The varying-flow peanut theorem and Miura's initially singular construction
do not realize the requested later-time oval in one flow with smooth initial
data. The direct scaled-curvature obstruction excludes only that implantation
method. The broader original non-globally-mean-convex question is retained.
