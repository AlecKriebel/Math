# Source audit and exact residual scope

The full original source is the Khanin contribution, joint with Bakhtin and Voltz, in [OWR 28/2014](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46518), printed pp.1542–1543. The rendered p.1543 was checked. Paths are lazy nearest-neighbour integer paths, the action is minimized, and the random potential is the product of independent iid spatial and temporal fields. The compact support and positive-interior density assumption is stated for the potential V, while only mean zero is explicitly required of the temporal factor. The original prose also describes a corner at zero; a near-zero linear piece therefore means a one-sided piece with endpoint zero. Its normalization and accompanying scaling assertions are not silently assumed valid under every possible decomposition of V.

The complete primary [BKMV v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08379v2), dated 28 January 2025, was read for the model, Theorems 2.1–2.4, Remark 2.5, Sections 3–4 and 8, and Appendix A. Its fair temporal signs and even spatial density are additional hypotheses. Theorem 2.4 assumes a positive endpoint power exponent. Remark 2.5 expects a possible extension and conjectures failure close to exponent −1; neither assertion is a theorem for the remaining regime.

The new partial note credits the source's loop ordering, loop removal, concentration mechanism and shape argument. It checks the bounded-environment shape argument without a power-law endpoint assumption and retains the entropy factor in the deleted-time-subset count. The proof gives its own uniform two-sided concentration bound including m=0. It uses only that a compressed path is admissible; it does not require the stronger assertion that such a path is still optimal. The discrepancy-tail event is written with its signs derived directly from the stated discrepancy. These choices avoid reliance on potentially misleading compressed displays in the source.

The sufficient condition in the note is an infinite series in the actual local discrepancy law. A logarithmic endpoint bound with exponent p>3/2 makes the series converge. It also includes the known positive-power case, which is not claimed as new. In the uniform case the discrepancy tail is of order k^(-2), and the series diverges; this is only failure of the sufficient argument. It is not a counterexample to a linear edge.
