# Mathematical approach log

Two approaches were used out of a maximum of five. Literature retrieval and identity verification are not counted as mathematical approaches.

## Approach 1 Direct extension and comparison

Try to transfer a resolutive function on the cut boundary to the original boundary and dominate its cut-domain solution by a global PWB solution. This yields the conditional transfer lemma in `PROOF.md`. The needed resolutivity implication is false: the explicit two-disk lens supports distance^(-5/4) data integrable for cut-domain harmonic measure but not for original-domain harmonic measure. The artificial-boundary data in this example are identically zero. Result: a proved obstruction to this route, with no conclusion against the target implication.

## Approach 2 Uniform harmonic measure localization

Use the known B regularity criterion to seek local domination for harmonic measures of shrinking cut domains. The global criterion concerns original-boundary sets; it does not automatically imply domination on the new spherical boundary. On inherited boundary pieces, domain inclusion gives the wrong one-sided information for pulling back integrability, as Approach 1 demonstrates. Furthermore, arbitrary bounded open sets and their cuts need not be connected or have finitely many components. No uniform estimate addressing these issues was obtained.
