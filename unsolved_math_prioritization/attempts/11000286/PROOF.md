# A bounded obstruction to integral homological stabilization for outer automorphism groups

## Scope and statement

Let `F_r` denote the free group of rank `r`. All group homology below has trivial integral coefficients unless coefficients are displayed otherwise.

**Proposition.** Suppose `n,m >= 8`, `n != m`, and `m < n(n-1)/2`. Every group homomorphism

`phi: Out(F_n) -> Out(F_m)`

induces the zero map on second integral homology. Both its source and target second homology groups are nonzero. Consequently no such homomorphism induces an integral homology isomorphism throughout the common stable range. In particular this excludes a homologically stabilizing homomorphism `Out(F_n) -> Out(F_(n+1))` for every `n >= 8`.

This is a bounded consequence of established results, with an elementary explanation of the required nonzero homology class. No novelty or resolution for arbitrary larger target ranks is claimed.

## 1. The three external inputs and their precise maps

1. Kielak, Theorem 6.8: for distinct positive integers `n,m`, with `n >= 6` and `m < binomial(n,2)`, every homomorphism `Out(F_n) -> Out(F_m)` has image contained in a subgroup isomorphic to `C_2`. There is no parity restriction on `n` in this theorem. The source rank is `n`, so its binomial bound must not be reversed. [Kielak, arXiv:1103.1624v2, pp.2 and 26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.1624v2); [published version](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jds077).
2. Galatius, Theorem 1.1: the specific inclusion `j_r: S_r -> Aut(F_r)` obtained by permuting a free basis induces an isomorphism on `H_k(-;Z)` when `r > 2k+1`. Thus it is an isomorphism on `H_2` for `r >= 6`. The integral convention is also explicit in the equivalent formulation on p.3. [Galatius, arXiv:math/0610216v3, pp.2-3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610216v3); [published version](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/173-2/p03).
3. Hatcher and Vogtmann, Corollary 1: the actual quotient homomorphism `q_r: Aut(F_r) -> Out(F_r)` induces an integral homology isomorphism in degree `k` for `r >= 2k+4`; at `r = 2k+3` that statement only guarantees surjectivity. Hence `(q_r)_*` is an isomorphism on `H_2` for `r >= 8`. [Hatcher and Vogtmann, *Homology stability for outer automorphism groups of free groups*, printed pp.1253 and 1271, PDF pp.1 and 19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406377).

No claim about stabilization homomorphisms between different outer-automorphism groups is hidden in inputs 2 or 3. They compare homology using `S_r -> Aut(F_r) -> Out(F_r)` at a fixed rank.

## 2. A self-contained nonzero integral class for `S_r`, `r >= 4`

### 2.1 An integral cycle and a detector lemma

In the normalized bar chain complex with trivial integral coefficients,

`d[g|h] = [h] - [gh] + [g]`,

`d[g|h|k] = [h|k] - [gh|k] + [g|hk] - [g|h]`.

A symbol containing the identity is interpreted as zero. If `a,b` commute, then

`c = [a|b] - [b|a]`

is an integral 2-cycle, since its boundary is `[ba]-[ab]=0`.

Consider any central extension `1 -> {+1,-1} -> E -> G -> 1`. Choose a set-theoretic section `s` with `s(1)=1`, and define `alpha(g,h)` in the additive group `F_2` by

`s(g)s(h) = (-1)^alpha(g,h) s(gh)`.

Associativity and centrality give

`alpha(h,k) - alpha(gh,k) + alpha(g,hk) - alpha(g,h) = 0` in `F_2`.

Thus alpha extends Z-linearly from the integral bar 2-chains to `F_2` and vanishes on every integral 2-boundary. For commuting `a,b`, cancellation of the common section value `s(ab)=s(ba)` gives

`[s(a),s(b)] = (-1)^(alpha(a,b)-alpha(b,a))`.

Here the commutator convention is `[x,y]=xyx^(-1)y^(-1)`. Therefore, if the two lifts anticommute, `alpha(c)=1`, so `c` cannot be an integral boundary.

This detects integral homology directly. It does not infer nonzero integral `H_2` from a nonsplit extension alone, nor merely from nonzero mod-2 homology. An integral boundary would have zero evaluation under this particular mod-2-valued cocycle, whereas this integral cycle has evaluation one.

### 2.2 Constructing the needed double cover and checking its signs

Use the real Clifford algebra `Cl_r^+` generated by `e_1,...,e_r` subject to

`e_i^2=1`, and `e_i e_j = -e_j e_i` for `i != j`.

The increasing monomials `e_I` indexed by subsets `I` form a basis. For completeness, the relations put every word in their span. Their linear independence follows from the representation on the exterior algebra of `R^r` in which `e_i` acts as exterior multiplication by `e_i` plus contraction by `e_i`: applying a product with distinct increasing indices to the scalar 1 gives the corresponding exterior basis vector.

For vectors `u,x` in `R^r`, the relations give `ux+xu=2<u,x>`. A unit vector has `u^2=1`, and hence

`-uxu = x - 2<u,x>u`.

This is reflection in the hyperplane orthogonal to `u`.

Set `u_i=(e_i-e_(i+1))/sqrt(2)` for `1 <= i < r`, and let `E_r` be the subgroup of the units of `Cl_r^+` generated by these vectors. Each element `w` has a well-defined parity `p` (even or odd), and twisted conjugation

`rho(w)(x) = (-1)^p w x w^(-1)`

defines a group homomorphism. Each `rho(u_i)` interchanges `e_i` and `e_(i+1)` and fixes the other basis vectors. Thus its image is precisely the permutation-matrix copy of `S_r`.

Here is a direct kernel check. If `rho(w)=1`, then `we_j=(-1)^p e_jw` for every `j`. Expand `w` in the monomial basis; all its monomials have parity `p`. For a nonempty monomial `e_I`, choose `j` in `I`. Moving `e_j` past `e_I` gives the sign `(-1)^(|I|-1)`, the opposite of `(-1)^p`. Because multiplication by `e_j` permutes the monomial basis up to signs, this discrepancy cannot cancel against another coefficient. Therefore every nonempty-monomial coefficient is zero and `w` is scalar. Reversal of a word defines an anti-automorphism fixing vectors; for a product of unit vectors, `w reverse(w)=1`. A scalar kernel element consequently satisfies `w^2=1` and is `+1` or `-1`.

When `r >= 4`, let `u=u_1=(e_1-e_2)/sqrt(2)` and `v=u_3=(e_3-e_4)/sqrt(2)`. They are orthogonal, so

`u^2=v^2=1`, `uv=-vu`, and `[u,v]=uvuv=-1`.

Thus `-1` actually belongs to `E_r`. We have proved the central extension

`1 -> {+1,-1} -> E_r -> S_r -> 1`,

without needing a classification of Schur multipliers or an unproved convention for the Pin cover. This is the relevant Clifford realization of that cover.

### 2.3 Detection

Take `a=(12)`, `b=(34)` in `S_r`, and choose the section so that `s(a)=u`, `s(b)=v`. They commute in `S_r`, but their chosen lifts have commutator `-1`. The detector lemma gives `alpha([a|b]-[b|a])=1`. Accordingly

`H_2(S_r;Z) != 0` for every `r >= 4`.

Only nonvanishing has been proved or used; the complete value of the Schur multiplier is unnecessary.

## 3. Vanishing of the induced map and completion

By Kielak's theorem, `phi` factors as

`Out(F_n) -> C_2 -> Out(F_m)`.

This also covers a trivial image by taking the first map to be trivial. To compute `H_2(C_2;Z)`, write `C_2=<t | t^2=1>`. Its periodic free resolution has differentials `t-1` in odd degrees and `1+t` in positive even degrees. After tensoring with the trivial integral module, the chain differentials in degrees 3, 2, and 1 are respectively `0`, multiplication by `2`, and `0`. Therefore

`H_2(C_2;Z) = ker(2:Z->Z) / im(0:Z->Z) = 0`.

Functoriality implies `phi_*=0` on integral second homology.

For either `r=n` or `r=m`, the hypotheses give `r >= 8`. The actual composite

`H_2(S_r;Z) --(j_r)_*--> H_2(Aut(F_r);Z) --(q_r)_*--> H_2(Out(F_r);Z)`

is an isomorphism by the two stability inputs. Its domain is nonzero by Section 2, so its target is nonzero. A zero map between the two resulting nonzero groups cannot be an isomorphism. Degree two lies in the common verified stable range because both ranks are at least eight.

Finally, `binomial(n,2)-(n+1)=(n^2-3n-2)/2>0` for every `n >= 8`, so `m=n+1` satisfies the required strict upper bound. This proves the proposition.

## 4. Relation to the original question and limits

The source is Bridson and Vogtmann, Chapter 20 of Farb's *Problems on Mapping Class Groups and Related Topics*, Question 3.6, printed p.326 (PDF p.333). The question asks for a rank-changing map that realizes the homology isomorphism in the stable range; the immediately preceding sentence explicitly specifies integral coefficients. The subsequent paragraph introduces different quotient questions and is not part of Question 3.6. [Original source](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf#page=333).

The isolated question does not explicitly write `m>n` or `m!=n`. Distinct ranks are required for a substantive interpretation, since `m=n` admits the identity. The nearby discussion concerns embeddings for `m>=n`, so increasing rank is the intended stabilization setting. Our proposition is explicit about its hypotheses and additionally covers smaller distinct target ranks within its bound.

With rational coefficients, positive-degree stable homology vanishes: for `k>0` and `r>=2k+4`, combine the same two comparisons with the rational homology vanishing of the finite group `S_r`. Hence every homomorphism gives an isomorphism `0 -> 0` in those positive degrees, and the induced map on degree zero is the identity on `Q`. Rational coefficients therefore remove this particular obstruction and are not interchangeable with the integral question.

The result concerns group homomorphisms, or equivalently homotopy classes of based maps between their classifying spaces. It does not exclude abstract isomorphisms of the stable homology groups; the cited stability results provide those. No conclusion is drawn for `m >= binomial(n,2)`, for arbitrary rank jumps, for ranks below eight, or outside the verified homological range. The full arbitrary-rank question is left unresolved here.
