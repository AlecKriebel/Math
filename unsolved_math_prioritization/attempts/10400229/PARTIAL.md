# One crossing from a mirror: algebraic constraints and a rational-diagram case

**Target:** 10400229 / AMR-103-0229, Stoimenow's Problem 12.24.  
**Status:** original question unresolved; three substantive approaches used. Independent review pending. No novelty claim.

The original asks whether a **prime knot** that becomes its mirror after one ordinary crossing change must be achiral or slice, with the parenthetical “algebraically?” explicitly offering a weaker sliceness conclusion. The source is Ohtsuki's collection, printed p.541, and Stoimenow's shorter problem list, Problem 7. Neither a noncoherent band attachment nor a Dehn surgery is substituted for a crossing change. We work with classical knots in S³. An arbitrary orientation may be chosen for algebraic concordance; reversing that orientation does not affect the conclusions below. The source does not impose additional concordance-category hypotheses, and we do not settle either its sliceness question or its weaker algebraic version. [Full original collection](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf), [proposer's list](https://stoimenov.net/stoimeno/homepage/papers/12pb.pdf).

We prove the proposed achiral-or-slice conclusion when the specified crossing occurs in a signed continued-fraction diagram of a rational knot. We also give an all-knot algebraic necessary condition and a separate rank-two Seifert-matrix criterion. The diagram and matrix qualifications are essential; neither is established for an arbitrary crossing in the original question.

## 1. The algebraic concordance class has order dividing two

Write G for Levine's integral algebraic concordance group, and use the symmetric normalization

\[
\Delta_K(t)=\Delta_K(t^{-1}),\qquad\Delta_K(1)=1.
\]

**Proposition 1.** If one crossing change takes K to its mirror, then

\[
2[K]=0\quad\hbox{in }G.
\]

**Proof.** Let K+ and K− be the two crossing resolutions. Kim–Livingston, Theorems 7.5–7.6, use the injective homomorphism

\[
G\longrightarrow W(\mathbb Q(t)),\qquad
V\longmapsto (1-t)V+(1-t^{-1})V^{\mathsf T},
\]

where the field involution is t↦t^{-1}. Their crossing-change formula represents the difference of the two Witt classes by

\[
\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\Delta_{K+}(t)\Delta_{K-}(t),-1\bigr).
\]

Mirroring preserves the normalized Alexander polynomial. Put its common value equal to D(t). Since D is symmetric and nonzero, scaling the first basis vector by D^{-1} identifies diag(D²,−1) with diag(1,−1), a hyperbolic form. Injectivity therefore gives [K+]=[K−] in G. Mirroring negates the algebraic concordance class, and orientation reversal leaves it unchanged. Hence [K]=−[K], as claimed. ∎

The theorem and its normalization were checked in the complete published primary paper, including its proof through the two-by-two Hermitian crossing block. This proposition is a direct application of that known theorem, not a new crossing-change formula. [Kim–Livingston, Pacific J. Math. 220 (2005), pp.94–98, especially Theorems 7.5–7.6](https://msp.org/pjm/2005/220-1/pjm-v220-n1-p05-s.pdf).

The oriented smoothing has zero Conway polynomial, as already noted in the original remark: the two knot Conway polynomials agree, and their skein difference is z times the smoothing polynomial. This does not prove that the smoothing bounds disjoint disks. If it did bound disjoint smooth disks, joining them by the smoothing band would give a smooth slice disk for K. The needed slice-link assertion is not established here.

## 2. Why order two cannot be discarded

The following is an algebraic diagnostic, not a counterexample to the knot question. Consider

\[
V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The skew part V−Vᵀ has determinant one, so V is an admissible integral Seifert form. Direct multiplication gives PᵀVP=−V and det P=−1. The graph of P inside Z²⊕Z² is a primitive rank-two subgroup on which V⊕V vanishes. Thus 2[V]=0.

But V is not metabolic, even over Q. An isotropic vector (x,y) would satisfy

\[
x^2-xy-y^2=0.
\]

If y=0 then x=0; otherwise x/y would be a rational root of u²−u−1, whose discriminant is 5. Therefore [V] is nonzero. Its normalized Alexander polynomial is 3−t−t^{-1}, and its determinant is 5. This explicit nonzero order-two form shows the precise limitation of Proposition 1. We do not infer the chirality or the existence of a mirror-changing crossing for a prime knot from a matrix.

Even adding a formal one-entry change and equal Alexander polynomials is insufficient at the matrix level. Define

\[
H_e=\begin{pmatrix}e&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
W_e=V\oplus H_e\quad(e=0,1).
\]

Each H_e is metabolic on the primitive subgroup generated by its second basis vector and has normalized Alexander polynomial one. Thus W0 and W1 have the same nonzero order-two class [V] and the same Alexander polynomial, while W1−W0 has just one nonzero diagonal entry, equal to one. These statements do not certify a pair of knots related by a geometrically specified crossing, much less a prime chiral knot isotopic to the mirror of the other resolution.

## 3. A conditional rank-two criterion

**Proposition 2.** Suppose the two resolutions of the specified crossing admit Seifert matrices of the form

\[
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V'=V+\varepsilon E_{11},\qquad\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},
\]

with det(V−Vᵀ)=1 and equal normalized Alexander polynomials. Then both forms are metabolic, so both knots are algebraically slice.

**Proof.** The determinant condition is (b−c)²=1. Direct expansion gives

\[
\det(V'-tV'^{\mathsf T})-\det(V-tV^{\mathsf T})
=\varepsilon d(1-t)^2.
\]

Both matrices have the same size, so equality of the normalized polynomials forces d=0. The primitive line spanned by the second basis vector is then a metabolizer for both forms. In addition,

\[
\Delta_K(t)=(b-ct)(b-ct^{-1}),\qquad
|\Delta_K(-1)|=(b+c)^2.
\]

This is consistent with the standard norm-factorization obstruction. ∎

The hypothesis is a **rank-two crossing-adapted pair of matrices**, not merely the assertion that K has genus one. The common Seifert surfaces supplied by a crossing-change construction may require stabilization. We have not shown that they can always be chosen with rank two, even if the individual knots have genus one. Proposition 2 does not assert smooth sliceness.

## 4. The complete conclusion for crossings in rational continued-fraction diagrams

For an integer z, put

\[
M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first column of M(a1)⋯M(an) is a primitive pair (p,q) representing the continued fraction [a1,…,an]. We use the usual numerator closure, so its determinant is |p| and it is a knot when p is odd. Simultaneously changing the signs of p,q leaves the fraction unchanged. After choosing p>0, the standard two-bridge knot classification identifies denominators congruent modulo p. The convention for the diagram may differ by a common mirror from another author's convention; the achiral and ribbon conclusions below are unaffected.

Changing one crossing in an integer twist region replaces its entry x by x−2ε for ε=±1. Negative coefficients, cancellation to a zero coefficient, and prefixes with zero numerator are all included in the calculation. The specified crossing must actually occur in that continued-fraction diagram; no conversion theorem for an arbitrary crossing is assumed.

**Proposition 3.** If one crossing in such a rational diagram changes a knot to its mirror, then the knot is achiral or ribbon. In particular it is achiral or smoothly slice.

**Proof.** Let the prefix and suffix products be

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R\binom10=\binom uw,
\]

where det Q=δ=±1 and gcd(u,w)=1. Empty products are permitted. The two fraction columns are

\[
\begin{aligned}
p&=(ax+b)u+aw, & q&=(cx+d)u+cw,\\
p'&=p-2\varepsilon au, & q'&=q-2\varepsilon cu.
\end{aligned}
\]

They are primitive because the products are unimodular. Since the two knots are mirrors, they have the same nonzero determinant: |p'|=|p|.

If p'=p, then au=0. If u=0, both p and q are independent of x, so the knots are identical. If a=0, unimodularity implies b,c=±1 and p=bu; hence q'−q=−2εcu is a multiple of p. The normalized denominator residue is unchanged, again giving the same knot type. Because the changed knot is its mirror, the knot is achiral. The case |p|=1 is the unknot and also causes no difficulty.

It remains to consider p'=−p and au≠0. Then

\[
p=\varepsilon au,\qquad
(x-\varepsilon)au+bu+aw=0.
\]

Unimodularity gives gcd(a,b)=1. Reducing the last equation modulo a shows a divides u. Reducing it modulo u and using gcd(u,w)=1 shows u divides a. Consequently u=ηa for η=±1, and

\[
p=\varepsilon\eta a^2.
\]

Substitution into q gives

\[
q=\eta(\varepsilon ac+\delta).
\]

Normalize the numerator to P=|p|=a². The denominator becomes

\[
\widetilde q=\operatorname{sign}(p)q=ac+\varepsilon\delta.
\]

Set m=|a|. If m=1 the knot is the unknot. Otherwise gcd(a,c)=1, again by unimodularity. Choose k congruent to sign(a)c modulo m with 0<k<m. Then gcd(m,k)=1 and

\[
\widetilde q\equiv mk\pm1\pmod{m^2}.
\]

Both numbers mk±1 lie strictly between zero and m² for m>1. As P is odd, m is odd. Thus the knot, up to the harmless convention of taking its mirror, is

\[
K(m^2,mk\pm1),\qquad m>k>0,\quad\gcd(m,k)=1.
\]

This is a classical two-bridge ribbon family: see Lisca, Definition 1.1(1) and Corollary 1.3, or the explicit symmetric-union construction in Horigome–Ichihara, Theorem 1. Hence K is ribbon and smoothly slice. ∎

The ribbon-family theorem is credited prior mathematics, not inferred from a square determinant alone. A square determinant for an arbitrary knot is only a necessary sliceness condition. The proof also shows why a purely numerical search for mirror pairs in this rational-diagram class cannot yield a counterexample.

As a sign check, [3,−1,−3]=9/4 and [3,1,−3]=9/2 come from first columns (9,4) and (−9,−2). The middle crossing changes sign, and 4·2≡−1 mod 9 identifies the second knot as the mirror of the first. Both belong to the ribbon family m=3,k=1.

The elementary continued-fraction matrix convention and the explicit symmetric-union ribbon proof were checked in the complete Horigome–Ichihara preprint, Sections 2–3. Its Conway diagram is explicitly the mirror of Lisca's diagram convention; this has no effect on the conclusion. Lisca's full published paper was consulted for the parameter family and classification. [Lisca, Geometry & Topology 11 (2007), pp.429–432](https://msp.org/gt/2007/11-1/gt-v11-n1-p07-p.pdf), [Horigome–Ichihara, Theorem 1 and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07539v4).

## 5. Exact remaining gap and finite checks

None of the arguments converts an arbitrary mirror-changing crossing of a prime knot into a crossing of the rational-diagram type above, or forces a rank-two crossing-adapted Seifert presentation. Even for a rational knot, an arbitrary diagram displaying the particular crossing is not assumed to be a continued-fraction diagram. Proposition 1 leaves genuine algebraic two-torsion, and the matrix examples do not supply the missing geometric realization. No prime chiral nonslice counterexample has been certified.

The verifier checks the normalized determinant identities, explicit metabolic/anti-isometric algebra, rank-two formulas, and the signed unimodular continued-fraction calculation, including zero-prefix and zero-suffix cases. These are exact finite controls supporting the proofs. They do not prove an arbitrary knot diagram is isotopic to a mirror, certify primeness, or replace the published ribbon theorem.

Recent work on chirally cosmetic noncoherent bands studies the H(2) operation, which is a different move. In particular, the 2026 version of the grid-diagram paper gives such bands for additional knots, but its own definition specifies band attachments. Those examples have not been used as crossing-change counterexamples. [Primary banding paper, version 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09749v3).

The full original question remains unresolved by this package. Priority for these elementary consequences and restricted deductions is not established.
