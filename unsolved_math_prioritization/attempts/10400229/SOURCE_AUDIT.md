# Source scope, prior work and literature limits

Audit date: 2026-09-30 UTC. This is a bounded primary-source audit, not a proof of continued worldwide openness or historical priority.

## Exact target

Ohtsuki's edited *Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), printed p.541, Problem 12.24, is the original source. The rendered page was inspected. Stoimenow's complete three-page *A Dozen of Knot Theoretical Problems*, dated20 August 2001, repeats the same question as Problem 7. The prime hypothesis, one ordinary crossing change, mirror target, achiral alternative and parenthetical algebraic-slice alternative are all retained.

The source does not assert that the specific crossing can be moved into a rational diagram, that the knot has genus one, or that crossing-adapted minimal Seifert surfaces exist. These additional hypotheses appear only in explicitly separated partial results. None of the partial arguments supplies a prime chiral counterexample. No orientation choice strengthens the original target: reversal leaves the algebraic concordance class unchanged, and the rational-diagram conclusion is about knot type and sliceness.

The adjacent smoothing observation is credited to Stoimenow. Vanishing Alexander polynomial is not promoted to a slice-link theorem.

## Primary ingredients checked

1. Se-Goo Kim and Charles Livingston, *Knot mutation: 4-genus of knots and algebraic concordance*, Pacific J. Math.220 (2005), 87–105. The complete published paper was obtained. Section 7, printed 94–98, was read, and printed 97 was rendered and checked. Theorems 7.5–7.6 give the injective Witt map and exact crossing-change formula with symmetric normalized Alexander polynomials. Their rank-two Hermitian reduction was checked. The order-two conclusion is an application of their theorem. URL: https://msp.org/pjm/2005/220-1/pjm-v220-n1-p05-s.pdf
2. Paolo Lisca, *Lens spaces, rational balls and the ribbon conjecture*, Geometry & Topology 11 (2007), 429–472. The complete published paper was obtained. The introduction defines two-bridge fractions and their isotopy/mirror conventions; Definition 1.1(1), Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 include the ribbon family K(m²,mk±1). Only this credited sufficient family is needed; the present work does not reproduce the full Donaldson-theoretic classification. URL: https://msp.org/gt/2007/11-1/gt-v11-n1-p07-p.pdf
3. Sayo Horigome and Kazuhiro Ichihara, *On two-bridge ribbon knots*, arXiv:2402.07539v4,26 May 2024; published J. Knot Theory Ramifications 33 (2024), 2450012. The complete preprint was obtained. Theorem 1 and Section 3 give an explicit symmetric-union presentation of the same ribbon family, and the continued-fraction matrix convention. The paper explicitly notes that its Conway diagrams are mirrors of Lisca's convention. Our conclusion is invariant under this common mirroring. No claim of a new ribbon family is made. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07539v4
4. Barbensi, Celoria, Ktenidis, with an appendix by Ichihara, Jong and Teragaito, *An exploration of low crossing and chiral cosmetic bands with grid diagrams*, arXiv:2504.09749v3,14 January 2026. The complete current version was obtained; its definitions and scope were checked. Its move is a noncoherent band attachment/H(2) move. The phrase “chirally cosmetic crossings” in its introductory discussion does not alter that definition. Its examples do not supply the ordinary crossing-change hypothesis here. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09749v3

Searches included the original question number and wording, one crossing from a mirror, chirally cosmetic crossing changes, reflection distance and Gordian distance. They also found papers about chirally cosmetic surgeries and H(2)-distance; these were not silently substituted. Zekovic's 2015 Gordian/H(2) paper has a primary search extract exhibiting the familiar one-crossing mirror pair in the stevedore ribbon family. Its complete publisher PDF retrieval failed with 502, so it is not used as a proof ingredient or an exhaustive knot-table source. Search absence does not establish novelty or current openness.
