# Exact source and modern scope comparison

## Original volume

The complete Chapter15 is by P. Hubert, H. Masur, T. Schmidt and A. Zorich, “Problems on billiards, flat surfaces and translation surfaces,” printed pp.248–260. Relevant definitions are in §2, pp.250–251. The original Problem21 is on p.257, in §2.4, “Topological and geometric properties of strata.” Both the PDF page and its surrounding discussion were read, and the equation/signatures were visually checked.

The chapter constructs translation/half-translation surfaces by polygon identifications and then identifies their underlying complex structures and differentials. It explicitly treats geometric and complex-analytic viewpoints as useful. The problem asks for a geometric invariant; it does not add a requirement that the invariant be computable solely from flat metric or polygon data. Its cited reference [La] is Lanneau's2003 preprint, later published in2008.

The Q notation refers to the nonsquare portion of integrable meromorphic quadratic differentials. Lanneau(2008), §1.1.1, pp.2–4, defines Q_g with q not a global square and with poles of order at most1. The original chapter's union includes the Abelian-square strata separately to recover the cotangent bundle. In particular one must not silently add square differentials from H(6) to Q(12).

## Reported algebraic invariant

The pair(X,q) determines its effective positive zero divisor div₀(q). In all four signatures its coefficients are divisible by3. Thus D=div₀(q)/3 is an integral divisor and O_X(D) is intrinsic. Pullback under an isomorphism preserves its section dimension; scalar multiplication of q and permutation of equal-order marked zeros leave it unchanged. It therefore defines algebro-geometric data on the unmarked/projectivized stratum as well as on a marked or unscaled version.

Chen–Möller's published classification assigns the two values1 and2 to the two components. For genus3 the geometric canonical-plane interpretation is respectively ordinary versus special alignment: the order9 zero is a flex; the line through the order6 and order3 zeros is tangent at the former; or the three order3 zeros are collinear. These interpretations require the non-hyperelliptic canonical-plane setting used in the proof. The intrinsic h⁰ formulation is the theorem being reported and is not restricted merely to generic plane models.

For Q(12), D=4z. Intrinsically h⁰(4z)>1 means that a nonconstant meromorphic function exists with no poles away from z and pole order at most4. This description does not assert a basepoint-free g¹₄ or an exact pole order without further hypotheses. In the non-hyperelliptic canonical model it corresponds to an unusually high-contact hyperplane section.

## Flat-geometric refinement remains open

Chen–Möller p.311 distinguishes the established algebro-geometric invariant from an unknown formula using flat geometry alone. Chen–Yu(2026), §3.5, p.8 and Problem3.2, repeats that distinction. Consequently the broad wording of the original problem admits a plausible prior-resolution reading, but a flat-only interpretation is not settled by this theorem report.
