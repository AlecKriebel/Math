## Component-postponement proof

In degree zero over F₂, each component has one surviving root: its earliest vertex. An edge either joins two components and kills the younger root, or closes a cycle. Since there are no two-simplices, every cycle-birth edge stays unpaired in degree one. Thus preserving all elder-rule labels, surviving roots, and cycle-birth edges is enough to preserve the entire labeled persistence pairing and unpaired simplices.

For an incident pair (v,e), just before e is added the component C containing its endpoint v has root exactly v. The other endpoint belongs to D, whose root u is older. Every vertex of C other than v is younger than v. There are no old prefix edges from C to its complement, by definition of a component. This proves both that the prefix subsequences can be separated and that replaying the C cells still respects face order.

After moving the outside prefix first and then inserting v,e, replaying the rest of C has only one new connection to the outside: the edge from v into D. The internal connectivity test on C is unchanged. Its root component now has root u instead of v, but both are earlier than every other C vertex. Hence each internal merger kills exactly its former labeled root, and each cycle edge remains a cycle edge. Internal components not yet joined to v preserve their original relative oldest-vertex order. All outside decisions are unchanged because their relative order and prefix adjacency have not changed.

At the start of the suffix the graph is identical, the component partition is identical, and every surviving root label is identical. All these root labels occur outside C; their relative order is unchanged. Consequently every later elder-rule comparison and cycle decision agrees. This is stronger than merely preserving the multiset of persistence intervals.

For the preservation of already consecutive pairs, a pair ending in the old prefix lies entirely in C or entirely outside C. A different pair ending in the suffix cannot have its birth vertex in C: every vertex of C other than v has already been paired, and v pairs with e. Therefore an already consecutive pair cannot be split by the moved subsequences. Insertions at the new junctions do not split a pair: the only distinguished vertex there is v, already reserved for e, and e cannot belong to another pair. Iterating over the finite incident matching therefore terminates with every matched pair an adjacent block, without undoing earlier blocks.

## Function and actual filtration

Blocks are disjoint because persistence is a matching. Give singleton positions their distinct even values; in a matched block starting at i assign 2i+1 to its vertex and 2i to its edge. Values in one block are strictly below all values in any later block.

For an unmatched incidence the face and coface occupy distinct blocks. The face-respecting order places the face's block first, so the unmatched arrow from edge to vertex strictly decreases the function. For a matched incidence the reversed arrow from vertex to edge strictly decreases by one. This proves all directed-path inequalities, not merely absence of directed cycles.

An edge has no higher coface in a graph, so its generated-subcomplex entry time is its function value. An unmatched vertex has smaller value than every incident edge, so it enters at its own value. A matched vertex has exactly one smaller-valued incident edge, its matched edge; it therefore enters simultaneously with that edge. No third simplex shares the event. Putting the vertex first within each two-cell event recovers exactly the constructed order. Thus the proof establishes the source's actual generated filtration and a valid tie refinement. Apparentness alone would not have sufficed, but is not the argument used here.

The empty graph is vacuous; isolated vertices and disconnected components require no modification. Cycle births are explicitly retained. Ordinary rather than augmented reduced H₀ is used for the unpaired-root bookkeeping, consistent with the submitted pairing conventions.
