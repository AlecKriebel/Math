# 30000991: realization of persistence pairings on finite graphs

**Status: scoped affirmative theorem for dimension at most one; the full higher-dimensional question remains unresolved.** Separate adversarial review is pending. No novelty or human-peer-review claim is made.

## 1. Exact original question and prior-work boundary

Knudson's [OWR contribution, printed pp. 1628–1630](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46173), starts with a finite simplicial complex and a simplex-wise filtration, with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$. Its persistence pairing $P$ is a set of labeled simplex pairs, not merely a multiset of interval lengths. The field $V_P$ retains exactly the pairs that are codimension-one incidences.

For any discrete gradient $V$, the source reverses its matched arrows in the Hasse diagram. It then chooses an integer-valued function $f$ strictly decreasing along every directed arrow and forms

$$K_t=\text{the subcomplex generated by }\{\sigma:f(\sigma)\le t\}.$$

A face-respecting refinement of simultaneous entries defines $P_{\{V,f\}}$. Conjecture 3 asks whether $f$ and such a refinement can be chosen so that $P_{\{V_P,f\}}=P$. An arbitrary original filtration index is not automatically an allowable $f$, because matched arrows point upward.

The adjacent problem 30000990 is Knudson's Conjecture 2. Alec Kriebel's [previous four-vertex counterexample](https://aleckriebel.github.io/Math/papers/knudson-gradient-path/) and its repository audits explicitly do not settle Conjecture 3. Those artifacts were read before beginning this attempt and were not edited. The known counterexample is a positive control for the present graph theorem, rather than a new counterexample claimed here.

## 2. The graph theorem

**Theorem.** Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex of dimension at most one, with any simplex-wise filtration and persistence pairing $P$. There exist an integer function $f$, strictly decreasing along the reversed Hasse diagram of $V_P$, and a refinement of the generated-subcomplex filtration such that

$$P_{\{V_P,f\}}=P.$$

The theorem allows disconnected graphs, isolated vertices, cycles, and the empty complex. It preserves the labeled pairing and unpaired simplices, not the original numerical birth and death times.

We use the usual elder rule for ordinary $H_0$: a newly added edge joining two distinct components kills the class born at the younger of their oldest vertices. An edge whose endpoints are already connected creates an $H_1$ class, which is never killed in a graph. Thus all finite persistence pairs are vertex–edge pairs. This is also exactly the elementary boundary-matrix reduction over $\mathbb F_2$.

### Component-postponement lemma

Let $\mathcal O$ be a simplex order inducing $P$. Suppose $(v,e)\in P$ is incident. One can change $\mathcal O$ so that $v,e$ occur consecutively, without changing $P$ or the set of $H_1$-birth edges. Moreover, any other persistence pair that was already consecutive remains consecutive.

**Proof.** Immediately before $e$ enters, it joins two distinct components $C,D$ of the prefix graph. Since $(v,e)$ is its persistence pair and $v$ is an endpoint of $e$, label these so that $v\in C$. The oldest vertex of $C$ is precisely $v$, whereas the oldest vertex $u$ of $D$ is older than $v$. Let $Q$ be the prefix before $e$. Write $Q_C$ for its subsequence of all vertices and edges belonging to $C$, and $Q_{\rm out}$ for the remaining subsequence. The earliest cell of $Q_C$ is $v$.

Replace the order

$$Q,\ e,\ \text{suffix}$$

by

$$Q_{\rm out},\ v,\ e,\ (Q_C\setminus\{v\}),\ \text{suffix},\tag{1}$$

keeping the original relative order inside each displayed subsequence.

This is a valid simplex filtration. There were no prefix edges between $C$ and the other prefix components. The other endpoint of $e$ remains present in $Q_{\rm out}$, and every replayed edge of $C$ still follows both endpoints. All cells of the old prefix together with $e$ have been restored before the unchanged suffix begins.

The cells in $Q_{\rm out}$ have the same persistence behavior: their components and relative vertex birth order are unchanged. The newly inserted vertex $v$ is younger than $u$, so $e$ pairs with $v$.

Now replay $Q_C\setminus\{v\}$. Its connectivity pattern is the same as in the original construction of $C$, except that the component containing $v$ is already attached to $D$ and has oldest vertex $u$ instead of $v$. Both $u$ and $v$ precede every other vertex of $C$. Therefore a merger involving that component kills the same younger root as before. Mergers between its other components retain their original vertex order and also kill the same root. An edge closes a cycle exactly when it did before. All original pairs internal to $C$ and all its $H_1$ births are preserved.

At the beginning of the suffix, the actual prefix complex, its component partition, and its surviving oldest-vertex labels are the same as before. The relative birth order among these surviving roots is unchanged, since they all lie outside $C$. Every subsequent elder-rule decision and cycle birth is therefore unchanged.

It remains to check consecutiveness of previously consecutive pairs. A pair ending before $e$ has both cells in $Q_C$ or both in $Q_{\rm out}$, because its edge and paired vertex belong to the same prefix component. A different pair ending after $e$ cannot have its vertex in $C$: the only vertex of $C$ still unpaired just before $e$ is $v$, whose partner is $e$. Thus no other persistence pair is split between the moved and unmoved subsequences. Internal consecutive pairs remain consecutive in their subsequence. The new insertion locations cannot split an unmoved consecutive pair, since the existing cell $e$ already separated the two sides of those locations and belongs only to the target pair. This proves the final assertion. ∎

### Making all incident pairs consecutive

Apply the lemma successively to every pair of $V_P$, skipping pairs already consecutive. The pairing remains $P$ throughout, so its incident subset remains exactly the same $V_P$. The lemma preserves every previously consecutive pair. After at most $|V_P|$ such steps we obtain an order

$$\mathcal O'=(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{N-1})$$

inducing $P$ in which every $(v,e)\in V_P$ is an adjacent block, with $v$ immediately before $e$.

### Constructing the required function

Initially set $f(\sigma_i)=2i$. For a matched block $(v,e)=(\sigma_i,\sigma_{i+1})$, replace those two values by

$$f(v)=2i+1,\qquad f(e)=2i.\tag{2}$$

Different blocks are disjoint, so the definition is unambiguous. Subtract the minimum value if necessary to obtain the source's normalization $\min f=0$; the empty complex is vacuous.

For a matched incidence, the reversed arrow is $v\to e$, and (2) gives $f(v)>f(e)$. For any unmatched incidence $w<d$, its cells belong to distinct adjacent blocks or singleton positions in $\mathcal O'$. The block containing $w$ precedes the block containing $d$, since $\mathcal O'$ respects faces and cannot interleave with the adjacent matched blocks. All function values in an earlier block are smaller than all values in a later block. Thus $f(d)>f(w)$, as required for the unmatched arrow $d\to w$. The strict arrow inequalities imply strict decrease along every nonempty directed path.

For an edge $e$, its entry time in the generated-subcomplex filtration is $f(e)$. A critical vertex enters at its own value, since every incident edge has larger value. A matched vertex $v$ enters with its unique matched edge $e$: that edge has smaller value than $v$, and all other incident edges have larger value than $v$. Consequently the only simultaneous entries are precisely the adjacent blocks $(v,e)$, at time $2i$ in (2). Refine each by putting $v$ before $e$. This recovers $\mathcal O'$ exactly, whose persistence pairing is $P$. ∎

## 3. Check on the credited prior example

For the previously published interval with order

$$a,b,c,d,cd,bd,ac,$$

one has $P=\{(d,cd),(c,bd),(b,ac)\}$ and $V_P=\{(d,cd)\}$. Its matched pair is already consecutive. The construction gives

$$f(a)=0,\ f(b)=2,\ f(c)=4,\ f(d)=7,\ f(cd)=6,\ f(bd)=10,\ f(ac)=12.$$

This function decreases along the reversed Hasse arrows. The generated filtration adds $d,cd$ together and, refined in that order, reproduces the original persistence pairs. Hence the absence of a fixed-field path from $ac$ to $b$ in Conjecture 2 does not obstruct realization in Conjecture 3. No prior result or publication is altered by this observation.

## 4. Reproduction, literature limits, and remaining gap

`construct_graph.py` implements (1)–(2). `verify.py` independently compares boundary reduction with the elder rule, checks every intermediate postponement, all final reversed-arrow inequalities, and the actual entry times of the generated subcomplexes. It also checks the credited example above. The deterministic test set comprises 467 graph filtrations with 524 postponements; all 8,852 exact assertions pass. These checks validate concrete controls; the proof above supplies the all-graph quantifier.

The original conjecture concerns all finite simplicial complexes. The component-postponement argument uses two graph-specific facts: every finite pair is in degree 0, and replacing the oldest vertex of a connected component by an older one preserves its later elder-rule behavior. There is no analogous argument here for higher-dimensional cycle representatives and relations. Therefore this result does not establish the original conjecture in dimension at least two.

The current [Bauer–Roll paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02345) constructs algebraic gradients after a change of chain basis. That does not by itself produce a descending function on the unchanged simplices required here. The current [depth-poset paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14364), v4 dated 23 May 2026, proves pairing-preserving cancellation results for shallow pairs; it also notes that cancellation of a non-shallow incident persistence pair can change other pairs. These results do not directly resolve this realization question for the fixed field of all incident pairs. No complete current resolution was verified.

A separate bounded search of small two-dimensional complexes found no counterexample to a weaker order-feasibility test: it sought orders preserving $P$ and making incident pairs apparent. Such an order was not, merely by being found, certified as a realization by the source's function construction. No continuum of filtrations, exhaustive higher-dimensional verification, or general affirmative conclusion is claimed from those diagnostics.

The original target remains **unsolved**, with two substantive approaches recorded: constrained finite-order exploration and the constructive graph theorem. Priority for the graph statement is unconfirmed; no novelty claim is made.
