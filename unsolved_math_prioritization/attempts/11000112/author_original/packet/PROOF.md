# Problem 11000112: a credited negative resolution

## Result and attribution

The proposed universal bound is false. Baykur's published construction gives twelve right-handed Dehn twists on a closed genus-three surface whose product is the identity and whose vanishing-cycle homology quotient is free abelian of rank four. Thus this example has `b1(G) = 4 > 3 = g`.

This is a reconstruction of an existing result, not a new counterexample or a novelty claim. The geometric construction is imported from the primary literature. The argument below proves the exact connection to the queued target and the integral quotient calculation. The accompanying executable verifies only the finite algebraic certificate and package integrity; it does not certify mapping-class-group equality from curve diagrams.

## Exact target and conventions

Korkmaz, *Problems on homomorphisms of mapping class groups*, Problem 2.7, printed p. 88 (physical PDF page 95) of Farb's collection asks: for a closed connected oriented genus-g surface S and a nonempty positive identity factorization, is the rank of H1(S; Z) modulo the subgroup generated by its twist curves always at most g? Here positive means right-handed; the quotient is of integral first homology, and its first Betti number is its rank after tensoring with Q. Curves may repeat. Orienting a twist curve either way changes the sign of its homology class but neither its twist nor the generated subgroup.

The succeeding paragraph imposing g >= 2 and introducing a marked point belongs to the setup of Problem 2.8. It is not an extra pointed-lift condition on Problem 2.7. The retained catalogue excerpt runs these passages together, and its previous research summary discusses a different classification task. The target is an inequality, not classification of all positive relations or existence of sections. We use g = 3, so no low-genus convention matters. The example has essential curves; no null-homotopic-twist loophole is used.

Primary problem source: https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf#page=95

## Geometric input

Use R. Inanc Baykur, *Small exotic 4-manifolds and symplectic Calabi-Yau surfaces via genus-3 pencils*, Open Book Series 5 (2022), 185-221, https://doi.org/10.2140/obs.2022.5.185 . In Sections 5.1-5.2, equations (44)-(47) and Figure 7, take the conjugating mapping class to be the identity. Equation (47) is then a twelve-factor positive relation in the closed genus-three mapping class group. Its factors are the six unprimed curves B0, B1, B2, A0, A1, A2 followed by their six primed partners. The four boundary twists in the lifted relation disappear on capping. Page 214, equations (48)-(59), provides the vanishing-cycle relators.

In the integral basis (a1, b1, a2, b2, a3, b3), abelianizing those relators gives a subgroup generated by

    u = (1, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0),
    v = (0, 1, 0, -1, 0, 1).

Every relator has vector u, u-v, v, or -v, and u and v themselves occur up to sign. In particular the subgroup is exactly Z u + Z v, not just a subspace containing it. This is the only homological input needed from the construction. The closed-surface relator, a product of commutators, contributes zero to abelianization.

Published PDF: https://msp.org/obs/2022/5-1/obs-v5-n1-p09-p.pdf#page=30

## Integral calculation and proof of failure

Let E = Z^6 on the displayed ordered basis and L = Z u + Z v. Define a homomorphism q:E -> Z^4 by

    q(x1,x2,x3,x4,x5,x6) = (x3, x2+x4, x5-x1, x6-x2).

It kills both u and v. Define s:Z^4 -> E by

    s(y1,y2,y3,y4) = (0,0,y1,y2,y3,y4).

Then q s is the identity, so q is onto. Direct subtraction, valid for arbitrary integers xi, gives

    x - s(q(x)) = x1 u + x2 v.

Consequently ker(q) is contained in L; the reverse inclusion was already checked. The first isomorphism theorem gives E/L isomorphic to Z^4, with no torsion. This proves the quotient calculation over Z rather than inferring it merely from rational rank.

Apply this to the twelve curves in the published positive identity relation. They satisfy all the target hypotheses with n=12 and g=3, and the target group is exactly E/L. Its first Betti number is four. A single such example refutes the universal bound.

For the geometric interpretation, the construction lifts to a boundary multitwist with four boundary components, hence yields a genus-three pencil and, after four blow-ups, a Lefschetz fibration with sections. In this setting van Kampen gives the surface group modulo the normal closure of vanishing cycles. Abelianization yields exactly E/L. Blowing up does not change H1: the connected-sum summand has zero H1, so Mayer-Vietoris preserves first homology. Thus the fibration interpretation also has b1=4. This additional interpretation is not needed for the direct mapping-class-group refutation.
