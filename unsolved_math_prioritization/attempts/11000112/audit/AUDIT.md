# Independent audit: positive Dehn-twist identity quotient

## Verdict

Accept the prior-negative disposition for problem 11000112 / AMR-109-0112, queue rank 1012. Recommend `already_solved`, `0/5` substantive proof-search turns. Baykur's published genus-three example supplies twelve positive twists with identity product and integral homology quotient Z^4. This refutes the proposed bound because 4 > 3.

The original author freeze is accepted without a correction patch. This is an independent mathematical/source-bridge review and executable-artifact audit, not human peer review, formal verification, or a new counterexample. The geometry remains explicitly dependent on the published construction.

## Exact target

I inspected the retained original PDF directly, rendered physical page 95 (printed page 88), and checked the current public PDF text. Korkmaz's Problem 2.7 concerns a nonempty product of right Dehn twists equal to the identity on a closed connected oriented genus-g surface. Its group is integral first homology modulo the subgroup generated by the twist curves' classes. The question asks whether its first Betti number is at most g.

There is no hypothesis that the curves are distinct, nonseparating, or that the relation lift to an identity with a marked point. The following marked-point discussion belongs to Problem 2.8. The catalogue record merges that discussion into its excerpt, while its retained research summary replaces the inequality with a classification question. Neither change belongs to the actual target. The counterexample has g=3, n=12, and in fact all twelve curves have nonzero primitive homology, so it also satisfies essentiality and nonseparation.

Source: M. Korkmaz, “Problems on homomorphisms of mapping class groups,” Problem 2.7, in Farb's collection, printed p. 88: https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf#page=95

## Published geometric bridge

The supporting article is R. Inanc Baykur, “Small exotic 4-manifolds and symplectic Calabi-Yau surfaces via genus-3 pencils,” Open Book Series 5 (2022), 185–221, DOI https://doi.org/10.2140/obs.2022.5.185 . The inspected PDF is https://msp.org/obs/2022/5-1/obs-v5-n1-p09-p.pdf .

I read the conventions in Section 2.2 and the relevant construction and homology argument in Sections 5.1–5.2. I independently rendered and visually inspected printed p. 203 and pp. 210–214, covering Figures 4–7 and equations (44)–(59). In particular:

1. Positive means right-handed, and the mapping classes preserve orientation. The boundary groups fix their boundaries pointwise. Capping is the homomorphism to the closed-surface group.
2. The genus-two/four-boundary starting relation (44) is credited by Baykur to Hamada. Its Hurwitz rearrangement replaces three curves by their images under a positive twist, preserving the positivity of each resulting factor.
3. The two embeddings described before (45) and (46) place the relation in genus three with four boundary circles. The surface topology is consistent: attaching a four-holed sphere along two circles to a genus-two/four-boundary surface gives Euler characteristic -8 and four remaining boundary circles, hence genus three.
4. Let D be the commuting product of the two unprimed auxiliary twists and the inverses of the two primed auxiliary twists. Equations (45) and (46) have the form P D = Delta_12 and P' D^(-1) = Delta_34. The boundary twists are central. Thus P = Delta_12 D^(-1), P' = Delta_34 D, and P P' = Delta_12 Delta_34. This verifies the cancellation step for the identity conjugating class without introducing a commutation assumption on the individual twelve factors.
5. The identity conjugating class satisfies the stabilizer requirement. Each of P and P' contains six positive factors. Capping all four boundary circles sends each boundary twist to the identity, so equation (47) is a twelve-factor identity on the closed genus-three surface, rather than merely a boundary multitwist. The page explicitly states this capping step.
6. The factor order is B0, B1, B2, A0, A1, A2, B0', B1', B2', A0', A1', A2'. The word list (48)–(59) is explicitly tied to these same curves and this order. Figure 4 supplies the ordered standard surface generators.

Limits: I did not reconstruct Hamada's starting relation from a presentation of the mapping class group, and I did not independently recover every based loop word from the drawings. Those geometric and curve-to-word identifications are imported from Baykur's published result. I checked the published derivation, its hypotheses, the relevant figures, the actual identity/capping distinction, and every subsequent abelianization and integral step. The acceptance is therefore a verified application of a published counterexample, not a first-principles or machine-certified geometric construction. No claim about Calabi-Yau classification, homeomorphism type, or the higher-genus generalization is required.

## Twelve homology classes and saturation

Use the source's basis (a1,b1,a2,b2,a3,b3), and write

- u = (1,0,0,0,1,0)
- v = (0,1,0,-1,0,1)

I separately counted exponents in each of the twelve displayed relators. Commutator terms have exponent sum zero; conjugating paths cancel. The resulting coefficient pairs (coefficient of u, coefficient of v), in the factor order above, are:

(1,0), (1,-1), (0,-1), (1,0), (1,-1), (0,-1),
(1,0), (1,-1), (0,1), (1,0), (1,-1), (0,-1).

Thus every class lies in Zu + Zv; u and v themselves occur, so this is equality of integral subgroups, not just equality of rational spans. Each vector has coordinate gcd one and is nonzero. An essential simple curve with nonzero homology on a closed oriented surface is nonseparating. In particular none becomes null-homotopic after capping.

The rank is two. The minor on the a1,b1 coordinates of u,v is 1, so the generated rank-two subgroup is saturated. As an independent exact arithmetic check, the auditor's script calculates all 990 rank-two minors of the full 12-by-6 matrix; their gcd is 1. It also checks that the ordered homological transvections multiply to the identity. This last check is only a necessary homological consistency check, and is not proof of the mapping-class identity.

For a direct integral quotient proof, define

q(x) = (x3, x2+x4, x5-x1, x6-x2),
s(y) = (0,0,y1,y2,y3,y4).

Then q(u)=q(v)=0 and q composed with s is the identity. For every integer vector x,

x - s(q(x)) = x1 u + x2 v.

Consequently the kernel of q is exactly Zu + Zv. The quotient is Z^4, with no torsion. This proves the asserted Betti number without confusing rank with absence of torsion. The surface's product-of-commutators relation adds nothing to its abelianization.

The optional fibration interpretation is also valid: the lifted four-boundary relation gives four sections after blowing up the pencil base points; in this setting van Kampen yields the quotient by the vanishing-cycle normal closure. Abelianization gives the same group. Blowing up adds a simply connected connected-sum summand, so it leaves H1 unchanged. The direct mapping-class counterexample does not require this interpretation.
