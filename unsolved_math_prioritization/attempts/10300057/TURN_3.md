# Author turn 3: the torsion mechanism is real for plane fields

**Partial analogue, not a solution of Question13.4.** There is a closed oriented hyperbolic 3-manifold M with two smooth cooriented plane fields xi_1,xi_4 such that:

- they are not homotopic even as unoriented plane fields downstairs;
- their pullbacks to a connected finite cyclic cover are homotopic as cooriented plane fields;
- consequently xi_1 is not a uniform plane-field limit of an identity-isotopy orbit of xi_4 downstairs.

This realizes the cover-killed topological obstruction suggested by TURN_1 on an actual hyperbolic manifold. It does **not** construct taut foliations in these classes or an upstairs isotopy-orbit limit. Those two dynamical/integrability requirements remain indispensable and unresolved.

## 1. Classical inputs and their precise use

We use the following classical Pontryagin–Thom facts for a closed connected oriented smooth 3-manifold Y.

1. A framed oriented link is the regular-value inverse image of a smooth map f:Y to S^2, and framed bordism is equivalent to homotopy of maps. The primary class f*u is Poincare dual to the link class. Every class in H^2(Y;Z) is realized.
2. With fixed primary class c, the secondary homotopy classes form a torsor for H^3(Y;Z)/(2c cup H^1(Y;Z)). In particular, with c=0, the integer Hopf invariant, or equivalently the ordinary self-linking of the nullhomologous Pontryagin link, classifies the maps.
3. If a link L is torsion in homology and L' its framed parallel, its rational self-linking is defined by choosing mL=boundary A and taking (A intersection L')/m. Its reduction modulo Z is the torsion linking pairing lambda([L],[L]). One full twist of the framing changes this number by one, with sign according to the twist.

The first two are stated in Auckly–Kapitanski, Theorem1 and Section2.1.1, and in DeTurck et al., the Pontryagin-link discussion on printed pp12–13. The latter describes the framed bordism obstruction directly. Rational Hopf invariants for torsion classes are also treated in Auckly–Kapitanski, Theorem11. The definition and nonsingularity of the rational homology sphere linking pairing are reviewed by Conway–Friedl–Herrmann, Section2.2. We use smooth maps only; no Sobolev extension is needed.

The self-linking statements in item3 also follow directly from the chain definition: changing a rational bounding chain alters the pairing by zero over Q, and changing a parallel by a meridional framing twist adds one intersection with the spanning chain per copy of the knot. Reduction modulo Z depends only on the two homology classes. All sign choices below are immaterial because only equality and the congruence 4^2=1 modulo15 are used.

Every oriented smooth 3-manifold is parallelizable. Fix an oriented trivialization of TY. A map f:Y to the unit sphere defines a cooriented plane field xi_f perpendicular to f. Its oriented tangent-plane Euler class is 2 f*u, since the bundle is f*(TS^2). We use the induced pullback trivialization on any cover.

## 2. An explicit hyperbolic manifold and its torsion cover

Take M to be positive integral 15-surgery on the figure-eight knot in S^3, in the usual meridian/longitude convention. Thurston's *Geometry and Topology of Three-Manifolds*, Chapter4, Theorem4.7 (printed p61), states that figure-eight fillings outside the slopes infinity, 0, ±1, ±2, ±3 and ±4 have hyperbolic structures. Thus this filling is a closed oriented hyperbolic manifold.

The knot exterior has first homology Z generated by its meridian, and its preferred longitude is zero in that group. Filling along 15 times the meridian plus the longitude imposes exactly the relation 15 meridian=0. Hence

H_1(M;Z)=Z/15,    H^1(M;Z)=0,    H^2(M;Z)=Z/15.

Let chi:pi_1(M) to Z/15 be the abelianization character, and let p:N to M be its connected kernel cover, of degree15. In the long exact sequence from

0 to Z --times15--> Z to Z/15 to 0,

the Bockstein beta:H^1(M;Z/15) to H^2(M;Z) is an isomorphism: the preceding H^1(M;Z) is zero and multiplication by15 on H^2 is zero. Therefore c=beta(chi) is a generator. Naturality and p*chi=0 give p*c=0, and also p*(4c)=0. The cover is itself closed oriented and hyperbolic.

## 3. Matching the secondary invariants

Let a=PD(c), a generator of H_1(M;Z). Nonsingularity of the torsion linking pairing implies

lambda(a,a)=v/15 modulo Z

for some integer v relatively prime to15. Its sign and exact unit are unnecessary here. Bilinearity gives

lambda(4a,4a)=16 lambda(a,a)=lambda(a,a) modulo Z.

Choose smooth oriented embedded links L_1,L_4 representing a and4a, with arbitrary framings. Their rational self-linking values h_1,h_4 differ by an integer, by the preceding equality. Twist the framing of L_4 by the negative of that integer. After this adjustment h_4=h_1 exactly in Q. Such a framing twist is permitted without changing the underlying homology class.

Let f_1,f_4:M to S^2 be the corresponding Pontryagin–Thom maps. Their primary classes are c and4c. The inverse-image links of their lifts f_i p are the full lifted framed links p^(-1)L_i. These lifted primary classes vanish because p*c=0. Their rational self-linking values satisfy

selflink_N(p^(-1)L_i)=15 selflink_M(L_i).              (1)

To verify (1), if boundary A=m L_i, transfer the 2-chain A and the full framed parallel. Each downstairs transverse intersection has fifteen lifts, while the boundary multiplicity stays m. Dividing the transferred intersection count by m gives the asserted factor. This calculation is valid even if N has nonzero first Betti number. The lifted links are integrally nullhomologous by the vanishing primary classes, so their rational self-linking numbers are their ordinary integer Hopf invariants.

The two lifted maps therefore have primary class zero and the same integer Hopf invariant. By the classical classification in Section1, they are homotopic. Their perpendicular plane fields are correspondingly homotopic as cooriented plane fields upstairs.

## 4. Downstairs obstruction survives orientation reversal

The Euler classes of xi_1 and xi_4 are 2c and8c. In Z/15,

2 is neither 8 nor -8.

A homotopy of their unoriented plane fields would lift to a homotopy of unit normals after choosing the starting coorientation: the normal line bundle over M times I has trivial first Stiefel–Whitney class because its restriction at one endpoint is trivial and that endpoint is a deformation retract. At the other endpoint the lift agrees with the specified normal or its negative, with a constant global sign on connected M. Thus such a homotopy would force e(xi_1)=±e(xi_4), contradicting the displayed arithmetic.

In particular, no sequence of identity-isotopy images of xi_4 can converge uniformly to xi_1 downstairs. Otherwise a sufficiently close endpoint would be homotopic to xi_1 through the small-angle Grassmannian homotopy, while its isotopy path makes it homotopic to xi_4, contradicting the obstruction.

## 5. Exact gap to the source target

The construction proves that finite-cover pullback is not injective on these plane-field homotopy classes even on a closed hyperbolic manifold. It shows that the torsion route is not ruled out by the topological ambient-manifold conditions alone.

It does not turn the displayed fields into taut foliations. Nor does homotopy of the lifted fields imply that one lifted foliation belongs to the closure of the other's identity-isotopy orbit. A foliation-existence h-principle, if invoked, would still not supply tautness or that orbit statement. No such inference is made.

Thus the original remains unresolved after three substantive turns. A next attempt must either realize and control the dynamics in a candidate torsion pair or find a cover-sensitive foliation invariant that rules out this realization.
