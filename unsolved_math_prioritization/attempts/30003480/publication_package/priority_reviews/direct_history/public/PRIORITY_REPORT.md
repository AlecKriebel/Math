## Target and success criteria

The target is the image G_n of real format 2×⋯×2 tensors with Frobenius norm at most one under the n Gram determinants. C_n=[0,1/4]^n; H_n is its intersection with d_i≤Σ_{j≠i}d_j. The original claims that for each n≥3 no real polynomial P in determinant coordinates satisfies G_n={d∈C_n:P(d)≥0}, and no such polynomial works with H_n instead. Its positive n=4 point separately disproves the sufficiency direction of Seigal's Q_1≥Q_2 proposal. These are different claims. Auxiliary-variable, radical, multi-inequality, and union descriptions are outside the impossibility assertion.

A decisive historical anticipation must actually establish this locus/domain/normalization conclusion or an equivalent explicitly applied argument. Merely supplying eigenvalue polygon inequalities, tensor realization, general polynomial sign facts, a computational picture, or a different invariant problem is insufficient to identify a historical published application. Conversely, equivalent mathematics counts as overlap even when examples and notation differ. n=1 and n=2 admit a single nonnegative-polynomial description and are outside the impossibility range. The original higher-dimensional argument uses exact zero-determinant faces of a closed domain; it does not establish every punctured or positive-only higher-dimensional reformulation.

## Mathematical comparison: explicit application versus ingredients

Let t_i=(1−√(1−4d_i))/2 and r_i=1−2t_i, so d_i=t_i(1−t_i)=(1−r_i²)/4. For n=3, the original boundary factor t_1−t_2−t_3 becomes −(1+r_1−r_2−r_3)/2. This is a Ferudun facet factor, up to a nonzero constant. The original reflection t_1↦1−t_1 is exactly r_1↦−r_1. Its companion interior plane Σt_i=1 is Σr_i=1. Both arguments force odd exact factor multiplicity from a local boundary sign crossing and even multiplicity from nonnegativity across an interior patch. The polynomial's reflection/sign invariance transfers the same exact multiplicity. This is proof-mechanism overlap, not only agreement of conclusions.

The original proves n=3 and transfers to every higher n by exact zero-determinant rank-one slices; the H_3 patch lies in int(H_3), and H_n restricts to that slice. Ferudun Theorem1.4, p.3, excludes a polynomial even on U=int(H_n) for every n≥3; Section5, pp.7–8, proves it directly in dimension n. Its boundary patch has r=(1−δ,…,1−δ,1−(n−1)δ), whose relevant determinant polygon margin is (n−1)(n−2)δ²/4>0. The reflected interior hyperplane Σr=n−2 passes through r_i=(n−2)/n. Thus Ferudun covers positive-only interior variants for higher n beyond the original slice proof. The original n=3 proof already uses positive patches; it is inaccurate to characterize the entire original obstruction as depending only on zero determinants.

Both texts contain the same additional unit-ball normalization mechanism. If a_s(d)=(s−√(s²−4d))/2 and d_j<d_i, then

    d/ds log(a_s(d_j)/a_s(d_i))
      =1/√(s²−4d_i)−1/√(s²−4d_j)>0.

Consequently a smaller-eigenvalue polygon violation at trace1 cannot be repaired by reducing common trace s≤1; zero determinants and the endpoint are handled by continuity. This step must be supplied when moving from normalized states to the closed ball. HSS/DSL normalized feasibility does not, by its title alone, establish this ball step.

HSS's actual construction is real in its three-factor basis, and its induction can remain real with real coefficients. That last all-n real implication is an inference from the read construction, not a separate quoted theorem. DSL's published Theorem1.5 explicitly supplies nonnegative real realizations for I=2 in every n. With σ_i²=1−t_i, its inequalities become t_i≤Σ_{j≠i}t_j. These sources therefore already determine the normalized feasible eigenvalue data needed for a determinant reformulation. Neither relevant body states the Gram-locus all-polynomial impossibility or the explicit Q_1−Q_2 refutation. No historical explicit sign-symmetry application was identified there. Describing the new deduction as assembled from known ingredients is accurate; describing the ingredients themselves as new is not.

Ferudun's n=4 point (9/100,9/100,9/100,6/25) has smaller eigenvalues (1/10,1/10,1/10,2/5), hence a strict polygon violation while satisfying the source's determinant hull constraints and Q_1>Q_2. The original (2401/10000,81/625,81/625,1/10000) is a different point with different arithmetic certificates. Both disprove the formula's sufficiency, so a distinct witness does not remove conclusion-level overlap. The external preprint also gives counterexamples for every n≥4 with nonempty interior; the original's explicit positive witness is specifically n=4.

The original's GHZ display caution and Ferudun Remark4.2 concern the same printed secondary-inequality direction. The published page354 was visually checked. The source's proof later speaks of the pair expressions being positive in a way inconsistent with the display. The thesis repeats the same display. Ferudun additionally derives a corrected n-ary union description; the original does not contain that theorem and must not receive credit for it merely from the shared caution.
