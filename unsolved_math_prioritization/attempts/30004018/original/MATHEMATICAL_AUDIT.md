# Objectwise Duflo–Serganova kernels and the socle filtration

## Scope and conclusion

Let g = gl(m|n) over C. Let O^Z be the category of Z/2-graded g-modules whose restriction to the even Lie algebra is in integral BGG category O. The choice is an even Borel; do not restrict this category to finite-dimensional modules. Put

K = C ⊗_Z (K_0(O^Z)/<[Pi M]+[M]>),

with its sl(infinity)-action induced by translation functors. For the self-commuting cone X in the odd part, let X_k consist of matrices of natural rank exactly k. Set

A_k = span_C{[M] : ker(x:M→M)/im(x:M→M)=0 for every x in X_k}.

Write S_0(K)=0 and S_{i+1}(K)/S_i(K)=soc(K/S_i(K)). The target is A_k=S_k(K) for 1≤k≤min(m,n). This is the OWR Conjecture 9 [OWR, printed p.3100], with the first layer normalized by its Theorem 2. If k=0 is included, both sides are zero because DS_0 is the identity. When min(m,n)=0 there are no positive indices to test.

**The full target is not resolved here.** The credited rank-one consequence below and the explicit obstruction checks are not a proof for k≥2. No novelty or priority is claimed. A bounded literature search through 8 October 2026 did not identify a later full resolution; this is not a certificate of current worldwide openness.

### Notation warning

HPS v4 §3.1 defines its socle filtration starting with the socle at index 0, whereas its §4.5 Conjecture 45 and Proposition 46 print a k+1 index alongside support rank at most k. OWR Theorem 2 instead indexes successive layers by S_{k+1}/S_k with contraction number k. This note uses S throughout, so the first layer is unambiguously the socle. An index shift is not treated as a mathematical counterexample.

HPS Proposition 42 concerns the tensor filtration of the finite-dimensional summand J; Proposition 43 concerns kernels of class maps associated with one orientation of odd root vectors. Neither is the target above. Replacing an acyclic object by a class killed in the reduced group loses information.

## 1. Credited facts and the rank-one boundary

We use these results as imports, not as results of this note:

1. Projective classes span soc(K), and the sl(infinity) socle layers are computed in HPS Theorem 24 (also OWR Theorems 2 and 5).
2. For category O, DS has a convergent bounded cohomological spectral sequence for sums of mutually orthogonal odd root vectors; it preserves category O. See HPS Proposition 33 and its proof. Its Lemma 35 identifies *class maps* under iteration, not objectwise functors in general.
3. Every self-commuting odd element is even-Borel conjugate to such a sum. Associated varieties of O-objects are even-Borel invariant. See CS Theorem 5.1 and its setup.
4. For gl(m|n), an O-object has trivial associated variety if and only if its projective dimension in O is finite. See CS Theorem 4.1. This statement must not be extended to an arbitrary Lie superalgebra.

**Consequence.** A_1=S_1(K), for all m,n with min(m,n)≥1.

Proof. Suppose DS_x M=0 for every rank-one x. For a sum x=x_1+...+x_r of orthogonal root vectors, filter its total cohomology by first taking x_1-cohomology. The corresponding spectral sequence is zero from that stage, hence DS_x M=0. The Borel-orbit statement covers every nonzero x. Thus X_M is empty for M=0 and {0} otherwise. The finite-projective-dimension theorem gives a finite projective resolution of M inside O^Z (take the integral block summands of the resolution). Its Euler characteristic writes [M] as a finite signed sum of projective classes. Therefore A_1⊆S_1(K). Conversely each projective has trivial associated variety by the same theorem and belongs to the defining class of A_1. Projective classes span S_1(K), proving equality. □

The same spectral-sequence argument shows that vanishing on all rank-k elements implies vanishing on every rank at least k: first take a rank-k partial sum and then the remaining commuting root vectors. Thus the exact-rank formulation agrees with support in ranks below k, and A_k⊆A_{k+1}. This uses the category-O spectral-sequence and orbit imports; it does not assume arbitrary infinite-dimensional supports are closed.

HPS §4.5 also supplies the known inclusion S_k(K)⊆A_k after normalizing indices: the relevant submodule is generated by weight spaces whose block atypicality is at most k−1; objects in those blocks have associated variety of rank at most k−1. The unresolved direction is A_k⊆S_k(K) for k≥2.

Finite projective dimension no longer follows from that higher-rank support bound. Consequently the rank-one resolution argument does not prove this remaining direction.

## 2. Exact failure of the ungraded class-kernel replacement

This standard gl(1|1) calculation isolates the distinction. Let e=E_12, f=E_21 and h_1=E_11, h_2=E_22. Thus [e,f]=h_1+h_2. Fix an integer a. Define a module C_a with even basis vector v and odd basis vector w by

h_1 v=a v, h_1 w=(a−1)w, h_2=−h_1,
fv=w, fw=0, e=0.

It is an integral category-O module. The rank-one self-commuting cone of gl(1|1) consists exactly of the two nonzero axes Ce and Cf, since (u e+v f)^2=u v I in the natural representation. On C_a,

DS_e(C_a)=C_a≠0, whereas DS_f(C_a)=0.

The target algebra after DS is gl(0|0). The reduced class of the first result is 1−1=0. Hence the nonzero class [C_a] lies in ker(ds_x) for *every* x∈X_1, although the object is not in the acyclic category.

There is a stronger class-level obstruction: [C_a] does not even belong to the span A_1 of acyclic objects. In gl(1|1), category O equals the category of finite-dimensional Cartan-semisimple modules. Work in the h_1+h_2=0 part. Let L_a be the even one-dimensional module of weight (a,−a), and identify its reduced class with t^a in R=C[t,t^−1]. Then

[C_a]=t^a−t^{a−1}.

Every projective in this part is a sum of parity shifts of the projective covers

P_a=Ind_h^g L_a.

To check this assertion, the central-zero odd algebra is the exterior algebra on e,f. Its induced module has basis 1,e,f,ef, with h_1-weights a,a+1,a−1,a and parities even,odd,odd,even. It is projective by the exact adjunction with restriction to the semisimple Cartan category. Its top is L_a; every simple is some L_a or its parity shift. Thus these are all projective covers. In particular

[P_a]=2t^a−t^{a+1}−t^{a−1}=−t^{a−1}(t−1)^2.

Consequently A_1 on this central-zero part is exactly (t−1)^2R, while the simultaneous class kernel is (t−1)R. The latter assertion follows because both reduced DS maps send each t^a to 1. Equivalently, the functional

mu(sum_a c_a t^a)=sum_a a c_a

vanishes on every projective class but equals 1 on [C_a]. Other central-eigenvalue blocks cannot contribute to this part. Therefore

A_1=S_1(K) is strictly smaller than the intersection of all ker(ds_x).

This is not a counterexample to the original conjecture. It is a counterexample to an incorrect replacement of its right-hand side. The Kac-module DS behavior also appears, for example, in Hirota's pinned v1 Example 4.6; the argument here is written out to be independently checkable.

## 3. Exact failure of reverse spectral-sequence induction

A proposed induction might argue that DS_{y+z}(M)=0 forces DS_z(DS_y(M))=0. That implication is false even for a four-dimensional module over a direct sum of two gl(1|1)'s.

Take a,b,c,d of bidegrees (0,1),(1,1),(1,0),(2,0). Parity is total degree modulo 2. Define odd maps u,v by

u(a)=b, u(c)=d, u(b)=u(d)=0;
v(c)=b, v(a)=v(b)=v(d)=0.

Then u^2=v^2=uv+vu=0. These are the positive odd generators of the two gl(1|1) factors; set the negative odd generators to zero. Let the first Cartan pair act by (p,−p) and the second by (q,−q) on bidegree (p,q). This verifies all Lie-superalgebra relations. The natural-matrix rank of u+v in the direct-sum algebra is two, even though its action on this module is a separate representation matrix.

The differential u+v sends a to b and c to b+d. It has rank two on a four-dimensional space, and its kernel and image are both span(b,d). Thus H(M,u+v)=0.

In contrast H(M,v) has representatives a,d. The induced u-map is zero, because u(a)=b is already a v-boundary. Hence H(H(M,v),u) has dimension two. A higher spectral-sequence differential cancels these terms.

This verifies why class-map composition in HPS Lemma 35 cannot be upgraded to the needed objectwise converse. This module is a Levi-algebra diagnostic, not a gl(2|2) counterexample satisfying all rank-two tests.

## 4. What the tensor-product/Levi route establishes

Consider only finite direct sums of external products M_1⊠...⊠M_r of gl(1|1) Cartan-semisimple modules. For each factor choose a nonzero odd root direction x_i, either e_i or f_i. Künneth for finite complexes gives

DS_{x_1+...+x_r}(M_1⊠...⊠M_r)
  ≅ DS_{x_1}(M_1)⊠...⊠DS_{x_r}(M_r).

It follows that the product is killed for every choice of root direction precisely when at least one factor is projective. If a factor is projective its two DS values vanish. If none is projective, CS Theorem 4.1 (whose bound is zero for gl(1|1), since its even Weyl group is trivial) gives for each factor a root direction with nonzero DS; their external product is nonzero. For a direct sum, each summand must pass this test because cohomology commutes with finite sums.

On the all-central-zero component the classes of these acyclic, externally decomposable objects therefore span the Laurent-polynomial ideal

((t_1−1)^2,...,(t_r−1)^2).

Indeed the other factors may be arbitrary one-dimensional L_a, giving all Laurent monomial multiples of each displayed generator. The quotient has basis products of (t_i−1)^epsilon_i with epsilon_i∈{0,1}, hence dimension 2^r.

This calculation supports a tensor-generation route, but it covers neither arbitrary indecomposable Levi modules nor arbitrary objects of O(gl(m|n)). In particular it does not supply a finite resolution of every rank-k-acyclic object by translation images from low-atypicality blocks. Assuming precisely that missing generation/resolution statement would simply assume a strong form of the needed direction.

## 5. Why ordinary universal cohomology does not repair the gap

One can try to replace objectwise DS fibers with a single coherent cohomology sheaf. Ordinary fiber formation is not exact enough for this.

For the natural gl(1|1) module, set A=C[u,v]/(uv), the coordinate ring of its self-commuting cone. On the free A-supermodule A^{1|1}, the universal differential is

d(a,b)=(u b,v a).

In A, ann(u)=(v) and ann(v)=(u). Therefore ker(d)=(u)⊕(v)=im(d), so the universal cohomology module is zero. At the origin u=v=0, however, the specialized differential is zero and its cohomology is C^{1|1}.

The example is GHSS Example 11.4, reproduced algebraically. It shows that an argument identifying the support of ordinary universal cohomology with every DS fiber is invalid even for a finite-dimensional object. This failure occurs at the origin and does not by itself refute a statement restricted to the punctured cone. Derived specialization and additional generation theorems would be required for this proposed route. General category-O objects also need not give coherent finite-rank bundles. No such theorem is proved here.

## Checks and remaining gap

The accompanying standard-library checker uses exact rational arithmetic and explicit failures, not Python assert statements. It verifies the gl(1|1) matrices, projective and Kac supercharacters, both DS parities, independent rank calculations by Gaussian elimination and minors, the four-dimensional bicomplex and its quotient calculation, and the finite algebra identities used above. Mutation controls must fail. These are checks of the finite examples, not a machine verification of the cited representation-theory theorems or the full conjecture.

The outstanding mathematical requirement is a valid proof that each full-category-O object with DS_x M=0 for all x∈X_k has [M] in S_k(K), for 2≤k≤min(m,n). None of the five mechanisms above supplies it.
