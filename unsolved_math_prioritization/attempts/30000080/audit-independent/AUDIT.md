## 3. Characteristic-zero theorem and field descent

The live primary arXiv pages confirm the two preprints and their respective submission dates:

- [arXiv:2609.12302v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12302v1): submitted 11 September 2026; Theorem A is over C[x0,...,xN] and gives I^t = m^(td) for t >= N for linearly presented m-primary ideals.
- [arXiv:2609.25385v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25385v1): submitted 21 September 2026; Theorem 1.2 is over C[x0,...,xN] and gives the partially virtually linear bound t >= ceil(N/p). Its PDF's internal date is 23 September; the report preserves that distinction.

Using N = n-1 and p = 1 gives precisely the target exponent. An ordinary linear presentation supplies the required sheafified exactness. Neither source's theorem supplies the missing arbitrary-positive-characteristic conclusion.

The field-descent argument in the report is sound. Choose the finitely generated coefficient field F over Q. Faithful flatness of F -> K descends finite colength and the absence of nonlinear first syzygies, because the graded quotients and graded Tor groups commute with a field extension. A finitely generated characteristic-zero field embeds in C. After applying the theorem through such an embedding, the cokernel of Sym^(n-1)(I_F)_d -> F[x]_(n-1)d vanishes by faithful flatness. Extension to K gives degree-(n-1)d surjectivity. Equigeneration promotes that surjectivity to equality of ideals. No assumption that the whole original K embeds in C is necessary.

### Shorter-proof dependency audit

Write N for projective dimension and p for the number of virtual linear steps, to keep those roles distinct from field characteristic.

1. The sheafified truncated linear strand A has H^0(A) = O(d) and only one other cohomology bundle E at degree -p. Its kernel B under A -> O(d) is quasi-isomorphic to E[p]. The kernel is a bounded complex of vector bundles; local splitting of the exact tail supplies local freeness at the left endpoint.
2. Terms of a degree-t hook Schur complex of A are sums of O(k) in cohomological degree k <= 0. In the termwise hypercohomology spectral sequence, any contribution to positive total degree vanishes by the cohomology of O(k) on projective space. For the possible top-row contribution, k = i-N > -N-1 when total degree i > 0.
3. In characteristic zero, the hook functor is a direct summand of the signed tensor power. Since B has only the locally free cohomology bundle E at -p, B tensor t has only E tensor t at -tp. The positive-degree groups needed in Lemma 3.1 consequently inject into H^(tp+j+1)(E tensor t), which vanishes above N when tp >= N.
4. The hook short exact sequence can be checked locally after splitting the underlying graded sequence A -> O(d). For a hook with t boxes, the nonzero tail of its Schur resolution is the stated symmetric-power factor tensored with the exterior Koszul tail. The kernel of that tail is the corresponding exterior power of B. This validates the use of Lemma 2.2 in characteristic zero; it is not being assumed characteristic-free.
5. The symmetric Koszul resolution of the quotient A/B produces the exact total complex used on page 4. The displayed diagonal and preceding-diagonal vanishings eliminate all higher incoming differentials to the (0,0) term and all other total-degree-zero terms. Thus its zero total hypercohomology is the specified cokernel.
6. The identification of that cokernel with (S/I^t)_(td) uses June Lemma 2.1 and June Lemma 4.1, not a new assertion of projective normality. In June Lemma 4.1 substitute its parameter t-1: its threshold becomes t >= N/p, exactly the required one. The augmentation/mapping-cone shift is important: the necessary vanishings are hypercohomology H^1 and H^2 of Sym^t(B), namely sheaf cohomology in degrees tp+1 and tp+2, both above N. It does not incorrectly require the possibly nonzero degree tp group to vanish when tp = N.
7. The June symmetric-power cohomology calculation explicitly uses exactness of the symmetric-group invariant functor over C. The report is therefore right to retain the characteristic restriction.

## 5. Full audit of the all-characteristic monomial proof

### Binary generators

Primaryness forces each pure power xi^d to be a generator, since the ideal is monomial and equigenerated. Fix two variables and multidegree M = xi^d xj^d. The degree-M free module on the generators dividing M has a basis indexed by those generators, with basis vectors (M/u)e_u. Its syzygy space is the space of linear combinations whose coefficients sum to zero.

Any degree-(d+1) syzygy is spanned by differences between generators with least common multiple of degree d+1. Its multiples in multidegree M therefore lie in the edge-difference span of the graph in Lemma 3.1. The image of that span in the vector space on connected components is zero. The pure-power relation can lie in it only if the two pure powers are in the same component. This argument works over every field, since the difference of two distinct basis vectors is nonzero even in characteristic two.

Writing a binary generator as xi^(d-a)xj^a, the least-common-multiple degree of two generators is d + |a-b|. Thus edges have |a-b| = 1. A path between 0 and d forces every intermediate exponent. All degree-d binary monomials belong to I.

### Packing induction

For completeness, here is a precise form of the packing argument, valid for every k >= 0 and d >= 1.

Suppose sum a_i = kd and at most k+1 coordinates are positive. The k = 0 case is empty. If there are at most k positive coordinates, some coordinate is at least d. Remove d from that coordinate; the remainder has degree (k-1)d and at most k positive coordinates, so induction applies.

Otherwise there are k+1 positive coordinates. At least one is <= d; select a largest one among those <= d and call it s. If s = d, remove it. If s < d and another coordinate exceeds d, that coordinate can supply the remaining d-s. If no coordinate exceeds d, then s is a global maximum. If s plus every other coordinate were < d, all pairs would have sum < d. Averaging pair sums would give total sum < (k+1)d/2 <= kd, a contradiction. Hence a suitable partner exists. Remove s from its coordinate and d-s from the partner. This removes at least one positive coordinate, leaving degree (k-1)d supported on at most k coordinates. Induction completes the factorization into k binary degree-d factors.

Taking k = n-1 gives every degree-(n-1)d monomial as a product of n-1 elements of I. The reverse containment follows from degrees. The monomial theorem is therefore a genuine unbounded proof over every field, independent of the finite control grid. It agrees with the p = 1 case of [EHU Theorem 8.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405373).

The author control rerun checks 234,026 exponent vectors. An independent stars-and-bars sum gives the same total. The grid is not used as a substitute for the induction.

## 6. Universal initial-ideal obstruction

Consider the report's I = (x^2-z^2, y^2-z^2, xy, xz, yz).

The five quadrics are independent over every field. In the quotient, all cross products vanish and the squares agree. Multiplying x^2-z^2 by z and using xz = 0 gives z^3 = 0; every cubic vanishes. Thus the quotient has basis 1,x,y,z,z^2 and Hilbert series 1+3t+t^2 in every characteristic. In particular, I is m-primary.

The integer polynomial identities FL = 0 and Lr = 0 reproduce exactly. The stated 4-by-4 minor is the monomial xyz^2, with coefficient one, so it remains nonzero after every field specialization. It follows that L has rank four: at least four by that minor and at most four from the nonzero kernel vector r.

The gcd of the entries of r is one over every field. This is stronger than merely running a rational-characteristic symbolic gcd: the entries include xy, xz, yz, whose common gcd is already one. If a rational multiple of r has polynomial coordinates, any denominator relatively prime to its numerator must divide all the entries of r. It is therefore a unit. This proves ker L = Sr over each field without a hidden characteristic-zero step.

The resulting graded presentation of im L has Hilbert series (5t^3-t^5)/(1-t)^3. Using the quotient Hilbert series, ker F has exactly this same Hilbert series. Since im L is contained in ker F, equality in every graded degree proves that the displayed five linear syzygies generate the full relation module. No untested higher-degree syzygies remain.

Now take any invertible linear change of coordinates and any monomial term order. An initial ideal preserves the Hilbert function and finite colength. If that initial ideal were generated in degree two and linearly presented, the binary-generator lemma would force it to contain every quadratic monomial. It would be m^2. But dim(S/I)_2 = 1, whereas dim(S/m^2)_2 = 0. This proves the claimed obstruction uniformly over every field and every such coordinate/order choice. The argument is intrinsic to the Hilbert function; checking only one lexicographic basis would not have sufficed.

The displayed lexicographic Groebner basis is also correct. Its only coefficients relevant to leading terms are units, so it works in characteristic two as well. The obstruction is to the proposed sufficient reduction, not to the target conjecture. Independent exact product-rank checks give I^2 = m^4 for this example in characteristics 2,3,5,7,11,101; the known three-variable theorem covers every field.

## 7. Finite-field controls and independent cross-check

Both author scripts were copied to audit-independent/reproduction and run there, so their result-writing behavior could not modify the frozen package. Their output JSON files agree byte-for-byte with the frozen results. They reproduce:

- 234,026 packing factorizations;
- 36 supplied presentations;
- 20 complete linear-presentation certificates;
- zero target failures among those 20;
- the stated positive/negative controls and polarization ranks.

The stopping bound for the linear-presentation test is valid. Every sampled quotient A contains the square relations, hence A_e = 0 for e > 4. The degree-e second Koszul module is A_(e-2) tensor exterior^2(K^4), which is zero for e > 6. Therefore Tor_2(A,K)_e, equivalently Tor_1(I,K)_e, vanishes above degree 6. Because the degree-two generator list is independent, there are no degree-two first relations. Checking that all relation spaces in degrees 4,5,6 are generated by the entire degree-three kernel is consequently enough. It is not merely a low-degree heuristic.

The separate independent_controls.py imports none of the author code and uses no NumPy. It constructs each Artinian quotient as the squarefree polynomial algebra modulo the supplied cross relations, computes quotient multiplication maps, and calculates graded second Koszul homology from its two adjacent differentials. It separately multiplies all unordered generator triples and ranks their coefficient vectors with a pure-Python modular echelon routine.

For all 36 samples, every linearity decision, degree-three Betti count, and cube rank agrees with the author output. Exactly 20 are linearly presented, each with cube rank 84. Among the 16 rejected presentations, three also have cube rank 84, and thirteen have smaller ranks. The report correctly excludes all sixteen from counterexample status. Sampling is neither exhaustive nor necessarily distinct; the report states both limitations.
