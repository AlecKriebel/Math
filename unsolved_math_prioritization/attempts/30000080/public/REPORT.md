# Powers of linearly presented primary ideals: partial resolution and source update

Problem 30000080 / OWR-734-005, original queue rank 600. Checked 4 October 2026.

## Verdict

**The unrestricted-field target is not resolved here.** Recommended research outcome: `unsolved`, five substantive approaches exhausted. There is an important new prior result: Fuxiang Yang's September 2026 preprints establish the target in characteristic zero. Consequently, describing the characteristic-zero case as open would be obsolete, but changing this arbitrary-field row to `already_solved` would discard part of its hypotheses. No novelty or first-resolution claim is made.

The exact target is: for every field K, every n≥1 and d≥1, and every homogeneous m-primary ideal I⊂S=K[x₁,…,xₙ] generated in degree d whose first syzygies are generated in degree d+1, must I^(n−1)=m^((n−1)d)? The zeroth power when n=1 is S by convention. “Primary” refers to the homogeneous maximal ideal m, not an arbitrary prime. Equality is equality of ideals, not merely equality of integral closures, saturations, or eventual Hilbert polynomials.

The original OWR Conjecture 6 in §3 uses a field K without a characteristic restriction. The report PDF retrieved from EMS has the statement at PDF page 18 (printed 1716); the final bibliographic pagination starts four pages later. The report concerns the July 2004 workshop and was published in June 2005. These two dates are compatible. Catalogue access returned HTTP 403, so the exact pinned dataset record was checked against the primary source. [Original report](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/734).

## 1. Characteristic zero: a prior theorem, with full field descent

Yang's September 11 preprint, Theorem A, proves the desired equality over C, writing n for projective dimension and hence using n+1 variables. Its exponent n is therefore exactly our n−1. The September 21 preprint, Theorem 1.2, proves the more general partially linear bound; ordinary linear presentation implies its virtual-linear hypothesis. Both explicitly restrict to characteristic zero. These are recent preprints, not a claim of refereed acceptance. The shorter proof uses hook Schur complexes and vanishing of sheaf cohomology above projective dimension. [September 11 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12302), [September 21 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25385).

Here is the descent argument needed to cover **every characteristic-zero field**, rather than just C. Choose the finitely generated subfield F⊂K over Q generated by all coefficients in fixed degree-d generators of I, and let I_F denote the ideal they generate in F[x]. The extension F[x]→K[x] is faithfully flat. In every degree, the quotient and its graded Tor spaces commute with field extension. Thus finite colength and vanishing of all nonlinear first syzygies descend from I to I_F. Any finitely generated characteristic-zero field embeds into C: embed a transcendence basis into an algebraically independent subset of C, then extend across its finite algebraic extension. Extend I_F through such an embedding and apply Yang's theorem.

More explicitly, put q=n−1 and V=(I_F)_d. The multiplication map Sym^q_F(V)→F[x]_(qd) has zero cokernel after tensoring with C, so its cokernel is zero by faithful flatness. Tensoring with K proves (I^q)_(qd)=S_(qd). Since both ideals are generated in degree qd, this gives I^q=m^(qd). For n=1 the conclusion is the zeroth-power convention and does not require the theorem.

This establishes the characteristic-zero subcase conditional only on the cited prior theorem. It is not a new proof of that theorem. The full shorter proof and its dependencies were read; no fatal issue was identified, but no formal or independent peer-review certificate is asserted here.

## 2. Attempted extension of the Schur argument: an actual small-characteristic obstruction

The characteristic restriction cannot simply be erased from the argument. For a two-dimensional vector space A=Kb⊕Kz and the quotient A→L=Kz, one natural polarization map is

D: Sym^r A → Sym^(r−1) A⊗L,
D(b^(r−j)z^j)=j b^(r−j)z^(j−1)⊗z.

If the characteristic does not divide any of 1,…,r, this map is onto and its kernel is Sym^r(Kb). In characteristic p with r=p it instead kills both b^p and z^p. Its rank is p−1 although the target has dimension p. Therefore the characteristic-zero short exact sequence obtained from this map fails. This is a proof by a diagonal matrix with entries 0,1,…,p; no numerical approximation is involved.

Yang's shorter argument also realizes relevant Schur complexes as direct summands of tensor powers. Characteristic-zero Young symmetrizers provide this splitting; they do not provide the same certificate when the necessary integers vanish in the field. Merely replacing a symmetric power by a divided power changes the required map and needs a new argument connecting its cohomology to the ordinary ideal power.

This obstruction is **not a counterexample to the original ideal conjecture**. It invalidates only an unqualified transfer of a characteristic-zero proof. A plausible large-characteristic adaptation has not been promoted to a theorem here: all exact-sequence and functorial dependencies would need checking over an appropriate localization of Z. In particular, no unsupported bound such as char K>n−1 is claimed.

## 3. All-characteristic monomial route, proved directly

The following is the p=1 monomial theorem already present in Eisenbud–Huneke–Ulrich, Theorem 8.1. We include an elementary argument and an explicit factorization control, without claiming novelty. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405373).

### Lemma 3.1: binary monomials are forced

If I is an m-primary degree-d monomial ideal with linear presentation, then it contains every degree-d monomial supported on at most two variables.

Proof. Primaryness and equigeneration imply x_i^d∈I for every i. Fix distinct i,j. Let G be the minimal monomial generators dividing x_i^d x_j^d. They are all binary degree-d monomials. The multigraded syzygy joining the two pure powers must be an S-linear combination of degree-(d+1) syzygies. In any multidegree, the kernel among monomials is spanned by pairwise differences; in degree d+1 these join exactly generators whose least common multiple has degree d+1. Consequently, in the graph on G with such edges, x_i^d and x_j^d must lie in the same component. A direct way to see necessity is to map every basis generator to the basis vector of its component: all linear syzygies map to zero, whereas the difference between different components does not.

Index a binary generator by its x_j exponent a∈{0,…,d}. An edge changes a by one. A path from 0 to d therefore visits every integer. All binary monomials are in G. ∎

### Lemma 3.2: binary packing

Let a be a vector of nonnegative integers with sum kd and at most k+1 positive coordinates. Then a is a sum of k nonnegative integer vectors, each with sum d and support at most two.

Proof by induction on k. The case k=0 is empty. If a has at most k positive coordinates, at least one coordinate is ≥d; remove d from that coordinate. The remainder has degree (k−1)d and support at most k, unless the original support equals k and the removed coordinate stays positive. In that latter situation this is still at most k, exactly the permitted support. If a has k+1 positive coordinates, choose a largest positive coordinate a_i≤d, which exists because their sum is kd. If a_i=d, remove it. Otherwise choose j≠i with a_i+a_j≥d. Such j exists: if some coordinate exceeds d, use it; if all coordinates are <d and no pair with the largest reaches d, then all coordinates except possibly the largest are <d−a_i, and also ≤a_i. Pair the largest with one other coordinate; their sum is <d, and every pair of remaining coordinates is <d. Thus the sum is <ceil((k+1)/2)d≤kd for k≥1, a contradiction. Remove a_i from coordinate i and d−a_i from coordinate j. This removes coordinate i from the support. Apply induction. ∎

(The shorter estimate when all pair sums are <d is sum a ≤(k+1)(d−1)/2<kd, obtained by averaging pair sums; for k=1 the single pair already contradicts sum a=d.)

### Theorem 3.3

Every linearly presented m-primary monomial ideal generated in degree d satisfies I^(n−1)=m^((n−1)d), over every field.

Proof. For any monomial of degree (n−1)d, apply Lemma 3.2 with k=n−1. Each factor furnished by the lemma belongs to I by Lemma 3.1. Thus every such monomial belongs to I^(n−1). The reverse inclusion follows from degrees. ∎

A deterministic implementation reconstructs all 234,026 exponent vectors for 2≤n≤6 and 1≤d≤5, checking each factorization exactly. These checks exercise the construction; the induction establishes the unbounded theorem.

## 4. Gröbner degeneration: a sufficient condition and a verified obstruction

### Proposition 4.1

If J=in(I) is generated in degree d and is linearly presented, then I satisfies the target over any field.

Proof. Initial ideals preserve finite colength. Theorem 3.3 gives J^(n−1)=m^((n−1)d). The standard inclusion (in I)^q⊂in(I^q), obtained by multiplying leading terms, then implies m^(qd)⊂in(I^q) for q=n−1. Since I^q has no elements in degrees below qd, its initial ideal is exactly m^(qd). Equality of graded Hilbert functions gives I^q=m^(qd). ∎

It is false that every ideal in the question can acquire such an initial ideal, even after changing coordinates. Here is an explicit all-characteristic obstruction in three variables:

I=(x²−z², y²−z², xy, xz, yz).

The quotient has basis 1,x,y,z,z²: cross products vanish, all squares coincide, and all cubics vanish. Its degree-two part is one-dimensional, so H_(S/I)=1+3t+t². Let F be the displayed generator row. The following columns give linear syzygies:

L =
[  0   0   0   z   y ]
[  0   x   0  −z   0 ]
[ −z  −y  −z   0  −x ]
[  y   z   0  −x   0 ]
[  0   0   x   y   z ].

Set r=(−x²+z², −yz, y²−z², −xy, xz)^T. Direct multiplication gives FL=0 and Lr=0. The upper-left 4×4 minor of L is xyz², which is nonzero over every field, so L has rank four over K(x,y,z). The entries of r have gcd one. Consequently ker L=Sr: a rational multiple of r whose coordinates are all polynomials has no nonunit denominator in the UFD K[x,y,z]. Therefore im L has Hilbert series (5t³−t⁵)/(1−t)³. On the other hand, H_I=(5t²−5t³+t⁵)/(1−t)³, so ker F has exactly the same Hilbert series as im L. Inclusion and equality of Hilbert series imply ker F=im L. Thus I is linearly presented, with a completely certified linear presentation.

Suppose some coordinate change and term order gave an initial ideal J that was equigenerated in degree two and linearly presented. Lemma 3.1 would force every quadratic monomial into J, since every quadratic has support at most two. Hence J=m². But initial ideals preserve Hilbert functions, while (S/I)_2 has dimension one and (S/m²)_2 has dimension zero. Contradiction.

For lexicographic order x>y>z, an explicit Gröbner basis is x²−z², xy, xz, y²−z², yz, z³. Its cubic minimal generator displays the failure concretely. The presentation identities and Hilbert numerator are checked over Z in the symbolic control. This example does not refute the target: it refutes a proposed universal reduction to the monomial case.

## 5. Exact finite-field search in a bounded nonmonomial family

We tested 36 degree-two ideal presentations in four variables over F₂,F₃,F₅. Each includes all four variable squares plus a full-rank subspace of the six cross-product monomials. Cross-subspace dimensions were 3,4,5,6, with three deterministically seeded samples for each field and dimension. The precise coefficient matrices are in `control-results.json`.

Each sample is m-primary because it contains all variable squares. The computation certifies the **entire** linear-presentation condition, rather than checking only a few syzygies without a stopping bound. Indeed A=S/I is zero above degree four: every monomial of degree five in four variables contains a square. The degree-e component of the second Koszul module of A is A_(e−2)⊗∧²K⁴, which vanishes for e>6. Hence Tor₂^S(A,K), equivalently Tor₁^S(I,K), vanishes there. It therefore suffices to compare all relation spaces in degrees 3,4,5,6. The script computes the degree-three kernel and, in degrees four through six, checks that its monomial multiples span the full multiplication-map kernel.

Twenty samples pass this complete test. Samples are not claimed to be distinct ideals; in particular every six-dimensional cross-subspace sample defines m². Every one has cube rank 84, the full dimension binomial(9,3)=84 of S₆, so its cube equals m⁶. The remaining sixteen are rejected as not linearly presented, regardless of their cube ranks. Separate positive and negative controls check m² and the pure-square complete intersection over each field: the former is linearly presented with cube rank 84, whereas the latter fails linear presentation and has cube rank 20. Thus no counterexample was found in this bounded family. Ranks use exact integer arithmetic modulo the field prime, with no floating-point tolerance. The larger assertion over all ideals, all degrees, and all positive characteristics is not inferred from these samples.

## Exact remaining gap and stopping condition

For arbitrary positive characteristic, n≥4, and general nonmonomial I without a suitable initial ideal, this investigation provides neither a universal proof nor a counterexample. A complete result must establish surjectivity of Sym^(n−1)(I_d)→S_((n−1)d), or exhibit an m-primary equigenerated linearly presented ideal where it fails. Replacing it by eventual equality, stronger linearity hypotheses, a monomial subclass, or characteristic zero would not settle this row.

The five approaches above have reached their stated endpoints. Further work would require a genuinely characteristic-sensitive mechanism or a new certified counterexample family. No sixth proof-search approach is included. Full-target completion estimate: 15%, a subjective planning estimate reflecting exact scope identification and substantial known coverage, not probability of truth. The elementary partial statements are fully proved; the all-field conjecture remains open in this attempt.
