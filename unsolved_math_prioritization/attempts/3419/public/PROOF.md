# Word problems of smooth/PL 2-knot groups: five attempts and an explicit obstruction

**Target:** ID 3419 / OPG-37237. **Result: unresolved.** No embedding of the required kind, and no word-problem algorithm for all such embeddings, is established here. The partial propositions below have proofs; they do not constitute a solution of the target. Checked 3 October 2026.

## 1. Exact question and conventions

Does some smooth embedding, or locally flat PL embedding, of the 2-sphere in the standard 4-sphere have a complement group with an undecidable word problem? This is the question in [OPG]. The conventional locally flat interpretation is explicit in [GGS, §2]. We do not substitute a wild embedding, a surface of positive genus, a sphere in another 4-manifold, a topologically locally flat sphere with no compatible PL structure, or a 3-knot in the 5-sphere.

The word problem here belongs to **one fixed finitely presented group**: given a word in fixed generators, decide whether it is the identity. It is different from recognizing which input presentations are knot groups, from recognizing the unknot, and from finding one algorithm uniform in a varying family of groups.

Write H_i(G) for integral group homology unless Q is specified. Write E for the compact exterior of a locally flat knot; its interior has the same fundamental group as the open complement.

### Proposition 1. Necessary homological and normal-generation conditions

For such a 2-knot, its group G is finitely presented, H_1(G)=Z, H_2(G)=0, and a meridian normally generates G.

**Proof.** A compact smooth/PL exterior has finite CW type, hence finitely presented fundamental group. Alexander duality for S² in S⁴ gives H_1(E)=Z and H_2(E)=0. Abelianization identifies H_1(G) with H_1(E). Attaching cells of dimensions at least 3 to E produces a K(G,1), and the induced map H_2(E)→H_2(G) is surjective; equivalently use the Hopf exact sequence. Thus H_2(G)=0. The oriented normal bundle is trivial because its Euler number is the self-intersection, which vanishes in S⁴. Gluing S²×D² back to E restores S⁴. Van Kampen identifies its group with G modulo the normal closure of the boundary meridian. Since S⁴ is simply connected, that quotient is trivial. ∎

## 2. Attempt 1: lower the known higher-dimensional construction

The established construction in [GGS, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.3] first embeds an arbitrary finitely presented group G in a finitely presented perfect group P. Its next stage can be checked explicitly. Form

B = ⟨P×P,s,t | s⁻¹(1,p)s=(p,1), t⁻¹(1,p)t=(p,p), p∈P⟩,

K = ⟨B,u | u⁻¹su=s², u⁻¹tu=t²⟩.

Relations indexed by p need only be imposed on a finite generating set. The maps between the three copies of P in P×P are injective. The quotient B→F(s,t) that kills P×P shows that ⟨s,t⟩ is free, and so is ⟨s²,t²⟩. Both stages are genuine HNN constructions; Britton's lemma preserves the embedding G≤P≤B≤K.

Here is the homology calculation, including the step relevant to dimension-lowering. Put A=H_2(P). Because P is perfect, H_2(P×P)=A⊕A and H_1(P×P)=0. In the homology sequence for the two-loop graph of groups defining B, the map from the two edge copies of H_2(P) is, up to changing signs,

(a,b) ↦ (a+b,−a).

Its inverse is (x,y)↦(−y,x+y), for every abelian group A. It follows that H_2(B)=0 and H_1(B)=Z², generated by s,t. At the u-stage the edge group is free of rank two, and its map to H_1(B) is I−2I=−I. Thus H_2(K)=0 and H_1(K)=Z, generated by u. Killing u forces s=s² and t=t², hence s=t=1; the diagonal relation then kills the first P factor, and the other relation kills the second. Consequently u normally generates K.

If G has undecidable word problem, K does too: substitute the fixed words for the images of the generators of G in K. Injectivity makes this a reduction of word problems. Kervaire's realization theorem realizes K as a 3-knot group. These are established results, not a new construction claimed here.

**Exact failure.** The same hypotheses do not provide a 2-knot realization. The original 2-knot characterization [GA94, Theorem 1] requires a connected, unlinked, saddled presentation of genus zero: its braid data, freeness of the two closure groups, and genus condition cannot be omitted. No presentation satisfying those requirements has been supplied for this K. Thickening an arbitrary embedded 2-complex to a 4-dimensional submanifold also supplies no identification of that submanifold with E, no peripheral S²×S¹ boundary, and no prescribed S⁴ filling. Merely checking Proposition 1 is insufficient.

**Source safeguard.** A finite Wirtinger presentation and cyclic abelianization characterize a larger class of orientable surface-complement groups in [GGS, Theorem 2.3]. Their §2 explicitly has K₂ strictly contained in K₃, with K₃ contained in that surface class. Thus “Kervaire conditions plus a Wirtinger presentation” cannot alone be used to identify K₂. In particular, the parenthetical in K3 (2026), Problem 4.35 Remark (5), must not replace the precise original theorem.

## 3. Attempt 2: force a ribbon realization by a labelled tree

Consider a tree T on generators x₀,…,x_{n−1}. For each oriented edge i→j choose a word w_e and the relation x_j=w_e x_i w_e⁻¹. Yajima's realization theorem [Y, §3] realizes this deficiency-one Wirtinger presentation as a ribbon 2-knot group.

### Proposition 2. Tree relators have the required homology, but triangular labels collapse

Every such presentation has abelianization Z, has H_2(G)=0, and is normally generated by x₀. Suppose, more restrictively, that the tree is rooted and its vertices ordered so that every parent precedes its child, and that the label for a child's edge uses only earlier generators. Then G=Z.

**Proof.** The exponent-sum column of a relator is e_j−e_i; label exponents cancel. The incidence matrix of a tree has rank n−1 and a maximal minor ±1, as follows inductively by removing a leaf. Its cokernel is Z. In the presentation 2-complex the cellular boundary C₂→C₁ is injective; therefore H₂ of that complex is zero, and its surjection to H₂(G) proves the assertion. Killing one vertex normally kills all adjacent vertices, and induction along the tree kills every generator.

For the last assertion, x₀ is the root. Inductively suppose all earlier generators equal x₀. The parent and every letter in w_e then become powers of x₀, so the relation gives x_j=x₀. Thus G is generated by x₀, and its already computed abelianization Z proves it is infinite cyclic. ∎

**Exact failure.** A direct triangular encoding of an undecidable presentation therefore destroys, rather than embeds, the intended group. Allowing cyclic dependencies among labels avoids this lemma but requires a faithful embedding/reduction proof; no such proof was obtained. The incidence-matrix checks cannot certify that property.

As a nontrivial control, [N26, proof of Proposition 1.3] gives a deficiency-one Wirtinger form of BS(1,2). This shows that the broader family does not always collapse to Z, but it is not an undecidable example. The HNN algorithm of the next section, with base Z and edge subgroups Z and 2Z, decides the word problem in BS(1,2). Indeed the kernel of BS(1,2)→Z is the increasing union of the cyclic subgroups t⁻ʲ⟨a⟩tʲ. Britton's lemma identifies this union with the additive group Z[1/2], and the relation tat⁻¹=a² puts a in the commutator subgroup, so this kernel is the commutator subgroup. Its unbounded denominators prevent finite generation. Restricting attention to finitely generated commutator groups would therefore omit genuine 2-knot groups.

## 4. Attempt 3: extend the fibred-knot algorithm to every 2-knot

### Proposition 3. Every fibred 2-knot group has solvable word problem

More generally, if H is a finitely generated group with solvable word problem and φ is an automorphism specified by words for images and inverse images of its generators, then H⋊_φ Z has solvable word problem.

**Proof.** Use t h t⁻¹=φ(h). Scan an input word while maintaining a pair (v,k) representing v t^k, where v is a word over generators of H and k∈Z. Appending t or t⁻¹ changes k by ±1. Appending h or h⁻¹ replaces v by v φ^k(h^{±1}); this is a finite computation using φ or φ⁻¹. At the end, if k≠0 the word is nonidentity by projection onto Z. If k=0, apply the word-problem algorithm in H to v. Every operation terminates. ∎

A fibred 2-knot exterior is the mapping torus of a compact 3-manifold with boundary S². Its group is of the displayed form, and the established word-problem theorem for compact 3-manifold groups applies. This is also the exclusion recorded in [H, p.7]. For one fixed knot, choosing the finite monodromy data is enough; this argument does not require recognizing a fibred knot uniformly from an arbitrary presentation.

**Exact failure.** There is no proof here that every 2-knot is fibred. Nor may finitely generated commutator subgroup simply be equated with fibering: the general discussion in [H, §2] states additional conjectural assumptions. The non-fibred case, including infinitely generated kernels of G→Z, remains outside this algorithm.

## 5. Attempt 4: finite quotients and a non-residually-finite candidate

A standard proposed negative answer dovetails two searches for a finitely presented G: enumerate products of conjugates of relators to recognize identity words, and enumerate all finite permutation representations to detect nonidentity words. The second search is complete when G is residually finite.

However, the 2-knot group described in [H, p.7] is

Γ = ⟨a,b,t | tat⁻¹=a², aba⁻¹=b⁻¹, b³=1⟩.

It defeats that method but still has decidable word problem.

### Proposition 4. Γ is not residually finite and has solvable word problem

**Proof.** The base group H=⟨a,b | aba⁻¹=b⁻¹, b³=1⟩ is C₃⋊Z, with unique normal form b^r a^n, r∈{0,1,2}, n∈Z. Its multiplication is

(r,n)(s,m)=(r+(−1)^n s mod 3,n+m).

The presentation of Γ is the HNN extension identifying the infinite cyclic subgroups ⟨a⟩ and ⟨a²⟩. Both injections are genuine, so H embeds and b has order exactly three.

In any finite quotient, conjugacy of a and a² implies that the order of the image of a is odd. Conjugation by a inverts b. Raising that action to this odd order therefore both fixes and inverts b, forcing b²=1. Together with b³=1 this gives b=1. Thus every finite quotient kills a nontrivial element b; Γ is not residually finite.

For decidability, first combine base-group blocks by the displayed multiplication. Membership in ⟨a⟩ means r=0; membership in ⟨a²⟩ means r=0 and n even. Reduce a pinch t a^n t⁻¹ to a^{2n}, or t⁻¹ a^{2n} t to a^n, whenever the intervening base block satisfies the corresponding test. Every reduction removes two stable letters, so the process terminates. A remaining stable letter makes the word nontrivial by Britton's lemma. With no stable letters, the answer is the decidable equality test (r,n)=(0,0) in H. ∎

**Exact failure in both directions.** The finite-quotient algorithm runs forever on b, so residual finiteness cannot be assumed for all 2-knot groups. Conversely, non-residual-finiteness itself is not evidence of an undecidable word problem: the full terminating algorithm above rules out this candidate. Finite permutation experiments only check the implementation of this argument on small examples.

## 6. Attempt 5: test the September 2026 small undecidable group

[KLR26] constructs a group Γ₆ with undecidable word problem, three generators, nine displayed relators, and one normal generator. Its additional homology is material to this question.

### Proposition 5. The group Γ₆ in [KLR26] satisfies dim_Q H₂(Γ₆;Q)≥2

In particular it cannot be the group of a smooth/locally flat sphere knot, in any dimension at least one.

**Proof.** We use precisely the injective HNN descriptions in [KLR26, equations (1), (4), Lemmas 2.2, 3.1–3.2 and Proposition 3.3]. These are cited mathematical inputs. Here are the homology consequences; they do not assume that a presentation's deficiency equals the group's deficiency.

For an HNN extension J of V with edge group W, the rational homology sequence includes

H₂(W) → H₂(V) → H₂(J) → H₁(W) → H₁(V),

where the maps from W are the differences of its two inclusions. A multi-HNN extension has the corresponding direct sum over its edges. If the HNN extension centralizes W, these difference maps are zero. In particular H₂(V) injects into H₂(J), and the cokernel is H₁(W). The sequence follows by applying cellular Mayer–Vietoris to the graph of classifying spaces, whose edge maps induce the given subgroup injections.

1. Γ₁ has vertex group F(d,e) and two free rank-two edge groups, with relations d⁴s_β=s_βd and es_β=s_βe⁴ for β=1,2. The map Q⁴→Q² on H₁ is, up to column signs,

   ((x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂)) ↦ (3x₁+3x₂,−3y₁−3y₂).

   Its rank is two. Since vertex and edge H₂ vanish, H₂(Γ₁;Q)=Q². The same sequence gives H₁(Γ₁;Q)=Q², represented by s₁,s₂.

2. Γ₂ is an HNN extension of Γ₁ along F₅, by the injectivity assertion in Proposition 3.3 of the source. Its sequence injects H₂(Γ₁;Q) and contributes the kernel of a map Q⁵→Q², of dimension at least three. Hence dim H₂(Γ₂;Q)≥5. No unspecified semigroup word has to be evaluated for this bound.

3. Γ₃ centralizes ⟨c,d⟩≅F₂ by adjoining t. Freeness follows from the source's Corollary 3.6. Consequently dim H₂(Γ₃;Q)=dim H₂(Γ₂;Q)+2≥7.

4. Γ₄ centralizes Δ=⟨c,e,P⁻¹tP⟩ by adjoining k. Therefore H₂(Γ₃;Q) injects into H₂(Γ₄;Q), irrespective of the homology of Δ. In particular dim H₂(Γ₄;Q)≥7. **We do not assume Δ is free.**

5. Γ₅ is an HNN extension of Γ₄ with edge group

   A=⟨s,c,d,e,t | d⁴s=sd, es=se⁴, sc=cs, ct=tc, dt=td⟩.

   We compute dim H₂(A;Q)=3. Begin with S=⟨d,e,s | d⁴s=sd, es=se⁴⟩. It is an HNN extension of F₂ along F₂, with invertible rational H₁-difference matrix diag(3,−3), giving H₂(S;Q)=0 and H₁(S;Q)=Q. Adjoining c centralizing ⟨s⟩ gives L with H₂(L;Q)=Q. The subgroup ⟨c,d⟩ in L is free of rank two: ⟨d⟩ intersects ⟨s⟩ trivially, and Britton reduction of an alternating word in c,d supplies no nontrivial pinch. Adjoining t centralizing that F₂ yields H₂(A;Q)=Q³. The source's Lemma 3.1 identifies this very A as the edge group. The image of H₂(A;Q) can therefore remove at most three dimensions from H₂(Γ₄;Q). Exactness gives dim H₂(Γ₅;Q)≥4.

6. Γ₆ has edge group C=⟨c,d,t | ct=tc, dt=td⟩, by Lemma 3.2 of the source. This is F(c,d)×Z(t); the Künneth formula gives H₂(C;Q)=Q². Its image can remove at most two dimensions, whence dim H₂(Γ₆;Q)≥4−2=2.

Proposition 1 gives H₂(G)=0 for a 2-knot group, and hence H₂(G;Q)=0. The identical Alexander-duality/Hopf argument applies to sphere knots in higher dimensions and to classical knots. Thus Γ₆ cannot be one. ∎

For completeness, Γ₆ has abelianization Z, so its failure is not explained by H₁. In the source's equations (2), abelianization identifies d=t=c=y=e=k and s₂=−s₁. The relations give 3d=2d=0, hence d=0. The semigroup property (c), applied in this abelian quotient, kills s₁,s₂. All remaining relations permit z to map freely to 1∈Z. Therefore H₁(Γ₆)=Z. This calculation does not certify any replacement quotient or modified group: adding relations to kill H₂ could also destroy the embedded undecidable group.

**Exact failure.** The recent small group itself is excluded. A construction that kills its H₂ while preserving an undecidable subgroup and providing a genuine 2-knot realization is still missing. The high-dimensional repair in Attempt 1 does not supply the last requirement.

## 7. What the modern decision results do and do not supply

The group-trisection correspondence [BKKLR25, §5C–D] gives recognition formulations and an undecidability corollary; its Question 5.9 still asks about an unknot-recognition algorithm in dimension four. It does not construct a fixed 2-knot group with undecidable word problem. Logically, failure of a uniform algorithm over varying input presentations does not imply failure of every algorithm for one fixed group. Nor does the implication “an undecidable 2-knot word problem would obstruct unknot recognition” reverse by itself.

The 2026 BNSR result [N26, §3.2] and the 2026 small-group result [KLR26] were checked at their actual relevant proofs, not treated as solutions from their titles. No source inspected in this investigation supplies the missing 2-knot example or a universal word-problem theorem. This is a bounded literature finding, not a proof that no further result exists.

## 8. Verification and remaining task

`verify.py` checks exact linear algebra, labelled-tree incidence minors, the base-group operations and HNN word-problem implementation in Proposition 4, and finite permutation controls. It proves no undecidability statement, verifies no geometric realization, and supplies no certificate that the target is solved. The unrestricted partial results rest on the written arguments and explicitly cited established theorems.

The remaining task is exactly the target: exhibit one smooth/locally flat PL 2-knot in standard S⁴ together with a rigorous undecidability reduction for its group, or prove decidability for every such group. Five attempts have been completed here without either outcome.
