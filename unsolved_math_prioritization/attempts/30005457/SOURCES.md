# Source and prior-work gate

The authoritative question is Open Problem 3 in Victor Kleptsyn's contribution to [OWR 12/2023](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/47008), printed pp. 653–656. The exact question page was rendered and inspected. The DOI is 10.4171/owr/2023/12.

This is WARM: independent Poisson clocks at each vertex v, with bounded positive rate p(v), select an incident edge with probability proportional to its current tally raised to alpha, then increase that tally by one. It is not a reinforced random walk. The target is the nearest-neighbor graph Z^d for some finite d≥2, some alpha>1, and bounded p with inf p=0, with an infinite surviving connected component with positive probability or almost surely. The printed question also asks whether such a component, if it exists, is unique. The short imported record omits the explicit rate hypotheses and uniqueness clause; both are retained here.

The workshop introduction permits specified initial counts but does not explicitly state their positivity or normalization. The cited [Hirsch–Holmes–Kleptsyn tree construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07682) starts every tally at one. This attempt uses that standard unit initialization and does not exploit zero or specially engineered infinite initial tallies.

The tree paper's Theorem 1 produces infinitely many infinite components of linearly reinforced edges on a sufficiently high-degree regular rooted tree with rates q^depth, for suitable q in (0,1). It proves a stronger survival property there, not on the lattice. Its finitely-joined-subgraph extension does not authorize infinitely many new boundary contacts or edge subdivisions. Its Galton–Watson crystallization construction uses independent vertex blocks and exponential tree growth.

The earlier absence theorem, [Hirsch–Holmes–Kleptsyn 2021](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AAP1587), rules out infinite components on bounded-degree graphs for sufficiently large alpha depending on maximal degree. Therefore arbitrarily increasing alpha cannot be a valid construction route. The [2023 strong-regime paper](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6544/acc9a0) studies finite components and homogeneous-line behavior; none of those scopes is silently substituted for the present question. Its university-hosted published PDF returned403 in this pass; only the primary repository abstract and the tree paper's references were inspected at this checkpoint.
