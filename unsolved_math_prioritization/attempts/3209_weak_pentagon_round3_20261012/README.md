## Accepted conclusions

- Every finite simple triangle-free subcubic graph with a matching cut complement split as one demand on one side and at most four on the other has a prescribed Clebsch-class extension. This theorem is elementary and needs no global optimality or signed choosability theorem.
- With one demand on one side and arbitrarily many on the other, the exact signed-list reduction yields a positive theorem when every boundary entity has degree at most four. This separate theorem retains the explicitly cited external signed degree-list result.
- The one-versus-many prescription fails on the explicit 18-vertex subcubic 5+1 graph and its explicit 28-vertex cubic 7+1 completion. The proof is a five-versus-four antipodal-pair contradiction, equivalently signed doubled K₅.
- Their obstructed cuts have sizes 20 and 34; their true maxima are 22 and 35. Every one of the 7 and 72 normalized global maxima, respectively, has a checked prescribed extension. Both graphs also have explicit unrestricted Clebsch maps.
- Thus four versus five is the sharp larger-side threshold opposite one demand in the subcubic setting. Cubic degree summation forces a five-demand matching cut to split 4+1, so all cubic five-demand cases are covered. Opposite one cubic demand the larger counts are 1,4,7,…, making seven the first possible failing count in that subclass.

The mixed 2+2 four-demand case remains unresolved in this round. Neither bad cut is global, and neither example disproves the original Weak pentagon conjecture or the universal global-maximum-cut strengthening.
