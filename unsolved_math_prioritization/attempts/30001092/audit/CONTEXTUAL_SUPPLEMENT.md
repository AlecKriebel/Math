# Contextual supplement to the accepted five-route packet

These details support Propositions 2 and 5 of the frozen `SCOPED_RESULTS.md`. They change no stated result, hypothesis or approach count. The originals remain unchanged.

## A. Explicit violation sets for the fixed-splitting disintegration argument

Work on a fixed invariant conull Borel set on which the action is free, all classes are infinite and the chosen decomposition R = * R_i holds exactly. Enumerate Gamma as (g_j). Let E_i be the infinite-class support of R_i and E = union_i E_i, as in Proposition 2. The following are fixed Borel subsets of the original space:

- O_(i,k) = E_i intersect E_k, for i distinct from k;
- V_(i,j) = {x in E_i : g_j x belongs to E but not E_i};
- W_(i,j) = {x in E_i : g_j x belongs to E_i and (g_j x,x) does not belong to R_i};
- C = X minus union_j g_j E.

The overlaps O test disjointness. The sets V test invariance of each E_i inside R restricted to E. The sets W test the only nonautomatic inclusion in R restricted to E_i = R_i restricted to E_i; the other inclusion follows from R_i being a subrelation of R. C tests completeness. Countable unions suffice throughout.

For any invariant probability measure nu under which the fixed free, aperiodic action and decomposition have full measure, the decomposition is nu-inessential if and only if every displayed failure set has nu-measure zero. Indeed Proposition 2 gives the forward implication. Conversely remove the union of these null sets and its R-saturation. On the remaining invariant conull set, the three conditions of Proposition 2 hold literally. Completeness survives because the removal was saturated.

Now disintegrate the original invariant probability measure as mu = integral mu_t d eta(t). Almost every mu_t is invariant, ergodic, and supported on the fixed conull Borel set. In particular every partial Borel isomorphism in R_i is mu_t-preserving: partition its domain into the countably many pieces where it equals g_j and use the invariance of mu_t.

If the fixed splitting were inessential for eta-almost every t, every displayed Borel failure set would have mu_t-measure zero for almost every t. Its mu-measure would then be zero by the disintegration identity. Proposition 2 would make the original splitting inessential, a contradiction. Equivalently, at least one fixed failure set has positive mu-measure, so it has positive conditional measure on a positive-eta-measure set of parameters. This yields the required non-FI ergodic action.

This argument does not select decompositions varying with t. It therefore supplies no converse statement about the FI behavior of almost all ergodic components of an FI action.

## B. Probability-preserving free generator for the amenable factor

In Proposition 5, TW v3 Lemma 5.1 supplies a full-domain aperiodic amenable free factor S of R. The primary theorem of Connes–Feldman–Weiss supplies a single nonsingular automorphism T generating an amenable countable measured relation. It does not require S to be ergodic. See the statement on p. 431, the discussion of single generators on p. 434, and Theorem 10 on p. 444 of:

A. Connes, J. Feldman and B. Weiss, *An amenable equivalence relation is generated by a single transformation*, Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems 1 (1981), 431–450. https://doi.org/10.1017/S014338570000136X

For this application T belongs to the full group of the p.m.p. relation S, hence T preserves the given probability measure. Its orbits are exactly the S-classes; aperiodicity of S makes every nonzero power of T fixed-point-free almost everywhere. Thus T is the free p.m.p. Z-generator used in the proof, without any assumption that S itself is ergodic. All exceptional sets may be removed after saturating them under the countable ambient relation.

## C. Measurable free-product cocycle extension

Orient relation arrows as (y,x), from x to y. On S define d_S(T^n x,x)=n and on Q define d_Q(y,x)=0, where R=S*Q. The first is a Borel function: the disjoint Borel graphs of the T^n enumerate S. On any finite composable factor path, assign the sum of the factor values.

Every pair in R is connected by such a path. Consecutive arrows in one factor may be replaced by their product without changing the sum, using that factor's cocycle identity. Identity arrows may be removed; an arrow and its inverse have total value zero. The free-product normal form gives a unique reduced alternating path for each nonidentity arrow. Thus every path with the same endpoints has the same sum, defining d on R.

Measurability can be checked without choosing arbitrary orbit representatives. A fixed countable enumeration of Gamma gives a countable enumeration of all finite candidate paths starting at x. The conditions that a candidate reaches y, that each step lies in its prescribed factor, and that it is reduced are Borel. Select the first valid candidate. On each such Borel piece the sum is a finite sum of the factor cocycles. Consequently d is Borel, and concatenating paths gives d(z,x)=d(z,y)+d(y,x).

The group cocycle is c(g,x)=d(gx,x). After superrigidity has made c a coboundary, one takes a conull set on which the formula holds for all g at once. The sets {x:Tx=g_j x} are Borel and, on the free set, form a partition. The point-dependent substitution yielding b(Tx)−b(x)=1 is therefore valid. No boundedness or integrability assumption on b is needed for the integer-level-set contradiction.

## D. Exact symbolic scope controls

The canonical criterion handles the following situations in the intended direction. These are mathematical controls of the existing criterion, not numerical experiments or additional candidate constructions.

1. In a genuine sliding R = R restricted to A * T, where R is aperiodic, A is a Borel complete section and T is a smooth connector with transversal A, recurrence makes the first factor aperiodic on A. The p.m.p. connector has finite classes almost everywhere. Thus the infinite-factor supports are A and the empty set modulo null sets. The criterion correctly recognizes the decomposition as inessential.
2. If a free-product decomposition has two full-domain aperiodic factors, both infinite-factor supports equal X. Their overlap has full measure, so the criterion correctly rejects inessentiality. This is the situation used for H*Z in Proposition 1.
3. Finite ambient relations are outside Proposition 2's aperiodicity hypothesis. They are FI by the finite-relation argument in the primary source, not by applying an infinite-class-support criterion with E empty.

No finite sample from an orbit can establish these measured statements. Their justifications use complete sections, infinite classes and measure preservation exactly as stated.
