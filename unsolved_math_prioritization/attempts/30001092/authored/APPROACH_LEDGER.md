## 1. Promote a quotient action to a free witness

Start with H = F_2 x F_2 and Gamma = H * Z. Pull a free Bernoulli H-action back along Gamma -> H, giving an ergodic FI relation. Compare it with a free Bernoulli Gamma-action. This constructs the requested distinction only in the incorrectly broadened category: the first action kills the Z generator. Test the standard repair by taking its diagonal product with a free Gamma-action. The repaired action is free, but its Gamma relation has the unavoidable essential H/Z free-product splitting. Thus freeness repair destroys the proposed FI certificate. The explicit construction and all checks are in Proposition 1 of SCOPED_RESULTS.md.

Outcome: source-category false positive diagnosed; no source solution. Count: 1/5.

## 2. Manufacture dependence by invariant mixing

Try to exploit the fact that the source does not require ergodicity, patching actions on invariant pieces. Prove a canonical criterion for inessentiality using the infinite-class supports of the factors. Use it to prove countable-mixture stability of FI and to extract an ergodic non-FI action from any non-FI free action. The missing FI action cannot be supplied by countably mixing known non-FI actions. An unrestricted claim that FI passes to almost every ergodic component would need a further measurable-selection argument and is not asserted.

Outcome: rigorous nonergodic reduction and a precise selection boundary; no mixed pair. Count: 2/5.

## 3. Use common extensions to transfer a splitting

Form diagonal products of two free actions of the same group. Check that projections are class-bijective morphisms and apply the correct direction of [AG, Lemma 4.15]. A non-FI free factor action forces the product to be non-FI. This gives an exact reformulation of the source question as the failure of FI under a class-bijective extension between free actions of one group. The reverse transfer, from an FI factor to an FI extension, is exactly what is unproved; assuming it would assume a negative answer.

Outcome: exact extension-instability reformulation and blocked transfer direction. Count: 3/5.

## 4. Induce and stabilize through finite group operations

Attempt to transport an FI certificate and a non-FI certificate to the same group using finite-index induction and finite kernels. Give the full induced-action formula, freeness, ergodicity and complete-section checks. Show mixed pairs ascend finite-index inclusions if a seed already exists. For a split finite direct factor K, prove that Gamma and Gamma x K have exactly the same attainable FI/non-FI types. A general finite normal quotient gives a one-way reduction; the reverse is not claimed without a splitting. Infinite-index coset induction cannot simply be normalized into a probability action.

Outcome: rigorous transport rules that preserve rather than create the missing seed. Count: 4/5.

## 5. Force FI by killing integer-valued cocycles

For an explicitly ergodic free action of a nonamenable group with Hom(Gamma,Z) = 0, show that Z-cocycle superrigidity implies FI. An essential split would supply an aperiodic amenable free factor by TW v3, whose generator gives an integer cocycle incompatible with a probability-preserving coboundary. This proves a conditional, action-specific FI criterion. Try the Bernoulli U_fin-superrigid realization; [PS, Corollary 1.2] forces beta_1^(2) = 0, which excludes a non-FI free action of that group. We do not confuse U_fin-superrigidity with the weaker Z-only hypothesis.

Outcome: conditional FI criterion, but no group/action simultaneously satisfying it and admitting a non-FI free action. Count: 5/5.
