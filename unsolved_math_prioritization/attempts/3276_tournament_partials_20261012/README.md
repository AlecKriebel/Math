## Exact target

Every nonempty finite tournament whose arcs use at most three colors should have either a directed cyclic rainbow triangle or a vertex from which every other vertex is reachable by a monochromatic directed path. The path color may depend on the destination. A transitive rainbow triple is allowed. The desired reachability is outgoing.

## Accepted results and limits

- The finite, computer-assisted conclusion covers every tournament of order at most eight. For obstruction Hamilton cycles whose colors repeat three distinct colors, lengths 3, 6 and 9 are excluded; a minimal counterexample with that periodic obstruction cycle would have at least 12 vertices. There is no unrestricted order-nine result.
- The exact all-order switch-reachability relaxation is characterized in full. Every genuinely three-color cyclic word survives it, including periodic 012 words. Every strictly alternating even binary word also survives, although the stronger, credited source proposition already excludes such obstruction cycles. Odd binary survivors require at least three nonswitches and the exact cyclic gap restriction.
- Repeatedly deleting a vertex missing one incident color or having fewer than two incoming or outgoing colors yields a canonical residual set. Every induced-minimal counterexample is contained in it, by credited source incidence theorems. Consequently a nonempty, cyclic-rainbow-free tournament whose residual set has at most eight vertices satisfies the target, conditional on those source theorems and the accepted computer-assisted finite bound. The original tournament may have arbitrarily many vertices. This does not say that a source of the residual set extends to a source of the whole tournament.
