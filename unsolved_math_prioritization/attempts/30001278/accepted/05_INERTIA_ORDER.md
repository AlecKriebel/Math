# Approach 5: bound the different directly from inertia order

This route deliberately avoids Hodge-theoretic comparison. It exploits a general local-field different estimate and the actual finite image of the lattice representation.

## An elementary different estimate

Let \(L/K\) be any finite Galois extension in the field setting of `NORMALIZATIONS.md`, and put

\[
m=e(L/K),\qquad t=v_p(m).
\]

Then

\[
v_K(\mathcal D_{L/K})\leq et+1-1/m.
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Let \(K'\) be the maximal unramified subextension of \(L/K\). The perfect-residue-field, complete-discrete-valuation hypotheses supply this subextension, and \(L/K'\) is totally ramified of degree \(m\). Let \(\varpi\) be a uniformizer of \(L\), with Eisenstein minimal polynomial

\[
f(X)=X^m+c_{m-1}X^{m-1}+\cdots+c_0\in\mathcal O_{K'}[X].
\]

The ring \(\mathcal O_L\) is generated by \(\varpi\), so its different over \(K'\) is generated by \(f'(\varpi)\). Every nonzero term \(i c_i\varpi^{i-1}\), including \(i=m,c_m=1\), has \(v_L\)-valuation congruent to \(i-1\pmod m\). These residues are distinct. Hence the least-valued term is unique and no cancellation increases the minimum. In particular

\[
v_L(f'(\varpi))\leq v_L(m)+(m-1)
=me\,v_p(m)+m-1.
\]

Divide by \(m\) and use \(\mathcal D_{K'/K}=1\). For \(m=1\), both sides of (1) are zero. ∎

This is the familiar elementary different estimate; the proof fixes its normalization and hypotheses explicitly.

## A sufficient condition for the target

If \(t\leq s=n+a\), subtracting (1)'s right side from \(B\) gives

\[
B-(et+1-1/m)
=e(s-t+b)+1/m-p^{-s}.
\]

Since \(s\geq1\), \(eb>1/p\geq p^{-s}\). Thus the difference is positive, and

\[
\boxed{v_p(e(L/K))\leq n+a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_K(\mathcal D_{L/K})<B.}
\tag{2}
\]

No crystalline or semistable assumption was needed for this implication. The ramification degree, rather than the full extension degree, appears in (2).

For \(T_n\) of rank \(d\), its finite image lies in \(\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)\). The inertia subgroup therefore has order dividing this group. Reduction modulo \(p\) has a kernel of size \(p^{d^2(n-1)}\), and

\[
|\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb F_p)|
=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(p^d-p^i),\qquad
v_p|\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb F_p)|=\frac{d(d-1)}2.
\]

Consequently a readily checked sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{d^2(n-1)+\frac{d(d-1)}2\leq n+a.}
\tag{3}
\]

In rank one the left side is \(n-1\), so **every** rank-one \(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\)-representation coming from a rank-one lattice satisfies the target numerical bound, independently of any Hodge condition. This also handles rank-one cases in the exceptional upper-break bands.

Condition (3) is only sufficient. Its failure says nothing adverse about a representation's actual inertia subgroup. The ambient general linear group can be much larger than that subgroup.

## Test case and limitation

At \(p=3,n=2,r=7\), one has \(s=4\). Rank one is covered. In rank two the ambient group valuation is \(4(2-1)+1=5\), so (3) no longer proves the claim. A specific rank-two representation with \(v_3(e(L/K))\leq4\) is still covered by (2), but the desired inertia-order restriction has not been proved for all semistable or crystalline representations with those weights.

For fixed \(d,n\), sufficiently large \(a\) makes (3) hold, so this argument also covers infinite high-weight portions of the exceptional bands at bounded rank. It does not establish a rank-independent inertia-order restriction.

**Outcome:** a full inertia-order criterion, an explicit rank-dependent criterion, and all rank-one cases. No universal bound on the rank or wild inertia order was assumed or established.
