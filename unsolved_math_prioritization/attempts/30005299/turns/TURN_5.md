## Theorem: the Weddle locus has dimension exactly 24

Fix four projective coordinates over C. Let W be the set of nonzero quartic equations obtained as Jacobian determinants of four independent quadrics, considered up to nonzero scalar. Its Zariski closure is irreducible of dimension **24**. Its smooth-quartic sublocus is nonempty and has the same dimension. The rational map

    Gr(4, Sym^2(C^4)^*) --> P(Sym^4(C^4)^*)

is generically finite onto its image closure. Thus a general realized Weddle quartic has only finitely many defining webs in fixed coordinates; the degree of that finite map is not computed here.

The upper bound is turn 1: the source Grassmannian has dimension 24. Irreducibility follows from irreducibility of the Grassmannian and taking image closure.

For the lower bound consider the affine map from four ordered quadratic forms (40 coefficients) to the 35 coefficients of their determinant. At the four explicit symmetric slices saved in the representation certificate, its differential has rank 25. Each derivative is computed directly as

    d(det M)[Delta M] = sum_ij cofactor_ij(M) Delta M_ij.

The checker constructs the full 35-by-40 integer coefficient matrix, then gives specified row/column indices for a 25-by-25 original minor whose determinant is nonzero modulo 101. A nonzero reduction proves the integer minor, hence the characteristic-zero differential rank, is nonzero. The affine image therefore has dimension at least 25. Its dimension is at most 25 because the ordered basis contributes only a nonzero scalar beyond the 24-dimensional Grassmannian image. Projectivizing gives exactly 24.

The same sample is **smooth over C**, not just numerically nonsingular. For its explicit integer quartic f, the checker forms the degree-nine Macaulay matrix of the ideal generated by the four cubic partial derivatives. It has 220 rows, one per degree-nine monomial, and 336 columns, the four partials multiplied by degree-six monomials. A certified 220-by-220 minor is nonzero modulo 101. Therefore all degree-nine forms lie in the Jacobian ideal over Q. Any common projective zero of the partials would annihilate every degree-nine monomial, impossible. This proves smoothness over C. Smoothness is an open condition in this family, so its nonempty smooth locus is dense and has dimension 24.

In particular the transpose obstruction from turn 2 already occurs for a **smooth** Weddle quartic. Turn 2 deliberately did not claim this before the smoothness certificate was available.

The generic-finiteness assertion now follows from equality of source and image dimensions for the dominant map onto its image closure. It does not assert uniqueness of the defining web, and does not classify exceptional fibers.

## Independent reconstruction of the 33-dimensional determinantal locus

Turn 1 cited the known 33-dimensional locus. The following certificate independently checks that result for this problem's ordinary 4-by-4 linear determinants.

The affine space of all 4-by-4 matrices of four-variable linear forms has dimension 64. SL_4 x SL_4 acts by constant left-right multiplication and preserves the determinant polynomial. The generic orbit has dimension 30: this is witnessed by a coefficient tuple with A_0=I, A_1=diag(1,2,3,4), and A_2 having every off-diagonal entry nonzero. An infinitesimal stabilizer X A_i+A_i Y=0 first gives Y=-X, then makes X diagonal by commuting with A_1, then scalar by commuting with A_2. Trace zero forces X=0. Thus the stabilizer has dimension zero, and generic fibers of the determinant map contain 30-dimensional orbits. The affine image has dimension at most 64-30=34.

For the explicit integer coefficient tuple recorded in `DIMENSION_CERTIFICATES.json`, the 35-by-64 differential coefficient matrix has a specified 34-by-34 minor nonzero modulo 101. Thus the affine image has dimension at least 34, proving equality, and its projectivization has dimension 33. This does **not** assume that each entire fiber is just one left-right orbit; the orbit argument gives only the upper bound, and the separate minor gives the matching lower bound.

A general such quartic is smooth: a general P^3 linear section of the determinant hypersurface in P^15 avoids the rank-at-most-two locus of codimension four and is transverse to the smooth rank-three stratum (Bertini in characteristic zero). This also agrees with the published dimension calculation of Antonelli–Casnati cited in turn 1.

Consequently the Weddle closure has codimension exactly nine inside the irreducible determinantal-quartic closure. In particular a general determinantal quartic is not Weddle. This settles the explicit generic subquestion, with classical inputs and no priority assertion.

## Why this does not finish the source characterization

The map is a linear projection in Plucker coordinates: the alternating determinant construction gives a linear map

    wedge^4 Sym^2(V^*) -> Sym^4(V^*) tensor det(V^*).

Its restriction to the Grassmannian sends a decomposable wedge of quadrics to their Jacobian determinant. The projection has a nonempty indeterminacy locus. For example four independent quadrics involving only x_0,x_1,x_2 have identically zero four-variable Jacobian determinant.

Thus membership in the projective **closure** of the image is not automatically membership in the set of actual nonzero determinants. Taking a projective graph closure or a blow-up resolves the rational map but may add exceptional images; proving that all those images are already realized would require an additional argument. No example of a non-realized closure point is asserted here, and no unsupported equality between closure and realization is used.

An elimination formulation that merely asks for a decomposable Plucker vector in the inverse image of a given quartic, outside the zero-determinant center, restates the problem in finite algebraic equations. It is a valid membership reduction, not a geometric classification or a new resolution of the outstanding boundary cases. Likewise, the exact fixed-matrix symmetrizer criterion from turn 2 cannot be promoted to an intrinsic criterion without controlling inequivalent representations.
