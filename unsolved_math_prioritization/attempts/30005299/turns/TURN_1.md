# Substantive turn 1: dimension obstruction

2026-10-01 05:49 UTC. Status: partial result, unreviewed. Completion estimate: 20% of the full characterization; the explicit generic subquestion has a negative answer by the following standard dimension argument. No novelty claim.

Let V be a fixed complex vector space of dimension four. Webs of quadrics are parametrized by Gr(4, Sym^2(V^*)), of dimension 4(10-4)=24. On the nonempty open subset where the Jacobian determinant is nonzero, sending a web to its Weddle equation defines a morphism to P(Sym^4(V^*)). This is well-defined because changing the basis of the web multiplies the determinant by the nonzero determinant of the basis change. Nonemptiness follows from the web spanned by x_0^2,...,x_3^2.

Consequently the closure of the locus of Weddle quartic equations has dimension at most 24. This already includes all coordinate changes of projective three-space: changing coordinates takes one web to another web in the same Grassmannian. Adding 15 dimensions for projectivities a second time would be incorrect.

The locus of smooth linear determinantal quartics in fixed projective three-space has dimension 33. This is a known result, explicitly recorded in Antonelli–Casnati, *Steiner representations of hypersurfaces*, accepted manuscript p. 17 before Proposition 5.7, published International Journal of Mathematics 36(4) (2025), DOI 10.1142/S0129167X24500812. Author-hosted institutional PDF: https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2998088/84586345-55a3-453e-99d2-8a31b701d3d2/SteinerPfaffianFINAL.pdf . Its dimension formula is 16h^0(O(1))-1-2 dim PGL(4)=33. We use the published dimension result, rather than assume without proof that left-right matrix equivalence accounts for every fiber.

Therefore a general determinantal quartic is not a Weddle quartic. The two loci differ in dimension by at least nine. This addresses the named specific subquestion, but does not characterize every special, possibly singular or nonreduced quartic that occurs. The full original problem remains unresolved here.

Next independent mechanisms to investigate: recoverability and fibers of the web-to-quartic map; intrinsic geometry of Jacobian/Steinerian quartics and line configurations; effective integrability tests across all determinant representations. A criterion that merely restates existence of the same web is not a full classification.
