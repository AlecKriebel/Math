# Independently verified classical adapter for the whole product

This is a newly spelled-out audit deduction of the exact target from pre-existing stronger embedded facts. It is not a claim that the final formula was printed explicitly in an earlier source. The broad outward-collar mechanism was supplied by the parent after this family's independent criterion seal and initial primary-source comparison. This family independently checked every premise against the primary bodies, worked out the collapse and cell-poset mechanisms, then inspected the separately sealed exact-family adapter for additional defects. No defect survives the checks below.

Fix d>=2 and 0<=i<=d-2. Let M be the boundary of the standard d-cross-polytope, K=B(i,d), and K' its complementary facet complex. Set r=i+1, q=d-i-1>=1 and let X be the coordinate cross-polytope sphere on the first r coordinate pairs. All neighborhoods, maps, manifolds and collapses in this argument are PL/polyhedral. The conclusion concerns **all of K**, including its boundary.

## 1. The strong embedded collapse is an actual collapse

Klee--Novik Theorem 1.2(c),(d) gives that K and K' are compact PL (d-1)-manifolds with boundary; K' is simplicially isomorphic to B(d-i-2,d). Remark 3.7 records K collapsing successively to B(i,d-1),...,B(i,i+1)=X in this same ambient coordinate realization.

For completeness, expand that recorded collapse using the authors' exact shelling restriction sets. At an intermediate j>=i+2 put L_x=lk(x_j,B(i,j)) and L_y=lk(y_j,B(i,j)). All facets of st(x_j) contain x_j, so deleting that vertex from the shelling in Lemma 3.5 gives a shelling of L_x with the same restriction Sel(tau). No switch is recorded at j, so Sel(tau) excludes x_j; moreover

    |Sel(tau)| <= i <= j-2 < j-1 = number of vertices of the link facet.

Thus every restriction set is a proper subset of its link facet. Reverse the shelling: for a facet F with restriction R, its currently new faces form the interval {G:R subset G subset F}. Choose a vertex v in F minus R and pair each G omitting v with G union {v}, deleting pairs in descending face size. Because the new-face interval has no face in the earlier shelling prefix, these are elementary free-face collapses removing that interval. Repetition leaves the first facet, a simplex, which collapses to a vertex. The sign-negating involution gives the identical conclusion for L_y. This supplies collapsibility directly, without replacing it by a bare homology argument.

For a collapsible L, its cone collapses to its base L, relative to L (Rourke--Sanderson Chapter 3, printed p.40, Example C). All removed faces in x_j*L_x contain x_j and cannot be faces of y_j*L_y. Hence this relative collapse remains valid in their union; likewise for y_j. The final equator is L_x union L_y=B(i,j-1): deleting a last sign does not increase switches, and every allowed shorter facet extends by repeating its final sign. We obtain B(i,j) collapsing to B(i,j-1). Iteration proves the asserted embedded K collapsing to X for every parameter.

The full-coordinate endpoint B(i,i+1) is used only as the last collapsed sphere. The links in intermediate collapses have j>=i+2, so the strict proper-restriction inequality is valid at every step.

## 2. The complementary manifolds have exactly the same frontier

K and K' cover M and share no facet. For an ambient ridge there are two incident ambient facets. It is a boundary ridge of K exactly when one is in K and the other in K', so it is then a boundary ridge of K' as well. Boundary complexes of these manifolds are generated by their boundary ridges.

To check all lower-dimensional common faces, let sigma lie in both K and K'. The facets of M containing sigma form the hypercube graph on the remaining coordinate choices. A path between a K-facet and a K'-facet has an adjacent crossing pair. Their common ridge contains sigma and has one facet in each complex, so sigma belongs to the common boundary. Conversely all boundary ridges and their faces are shared. Thus

    K intersect K' = partial K = partial K'.

In the case of no remaining coordinates sigma is a facet, so it cannot be shared; there is no exceptional empty-link argument. The frontier in M is precisely this common boundary since interiors have a full local manifold neighborhood and the complementary facet partition changes at exactly these ridges and faces.

## 3. Outward collaring fixes the earlier boundary-core gap

By Rourke--Sanderson Corollary 2.26 (printed p.26, following the collaring theorem 2.25, pp.24--26), the compact boundary of each PL manifold has a PL collar. Choose a collar c:partial K' times [0,2] into K', with c(z,0)=z, and set

    N = K union c(partial K times [0,1]).

N is a compact embedded PL (d-1)-manifold with boundary the new collar level 1. The two collars join to a product neighborhood of the former common boundary, so there is no unproved local-flatness assertion at that seam. By compressing the combined inward collar of K and outward collar interval with a piecewise linear increasing interval homeomorphism, then extending by the identity off this collar, N is PL homeomorphic to K. This does not preserve every old boundary point and need not do so.

The collar cylinder collapses to its base relative to that base. Rourke--Sanderson Chapter 3, p.40, Example D gives compact P times I collapsing to P times {0}. Its product triangulation/face collapses delete no base face, so adjoining K does not invalidate any free pair. Hence N collapses to K, and therefore to X. The embedded collar is PL, so its product triangulation and the triangulation of K can be made compatible by finite subdivisions (Rourke--Sanderson Section 2, subdivision/triangulation results); the notion of polyhedral collapse used in Corollary 3.30 is exactly the one used here.

Every point of K is in the ambient interior of N: old interior points already have neighborhoods in K, while old boundary points have the outward collar neighborhood on the complementary side as well as K on their own side. In particular X is in int N, although X met the boundary of K before the enlargement. Thus N is an actual neighborhood of the compact X in int M=M.

No classification of an abstract disc bundle is used. No claim that K itself was a neighborhood of X is used. The universal e_1 boundary control in CONTROL_PROOFS.md remains correct but no longer blocks this repaired route.

## 4. Exact regular-neighborhood recognition and uniqueness

Rourke--Sanderson Corollary 3.30 on printed p.41 assumes an actual neighborhood N of X in int M and characterizes regular neighborhoods by compact manifold with boundary plus collapse to X. Section 3 checks every hypothesis. Thus N is a regular neighborhood of this specific embedded coordinate sphere X.

Theorem 3.8 on p.33 gives an ambient PL homeomorphism fixing X between two regular neighborhoods of X in M; its proof is by derived-subdivision isomorphisms. Therefore N is PL homeomorphic to the standard epsilon-neighborhood N_0 of the same X. There is no appeal to regular-neighborhood uniqueness for merely abstractly homeomorphic spines.

## 5. The standard epsilon-neighborhood is a PL product

Write Y for the complementary coordinate cross-polytope (q-1)-sphere. Then M=X*Y as a finite simplicial join. Let f be the simplicial function zero on the X vertices and one on the Y vertices. Since X is a full subcomplex, the epsilon-neighborhood

    N_0 = {f<=1/2}

is a regular neighborhood of X by the epsilon/derived-neighborhood construction on Rourke--Sanderson pp.32--33: choose the new derived vertices on f=1/2. This is a compact polyhedral neighborhood.

For nonempty simplices A in X and B in Y, intersect A*B with f<=1/2. The resulting convex truncated-join cell has vertices the vertices a of A, together with the midpoints (a+b)/2 of each A--B edge. Its face lattice agrees with the convex product A times cone(B): a corresponds to (a,apex), and (a+b)/2 to (a,b). Its lateral faces are truncated subjoins A'*B or A*B'; its top truncation faces are products A' times B'. These match respectively the product faces A' times cone(B), A times cone(B'), and A' times B'. Empty B gives the base cells A, corresponding to A times {apex}. Thus these correspondences are compatible on common faces throughout X*Y.

The finite cell complex of N_0 is consequently abstractly isomorphic to the product cell complex X times cone(Y). By Rourke--Sanderson Lemma 2.18 and its proof (p.20), derived subdivisions of abstractly isomorphic cell complexes are simplicially isomorphic, yielding a PL homeomorphism. The naive join-parameter expression is bilinear in moving coordinates and is **not** claimed to be PL; the finite cell-poset argument supplies the PL map.

Since X is the standard PL i-sphere and cone(Y) is a standard PL q-ball,

    K ≅PL N ≅PL N_0 ≅PL S^i times D^(d-i-1).

This is the entire exact target. It uses neither the candidate spectral flow nor the semialgebraic Hauptvermutung.

## 6. Endpoints and priority interpretation

For i=0, X is two points and this same construction gives two q-balls. For i=d-2, q=1 and Y is two points, whose cone is an interval; the complementary K' is a union of two balls and the outward collars cover both common-boundary components. The smallest case d=2,i=0 uses intervals and collared endpoints, with the same hypotheses. The trivial i=d-1 and d=1,i=0 endpoints are the whole cross-polytope sphere times D^0 and require no collar argument.
