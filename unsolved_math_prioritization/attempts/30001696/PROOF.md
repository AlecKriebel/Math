## 1. The exact conclusion

Label the vertices of the standard d-cross-polytope by
\(x_j=e_j\), \(y_j=-e_j\), \(1\le j\le d\). A facet is encoded by its
word of d signs. Let \(B(i,d)\) be the subcomplex generated by facets
having at most i changes between successive signs.

**Theorem.** For all integers \(d\ge2\) and \(0\le i\le d-2\),
\[
 |B(i,d)|\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ S^i\times D^{d-i-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]
Thus \(B(i,d)\) is a combinatorial triangulation of the proposed
sphere--ball product. The endpoint \(i=d-1\) is the entire cross-polytope
boundary and gives the trivial product \(S^{d-1}\times D^0\); it also
covers \(d=1,i=0\).

Klee--Novik [1], Remark3.7, already record an actual embedded collapse
through B(i,d-1), ..., B(i,i+1) to the standard coordinate sphere, and
a disc-bundle consequence, in addition to the manifold, complementary
manifold, homology, boundary and low-dimensional product facts. The
whole product follows from that stronger embedded result and classical
PL collaring and regular-neighborhood recognition/uniqueness [8].
CLASSICAL_PRIOR_ADAPTER.md gives the complete typed deduction, including
an outward collar because the coordinate core initially meets the boundary.
This is a newly spelled-out audit deduction from stronger prior facts;
it is not evidence that an earlier author printed or recognized the
final product statement. CURRENT_PRIORITY_SCOPE.md records the precise
conservative classification and historical limits.

We construct a semialgebraic homeomorphism with the full ball product.
The PL assertion follows from the semialgebraic Hauptvermutung. The
construction uses a classical totally positive flow and an explicit
spectral cross-section; the extension across its attracting sphere is
made the identity near that sphere.

## 2. Sign variation and the geometric realization

For a nonzero real vector \(v\), write \(s^-(v)\) for its number of sign
changes after removing zero entries, and \(s^+(v)\) for the maximum
number after independently filling every zero with either sign. Define
\[
 K_i=\{v\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}:s^-(v)\le i\},\qquad
 C_i=K_i\cap S^{d-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The cone \(K_i\) is closed relative to \(\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\):
an excluded vector has a nonzero alternating subsequence of length
\(i+2\), and this persists under sufficiently small perturbations.

The minimum number of changes among sign completions is exactly
\(s^-\). Zeros between equal signs can be filled with that sign; zeros
between opposite signs can be filled with a single transition; leading
and trailing zeros need add no change. Consequently the usual realization
of \(B(i,d)\) on the \(\ell^1\)-unit sphere is precisely
\(K_i\cap\{\|v\|_1=1\}\). Radial normalization identifies it
semialgebraically with \(C_i\).

The relative interior in the round sphere is
\[
 \operatorname{int}(C_i)
 =\{v\in S^{d-1}:s^+(v)\le i\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Indeed, near a fixed vector the signs of its nonzero entries do not change,
while its zero entries may acquire either sign. All nearby possibilities
are allowed exactly when every completion is allowed. If a forbidden
completion exists, arbitrarily small perturbations realizing it show that
the vector is not interior. Both sets in (2.1)--(2.2), and their boundary,
are semialgebraic finite unions of sign strata.

## 3. A totally positive flow with algebraic spectral coordinates

Put \(N=d-1\) and temporarily index coordinates by \(j=0,\ldots,N\).
Let \(w_j=\binom Nj\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{w_j})\), and
define the tridiagonal operator
\[
 (Lf)(j)=(N-j)f(j+1)+j f(j-1),
\tag{3.1}
\]
where the out-of-range terms have coefficient zero. Set \(J=DLD^{-1}\).
Detailed balance \(w_j(N-j)=w_{j+1}(j+1)\) shows that J is symmetric,
with zero diagonal and positive adjacent entries
\[
 J_{j,j+1}=J_{j+1,j}=\sqrt{(j+1)(N-j)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The operator L preserves the evaluation vectors of polynomials of degree
at most k, and on a polynomial with leading term \(x^k\) its leading
term is \((N-2k)x^k\). The eigenvalues are therefore the distinct
numbers
\[
 \lambda_k=N-2k,\qquad k=0,\ldots,N.
\tag{3.3}
\]
For each k there is a rational polynomial \(p_k\) of degree exactly k
whose evaluation vector is an eigenvector of L with eigenvalue
\(\lambda_k\): solve the triangular coefficient equations, whose
diagonal differences are nonzero. Hence J has an orthonormal algebraic
eigenbasis \(v_0,\ldots,v_N\), obtained by normalizing
\(D(p_k(0),\ldots,p_k(N))\). These are the familiar Krawtchouk spectral
coordinates, but the elementary argument above is sufficient here.

**Lemma 3.1.** For every \(t>0\), all minors of \(e^{tJ}\) are
strictly positive.

**Proof.** In the standard ordered wedge basis, the k-th additive
compound of J has nonnegative off-diagonal entries. A nonzero such entry
moves one occupied index by one position to an adjacent unoccupied index.
Because indices do not cross, its wedge sign is positive. These moves
connect all k-subsets of \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\). Thus, for
\(1\le k<d\), the additive compound is an irreducible matrix with
nonnegative off-diagonal entries, and its exponential has all entries
strictly positive for \(t>0\). This follows directly by adding a scalar
multiple of the identity to make every entry nonnegative and using the
power series: a positive-weight path supplies a positive term for each
matrix entry.

The identity between the multiplicative compound of \(e^{tJ}\) and
the exponential of the additive compound says precisely that these
entries are its k-by-k minors. For \(k=d\), its determinant is
\(e^{t\operatorname{tr}J}=1\). \(\square\)

We use the classical strict variation-diminishing theorem:
\[
 A\text{ strictly totally positive},\ x\ne0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s^+(Ax)\le s^-(x).
\tag{3.4}
\]
This precise zero-sensitive form is stated in Margaliot--Sontag [3,
Theorem 3], and underlies Schwarz's totally positive differential systems
[2]. It is an explicit classical input, not an assumed novel lemma.

Normalize the linear flow to the sphere:
\[
 \Phi_t(x)=\frac{e^{tJ}x}{\|e^{tJ}x\|}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Equations (2.2), (3.4), and Lemma 3.1 give the strict trapping property
\[
 \Phi_t(C_i)\subset\operatorname{int}(C_i)\qquad(t>0).
\tag{3.6}
\]

## 4. Attracting and repelling subspaces

Set \(r=i+1\), and split orthogonally
\[
 E=\operatorname{span}\{v_0,\ldots,v_{r-1}\},\qquad
 F=E^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{v_r,\ldots,v_N\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Both are nonzero in the nontrivial parameter range. We need the two
uniform geometric facts
\[
 S(E)\subset\operatorname{int}(C_i),\qquad S(F)\cap C_i=\varnothing.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the first, a nonzero vector in E has coordinates
\(\sqrt{w_j}p(j)\) for a nonzero polynomial of degree at most
\(r-1=i\). Such a vector has \(s^+\le r-1\). To check the assertion
even at zeros, suppose a sign completion contains an alternating
subsequence of length \(r+1\), at nodes \(j_0<\cdots<j_r\). The
Lagrange interpolation identity is
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^r
 \frac{p(j_\ell)}{\prod_{h\ne\ell}(j_\ell-j_h)}=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]
The denominator signs alternate. All nonzero summands in (4.3) therefore
have the same sign, up to a common reversal. At least one is nonzero
because p cannot vanish at \(r+1\) distinct nodes. This is a
contradiction. Positive factors \(\sqrt{w_j}\) do not affect signs.
Equation (2.2) proves the first assertion of (4.2).

For the second, suppose \(0\ne z\in F\) has at most \(r-1\) sign
changes after deleting zeros. Choose a polynomial p with one root
strictly between each consecutive pair of opposite-sign nonzero blocks,
and no other roots. Its degree is at most \(r-1\), and its overall
sign can be chosen so that \(p(j)\) has the sign of \(z_j\) whenever
\(z_j\ne0\). Then \(y_j=\sqrt{w_j}p(j)\) defines a vector y in E
with \(\langle y,z\rangle>0\), contradicting orthogonality. This
proves (4.2).

The attracting sphere and repelling sphere here are explicitly linear
spheres in the standard ambient sphere; no classification of arbitrary
sphere embeddings or inference from homology is used.

## 5. A global spectral cross-section

Let \(P_k\) denote orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbb Rv_k\).
For \(a>0\), define the algebraic family
\[
 M_a=\sum_{k=0}^{N}a^kP_k,
 \qquad \Psi_a(x)=\frac{M_ax}{\|M_ax\|}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
It satisfies \(\Psi_a\Psi_b=\Psi_{ab}\), and
\(\Psi_a=\Phi_{-(\log a)/2}\), because the common factor
\(e^{Nt}\) in (3.3) disappears after normalization. Unlike time t,
the parameter a makes (5.1) semialgebraic: it uses integer powers,
algebraic matrices, and a positive square root.

For \(x=y+z\in S^{d-1}\), \(y\in E,z\in F\), with both nonzero,
write \(R(x)=\|z\|/\|y\|\), and put
\[
 \Sigma=\{x\in S^{d-1}:R(x)=1\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
This compact cross-section is naturally \(S(E)\times S(F)\).
For every such x, \(R(\Psi_ax)\) is a strictly increasing function
of a, ranging from zero to infinity. Indeed, logarithmic differentiation
of its squared norm ratio gives the difference between a weighted
average of indices in \(\{r,\ldots,N\}\) and one in
\(\{0,\ldots,r-1\}\). The logarithmic derivative of R is at least
one and at most N. In particular, for \(s\in\Sigma\),
\[
 a^N\le R(\Psi_as)\le a\quad(0<a\le1),\qquad
 a\le R(\Psi_as)\le a^N\quad(a\ge1).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently
\[
 \Gamma:\Sigma\times(0,\infty)\longrightarrow
 S^{d-1}\setminus(S(E)\cup S(F)),\qquad
 \Gamma(s,a)=\Psi_a(s)
\tag{5.4}
\]
is a homeomorphism. To invert it, flow a point until its E and F
components have equal norm. The unique positive parameter depends
continuously on the point by strict monotonicity and the bracketing
inequalities; equivalently use the nonzero logarithmic derivative.
Its inverse is semialgebraic as well: its graph is the graph of the
bijective semialgebraic map (5.4) with factors interchanged.

## 6. The boundary is a continuous semialgebraic hitting graph

**Lemma 6.1.** There is a positive continuous semialgebraic function
\(\beta:\Sigma\to(0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 \Gamma(s,a)\in C_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad0<a\le\beta(s),
\tag{6.1}
\]
and equality \(a=\beta(s)\) is exactly the boundary.

**Proof.** By (4.2), compactness supplies a neighborhood of S(E)
inside the interior of \(C_i\), while \(C_i\) stays a positive
distance from S(F). Along every orbit (5.3) therefore places all
sufficiently small a in the interior and all sufficiently large a
outside. The strict trapping property (3.6), expressed by
\(\Psi_b(C_i)\subset\operatorname{int}(C_i)\) for \(0<b<1\), says
that membership is a lower interval in a, with every point strictly
below a member already interior. Closedness makes this interval
\((0,\beta(s)]\), for a finite positive endpoint. Its endpoint is on
the boundary, and no other point on the orbit can be on the boundary.

For continuity, fix s and choose numbers on either side of \(\beta(s)\).
The lower point is interior and the upper point is in the open complement.
These two facts persist under a sufficiently small change in s. The new
endpoint is bracketed between the chosen numbers. Let them approach
\(\beta(s)\).

Finally, the graph is the inverse image of \(\partial C_i\) under
the semialgebraic map \(\Gamma\). That boundary is semialgebraic by
Section 2, and each fiber contains exactly one point. Hence this is a
semialgebraic function. \(\square\)

Since \(\Sigma\) is compact,
\[
 0<\min_{s\in\Sigma}\beta(s)\le\max_{s\in\Sigma}\beta(s)<\infty.
\tag{6.2}
\]
This uniform bound, rather than just pointwise convergence along flow
lines, is important in extending the resulting homeomorphism across S(E).

## 7. Straightening the hitting graph while fixing the core

Choose a constant
\[
 0<\epsilon<\min\{1,\min_{s\in\Sigma}\beta(s)\}.
\]
For \(0<a\le\beta(s)\), define
\[
 h_s(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 a,&0<a\le\epsilon,\\
 \epsilon+(1-\epsilon)\dfrac{a-\epsilon}{\beta(s)-\epsilon},
       &\epsilon\le a\le\beta(s).
 \end{cases}
\tag{7.1}
\]
This is a continuous semialgebraic family of increasing homeomorphisms
\((0,\beta(s)]\to(0,1]\), fixing \((0,\epsilon]\). Let
\[
 D_E=\{y+z\in S^{d-1}:y\in E,z\in F,\ \|z\|\le\|y\|\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Define \(H:C_i\to D_E\) by
\[
 H(\Gamma(s,a))=\Gamma(s,h_s(a))\quad(x\notin S(E)),
 \qquad H(x)=x\quad(x\in S(E)).
\tag{7.3}
\]
It is a bijection by (6.1) and the fact that \(D_E\) has orbit
parameters \(0<a\le1\). Away from S(E), it and its inverse are
continuous and semialgebraic.

There is no hidden extension problem at S(E). If
\(R(x)<\epsilon^N\), inequalities (5.3) force the parameter of x
to satisfy \(a<\epsilon\): a parameter at least one gives ratio
at least one, and a parameter in \([\epsilon,1]\) gives ratio at
least \(\epsilon^N\). Thus (7.3) is exactly the identity on an entire
neighborhood of S(E). The inverse has the same property. Hence H is
a global semialgebraic homeomorphism.

The identity cutoff in (7.1) is essential to this proof. A bare orbitwise
time translation could change the limiting E-coordinate in a
direction-dependent way and would not, without further work, justify
continuity at the attracting sphere.

Finally,
\[
 S(E)\times\{v\in F:\|v\|\le1\}\longrightarrow D_E,
 \qquad (u,v)\longmapsto\frac{u+v}{\sqrt{1+\|v\|^2}}
\tag{7.4}
\]
is a semialgebraic homeomorphism. Its inverse sends y+z to
\((y/\|y\|,z/\|y\|)\). As \(\dim E=r=i+1\) and
\(\dim F=d-r=d-i-1\), this proves the sphere--ball product
homeomorphism in every required dimension.

## 8. The PL conclusion

The semialgebraic Hauptvermutung states that compact polyhedra which are
semialgebraically homeomorphic are PL-homeomorphic. A precise formulation
is given in Hardt--Lambrechts--Turchin--Volić [4, Theorem 2.6], citing
Shiota--Yokoi [5, Corollary 4.3].

The original cross-polytope realization is a compact polyhedron and its
radial identification with \(C_i\) is semialgebraic. Standard
polyhedral models for \(S(E)\) and the closed ball in F are
semialgebraically homeomorphic to their round models by radial maps.
Take a standard finite triangulation of their product. Combining those
maps with (7.3)--(7.4) gives a semialgebraic homeomorphism between the
compact polyhedron \(|B(i,d)|\) and that standard PL product.
The Hauptvermutung gives (1.1), not merely a homotopy equivalence or
a boundary classification. \(\square\)
