# KOU-21.61: scoped algorithms and limits

## Status and precise scope

The target remains unresolved here. These are authored reconstructions and elementary controls, not a claimed new solution or priority claim. The retained record is 2570; record 20001495 describes the same effective-coherence target and is not a second attempted problem.

Fix a finite rank n and an automorphism phi of the free group F_n. Write G = F_n semidirect_phi Z with t f t^{-1} = phi(f). The requested algorithm takes any finite tuple S of words in this fixed G and returns a finite presentation of H = <S>. No bound on the running time is requested. The ambient group need not be finite, soluble, hyperbolic, or of prime-power order; there is no prime parameter. The cyclic quotient in the source is infinite cyclic. Finite rank refers to the ambient free kernel, not to H intersect F_n, which can have infinite rank. The source asks for a presentation of H; our successful restricted algorithms also supply generator maps, a stronger marked output. We do not replace the fixed-group question by the stronger uniform question over all automorphisms. The empty tuple and rank zero produce the trivial subgroup and subgroups of Z, respectively.

Use normal-form pairs (f,k), with multiplication (f,k)(g,l) = (f phi^k(g),k+l), and let p(f,k)=k. Free reduction and substitution solve the word problem. An inverse automorphism can be found, if not supplied, by enumerating words that map to each free basis element; surjectivity guarantees termination.

## 1. Orbit saturation: an exact certificate for finitely generated height kernel

Let p(H)=dZ. If d=0, H is a subgroup of F_n, and Stallings folding computes a free basis and therefore a presentation. Suppose d>0. Extended integer gcd calculations give a word s in S with p(s)=d. For each h_i in S put q_i=p(h_i)/d and u_i=h_i s^{-q_i}. Then p(u_i)=0 and H=<s,u_1,...,u_r>. Write s=(c,d) and beta(f)=c phi^d(f)c^{-1}.

**Proposition 1.** The height kernel K=H intersect F_n equals <beta^j(u_i) : j in Z, 1<=i<=r>.

**Proof.** The right side is normalized by s, contains all u_i, and lies in F_n. Moving every s letter to the right in an H-word writes it as k s^e with k in that right side. If its height is zero then e d=0, so e=0. The reverse containment is immediate. The same normal form proves H = K semidirect_beta <s>. This includes U empty. QED.

Set K_N=<beta^j(u_i): -N<=j<=N>. Each has a computable finite Stallings graph. Equality of two finitely generated free subgroups is decidable by membership tests on their generating sets.

**Proposition 2.** If K_N=K_{N+1}, then K_N=K. This occurs at some N if and only if K is finitely generated.

**Proof.** beta(K_N) and beta^{-1}(K_N) are contained in K_{N+1}=K_N, so beta(K_N)=K_N. Since U lies in K_N, all its iterates lie there, proving K=K_N. Conversely, if K has a finite generating set, each generator is in some K_N by Proposition 1. One common N then contains that whole generating set, giving K_N=K and hence equality at the next step. QED.

On detecting equality, compute a free basis B of K_N and rewrite beta(B) in B. The finite presentation <B,s | s b s^{-1}=beta(b), b in B> with its evident maps presents H. Its validity follows from the semidirect-product normal form, not just from relation testing.

**Exact nontermination control.** In the fixed direct product G=F(a,b) x <t>, put s=a t and H=<s,b>. Projection to F(a,b) restricts to an isomorphism H -> F(a,b), so H is free of rank two. However K has free basis b_j=a^j b a^{-j}, j in Z. One proof is Reidemeister-Schreier rewriting of the kernel of F(a,b)->Z, a->1, b->0, with transversal a^j. Equivalently its covering graph is a bi-infinite a-line with one b-loop at every vertex. Thus rank(K_N)=2N+1 and no saturation occurs. Failure to halt does not refute effective coherence or even finite presentability.

## 2. Periodic outer monodromy: a complete restricted algorithm

### Direct products

**Proposition 3.** A finite generating tuple in F_n x Z has an effectively computable finite presentation and a constructive membership algorithm.

**Proof.** Write h_i=(f_i,k_i), and use free-group folding to compute a basis B_1,...,B_r of P=<f_i>. Obtain explicit lift words l_j in the h_i whose projections are B_j. They can be found by enumerating words on h_i and checking their projected free words; the required lifts exist. Since P is free, B_j -> l_j extends to a homomorphic section sigma:P->H of projection. Rewrite each f_i as a word W_i(B), and compute h_i sigma(f_i)^{-1}=(1,e_i). Let e be the nonnegative gcd of the e_i, with e=0 when they are all zero. Bezout gives a word representing (1,e) if e>0. The original generators are generated by the l_j and these central residuals; conversely all those elements lie in H. Because sigma is injective and its image intersects the central kernel trivially,

H = sigma(P) x <(1,e)>.

If e=0 the second factor is trivial, giving a free presentation on r generators. If e>0, use generators L_1,...,L_r,Z and relators [L_j,Z]=1. For an ambient (f,k), first test f in P; if not, it is not in H. Otherwise rewrite f in B, compute sigma(f)=(f,z), and test k-z in eZ, interpreting 0Z={0}. Successful tests give an explicit H-word. All steps terminate. The cases r=0 and S empty are included. QED.

### Finite extensions

**Proposition 4.** If the outer automorphism class of phi has finite order, the target algorithm exists for every S in G.

**Proof.** Find m>=1 and c in F_n such that phi^m(f)=c f c^{-1} on the free basis. Under the stated promise, enumeration of pairs (m,c), with finite word checks, terminates. Put G_0=p^{-1}(mZ). The element z=c^{-1}t^m centralizes F_n and has height m. Hence G_0=F_n x <z>; uniqueness follows by height. Explicitly f t^{m l}=f c^l z^l.

The finite homomorphism H->Z/mZ has computable image D. Enumerate its residue classes and corresponding H-word representatives. Schreier generators r h_i overline(r h_i)^{-1} compute H_0=H intersect G_0. By Proposition 3, find a marked presentation <B|R> of H_0 and solve membership in it constructively. Let q=|D|, and choose s in H whose residue generates D. Then H/H_0 is cyclic of order q and H=<H_0,s>. Compute B-words U_b for s b s^{-1} and V for s^q by the constructive membership procedure. Output

<B,s | R, s b s^{-1}=U_b (b in B), s^q=V>.

These relations rewrite every element into h s^j with h represented in <B|R> and 0<=j<q. If such a word maps to the identity in H, its residue forces j=0, and then injectivity of the marked presentation of H_0 makes h trivial. Thus the output maps isomorphically onto H. This proves completeness, including q=1. QED.

This covers n=0, n=1, inner monodromy, and periodic outer monodromy. It does not cover arbitrary exponentially or higher-degree polynomially growing monodromy. Periodic-monodromy results were already present in the duplicate catalog description; no novelty is claimed.

## 3. Finite HNN windows: eventual correctness does not give a stopping test

Retain H=<s,U> and beta from Section 1. For N>=1 let L_N=<beta^j(U): 0<=j<=N> and A_N=L_{N-1}. beta maps A_N isomorphically onto beta(A_N), both subgroups of the free group L_N. Form the finite HNN presentation

P_N = <L_N,s | s a s^{-1}=beta(a) for a in a computed basis of A_N>.

The base and attaching maps are computable by free-group folding and rewriting. The natural map P_N->H is onto, because its image contains s and U. All its defining relations hold in H, but that only proves surjectivity.

**Proposition 5.** These marked presentations eventually become presentations of H. No general computable point of eventual correctness is established here.

**Proof.** Eliminate the finite base generators using expressions in s and U, obtaining a presentation on the fixed tuple s,U. The kernels R_N of F(s,U)->P_N increase: every relation of the Nth presentation is valid in the next. Their union equals the kernel R of F(s,U)->H. To see the nontrivial inclusion, a word in R has height zero; conjugate it by a large power of s so all the indices in its beta-orbit rewriting are nonnegative. Only finitely many indices occur, so the relation holds in some free subgroup L_N and is a relation of P_N. Undoing the conjugation gives membership in R_N.

Feighn-Handel's coherence theorem gives finite presentability of H. Consequently R is normally generated by finitely many words on this fixed finite tuple. Each lies in some R_N, so one common N has R_N=R. This is an existential argument. It gives neither an effective bound nor a positive recognizer for the last proper kernel. QED.

The original Feighn-Handel argument also has a terminating tightening procedure, but the proof of its final presentation starts with a graph pair of globally minimal relative rank. Reading the terminating local procedure as a test for that global minimum omits a necessary step.

**Proposition 6 (arbitrarily long false plateaus in one fixed group).** For every integer M>=1, consider the same ambient group F(a,b) x <t>, set s=a t, b_j=a^j b a^{-j}, and use the generating tuple (s,b_0,b_M). For the one-sided window N>=0 define

I_N={0,...,N} union {M,...,M+N},
J_N={0,...,N-1} union {M,...,M+N-1},

where J_0 is empty. The presentation

Q_N=<s,x_i (i in I_N) | s x_i s^{-1}=x_{i+1}, i in J_N>

is free of rank three when N<M, and free of rank two when N>=M. The natural maps Q_N->Q_{N+1} are marked isomorphisms for every N<M-1, yet none of Q_0,...,Q_{M-1} presents the subgroup H. At N=M the missing relation becomes visible.

**Proof.** The b_i are freely independent for distinct i, by the kernel basis in Section 1, so the displayed presentation is exactly the finite-window HNN construction, with the N=0 free-product convention. When N<M, the integer graph with vertices I_N and edges (i,i+1), i in J_N, has two path components. Eliminate all internal x_i using their path relations. Only s,x_0,x_M remain, with no relator. When N>=M, it has one path component and only s,x_0 remain. In the actual H, x_M=s^M x_0 s^{-M}, so H=<s,b_0> is free of rank two by projection to F(a,b). Before merger, x_M^{-1}s^M x_0 s^{-M} is a nonempty reduced word of length 2M+2 in a free basis of Q_N and lies in the kernel of Q_N->H. After merger this is exactly the eliminated relation. QED.

Taking M arbitrarily large disproves any rule that declares this finite-window construction correct merely after a fixed number of adjacent isomorphism checks or a fixed-length rank plateau. It does not disprove an input-dependent effective bound or effective coherence. In particular, these examples are all solved by Section 2.

Residual-finiteness testing does not repair this inference: checking a finite list of true relations or finite quotients does not establish that every ambient relation lies in the proposed normal closure. For these HNN presentations, their word problem is decidable using free-group edge membership and Britton reduction. Thus a noninjective candidate can be rejected by searching for a word trivial in H but nontrivial in P_N. No corresponding terminating acceptance test is supplied.

## 4. Finite-index and virtual-retraction certificates: an explicit obstruction

A finite-index H can be presented by coset enumeration followed by Reidemeister-Schreier rewriting. Under the finite-index promise, ordinary fair coset enumeration terminates. Without that promise it supplies no universal algorithm. The subgroup <a t,b> above already has infinite index: in each coset of H the central height discrepancy can be any integer, since H is the graph of a homomorphism F(a,b)->Z.

One might try instead to locate a finite-index overgroup that retracts onto H. The following shows that this strategy does not cover all the subgroups in the original question.

**Proposition 7.** In the mapping torus of phi(a)=ab, phi(b)=a, the fibre H=F(a,b) is not a retract of any finite-index subgroup of G containing H.

**Proof.** The substitution is an automorphism: its inverse sends a->b and b->b^{-1}a. Iterates phi^k(a) are positive words and have length F_{k+2}, for F_1=F_2=1; this follows from the two-component Fibonacci length recurrence. In G, the same element is t^k a t^{-k}, of length at most 2k+1 in {a,b,t}. Therefore the fibre is exponentially distorted, in particular not undistorted.

If r:G_1->H were a retraction with [G:G_1]<infinity, choose finite generating sets for G_1 and H. The maximum H-length of the finitely many images under r gives |h|_H <= C |h|_{G_1} for all h in H. Finite-index subgroups are undistorted: Schreier rewriting along a finite coset transversal gives |g|_{G_1} <= C' |g|_G + C'' on G_1. These two inequalities contradict the Fibonacci sequence above. QED.

This is not a difficult-to-present H: its presentation is visibly free. It only falsifies the proposed claim that virtual-retraction certificates suffice for every input subgroup. It makes no claim that this particular mapping torus is hyperbolic.

## 5. Geometric folding and relatively hyperbolic reductions

Dahmani-Touikan, arXiv:2305.11274v2, Section 4, especially Definition 4.2, Theorem 4.4 and Propositions 4.5-4.6, provides a genuine effective-coherence algorithm for globally fibre-preserving piecewise trivial suspensions and applies it to unipotent linearly growing automorphisms. Their proof uses graphs of groups with free-times-cyclic vertex groups and slender abelian edge groups, with effective edge-coset intersection and subgroup-intersection computations. The restricted conclusion is credited to those authors; it is not proved afresh here.

The obvious one-loop graph of groups for an arbitrary mapping torus has vertex group F_n and edge group F_n. For n>=2, this edge group is not slender: the free subgroup with basis a^j b a^{-j}, j in Z, is infinitely generated. Hence that obvious splitting fails a necessary hypothesis of the cited benign-graph theorem. Decidability of free-group membership by itself does not remove that hypothesis. Finding some other suitable computable splitting is a substantive additional problem, not a completed reduction.

Linton, arXiv:2510.03145v1, introduction page 3, explicitly keeps effective coherence for finite-rank mapping tori open and explains a conditional transfer through peripheral mapping tori. Using that transfer as a general solution would assume the remaining finite-rank effective-coherence cases in its peripheral hypotheses. No new mechanism for those hypotheses was found in this attempt.

### A 2026 rewriting-system result and its input boundary

Gray-Linton, arXiv:2607.07496v1, Theorem 1.5, gives existence of finite complete rewriting systems for all finitely generated subgroups of the relevant mapping tori. Theorem 3.3 is algorithmic but its input is an already-given finite presentation of the group to be processed. Its proof starts by enumerating finite-index subgroup presentations and Tietze transformations from that input. Applying it to H from ambient generators alone would presuppose the desired output of KOU-21.61. Theorem 1.6 supplies subgroup-from-generators computation under the additional locally quasi-convex hyperbolic and virtually compact special hypotheses; its proof imports effective coherence in that restricted class. These precise distinctions were checked in the full paper, pages 3-4 and 12-13. We do not infer a general subgroup algorithm from the abstract.

## Conclusion

All five approaches leave the general fixed free-by-cyclic algorithm unresolved. The exact positive output consists of the height-kernel saturation certificate and the periodic-outer-monodromy algorithm. The exact negative controls refute specific overstrong termination and certificate strategies, not KOU-21.61 itself. There is no counterexample to effective coherence, no proof of undecidability, and no claim that the known coherence theorem alone is effective.
