## 1. Finite-generation saturation and the direct-product prerequisite

The height normalization correctly gives a stable letter of positive minimal height and a finite family of height-zero elements. Its two-sided orbit generates exactly the height kernel. Equality of adjacent two-sided orbit subgroups forces invariance under both the automorphism and its inverse. Conversely, a finite basis of the full height kernel lies in a common finite orbit window. Thus saturation halts exactly when that kernel is finitely generated.

This is a semialgorithm on unrestricted subgroup inputs. The fixed direct-product example really has a free subgroup of rank two with infinitely generated height kernel. Consequently finite presentability of the input subgroup cannot be used to promise termination of this saturation procedure.

Proposition 3 does not silently assume an input presentation of the subgroup. All projection bases, lifts and residual central powers are obtained from its ambient generating tuple. The section of the projection is injective because its composition with projection is the identity on a free group. The central residuals generate the whole central kernel because the original input generators are generated by those residuals and the section. The cases of zero central gcd, trivial projected subgroup and empty input are all accounted for.

## 2. Proposition 4: periodic outer monodromy

**Pass.** Enumerating pairs `(m,c)` and checking the finite free basis gives a terminating search under the finite-order outer-class promise. No test for that promise is being claimed. For the fixed-group problem, this can be regarded as finite preprocessing of the ambient group.

The centralizing element `z=c^{-1}t^m` and coordinate identity `f t^{m ell}=f c^ell z^ell` are valid for negative as well as positive `ell`. They require commutation of `z` with the fibre, which the inner-power equation supplies; no unjustified assertion that `t` commutes with `c` is needed.

The residue homomorphism has effectively enumerable finite image. Schreier generators of its kernel are explicit ambient words. The constructive membership procedure from Proposition 3 therefore really supplies the conjugation words and the power word used in the finite-extension presentation. It is not an appeal to the unknown general subgroup-membership problem.

The stated extension presentation is valid even when the extension does not split. Its power relation reduces stable-letter exponents modulo the image order. Relations moving stable letters through kernel generators then give a kernel word followed by one of the finite residue representatives. A word mapping to the identity must have zero residue; faithfulness of the marked kernel presentation then makes that word trivial. The trivial-image case `q=1` merely adds an eliminable generator.

Independent controls used a genuinely non-inner automorphism with outer order two: conjugation by `a` after swapping `a` and `b`. Its square is conjugation by `ab`. All 75 tested coordinate conversions, including negative powers, passed. The finite-image arithmetic passed 4,116 cases, including trivial and proper images. These are bounded sanity checks; the all-input guarantee comes from the proof above.

## 3. Proposition 5: eventual correctness without an effective stopping point

**Pass, conditional only on the cited coherence theorem.** The maps between successive HNN windows fix the original marked tuple. Their presentation kernels on the free group on that tuple are nested. Every ambient relation has zero stable-letter exponent and can be conjugated to involve only finitely many nonnegative orbit indices. A sufficiently large window contains those indices, and the relation then follows from the free base group and the window conjugation relations. This proves equality of the union kernel with the actual subgroup kernel.

Finite presentability gives finite normal generation of the actual kernel on this fixed generating tuple, even when that tuple is redundant. All those finitely many relators lie in one window, yielding eventual equality. The proof does not identify that window or give an acceptance certificate for it.

The Feighn–Handel source's final argument does start with globally minimal relative rank. Its existence is not supplied by running the local tightening procedure until it stops. The payload accurately avoids inferring such a global certificate. Decidable word problems in the finite HNN candidates give a search for a counterexample to injectivity, not an acceptance algorithm.

## 4. Proposition 6: arbitrarily long false plateaus

**Pass, including endpoint and marking checks.** Before `N=M`, the interval graph has two components. At `N=M-1`, its vertex set is contiguous but the edge from `M-1` to `M` is still missing. Thus the rank-three phase includes that endpoint. At `N=M`, the edge appears and the graph becomes one path, leaving a free group of rank two.

For every pre-merger window, eliminating path vertices leaves the same marked free generators `s,x_0,x_M`. The natural maps between those windows are consequently marked isomorphisms, not merely groups with matching ranks. The missing word `x_M^{-1}s^M x_0 s^{-M}` remains freely reduced and nontrivial in that basis while mapping to the identity in the actual subgroup. The actual subgroup is freely generated by `s,b_0`, as projection to the free factor supplies an inverse to the obvious homomorphism from a rank-two free group.

An independent graph-component implementation checked 2,336 `(M,N)` pairs, with `1 <= M <= 64` and `0 <= N <= M+3`, including `M=1`. It checked acyclicity and the missing word's nontriviality before merger and trivial ambient image. All passed.

The conclusion must stay exactly as written: any fixed number of adjacent-isomorphism checks or fixed-length rank plateau can be misleading for these windows. The example gives no obstruction to an input-dependent bound, a different certificate, or effective coherence. Its ambient group is already covered by Proposition 3.

## 5. Proposition 7: virtual-retraction obstruction

**Pass.** The proposed inverse substitution is correct. Positive iterates have Fibonacci lengths, so no free cancellation undermines the distortion estimate. A retraction is Lipschitz on word metrics, and a finite-index inclusion is undistorted, contradicting this fibre distortion.

There is also a short independent algebraic check. Any finite-index overgroup of the fibre is the inverse image of `mZ` for some `m >= 1`, hence contains `t^m`. A retraction fixing the fibre would send `t^m` to some fibre element `c`, forcing `phi^m` to be conjugation by `c`. On abelianization, however, the matrix `[[1,1],[1,0]]` has no positive power equal to the identity: its upper-right entry stays positive. Inner automorphisms act trivially on abelianization, a contradiction. This independently confirms the universal failure of that certificate strategy on the exhibited input, without changing the full-target conclusion.

The inverse identities, 19 Fibonacci iterates and 64 positive matrix powers also passed bounded controls. Neither this proof nor the controls assert hyperbolicity of the mapping torus or difficulty presenting its free fibre.
