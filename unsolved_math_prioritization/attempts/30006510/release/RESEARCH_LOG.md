# Five substantive approaches

All five were performed on 3 October 2026. They are distinct mathematical approaches within this research pass, not a claim of five independent investigators. The full derivations are in `RESULT.md`.

## 1. Retain ordinary Palm theory on the finite-volume sector

Question: does unboundedness itself prevent a center?

Constructed the difference-of-distances geometric-median objective, proved finiteness without moments, strict convexity, coercivity, equivariance, membership in the convex cell and Borel measurability. Then used volume truncation to prove the inverse-volume intensity identity even before finite expected intensity is assumed. Wrote both the median-centered and volume-rooted probability laws and separated them explicitly.

Result: a complete construction for finite-volume cells if reciprocal-volume intensity is finite and positive. For mixed tessellations it counts only the finite-volume subcollection; its mean-volume identity has spatial fraction p_f on the right. This does not solve the infinite-volume case.

Adversarial checks: finite volume versus boundedness; unproved Lorentz-coordinate moments; local finiteness versus finite expectation; intensity independence versus center-dependent embedded shape.

## 2. Try randomized probability roots

Question: can a kernel or finite cloud replace a forbidden point center?

Constructed the expected transport from each infinite-volume cell to its proposed probability kernel, allowing kernels outside their cells, dependence on the entire tessellation and invariant auxiliary randomness. Proved outgoing mass has finite local intensity and that some bounded receiving ball has infinite expected incoming mass. This is a contradiction by the hyperbolic MTP.

Result: no such ambient probability-root extension exists. The proof is a direct kernel version of a known one-point obstruction and is credited as such. It does not exclude every notion of typicality.

Adversarial checks: no unsupported expectation of number of centers; no selection of an individual exceptional cell; countable-union receiving-ball argument; no undefined infinity-times-zero algebra; full unimodular isometry group retained.

## 3. Try large-window cell counts

Question: can counting hit or contained cells avoid centers and recover the usual scalar intensity?

Proved explicit lower and upper bounds from a uniform inball, then used the hyperbolic volume ratio to obtain a strict persistent bias in expected normalized counts. Built stationary examples from cofinite reflection lattices. Ideal triangles yield unbounded cells of finite area, intensity 1/pi and zero contained-cell count in every finite ball. An ideal-triangle/quadrilateral mixture separates zero-cell and count-typical shape probabilities exactly.

Result: these natural window substitutes do not have the required consistency, even on bounded-cell examples. No unsupported almost-sure ball ergodic theorem was used.

Adversarial checks: stationary randomization via G/Gamma, never a uniform isometry of G; area pi versus 2pi; count bias 2/3 versus 1/3 rather than 1/2 each; finite counts may still have infinite expectations.

## 4. Try boundary or corona Palm roots

Question: can hyperbolic volume simply be replaced by a measure at infinity?

Proved nonexistence of a finite invariant measure on the bare ideal boundary using north-south dynamics of two loxodromic maps with disjoint fixed sets. Read the actual ideal Voronoi construction and distinguished its marked corona, invariant sigma-finite intensity, noncompact isotropy and insertion Palm disintegration. Checked recent work on IPVT lower faces and horoball projections.

Result: the naive boundary replacement fails; the known model-specific corona construction remains a valid precedent. No general canonical corona for arbitrary infinite-volume cells was obtained.

Adversarial checks: do not confuse a finite boundary probability with a sigma-finite marked-corona intensity; do not declare the special IPVT result a new solution; do not promote a projected Euclidean tessellation theorem to full hyperbolic invariance.

## 5. Keep a measure-valued cell intensity

Question: what intrinsic object survives without any center?

Defined the mean cell-counting measure on embedded closed convex cells. Proved sigma-finiteness through a deterministic lower-volume-in-a-ball cover, avoiding the unavailable local count first moment. Proved the incidence identity and an exact reconstruction from zero-cell laws using an arbitrary positive integrable weight.

Result: a universal invariant sigma-finite cell measure, but no canonical scalar normalization and no general count-typical probability law. Restriction to cells through a fixed location gives the existing zero-cell law and fails the finite-volume consistency test from approach 3.

Adversarial checks: no claim that the mean measure is Radon for the Fell topology; sigma-finite cover is explicit; weight normalization is positive and finite even on infinite-volume cells; reconstruction is a Tonelli identity, not a finite-intensity assertion.

## Stopping decision

Five distinct approaches produced rigorous scoped results and precise failures, but none supplies a general natural typical-cell/intensity pair for infinite-volume cells. Recommend `unsolved`, 5/5. Freeze the author packet for a fresh independent audit. Do not publish an original-problem solution claim.
