# Additive sets preserving piecewise syndeticity: five-approach audit

Target: ordered row 1881, problem 30002208, Griesmer Question 5.4. This note addresses the piecewise-syndetic alternative only. It neither identifies it with the piecewise-Bohr alternative nor claims a structural characterization. No queue or publication changes are included.

## Definitions and outcome

Write P(A) for: A+B is piecewise syndetic for every B⊂Z with positive upper Banach density. A set S is piecewise syndetic (PS) when F+S is thick for some finite F; thick means containing intervals of every finite length. A set H is syndetic when its gaps have a uniform finite bound.

**Outcome: NOT SOLVED.** The five approaches below yield auxiliary reductions and an exact obstruction test, but no converse to the published sufficient condition, no novel classification, and no acceptance claim. The dataset's statement that A is undefined is erroneous. The primary source specifies A⊂Z and the universal condition.

## Approach 1: spectral functional separation

Let D be the real linear space of bounded real functions generated by real and imaginary parts of unitary matrix coefficients on Z, including constants. Every invariant mean takes the same value Λ(f) on f∈D, by the mean ergodic theorem. FS-means are precisely positive unital extensions of Λ from D to all bounded real functions.

The positive-extension form of Hahn–Banach gives the exact dual formula

    sup{η(A): η is an FS-mean}
      = inf{Λ(f): f∈D and f(n)≥1_A(n) for every n∈Z}.

Proof detail: for a bounded real g, a positive extension to D+Rg may assign g any value between sup{Λ(f): f∈D,f≤g} and inf{Λ(f): f∈D,f≥g}. The interval is nonempty by positivity; its endpoints are finite because D contains constants. Extend the resulting positive unital functional to all bounded functions, preserving positivity. Apply this to g=1_A. Conversely every extension is bounded above by each majorant. If g already lies in D, both bounds equal Λ(g).

Thus failure of the published sufficient condition is certified by arbitrarily small-Λ spectral majorants of 1_A. If P(A) fails, the majorants necessarily exist, by the contrapositive of the published theorem. The missing step is the reverse implication: spectral majorants with arbitrarily small Λ do not presently yield a dense B with A+B non-PS. This reformulates the known sufficient class; it does not prove that the class exhausts P.

## Approach 2: compact-group obstruction and finite-quotient limits

The original Proposition 4.2 already excludes A whose closure in a compact monothetic group under the integer orbit has Haar measure zero. It yields witnesses B of density arbitrarily close to one. This includes the original paper's listed polynomial and arithmetic examples; these must not be reclaimed as new results.

A finite quotient by itself cannot refute P(A) for any nonempty A: every nonempty subset of a finite cyclic group pulls back to a periodic syndetic set, and a nonempty sum of periodic sets remains periodic syndetic. The useful obstruction requires compatible refinements with vanishing Haar mass, not a failure of nonempty sumsets in one finite quotient. For a profinite completion represented by a divisibility chain q_j, the Haar mass of the closure of A is the decreasing limit of |A mod q_j|/q_j. Zero limit invokes the known compact-group obstruction. Positive limit does not prove P(A).

Replacing Haar-positive closure by the FS condition is unjustified: spectral control of continuous-spectrum matrix coefficients is additional input. In particular, identifying Hartman-uniform means with FS-means is expressly outside the established boundary (2026 Question 10.5). Nearby measurable-recurrence results do not settle this target.

## Approach 3: algebraic stability and its limits

These are elementary reductions, not a new classification.

1. If A⊂A' and P(A), then P(A'). Translation and reflection preserve P.
2. For any nonempty finite F, P(F+A) iff P(A). Indeed, for every S, F+S is PS iff S is PS: one implication is monotonicity after choosing one translate, and the other composes the finite thickening witnesses.
3. For any nonzero integer m, P(mA) iff P(A). For the forward implication test mB and use that S is PS iff mS is PS. For the reverse implication, partition B into its |m| residue classes. One has positive upper Banach density, and its rescaling C has positive upper Banach density. The set m(A+C)+r is PS and contained in mA+B. Negative m follows by reflection.
4. Finite modifications preserve P. For nonempty A, if B is PS then A+B is PS. If B is not PS, F+B is not PS for finite F. Partition regularity of PS then shows that adjoining finitely many points to A cannot repair a failing witness; deleting finitely many points is the same argument in reverse. The empty-set edge is handled by noting that no finite A has P: there exist positive-density non-PS B, and finite thickening does not make B PS. Such B are already supplied by the compact-group construction in the original paper with A a singleton.

One must not infer that a finite union of arbitrary preservers/nonpreservers has an analogous exact law. From “for every B at least one A_i+B is PS” it does not follow formally that a fixed i works for every B. That is an invalid interchange of quantifiers.

## Approach 4: normalize every failure to arbitrarily high density

**Lemma.** For every B with d*(B)>0 and ε>0 there is a finite F⊂Z with d*(F+B)>1−ε.

Proof: Let X_B be the shift-orbit closure of the indicator of B. Averages along intervals realizing positive upper Banach density yield an invariant probability assigning positive mass to the cylinder E={x:x(0)=1}. An ergodic component μ still has μ(E)>0. Ergodicity implies μ(⋃_{n∈Z}T^nE)=1, so finitely many translates cover μ-mass >1−ε. Their union is a clopen cylinder C. Its μ-mass is at most the upper Banach density of the corresponding finite union F+B: every finite cylinder-average in any point of X_B occurs in a translate of the original indicator, and integration bounds μ(C) by the supremum of these averages. Taking interval lengths to infinity gives the inequality. Signs can be absorbed into F.

**Corollary.** P(A) fails iff, for every ε>0, some B_ε has d*(B_ε)>1−ε and A+B_ε is not PS. Starting with any failing B, use the lemma; A+(F+B)=F+(A+B) remains non-PS by Approach 3. The reverse implication is immediate.

Equivalently, testing all B with d*(B)>c for any one fixed 0<c<1 already tests P(A). This is a useful threshold reduction, but does not eliminate the universal quantifier or distinguish A structurally.

Failed strengthening: high upper Banach density does not make independently constructed witnesses intersect with positive density. Disjoint thick sets can both have upper Banach density one (use alternating blocks of unbounded length). Therefore intersecting separately amplified witnesses is invalid. Nor does the lemma produce one density-one failing witness: the finite set F depends on ε, and an infinite union can destroy the non-PS conclusion.

## Approach 5: syndetic holes and a finite-state variational criterion

**Exact dual obstruction.** P(A) fails iff there exist syndetic sets H_k⊂Z, k≥1, for which

    D = Z \ ⋃_{k≥1}(H_k − A + [−k,k])

has positive upper Banach density.

Proof: If S=A+B is not PS, then S+[−k,k] is not thick for every k. Its complement H_k is syndetic. The exclusions imply B⊂D. Conversely, B=D avoids all H_k−A+[−k,k], so H_k is disjoint from A+B+[−k,k]. A set disjoint from a syndetic set cannot be thick. Every finite thickening is contained in a translate of some interval thickening, so A+B is not PS. The interval's symmetry handles the sign in the displayed expression.

Here is a finite-state optimization form of this obstruction, avoiding a density test on an unknown B, but not avoiding all complexity.

Fix a sequence of positive integers L=(L_k)_{k≥1}. Consider a shift space X(A,L) whose coordinates are b(t)∈{0,1} and h_k(t)∈{0,1}, t∈Z, k≥1. Require:

- every length-L_k window of h_k contains a 1;
- b(t) and h_k(s) cannot both be 1 when t=s−a+j, a∈A, |j|≤k.

These are closed shift-invariant constraints in a compact metrizable product space. Let α(A,L) be the maximum of μ(b(0)=1) over shift-invariant probability measures on X(A,L). Such measures exist: the configuration b≡0,h_k≡1 is allowed. Compactness makes the maximum attained.

Then

    P(A) holds iff α(A,L)=0 for every sequence L of positive integers.

Proof: A failing B and its syndetic holes give a point in X(A,L) for suitable gap bounds. Orbit averages over density-realizing intervals give an invariant measure with positive b(0)-mass. In the reverse direction, positive α gives an ergodic invariant measure with positive b(0)-mass; a generic point for this coordinate yields B of positive density and syndetic holes. The preceding dual obstruction applies.

There is also an explicit decreasing finite-state approximation. Let X_n enforce only k≤n and a∈A∩[−n,n]; all other hole tracks are unrestricted. On the finitely many constrained tracks this is a one-dimensional shift of finite type. If α_n is its maximum invariant b-density, then

    α(A,L)=inf_n α_n.

Indeed the full invariant-measure sets form a nested sequence of nonempty compact sets, and a convergent subnet of maximizing measures proves equality. Given the finite membership set A∩[−n,n] (or access to a membership oracle for A), each α_n is the maximum cycle mean in the finite directed block graph of allowed words, with the b-coordinate as vertex/edge weight. Any invariant measure yields a stationary edge flow, decomposable into cycles; a maximizing cycle supplies a periodic maximizing configuration. Empty/unrealized vertices can be pruned or simply ignored because they lie on no cycle.

**Important limitation.** This is an exact obstruction/variational reformulation, not a resolution of Question 5.4's structural aim. It quantifies over arbitrary sequences L, depends on all membership information in A, and supplies neither a convergence rate nor a finite terminating decision procedure. It does not prove an FS-mean converse or a Bohr-closure characterization. Labeling this as the missing characterization would overstate the result.

## Elementary baseline example (independent reviewer)

For a completely explicit positive-density non-PS set, put p_k=2^(k+4),

    U = ⋃_{k≥1}(p_k/2 + p_k Z + [−k,k]),     B=Z\U.

For each k, B+[−k,k] misses p_k/2+p_kZ, a syndetic arithmetic progression. Hence B is not PS. A hole family can meet [−N,N] only when 2^(k+3)−k≤N, so only O(log N) families contribute. Counting each periodic family gives

    |U∩[−N,N]|/(2N+1) ≤ Σ_{k≥1}(2k+1)/2^(k+4) + O((log N)^2/N)
                         = 5/16 + o(1).

Therefore B has lower symmetric density at least 11/16, in particular positive upper Banach density. Every finite A+B is non-PS. This explicit example is a baseline illustration, not claimed as a new counterexample to an open conjecture.
