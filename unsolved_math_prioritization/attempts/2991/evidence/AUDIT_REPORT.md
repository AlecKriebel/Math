## 2. Approach 2: circle surgery and its limitation

### Construction checked

A generator of `pi1(X)=C8` is represented by an embedded smooth circle by general position in dimension four. Its oriented rank-three normal bundle over a circle is trivial: its structure group `SO(3)` is connected, so there is no obstruction over the one-dimensional base. The construction chooses a framing; it does not claim the framing is unique.

Let `E_gamma` be the compact exterior. Inclusion in `X` induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups. A one-dimensional loop can be perturbed off the embedded circle because `1+1<4`. A null-homotopy can be perturbed off it, relative to its boundary, because `2+1<4`. The deleted-circle complement retracts to the compact exterior by radial motion in punctured normal fibers. These facts prove both surjectivity and injectivity, rather than only the former.

The common boundary is `S1 x S2`, with fundamental group generated by its longitude. Under inclusion in `E_gamma` and then in `X`, that longitude represents the original generator `gamma`. The inserted `D2 x S2` is simply connected. Van Kampen therefore gives the quotient by the normal closure of `gamma`, which is trivial. The surgery changes Euler characteristic by `2-0=2`; the boundary has Euler characteristic zero. Since the spin has Euler characteristic two, the surgery result is closed, smooth, oriented, simply connected and has `chi=4` and `b2=2`.

No identification with a standard smooth `b2=2` manifold follows from these particular calculations alone. The report correctly makes no such identification.

### Functoriality limitation checked

If the framed embedding is `e:S1 x D3 -> X`, the transported framed embedding is exactly `F composed with e`. Thus the exterior map induced by `F` is the identity in the chosen boundary coordinates and extends by the corresponding product-coordinate map on `D2 x S2`. It gives an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism from surgery on `gamma` to surgery on `F(gamma)`. The latter loop is also a generator, since multiplication by three is an automorphism of `C8`.

When the second trisection construction is the transported image of the first, this ambient diffeomorphism identifies the outputs by construction. In addition, every old sector Nielsen tuple has trivial ambient-group image after simple connectivity is imposed. Hence the old invariant cannot certify non-diffeomorphism. This is a limitation of the transported construction and the particular invariant, not a theorem excluding every possible non-equivariant surgery approach. The report preserves that quantifier.

## 4. Approach 4: efficient homological triples

The integral proof is valid. For a unimodular symplectic lattice with complementary Lagrangians `A,B`, the cross-pairing is a unimodular integer matrix, so dual integral bases exist. Complementarity of `C` to `A` identifies `C` with `B` integrally, giving `C={(Sx,x)}`. Isotropy forces `S=S^t`; complementarity to `B` forces `det(S)=+1` or `-1`. A symplectic automorphism preserving the two ordered coordinate summands has form `diag(P,P^{-t})`, sending `S` to `P S P^t`, and every such congruence is realized. This is classification of the ordered homological triple, not of diagrams.

For an efficient `(g,0)` trisection, each pair gives a Heegaard splitting of `S3`. The quotient of `H1(Sigma;Z)` by the sum of the two kernel lattices is therefore zero; the rank calculation makes their sum a direct sum over the integers. The hypotheses are genuinely integral, not only rational.

Feller–Klug–Schirmer–Zemke, Definition 3.5 and Theorem 3.6, give the intersection pairing. In the stated graph coordinates it evaluates as `omega((Sx,0),(Sy,y))=x^t S y`; another consistent global convention can negate every matrix. A separate sign choice for each fixed-oriented-manifold trisection is not allowed. Thus the same oriented ambient form determines the same symplectic isomorphism class of these efficient ordered triples. Theorem 4.4 covers this in a wider setting, as the report credits.

This neither identifies geometric diagrams nor certifies Torelli regluings. An unchanged homological form cannot certify pairwise `S3` boundaries or the same smooth ambient manifold. There is no asserted classification of the `(5,1)` triples. The source review confirms these scope limits.

## 5. Approach 5: exterior homology

The exterior theorem is correct integrally for a connected closed oriented simply connected smooth `X`.

The central surface is the boundary of an embedded pairwise three-dimensional handlebody, so `[Sigma]=0` in `H2(X;Z)`. The collar gives a nonzero normal section; equivalently the oriented normal Euler number is the self-intersection of the zero class. Thus the rank-two normal bundle is trivial. Connectivity of the exterior follows by general position (and can also be read at the low-degree end of the relative sequence).

For `E=X minus int(nu Sigma)`, excision and the oriented Thom isomorphism give `Hj(X,E)=H(j-2)(Sigma)`. The relevant map `H2(X)->H2(X,E)=Z` is intersection with `[Sigma]`, hence zero. Since `H1(X)=0`, the connecting homomorphism identifies the relative fiber-disk generator with an oriented meridian and gives `H1(E)=Z`.

Poincare duality gives `H3(X)=0`. The next portion of the same sequence is consequently

    0 -> H1(Sigma) -> H2(E) -> H2(X) -> 0.

The last term is free: integral duality and the universal coefficient theorem give this for closed simply connected oriented four-manifolds. Therefore the sequence splits as abelian groups, without a canonical choice of splitting. This proves `H2(E)=H2(X) direct_sum Z^(2g)`.

The map `H4(X)=Z -> H4(X,E)=H2(Sigma)=Z` sends the oriented ambient fundamental class to the oriented surface fundamental class by local Thom orientation and is an isomorphism. Exactness gives `H3(E)=0`. Finally, a connected compact manifold with nonempty boundary has zero absolute top homology, so `H4(E)=0`.

Thus the listed ranks `(1,1,b2+2g,0,0)` and the examples `12` and `66` are correct. As a consistency check, their alternating sum equals `chi(X)-chi(Sigma)`. The sequence does not compute `pi1(E)`, its peripheral structure, or even show that `pi1(E)` is cyclic. The report does not make any of those stronger claims. At fixed `X,g`, ordinary integral exterior homology cannot distinguish these central surfaces.
