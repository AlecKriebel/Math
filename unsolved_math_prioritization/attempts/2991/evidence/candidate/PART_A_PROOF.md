# A candidate answer to Kirby Problem 4.115(a)

## Claim and scope

Let M be the oriented quotient of the unit sphere S^3 in C^2 by the free cyclic action generated by

    d(z_1,z_2) = (zeta z_1, zeta^3 z_2),  zeta = exp(2 pi i / 8).

Thus M is the lens space L(8,3), in the explicitly specified quotient convention. Let X be its untwisted spin. There are two orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic balanced (3,1)-trisections of X that are not ambiently isotopic. Non-isotopy holds even if an isotopy is allowed to permute the three sectors. Both have minimal trisection genus.

This answers only part (a), if the proof below survives independent review. X has fundamental group C_8, so it does not answer the simply connected, non-diffeomorphic requirement in part (b). No novelty claim is made. The sole specialized existence input is Meier's spun-trisection theorem, stated precisely below; the distinguishing argument is proved directly in first homology.

## 1. The self-diffeomorphism of the lens space

The C_8 action is free: if d^j fixes a point with nonzero first coordinate then 8 divides j; if only the second coordinate is nonzero then 8 divides 3j, hence j. The quotient is therefore a smooth closed oriented 3-manifold, and S^3 is its universal cover. Identify its fundamental group and first integral homology with C_8 = Z/8.

The complex-coordinate swap

    s(z_1,z_2) = (z_2,z_1)

is orientation preserving on S^3. Indeed, its real determinant on R^4 is +1 and it preserves the outward normal. The relation

    s d = d^3 s

follows from 3^2 = 1 modulo 8. Thus s descends to an orientation-preserving isometry s_bar of M. Its induced map on C_8 is multiplication by 3. One way to check the last claim is to lift a loop to a path from x to d x: its image lifts from s x to s d x = d^3 s x. Since C_8 is abelian, changing basepoints does not change this automorphism.

We need a representative fixing a ball, rather than just preserving one. Here this can be done explicitly. The point p represented by (1/sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2)) is fixed by s_bar. On T_p M its derivative is identity on a real line and minus identity on an orthogonal real 2-plane. Choose a small normal-coordinate ball at p, on which the quotient exponential chart is injective. Isometries commute with exponential maps at fixed points, so s_bar is exactly the linear rotation R_pi in these coordinates, where R_theta rotates the 2-plane and fixes the line.

Choose a smooth radial cutoff chi, equal to 1 on a smaller ball and 0 near the outer boundary, and define a diffeomorphism b in this chart by

    b(exp_p(v)) = exp_p(R_{-pi chi(|v|)} v).

Extend b by the identity outside the chart. This is smooth because chi is constant near zero and near the outer boundary. It is a diffeomorphism because it preserves |v| and its inverse rotates by the opposite angle at that same radius. Replacing the angle by -t pi chi(|v|) gives an isotopy from the identity to b. Consequently

    f = b composed with s_bar

is orientation preserving, induces multiplication by 3 on H_1(M;Z), and is the identity on a neighborhood of a small closed ball B about p.

## 2. Spinning preserves the required automorphism

Put M^circ = M minus int(B), and form

    X = (M^circ x S^1) union_id (S^2 x D^2).

The gluing identifies the common boundary S^2 x S^1 in the standard product way. Corners are smoothed. Because f is the identity near B, the maps f|M^circ x id_{S^1} and id_{S^2 x D^2} agree on a collar of the gluing locus. They give a smooth orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphism F of X.

Removing an open ball from a 3-manifold does not alter its fundamental group, by van Kampen. A second application of van Kampen to the displayed decomposition of X gives

    pi_1(X) = (pi_1(M^circ) x Z) / normal_closure(Z) = C_8.

The Z factor is killed because it bounds a disk in S^2 x D^2. This identification is induced by a fixed slice M^circ x {theta} and is natural with respect to F. Hence F_* on H_1(X;Z) is multiplication by 3.

## 3. Existence of a balanced (3,1)-trisection

The two subsets |z_1| <= |z_2| and |z_2| <= |z_1| give the usual genus-one Heegaard splitting of this lens-space quotient. For completeness, on the first solid torus use w = z_1/z_2 and u = z_2/|z_2|. The deck action is (w,u) -> (zeta^{-2}w,zeta^3 u); a fundamental domain for the free longitudinal rotation presents its quotient as the mapping torus of a disk rotation, hence as a solid torus. On the other solid torus the same argument uses u = z_1/|z_1|, with longitudinal rotation zeta. Their common boundary is a torus.

Meier's Theorem 1.2 in *Trisections and spun 4-manifolds* says: if a closed connected orientable 3-manifold has a genus-k Heegaard splitting, its spin has a balanced (3k,k)-trisection. Apply it with k=1. Fix any resulting trisection

    T = (X_1,X_2,X_3),  X_i diffeomorphic to S^1 x B^3.

Its image F(T) is another balanced (3,1)-trisection, diffeomorphic to T by construction. No naturality of Meier's construction under f is needed: F(T) is defined by taking the actual image of the chosen trisection.

Source: Jeffrey Meier, Theorem 1.2, https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01214 ; published in Mathematical Research Letters 25 (2018), 1497-1524, https://doi.org/10.4310/MRL.2018.v25.n5.a7 .

## 4. Sector homology generates ambient homology

For every trisection of a closed 4-manifold, the inclusion of each sector induces a surjection on fundamental groups, and therefore on first homology. Here is a direct justification. A pairwise intersection H_ij is one handlebody in a Heegaard splitting of the boundary of X_j. Its inclusion into that boundary is surjective on fundamental groups, since the complementary handlebody adds only 2- and 3-handles relative to H_ij. The boundary inclusion into a 4-dimensional 1-handlebody is an isomorphism on fundamental groups (use the standard boundary connected-sum model). Thus pi_1(H_ij) -> pi_1(X_j) is surjective. Van Kampen then says pi_1(X_i) -> pi_1(X_i union X_j) is surjective. Adding the third sector creates no new generators: the intersection of the previous union with X_k is its entire boundary, which already surjects onto pi_1(X_k). Collars give open covers to which van Kampen applies. This proves the assertion for every i.

In our case let u_i be either generator of H_1(X_i;Z)=Z, and let r_i be its image in H_1(X;Z)=Z/8. Each r_i is a unit modulo 8. Reversing u_i changes r_i to -r_i, so the unordered pair [r_i] = {r_i,-r_i} is a well-defined invariant of the embedded sector. An ambient isotopy acts as the identity on H_1(X;Z). Its restriction to a sector induces an isomorphism of its infinite cyclic first homology, thus changes its chosen generator by at most sign. Therefore [r_i] is invariant under an ambient isotopy carrying that sector to another sector.

The only unit classes modulo sign in Z/8 are

    A = {1,7},  B = {3,5}.

Multiplication by 3 exchanges A and B. In particular, the sector F(X_i) has the opposite class from X_i.

## 5. Non-isotopy, including arbitrary sector permutations

Suppose an ambient isotopy h_t with h_0=id has h_1(X_i)=F(X_{sigma(i)}) for some permutation sigma of {1,2,3}. By the preceding invariant,

    [r_i] = [3 r_{sigma(i)}]  for all i.

Let a be the number of sectors whose class is A. T has class multiset with a copies of A and 3-a copies of B; F(T) has 3-a copies of A and a copies of B. A permutation preserves multiplicities, so the displayed equality forces a=3-a, impossible for integer a. Hence no such isotopy exists. For labeled sectors the simpler individual contradiction r_i = +/- 3 r_i suffices.

This establishes diffeomorphic but non-isotopic trisections in the stronger, sector-unordered sense, and therefore also in the sector-preserving sense used in Problem 4.115.

## 6. Minimal genus and exact stabilization limits

Euler characteristic of X is 2, by its product-and-gluing description. For a possibly unbalanced (g;k_1,k_2,k_3)-trisection, inclusion-exclusion gives

    chi(X)=2+g-k_1-k_2-k_3.

Since pi_1(X) is nontrivial and each sector surjects onto it, every k_i is at least 1. Consequently g=k_1+k_2+k_3 >= 3, and our genus-three trisections are minimal.

This is an unstabilized distinction. Gay-Kirby's uniqueness theorem allows enough stabilizations to make trisections of the same fixed manifold isotopic. There is no claim that the displayed pair stays distinct after balanced stabilization. Indeed, the cyclic rank-one homology obstruction need not survive an increase in the number of sector generators. Nor does the construction give non-diffeomorphic trisections: F explicitly identifies the two.

## Source normalization and status

The 2026 K3 problem list, Problem 4.115, page 286, asks separately for a diffeomorphic/non-isotopic pair on one 4-manifold and a non-diffeomorphic balanced equal-genus pair on a closed simply connected 4-manifold. The simply connected qualifier occurs in the second question. The list's remarks use orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms and sectors fixed setwise. The present proof satisfies the first question without transferring the simply connected condition from the second.

The first-homology argument is a rank-one special case of the Nielsen-class invariant developed by Gabriel Islambouli, *Nielsen equivalence and trisections*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06978 and https://doi.org/10.1007/s10711-021-00617-y . We credit that framework; the proof above does not assume a classification of Nielsen classes beyond Aut(Z)={+1,-1}. A literature search is not evidence of novelty.
