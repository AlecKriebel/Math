# Mathematical scope and attribution

## Target, restated

Let n be a positive integer and m an integer greater than 2n. Let P and Q be real homogeneous polynomials in two variables of exact degrees 2n and m. For H=P+Q, assume that the planar Hamiltonian field X=(-H_y,H_x) has an isolated center at the origin. On the maximal connected family of periodic orbits surrounding this center, let T(h) denote minimal period, with regular energy h as parameter. The question is whether there can be more than one interior energy with T'(h)=0.

This concerns critical points of a period function, not isolated periodic trajectories (limit cycles). The center and the outer boundary are not interior energy levels. It does not assume that P is positive definite, that the center is nondegenerate, that the annulus is global, or that m is even. Exact nonzero degrees are retained; the unrelated purely quadratic linear oscillator is not inserted as a zero-Q case.

Gasull's original Problem 12 is on page 13 of [arXiv:2012.02524v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02524). The adjacent discussion credits an affirmative result for m >= 4n-2, leaving 2n < m < 4n-2, with (n,m)=(2,5) the first residual pair. This is historical context, not the current status conclusion.

## Published attribution

Ziwei Zhuang and Changjian Liu, *Critical Periods of the Sum of Two Quasi-Homogeneous Hamiltonian Vector Fields*, Qualitative Theory of Dynamical Systems 22, 90 (2023), DOI [10.1007/s12346-023-00786-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12346-023-00786-z), published 24 April 2023.

The publisher's public abstract states a one-simple-critical-period bound for the two-quasi-homogeneous Hamiltonian family with common weights and differing quasi-degrees. Crucially, it also explicitly identifies Gasull's twelfth problem as answered positively. That direct identification is the basis of the already_solved literature classification. A bound on simple zeros alone would not logically exclude arbitrarily many nonsimple zeros, so this packet does not replace that explicit problem-resolution attribution with an unsupported interpretation of the word “simple.”

## Exact family map

For any homogeneous polynomial F of ordinary degree d,

    F(lambda*x, lambda*y) = lambda^d F(x,y).

Thus ordinary homogeneity is (p,q)=(1,1) quasi-homogeneity. P and Q have identical weights (1,1), distinct quasi-degrees 2n and m, and their Hamiltonian vector fields have quasi-degrees 2n-1 and m-1. The center and its period annulus are the same objects: this specialization performs no coordinate or time change. It does not introduce a constraint on n or parity of m.

This identity verifies the advertised family specialization. It does not verify any extra hypotheses that might appear in the unread full theorem.
