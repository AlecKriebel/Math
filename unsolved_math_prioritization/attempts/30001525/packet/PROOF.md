# Integral skyline bases: partial results and an explicit obstruction

## Scope

The proved scope is all degrees for S_n with n<=7, plus an explicit order-8 third-Bockstein family on S8. The complete S8 basis and the unrestricted conjecture remain unresolved.

This note does not prove or disprove the Bockstein splitting conjecture for all symmetric groups. All coefficients denoted R are Z localized at 2. In positive degrees an additive basis means generators of a direct sum of finite cyclic R-modules, with their orders specified; it cannot mean a free R-basis. A higher integral Bockstein requires a surviving mod-2 class and a compatible lift, and is not an iteration of Sq^1.

## 1. Every degree for S2 and S3

**Proposition.** For n=2 or 3, H^0(BS_n;R)=R, H^{2k}(BS_n;R)=R/2 for k>=1, and H^{2k+1}(BS_n;R)=0 for k>=0. If t is the degree-one mod-2 class, the positive-degree cyclic generator is beta(t^{2k-1}), where beta is the connecting map for 0 -> R --2--> R -> F2 -> 0. Since H^j(BS_n;F2) is one-dimensional for every j>=0, its unique nonzero class is the skyline basis class. Thus the source conjecture holds for n<=3, including the trivial groups n=0,1.

**Proof.** For C2=S2, its periodic free group-ring resolution alternates multiplication by g-1 and 1+g. Applying Hom(-,R) with trivial action gives one copy of R in every nonnegative degree; the cochain differential is 0 in even degree and 2 in odd degree. This proves the asserted integral groups. Modulo 2 the differential vanishes and the familiar cup-product generator t gives F2[t]. Lifting the odd-degree cocycle to the integral complex and dividing its differential by 2 gives 1 in the next degree. It therefore generates R/2. For S3, the exact sequence 1 -> C3 -> S3 -> C2 -> 1 has kernel of invertible order in R and F2. Averaging contracts the positive-degree cochains of the kernel. The associated Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence has only its q=0 row, so inflation from C2 is a ring isomorphism for both coefficient rings. It commutes with the coefficient connecting map. This transfers the calculation and the basis assertion to S3. The degree-zero unit handles S0 and S1. QED.

This is an elementary special case, not a claim of novelty and not a uniform solution.

## 2. Why unrestricted Smith reduction is insufficient

The following example is not a symmetric-group counterexample. It isolates the missing preferred-basis assertion.

Take a free R-cochain complex concentrated in degrees 3,4,5. Its preferred bases are (u,v), (a,x,y), and (z). Define

    du = 2a + 4x - 4y,   dv = 2a,
    da = 0,             dx = dy = 2z.

Then d squared is zero. All differentials are even, so every preferred basis vector defines a mod-2 cohomology basis vector. Put k=x-y. Directly, the kernel in degree 4 is Ra + Rk, and its boundaries are generated by 2a and 4k. Thus H^4 = (R/2){a} direct-sum (R/4){k}. The only preferred degree-4 cochain that is an integral cycle is a. The primary integral Bocksteins of the two preferred degree-3 classes are a+2k and a. Their span with a is the proper subgroup generated by a and 2k, of index 2.

Neither u nor v survives the first Bockstein: d_1(u)=d_1(v)=a, which is nonzero in mod-2 degree 4. Hence neither has a higher Bockstein defined as a surviving class. Nevertheless u-v survives and has d(u-v)=4k, so its secondary integral Bockstein is k. This source is a sum of two preferred classes, not a preferred class.

Consequently a general theorem about splitting finite free complexes over a discrete valuation ring does not guarantee splitting relative to a specified mod-2 basis. Extra structure of skyline representatives is essential. This observation is elementary and no novelty is claimed.

## 3. Every degree for S4

Write x=gamma_(1,1) transfer 1_2, y=gamma_(1,2), and z=gamma_(2,1), of degrees 1,2,3. The mod-2 Hopf-ring presentation [GSS, Section 6, pp. 17–18 of the preprint] gives

    A = H*(BS4;F2) = F2[x,y,z]/(xz).

These are also the first three Stiefel–Whitney classes of the four-dimensional permutation representation. The identification can be checked on a transposition pair and on the transitive Klein four subgroup; these restrictions detect the mod-2 ring. In particular, the Wu formula gives

    d1(x)=x^2,    d1(y)=xy+z,    d1(z)=0,

where d1=Sq^1 is the first differential of the coefficient Bockstein spectral sequence. All its differentials increase cohomological degree by one. These identities also follow directly from the Steenrod rule in [GSS, Section 8].

### 3.1 The first page calculation

Set v=y^2. We claim, as a graded algebra,

    E2 = H(A,d1) = F2[v] tensor Lambda(z),  |v|=4, |z|=3.

Here z denotes its homology class, and z^2 is zero on E2, since d1(yz)=z^2.

A full vector-space verification follows. Every monomial in A is either x^a y^b with a>=1, or y^b z^c with b,c>=0. On the former,

    d1(x^a y^b) = (a+b mod 2) x^(a+1) y^b.

For b even this pairs every odd a with the following even a, leaving nothing. For b odd the even a>=2 pair with the following odd a, leaving only xy^b. On the part with no x,

    d1(y^b) = (b mod 2)(xy^b + y^(b-1)z),
    d1(y^b z^c) = (b mod 2)y^(b-1)z^(c+1),  c>=1.

Thus d1(y^(2k+1)) identifies the remaining xy^(2k+1) with y^(2k)z. For c>=2 every even-y-power monomial y^(2k)z^c is the boundary of y^(2k+1)z^(c-1); odd-y-power monomials with c>=1 are not cycles. The surviving representatives are exactly v^k and v^k z, k>=0. This proves both spanning and independence, and the multiplication assertion follows from z^2=d1(yz).

Crucially, every one of these representatives is a single skyline basis class. Indeed y^b z^c is a gathered single-column monomial; its standard mod-2 Fox–Neuwirth representative is [b+c,c,b+c] [GS, Theorem 3.1 and Figure 2]. No assertion that this particular integral cochain is closed is needed.

### 3.2 Detecting the secondary differential

Let C4 be the subgroup generated by a four-cycle. Its real permutation representation decomposes as a trivial line, a sign line, and the underlying oriented plane of a faithful complex character. Write

    H*(BC4;F2)=Lambda(s) tensor F2[t],  |s|=1, |t|=2.

The Whitney product formula then gives

    x restricts to s,   y restricts to t,   z restricts to st.

The periodic resolution of C4 has integral cochain differential 4 in odd degree and 0 in even degree. Hence d1=0, d2(s)=t, and d2(t)=0. Naturality implies that d2(z) restricts to t^2, which is nonzero. The degree-4 part of E2(BS4) is the one-dimensional space generated by v, so

    d2(z)=v.

Also d2(v)=0 since E2^5=0. By the derivation rule, d2(v^k z)=v^(k+1). Thus E3 consists only of the degree-zero unit. In particular, no positive-degree torsion of order 8 or greater is possible.

### 3.3 Integral cyclic generators, rather than just page dimensions

For completeness, the algebra connecting this page calculation to integral orders is recalled here. A finite-rank free cochain complex over R can, degree by degree, be split into two-term summands

    R --2^r--> R

and one-term free summands; odd unit factors may be absorbed into a basis. To see this, choose a splitting of each cochain group onto its free image in the next degree, and put the inclusion of boundaries into cycles into Smith normal form. Each displayed two-term summand has a source and target on the mod-2 page; it has its sole nonzero Bockstein differential on page r and contributes R/2^r in the target degree. A finite-group cohomology complex has finite-rank terms in each degree, and positive-degree cohomology is finite and killed by the group order (transfer from the trivial subgroup proves this). This applies the elementary calculation here without a convergence ambiguity.

Consequently H^d(BS4;R), d>0, has exactly one order-4 cyclic factor if d is divisible by 4, and none otherwise. Its order-2 factor count is rank(d1:A^(d-1) -> A^d).

The order-4 generator in degree 4k+4 can be chosen to be a secondary integral Bockstein of the single skyline class y^(2k)z. Explicitly, start with any integral lift c of its mod-2 cocycle. Since d1 of that class vanishes, choose h so that d(c+2h) is divisible by 4, and put

    g_k = [d(c+2h)/4] in H^(4k+4)(BS4;R).

Its order divides 4. Its reduction represents d2(v^k z)=v^(k+1), nonzero on E2, so its order is exactly 4. The choices of h can change g_k by lower-order terms; the claim is existence of such generators, not a choice-free secondary operation. In particular, it does not assert that the uncorrected canonical cochain has differential divisible by 4.

For the order-2 factors, take any degree-(d-1) skyline basis and select columns whose d1 images form a basis of im(d1). The primary integral Bocksteins of these selected individual skyline classes have independent mod-2 reductions and exact order 2. Their reductions, together with that of g_k when 4 divides d, are independent in H^d(BS4;R)/2H^d(BS4;R): the primary reductions are independent boundaries on E1, whereas the secondary reduction is nonzero after passage to E2. The known factor counts imply that these classes generate, by the elementary Nakayama argument for a finite 2-group; their stated orders give a surjection from a direct sum of the same finite order, which is therefore an isomorphism.

This proves the source conjecture for S4 in every degree using only primary and secondary Bocksteins of individual skyline classes, plus the degree-zero unit. No positive-degree integral skyline cycle is required for this special case. This is an elementary consequence of known low-rank cohomology and is not presented as a novel computation of H*(BS4;R).

### 3.4 An explicit deterministic selection rule

For use in the verification script, write M(b,c)=y^b z^c and Q(i,j)=gamma_(1,1)^i transfer gamma_(1,1)^j, 0<=i<j. These are precisely the skyline basis elements of A (with M(0,0) the unit). Put P_0=0, P_1=x, and P_m=x P_(m-1)+y P_(m-2). Then Q(i,j)=y^i P_(j-i). For each degree, order the Q terms by increasing i, followed by the M terms by increasing c. Greedily retain the sources whose d1 images increase rank over F2. Primary Bocksteins of those sources, together with the secondary source M(2k,1) in degree 4k+3, give the cyclic basis just proved. `TEST_RESULTS.json` lists these source choices through target degree 80.

## 4. Odd-index induction: extending the theorem through S7

**Theorem.** For every 0<=n<=7 and every degree, the 2-local cohomology of S_n has an additive cyclic basis of the required type. For 4<=n<=7, there is exactly one order-4 factor in each positive degree divisible by 4; all other positive-degree cyclic factors have order 2. The order-4 factors can be generated by secondary integral Bocksteins of

    (y^(2k)z) transfer 1_(n-4),  k>=0.

The primary factors can be selected from primary Bocksteins of individual skyline classes.

**Proof.** The cases n<=4 were proved above. For n=5,6,7, take H=S4 x S_(n-4) inside S_n. Its index is respectively 5,15,35, hence invertible in R. The identity transfer after restriction = [S_n:H] implies that restriction is injective on every page of the mod-2 coefficient Bockstein spectral sequence, and integral restriction is split injective. Kunneth over F2 and the derivation rule identify the first Bockstein complex of H with the tensor product of the two first Bockstein complexes. Since E2(BS_r)=F2 for 0<=r<=3,

    E2(BH) = F2[v] tensor Lambda(z).

The gathered class alpha_k=y^(2k)z is primitive for the standard coproduct: z is primitive in component 4, so every nontrivial split term of the coproduct of y^(2k) is annihilated on multiplying by the coproduct of z. Let T_k=alpha_k transfer 1_(n-4), a single skyline basis class. The Hopf-ring coproduct and its distributivity formula give

    restriction to H of T_k = alpha_k tensor 1.

There cannot be a term with alpha_k in the second factor, whose component has fewer than four letters. T_k is a d1-cycle by compatibility of transfer with coefficient Bocksteins. Hence every odd-degree E2 generator v^k z of BH is in the image of restriction from BS_n. Applying d2 shows that the even-degree generators v^(k+1) are also in this image. Along with the unit these exhaust E2(BH), so restriction is an isomorphism on E2, and E3(BS_n)=F2. The cyclic-generator argument of Section 3.3 now applies verbatim, with T_k as the secondary source and a pivot subset of the complete S_n skyline basis as primary sources. QED.

### Where this induction stops

For a general splitting n=a+b, the index of the Young subgroup S_a x S_b is binomial(n,a). Its 2-adic valuation is the number of carries in binary addition of a and b (equivalently, this follows by applying the factorial valuation formula). It is odd exactly when a and b have disjoint binary digits. At n=2^j every nontrivial such index is even, so the preceding split-injectivity argument cannot reach a new power-of-two component. Moreover, integral Kunneth has Tor terms; one cannot simply replace the field-valued Hopf-ring tensor product by an integral tensor product and regard that as a proof. These are genuine gaps in this approach to the unrestricted conjecture.

## 5. A genuine third-Bockstein skyline family on S8

The first new power-of-two component is not exhausted by the preceding basis theorem. Nevertheless a transfer-square cancellation constructs the following explicit infinite family.

**Proposition.** For each a>=0, the single skyline class

    U_a = (z y^(2a)) transfer y^(2a+2) in H^(8a+7)(BS8;F2)

survives to the third page of the coefficient Bockstein spectral sequence. Its third integral Bockstein can be chosen to have exact order 8 in H^(8a+8)(BS8;R).

The two factors are on S4; their gathered profiles differ because exactly one contains z, so this transfer product is indeed one skyline basis element, rather than a sum.

**Proof of survival.** In the E2 page for S4 x S4, the source is z v^a tensor v^(a+1). Its d2 image is v^(a+1) tensor v^(a+1). Transfer commutes with Bocksteins. The transfer product of the two equal gathered classes v^(a+1) is zero already in mod-2 cohomology: merging the equal width-four profiles has coefficient binomial(4,2)=6, which is even [GSS, Theorem 6.8]. Thus d1(U_a)=0 and d2(U_a)=0. To prove that U_a has not become a boundary on the second page, and to detect its next differential, use C8.

**Mackey restriction to C8.** Identify the eight letters with residues modulo 8 and let C8 act by translation. Cosets of S4 x S4 are four-element subsets. There is exactly one orbit with stabilizer C4, namely the orbit of the even and odd subsets. There is one orbit with stabilizer C2 and eight free orbits. In the C4 orbit, the generator of the stabilizer cyclically permutes each of the two four-element blocks. As in Section 3.2 the restriction of the source to that stabilizer is

    (s_4 t_4^(2a+1)) t_4^(2a+2) = s_4 t_4^(4a+3).

On a C2-stabilized subset the nonidentity element acts as two disjoint transpositions. The permutation representation on that subset is two trivial lines plus two sign lines. Its third Stiefel–Whitney class is zero, so the factor z vanishes and that Mackey term is zero. Positive-degree restriction to a trivial stabilizer is also zero.

Write H*(BC8;F2)=Lambda(s_8) tensor F2[t_8]. Restriction sends t_8 to t_4. Corestriction sends s_4 to s_8: in degree one this is precomposition with the group transfer C8 -> C4, which sends a generator to its square. The projection formula therefore yields

    restriction of U_a to C8 = s_8 t_8^(4a+3).

For C8, the periodic resolution has differential 8 in odd degree. Hence its first two coefficient Bocksteins vanish, and its third differential sends s_8 to t_8 while killing t_8. In particular, the displayed restriction is nonzero on E3, which proves that U_a is not an earlier boundary, and

    restriction of d3(U_a) = t_8^(4a+4) != 0.

Choose a compatible integral lift c of U_a with dc divisible by 8. The integral class [dc/8] is killed by 8. Its restriction has nonzero mod-2 reduction in H^(8a+8)(BC8;R)=R/8, hence is an odd multiple of its generator and has order 8. The original class consequently has exact order 8 as well. QED.

This establishes an infinite family of allowed higher-Bockstein generators. It does not say that the family extends to a cyclic basis of the whole cohomology of S8, and it does not compute every differential on E2 or later pages for S8. The first incomplete component in the all-degree basis theorem remains S8.

### A route that did not close the gap

The initially natural candidate (z y^2) transfer 1_4 has secondary differential y^4 transfer 1_4. Restriction to C8 alone does not decide whether that target vanishes globally, so it does not prove survival of the candidate. Replacing it by z transfer y^2 makes the secondary image a transfer square, which vanishes by the divided-power coefficient. This cancellation is sufficient for the family above; it is not a systematic selection rule for every skyline class.

## 6. Exact remaining obstruction

A uniform solution still requires a compatible family of surviving individual skyline sources whose normalized Bocksteins, together with genuinely integral skyline cycles, account for every cyclic factor on every S_n. In particular, one must rule out the preferred-basis mixing exhibited by the artificial complex in Section 2 inside the actual integral Fox–Neuwirth complex, or explicitly repair it by the allowed integral skyline cycles. The known mod-2 basis theorem, an abstract decomposition into cyclic modules, and the Curtis–Wellington width spectral sequence do not establish this compatibility. No unrestricted solution or counterexample is claimed here.

## 7. Reproducibility and test limits

Run `python3 verify.py` in this directory. It uses only the Python standard library and writes `TEST_RESULTS.json`.

The checks cover the S4 mod-2 ring differential, d1 squared equal to zero, independence and completeness of the proposed E2 representatives, and the deterministic skyline pivot rule in degrees 0 through 80; the abstract preferred-basis counterexample; periodic cyclic resolutions of orders 2,4,8,16; odd indices used in Section 4; all 70 cosets and all C8 orbit stabilizers in the Mackey calculation; and the transfer-square parity coefficient. These are exact integer/F2 calculations. They are not integral Fox–Neuwirth chain computations, an independent derivation of the published mod-2 ring, or evidence that the unrestricted conjecture is true. The uniform degree assertions in this note follow from the proofs, not from finite tests.

During test development the added Mackey check first failed because the standard-library `math` import was missing. Adding the import fixed that harness error; the complete test suite then passed. No failed mathematical test was silently discarded.
