# Independent mathematical supplement

Problem 30001525, rank 977. Coefficients are R = Z localized at 2.

## 1. Meaning of the claim and dependence on primary results

The target is Sinha's Conjecture 5.1 in the 2010 Oberwolfach report, printed page 1936. It asks for a cyclic additive generating basis obtained from integral skyline cycles and higher Bocksteins of skyline classes. In positive degree this means a direct sum of finite cyclic R-modules, not a free R-module. The source is a mod-2 cohomology class. The statement does not impose the additional requirement that its uncorrected canonical Fox–Neuwirth representative already have differential divisible by the desired power of two.

The imported mathematical inputs are these specific established results:

- Giusti–Salvatore–Sinha: the mod-2 Hopf-ring presentation, the S4 specialization, the gathered-monomial basis, coproduct/transfer rules, elementary-abelian detection and Steenrod action. The relevant locations are Theorem 1.2, Section 6 (especially Proposition 6.4, Proposition 6.6 and Theorem 6.8), Corollaries 7.6–7.7 and Section 8.
- Giusti–Sinha: the integral Fox–Neuwirth model (Theorem 2.9) and its mod-2 S4 skyline representatives (Theorem 3.1 and Section 4). The audit does not assume that mod-2 cocycles are automatically integral cocycles.
- The coefficient exact couple, its naturality and multiplicative differential, and the relationship between a page-r differential and a cyclic factor of exact order 2^r. These also follow from the elementary free-complex argument below. May–Ponto, Examples 24.2.3 and 24.4.3, provide a primary textbook reference.
- Restriction, corestriction, their index identity, the projection formula and the Mackey double-coset formula with trivial coefficients. The Mackey formula is also explicitly recorded in Section 2.4 of Guerra–Salvatore–Sinha.

The field-coefficient extended-power presentation and Hunter's width/unstable-Adams spectral sequence are not substituted for integral cohomology or for preferred-basis compatibility.

## 2. Actual integral lifts of a fixed source

Let C be a free R-cochain complex. Fix a mod-2 cocycle a and an integral lift c. Write dc = 2b. If d1[a] = 0, then b modulo 2 is an ordinary coboundary. Choose h such that b - dh is even. The corrected lift c4 = c - 2h has dc4 = 4b2. Its reduction is still exactly a.

Suppose also that d2[a] = 0 on E2. By the definition of zero in ker(d1)/im(d1), there is a mod-2 cocycle u of the same degree as a, an integral lift u0, and an integral cochain q of that degree with

    b2 - du0/2 - dq = 0 modulo 2.

The expression du0/2 is integral because u is a mod-2 cocycle. Then

    c8 = c4 - 2u0 - 4q
    dc8 = 4(b2 - du0/2 - dq)

is divisible by 8. Again c8 reduces exactly to a. This supplies the missing explicit implementation of the page-three lift assertion. The correction uses other integral cochains, but does not replace the mod-2 source by a sum of distinct skyline classes.

For r = 2 or 3 the class [dc_(2^r)/2^r] is an actual integral cohomology class: applying d again gives zero because the cochain groups have no 2-torsion. Its order divides 2^r. For two compatible lifts with the same mod-2 reduction, their difference is 2e. Therefore the two normalized integral classes differ by [de/2^(r-1)], an integral class killed by 2^(r-1). This is the expected lower-order indeterminacy, not a failure of the individual-source condition.

This interpretation is essential to acceptance. A stronger demand concerning only unmodified canonical integral cochains is neither asserted by the packet nor proved here.

## 3. Uniform S4 first-page calculation

Write A = F2[x,y,z]/(xz), with degrees 1,2,3. The first Bockstein is the derivation D with

    Dx = x^2, Dy = xy + z, Dz = 0.

For b even, the x-positive monomials x^a y^b are paired by D from odd a to even a. For b odd, pairing starts at even a >= 2 and leaves xy^b. On the x-free part, D(y^(2k+1)) = xy^(2k+1) + y^(2k)z links this leftover to y^(2k)z. For c >= 1,

    D(y^(2k+1)z^c) = y^(2k)z^(c+1).

Thus every monomial with z exponent at least two is eliminated, and the only remaining classes are y^(2k) and y^(2k)z. None is an unaccounted boundary: the listed pairings form an exhaustive decomposition of the monomial basis. Multiplication gives

    E2 = F2[v] tensor Lambda(z), v = y^2, degrees 4 and 3.

This is a proof for every degree. The independent degree-0–100 calculation checks implementations and examples; it is not used to extrapolate the theorem.

The Stiefel–Whitney identifications can be checked concretely. On the subgroup generated by two disjoint transpositions, the restrictions of x,y,z are u+v, uv, 0. On the regular Klein four subgroup they are 0, u^2+uv+v^2, u^2v+uv^2. These agree with the total Stiefel–Whitney classes of the permutation representation, and the two restrictions jointly detect the ring. This confirms the convention used for C4 restriction.

## 4. C4, order four and a genuine cyclic basis

For a four-cycle, the real permutation representation is a trivial line, a sign line and an oriented faithful character plane. Consequently x,y,z restrict to s,t,st in Lambda(s) tensor F2[t]. The periodic integral cyclic resolution has differential 4 in odd degree, so the first differential is zero and d2(s)=t. Hence d2(z) restricts to t^2. The S4 target E2 in degree four is one-dimensional, forcing d2(z)=v. The derivation rule gives

    d2(v^k z) = v^(k+1), d2(v^k) = 0.

Therefore E3 has only its degree-zero unit.

Here is why actual orders and generation follow. In a degreewise finite free R-complex, split each group onto the free image of its outgoing differential and diagonalize the inclusion of boundaries in cycles. The result is a sum of free terms and two-term maps R --2^r--> R, with unit maps harmless. Such a map contributes one R/2^r in the target degree and exactly one nonzero page-r Bockstein. For a finite group, positive-degree cohomology is finite and annihilated by the group order, so no free or unbounded-exponent ambiguity occurs.

S4 therefore has one order-four factor in each positive degree divisible by four and no higher-order factors. Its number of order-two factors in degree d is rank(D:A^(d-1) -> A^d). A normalized secondary Bockstein of the single source y^(2k)z realizes each order-four factor. If its order were at most two, its reduction would lie in im(D), contradicting its nonzero E2 target.

Choose individual skyline sources whose primary D-images form a basis of im(D). Their integral primary Bocksteins have order two and linearly independent reductions. The coefficient long exact sequence identifies H^d(R)/2H^d(R) with its image in H^d(F2). Adding the secondary generator gives an independent set there, since its reduction is nonzero in E2. Its size is the number of cyclic factors, so Nakayama's elementary finite-2-group argument gives generation. The orders of the chosen generators give a surjection from a finite direct sum with exactly the same cardinality as H^d(R); the surjection is an isomorphism. This checks independence and generation rather than only associated-graded dimensions.

The deterministic Q/M selection rule is consistent with the actual skyline basis. Independently, its Q(i,j) expressions can be obtained from u^i v^j+u^j v^i, with x=u+v and y=uv. The audit uses a closed integer coefficient formula rather than the submitted recurrence, and reproduces every submitted pivot through degree 80.

## 5. Why the argument reaches S5, S6 and S7

For n=5,6,7, the subgroup H=S4 x S_(n-4) has odd index 5,15,35. On each coefficient Bockstein page, corestriction after restriction is the identity over F2; on R-cohomology it is multiplication by an invertible odd number. Restriction is therefore injective on every page and split injective integrally.

The first Bockstein complex for the product is the tensor product over F2. Since E2(S_r)=F2 for r<=3, its homology is E2(H)=F2[v] tensor Lambda(z). No integral Kunneth or absence of Tor is being assumed.

For alpha_k=y^(2k)z on S4, the z factor is primitive. Its coproduct has no positive-component split, and multiplying by the coproduct of y^(2k) cannot create one. Thus alpha_k is primitive. For the single skyline T_k=alpha_k transfer 1_(n-4), the component-(4,n-4) coproduct is exactly alpha_k tensor 1. There is no second placement of alpha_k, because n-4<4. This is an equality of mod-2 classes, not merely a dimensional assertion.

T_k is a d1-cycle and restricts to the nonzero E2 class v^kz. Naturality of d2 then supplies a preimage of every positive even-degree generator v^(k+1). With the unit, these exhaust E2(H), proving E2 surjectivity. Combined with injectivity, restriction is an E2 isomorphism, and the same cyclic-basis argument applies. At n=8 all nontrivial Young-subgroup indices are even, so this proof does not extend by the same step.

## 6. S8: cancellation, every Mackey orbit and exact order eight

The class U_a=(z y^(2a)) transfer y^(2a+2) has two distinct gathered profiles. One contains gamma_(2,1) and the other does not. It is consequently a single skyline basis class, not an arbitrary linear combination.

On E2(S4 x S4) its d2 image is v^(a+1) tensor v^(a+1). Transfer of this class is zero in mod-2 cohomology. More precisely, each of the two equal profiles is supported on four letters and has combinatorial width two in the convention of GSS Definition 6.3. Merging gives coefficient binomial(4,2)=6. Distinguishing support size from this width resolves an innocuous notation ambiguity in the frozen proof.

For restriction to C8, cosets of S4 x S4 are ordered choices of the first four-letter block, so there are 70 four-element subsets. Translation has ten orbits: eight of size eight with trivial stabilizer, one of size four with C2 stabilizer, and one of size two with C4 stabilizer. The latter consists of the even and odd subsets and is one orbit, so its transfer term occurs once rather than twice.

The C4 stabilizer acts regularly on both four-letter blocks. The source restricts there to s4 t4^(4a+3). The C2 stabilizer acts as two transpositions on each block; its four-dimensional representation has two trivial lines and two sign lines, making its third Stiefel–Whitney class zero. Every such term therefore vanishes because one source factor contains z. Positive-degree classes restrict to zero at every trivial stabilizer. This accounts for all 70 cosets, including the often-dangerous C2 term.

Restriction sends t8 to t4 but sends s8 to zero. The argument correctly uses corestriction in degree one: the group transfer C8 -> C4 sends a generator to its square, so cor(s4)=s8. The projection formula gives

    res_(C8)(U_a) = s8 t8^(4a+3).

Both earlier differentials vanish on C8. Hence this nonzero restriction rules out an earlier boundary for U_a, and U_a defines a nonzero E3 class. The cyclic resolution gives d3(s8)=t8, so d3(U_a) restricts to t8^(4a+4), which is nonzero.

By Section 2, an integral lift of the same source can be corrected to have differential divisible by eight. Its normalized integral class is killed by eight. Its C8 restriction has nonzero ordinary mod-2 reduction, since E3(C8) is still E1(C8). The even positive-degree integral group of C8 is R/8, in which precisely the odd multiples of a generator have nonzero mod-2 reduction. The restriction, and hence the original class, has exact order eight.

For an independent chain-level check on cyclic groups, the inhomogeneous integral one-cochain c(i)=i, with representatives 0<=i<m, satisfies dc(i,j)=m floor((i+j)/m). The carry two-cocycle is closed. Its reduction gives t, and c modulo 2 gives s. Cup powers give d(c cup t^q)=m t^(q+1) in every degree. The parity-valued uncorrected lift can fail divisibility by m; c differs from it by an even cochain. This illustrates precisely why compatible lifting is legitimate and necessary.

There is one such chosen S8 class in each degree 8a+8. Abstractly it can generate an order-eight direct summand: in a Smith decomposition, a class killed by eight has even coefficients in all factors of order at least sixteen, while targets in order-two and order-four factors vanish before E3. Its nonzero reduction on E3 therefore has an odd coefficient in some order-eight factor. Projection onto that factor, followed by an odd unit rescaling, is a retraction onto the cyclic subgroup generated by the chosen class. This does not supply an allowable skyline basis of the complementary summand, so it does not complete the conjecture for S8.

## 7. Artificial complex and the general obstruction

In the artificial complex, degree-four cycles are Ra + R(x-y), and the boundary lattice has columns (2,4) and (2,0). Its Smith invariants are 2 and 4. The preferred integral cycle a and the primary images a and a+2(x-y) generate only the subgroup spanned by a and 2(x-y). Each individual preferred degree-three source has nonzero first differential, so neither admits a higher Bockstein. Their difference does, and supplies x-y at the next stage. This is a valid counterexample to a theorem for arbitrary preferred bases, but is not a counterexample concerning symmetric groups.

The full conjecture would require an argument excluding or resolving analogous preferred-source mixing in all symmetric-group components. Nothing in the verified low-rank calculation supplies that argument. General status remains unresolved by this packet.
