# Primary sources and exact use

Date: 12 October 2026 UTC. The proof in this packet is authored mathematics. No copied source document or extracted source text is included.

1. Open Problem Garden, “List colorings of edge-critical graphs.”
   https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/list_colorings_of_edge_critical_graphs
   Directly inspected in the preceding research and audit rounds. This is the intended nonconstant-list problem statement. The subcubic proof handles maximum degree three and at most five exceptions to one common palette. The combined theorem adds the separate higher-degree proof and the accepted degree-two case; arbitrary nonconstant assignments remain outside this result.

2. Bojan Mohar, July 2001 problem page, “List edge-colorings of critical graphs.”
   https://www.sfu.ca/~mohar/Problems/P7ListEdgeCriticalGraphs.html
   Directly inspected previously. Its printed equivalence has the known reversed wording. This typo is not a counterexample or proof of the intended mathematical claim.

3. Hemanshu Kaul and Jeffrey A. Mudrock, “Criticality, The List Color Function, and List Coloring the Cartesian Product of Graphs,” arXiv:1805.02147v2.
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02147v2
   The actual version-2 PDF was captured and inspected during the first research round, including Conjecture 6 on printed page 11. The line-graph formulation supplies conceptual context. The subcubic theorem explicitly assumes that G is not three-edge-colorable, rather than relying on a compressed criticality sentence that omits that phrase. Published bibliographic record: Journal of Combinatorics 12(3) (2021), 479–514, https://doi.org/10.4310/JOC.2021.v12.n3.a4 . The full published PDF was not successfully captured; its status is not substituted for the inspected arXiv version.

4. Anton Bernshteyn, Hemanshu Kaul, Jeffrey A. Mudrock and Gunjan Sharma, “On Strongly and Robustly Critical Graphs.”
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgt.70033
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04538
   The actual arXiv version-2 PDF and relevant publisher definitions/theorem text were inspected in the first round. Theorem 1.6 concerns joins with sufficiently large cliques, and does not establish the original line-graph conjecture. A round-three web search again surfaced the official publisher's definition and theorem. No new claim of a full published-proof audit is made. The current arXiv record was directly reopened in this round and identifies version 3, dated 25 February 2026. Its HTML introduction, Definition 1.5 and Theorem 1.6 were directly inspected at https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04538v3 ; the cited clique-join context is unchanged. Version 2 is the previously captured PDF, not the latest version.

5. Martin Juvan, Bojan Mohar and Riste Škrekovski, “On list edge-colorings of subcubic graphs,” Discrete Mathematics 187 (1998), 137–149.
   https://www.sfu.ca/~mohar/Reprints/1998/BM98_DM187_Juvan_ListEdgeColorings.pdf
   The actual thirteen-page PDF was inspected previously. Its mixed three/four-list result is not used as a theorem for arbitrary three-lists in this packet.

## Bounded round-three search

Searches made on 12 October 2026 UTC included the phrases “edge-critical list nonconstant,” “strongly chromatic-choosable five,” and “list edge critical subcubic graphs.” The official 2026 strong-criticality paper and older subcubic list-coloring work appeared. Results about strong, star, injective, or normal edge colorings are different coloring notions and were not used as substitutes for ordinary list edge coloring.

This bounded search does not establish historical novelty, comprehensive absence of a prior theorem, or continued openness of every broader formulation. No such claim is made. None of these sources is cited as proving the new five-exception argument.

## Authored mathematical dependency

For the subcubic result, only the already independently accepted [one-through-four-exception theorem](../3200_critical_list_partials_20261012/FOUR_EXCEPTIONS.md) is used to extend the new exactly-five proof to “at most five.” The new subcubic exactly-five proof restates and proves all of its own completion, list, and support lemmas. Its discovery benefited from the independently enumerated exterior-certificate frontier, but that enumeration is not a logical premise of the theorem.

The higher-degree exactly-five proof additionally uses the first-round [arbitrary-star theorem](../3200_critical_list_partials_20261012/NEAR_CONSTANT.md) to handle five exceptional edges sharing an endpoint. Its nonstar case is proved in full in HIGHER_DEGREE.md. The combined degree-two case is the complete [odd-cycle argument](../3200_critical_list_partials_20261012/ADDITIONAL_CASES.md); maximum degree at most one is vacuous. THEOREM.md gives the exact synthesis. The earlier four-exception statement corrections are preserved in [STATEMENT_CLARIFICATIONS.md](../3200_critical_list_partials_20261012/STATEMENT_CLARIFICATIONS.md) and [AUDIT_NEAR_CONSTANT.md](../3200_critical_list_partials_20261012/AUDIT_NEAR_CONSTANT.md).

During this edition preparation, the official arXiv records at https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02147 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04538 were reopened. They verify the Kaul–Mudrock title used above and the latter paper's version-3 date of 25 February 2026. This bibliographic check is not a new full-proof audit or an exhaustive priority search.
