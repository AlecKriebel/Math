# Purely cyclic Veech groups: compactness and torus-cover obstructions

**11000213 / AMR-109-0213. Scoped partial results; the compact existence question remains unresolved. Two substantive approaches. Separate review pending; no historical-priority claim.**

The simple strategy of lifting a hyperbolic torus map through a finite translation cover cannot produce a purely cyclic Veech group when its finite branch set is invariant. The cover necessarily retains two noncommuting parabolic derivatives. The known orbit-deletion construction in finite area uses noncompactness, and its direct compact analogue fails for the reasons below.

## 1. Source conventions and present literature

Problem 6 in the Hubert–Masur–Schmidt–Zorich chapter of [Farb's full manuscript, printed p.252, PDF p.259](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf) asks whether the full Veech group can be generated by one hyperbolic element. The preceding definitions use finitely many polygons with all sides paired, hence compact translation or half-translation surfaces with finitely many conical singularities. The Veech group there is the derivative image in \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\). Merely finding a pseudo-Anosov, or its cyclic subgroup inside a larger affine group, does not answer the question. Derivative images, full affine groups and their finite kernels are not identified without justification.

[Lenzhen–Souto, *Cyclic hyperbolic Veech groups in finite area* (2017)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06290), Theorem 1.1, constructs noncompact finite-area examples using the Chamanara surface. Its introduction explicitly separates the compact problem. The proof removes an infinite discrete orbit of a point with trivial affine stabilizer. The full seven-page preprint was read; this result is credited and not transferred to the compact source category.

[Leininger–Rafi–Rouse–Shinkle–Verberne, *Fibered 3-manifolds and Veech groups*, AGT 25 (2025), 1897–1915](https://msp.org/agt/2025/25-3/agt-v25-n3-p16-p.pdf), still states the lonely pseudo-Anosov conjecture, which asks even for virtual cyclicity in the finite-type setting. Its Theorem 1.3 is a conditional absence-of-parabolics result, and Theorem 1.4 concerns infinite-type leaves. Neither is a compact cyclic example. The relevant statements in the full published text were checked; no independent reproof of those results or exhaustive later-status certification is claimed.

## 2. Periodic points of a hyperbolic torus map

Use \(\mathbb T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2\), and let
\[
\phi(x)=Ax+b,\qquad A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z),\quad|\operatorname{tr}A|>2 .
\]
Because \(I-A\) is invertible over \(\mathbb R\), \(\phi\) has a fixed point: choose a lift of \(b\) and solve \((I-A)x_0=b\). Translating \(x_0\) to the origin conjugates \(\phi\) to \(x\mapsto Ax\).

If \(x\) is periodic of period \(k\), then
\[
(A^k-I)\widetilde x\in\mathbb Z^2 .
\]
The matrix \(A^k-I\) is an invertible integer matrix, since the hyperbolic eigenvalues have no positive power equal to one. Its inverse has rational entries. Thus \(x\in\mathbb Q^2/\mathbb Z^2\), in coordinates centered at \(x_0\).

Consequently every finite \(\phi\)-invariant set \(B\) is contained, after that translation, in
\[
N^{-1}\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z^2
\tag{1}
\]
for some positive integer \(N\). The principal congruence group
\[
\Gamma(N)=\{M\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):M\equiv I\pmod N\}
\]
then fixes every point of \(B\).

## 3. A finite cover retains parabolics

**Theorem 1.** Let \(p:X\to\mathbb T^2\) be a connected finite-degree translation covering from a compact translation surface, branched only over a finite set \(B\) invariant under a hyperbolic affine torus map. Then the derivative group of \(X\) contains a finite-index subgroup of a conjugate of \(\Gamma(N)\), for an \(N\) as in (1). In particular, its projective Veech group contains two noncommuting parabolic elements and is not cyclic.

Here a translation covering is locally a translation away from its finitely many branch points, with the translation structure pulled back from the base. The base hyperbolic map need not already lift for the obstruction to apply.

**Proof.** Translate the base fixed point to zero and remove \(B\) and its full preimage. This gives a connected unbranched degree-\(D\) covering
\[
X^\circ\longrightarrow Y=\mathbb T^2\setminus B.
\]
The fundamental group of \(Y\) is finitely generated, using at most \(r+2\) generators where \(r=|B|\), including the \(r=0\) case. Connected degree-\(D\) covers are classified by conjugacy classes of index-\(D\) subgroups. There are only finitely many: their monodromy actions are represented by homomorphisms to \(S_D\), of which there are at most
\[
H=(D!)^{r+2}.
\tag{2}
\]
This deliberately loose bound suffices.

The group \(\Gamma(N)\) acts on these finitely many unbased covering classes by changing the base marking. Its stabilizer of the class of \(X^\circ\) has finite index. For every element of that stabilizer, the covering-lift criterion gives a homeomorphism of \(X^\circ\) covering its affine base map. In translation charts its derivative is exactly the base matrix. The lift and its inverse are bi-Lipschitz in the pulled-back flat metric, with bounds given by that matrix and its inverse. They therefore extend over the finite metric completions at the removed points to inverse affine homeomorphisms of \(X\). Thus the stabilizer is contained in the derivative group.

Explicitly, consider
\[
U_N=\begin{pmatrix}1&N\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
L_N=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\N&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each permutes the finite set of covering classes. Its orbit of the chosen cover has a positive length at most \(H\). Hence \(U_N^a\) and \(L_N^b\) lift for some \(1\le a,b\le H\). Both remain nontrivial parabolics in \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\). Their products in opposite orders are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1+abN^2&aN\\bN&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&aN\\bN&1+abN^2\end{pmatrix},
\]
which differ even modulo multiplication by \(-I\). They therefore do not commute projectively. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

The same initial rationality argument shows that marking finitely many periodic torus points does not remove the congruence subgroup. These are elementary versions of the familiar arithmetic/square-tiled cover obstruction; no priority is claimed. The theorem does not exclude covers over general higher-genus surfaces or arbitrary irrational branch sets with no finite hyperbolic orbit.

## 4. Why direct orbit deletion does not solve the compact problem

The following facts isolate the use of noncompactness in the credited finite-area construction.

1. A closed discrete subset of a compact space is finite. Thus an infinite orbit in a compact surface cannot be both closed and discrete. Removing an orbit with an accumulation point retained in the surface does not give an open translation subsurface near that point. Removing the closure instead is a different construction and need not produce the finite conical-singularity structure of the source.

2. Removing a nonempty finite set of regular points from a connected compact surface produces a noncompact punctured surface. Filling those punctures by their original regular metric completions restores the original translation surface, including its original affine symmetries. Treating them as distinguished marks is additional data.

3. Suppose a finite-area translation surface carries an area-preserving affine map \(\phi\), and a proposed equivariant modification inserts pairwise disjoint positive-area pieces \(E_n=\phi^n(E_0)\) along an infinite orbit. Every piece has the same area, so their union has infinite area. Such a modification cannot supply a compact finite-area example. This statement concerns positive-area insertions; it is not an obstruction to every conceivable zero-area surgery.

4. A finite orbit of length \(k\) cannot have a trivial point stabilizer when \(\phi\) is hyperbolic: \(\phi^k\) fixes its points and is not the identity. Thus the trivial-stabilizer point used in the noncompact argument cannot simply be replaced by a periodic point.

These observations do not prove nonexistence of a compact example. They rule out specific direct transfers of the noncompact mechanism.

## 5. A conditional finite-orbit criterion, with the missing step explicit

There is a useful group-theoretic version of the marking strategy. Let \(X\) be a compact translation surface, let \(\phi\in\operatorname{Aff}^+(X)\) have hyperbolic derivative, and let \(z\) be a regular point of least period \(k\). Put \(O=\{\phi^jz:0\le j<k\}\).

**Lemma 2.** If the stabilizer of \(z\) in the full affine group is exactly
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Aff}^+(X)}(z)=\langle\phi^k\rangle,
\tag{3}
\]
then the setwise stabilizer of \(O\) is exactly \(\langle\phi\rangle\).

**Proof.** Any affine \(\psi\) preserving \(O\) has \(\psi(z)=\phi^j(z)\) for some \(0\le j<k\). Thus \(\phi^{-j}\psi\) fixes \(z\), and (3) makes it a power of \(\phi^k\). Hence \(\psi\) is a power of \(\phi\). The reverse inclusion is immediate. \(\square\)

Thus a surface **with that finite regular orbit marked** would have the desired cyclic affine group and cyclic projective derivative group. No such compact example satisfying (3) is supplied here. Choosing a generic point with trivial stabilizer does not establish (3) on the special set of periodic points.

Furthermore the original unmarked compact question is not answered by a marked object. Converting marks into intrinsic singularities by a finite branched covering would require a construction and a proof that all affine symmetries of the cover descend appropriately. Neither follows from Lemma 2. On a torus, Theorem 1 already blocks the proposed finite periodic-branch construction.

## 6. Outcome and verification limits

The two attempted routes were the finite torus-cover construction and the transfer of the noncompact orbit-removal/marking construction. The first is obstructed by Theorem 1. The second stops at the actual compact point-stabilizer and unmarked-realization requirements. Neither is a proof that compact purely cyclic Veech groups cannot exist.

The exact source problem remains **unsolved, 2/5** in this attempt. The known noncompact finite-area theorem, the 2025 conditional/non-finite-type results, and the source's compact projective convention must be preserved in any status record.

The verifier provides finite exact controls for hyperbolic periodic-point rationality, congruence fixing, noncommuting parabolics, finite permutation-orbit return bounds, and the abstract orbit-stabilizer lemma. It does not verify a hypothetical compact surface or replace the covering-space and compactness proofs.
