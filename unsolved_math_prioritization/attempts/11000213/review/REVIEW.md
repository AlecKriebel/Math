## 2. Independent proof audit of the finite-cover obstruction

### Periodic branch points

For an affine torus automorphism `x -> Ax+b`, hyperbolicity gives invertibility of `I-A` over R. Solving for a fixed point and translating it to zero is valid even when the translation is irrational. The periodic points then lie in a rational coset relative to that fixed point: if a point has period k, `(A^k-I)x` is integral, and the integer matrix `A^k-I` has nonzero determinant. Negative hyperbolic eigenvalues cause no exception: their absolute values differ from one, so no positive power is one.

A finite invariant set is a finite union of periodic orbits. A common denominator therefore exists, and every matrix congruent to the identity modulo that denominator fixes every branch point in the translated coordinates. Translating the base changes affine constants, not derivative matrices. The proof's allowance for conjugacy of the displayed subgroup is harmless and accommodates a different initial lattice basis.

### The finite-index stabilizer really lifts

Removing the finite branch-value set and its full preimage leaves a connected covering of the punctured torus. A connected surface remains connected after removal of finitely many points. The base group has a finite generating set, with at most r+2 generators when r points were removed, including r=0. Degree-D connected unbased covers correspond to conjugacy classes of index-D subgroups. Each can be encoded by a transitive homomorphism into S_D, so the number of classes is at most `(D!)^(r+2)`.

The congruence group acts on **unbased covering classes**. This qualification handles the fact that a chosen basepoint need not be fixed. Fixing the covering class means there is an isomorphism between the pulled-back cover and the original cover, giving a homeomorphic lift of the base map. It does not merely assert that one sheet is carried to another by a noninvertible map. The stabilizer is a subgroup of finite index.

On the punctured surface, the lift has the same constant derivative as the affine base map because the covering charts differ by translations. Its inverse has the inverse derivative. For any rectifiable path, image length is bounded by the operator norm of the derivative times original length; the same holds for the inverse. Thus the lift is globally bi-Lipschitz for the intrinsic flat length metric.

The metric completion of the punctured compact translation surface is exactly the original surface: a finite cone angle and the ordinary regular-point case give a single completion point at each removed preimage. A bi-Lipschitz map and its inverse extend uniquely to these completions and remain inverse homeomorphisms. The extension is affine away from the intrinsic cone singularities, as follows from the local translation charts; at a removed regular point it is the continuous extension of a fixed affine formula. This justifies the potentially delicate completion step and rules out losing the derivatives during the filling operation.

### Two parabolics, including the projective issue

Each elementary congruence shear permutes the finite set of cover classes. The orbit of the chosen class has length between one and the total number of classes. Therefore some positive power of each shear, with exponent at most the stated H, fixes the class and lifts. It is not necessary to use H! or to require the same exponent for both shears.

The two lifted derivative matrices are nontrivial unipotents with nonzero upper and lower off-diagonal entries. Their products have the different diagonal entries displayed in the candidate. They are neither equal nor negatives of one another: their off-diagonal entries are the same nonzero positive numbers, so equality after negation is impossible. Hence they fail to commute in PSL(2,R), not merely in SL(2,R). The full projective derivative image cannot be cyclic. No assumption that the original hyperbolic map itself lifts was used.

The proof covers empty branch sets and extra designated unbranched points as well. It does not control arbitrary irrational branching, higher-genus bases or general compact surfaces. The arithmetic/square-tiled interpretation is credited, without novelty claims.

## 3. Compactness and area arguments

Every stated obstruction is correct within its expressly limited route:

1. A closed discrete subset of a compact space is compact and discrete, hence finite. An infinite orbit cannot simultaneously have both properties. If an accumulation point remains after orbit deletion, the complement is not an open subset at that point. Deleting the entire closure is a different operation and gives no automatic finite-cone compact surface.
2. Removing a nonempty finite set from a connected compact surface gives a noncompact punctured surface. Filling those punctures by the original regular metric completions recovers the original unmarked translation structure and its old affine symmetries. Declaring the points to be marks changes the object.
3. Disjoint iterates of a measurable positive-area insertion under an area-preserving affine map all have the same positive area. Countable additivity forces infinite total area. This does not obstruct all zero-area surgeries, and the candidate correctly says so. In finite area, an affine self-homeomorphism preserves area because its constant determinant must multiply the total nonzero finite area to itself.
4. A point of finite period k is fixed by the k-th power. The hyperbolic derivative makes that power nonidentity, including projectively. Thus periodic points cannot supply the trivial point stabilizer used in the noncompact construction.

None of these observations is promoted to general nonexistence of compact purely cyclic Veech groups.

## 4. Marked-orbit criterion and exact remaining gap

The conditional group lemma is valid in any group action. If `psi(z)=phi^j(z)` and the full point stabilizer is exactly the subgroup generated by `phi^k`, then `phi^(-j) psi` belongs to that subgroup, so psi is a power of phi. Conversely every power of phi preserves the orbit. Least period ensures the orbit indexing is well defined; the proof only needs existence of an appropriate j.

Here it is essential that the hypothesis concerns the **full affine point stabilizer**, rather than a derivative stabilizer or the cyclic subgroup already under consideration. The latter weaker statements would not exclude extra orbit-preserving symmetries. The candidate supplies no periodic point satisfying the full hypothesis. Generic trivial stabilizers do not provide such a point in the special periodic set.

If that hypothesis were realized, marking the orbit would indeed make the full marked affine group cyclic, and its projective derivative image would be infinite cyclic because no nonzero power of a hyperbolic matrix becomes ±I. That deduction does not construct an unmarked compact surface. Branching over marks would require an additional argument controlling descent of **all** affine symmetries, not merely lifts of the desired subgroup. The torus case is already excluded by the finite-cover theorem. These unresolved steps are stated precisely and remain the reason the original status is unsolved.
