# A first-order obstruction to the printed rank-interpolation conjecture

Problem 10400145 / AMR-103-0145, queue rank 1010.

## Conclusion and scope

**Author conclusion: the literal Conjecture 7.30 is false.** An integral homology 3-sphere with nonzero Casson invariant cannot have the proposed rank-unifying element in the printed ring. In particular, the oriented Poincare homology sphere specified below is a counterexample. The obstruction already uses ranks 1, 2 and 3 and the coefficient of q−1.

This is a deduction from standard, cited quantum-invariant results, not a novelty claim. It does not refute existence of rank-unifying invariants in a different completion, with poles in q−1, or with different rank-dependent normalizations. It does not challenge fixed-Lie-algebra unification (Conjecture 7.29, subsequently proved). No human referee review or formal proof-assistant verification is asserted.

## 1. Recovered problem and conventions

The complete printed heading is **Conjecture 7.30 (K. Habiro)**, in Ohtsuki's problem collection [O, printed p. 492; PDF p. 120]. There is no longer descriptive title after the proposer. The catalogue's truncated title is not the primary statement.

The coefficient ring, defined on printed p. 490, is

R = lim_m Z[q,q^(-1)] / ((q−1)(q²−1)...(q^m−1)).

Put E_m(t)=∏_{j=1}^m(t−q^j). The proposed rank completion is

S = lim_m R[t]/(E_m(t)).

The bonding maps are the natural quotient maps, since E_m divides E_(m+1). The statement asks for an invariant F_M in S for every integral homology 3-sphere M with evaluations

F_M(q^n)=I^(sl_n)(M) for every n≥1,

where I^(sl_1)(M)=1 and the n≥2 invariants are those of Conjecture 7.29. Thus n labels sl_n, not its Lie rank n−1. The premise that Conjecture 7.29 holds is part of the printed wording. The same page contains an update reporting its proof; [HL, Theorem 1.1] supplies a subsequent complete source.

The only inversion in the definition of R is inversion of q. There is no inversion of q−1, no divided Newton basis, and no rank-dependent rescaling of q in the stated problem. The two signs ∏(q^j−1) and ∏(1−q^j) define the same ideals. At each finite level, q is already a unit in Z[q]/∏(1−q^j), because that polynomial has constant term 1. Consequently R agrees canonically with the usual Habiro ring lim Z[q]/∏(1−q^j) used in [HL].

## 2. The algebraic obstruction

Write D=Z[x]/(x²). Substitution q=1+x and q^(-1)=1−x defines a homomorphism

T_1 : R → D.

To see this without a convergence assumption, project to level m=2. Its defining ideal is generated by (q−1)(q²−1), which maps to zero in D. Hence the quotient maps to D and so does the inverse limit. This map extracts the constant and first Taylor coefficients. In particular, q−1 cannot be invertible in R, since its image x in the nonzero ring D is nilpotent.

**Lemma (three-node first-jet constraint).** For F in S, write T_1(F(q^n))=u_n+v_n x. Then

u_1=u_2=u_3 and v_3−2v_2+v_1=0.

**Proof.** The third component of F lies in R[t]/(E_3). Since E_3 is monic, it has a representative P(t)=a+bt+ct² with a,b,c in R. For n=1,2,3, evaluation at q^n is well-defined on this quotient because E_3(q^n)=0. It is also the inverse-limit evaluation: every level m≥3 has the same value, by compatibility and the factor (t−q^n) in E_m.

Write T_1(a)=a_0+a_1x, T_1(b)=b_0+b_1x and T_1(c)=c_0+c_1x. The elementary identities

(1+x)^n=1+nx and (1+x)^(2n)=1+2nx in D

give

T_1(P(q^n))=(a_0+b_0+c_0)+(a_1+b_1+c_1+n(b_0+2c_0))x.

Thus the constant term is independent of n and the first coefficient is affine in n. Its second difference at 1,2,3 vanishes. This proves the lemma. No infinite sum is differentiated, and injectivity of Taylor expansion is unnecessary. □

If F(q)=1, the lemma says v_1=0 and therefore

v_3=2v_2.                                                   (A)

## 3. Primary-source bridge to the actual quantum invariants

Three inputs are used, with their normalizations fixed.

1. For integral homology spheres, [HL, Proposition 1.9, published p. 2693; arXiv v2 p. 9] identifies the Taylor expansion at q=1 of the unified g-invariant with its Ohtsuki/perturbative series. Proposition 1.2 identifies its evaluations with the projective g-invariant as well. The preceding discussion explicitly points to Lê's perturbative construction. The root-length convention is (α,α)=2 for short roots, and published §3A2 (manuscript §3.1.2) sets q=v²=exp(h), with v=exp(h/2). In type A these conventions are uniform in n.
2. [L, published p. 821, PDF p. 9, immediately following Conjecture 1.9] gives the first coefficient of the PSU(n) perturbative invariant, expanded in x=q−1, as

   c_1^(n)(M)=|H_1(M;Z)|^(-n(n−1)/2) n(n²−1) λ_C(M).

   Here λ_C is the Casson–Walker invariant in Lescop normalization. For an integral homology sphere |H_1| means its group order, which is 1, so

   c_1^(n)(M)=n(n²−1)λ_C(M).                              (B)

   The same formula occurs in the earlier arXiv v2 preprint on p. 8, where a Legendre-symbol factor is present in the rational-homology normalization. That factor is also 1 here. The published version is the controlling source for (B).
3. [O, pp. 490–492] already connects I^(sl_n) to the perturbative series and identifies PSU(2)=SO(3). The explicit Poincare-sphere series on p. 492 is also given in [LQ, published p. 133] for the sl_2 WRT invariant and in [H, §16.1, formula (16.1)] for the unified invariant. These supply a direct normalization check, below.

Accordingly, if f_n=I^(sl_n)(M), n≥2, then the coefficient of x in T_1(f_n) is the number in (B). This is a use of cited prior results; the packet's elementary arithmetic checker does not prove those topology theorems.

A change from q−1 to h=log q does not affect the first coefficient, since q−1=h+O(h²). A common replacement of the quantum parameter by its square or inverse multiplies every first coefficient by the same nonzero scalar and cannot repair the ratio 4 versus 2 below. No such change is actually needed for the cited conventions.

## 4. Counterexample

Let P be the oriented Poincare homology sphere obtained by −1 surgery on the left-handed trefoil, as explicitly specified in [O, p. 492] and [LQ, p. 133]. Its sl_2 series is

J_P(q)=Σ_(k≥0) q^k (1−q^(k+1))...(1−q^(2k+1))/(1−q).

For k=0 the summand is 1. For k=1 it is

q(1−q²)(1−q³)/(1−q)
= q(1+q)(1−q³)
= −6x+O(x²),  x=q−1.

For every k≥2 the numerator has k+1 factors divisible by x and the quotient has at least k factors divisible by x; hence such summands have no constant or linear term. Therefore c_1^(2)(P)=−6. Formula (B) at n=2 gives λ_C(P)=−1, without needing a separate orientation convention for Casson's invariant. The same formula at n=3 gives c_1^(3)(P)=−24.

If the required F_P existed, its rank-one normalization would yield v_1=0, while the actual ranks two and three yield v_2=−6 and v_3=−24. But (A) would require v_3=−12. Equivalently,

v_3−2v_2+v_1=−24−2(−6)+0=−12 ≠ 0.

This contradiction rules out F_P in the printed S. More generally, (A) and (B) give 12λ_C(M)=0, so every integral homology sphere with λ_C(M)≠0 is excluded. □

## 5. Boundaries, status checks, and what is not proved

The contradiction concerns exactly the inverse limit S over R written above. Removing rank one alone would not cure the problem: the same first-jet argument applied to the fourth quotient makes the coefficients at ranks 2,3,4 affine, but formula (B) gives second difference 18λ_C(M). Allowing coefficients with poles in q−1 invalidates the map to D and can evade this particular obstruction. A modified completion, a divided basis, removal of the specified rank-one normalization, or subtraction of a rank-dependent Casson term changes the question. This report does not assert that any of those changes suffices, or that an intended corrected version has been identified.

Targeted searches for the exact problem number together with 'erratum', 'counterexample', and 'Casson' did not locate an erratum or prior resolution. The arXiv record of [O] has one version, and the currently accessible publisher text still has the displayed ring. This is a bounded search result, not proof of absence or originality. Related fixed-rank cyclotomic constructions and HOMFLY interpolation-polynomial stability in [BG] do not themselves establish the printed S-valued claim. No stronger claim about the entire literature is made.

One substantive mathematical approach was needed: test finite-rank interpolation in the first Taylor jet. The argument resolves the literal statement before a five-approach budget is exhausted. Source recovery, literature checking, packaging, and replay controls are not additional approaches.
