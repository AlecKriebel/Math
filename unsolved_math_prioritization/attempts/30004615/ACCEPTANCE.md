## Accepted mathematical scope

Let M be an even integral lattice of signature (n,2), with n >= 3. Fix one type-IV component and its punctured affine null cone. Use the full ring of nonnegative integral-weight modular forms with trivial character and homogeneity F(tZ)=t^(-k)F(Z). If Gamma is finite-index in O+(M), is generated by actual linear reflections, and H is a finite-index normal subgroup with polynomial modular-form algebra, then the algebra for Gamma is polynomial on n+1 homogeneous generators in the original positive integral weights.

The proof constructs an equivariant homogeneous generator space by averaging graded sections of the indecomposables and proves generation by induction on positive weight. The quotient Gamma/H acts on A_H with invariant ring exactly A_Gamma. Its effective image is used only after checking the action kernel. A central -Id is not discarded without checking its action on every weight.

The key geometric input is an affine statement: the punctured cone quotient embeds into the open part of Spec A_H. An actual reflection fixes a nonempty cone hypersurface pointwise, whose local finite-quotient image has dimension n. After graded linearization this gives either the identity or a pseudoreflection in dimension n+1. A fixed divisor only in Proj would not suffice. Chevalley--Shephard--Todd gives abstract polynomiality, and the second graded lemma recovers generators in the original modular weights.

The normal-tower extension requires every upper group at each step to be reflection-generated. In the specified reflection-normalizer iteration, Wang's Proposition 3.3 supplies containment of each polynomial subgroup in the next. The indices ensure stabilization, without ensuring arrival at Gamma. Arbitrary subnormality alone is not claimed sufficient.

The explicit dihedral group of order 12, its two matrices, preserved positive-definite integral form, reflection subgroup H, normal core K, and self-normalizer are retained in full. Although C[x,y]^H is polynomial, the normal-core algebra is C[A,B,C]/(AC-B^2). The all-degree monomial normal form and the origin's cotangent-dimension obstruction are proved. Fixed-coordinate extensions preserve the obstruction. This is a finite invariant-theory obstruction to a normal-core shortcut, not an orthogonal modular counterexample.

The source-dependent arithmetic warning uses the Wang--Williams lattice U direct-sum U(3) direct-sum A1 and its actual-reflection subgroup R. Their ring has generator weights 1,3,4,6. Adjoining central -Id gives an index-two overgroup with parity algebra C[A,B,C,D,E]/(AC-B^2), generator weights 2,4,6,4,6, relation weight 8, dimension 4, and origin cotangent dimension 5. The written all-degree argument is retained. R and its overgroup have identical projective images, but the overgroup is not generated by actual reflections. This refutes only the projective-reflection weakening, including in the normal case. It does not refute the actual-reflection conjecture or require identifying the overgroup with the full orthogonal group.

For a nonnormal subgroup, there is no quotient action on A_H. Its normal core K supplies finite actions on A_K, but polynomiality of A_K is not supplied by polynomiality of A_H. The finite example shows why generic invariant theory cannot supply that implication; an additional arithmetic or geometric argument remains necessary.
