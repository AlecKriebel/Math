# Independent audit of modular freeness descent

This AI-assisted manuscript is unrefereed. Acceptance records an independent internal AI audit, not external human peer review, journal acceptance, or formal proof-assistant certification.

The complete written proofs and analytical constructions are retained. This is not a computational reproduction package: executable code, raw datasets and certificate tables, copied source documents and images, and private coordination material are omitted. ## Verdict and accepted scope

**ACCEPTED AS A RESTRICTED RESULT, SUBJECT TO THE EXPLICIT CLASSICAL AND SCHOLARLY DEPENDENCIES.** No correction to the sealed mathematical argument is required. This is not acceptance of a solution to the general conjecture.

The accepted results are:

1. Polynomiality descends from a finite-index normal subgroup H to an arithmetic group Gamma generated by actual linear reflections, for the full ring of nonnegative integral-weight, trivial-character orthogonal modular forms in signature (n,2), n >= 3.
2. The same conclusion holds along the stated finite normal tower of reflection-generated upper groups. The reflection-normalizer procedure preserves polynomiality, but may stabilize before the full overgroup.
3. The specified order-12 finite reflection group has a free reflection-subgroup invariant algebra whose normal-core invariant algebra is nonpolynomial. The subgroup is self-normalizing. This obstructs a general invariant-theoretic normal-core argument.
4. Using Wang--Williams' specified arithmetic example, adjoining central -Id produces a nonpolynomial parity-invariant algebra. It refutes only the weakening to projective reflection generation.

The general nonnormal arithmetic descent problem remains **unresolved by this work**. This wording describes the result of the attempt; it is not a certification that no later literature resolves the conjecture. No novelty conclusion is made.

## Full logical review

### 1 The target and conventions

The target is the unrestricted finite-index subgroup question in Wang, Conjecture 5.2, and OWR Conjecture 6. Neither formulation assumes the smaller subgroup normal. The candidate explicitly adds normality for its theorem and does not present this restricted theorem as the full conjecture.

Signature reversal does not affect the argument: replacing the bilinear form by its negative preserves the orthogonal group and null cone, while reversing the norm sign used to identify a mirror meeting the chosen domain. For the (n,2) convention the reflecting vector has positive norm. The chosen cone has complex dimension n+1, while the projective domain has dimension n. Both dimensions are used correctly.

All relevant modular forms have integral nonnegative weights and trivial character. The rule F(tZ)=t^(-k)F(Z) is retained, including t=-1. Consequently central -Id acts by (-1)^k; it cannot in general be discarded. The connected positive grading and finite-dimensional graded pieces needed by the algebra lemmas hold for the modular-form ring. Weight zero consists of constants. The n >= 3 hypothesis permits the stated Koecher boundary-holomorphy convention.

The exact reflection formula fixes the cone vectors in r-perp, not just their projective classes. The negative of this transformation acts as multiplication by -1 on those vectors. This distinction is substantive, and the candidate consistently preserves it. No two-hyperbolic-plane splitting or containment of a discriminant kernel is used in the normal-descent theorem. Those restrictions belong to other classification results.

### 2 Affine realization and the needed geometry

The proof uses a strong enough geometric input. In the polynomial case, the quotient of the punctured domain cone embeds as an open part of the affine modular-form spectrum with evaluation coordinates. Wang's proof of Theorem 3.5(2), PDF p10, explicitly supplies this affine statement. The source does not merely identify a projective compactification with a weighted projective space. PDF p9 supplies additional affine-spectrum context.

Only the polynomial case of this geometric input is needed. Although dependency D1 is introduced for arithmetic groups generally, the application always has A_H polynomial; this audit accepts the exact needed specialization. It does not independently re-prove the broader Baily--Borel construction.

At a nonzero cone point, proper discontinuity gives a small neighborhood whose quotient is modeled by a finite stabilizer. One may choose an invariant neighborhood and take the finite union of the stabilizer translates of a hypersurface germ. A finite map preserves the dimension of that union, so its image has dimension n. The embedding into Spec A_H then retains this dimension. Stabilizers may ramify this map; they do not collapse the hypersurface to lower dimension.

For an actual reflection, the intersection of its mirror with the cone is nonempty. It is smooth of dimension n: the null cone is smooth off its vertex, and r has nonzero norm, so the extra linear equation is transverse there. This supplies precisely the codimension-one analytic germ required in affine dimension n+1. The proof does not assume that the hypersurface globally descends without identifications, nor does it need the image itself to be a linear or irreducible divisor.

A divisor fixed only in Proj would not give this argument. Weighted scalar identifications can make a projective action trivial while its affine fixed locus has codimension two or more; the arithmetic parity example makes this failure explicit.

### 3 Equivariant homogeneous generators

Lemma 2.1 is valid under its stated hypotheses. The positive-degree ideal m is invariant under the finite graded group action. Its indecomposable quotient m/m^2 is finite-dimensional and graded. In each relevant weight, averaging a linear section produces an equivariant homogeneous section, since the characteristic is zero. The average still composes to the identity on m/m^2.

Its image W generates A by induction on weight. After subtracting an appropriate element of W in degree k, the difference lies in the degree-k component of m^2. Every homogeneous product in that component has factors of smaller strictly positive degree. This avoids any need to invoke an ungraded form of Nakayama's lemma for global generation.

Because A is a polynomial algebra and m has residue field C, its cotangent dimension equals dim A. Thus Sym(W) surjects onto A with the same finite dimension. The kernel is prime; a nonzero prime of the polynomial domain would have positive height and decrease the quotient dimension. The kernel is zero. The construction is graded and equivariant. An element acts trivially on W exactly when it acts trivially on the entire algebra generated by W.

Different generator weights do not cause a problem. An arbitrary initial generating set need not have a linear group action, but the constructed homogeneous generating set does. This is a genuine step of the proof, not an assumption hidden in the invocation of invariant theory.

### 4 Forming the finite action and its invariants

Normality is used at the first group-action step. For g in Gamma and h in H, the conjugate g^(-1)hg lies in H. Thus precomposition by g^(-1) preserves H-invariance, as well as weight and holomorphy. H acts trivially on its own trivial-character forms, so the action factors through finite Gamma/H.

Conversely, an H-form fixed by this quotient is exactly a Gamma-invariant form of the same weight and trivial character. The equality A_H^(Gamma/H)=A_Gamma is therefore correct weight by weight. Boundary regularity does not introduce a conflicting condition in the stated dimension.

Passing to the effective image is legitimate: an action kernel changes neither invariant elements nor the generated linear image. The argument does not assume that the quotient is effective before linearization and does not identify a scalar action with the identity without checking it on the forms. The dual action on W* is compatible with the evaluation map: evaluating at gZ is obtained by applying the natural induced action to evaluation at Z.

### 5 From an actual reflection to a pseudoreflection

Let sigma be one of the actual reflections generating Gamma. Every point Z on its cone mirror is fixed, so equivariance makes q_H(Z) fixed by the induced element on X=Spec A_H. The geometric step above gives an analytic germ of dimension dim X-1 in that fixed locus.

After the equivariant generator construction, this action on X=W* is linear. Its fixed locus is therefore a linear subspace. Containment of the germ forces its dimension to be at least dim X-1. If the action is nontrivial, this subspace is proper and hence has exactly codimension one. The element is a pseudoreflection. If the action is trivial, it can be omitted from a generating set of the effective image.

Images of the generating reflections generate the effective finite group, so that group is generated by pseudoreflections. The argument works equally on W or W*, since a linear transformation and its dual have the same multiplicity of eigenvalue 1. No faithfulness claim about the original orthogonal group is needed. This closes the substantive geometric-to-algebraic gap that would be present in a proof based solely on projective fixed divisors.

### 6 Applying invariant theory and retaining the grading

The sufficient direction of the characteristic-zero Chevalley--Shephard--Todd theorem applies to the effective finite linear group. It makes the invariant algebra a polynomial C-algebra. The audit accepts this classical theorem as a named dependency, rather than claiming to have re-audited the original proofs.

The candidate then correctly recovers polynomial generators in modular weights. At the homogeneous maximal ideal of the polynomial algebra, the local ring is regular. Homogeneous lifts of a basis of its cotangent space generate by positive-weight induction. The same dimension argument proves algebraic independence. For a finitely generated C-domain, a closed maximal point has the full dimension, as used here.

Finally, the orbit polynomial product over g of (T-g(a)) is monic with invariant coefficients. Thus A_H is integral over its finite-group invariants, and the two rings have equal dimension n+1. The resulting homogeneous polynomial generators have positive integral degrees. This establishes the exact conclusion of Theorem 3.1.

### 7 The normal tower and reflection normalizer

Repeated application along the specified chain is valid only when each upper group at that step is reflection-generated. The candidate states this extra condition and does not claim that arbitrary subnormality is sufficient.

For the specific enlargement procedure, the optional dependency D3 supplies actual-reflection generation of H_i when its modular-form ring is free. Every such reflection in H_i normalizes H_i, so H_i is contained in the proposed H_(i+1). Each generating reflection of H_(i+1) normalizes H_i, which gives normality. The upper group is reflection-generated by construction and finite-index because it contains finite-index H_i. The normal theorem therefore applies at the next step.

Strict inclusions decrease the positive integer [Gamma:H_i], so there are only finitely many of them. This proves stabilization, not arrival at Gamma. Wang Proposition 3.3 is used only to justify containment at this optional step; it is unnecessary for the normal-descent theorem itself. Its use is consistent with the source's cone-level reflection convention.

### 8 The finite normal-core obstruction

The matrices reconstruct a dihedral group of order 12, with r of order 6, r^3=-I, s of order 2, and srs=r^(-1). Both s and rs have fixed-space codimension one and generate the group. The displayed positive-definite integral Gram matrix is invariant under every enumerated group element.

The subgroup H generated by r^3 and s has exactly four elements I,-I,s,-s. In the coordinates u=x+y and v=x-y, s and -s independently change the signs of the two coordinates. Hence the invariant ring is C[u^2,v^2], with no algebraic relation.

There are three distinct conjugates of H. Their intersection is {I,-I}. Already intersecting H with its r-conjugate yields those two elements; their centrality shows they survive every further conjugate. This proves the full normal-core assertion. The core's only nonidentity transformation has fixed-space codimension two and is not a pseudoreflection.

For the invariant presentation, an invariant monomial x^a y^b has a+b even, so a and b have equal parity. It uniquely equals (x^2)^i(y^2)^j or (x^2)^i(xy)(y^2)^j. In the proposed presentation, replacing B^2 by AC reduces every monomial to one with B-exponent zero or one. These reduced monomials have distinct images. This proves surjectivity and that the entire kernel is generated by AC-B^2, in every degree.

The resulting domain has dimension 2. At the origin the three generator classes are independent modulo the square of the maximal ideal because the relation has no linear term. The local cotangent dimension is 3, proving singularity and nonpolynomiality as an abstract algebra. Adding any number of fixed coordinates increases dimension and cotangent dimension equally, preserving their difference. This supplies the asserted obstruction in dimensions n+1 >= 4 too.

The normalizer calculation is also correct. A rotation r^a conjugates s to r^(2a)s. Membership in H requires 2a congruent to 0 or 3 modulo 6, and the second possibility is impossible. Thus a is 0 or 3 modulo 6. Elements r^a s have the same criterion, giving exactly H. Its reflection-normalizer enlargement is H again. This finite model disproves a purely group-theoretic guarantee of reaching the overgroup, without disproving a possible special arithmetic theorem.

There is no descent counterexample here: the overgroup itself is a finite reflection group and has polynomial invariants. The failure is specifically that polynomial invariants for H need not imply polynomial invariants for its normal core.

### 9 The arithmetic projective-reflection warning

The needed Wang--Williams statements are accurately identified. For U direct-sum U(3) direct-sum A1, the source's maximal actual-reflection subgroup R has a polynomial modular-form ring on weights 1,3,4,6. The weight-6 generator is the square of a weight-3 form. The proof of their Theorem 5.2 identifies the discriminant kernel with its reflection subgroup, so R contains a finite-index subgroup of the full orthogonal group. These results are scholarly inputs, not computations independently reproduced here.

The nonzero weight-1 generator prevents -Id from belonging to R: trivial-character invariance under -Id would force that generator equal to its own negative. Centrality gives R normal of index two in Rplus=<R,-Id>, and Rplus remains a finite-index arithmetic group.

The sign rule in the original weights gives two negative and two positive generator eigenvalues. The invariant ring is consequently generated by f1^2, f1*f3, f3^2, f4, g6, with weights 2,4,6,4,6. The same all-degree parity normal form from the finite example, now with two additional fixed coordinates, proves that its only relation is AC-B^2. Its weight is 8. Dimension 4 and cotangent dimension 5 at the origin prove nonpolynomiality.

The projective action images of R and Rplus coincide because -Id is projectively trivial. Thus the projective reflection-generation weakening genuinely fails even with a normal subgroup. At the same time, every actual reflection in Rplus is an actual reflection in the ambient O+(L), and by definition all such reflections belong to R. Their generated subgroup is precisely R, which is proper in Rplus. The actual-reflection hypothesis of the normal theorem is absent. This fully explains why the example does not contradict it.

The argument does not need Rplus to equal the full orthogonal group. The source's corollary gives a compatible full-group presentation, but identifying those groups is neither asserted nor silently required. No even-weight truncation or all-character ring is substituted for the ring in the conjecture.

### 10 The unresolved nonnormal step

For nonnormal H, precomposition by arbitrary Gamma elements does not give an action on A_H. Passing to K=core_Gamma(H) legitimately gives finite quotient actions on A_K and identities A_H=A_K^(H/K) and A_Gamma=A_K^(Gamma/K). What is missing is polynomiality of A_K or a replacement argument that does not require it.

The finite example establishes that this missing implication cannot be inserted as a generic invariant-ring principle. It does not establish that the implication fails in the arithmetic modular setting. Similarly, the finite normalizer stall does not rule out an additional arithmetic enlargement theorem. The candidate's final limitation is therefore precise: it identifies the missing argument without claiming either a full proof or a disproof.
