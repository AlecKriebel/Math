# Algebraic details supporting the characteristic transfer

This supplement concerns only the cyclic colength step in the accepted partial result. It records the independent audit of the argument patterned on [Viehmann, Lemmas 4.12–4.13 and Theorem 4.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0502320). It does not assert the unresolved orbit-uniqueness conclusion.

Let O be W(k) with uniformizer p or k[[t]] with uniformizer t. Let sigma act on residue coefficients by the q-th power and fix epsilon. Write F for Phi and work in

    R = O[F,V]/(FV=VF=epsilon, Fc=sigma(c)F, Vc=sigma^(−1)(c)V).

The coefficients are already epsilon-adically complete. An element of R can be expanded as a possibly infinite sum [c_ab] V^a F^b with a,b nonnegative and |a−b| bounded. Indeed, reduction of mixed monomials by FV=epsilon leaves only finitely many pure F and V powers, with convergent coefficient sums. In equal characteristic [c] means the constant lift; in mixed characteristic it means the Teichmuller lift. Additions may produce higher-order carries in mixed characteristic, but never lower the current epsilon order.

## 1. One simple factor

Take a normalized projected vector w=e0+sum_(l>0)[c_l]e_l in a simple slope m/(m+n). Every nonnegative combination am+bn is the leading index of a monomial applied to w. Every missing index is below the semigroup conductor (m−1)(n−1), hence below mn. At weight below mn there is at most one pair of nonnegative exponents solving an+bm=k: two distinct pairs differ by a nonzero multiple of (m,−n), which requires weight at least mn. Thus the lowest nonzero monomial coefficient cannot cancel at such a weight. No missing semigroup index can be the leading index of an element of Rw.

The simple projected lattice therefore has exactly (m−1)(n−1)/2 missing leading indices. Completeness turns successive leading elimination into the usual lattice basis and colength calculation.

The first terms of (F^n−V^m)w cancel at index mn. For each k>mn choose the unique positive a(k),b(k) satisfying

    a(k)n+b(k)m=k,       −n<a(k)−b(k)≤m.

The weight equation and coprimality determine the difference modulo m+n. The displayed interval selects it uniquely; k>mn ensures both exponents are positive. Successive cancellation constructs

    A=F^n−V^m+sum_(k>mn)[alpha_k] V^a(k) F^b(k)

with Aw=0. Because a(k)−b(k) is bounded, min(a(k),b(k)) tends to infinity. The correction sum is an element of R with complete coefficients, not merely an arbitrary formal series whose action or convergence remains to be justified.

## 2. Why this annihilator generates the kernel

Expand B in R in normal monomials and assume Bw=0. At its smallest nonzero weight k, at least two exponent pairs must occur: a single pair would leave a nonzero leading coefficient of Bw. At a fixed weight, the differences a−b of distinct pairs are spaced by m+n.

Choose a term with maximal difference, say (a,b). Another pair has smaller difference, so a≥m. Subtract an appropriate left multiple of V^(a−m)F^b A to remove that term at weight k. The other leading term of A moves the exponent difference down by m+n. All correction terms have weight greater than k. Repeating reduces the finite width of the current weight and removes it entirely. Carry terms likewise have higher weight.

Crucially, exponent differences stay within the original bounded interval throughout this reduction: after multiplying by V^(a−m)F^b, the correction differences lie strictly above a−b−(m+n) and at most a−b. The two leading differences are the endpoints a−b−(m+n) and a−b. At each stage the former endpoint is no smaller than the existing minimum because at least two pairs were present. The quotient terms also remain in a bounded difference range.

Iteration through increasing weights consequently gives a convergent C in R with B=CA. If v is the original vector in several simple factors and w its first projection, every element of Rv with zero first projection is therefore in RAv. Conversely Av has zero first projection. Thus the projection kernel is cyclic, generated by Av.

## 3. Correction weights on another slope

Suppose the first slope m/(m+n) is no larger than m_j/(m_j+n_j). Equivalently m_j/n_j≥m/n. For every correcting monomial with an+bm>mn,

    a n_j+b m_j = n_j(a+b m_j/n_j)
                    ≥ n_j(a+b m/n) > m n_j.

This directly proves the necessary cross-slope correction bound. On a strictly higher slope, V^m has smaller leading index m n_j than F^n, whose index is n m_j. Its leading coefficient is a nonzero signed sigma^(−m) power of the old one. Independence over F_(q^(m_j+n_j)) is preserved because sigma permutes that field.

On another copy of the same slope, the new leading coefficient at index mn is

    beta_i = sigma^n(c_i)−sigma^(−m)(c_i),

where the first normalized coefficient was c_1=1. If sum_(i>1)d_i beta_i=0 with d_i in F_(q^(m+n)), applying sigma^m gives

    (sigma^(m+n)−1)(sum_(i>1) sigma^m(d_i)c_i)=0.

The parenthesized sum belongs to F_(q^(m+n)). This gives a relation among 1,c_2,... over that same finite field, contradicting their independence unless every d_i=0. This argument works for arbitrary q and does not depend on identifying q with p.

## 4. Induction and length

The kernel vector thus has independent leading coefficients on every remaining isotypic block and has its indices shifted by m n_j on simple factor j. Shifting one simple canonical basis by an integer d increases its lattice colength by d. By induction the kernel is full rank in the remaining factors and has colength

    sum_(j>1)m n_j + C(remaining factors).

The first projected factor is full rank and has colength (m−1)(n−1)/2. The exact sequence for projection of the lattice quotient adds these lengths. This proves both full rank and the stated multi-slope formula.

Only completeness, residue-field leading arithmetic, bijective q-Frobenius, the skew relations, integer weights, and finite-field independence enter this calculation. The carry rule for Witt vectors satisfies the required filtration property; constant coefficients in k[[t]] satisfy it as well. This is the substantive reason the cyclic formula transfers to both rings. A resemblance of the notation alone would not have sufficed.
