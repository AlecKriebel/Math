# Nonbasic a-number-one strata: a saturation reduction

**Problem:** 30002950, Chen–Viehmann Conjecture 5.1. **Outcome:** partial result; the full nonbasic single-orbit assertion is not proved or disproved. No novelty claim is made for the reductions below.

## 1. Exact setup and reference-lattice convention

Let k be an algebraic closure of F_q, and let (O,L,epsilon,sigma) be either (W(k),W(k)[1/p],p,Frob_q) or (k[[t]],k((t)),t,Frob_q), with sigma(epsilon)=epsilon. Work only with GL_h, h>0. A Dieudonne lattice M in N is O-free of rank h, spans N, and satisfies Phi M subset M and V M subset M, where Phi=b sigma and V=epsilon Phi^{-1}. For minuscule mu=(1^m,0^{h-m}), the lattices in X_mu(b) satisfy these conditions, with length(M/Phi M)=m. Define

    a(M)=dim_k M/(Phi M+V M).

Use the slope-adapted **minimal reference lattice M0** of Chen–Viehmann Section 5: each simple summand of slope m_i/(m_i+n_i) has basis e_{i,l}, l in Z, with e_{i,l+h_i}=epsilon e_{i,l}, Phi e_{i,l}=e_{i,l+m_i}, and V e_{i,l}=e_{i,l+n_i}; M0 is generated by l>=0. Repeated simple summands are listed separately. This convention matters. Chen–Viehmann Remark 2.1 explains that changing b arbitrarily while keeping an unrelated standard reference lattice fixed can change the stratification. We do not assert invariance under that operation.

Let J=Aut(N,Phi), and f_M(j)=inv(jM0,M). The desired conclusion is existence of a function f:J→Z^h_dom for which, for **every** M in X_mu(b),

    a(M)=1  if and only if  f_M belongs to J·f.

This is an orbit of invariant functions, hence of strata. It is not point transitivity and does not permit replacing the a=1 locus by a smaller generic subset. The saturated-locus result below does not establish that there is only one orbit.

## 2. Minimal hull and a generic cyclic sublattice

Write N=N_0 direct-sum N_bi direct-sum N_1, where N_bi has slopes strictly between 0 and 1. Each Dieudonne lattice has the corresponding integral splitting

    M=M_0 direct-sum M_bi direct-sum M_1.

Here the subscript 0 on M_0 denotes the ordinary slope-zero factor, not the reference M0. One algebraic justification, valid in both characteristics, is the Fitting decomposition for the semilinear endomorphism Phi on each finite-length module M/epsilon^r M. The stabilized image is its invertible part, and the complementary kernel is nilpotent. These decompositions are compatible with reduction in r. Their inverse limits split off the slope-zero lattice. Applying the same argument to V on the remaining factor splits off slope one. This uses the perfect residue field and bijectivity of sigma. Consequently a(M)=a(M_bi); the ordinary factors contribute zero.

On an interior isotypic summand of slope m/h choose integers a,b with ah+bm=1, and put

    tau=epsilon^{-m} Phi^h,        pi=epsilon^a Phi^b.

They satisfy pi^h=epsilon tau^b and Phi=pi^m tau^a. There is a smallest lattice P(M_bi) containing M_bi and stable under the slope projections and these operators. To construct it, take the O-span of all iterated images. This span is bounded by epsilon^{-c}M0 for some c, because all the operators preserve the canonical M0. Noetherianity makes it a lattice. Stability under tau is equality: tau has determinant valuation zero.

On each isotypic summand a tau- and pi-stable lattice is a J-translate of its minimal reference lattice. This is precisely the elementary basis argument in Chen–Viehmann Lemma 5.10 applied separately to each slope. For clarity, choose tau-fixed lifts of a basis modulo pi; over k these lifts exist by Frobenius descent. Their pi iterates through exponents 0,...,h-1 form a basis, with pi^h acting by epsilon and Phi shifting the basis index by m. The resulting change-of-basis map commutes with Phi. Thus P(M_bi)=j_bi M0_bi. Every minimal lattice containing M_bi is stable under the same operators, so P(M_bi) is the unique smallest one.

For the ordinary factors set P(M)_0=M_0 and P(M)_1=M_1. They are already J-translates of the corresponding reference factors, by Frobenius descent. Altogether P(M) is the unique smallest lattice of the form jM0 containing M. This argument is for **all** M, not only cyclic M.

There is a vector v in M_bi whose cyclic Dieudonne submodule Dv has the same minimal hull as M_bi. Here Dv means the O-submodule generated by all Phi^a V^b v, a,b>=0. To see the assertion, normalize P(M_bi)=M0_bi. For a slope m_z/h_z with multiplicity l_z, let U_z be the image of M_bi in

    M0_z/pi_z M0_z = k^{l_z}.

The subspace U_z cannot be contained in a hyperplane defined over F_{q^{h_z}}. The inverse image of such a hyperplane would be a proper tau_z- and pi_z-stable lattice; together with the other slope factors, it would contradict minimality of P(M_bi). There are only finitely many such rational hyperplanes. Since k is infinite, choose v avoiding their inverse images for every z. Its leading coefficients in each isotypic factor are then linearly independent over F_{q^{h_z}}. The Moore determinant criterion shows that v generates the full quotient modulo pi_z under tau_z; Nakayama's lemma gives P(Dv)=M0_bi. The cyclic colength lemma in the next section also proves that Dv has full rank.

## 3. The imported colength formula and its scope

List the simple factors of N_bi in nondecreasing slope order, with coprime positive pairs (m_i,n_i). Define

    C(N_bi)=Σ_i ((m_i-1)(n_i-1)/2) + Σ_{i<j} m_i n_j.

Equivalently the pair term is min(m_i n_j,m_j n_i), without choosing an order. For repeated equal slopes it is m_i n_i per pair. In particular the mixed-slope terms must **not** be omitted.

**Cyclic colength lemma.** If a vector v has minimal hull M0_bi, then Dv is a lattice and

    length(M0_bi/Dv)=C(N_bi),       a(Dv)=1.

For O=W(k) and sigma=Frob_p, the length assertion is Viehmann, *Moduli spaces of p-divisible groups*, arXiv:math/0502320v3, Theorem 4.11(i)–(ii), equation (4.21), with g=1. Its hypothesis (4.15) is exactly the leading-coefficient independence just established. The theorem explicitly permits multiple slopes; Section 4.1 orders the distinct slopes, and only bi-infinitesimality, not isoclinicity, is imposed through Section 4.4. Ordinary factors are discussed separately in Section 4.5.

Here are the algebraic details of the transfer to Frob_q and to k[[t]], rather than silently treating the original p-divisible-group theorem as a statement in those settings. Expand coefficients in epsilon with Teichmuller representatives in mixed characteristic and with constant coefficients in equal characteristic. In both settings sigma acts on leading coefficients by q-th power, fixes epsilon, and is bijective. The leading-term elimination used in Viehmann Lemmas 4.12–4.13 therefore takes place in the same completed skew-operator algebra

    Phi c=sigma(c)Phi,  V c=sigma^{-1}(c)V,  Phi V=V Phi=epsilon.

The steps needed for the length assertion are as follows.

1. On a simple slope m/(m+n), the projection of Dv has possible leading indices am+bn, a,b>=0. Indices missing from this numerical semigroup number (m-1)(n-1)/2. The elimination has leading annihilator Phi^n−V^m after scaling the first leading coefficient to 1; all correction monomials V^a Phi^b have an+bm>mn. Successive cancellation converges epsilon-adically. The division argument of Lemma 4.13(iii), ordered by an+bm and then a−b, shows that this annihilator generates the kernel of projection as a left operator ideal. It uses no characteristic-zero operation.
2. Remove the first simple factor in the ordered list. On every higher-slope factor (m_j,n_j), the first term of the resulting kernel generator has index m_i n_j. Indeed n_i m_j>m_i n_j, and every correction term has strictly larger index by Lemma 4.12. On another factor of the same slope, its new leading coefficient is the difference of the q^{n_i} and q^{-m_i} powers of the old one. Independence over F_{q^{h_i}} of the remaining coefficients is retained: a rational linear dependence of these differences would give a dependence of the original coefficients together with the normalized first coefficient 1. This is the only finite-field step; replacing p by q changes the fixed field accordingly.
3. The kernel is cyclic on the remaining summands, with each basis index shifted by m_i n_j. Induction, together with additivity of length for the exact sequence consisting of the kernel and projected lattice, gives

       C(N)=((m_i-1)(n_i-1)/2)+Σ_{j>i}m_i n_j+C(N without i).

This proves full rank and the stated formula. Completeness, the leading-coefficient rules, and the skew relations hold in both coefficient rings. No display deformation or geometric dimension theorem is used in this transfer.

Finally Dv/(Phi Dv+V Dv) is generated by the class of v, hence has dimension at most one. It cannot be zero: Phi and V are both topologically nilpotent on a lattice with all slopes in (0,1), and their commuting products of sufficiently large total degree map it into epsilon times itself. Iterating a hypothetical equality Dv=Phi Dv+V Dv and applying Nakayama would give Dv=0. Thus a(Dv)=1.

## 4. Maximal colength characterizes a=1, in both directions

Assume N_bi is nonzero. For every Dieudonne lattice M,

    d(M):=length(P(M)/M) <= C(N_bi),
    a(M)=1  if and only if  d(M)=C(N_bi).                 (1)

The ordinary splitting reduces this to M_bi. Choose the cyclic sublattice M'=Dv subset M_bi with P(M')=P(M_bi). The cyclic colength lemma gives

    C(N_bi)=length(P(M_bi)/M')
           =length(P(M_bi)/M_bi)+length(M_bi/M').

This proves the bound. If equality holds, length(M_bi/M')=0, so M_bi=M' and a(M)=1. Conversely if a(M)=1, a lift of a basis of M_bi/(Phi M_bi+V M_bi) generates M_bi as a Dieudonne module. The proof is the same topological Nakayama iteration: words of large degree land in arbitrarily high powers of epsilon. Applying the cyclic colength lemma to this generator proves equality. No basicness assumption appears in either direction.

**Saturation consequence.** The function f_M determines whether M subset jM0: this holds exactly when every coordinate of f_M(j) is nonnegative. It therefore determines the full set of minimal lattices containing M, including its unique smallest member P(M)=j_0M0. It also determines

    d(M)=Σ_r (f_M(j_0))_r.

Equation (1) now shows that a=1 is constant on each J-stratum. It is also constant on each J-orbit of strata, since P(jM)=jP(M) and d(jM)=d(M). This proves the full-locus saturation assertion in the genuinely nonbasic setting. It does **not** show that the saturated locus has only one J-orbit.

If N_bi=0, every lattice is ordinary and a=0. Do not apply (1) in that case: d=0=C would otherwise give a false criterion. The literal existence-of-function formulation of the conjecture has no nonempty-orbit requirement. For example the constant-zero function is not f_M for any lattice, since

    f_M(epsilon^r I)=f_M(1)−r(1,...,1).

Its J-orbit is also constant-zero, so it realizes the required empty locus.

## 5. A nonbasic class reduced to the known basic case

If N_bi has only one Newton slope, arbitrary slope-zero and slope-one summands cause no new single-orbit obstruction. Normalize the ordinary lattice factors using J_0 and J_1. The known Chen–Viehmann Proposition 5.11 applies to the interior isoclinic factor. Relative positions of direct-sum lattices are the sorted union of the relative positions of the factors, and J is the product of the three automorphism groups. Thus any two a=1 functions are in one J-orbit. The saturation consequence excludes a>1 lattices from that orbit. This proves the conjecture for this nonbasic class as a formal consequence of the published basic theorem, without claiming a new resolution.

The remaining case has at least two distinct slopes strictly between zero and one.

## 6. Explicit obstruction to a slopewise direct-sum argument

Take two simple summands with pairs (m,n)=(1,2) and (2,1), using the canonical bases e_0,e_1,e_2 and f_0,f_1,f_2. This construction works in both characteristics. Let

    L=O(e_0+f_0)+O e_1+O e_2+O f_1+O f_2+O epsilon f_0.

It has colength one in M0. It is Phi- and V-stable, and it is generated as a Dieudonne lattice by v=e_0+f_0. In fact

    Phi v=e_1+f_2,        V v=e_2+f_1,
    Phi^2 v=e_2+epsilon f_1,  V^2 v=epsilon e_1+f_2.

Since 1−epsilon is a unit, these identities recover e_1,f_2,e_2,f_1; applying Phi to e_2 and V to f_2 recovers epsilon e_0 and epsilon f_0. Hence a(L)=1. Both slope projections of L equal their full minimal lattices, but L is not their direct sum. The direct sum M0 has a=2. This is not a counterexample to the conjecture. It is an exact adverse control against a proof that adds the a-numbers of the slope projections or drops the pair terms in C: here both individual terms are zero and C=m_1 n_2=1.

## 7. What remains unresolved

The argument determines only the minimal hull and one length. The entire f_M contains relative positions against every jM0, equivalently all the intersection volumes in Chen–Viehmann Example 2.2. Establishing (1) does not make those additional data constant, or show that they differ only by J-translation.

In particular the induction in Chen–Viehmann Proposition 5.9 has an isoclinic step in which a selected operator applied to a generator has the same valuation in every simple summand. In mixed slopes Phi raises the indices by the vector (m_i), and V by (n_i), so that step cannot be copied. The explicit lattice above already shows why projections alone lose essential gluing data. The missing assertion is a uniform description, up to J, of all these intersection volumes for normalized cyclic lattices with multiple interior slopes.

A bounded exact GF(4) computation additionally tested 12 cyclic generators of slope types (2,3),(3,2), through epsilon^4 and 144 component-threshold profiles. All had colength six and a=1 and the same sampled profiles. This is compatibility evidence only: it does not cover all coefficients, all thresholds, all J, multiplicities, or mixed characteristic, and supplies no universal orbit proof.
