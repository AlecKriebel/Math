## 1. Exact target and scope

Let F be freely generated by a,b,c, and let phi(a)=a, phi(b)=ab,
phi(c)=bcb. The target is a proper cocompact action of

    G = <a,b,c,t | tat^-1=a, tbt^-1=ab, tct^-1=bcb>

on a CAT(0) cube complex. We use the convention txt^-1=phi(x).
This is the exact statement in the pinned canonical record for AIM 5.2.
The workshop summary instead discusses b -> aba. We do not silently identify
that different formula with this one; see SOURCE_GATE.md.

We construct a finite nonpositively curved cube complex Z with one vertex,
seven edges, eight squares, and two three-dimensional cubes, and prove that
pi_1(Z) is G. Its universal cover supplies the requested action.

Capital letters in words below mean inverses of the corresponding lowercase
letters. Names of vertices A,B,C,D are unrelated to this word convention.

## 2. The base square complex

Let K have one vertex, three oriented unit edges x,y,b, and two unit squares
with boundary words

    x y X Y,        b x B Y.

Thus

    H = pi_1(K) = <x,y,b | [x,y]=1, b x b^-1=y>.

Its vertex link has vertices x+,x-,y+,y-,b+,b-. Its edges are all four
x-sign/y-sign pairs, together with

    {b-,x+}, {b-,x-}, {b+,y+}, {b+,y-}.

This is a simple bipartite graph, so it is flag. By the cubical link criterion,
K is nonpositively curved. In particular its universal cover X is a CAT(0)
cube complex, and H acts freely, properly, and cocompactly on X.

The substitutions

    lambda(x)=y, lambda(y)=x, lambda(b)=b^-1

define a cubical involution lambda:K -> K. They send each square boundary to
the boundary of the same square, with a suitable change of starting corner
and orientation. In particular lambda is an isometry of K.

## 3. A compact cyclic local isometry

Define a square complex Q with four distinct vertices A,B,C,D and six
oriented edges:

| Edge | Initial vertex | Terminal vertex | Image label in K |
|---|---|---|---|
| p | B | A | x |
| q | A | C | y |
| r | D | C | x |
| s | B | D | y |
| d | A | B | b |
| e | C | A | b |

Attach two squares with boundary edge words

    p q r^-1 s^-1,       d p e^-1 q^-1.

The first begins at B, and the second at A. Their image boundary words are
xyXY and bxBY, respectively. Consequently the labels define a cubical map
j:Q -> K.

We verify that j is a local isometry, without assuming the existence or the
shape of a convex hull. The image of the link at each vertex of Q is:

| Vertex | Link vertices | Link edges, written as a path |
|---|---|---|
| A | b-,x-,y+,b+ | b- -- x- -- y+ -- b+ |
| B | b-,x+,y+ | b- -- x+ -- y+ |
| C | x-,y-,b+ | x- -- y- -- b+ |
| D | x+,y- | x+ -- y- |

Each link map is injective, and each displayed path is the **full induced
subcomplex** on its vertices in the link of K. Thus j is a cubical local
isometry. The same is true of lambda composed with j.

To compute pi_1(Q), take the three edges p,q,s as a spanning tree. Collapsing
the tree makes the first square kill r and the second identify d with e.
There are no further relators. Therefore pi_1(Q,A) is infinite cyclic,
generated by the loop d p. Its two images under our local isometries are

    j_*(d p)=b x,        (lambda j)_*(d p)=b^-1 y.

This local-isometry construction is the new ingredient beyond the prior
conditional axis-matching reduction.

## 4. The compact three-dimensional complex

Form Z by taking the disjoint union of K and Q times [0,1] and identifying

    (q,0) with j(q),        (q,1) with lambda(j(q)).

The interval is a unit edge and Q times [0,1] has the product cubical
structure. This is the usual graph-of-cube-complexes construction; the
attaching maps need not be globally injective. We verify its link directly,
so global injectivity is not an unstated hypothesis.

Both ends of all four vertical edges have been identified with K's unique
vertex. Write their eight link germs as z_A+,z_A-,...,z_D+,z_D-.
The link L of Z consists of the link of K, plus one cone for each of those
eight germs. For a positive germ the cone base is the corresponding image
of the link of Q under j; for a negative germ it is the image under lambda j
(interchanging positive/negative conventions has no effect on the argument).
Different cone apices are not adjacent. Every cone base is a full
subcomplex of the link of K, as established in Section 3.

Consider any clique of vertices of L. If it contains no cone apex, it is a
clique in the flag link of K. If it contains an apex, it contains exactly one,
and all its other vertices lie in that apex's full cone base. The latter
vertices span a simplex of the base, and adjoining the apex gives a simplex
of L. Thus L is flag. There are no duplicate edges or triangles: the link
maps in Section 3 are injective and the apices are distinct.

It follows that Z is a finite nonpositively curved cube complex of dimension
three. For reference, the sole vertex link has 14 vertices, 32 edges and
16 triangles; it has no four-vertex clique. The two three-cubes are precisely
the products of the two squares of Q with the interval.

The cell count is

    #vertices=1, #edges=3+4=7, #squares=2+6=8, #three-cubes=2.

Its Euler characteristic is 0, as expected for a free-by-cyclic group.

## 5. Identifying the fundamental group exactly

Van Kampen applied to this graph of spaces gives

    pi_1(Z)=<H,z | z^-1 (b x) z = b^-1 y>.

Here z is the vertical edge at A, directed from the j end to the lambda j
end. The attaching subgroups are injective because j and lambda j are
local isometries. Alternatively the same presentation follows by eliminating
the other three vertical-edge generators from the six product-square
relations; the verifier records these relations explicitly.

Set

    t=x,       a=x y^-1,       c=(z x)^-1.

The change of generators is reversible:

    x=t,       y=a^-1 t,       z=c^-1 t^-1.

The two base relations become

    [a,t]=1,        b t b^-1=a^-1 t,

equivalently [a,t]=1 and t b t^-1=a b. To see the top relation without a
sign or conjugacy ambiguity, put u=b^-1 x and v=b x. The base relation
implies b^-1 y=x b^-1=x u x^-1. Hence

    z^-1 v z=x u x^-1
    if and only if
    (z x) u (z x)^-1=v
    if and only if
    c^-1 (b^-1 t) c=b t.

The last relation is equivalent, by multiplication on the indicated sides,
to t c t^-1=b c b. Thus pi_1(Z) is exactly G, not a power of its monodromy,
a finite-index subgroup, or the nearby b -> aba mapping torus.

By the cubical Cartan-Hadamard theorem, the universal cover of Z is CAT(0).
Because Z is finite, that cover is locally finite and the deck action is
proper and cocompact. This proves the requested affirmative conclusion,
subject to the stated review and source-verification qualifications.

## 6. Relation to the convex-hull route

The argument above does not need to identify a hull. The lift of j is a
convex cyclic-cocompact subcomplex of X. It can also be visualized explicitly:
put v=bx and g_i=v^i. In the vertex flat g_i<x,y>, take the unit square with
x-coordinate in [-1,0] and y-coordinate in [0,1]. The annulus square between
that flat and g_{i+1}<x,y> joins the first square's right side to the next
square's bottom side. The bi-infinite chain has two squares per v-period.

For the lift of lambda fixing the identity vertex, the isometry
f=b^-1 lambda satisfies f^2=1 and f u f^-1=v, because
lambda(u)=b y=b v b^-1. This also explains why compatible cyclic convex
subcomplexes exist here. But Sections 3--5 are a completely finite
construction, and do not depend on an unproved general cocompactness theorem.

## 7. Checks and limitations

- verify.py derives all links from the specified attaching words and endpoints.
  It checks face boundaries, local injection/fullness, the involution,
  the complete 14-vertex flag link, and the cell counts using exact finite
  combinatorics. It is not a numerical or exhaustive search.
- The global conclusion uses the standard cubical link criterion and
  Cartan-Hadamard theorem, as stated in Bridson--Haefliger, *Metric Spaces of
  Non-Positive Curvature*, Chapter II.5 (cubical link condition), and II.4
  (Cartan-Hadamard). The finite presentation calculation is written above.
- A geometric cubulation does not establish virtual specialness here.
- The candidate settles only the displayed ab,bcb formulation. Source
  recovery and historical novelty remain separate issues. The known free
  cubulation and quasi-isometry results are credited in SOURCE_GATE.md.
- No claim is made that a two-dimensional geometric cubulation is impossible.
