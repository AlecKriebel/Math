# Ordinary thick closure of representation spheres for finite groups

## Scope and conclusions

This AI-assisted note reconstructs and audits an inherited partial result for AIM Problem 2.5, attributed there to J. Greenlees. It does not claim discovery or priority. The target is to characterize the ordinary thick subcategory generated by representation spheres in equivariant spectra.

The problem's sentence does not specify that $G$ is finite. Its surrounding section discusses finite groups, and extensions to other types of groups have a separate section. The results below adopt the explicit finite-group, genuine-equivariant, ordinary-thick interpretation. They are a partial answer to the original question, not a complete integral classification. [AIM]

Let $G$ be finite. Write $\mathcal C_G=(Sp^G)^\omega$ for compact genuine $G$-spectra indexed on a complete universe. Here "finite spectra" means the idempotent-complete category of finite genuine cell spectra: compact objects are retracts of finite constructions from integer suspensions of the orbit spectra $\Sigma^\infty G/H_+$. We abbreviate the latter to $G/H_+$. Orbit spectra are compact generators of the genuine category. [MNN, Example 5.11 in the inspected author version]

An ordinary thick subcategory is full and closed under finite sums, cofiber sequences, integer suspension and desuspension, and retracts. Arbitrary smash products or arbitrary colimits are not closure operations. Define

$$
\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}=\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal C_G}\{S^V: V\text{ actual finite-dimensional real }G\text{-representation}\},
$$
$$
\mathcal T_G=\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal C_G}\{S^\alpha:\alpha\in RO(G)\}.
$$

An invariant inner product is available because $G$ is finite. For actual $V$, $S^V$ is its based one-point compactification. If $\alpha=V-W$, then $S^\alpha=S^V\wedge S^{-W}$ in the genuine category. These spheres are compact. For example, compactness of the unit and invertibility of $S^\alpha$ imply compactness, since its inverse smash functor preserves colimits. See also [GQ, Lemma 1.1].

**Theorem A.** For every finite group $G$ the following are equivalent:

1. $\mathcal T_G=\mathcal C_G$.
2. $\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}=\mathcal C_G$.
3. $G\cong(C_2)^r$ for some $r\geq 0$.

If (3) fails, the compact free orbit $G/e_+$ belongs to neither closure. Its exclusion is already detected rationally.

**Theorem B.** For each subgroup $H\leq G$, put $W_H=N_G(H)/H$ and

$$
o_H(\alpha)=\det(\alpha^H)\in\operatorname{Hom}(W_H,\{\pm1\}),\qquad
D_H(G)=\operatorname{im}(o_H:RO(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_H,\{\pm1\})).
$$

Let $\mathbb Q_\omega$ denote the rational one-dimensional representation with character $\omega$. In the compact rational genuine category, set

$$
\mathcal T_{G,\mathbb Q}=\operatorname{Thick}_{(Sp^G_{\mathbb Q})^\omega}
\{S^\alpha_{\mathbb Q}:\alpha\in RO(G)\}.
$$

Using the geometric-fixed-point realization of the rational algebraic model, one has

$$
(Sp^G_{\mathbb Q})^\omega\simeq
\prod_{(H)\leq G}\operatorname{Perf}(\mathbb Q[W_H]),
$$
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal T_{G,\mathbb Q}\simeq
\prod_{(H)\leq G}\operatorname{add}
\{\Sigma^n\mathbb Q_\omega:n\in\mathbb Z,\ \omega\in D_H(G)\}.
\quad}
$$

The product is over conjugacy classes, hence is finite. Here $\operatorname{add}$ permits finite direct sums and their direct summands. Equivalently, in every degree, every simple $\mathbb Q[W_H]$-constituent of $\pi_*(\Phi^H X)$ must be an allowed orientation line. Since $X$ is rational, another tensor with $\mathbb Q$ is unnecessary. The same formula holds if only actual representations are used as generators.

## 1. The coordinate-face construction

Let $E=(C_2)^r$, let $K\leq E$, and put $d=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(E/K)$. For $d>0$, choose a basis $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_d$ of the characters of $E/K$ and inflate it to $E$. Regarding these characters as one-dimensional real sign representations, form

$$
V_K=\chi_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\chi_d.
$$

The map $E/K\to\{\pm1\}^d$ furnished by the characters is an isomorphism. Thus $E/K$ acts by independent coordinate sign changes on $V_K=\mathbb R^d$.

For nonempty $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}$ and a sign choice $\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}^I$, define a face of the unit sphere by

$$
C(I,\epsilon)=\{x\in S(V_K):x_i\ne0\iff i\in I,
\quad \operatorname{sign}(x_i)=\epsilon_i\text{ for }i\in I\}.
$$

The closed simplex with coordinates $t_i\geq0$, $\sum_{i\in I}t_i=1$, maps homeomorphically onto the closure of this face by

$$
x_i=\epsilon_i\sqrt{t_i}\ (i\in I),\qquad x_j=0\ (j\notin I).
$$

Its interior is $C(I,\epsilon)$ and its boundary consists of the corresponding proper-support faces. Consequently these are genuine cells of dimension $|I|-1$, rather than merely an isotropy partition.

The stabilizer of the entire face, also its pointwise stabilizer, is

$$
L_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}\ker\chi_i.
$$

In particular,

$$
K\leq L_I,\qquad [E:L_I]=2^{|I|},\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(E/L_I)=|I|.
$$

Independence of the characters implies that $E$ acts transitively on the $2^{|I|}$ choices of $\epsilon$ for fixed $I$. The simplex parameters $t_i$ are preserved by this action. The cell attachment is therefore of the form $E/L_I\times D^{|I|-1}$ with trivial stabilizer action on the disk; no representation twist has been omitted.

Let $F_n$ be the union of faces of dimension at most $n$, with $F_{-1}=\varnothing$. This gives a finite $E$-CW filtration of $S(V_K)$ and based cofiber identifications

$$
F_{n,+}/F_{n-1,+}\cong
\bigvee_{|I|=n+1}(E/L_I)_+\wedge S^n
\qquad(0\leq n\leq d-1).
\tag{1}
$$

For $I$ proper, $L_I$ strictly contains $K$. In top dimension there is exactly one support set, $I=\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and $L_I=K$. Thus

$$
F_{d-1,+}/F_{d-2,+}\cong\Sigma^{d-1}E/K_+.
\tag{2}
$$

For $d=1$, this is interpreted using $F_{-1,+}=*$, whose suspension spectrum is zero. The unit sphere itself is the orbit $E/K$ in this case.

## 2. Integral generation for elementary abelian groups

We prove $E/K_+\in\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$ by induction on $d=\dim(E/K)$, simultaneously for all subgroups $K$.

When $d=0$, $K=E$ and $E/E_+=S^0$ is a generator. Suppose $d>0$ and the assertion holds whenever the quotient rank is smaller than $d$.

The inclusion of the unit sphere in the unit disk yields a based cofiber sequence. Since the disk contracts equivariantly to its origin, the sequence is

$$
S(V_K)_+\longrightarrow S^0\longrightarrow S^{V_K}.
\tag{3}
$$

Both right-hand terms belong to $\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$, so closure under triangles gives $S(V_K)_+\in\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$.

For every cell of $F_{d-2}$, equation (1) involves an orbit $E/L_I$ with quotient rank $|I|<d$. The induction hypothesis puts each $E/L_I{}_+$ in $\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$. Starting with the zero spectrum $F_{-1,+}$, the finite skeletal cofiber sequences show successively that every $F_{n,+}$ with $n\leq d-2$ lies there. This argument uses the existing attaching maps; it requires neither their splitting nor their nullity.

Taking the cofiber of $F_{d-2,+}\to S(V_K)_+$ and applying (2) gives $\Sigma^{d-1}E/K_+\in\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$. Ordinary integer desuspension gives $E/K_+\in\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}$.

All orbit generators have now been obtained integrally. Finite cell constructions and retracts give every compact genuine $E$-spectrum. Therefore

$$
\mathcal T_E^{\rm act}=\mathcal T_E=\mathcal C_E.
\tag{4}
$$

The construction used actual representations only. No induction functor was assumed to preserve the subcategory; "induction" here was induction on the integer $d$. No tensor-ideal closure was used.

## 3. A homology obstruction for other finite groups

Let $U:Sp^G\to\operatorname{Fun}(BG,Sp)$ forget the genuine indexing while retaining the $G$-action. For a field $k$ whose characteristic does not divide $|G|$, consider the graded $k[G]$-module

$$
H_*(UX;k)=\pi_*(Hk\wedge UX).
$$

The use of the $G$-module, rather than just the underlying vector space or its invariants, is essential.

For $\alpha=V-W\in RO(G)$, this homology of $S^\alpha$ is concentrated in degree $\dim\alpha$ and is the line with character

$$
\det(\alpha)=\det(V)\det(W)^{-1}:G\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}\longrightarrow k^\times.
\tag{5}
$$

For actual $V$, this follows from the degree of an orthogonal linear map on its one-point compactification. The virtual case follows by smash inversion, or the dual orientation line. Each one-dimensional determinant character has values $\pm1$ before reduction to $k$.

By Maschke's theorem $k[G]$ is semisimple. The modules whose simple constituents are determinant/sign lines form a subcategory closed under submodules, quotients, extensions and summands. Accordingly the compact spectra satisfying this condition in each homology degree form a thick subcategory. In a cofiber sequence $A\to B\to C$, the long exact homology sequence gives

$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(H_nA\to H_nB)
\longrightarrow H_nC
\longrightarrow\ker(H_{n-1}A\to H_{n-1}B)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
$$

This verifies the cofiber step; rotations give the other two-of-three cases. Shifts, finite sums and retracts preserve the condition as well. Equation (5) therefore imposes this necessary condition on every object of $\mathcal T_G$.

Take $k=\mathbb Q$. Every sign character is itself an actual one-dimensional real representation, so the allowed lines at the trivial subgroup are exactly all homomorphisms $G\to\{\pm1\}$. But

$$
H_0(U(G/e_+);\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q[G],
$$

with the faithful left regular action. If this module were a direct sum of sign lines, a basis adapted to that sum would make every group element diagonal with entries $\pm1$. Its image would consist of commuting involutions. Faithfulness would force $G$ to be abelian and every element to have order at most two. A finite such group is $(C_2)^r$.

Thus, when $G$ is not elementary abelian of exponent two, the regular module contains a simple constituent outside the sign lines. By (6), $G/e_+\notin\mathcal T_G$. Combined with (4) and $\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}\subseteq\mathcal T_G$, this proves Theorem A. The negative direction does not depend on the full rational algebraic model.

## 4. The full rational closure

### 4.1. The correct invariant at a subgroup

For nonnormal $H$, first restrict a genuine $G$-spectrum to $N_G(H)$ and take geometric $H$-fixed points. The result has residual genuine $W_H=N_G(H)/H$-structure. The rational invariant used in the $H$-factor retains the underlying $W_H$-action on its homotopy groups; it does not discard that action or apply categorical fixed points.

Geometric fixed points commute with representation suspension, giving

$$
\Phi^H(S^\alpha)\simeq S^{\alpha^H}.
$$

The subgroup $H$ acts trivially on the fixed space, so the normalizer action factors through $W_H$. Its rational homotopy is the orientation line in degree $\dim(\alpha^H)$. The factor invariant is therefore

$$
S^\alpha_{\mathbb Q}\longmapsto
\Sigma^{\dim(\alpha^H)}\mathbb Q_{o_H(\alpha)}.
\tag{7}
$$

The homotopy-theoretic model is supplied by Barnes, Theorem 8.1, and the geometric-fixed-point identification and orientation calculation are explicit in Greenlees--Quigley, Theorem 2.1 and Sections 3.2--3.3 of the published version. [B; GQ]

The model restricts to the compact subcategories. As there are finitely many subgroup conjugacy classes and each rational group algebra is semisimple and finite-dimensional, compact objects in the product are precisely finite products of perfect complexes, equivalently bounded graded finite-dimensional modules.

### 4.2. Why the factors can be separated in an ordinary thick category

The rational mark map identifies

$$
A(G)\otimes\mathbb Q\cong\prod_{(H)\leq G}\mathbb Q.
$$

Let $e_H$ be the primitive idempotent whose mark is one at $(H)$ and zero elsewhere. It is an idempotent endomorphism of the rational unit. Acting on a particular generator $S^\alpha_{\mathbb Q}$ gives an idempotent endomorphism of that generator. Its image is a retract of the generator and hence belongs to the ordinary thick closure. Under the model, it retains exactly the $H$-factor in (7). [B, Section 5]

This uses an allowed retract, not an assertion that smashing any member of the subcategory with an arbitrary spectrum stays inside it. An ambient telescope can construct the idempotent image, but the resulting object is a retract of a compact object, so no closure under infinite colimits is required of the thick subcategory.

For any $\omega\in D_H(G)$, select an $\alpha$ with $o_H(\alpha)=\omega$. The retract just obtained, followed by an ordinary integer shift by $n-\dim(\alpha^H)$, produces the object supported only at $H$ with value $\Sigma^n\mathbb Q_\omega$. Thus every displayed generator in Theorem B belongs to the rational thick closure, independently in each factor.

### 4.3. Why no additional simple modules occur

A bounded complex over a semisimple algebra splits, in its derived category, as the finite direct sum of its homology modules placed in their degrees. One can see this by choosing complements to the boundary and cycle submodules; the complementary acyclic pieces are contractible. Each homology module in turn splits as a finite sum of simple modules.

Consequently the category of complexes with simple constituents in a specified set is thick. Conversely, it is generated by all integer shifts of that set. Apply this fact separately to each $\mathbb Q[W_H]$ and the set of lines indexed by $D_H(G)$. Equation (7) gives containment in the claimed subcategory, and the isolated retracts from Section 4.2 give the reverse containment. This proves Theorem B.

There is no need to realize prescribed orientation characters at all subgroups simultaneously: the rational idempotents isolate individual factors. There is also no requirement that every sign character of $W_H$ occur; the exact set is the image $D_H(G)$ of global representations.

### 4.4. Actual representations and rationalization

If $\alpha=V-W$ with $V,W$ actual, then for every $H$

$$
o_H(\alpha)=\det(V^H)\det(W^H)^{-1}
             =\det(V^H)\det(W^H)=o_H(V\oplus W),
$$

since sign characters square to one. Integer shifts remove the difference between their fixed dimensions after each factor is isolated. Hence actual spheres give the same rational formula.

This argument does **not** assert $\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}=\mathcal T_G$ integrally for arbitrary finite $G$. Theorem A needs no such assertion.

Likewise, $\mathcal T_{G,\mathbb Q}$ is formed in the compact **rational** category. Rationalized spheres need not be compact in the integral category. Exact rationalization carries $\mathcal T_G$ into $\mathcal T_{G,\mathbb Q}$, and the latter is the thick closure of its image. Equality with the bare essential image is not required or asserted; target retracts may arise only after rationalization.

## 5. Rational membership cannot settle the integral problem

The gap is concrete, rather than only a lack of an argument.

**Proposition C.** If a finite $G$ is not elementary abelian of exponent two, choose an odd prime $\ell\nmid |G|$. Let $M_G(\ell)=\operatorname{cofib}(\ell:S_G^0\to S_G^0)$, and set

$$
X=G/e_+\wedge M_G(\ell).
$$

Then $X$ is compact, its genuine rationalization is zero, and $X\notin\mathcal T_G$ (hence $X\notin\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}$).

**Proof.** It is also the cofiber of multiplication by $\ell$ on the compact orbit $G/e_+$, so it is compact. Rationalization inverts $\ell$, making this cofiber zero. On mod-$\ell$ underlying homology, multiplication by $\ell$ is zero, so the cofiber has the regular $\mathbb F_\ell[G]$-module in degree zero and another copy in degree one. Since $\ell\nmid|G|$, Maschke applies. If every regular-module constituent were a reduced sign line, the faithful regular representation would again embed $G$ in a group of commuting involutions. This would make $G$ elementary abelian of exponent two, a contradiction. The thick obstruction of Section 3 therefore excludes $X$. $\square$

Thus the implication

$$
X_{\mathbb Q}\in\mathcal T_{G,\mathbb Q}\ \Longrightarrow\ X\in\mathcal T_G
$$

is false for every other finite group. Proposition C is an audit consequence of the same elementary detector, with no novelty claim. It does not classify all torsion objects in the closure or prove sufficiency of any collection of coefficient-field tests. For instance, $M_G(\ell)$ itself lies in the closure because it is a cofiber of a map between copies of $S_G^0$.

## 6. An exact orbit-level consequence

There is a second route to the positive construction which also describes all orbit spectra in the closure. It is important to justify its smash-product step rather than silently replace ordinary thick closure by tensor-ideal closure.

**Internal smash lemma.** Both $\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}$ and $\mathcal T_G$ are closed under smashing two of their own objects.

**Proof.** Let $\mathcal T$ be either subcategory. For a fixed generating sphere $S^V$, the objects $Y$ for which $S^V\wedge Y\in\mathcal T$ form a thick subcategory. It contains every generating sphere $S^W$, since $S^V\wedge S^W=S^{V+W}$ is a generator. Hence it contains $\mathcal T$. Now fix $Y\in\mathcal T$. The objects $X$ for which $X\wedge Y\in\mathcal T$ again form a thick subcategory, and the first argument shows that it contains every generating sphere. It therefore contains $\mathcal T$. This proves the lemma. It makes no assertion about smashing with objects outside $\mathcal T$. $\square$

**Proposition D.** For every finite group $G$ and subgroup $H\leq G$, the following are equivalent:

1. $G/H_+\in\mathcal T_G$.
2. $G/H_+\in\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}$.
3. $H$ is normal in $G$ and $G/H$ is elementary abelian of exponent two, allowing the trivial quotient.

**Proof of sufficiency.** Suppose $H$ is normal and $G/H\cong C_2^d$. For $d=0$ the orbit is the unit. Otherwise choose a basis $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_d$ of quotient sign characters and inflate them to $G$. The unit sphere of a sign line $\chi_i$ is $G/\ker\chi_i$. Its Euler triangle puts $G/\ker\chi_i{}_+$ in the actual-sphere closure. The internal smash lemma now puts their smash product there as well. The map of finite $G$-sets

$$
G/H\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^d G/\ker\chi_i,\qquad
 gH\longmapsto(g\ker\chi_i)_i,
$$

is a bijection: it is injective because the intersection of the kernels is $H$, and both sides have $2^d$ points. The based product identity identifies the suspension spectrum of its right-hand side with the smash of these based orbit spectra. Hence $G/H_+$ lies in $\mathcal T_G^{\rm act}$.

**Proof of necessity.** If $G/H_+\in\mathcal T_G$, its rational underlying permutation module $\mathbb Q[G/H]$ has only sign-line constituents by Section 3. Semisimplicity makes the action image a group of commuting involutions. Let $C$ be its kernel, namely the core of $H$. The image $G/C$ acts faithfully and transitively on $G/H$. In a transitive action of an abelian group, any element fixing one point fixes every point, so a faithful such action has trivial point stabilizers. Thus $H/C$ is trivial, giving $H=C$ normal. Its quotient $G/H$ is the elementary abelian action image. $\square$

For $E=C_2^r$, every subgroup is normal with elementary abelian quotient. Proposition D therefore independently confirms the positive part of Theorem A. This is a further consequence of the audited arguments, not a novelty claim. It sharpens the remaining gap: membership is now settled for orbit spectra themselves, while membership of arbitrary compact spectra built with integral attaching maps is not classified.

## 7. Small cases and exact remaining scope

- For $G=e$, the usual sphere thick-generates finite spectra; this is $r=0$.
- For $G=C_2$, the sign Euler sequence directly supplies $C_2/e_+\to S^0\to S^\sigma$.
- For $G=C_2^2$ and $K=e$, the four quadrants of a circle are one free orbit of one-cells. The two coordinate-axis vertex orbits have index-two stabilizers already covered by the preceding induction step.
- For $G=C_3$, $\mathbb Q[G]\cong\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ as modules, while only the trivial sign line occurs. The two-dimensional summand excludes the free orbit. Taking $\ell=5$ also produces the rationally invisible example in Proposition C.
- For $G=C_4$, the two-dimensional rational cyclotomic constituent excludes the free orbit even though a nontrivial sign character is available.

The established results completely decide **when the ordinary integral closure is the entire compact genuine category**, and completely describe **the closure in the rational compact category**. Proposition D also decides membership of every orbit spectrum for every finite group. A necessary-and-sufficient integral membership criterion for arbitrary compact spectra when $G$ is non-elementary-abelian is not established here. In particular, the interaction of integral lattices, torsion and isotropy attaching maps is not determined by Theorem B; Proposition C explicitly disproves a rational-only membership criterion.

These results do not resolve compact Lie, profinite or other interpretations of the unrestricted AIM question. For compact Lie groups, $G/H_+$ for closed $H$ still gives compact generators; compactness of those orbits is not the obstruction to applying this proof. The finite semisimple rational factor model, finite subgroup-class set, and finite-group regular-module argument do not simply carry over. A different group category must be specified and analyzed separately. The naive-equivariant category is also not being classified.

Finally, thick **tensor-ideal** generation is a different question. Since $S^0$ is among the generators, a tensor ideal in the compact category contains $S^0\wedge X=X$ for every compact $X$, for every finite group. That observation does not license such smash products in an ordinary thick construction. In the noncompact ambient category the same tensor-ideal observation concerns all objects, whereas the ordinary thick closure of compact generators still consists of compact objects.
