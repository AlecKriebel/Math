# Marked peripheral obstructions in the glued figure-eight problem

Problem **10900004 / AMR-108-0004**, Danciger's Question 1.4 in Delp--Hoffoss--Manning (2015).

**Disposition: unsolved, five substantive approaches.** No proof or counterexample for arbitrary gluing is obtained. All obstructions below concern explicitly stated construction mechanisms. None excludes every convex projective structure on any particular glued manifold. The elementary deductions are supplied with proofs and do not carry a historical novelty claim.

## Target, conventions, and known input

Let `M` be the compact exterior of the figure-eight knot, with boundary `T`. For each homeomorphism `f:T_1 -> T_2`, put `N_f = M_1 union_f M_2`. The target asks whether every such closed manifold admits a convex real projective structure. The compact exterior is intended when referring to the torus boundary of the knot complement.

Choose meridian/preferred-longitude bases `(mu_i,lambda_i)` on the two boundary tori. In this note, vectors are columns and

`A = [[a,b],[c,d]] in GL(2,Z)`

means

`f_*(mu_1) = mu_2^a lambda_2^c`,

`f_*(lambda_1) = mu_2^b lambda_2^d`.

Thus a slope column `(p,q)` is sent to `(ap+bq,cp+dq)`. These conventions matter: several displayed obstructions would involve `c` instead of `b` under a transposed convention. Every matrix in `GL(2,Z)` is induced by a torus homeomorphism, and its isotopy class determines the gluing. Both determinant signs are included. With fixed orientations on both pieces, oriented gluing reverses their induced boundary orientations. But in the present unoriented statement one can reverse the orientation of the second piece, so either sign produces an orientable closed manifold. In particular the identity gluing is the double, with the second copy oppositely oriented in the usual oriented-double convention.

A convex structure has an injective developing map with convex image in `RP^3`. A properly convex image is bounded in some affine chart. Strict convexity additionally prohibits nontrivial boundary segments. We do not silently impose strict convexity or finite Hilbert volume on either open piece. Our positive criterion produces properly convex structures and therefore would answer the stated convex question whenever its hypotheses can be established.

The imported geometric input is Ballas--Danciger--Lee [BDL], Theorems 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4: the figure-eight exterior has suitable properly convex structures with principal totally geodesic torus boundary, the double exists, and pieces with such boundary can be glued when their full peripheral holonomies are conjugate under the prescribed marking. Principal boundary includes the requisite convex thickening. This does not say that arbitrary boundary holonomies extend over `M`. Its precise use appears in Approach 2. See [SOURCE_AUDIT.md](SOURCE_AUDIT.md) for theorem locations and further literature.

## Approach 1: topology and a strict-convexity obstruction

### Proposition 1.1: homology separates an infinite family from the double

With the column convention above,

`H_1(N_A;Z) = Z/bZ`,

where `Z/0Z` means `Z`.

**Proof.** Each exterior has first homology `Z`, generated by its meridian, and its preferred longitude is null-homologous. The connected-intersection part of Mayer--Vietoris gives the quotient of `Z<mu_1> + Z<mu_2>` by the relations

`mu_1 - a mu_2 = 0`, `b mu_2 = 0`.

The first relation eliminates `mu_1`, leaving precisely the asserted cyclic group. The subsequent `H_0(T) -> H_0(M_1) + H_0(M_2)` is injective, so there is no additional free summand. Equivalently the relation matrix has Smith factors `1,|b|` when `b != 0`. QED.

For

`A_k = [[1,k],[0,1]]`,

one gets `H_1(N_{A_k}) = Z/kZ`. Thus `k=1` gives an integral homology sphere, while `k=2,3,...` gives mutually nonhomeomorphic manifolds distinguished by homology. The identity double has `H_1=Z`. This makes explicit why knowledge of the double cannot cover arbitrary gluing merely by forgetting the marking.

### Proposition 1.2: no member has a strictly properly convex structure

Each peripheral group injects into the hyperbolic knot group. Van Kampen identifies

`pi_1(N_A) = pi_1(M_1) *_{Z^2} pi_1(M_2)`,

where the edge embeddings are the peripheral inclusions, with the second precomposed by `A`. The normal-form theorem for amalgamated products injects the edge group, so `pi_1(N_A)` contains `Z^2` for every `A`.

If `N_A` were a closed strictly properly convex projective manifold, Benoist's strict-convexity/word-hyperbolicity theorem [B] would make its fundamental group word hyperbolic. Abelian subgroups of a word-hyperbolic group are virtually cyclic, contradicting the embedded `Z^2`.

This is a complete negative answer only to the *strictly properly convex variant*. It is fully compatible with the known double and is not a counterexample to Danciger's question. The torus necessarily survives; hyperbolic Dehn filling of a boundary torus is a different operation.

**Remaining gap.** Homology and the surviving flat torus do not obstruct the requested non-strict convex geometry.

## Approach 2: transfer from the double and the exact amalgamated matching problem

### Proposition 2.1: the known double transfers only through extendable markings

Suppose `h_i:M_i -> M_i` are homeomorphisms and `E_i` are their boundary matrices. Then

`N_A` is homeomorphic to `N_{E_2 A E_1^{-1}}`.

Indeed the piecewise map `(h_1,h_2)` respects the relation `x ~ f(x)` exactly when the new gluing is `h_2 f h_1^{-1}`. Consequently every gluing in the orbit of the identity under these left/right actions inherits the double's properly convex structure by pullback.

But an extendable boundary matrix preserves the kernel of

`H_1(T;Z) -> H_1(M;Z)`,

which is exactly `Z lambda`. Thus its second column is `(0,+1)` or `(0,-1)`. A product or inverse of such matrices still has upper-right entry zero. Hence `A_k` with `k != 0` cannot lie in this orbit. No classification of the figure-eight symmetry group is needed for this obstruction.

This proves a limitation of the topological transfer route, rather than an assertion that every upper-right-zero map extends.

### Proposition 2.2: exact representation extension

Let `rho_i:pi_1(M_i) -> PGL(4,R)` be specified representations. A representation of the amalgam whose restrictions are `rho_1` and a conjugate of `rho_2` exists if and only if there is `C in PGL(4,R)` satisfying

`rho_1(h) = C rho_2(Ah) C^{-1}` for every `h in Z^2`.    (2.1)

This is exactly the universal property of the amalgamated product: define the second restriction as `C rho_2 C^{-1}` and demand equality on the identified subgroup. Since the peripheral generators commute, equality on `mu_1` and `lambda_1` suffices, **with the same C**.

For arbitrary representations, (2.1) alone implies neither faithfulness, discreteness, a developing embedding, nor a preserved proper convex domain. The trivial representations satisfy it for every matrix and demonstrate this logical gap.

When each `rho_i` actually comes from a properly convex structure on the piece with principal totally geodesic torus boundary, [BDL, Theorem 1.4] supplies the missing geometric conclusion: (2.1) produces a properly convex structure on `N_A`. A smooth topological torus map, a torus's Euclidean cusp metric, or its projective surface holonomy alone is not a substitute for the full neighborhood holonomy in `PGL(4,R)`.

**Remaining gap.** Produce extending convex-piece representations satisfying (2.1) for every `A`, or rule out all possible convex structures for a particular `A`.

## Approach 3: exact simultaneous-character matching

This approach turns (2.1) into a finite exact test **after** candidate positive diagonal peripheral holonomies have been supplied. It does not find the extending representations.

Assume the peripheral actions admit simultaneous positive diagonal determinant-one lifts. Write

`rho_i(v) = diag(exp((W_i v)_1),...,exp((W_i v)_4))`,

where `W_i` is a real `4 x 2` matrix, its column sums are zero, and its four rows are distinct joint characters. A different logarithm base simply rescales both matrices by a fixed nonzero scalar.

### Proposition 3.1: simultaneous conjugacy criterion

The two marked peripheral representations satisfy (2.1) exactly when

`W_1 = P W_2 A`                                                   (3.1)

for one permutation matrix `P` of size four. If the prescribed principal triangle is spanned by the first three eigenlines on both sides and must be preserved, require `P` to fix the fourth line.

**Proof.** Distinct joint characters imply that the common eigenspaces of the whole `Z^2` action are precisely its four one-dimensional coordinate lines. A conjugacy must permute these lines. Equality of normalized positive eigenvalues then gives (3.1). A projective scalar cannot create an extra character: both lifts have determinant one and positive eigenvalues, so such a scalar has positive fourth power one and equals one. Conversely the appropriate permutation of coordinate lines realizes the equality. QED.

The same proof works even if each generator separately has repeated eigenvalues, provided the *joint* characters are distinct. Outside simultaneous positive diagonalizability, Jordan blocks and possible signs require additional information; this test is not claimed to apply there.

### Exact counterexample to matching each generator separately

Consider the commuting pairs of rational `SL(4,R)` matrices

`U = diag(8,2,1/16,1)`,

`V = diag(1,4,1/4,1)`,

`V' = diag(4,1,1/4,1)`.

The first pair is `(U,V)` and the second is `(U,V')`. The two meridian matrices are identical, and the longitude matrices have identical eigenvalue multisets. Yet the pairs are not simultaneously conjugate for the identity marking. Since `U` has four distinct eigenvalues, any conjugator fixing `U` is diagonal and therefore cannot change `V` to `V'`.

The base-two joint character matrices are

`W = [[3,0],[1,2],[-4,-2],[0,0]]`,

`W' = [[3,2],[1,0],[-4,-2],[0,0]]`.

These examples are not merely defective rank-one torus actions. For the open projective triangle

`T = {[x_1:x_2:x_3:0] : x_1,x_2,x_3 > 0}`,

the logarithmic coordinates `(log(x_1/x_3),log(x_2/x_3))` turn the actions into translations. Their lattice matrices, with the inessential factor `log 2` suppressed, are respectively

`[[7,2],[5,4]]` and `[[7,4],[5,2]]`,

of determinants `18` and `-6`. Thus both act freely, properly, and cocompactly on `T`. The fourth character is zero and the other three sum to zero and span the plane, so it lies strictly between their minimum and maximum on every nonzero real vector. They meet this peripheral middle-eigenvalue test. **No extension of either pair over the figure-eight group is asserted.**

### A quadratic invariant is necessary but incomplete

Set `Q_i = W_i^T W_i`. If `W_i` has rank two, `Q_i` is positive definite. Equation (3.1) implies

`Q_1 = A^T Q_2 A`.                                                 (3.2)

However (3.2) does not imply (3.1). Let

`W_0 = [[1,0],[0,1],[-1,-1],[0,0]]`.

The matrices `W_0` and `-W_0` have the same Gram matrix `[[2,1],[1,2]]`, but their row multisets differ, so no permutation identifies them for `A=I`. Both give full-rank triangle lattices with the zero transverse character. Thus metric-like or second-moment matching alone misses the marked joint spectrum.

**Remaining gap.** Character matrices must arise from genuine convex figure-eight structures, and their reachable set must meet its `A`-transform. The exact finite test is a verification layer, not a solution of this extension problem.

## Approach 4: bending and one-ray deformations cannot cover infinite-order maps

### Proposition 4.1: centralizer bending does not change the marking

Suppose (2.1) holds and `Z` centralizes `rho_1(Z^2)`. Replacing `C` by `ZC` preserves (2.1), since

`ZC rho_2(Ah) C^{-1}Z^{-1} = Z rho_1(h) Z^{-1} = rho_1(h)`.

This is an algebraic bending family of representations of the **same amalgam**. It does not replace the discrete gluing matrix `A` by another matrix. Any assertion that the whole family is convex would additionally need a geometric gluing or deformation theorem. Merely increasing a centralizer parameter cannot absorb an arbitrary Dehn twist in the marking.

### Proposition 4.2: a fixed diagonal model has only finitely many matching markings

Let `W` have rank two. If

`W A = P W`                                                       (4.1)

for a permutation `P`, then `A` has finite order. In fact if `P^m=I`, iterating (4.1) gives `W A^m=W`; injectivity of `W:R^2 -> R^4` gives `A^m=I`. For each permutation, at most one `A` satisfies (4.1), so there are at most 24 matrices in this stabilizer (at most six when the fourth line is fixed).

In particular no nontrivial unipotent shear `A_k=[[1,k],[0,1]]` can work with two identical fixed peripheral models: `A_k^n=[[1,nk],[0,1]]` is not the identity for any positive `n` when `k != 0`.

### Proposition 4.3: allowing independent scaling of the same ray does not rescue this route

Suppose `s,t` are nonzero real numbers and

`t W = s P W A`.                                                  (4.2)

Writing `r=t/s` gives `W A = r P^{-1}W`. If `P^m=I`, then `A^m=r^m I`. Since `|det A|=1`, taking absolute determinants yields `|r|=1`. Therefore `|t|=|s|`, and `A^{2m}=I`. For a positive ray, `t=s`; permitting both signs adds only the sign-reversed cases.

This rules out the entire fixed-shape scaling-ray mechanism for every infinite-order gluing matrix, not just one numerical parameter choice. It applies only when the *whole reachable peripheral family under consideration* has the stated form. It does not assert that the full deformation space of the figure-eight exterior is a ray. The highly symmetric constructions discussed in [BDL, Section 7] motivate studying such subfamilies but do not license replacing the full space by them.

For the explicit `W_0` above, solving (4.1) exactly gives the six matrices

`[[1,0],[0,1]]`, `[[0,1],[1,0]]`,

`[[-1,-1],[0,1]]`, `[[1,0],[-1,-1]]`,

`[[0,1],[-1,-1]]`, `[[-1,-1],[1,0]]`.

They have orders `1`, `2`, or `3`. Including the sign-reversed ray adds their negatives. These are a completely verified peripheral-model calculation, not a list of all gluing maps admitting convex structures on `N_A`.

At a singular degeneration, rank can fail, eigenlines can collide, or unipotent Jordan data can survive while all logarithmic eigenvalues vanish. Propositions 4.2--4.3 do not continue through such a degeneration by substituting `W=0`.

**Remaining gap.** One needs genuinely varying peripheral shape, beyond conjugation or a fixed ray, together with global convex extension over each piece.

## Approach 5: finite covers and the descent obstruction

The reflection-orbifold literature makes finite covers a natural route. The following calculation isolates what covering can and cannot fix.

A finite cover of a torus is encoded by a nonsingular integer matrix `B_i`: a cover-basis vector `v` maps to `B_i v` in the original peripheral group. A map between those covers, with matrix `D in GL(2,Z)`, covers the original map `A` precisely when

`A B_1 = B_2 D`.                                                  (5.1)

### Proposition 5.1: inherited diagonal holonomies cannot gain a missing match upstairs

Assume the positive diagonal, distinct-character hypotheses of Approach 3. For the pullbacks of two *fixed* peripheral representations, matching on the covers means

`W_1 B_1 = P W_2 B_2 D`.

Using (5.1) gives

`(W_1 - P W_2 A) B_1 = 0`.

Since `B_1` is invertible over `R`, this is equivalent to the original equality `W_1=P W_2 A`. The restriction to a finite-index lattice does not merge distinct real characters, since it spans `R^2`. Thus this argument includes simultaneous conjugacy, not just a prechosen eigenbasis.

Consequently a fixed-model obstruction from Approach 4 persists on every finite peripheral cover that covers the prescribed map. One may choose new convex structures upstairs, but this leaves the inherited-holonomy setting and incurs a separate descent problem.

### Descent is an extra condition, even after a representation upstairs is found

If a finite-index normal subgroup `H` of a group `G` has a representation `sigma`, any extension `rho:G -> PGL(4,R)` must supply matrices `C_g` such that

`C_g sigma(h) C_g^{-1} = sigma(ghg^{-1})` for every `h in H`,

and these matrices must satisfy the multiplication relations in `G`. If a coset representative has `g^n in H`, one in particular needs `C_g^n = sigma(g^n)`. For a convex structure to descend, the developing domain must also be preserved by these extra transformations with the required free, proper action. None of this follows from a convex structure on a finite cover alone.

A small exact model demonstrates the first obstruction. Let `H=Z^2`, let the involution exchange its two generators, and form `G=Z^2 semidirect Z/2`. Define

`sigma(e_1)=diag(2,1/2,1,1)`,

`sigma(e_2)=diag(1,1,3,1/3)`.

These are commuting determinant-one positive matrices. Any extension would conjugate one displayed matrix to the other. Their projective spectra cannot agree: normalizing positive determinant-one lifts removes the scalar freedom, and the eigenvalue multisets differ. Therefore this representation does not extend. The semidirect product here is an algebraic illustration, **not** the fundamental group of a counterexample `N_A`.

**Remaining gap.** Covers with newly chosen peripheral structures could still succeed. One must construct them compatibly with (5.1), prove the full deck-equivariant extension and convex descent, or find a different argument. We have not done so.

## Conclusion and verification scope

The strongest outputs are exact restrictions on several plausible but insufficient mechanisms:

1. the shears give explicitly different homology types and are not transferred from the double by extendable reparametrizations;
2. separate generator spectra and Gram matrices do not verify full marked peripheral conjugacy;
3. centralizer bending keeps the gluing marking fixed;
4. fixed-shape scaling families cannot handle infinite-order markings;
5. finite covers of fixed positive diagonal holonomies cannot repair a failed match for the original map.

No global figure-eight holonomy matrices with a new arbitrary gluing are constructed, and no all-representations obstruction is proved. The full source question and its more general hyperbolic-JSJ analogue remain unresolved by this work.
