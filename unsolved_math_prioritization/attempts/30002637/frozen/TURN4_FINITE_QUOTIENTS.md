# Author turn 4: can a finite quotient remove all unstable modes?

Date: 2026-10-03. Target 30002637 / OWR-13106-010. Outcome: unsuccessful counterexample route. No compact stable non-Einstein quotient is constructed.

## Mechanism being tested

The target has no simple-connectedness assumption. An unstable covering soliton need not automatically have an unstable smooth quotient: only invariant eigenmodes descend. This offers a counterexample mechanism materially different from the tensor test families.

Let a finite group G act freely and isometrically on a compact gradient shrinker (M,g,f). The potential normalized by weighted mean is invariant: f∘γ−f has zero Hessian, so is constant on compact connected M, and the normalization fixes that constant. The weighted divergence, Ricci pairing, and N operator commute with G. Smooth quotient tensors identify with G-invariant tensors upstairs, and the Rayleigh numerator is unchanged up to the harmless covering normalization. Therefore the quotient is linearly unstable exactly when the positive N-eigenspace upstairs has a nonzero G-invariant vector.

Averaging an arbitrary unstable tensor is insufficient: its average may vanish. A sign representation on a positive eigenspace is the elementary linear-algebra obstruction, not a constructed Ricci-soliton example.

## Known four-dimensional non-Einstein compact Kähler examples

The Koiso–Cao and Wang–Zhu metrics are on CP² blown up at one and two points respectively. Their instability is prior work of [Hall–Murphy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1023); their role among known examples is also reviewed in [Cao–Zhu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01453). We tested whether free isometric quotients of these examples could evade the known unstable directions.

Here is a standard geometric obstruction, not a new instability theorem. Both metrics are irreducible and non-Ricci-flat Kähler surfaces. If a compact shrinking Kähler surface of this type split as a product, the compact two-dimensional shrinking factors would be round spheres; the resulting product has the topology of S²×S², different from either surface here. Their parallel complex structures are consequently unique up to sign: two independent parallel complex structures in the irreducible four-dimensional case would give hyperkähler, hence Ricci-flat, holonomy. Thus every isometry is holomorphic or antiholomorphic.

The holomorphic subgroup of a freely acting finite group must be trivial. A free finite holomorphic action on a Fano manifold gives a finite étale quotient, whereas χ(O)=1 for a Fano manifold by Kodaira vanishing. Multiplicativity of holomorphic Euler characteristic under finite étale covers forces the degree to divide 1. Equivalently, the holomorphic Lefschetz number is 1, preventing a fixed-point-free finite holomorphic automorphism.

It follows that a nontrivial free isometry group here could only be order two and generated by an antiholomorphic involution.

### Wang–Zhu surface

The Euler characteristic of CP²#2(−CP²) is 5. A free involution would make this twice the Euler characteristic of its quotient, impossible. Thus this example has no nontrivial free finite isometric quotient.

### Koiso–Cao surface

Write the complex surface as the Hirzebruch surface F₁, the blowup of CP² at a point. Any antiholomorphic involution preserves its unique exceptional (−1)-curve and hence descends under blowdown to an antiholomorphic involution of CP².

Every antiholomorphic projective automorphism is induced by an invertible antilinear map A on C³. If its projectivization is an involution, then A²=cI. Here c is real (commuting A with A² conjugates c) and det(A²)>0 implies c³>0, so c>0. Rescaling A gives an antilinear involution. Its real fixed subspace has dimension three, and its projectivization is RP². There are fixed points away from the blown-down point, and those lift to fixed points on F₁. Hence the antiholomorphic involution is not free.

Thus this example also has no nontrivial free finite isometric quotient. This argument can alternatively be viewed through the parity obstruction for a real surface with an invariant exceptional curve; see the discussion of real surfaces in [Billi–Fu–Grossi–Kharlamov](https://irma.math.unistra.fr/~lfu/articles/Maximal_Branes_HK.pdf).

## Exact limitation

The preceding case checks close this proposed quotient shortcut only for the two indicated four-dimensional examples. They do not classify all compact non-Einstein shrinkers, prove that every such metric is Kähler, or rule out relevant quotients in higher dimension. Orbifold quotients with fixed points are outside the smooth source target. Product constructions and other finite group representations would require separate analysis.

No stable compact non-Einstein quotient or positive general instability theorem is obtained. The original target remains unresolved. The ancillary topology arguments are standard deductions with no asserted novelty and should be checked independently before use.
