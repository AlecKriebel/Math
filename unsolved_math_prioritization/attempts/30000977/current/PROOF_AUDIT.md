# A degree-12 canonical cover of a singular quadric cone

## Result and attribution

The proposed bound `deg(phi) <= 4` in OWR-1971-010 is false with its stated hypotheses. A previously published construction of Christian Gleissner, Roberto Pignatelli and Carlos Rito supplies a normal projective surface X over C with precisely nine A2 singularities, ample and base-point-free canonical bundle, p_g(X)=4, and COMPLETE canonical morphism of degree 12 onto a singular quadric cone in P3.

This report verifies that specific construction directly; it does not depend on the correctness or exhaustiveness of the source's computer classification. It is a credited audit of a prior counterexample, not a new counterexample claim. It also repairs two typographical inconsistencies in the inspected preprint. The independent second audit accepted the construction and every target hypothesis; see SECOND_AUDIT.md for its separate geometric proof and executed verification record.

Primary construction: Gleissner–Pignatelli–Rito, *New surfaces with canonical map of high degree*, Section 6, p.8 of [arXiv:1807.11854v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.11854v2), revised 25 March 2020; published in *Communications in Analysis and Geometry* 30 (2022), no.8, pp.1811–1823, DOI [10.4310/CAG.2022.v30.n8.a5](https://doi.org/10.4310/CAG.2022.v30.n8.a5). The authors' current publication pages confirm publication. Crossref's freshly retrieved DOI registration confirms pp.1811–1823; one author's publication list instead gives 1809–1821, so the DOI registration controls the bibliographic citation. The mathematical text audited here is the identified arXiv preprint, not an uninspected publisher PDF.

Original question: [OWR 26/2008, pp.1462,1464](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46169). Its definition requires canonical singularities, ample base-point-free canonical divisor, and the complete canonical system. It does not require the canonical map to be Galois or X to be irregular/regular. The ambient convention in the directly cited classification paper is algebraically closed characteristic zero: [Gallego–Purnaprajna, arXiv:math/0312174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0312174), Section 1, pp.5–6. Constructing the example over C suffices to refute this target.

## 1. An actual global algebraic construction

Let G=(Z/3Z)^3, written additively. Choose the four points 0,1,2,infinity on each copy of P1. Set the branch monodromies, in this order, to

E: (1,1,2), (2,2,0), (1,2,1), (2,1,0),

F: (2,1,1), (0,1,2), (1,2,1), (0,2,2).

Each row sums to zero in G, and each spans G. For example the first three E vectors have determinant 1 modulo 3, and the first three F vectors have determinant 1 modulo 3. Thus these are connected degree-27 G-covers of P1.

Existence need not be inferred from numerical branch data: here are explicit function fields. Let A be the smooth projective normalization of C(t) in the extension generated by u1,u2,u3 satisfying

u1^3 = t(t-1)^2(t-2),

u2^3 = t(t-1)^2(t-2)^2,

u3^3 = t^2(t-2).

Let B be the smooth projective normalization of C(s) in the extension generated by v1,v2,v3 satisfying

v1^3 = s^2(s-2),

v2^3 = s(s-1)(s-2)^2,

v3^3 = s(s-1)^2(s-2).

The determinant test on valuations at 0,1,2 proves that the three Kummer classes are independent, so both extensions have degree 27 and deck group G. For g=(g1,g2,g3), let g act by uj -> zeta^gj uj, and similarly on the vj, where zeta is a primitive cube root. The valuation at infinity is minus the sum of the three finite valuation vectors, giving exactly the displayed fourth monodromy in both rows. The only branch points are the four specified points, each with inertia order 3.

Riemann–Hurwitz gives

2g(A)-2 = 27(-2 + 4(1-1/3)) = 18,

and the same for B. Therefore g(A)=g(B)=10.

Define H={(g,-g):g in G} in G x G and

X=(A x B)/H.

This is a normal connected projective algebraic surface: a finite group quotient of the smooth connected projective surface A x B. It comes with a finite degree-27 morphism

psi: X -> Y=P1 x P1,

whose residual group (G x G)/H is identified with G by (g,h) -> g+h. Consequently its local monodromy vectors are precisely E and F above. Let Ei and Fj denote the corresponding vertical and horizontal branch fibers on Y.

## 2. All singularities of X are canonical

A nonidentity deck transformation of a smooth curve has only finitely many fixed points. Thus the H-action on A x B is free away from finitely many pairs of ramification points; there is no divisorial ramification for A x B -> X.

At a pair over Ei and Fj, the stabilizer of H is nontrivial exactly when the one-dimensional subgroups generated by the monodromy vectors ei and fj agree. Inspection of the sixteen pairs shows that the ONLY such pair is i=j=3, and e3=f3=(1,2,1). In particular, no equality with the opposite generator occurs at another crossing.

At this pair, choose uniformizers a,b upstairs so that e3 acts by a -> zeta a on A and f3 acts by b -> zeta b on B. The generator (e3,-e3) of H's stabilizer acts by

(a,b) -> (zeta a,zeta^{-1} b).

The invariant local ring is C[[a^3,b^3,ab]], equivalently C[[U,V,T]]/(UV-T^3), a rational double point of type A2. All other points of X are smooth. There are 9 ramification points on each curve over the third branch point, hence 81 point-pairs. Each H-orbit there has size 27/3=9, producing exactly 9 A2 points on X.

Thus X is Gorenstein, K_X is Cartier, and X has only canonical singularities. We have checked the actual local analytic type, not merely a discrepancy-compatible number. Over C the completed local computation identifies the rational double point in the usual analytic/algebraic sense.

## 3. Ampleness, general type and K squared

The finite quotient pi:A x B -> X is unramified in codimension one, so

pi^* K_X = K_(A x B).

The right-hand side is ample because both curve genera are 10. Ampleness descends and ascends along a finite surjective morphism; therefore the Cartier divisor K_X is ample. In particular X is of general type.

Since K_(A x B)^2 = 8(g(A)-1)(g(B)-1)=648 and deg(pi)=27,

K_X^2 = 648/27 = 24.

There is also an independent ramification-formula check using psi:

K_X = psi^*(K_Y+(2/3)(sum Ei+sum Fj))
    = psi^*((2/3,2/3))

as Q-divisor classes. This gives K_X^2=27*2*(2/3)^2=24 and again shows ampleness. The displayed Q-class does not assert that O_Y(2/3,2/3) is a line bundle; Cartierness of K_X was proved from the local singularities above.

## 4. The FULL canonical system has exactly four sections

For c=(c1,c2,c3) in G^vee, put

r_i(c) = least residue of c dot ei modulo 3,

s_j(c) = least residue of c dot fj modulo 3,

d_E(c) = (sum r_i(c))/3,  d_F(c) = (sum s_j(c))/3.

The tuple sum-zero conditions make these integers. The elementary eigensheaf formula for the curve covers, followed by taking H-invariants on their product, gives

psi_* O_X = direct-sum over c of O_Y(-d_E(c),-d_F(c)).

One can verify the degree formula locally: if t=a^3, a character c with residue r has eigenfunction a^r. Write M_c for the function eigensheaf on a curve. Multiplication M_c^3 -> O has image O(-sum r_i p_i), so M_c has degree -sum r_i/3. Equivalently the dual building-data line L_c=M_c^dual has L_c^3=O(sum r_i p_i). The same calculation applies to the F cover. The H-invariant pairs of function characters are the equal pairs, since H acts as (g,-g). This fixes which line is dualized in the following canonical formula.

Finite duality gives

psi_* omega_X = direct-sum over c of O_Y(d_E(c)-2,d_F(c)-2).

This application is legitimate on the whole surface: X is Cohen–Macaulay (smooth or A2), Y is smooth, and the finite morphism psi is flat by miracle flatness. Alternatively use finite duality for a finite CM map directly. The canonical summand here has character -c if functions are labeled by c; the labels below are duality labels, which fixes any sign ambiguity.

For c=0, both d's are 0. For c nonzero, each is either 1 or 2: the four residues sum to a nonzero multiple of 3 at most 8, since the branch vectors span G. Hence a canonical summand has a section if and only if (d_E,d_F)=(2,2), and then it has dimension one. Here is the complete 27-character degree table, written in three rows of nine, so no uncomputed summands are assumed absent:

000:(0,0), 001:(1,2), 002:(1,2), 010:(2,2), 011:(1,1), 012:(2,1), 020:(2,2), 021:(2,1), 022:(1,1).

100:(2,1), 101:(2,2), 102:(2,1), 110:(1,1), 111:(1,1), 112:(1,2), 120:(1,2), 121:(1,1), 122:(1,1).

200:(2,1), 201:(1,2), 202:(1,1), 210:(1,2), 211:(2,1), 212:(1,1), 220:(1,1), 221:(1,1), 222:(2,2).

Thus the only four nonzero canonical eigenspaces have labels 010,101,020,222. They each have dimension one and exhaust H^0(X,K_X). Therefore p_g(X)=4. The same splitting and the Kunneth formula give q(X)=0, since every nontrivial summand has both bidegrees negative, and H^1(O_Y)=0. Regularity is a conclusion for this example, not an added target hypothesis.

## 5. Canonical divisors and absence of base points, including singular points

Put Ehat_i=(1/3)psi^* Ei and Fhat_j=(1/3)psi^* Fj as reduced Weil divisors. Individual Ehat/Fhat divisors need not be Cartier. The combinations below are Cartier because they are the divisors of global sections of the invertible canonical bundle.

In a nonzero canonical eigenspace c, the coefficient of Ehat_i in its section's divisor is 2-r_i(c), and that of Fhat_j is 2-s_j(c). Indeed dt=3a^2 da in the local ramified coordinate, and finite duality divides its pullback by the eigenfunction a^r, leaving the zero order 2-r. The base canonical summands are O_Y(0,0), so there are no further moving divisors downstairs. Reflexive extension across the finite exceptional set gives the same global divisor formula.

In the order 010,101,020,222, choose nonzero sections with respective divisors

A = Ehat_1+Ehat_4+Fhat_1+Fhat_2,

B = 2Ehat_1+2Fhat_1,

C = Ehat_2+Ehat_3+Fhat_3+Fhat_4,

D = 2Ehat_4+2Fhat_2.

The only possible common support of B and D lies over E1 intersection F2 or E4 intersection F1: fibers in the same ruling are disjoint. Neither point lies on the downstairs support of C. Thus B,C,D already have no common zero, and the complete canonical system is base-point-free.

This support argument also checks the singular points: they lie over E3 intersection F3, where A, B, and D do not vanish. An invertible-sheaf section on a normal integral surface cannot have an isolated zero outside its effective Cartier divisor's support. Hence no hidden isolated base points are omitted by the divisor calculation.

## 6. The image is the SINGULAR cone, and the degree is TWELVE

The explicit divisors satisfy the exact equality

2A = B+D.

Let x0,x1,x2,x3 be the chosen basis of the complete canonical system in the order A,B,C,D. The nonzero rational function x0^2/(x1*x3) has divisor zero. On a normal connected projective variety it is a nonzero constant. Rescale the basis to obtain

x0^2 = x1*x3.

Therefore the canonical image is contained in the rank-three quadric Q={x0^2=x1*x3} in P3. Its vertex is [0:0:1:0]; it is a normal irreducible singular quadric surface and has minimal degree 2.

The base-point-free canonical morphism phi has phi^*O_P3(1)=K_X. Since K_X is ample, phi cannot contract a curve. Being proper, it is finite onto its image. Its image has dimension two (also follows from K_X^2>0), so its irreducible image contained in the irreducible surface Q must be all of Q. Finally the projection formula gives

24=K_X^2=deg(phi)*deg(Q)=2 deg(phi),

so deg(phi)=12>4.

The degree-27 abelian cover psi:X->P1 x P1 used to construct X is different from the degree-12 canonical cover phi:X->Q. No Galois property of phi is asserted or required. The example does not import a smooth-target canonical cover by changing the word “singular”; its canonical image has just been proved to be the actual singular quadric cone.

## 7. Explicit repairs to the inspected preprint

Visual inspection of p.8 confirms that the errors are present in the typeset PDF, not introduced by text extraction:

1. The displayed branch vectors have three components and span F3^3, whereas the nearby prose writes a rank-two elementary abelian 3-group. The verified construction uses G=F3^3, order 27. This is independently forced by the spanning determinants, explicit Kummer fields, genus, singularity count, and K squared.
2. The displayed four section divisors imply 2A=B+D, whereas the subsequent prose uses B+C. With the ordered basis A,B,C,D, the correct equation is x0^2=x1*x3, not x0^2=x1*x2. Renaming the last two coordinates can give the latter equation, but then the stated ordering must also be changed. This repair is direct coefficient comparison, not an inference from the claimed cone image.

These repairs preserve the actual construction and the conclusion. No claim is made about whether later publisher versions already corrected them.

## 8. Dependency and verification boundary

Geometric/algebraic dependencies used:

- Kummer theory over C, normalization of a curve, and smooth projective completion;
- finite projective normal quotients by finite groups in characteristic zero;
- Riemann–Hurwitz and the elementary local invariant ring C[[a,b]]^(1/3(1,2));
- the A2 rational-double-point classification as Gorenstein canonical;
- canonical pullback for a finite quotient free in codimension one;
- descent of ampleness under a finite surjective map;
- eigenline-bundle decomposition, finite duality, and cohomology of P1 x P1;
- the fact that a divisor-zero rational function on a normal projective integral variety is constant;
- proper plus quasi-finite implies finite, and the intersection projection formula.

The explicit field construction eliminates dependence on the source's enumeration, its MAGMA script, or a mere existence statement about branch data. No external proof code was executed. The small accompanying Python program rechecks all 27 characters, the inertia intersections, exact divisor identity, 25 branch strata, and invariant arithmetic. It does NOT prove the geometric dependencies above and is not presented as a substitute for them. All guards use explicit exceptions, not assertions; normal, -O and -OO runs pass, and five corrupted claims fail in each mode.

Together with SECOND_AUDIT.md, this establishes an independently accepted, complete credited audit of the prior counterexample. It does not establish any upper bound on canonical degrees, classify all singular-target covers, or make any novelty/worldwide-openness assertion.
