# Independent primary-source review

The original source is Noam Berger, “Quenched Local Limit Theorem for Space-time Random Walk in Random Environment,” in OWR 38/2009, printed pp.2151–2152, DOI 10.4171/OWR/2009/38. Full official report: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46239

The official PDF was opened independently and printed p.2151 visually inspected. It defines the dynamic resampling model, then states a superpolynomial environmental-probability estimate for iid nearest-neighbor elliptic space-time RWRE in transverse dimension at least two, at every diverging deterministic cube scale. Conjecture 1 denies the analogous statement in transverse dimension one. The present review certifies the candidate's positive d=1 assertion with the same quantifiers, not the conjectured negative answer.

The printed hypothesis is ellipticity, without an explicit common ellipticity constant. In one transverse dimension the candidate's fixed iid law with 0<p<1 a.s. covers that hypothesis. The imported summary's stronger uniform wording does not create a mismatch, since the candidate also covers that subclass. Consecutive real interval lengths are handled in the candidate, as are integer lattice cells. The source's higher-dimensional Z/Z^d notation has no consequence at d=1.

Balázs, Rassoul-Agha and Seppäläinen, “The random average process and random walk in a space-time random environment in one dimension,” CMP 266 (2006), 499–545, provides established replica-chain and quenched-mean context. The full author manuscript's Theorem 3.1 states the order-sqrt(n) quenched-mean variance and a quenched invariance principle. Neither statement substitutes for the superpolynomial all-diverging-scale estimate. Full manuscript: https://www.math.utah.edu/~firas/Papers/rap.pdf

Joseph, Rassoul-Agha and Seppäläinen, “Independent Particles in a Dynamical Random Environment,” Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics 283 (2019), 75–121, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-15338-0_4, gives invariant-particle results and uses matching/coalescing couplings in its Section 2. Theorem 1.1 and its hypotheses are different from the present endpoint tail assertion. Its aperiodicity assumptions are not imported into the nearest-neighbor parity calculation. Full final PDF: https://www.math.utah.edu/~firas/Papers/walkers.pdf

The full Yilmaz–Zeitouni primary PDF, DOI 10.1007/s00220-010-1119-3, was hash-verified as prior-work context; its ballistic large-deviation comparison is not a theorem used to infer the target claim. No novelty or current-priority certification follows from this source check.
