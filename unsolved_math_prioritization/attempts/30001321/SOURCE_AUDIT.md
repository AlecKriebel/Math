The full pinned statement and its dated triage were read. There is no
separate upstream research-results entry. The official OWR38/2009 PDF,
Berger contribution printed pp.2151–2152, was read and p.2151 visually
inspected. Theorem1 asserts superpolynomial environment-probability
decay of the coarse-grained endpoint L1 discrepancy for every diverging
box scale in transverse dimension at least two. Conjecture1 says this
does not hold in transverse dimension one.

The printed theorem says i.i.d., nearest neighbor and **elliptic**; it
does not explicitly say uniformly elliptic. The imported original
summary uses “uniformly elliptic,” while its clean statement retains
“elliptic.” The first partial result covers p in (0,1) almost surely,
without a common lower bound, so it does not exploit this discrepancy
to change the question. The report prints Z for the transverse lattice
even in its d-dimensional statement; the intended Z^d is clear from
the cubes. At d=1 it is simply Z, with deterministic interval partitions.

Failure of the universal d=1 theorem should not be read as failure for
every environment law. A deterministic elliptic law gives identical
quenched and averaged distributions and zero discrepancy. An admissible
counterexample to the universal claim would need a genuine law and a
fixed epsilon with a non-superpolynomial tail along a diverging scale.

The Balázs–Rassoul-Agha–Seppäläinen full author manuscript was read at
Theorem3.1 and its difference-walk preliminaries. It credits the
N^(1/4) quenched-mean fluctuation scale and perturbed-at-zero replica
chain. These are known methods. The nearest-neighbor parity in the
present model is handled explicitly by (X−Y)/2, rather than silently
using an aperiodic lattice statement.

The full open-access Yilmaz–Zeitouni2010 PDF was checked at its model
and Theorem1.5. Its uniformly elliptic large-deviation rate comparison
concerns ballistic deviations, not the fixed-epsilon coarse-L1 tail
quantified here. The result is not substituted for Berger's conjecture.
