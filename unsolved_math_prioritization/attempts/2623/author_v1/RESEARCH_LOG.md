# Five-approach research record

Target: UnsolvedMath 2623, KOU-21.114. Checked 5 October 2026. All outcomes below are partial; the target remains unresolved in this investigation.

## 1. Structural descent and the original theorem

Read the governing October 2026 problem and the 2024 Lisi–Sabatini paper, particularly Theorem 1.2, Lemmas 2.3–2.4, Section 3.1, and Question A. Reconstructed quotient/direct-product closure, derived the minimal-counterexample constraints, and proved an elementary order/derived-length bound depending on log_p|P/P'|.

Outcome: the target reduces to finite p-groups. A minimal counterexample to a candidate d has a unique central order-p minimal normal subgroup equal to P^(d). The parameter-dependent bound cannot be made absolute by these arguments. Subgroup inheritance is false, so an unrestricted subgroup induction is unavailable.

## 2. Unbounded-class semidirect products

Reconstructed the known cyclic-holomorph family with a single valuation/counting proof for all primes, avoiding a presumption of cyclic 2-power automorphism groups. Exhaustively checked p,n=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(5,2). Their subgroup counts are 10, 58, 196, 10, 36, and 14 respectively.

Outcome: arbitrary nilpotency class, but exactly derived length 2. The inversion subgroup of the C8 holomorph gives an exact failure of subgroup inheritance. This route does not disprove bounded derived length.

## 3. Wreath-product growth

Computed the general regular-wreath abelianization and compared it with the base section. The inequality is strong enough to classify the weakly ab-maximal regular wreath products of two nontrivial finite p-groups: only C2 wr C2 qualifies. Checked C2 wr C2, (C2 wr C2) wr C2, and C3 wr C3 from exact multiplication tables.

Outcome: standard iterative wreath constructions cannot answer the question negatively. The order-128 example has derived length 3 but fails weak ab-maximality, with base-abelianization order 16 versus ambient 8.

## 4. Unitriangular constructions

Computed the abelianization of UT_n(F_q) and displayed a rectangular abelian subgroup of strictly larger order for every n≥4. Exhaustively checked UT_4(F_2), including all 225 subgroups.

Outcome: the full unitriangular family cannot provide the required counterexamples. This does not exclude other matrix groups or carefully chosen subgroups.

## 5. Center enlargement and central products

Used direct-product closure to reject abelian padding. Then computed exact abelianization ratios under central amalgamation along a central subgroup lying in the relevant derived subgroups. Applied this to the derived-center subgroup of the bad order-128 wreath product.

Outcome: amalgamation with an extraspecial group cannot repair this wreath obstruction, since the offending base section persists with the same ratio. More general extensions remain outside this exclusion.
