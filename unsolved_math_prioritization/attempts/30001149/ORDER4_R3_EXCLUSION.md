# Excluding the maximal orbit tower for weak order-four automorphisms

## Statement and scope

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic 2. Let sigma be an automorphism of k[[t]] of exact order 4 satisfying the weak Artin–Schreier hypothesis: sigma(t) belongs to a cyclic degree-2 extension of k(t) inside k((t)). Put

- t_i = sigma^i(t), with indices interpreted modulo 4;
- L = k(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3).

The orbit-tower theorem proved in [ORBIT_TOWER_PARTIAL.md](ORBIT_TOWER_PARTIAL.md) gives [L:k(t_i)] = 2^r, where 1 <= r <= 3, and proves that these extensions are Galois.

**Proposition. The case r=3 is impossible.** Consequently a weak order-four automorphism has an orbit field of degree either 2 or 4 over k(t).

This proposition does not exclude r=2, prove the weak-to-strong bridge, or construct a counterexample.

## Proof

Assume for contradiction that r=3. For every interval of ell consecutive coordinates, with 1 <= ell <= 4, the orbit-tower theorem gives degree 2^(ell-1) over each coordinate field in that interval. In particular, each consecutive pair field has index 4 in L, each consecutive triple field has index 2, and any four consecutive coordinates generate L.

Define

- K_i = k(t_i,t_(i+1),t_(i+2));
- A_i = Gal(L/K_i), a group of order 2;
- H_i = Gal(L/k(t_i)), a group of order 8.

Write a_i for the unique nonidentity element of A_i. All indices below are modulo 4.

### 1. The four involutions are distinct, and neighbors commute

If K_i=K_j for distinct indices i,j, their common field contains the union of two different triples of the four coordinates, hence all four coordinates. It would equal L, contradicting [L:K_i]=2. Thus the four fields K_i, the four groups A_i, and the four involutions a_i are distinct.

For neighboring triple fields K_i and K_(i+1), their common pair field is

C_i = k(t_(i+1),t_(i+2)).

Both K_i/C_i and K_(i+1)/C_i have degree 2, and their compositum is L, of degree 4 over C_i. Consequently K_i intersect K_(i+1)=C_i.

The subgroup generated by A_i and A_(i+1) fixes C_i, so it is finite. Its fixed field is exactly K_i intersect K_(i+1)=C_i. Artin's fixed-field theorem therefore gives

|<A_i,A_(i+1)>| = [L:C_i] = 4.

A group of order 4 generated by two distinct involutions is the Klein four group. Thus a_i and a_(i+1) commute.

### 2. Each H_j is generated by the three involutions that fix t_j

The groups A_(j-2), A_(j-1), and A_j all fix t_j, so their generated group is contained in H_j.

By the preceding step, B_j=<A_(j-1),A_j> has order 4 and fixed field k(t_j,t_(j+1)). The group A_(j-2) cannot be contained in B_j. If it were, it would fix k(t_j,t_(j+1)) pointwise, and therefore

k(t_j,t_(j+1)) would be contained in K_(j-2).

But K_(j-2)=k(t_(j-2),t_(j-1),t_j) already contains the other three coordinates. The displayed containment would put all four coordinates in K_(j-2), forcing K_(j-2)=L, a contradiction.

Thus adjoining A_(j-2) strictly enlarges the order-4 subgroup B_j inside the order-8 group H_j. It follows that

H_j = <A_(j-2),A_(j-1),A_j>.

### 3. Every H_j is elementary abelian

Consider three consecutive involutions a_i,a_(i+1),a_(i+2). They generate the order-8 group H_(i+2). The middle involution a_(i+1) commutes with both endpoints, so it is central in this generated group.

If the group is nonabelian, only the two endpoint generators can fail to commute. The quotient by its central order-2 subgroup <a_(i+1)> has order 4 and is abelian. Therefore the nontrivial endpoint commutator is exactly a_(i+1). Here the commutator convention is [a,b]=a^(-1)b^(-1)ab.

Conjugation by sigma sends H_j to H_(j+1). Hence either every H_j is abelian or every H_j is nonabelian. Suppose they are nonabelian. Apply the preceding commutator calculation first to (a_0,a_1,a_2) and then to (a_2,a_3,a_0). It gives

[a_0,a_2]=a_1, and [a_2,a_0]=a_3.

Since a_0 and a_2 are involutions, these two commutators are inverses. The first is the involution a_1, so its inverse is itself. Thus a_1=a_3, contradicting their distinctness.

Every H_j is therefore abelian. Since each is generated by involutions and has order 8, each is elementary abelian of rank 3.

### 4. A larger elementary abelian group has rational quotient

Every pair among a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3 occurs in one of the consecutive triples generating an H_j. Hence all four involutions commute. Let

P = <A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3>.

This is an elementary abelian group generated by four involutions and containing every H_j. Its order is therefore either 8 or 16. If its order were 8, all H_j would equal P, and their fixed fields k(t_j) would be equal. That contradicts the fact that a neighboring pair field has degree 2 over either coordinate field. Therefore |P|=16.

Let F=L^P. Since H_0 is contained in P,

F is contained in L^(H_0)=k(t_0).

Artin's fixed-field theorem gives [L:F]=16, whereas [L:k(t_0)]=8. Thus [k(t_0):F]=2. In particular F is a nonconstant intermediate field of the rational function field k(t_0). By Lüroth's theorem, F is itself rational over k.

### 5. The rational quotient would admit an automorphism of order four

Conjugation by sigma sends A_i to A_(i+1), so sigma normalizes P and preserves F. Moreover, sigma^2 does not belong to P: its conjugation sends A_0 to the distinct subgroup A_2, while every element of the abelian group P acts trivially on P by conjugation.

It follows that <sigma> intersect P is trivial. Indeed, every nontrivial subgroup of the cyclic order-4 group <sigma> contains sigma^2.

By Artin's theorem, Aut(L/F)=P. Consequently the kernel of the restriction homomorphism from <sigma> to Aut(F/k) is exactly <sigma> intersect P, which is trivial. The induced automorphism of the rational field F therefore has exact order 4.

This is impossible in characteristic 2. An automorphism of a rational function field over k is represented by a matrix in PGL_2(k). If its order divides 4, the ratio of its two eigenvalues is a fourth root of unity. In characteristic 2 the only such root is 1. The matrix is therefore scalar or, up to scalar and conjugacy, a two-by-two unipotent Jordan block. Such a block has square equal to the identity. Thus a 2-power torsion element of PGL_2(k) has order at most 2.

The contradiction proves the proposition.

## What remains

For weak order-four automorphisms, the orbit degree is now reduced to 2 or 4. Degree 2 is the strong whole-orbit condition. The degree-4 possibility remains unexcluded by this proposition; the companion genus bound gives g(L)<=5 in that remaining case.

This proof uses the fully stated orbit-tower reductions in the companion note, Artin's fixed-field theorem, Lüroth's theorem, and elementary finite-group and matrix arguments. It does not use a classification of genus-four curves or any unproved assertion about the remaining degree-4 case.
