# A scope correction for the two-degree Nichols Hilbert-series factorization

## Result and limits

There is a finite-dimensional Nichols algebra over the complex numbers whose ordinary Hilbert series cannot be expressed as a finite product of quantum integers in degrees 1 and 2. One example is a standard diagonal Nichols algebra of Cartan type A3, at a primitive fifth root of unity. Its Hilbert series is

\[
H(t)=(5)_t^3(5)_{t^2}^2(5)_{t^3},
\qquad (r)_u=1+u+\cdots+u^{r-1}.
\]

This disproves only the unrestricted universal statement. The example is a decomposable Yetter–Drinfeld module, and therefore is outside the absolutely-simple-module hypothesis in the intended conjecture. It is a standard example from established structure theory. No novelty, solution of the intended conjecture, or current-status determination for that conjecture is claimed.

## 1. What the sources actually say

Lochmann's contribution to Oberwolfach Report 47/2012, printed pages 2901–2902, first discusses an indecomposable faithful braided quandle. Its displayed equation (1) allows only factors in degrees 1 and 2. An explicitly labeled conjecture on page 2901 assumes an absolutely irreducible Yetter–Drinfeld module over a group generated by its support. The closing sentence crossing the page break then states the factorization without restating those restrictions. Thus the isolated final sentence is literally broader than the surrounding setting; a standalone extraction of it loses that setting. This is not evidence that the authors meant to include every diagonal Nichols algebra. [1]

Vendramin's 2014 notes, equation (4.3.1) and Conjecture 4.4 on page 6, explicitly impose absolute simplicity for a group-type Nichols algebra. The accompanying footnote traces the conjecture to the October 2012 Oberwolfach meeting. Definition 5.2, page 7, requires simplicity after every field extension. These are primary sources for the narrow reading relevant here. [2]

The narrow factorization claim and the broad factorization claim must consequently be distinguished. The theorem below addresses the broad claim alone.

## 2. The braided vector space and a finite-group realization

Let \(\zeta\in\mathbb C\) have multiplicative order 5. On the vector space
\(V=\mathbb Cx_1\oplus\mathbb Cx_2\oplus\mathbb Cx_3\), define

\[
c(x_i\otimes x_j)=q_{ij}x_j\otimes x_i,
\qquad
Q=(q_{ij})=
\begin{pmatrix}
\zeta&\zeta^{-1}&1\\
1&\zeta&\zeta^{-1}\\
1&1&\zeta
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every entry is nonzero, so this map is invertible. On a basis tensor \(x_i\otimes x_j\otimes x_k\), the two sides of the braid equation both reverse the order of the three tensor factors and multiply by \(q_{ij}q_{ik}q_{jk}\). Hence this is a braided vector space without any further matrix condition.

It is also of group type. Let \(G=(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^3\), with canonical generators \(g_1,g_2,g_3\). Define characters \(\chi_j:G\to\mathbb C^\times\) by
\(\chi_j(g_i)=q_{ij}\). These are well-defined because the generators commute and all assigned values have fifth power 1. Give \(V\) the action and coaction

\[
g\cdot x_j=\chi_j(g)x_j,\qquad
\delta(x_i)=g_i\otimes x_i.
\]

For an abelian group, the Yetter–Drinfeld compatibility says that the action preserves each homogeneous degree. It does so here, since every action is diagonal. Its categorical braiding is exactly \(c(x_i\otimes x_j)=g_i\cdot x_j\otimes x_i\). The support is \(\{g_1,g_2,g_3\}\), which generates \(G\). This standard realization is also described in [3, Remark 6.1] and [4, §2.1].

Nevertheless, each line \(\mathbb Cx_i\) is both an action submodule and a coaction subcomodule. Thus

\[
V=\mathbb Cx_1\oplus\mathbb Cx_2\oplus\mathbb Cx_3
\]

is a direct sum in the Yetter–Drinfeld category. In particular, \(V\) is not simple even before a field extension, and cannot be absolutely simple. This directly verifies the missing hypothesis; it is not inferred merely from the word “diagonal.”

## 3. Exact applicability of the standard PBW theorem

The pairwise products are

\[
q_{12}q_{21}=q_{23}q_{32}=\zeta^{-1},
\qquad q_{13}q_{31}=1,
\qquad q_{11}=q_{22}=q_{33}=\zeta.
\]

For the Cartan matrix

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}
\]

we therefore have \(q_{ij}q_{ji}=q_{ii}^{a_{ij}}\) for every \(i,j\). All off-diagonal entries satisfy \(-5<a_{ij}\le0\). This is the connected finite Cartan type A3, with vertex parameter of order \(N=5\).

The applicable published input is the type-A PBW theorem of Andruskiewitsch–Schneider [3, §6.1, Theorem 6.13]. The assumptions in that section are:

1. An algebraically closed base field of characteristic zero.
2. A parameter \(q\) of finite order \(N>2\).
3. Root-of-unity entries \(q_{ij}\), with diagonal entries \(q\), adjacent products \(q^{-1}\), and nonadjacent products 1.

Our choices satisfy each assumption, with \(n=3\), \(q=\zeta\), \(N=5\), and the finite-group realization above. In particular, there is no need to impose a classification theorem's unrelated lower bound on primes dividing \(|G|\).

For clarity, write the root vectors with inclusive interval notation:

\[
x_{[i,i]}=x_i,\qquad
x_{[i,j]}=[x_i,x_{[i+1,j]}]_c \quad (i<j),
\]

where for homogeneous elements of multidegrees \(\alpha,\beta\),
\([u,v]_c=uv-q_{\alpha\beta}vu\), with
\(q_{\alpha\beta}=\prod_{r,s}q_{rs}^{\alpha_r\beta_s}\).
The six multidegrees are

\[
\alpha_1,\quad\alpha_2,\quad\alpha_3,\quad
\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\quad\alpha_2+\alpha_3,\quad
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3.
\]

The theorem gives an ordered-monomial basis using each root vector with exponent from 0 through 4. One order, as recorded in [4, §4.1.4], is

\[
x_3^{e_3}x_{[2,3]}^{e_{23}}x_2^{e_2}
 x_{[1,3]}^{e_{13}}x_{[1,2]}^{e_{12}}x_1^{e_1},
\qquad 0\le e_*<5.
\]

Reference [3] instead uses endpoints \(e_{i,j}\) for the interval \([i,j-1]\), a forward monomial order, and left-nested commutators. Its Lemma 6.4 identifies the alternative bracketings needed here. Either ordered basis gives the same graded dimension. The later explicit root and PBW descriptions in [4, §§4.1.1–4.1.4] independently corroborate the specialization.

As a check on root orders, for an interval root \(\alpha\) of length \(h\), its self-braiding scalar is

\[
q_{\alpha\alpha}=\zeta^{h}(\zeta^{-1})^{h-1}=\zeta.
\]

There are \(h\) diagonal factors and \(h-1\) nontrivial adjacent pair products. Therefore every root has self-braiding order 5, consistent with the uniform exponent bound. This scalar calculation alone is not used as a substitute for the PBW theorem.

## 4. Hilbert series, dimension, and degree

Use the ordinary grading \(\deg x_i=1\), not a regrading in which all root vectors have degree one. A root vector for an interval of length \(h\) is homogeneous of ordinary degree \(h\). The six degrees are therefore \(1,1,1,2,2,3\).

A PBW monomial has degree equal to the sum of its root-vector degrees weighted by the six exponents. Summing over the six independent exponent choices yields

\[
H_{\mathfrak B(V)}(t)
=\prod_{h\in\{1,1,1,2,2,3\}}
 (1+t^h+t^{2h}+t^{3h}+t^{4h})
=(5)_t^3(5)_{t^2}^2(5)_{t^3}.
\]

There are \(5^6=15625\) basis monomials, so the algebra is finite-dimensional. Its top degree is
\(4(1+1+1+2+2+3)=40\). Thus \(H(1)=15625\), \(\deg H=40\), and the top coefficient is 1.

## 5. Obstruction to a factorization in degrees 1 and 2

**Lemma.** If a polynomial has the form

\[
P(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(a_i)_t\prod_{j=1}^{s}(b_j)_{t^2}
\]

for positive integers \(a_i,b_j\), and it vanishes at a primitive fifteenth root of unity, then it vanishes at every primitive cube root of unity.

**Proof.** Let \(\eta\) have order 15 and suppose \(P(\eta)=0\). One of the finitely many factors must vanish. If \((a)_\eta=0\), then
\((a)_\eta=(\eta^a-1)/(\eta-1)\) implies \(15\mid a\). If \((b)_{\eta^2}=0\), the same reasoning gives \(15\mid b\), since \(\eta^2\) also has order 15. Let \(\omega\) have order 3. In the first case \((a)_\omega=0\); in the second \((b)_{\omega^2}=0\), since \(\omega^2\) also has order 3. Thus \(P(\omega)=0\). Factors with parameter 1 are identically 1 and cause no exception. Empty products cannot vanish. ∎

For our Hilbert series, \(\eta^3\) has order 5, whence
\((5)_{\eta^3}=0\) and \(H(\eta)=0\). On the other hand, for a primitive cube root \(\omega\),

\[
(5)_\omega=1+\omega=-\omega^2,\qquad
(5)_{\omega^2}=1+\omega^2=-\omega,\qquad
(5)_{\omega^3}=5.
\]

Consequently

\[
H(\omega)=(-\omega^2)^3(-\omega)^2\,5
=-5\omega^2=5(1+\omega)\ne0.
\]

The lemma rules out the required factorization.

The same obstruction has an exact cyclotomic certificate:

\[
(5)_t=\Phi_5(t),\quad
(5)_{t^2}=\Phi_5(t)\Phi_{10}(t),\quad
(5)_{t^3}=\Phi_5(t)\Phi_{15}(t),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{H(t)=\Phi_5(t)^6\Phi_{10}(t)^2\Phi_{15}(t).}
\]

The multiplicity of \(\Phi_{15}\) is 1 and that of \(\Phi_3\) is 0. Every factor allowed in the proposed form that contributes a \(\Phi_{15}\) also contributes a \(\Phi_3\). Since the expression is a product of polynomials, no cancellation can remove the latter.

An additional check uses dimension alone to constrain a hypothetical factorization: evaluating at 1 gives \(\prod a_i\prod b_j=5^6\), so each nontrivial parameter must be a power of 5. Such degree-1 or degree-2 factors cannot vanish at a primitive fifteenth root. This is a second arithmetic route to the same contradiction.

## 6. Conclusion

The displayed A3 Nichols algebra is a valid counterexample to the assertion about **every finite-dimensional Nichols algebra**. It remains group type and its support generates its realization group, but its degree-one Yetter–Drinfeld module is decomposable. Connectedness of the generalized Dynkin diagram records nontrivial interactions between these summands; it does not make their direct sum simple.

The example also already has a cyclotomic Hilbert series and a product of quantum integers when degree 3 is permitted. Thus it does not refute a general cyclotomic-factorization assertion or a version allowing arbitrary positive grading steps. The conclusion is precisely a scope correction for the degree-1/2 statement. The PBW theorem is an explicitly identified standard input; this note proves the specialization and obstruction in full, rather than claiming to reprove the foundational PBW theorem.
