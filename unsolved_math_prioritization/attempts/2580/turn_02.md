# Attempt 2 of 5: universal coefficients and a genuine near-counterexample

**Verdict: NO RESOLUTION.** Universal coefficients give an exact Euler-defect identity. They exclude groups with degreewise finitely generated integral homology, but do not force equality in general. The explicit group Z[1/p] has different finite homological Euler characteristics and fails the required FL hypothesis already at FP1.

## Attempt to telescope all characteristic dependence

Put A_i=H_i(G;Z), with A_{-1}=0. For a prime p, the universal-coefficient theorem applied to the integral bar complex gives a short exact sequence

    0 -> A_i/pA_i -> H_i(G;Fp) -> A_{i-1}[p] -> 0,

where A[p]={a in A: pa=0}. Define

    a_i(p)=dim_Fp A_i/pA_i,
    t_i(p)=dim_Fp A_i[p].

Whenever G has finite-dimensional, finitely supported homology over Fp, all a_i(p) and t_i(p) are finite and vanish for sufficiently large i: each is a subspace or quotient of H_i or H_{i+1}. Taking dimensions and telescoping therefore gives the exact finite identity

    chi_p(G)=sum_i (-1)^i (a_i(p)-t_i(p)).                 (1)

This applies if G is FL over any field of characteristic p, without asserting that FL descends to Fp.

For two positive characteristics p and q,

    chi_p(G)-chi_q(G)
      =sum_i (-1)^i [a_i(p)-t_i(p)-a_i(q)+t_i(q)].       (2)

If rational homology is also finite-dimensional and finitely supported, set r_i=dim_Q(A_i tensor Q). Then

    chi_p(G)-chi_0(G)
      =sum_i (-1)^i [a_i(p)-t_i(p)-r_i].                (3)

The rational-rank expression is NOT asserted for arbitrary two-positive-characteristic data: both reductions can miss an infinite-dimensional rational vector-space summand of A_i.

## Equality theorem with an honest extra hypothesis

Suppose each A_i is finitely generated abelian and G has finite-dimensional, finitely supported homology over the two fields in question. The structure theorem gives

    A_i = Z^{r_i} direct_sum T_i,
    a_i(p)=r_i+dim_Fp T_i[p],
    t_i(p)=dim_Fp T_i[p].

Thus a_i(p)-t_i(p)=r_i for every p. For either positive-characteristic field whose homology eventually vanishes, r_i eventually vanishes; characteristic zero already gives this conclusion directly. Equations (1)-(3) imply equality of Euler characteristics over both fields. Hence any sought example must have non-finitely-generated integral homology in some degree. Since FL over any nonzero field implies finite generation of G, H_0(G;Z) and H_1(G;Z) are finitely generated; the first problematic degree must be at least 2.

The finiteness of every A_i follows, for example, from FP_infinity over Z. This theorem needs neither a common finite resolution nor a finite integral cohomological dimension. Conversely, assuming degreewise integral finiteness from the two FL hypotheses is the missing step and is not justified. Kropholler's 2021 examples of groups FP2 over Q and every Fp but not FP2 over Z warn against coefficientwise-to-integral finiteness inferences; that paper does not supply the full FL hypotheses or resolve this problem.

## Candidate: additive Z[1/p]

Let A be the additive group Z[1/p], expressed as a filtered union of infinite cyclic groups with inclusion maps inducing multiplication by p. Group homology with trivial coefficients commutes with this filtered direct limit, as follows directly from the bar construction and exactness of filtered colimits of modules. Since H_i(Z;F)=0 for i>1 and H_1(Z;F)=F, we obtain

    H_i(A;F)=0 for i>1,
    H_1(A;F)=colim(F --p--> F --p--> ...),
    H_0(A;F)=F.

For F=Q this direct limit is Q; for F=Fp it is zero. Thus

    chi_Q(A)=0,    chi_Fp(A)=1.

This is an actual group, not merely a proposed homology list, and both Euler sums are finite. It proves that finite ordinary homology alone cannot settle the question negatively. In (3), A_1=Z[1/p] has r_1=1 and a_1(p)=t_1(p)=0, producing the difference +1.

But A is not finitely generated: a finite subset has denominators bounded by p^n and cannot generate p^{-(n+1)}. Thus it is not FP1, hence not FL, over any nonzero field.

For completeness, the coefficient-independent finite-generation implication is algebraic. If the trivial FG-module is FP1, the augmentation ideal I is finitely generated (compare its standard presentation with an FP1 presentation by Schanuel's lemma). Choose finitely many generators of I and let H be generated by all group elements in their finite supports. Each generator has augmentation zero and lies in FG I_H. Thus I=FG I_H. But FG/FG I_H has an F-basis indexed by cosets of H; it is F only if H=G. Therefore G is finitely generated.

## Outcome and next route

The defect is demonstrably possible, but this construction puts it into H_1, where finite generation forbids it. A viable construction must preserve a defect in higher integral homology while providing finite free resolutions in both characteristics. The next attempt tests whether finite-generation repairs of this localization example retain the defect.
