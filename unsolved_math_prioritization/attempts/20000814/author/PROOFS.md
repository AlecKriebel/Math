# Proofs and exact boundary controls

Notation: k is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3], and I_C denotes the saturated ideal sheaf or homogeneous ideal according to context. A complete intersection means a scheme-theoretic intersection, equivalently a homogeneous regular sequence of two forms. A locally complete intersection need not be a global complete intersection.

## 1. Specialization of numerical and canonical data

Let C_R inside P^3_R satisfy the assumptions in REPORT.md, with R=k[[t]]. A flat projective family has constant Hilbert polynomial. A CI(a,b) curve has

    P(n)=ab n + 1-g,     g=1+ab(a+b-4)/2.

This follows from the Koszul resolution

    0 -> O(-a-b) -> O(-a) + O(-b) -> I -> 0.

Consequently d=ab and 2g-2=ab(a+b-4) on the special fiber. Among complete-intersection curves these invariants recover the unordered pair {a,b}: its elements are the roots of

    X^2 - (4+(2g-2)/d)X + d.

A flat finitely presented morphism with smooth geometric fibers is smooth. Here the trait has just a generic and a closed point, so the given family is smooth. Its total space is regular, since R is regular. In a smooth proper family the number of geometric connected components is locally constant. A smooth complete-intersection curve is geometrically connected, hence so is C; its smooth irreducible components are disjoint, so connectedness makes C integral.

Write e=a+b-4 and L=omega_(C_R/R) tensor O(-e). Adjunction trivializes L on the generic fiber. A generic trivialization is a nonzero rational section on the regular total space; its Cartier divisor is supported on the special fiber. There is just one vertical prime divisor, namely the integral special fiber C. The divisor is therefore mC for an integer m. Since C is div(t), O(mC) is trivial. Restriction gives

    omega_C = O_C(e).

If a generic trivialization is initially stated over an algebraic closure of the generic field, ordinary field extension for H^0 descends it: a degree-zero line bundle on a geometrically integral proper curve which becomes trivial has a one-dimensional space of sections already over the original field, and any nonzero such section has no zeros.

Finally I_(C_R) is R-flat, from the exact sequence with O_(P^3_R) and O_(C_R), both flat. Semicontinuity gives h^0(I_C(a)) at least its generic value, which is 1 if a<b and 2 if a=b. Thus there is an equation of degree a.

## 2. The Rao criterion and the Serre bundle

Define

    M_C = direct sum over n in Z of H^1(P^3,I_C(n)).

For a locally Cohen–Macaulay curve, M_C=0 is equivalent to its homogeneous coordinate ring A=S/I_C being Cohen–Macaulay. One way to see the relevant step is the usual local-cohomology sequence: saturation removes H^0_m(A), and H^1_m(A) measures the difference between A and all the sections of O_C(n). These differences are exactly the displayed H^1(I_C(n)). Since dim A=2, their vanishing gives depth two.

If A is Cohen–Macaulay, its canonical module is recovered from all the sections of its canonical sheaf. The equality omega_C=O_C(e), together with A=H^0_*(O_C), then identifies the canonical module with A(e). Thus A is Gorenstein. A height-two graded Gorenstein ideal in S is a complete intersection: in its minimal Hilbert–Burch resolution, the last free module has rank one (the Cohen–Macaulay type), and the preceding module has rank two by the rank equality. A height-two ideal with two generators in a regular ring is generated by a regular sequence. Conversely the Koszul resolution of a complete intersection gives M_C=0. The numerical calculation in Section 1 fixes its type as (a,b).

We will also use the following standard Hartshorne–Serre construction, with its hypotheses made explicit. If C is a smooth integral curve in P^3 with omega_C=O_C(e), and N=e+4, there is an extension

    0 -> O -> E -> I_C(N) -> 0                                      (2.1)

whose middle term E is locally free of rank two. Here H^1(O(-N))=H^2(O(-N))=0, and local duality identifies the relevant extension classes with H^0(omega_C(4-N))=H^0(O_C). Choose the class corresponding to 1. Locally along C, the ideal is generated by a regular sequence of length two; a generator of the local extension module is represented by its Koszul extension, whose middle term is free. This verifies local freeness at C, and away from C the assertion is immediate. The left-hand map gives a section with zero scheme exactly C. Therefore

    det E=O(N),       c2(E)=deg C.

In our application N=a+b and c2(E)=ab. Projective-space cohomology applied to (2.1) also gives

    H^0(E(-a-b))=0,                                                (2.2)

and a surjection H^0(E(m-a-b)) -> H^0(I_C(m)) for every m, because H^1(O(m-a-b))=0.

These are standard foundational results, not claimed as newly established here. The displayed explanation specifies the precise Serre correspondence used and the local-freeness requirement; arbitrary non-subcanonical curves do not admit this extension with an everywhere locally free middle term.

## 3. An integral containing surface suffices

**Proposition.** If the curve C of Section 1 lies on an integral degree-a surface, then C is CI(a,b).

**Proof.** Let F be its irreducible homogeneous defining equation. Lift F through (2.1) to a section sigma of E(-b).

Suppose sigma has a nonzero divisorial zero D. On P^3 it has equation G of positive degree r; dividing sigma by G gives a regular section of E(-b-r). Its image under (2.1) shows that G divides F. Irreducibility of F forces r=a and G to be F up to a nonzero scalar. But then the divided section is in H^0(E(-a-b)), contrary to (2.2). Hence sigma has no divisorial zero.

The zero scheme of a section of a rank-two bundle on the smooth threefold P^3, if nonempty and without a divisorial component, is a locally complete-intersection curve: its local ideal has two generators and height two, hence they are a regular sequence. It cannot have only an isolated point, since an ideal with two generators has height at most two. Its degree would be c2(E(-b)). The rank-two twist formula gives

    c2(E(-b)) = ab - b(a+b) + b^2 = 0.

Every nonempty projective curve has positive degree, so the zero scheme is empty. There is an exact sequence of vector bundles

    0 -> O -> E(-b) -> O(a-b) -> 0.

It splits because Ext^1(O(a-b),O)=H^1(O(b-a))=0. Thus E=O(b) direct sum O(a). Its section in (2.1) has two homogeneous components of degrees b and a and has codimension-two zero scheme C. They are a regular sequence, proving the assertion. QED.

This is a specialized proof of the integral-surface equality case of the speciality lemma; compare Di Gennaro–Franco, Lemma 2. No Hodge index argument, torsion-freeness of a Picard group, resolution of the containing surface, or smoothness of that surface is needed.

Let c be the least positive degree with I_C(c) nonzero. Since C is integral, I_C is prime; a reducible minimal equation would have a factor in I_C of smaller positive degree. Thus a minimal equation is irreducible. We know c<=a. If c=a the proposition applies. Consequently any non-CI curve under consideration satisfies c<a.

## 4. Smooth subcanonical curves on quadrics

**Lemma.** A smooth integral subcanonical curve C in P^3 which is contained in a quadric is a complete intersection.

We prove the lemma for any integer e with omega_C=O_C(e).

### 4.1 Reducible or nonreduced quadrics

If the quadric is a union of planes or a double plane, the reduced integral curve C lies in one plane, scheme-theoretically. A plane curve is cut out by that plane and one form, so it is a complete intersection.

### 4.2 Smooth quadric

Identify Q with P^1 x P^1 and its hyperplane bundle with O_Q(1,1). Write C of divisor class (u,v). Effectivity gives u,v>=0. If one is zero, smooth integrality forces C to be a single ruling line, already a complete intersection. Otherwise u,v>0.

Adjunction gives omega_C=O_C(u-2,v-2). Hence the restriction of O_Q(u-2-e,v-2-e) to C is trivial. The divisor restriction sequence is

    0 -> O_Q(-2-e,-2-e)
      -> O_Q(u-2-e,v-2-e)
      -> O_C -> 0.

For every integer j, H^1(Q,O_Q(j,j))=0. This follows directly from the Künneth formula and the cohomology of O(j) on P^1: for j>=-1 the H^1 terms vanish, and for j<=-2 the H^0 terms vanish. Thus 1 on C lifts to a global section on Q. Its zero divisor D is effective, with class (u-2-e,v-2-e), and is disjoint from C.

For any nonzero effective divisor of class (p,q), p,q>=0 and

    D.C=pv+qu>0

when u,v>0. Therefore D is zero. Its class is (0,0), yielding u=v=e+2. It follows that C is a divisor of O_Q(u,u)=O_Q(u), whose defining section lifts to P^3 by H^1(P^3,O(u-2))=0. Thus it is CI(2,u).

### 4.3 Quadric cone, including curves through the vertex

Let Q be an integral singular quadric. Its minimal resolution is the Hirzebruch surface F_2, with exceptional section E and fiber F satisfying

    E^2=-2,    E.F=1,    F^2=0,
    H=E+2F,    K_(F_2)=-2E-4F.

This resolution is the blowup of the vertex. Write the strict transform as C'=rE+nF. The coefficient n is deg C, because C'.H=n. Since C is smooth, blowing up its vertex point, if present, does not alter C: a point ideal on a smooth curve is invertible. The strict transform is therefore isomorphic to C.

Moreover delta=C'.E is zero if C avoids the vertex and one if C contains it. To verify the latter multiplicity, the vertex ideal restricts to the maximal ideal of the smooth curve at that point; pulling it to the blowup gives O(-E)|_(C')=O_C(-p). Thus

    n-2r=delta,          delta in {0,1}.

Adjunction on F_2 gives

    g(C)=-r^2+rn-n+1.                                           (4.1)

If delta=1, then n=2r+1 and g=r(r-1). Subcanonicity requires n to divide 2g-2. But

    2(2g-2)=n^2-4n-1,

so n divides 1. Therefore n=1 and C is a line.

If delta=0, then n=2r. The class group of Q is Pic(F_2) modulo the exceptional class, generated by the ruling class, and its hyperplane class is twice that generator. Hence the Weil divisor class of C is rH. It is Cartier and O_Q(C)=O_Q(r). Its defining section lifts to P^3, again by H^1(O(r-2))=0, giving a complete intersection with Q.

This proves the lemma. In particular a non-CI special curve from Section 1 has c>=3. Together with c<a, this proves the answer for every a<=3. The quadric fact is classical; see also Chiodera–Ellia, p. 419.

## 5. The lower-degree residual curve and mandatory fixed factor

Assume C is not a complete intersection, and let F_c be a minimal equation, with 3<=c<a<=b. Let

    l=c-a-b,        G=E(l).

Lift F_c to sigma in H^0(G). The same divisorial-factor argument as in Section 3 shows that sigma has no divisorial zero: a possible divisor would have to be the entire irreducible F_c, leaving a forbidden section of E(-a-b).

The rank-two twist formulas give

    c1(G)=2c-a-b,
    c2(G)=ab+(c-a-b)(a+b)+(c-a-b)^2=(a-c)(b-c)=D>0.

Thus its zero scheme Z is a nonempty locally complete-intersection curve of degree D. Adjunction for a rank-two section gives

    omega_Z=O_Z(2c-a-b-4),
    p_a(Z)=1+D(2c-a-b-4)/2.                                    (5.1)

This adjunction assertion applies to the possibly nonreduced locally complete-intersection scheme Z, not just its support.

Set e_Z=2c-a-b-4. Since c<=a-1 and b>=a, e_Z<=-6. If Z were reduced, its number of connected components would be at most D, as each has positive degree. On a reduced proper curve over k, h^0(O_Z) is the number of connected components. Hence

    p_a(Z)=1-h^0(O_Z)+h^1(O_Z) >= 1-D.

But (5.1) gives p_a(Z)<=1-3D<1-D, a contradiction. Therefore Z is nonreduced.

Furthermore D cannot be one. A degree-one pure locally Cohen–Macaulay curve has one generic component, a line, of multiplicity one. Any extra structure would be supported on finitely many points; purity excludes such a finite-length subsheaf of O_Z. Thus the curve is the reduced line. This contradicts the nonreduced conclusion.

If a=b and c=a-1 then D=1, so this possibility is excluded. In particular, for (a,b)=(4,4), the only possible value 3<=c<a would be c=3, and it is excluded. This settles type (4,4).

Now let G_m be any degree-m equation of C with 0<=m<=b. If it is not divisible by F_c, the two hypersurfaces have no common surface component, so their scheme-theoretic intersection has degree cm and contains C. Bézout would give

    ab=deg C <= cm <= cb < ab,

an impossibility. Therefore

    (I_C)_m = F_c S_(m-c)                  for every 0<=m<=b,     (5.2)

where S_j=0 for j<0. In degree c this also proves uniqueness of F_c up to scalar. In degree a it proves

    h^0(I_C(a))=binomial(a-c+3,3).

The first equation not divisible by F_c must have degree at least ceil(ab/c)>b. No conclusion about existence of such C or its membership in a particular Hilbert component follows from these numerical conditions alone.

## 6. An exact singular-limit control in characteristic zero

Work over Q, or after scalar extension to an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Use coordinates x,y,z,w and parameter t. Set

    A=y^2+z^2,
    B=x^2+yz+w^2,
    q1=xz+tA,
    q2=xw+tB,
    f=wA-zB,
    J=(q1,q2,f).

These are the signed maximal minors of

    [ -w    B  ]
    [  z   -A  ]
    [  t    x  ].

The polynomial q1 is irreducible: it is primitive and linear in x over Q[t,y,z,w], since z and t(y^2+z^2) are coprime. It does not divide q2. Therefore J has height at least two; the determinantal height bound gives height exactly two. Hilbert–Burch supplies the graded resolution

    0 -> S_R(-3) + S_R(-4)
      -> S_R(-2)^2 + S_R(-3)
      -> S_R -> S_R/J -> 0,                                   (6.1)

where S_R=Q[t,x,y,z,w] and t has projective degree zero. The quotient is Cohen–Macaulay.

At t=0, put

    f0=f|_(x=0)=wy^2+wz^2-yz^2-zw^2.

Since f=f0-x^2z,

    J_0=(xz,xw,f0)=(x,f0) intersection (z,w).                  (6.2)

The intersection equality is elementary: f0 is in (z,w), and if xh is in the prime ideal (z,w), then h is in (z,w). Thus the central fiber is the plane cubic E=(x,f0) together with the line L=(z,w).

The ideal J_0 has height two in Q[x,y,z,w], so (J,t) has height three. Since S_R/J is Cohen–Macaulay of codimension two, t lies in none of its associated primes; it is a nonzerodivisor. Over the DVR Q[t]_(t), the quotient is torsion-free and therefore flat. This proves the required flatness near t=0. Tensoring (6.1) with the special fiber stays exact; its Hilbert numerator is

    1-2u^2-u^3+u^3+u^4=(1-u^2)^2,

the CI(2,2) numerator. In particular the central ideal is saturated (its quotient is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension two).

For t invertible, the identity

    -wq1+zq2+tf=0

shows J=(q1,q2). The exact control program checks that the t=1 intersection is smooth: on each of the four affine projective charts, the ideal consisting of q1,q2 and the six two-by-two Jacobian minors has Gröbner basis {1} over Q. Consequently the geometric generic fiber is a smooth CI(2,2), since projective smoothness is an open condition. The same unit-ideal test in three charts proves that the plane cubic E is smooth.

The curves E and L meet just at p=[0:1:0:0]. At that point partial f0/partial w=1. The cubic's tangent lies in x=0, while L has the independent x-direction. Their union is singular at p. The central saturated ideal has three minimal generators: two quadrics and a cubic not divisible by x. It is not a complete intersection.

This is not a counterexample to AIM Problem 13 because its special fiber is singular. It is a reproducible check that the smooth-special-fiber assumption is essential. It also shows why constant Hilbert polynomial and generic complete-intersection equations do not preserve their minimal generating set at a boundary point.

## 7. A characteristic-change control, not a lifting argument

For independent variables alpha,beta,gamma,rho,tau, set

    s=alpha rho^3-beta gamma^3,
    sbar=alpha rho^3+beta gamma^3,
    f1=rho^6 tau+beta^2 s,
    f2=gamma^6 tau+alpha^2 s.

Direct expansion gives

    alpha^2 f1-beta^2 f2=tau s sbar.

In characteristic two sbar=s; in characteristic zero they are different. The program checks both the polynomial identity and the nonzero difference s sbar-s^2 over Q, whose coefficients vanish modulo 2. This is the elementary mechanism behind one of the cautions in Kumar–Peterson–Rao, Section 3. Their positive-characteristic constructions cannot be promoted to characteristic zero by silently exchanging saturation, reduction, and specialization.

## 8. What remains

No proof here excludes the remaining nonreduced residual curves Z, constructs a smooth non-CI special curve C, or proves such a curve lies in the closure of the complete-intersection component. Those are genuine missing steps. The statement that the problem was not solved by this investigation must not be changed to a theorem asserting that it is unsolved throughout the literature.

The five approaches exhaust this attempt's allotted mathematical routes. All displayed positive conclusions are partial results, and all negative controls have their excluded hypothesis stated explicitly.
