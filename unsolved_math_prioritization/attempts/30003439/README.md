# A negative-curvature normal barrier on a nonhyperbolic cone

Problem 30003439 / OWR-15219-008. Complete constructive solution accepted
by the accompanying independent mathematical audit. This answers the
original existential question affirmatively.

The cone is K = {(x,y,z) in R^3: x,y,z >= 0, xy^2 >= z^3}. Its barrier is
F = -25 log[z(xy^2-z^3)] on its interior, extended by +infinity elsewhere.
The proof establishes a 100-normal barrier, negative directional
third-derivative curvature in every cone direction, and the impossibility
of representing K as the hyperbolicity cone of any homogeneous polynomial
in the original space.

The cone itself is pre-existing: Hildebrand, Theorem 3.2 family 5,
p=3 and q=3/2, with (x1,x2,x3)=(x,z,y). No novelty or priority is claimed.
The broader question of existence on every regular cone is not resolved.
