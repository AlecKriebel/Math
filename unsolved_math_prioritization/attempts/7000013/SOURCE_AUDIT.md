The discrete question is broad: it does not display a convexity, genericity, nonflat-edge or maximal-facet hypothesis. Its preceding smooth problem requires closed orientable surfaces and a Gauss-map/curvature-preserving diffeomorphism; the current construction does not claim a solution of that smooth problem. It supplies the stronger closed embedded spherical category for the discrete example.

The decisive convention is what counts as a face. The counterexample uses a finite triangulated PL surface, so its faces are nondegenerate convex triangles even when adjacent faces lie in the same plane. [V. Alexandrov, Minkowski-type and Alexandrov-type theorems for polyhedral herissons, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0211286), is a primary example of this cell-complex definition. It does not require adjacent two-cell images to lie in distinct planes. This is corroborating standard usage, not a claim that Ghomi explicitly adopts that author's definition. The survey does not define the term there. The independent reviewer must decide whether the explicit broad convention is adequate for the literal target; any stricter maximal-facet/nonflat version must remain separate.

Alexandrov's Theorem 4.6 is a positive uniqueness theorem for a more restrictive class with a compatible injective spherical image and oriented area data. Its extra conditions cannot be removed. The present flat-subdivision construction does not satisfy them. The classical convex Minkowski theorem likewise does not transfer to these nonconvex boundaries; allowing triangulation does not invalidate that convex theorem because equal areas can still be summed by outward normal.
