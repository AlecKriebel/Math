# Exact target and current credited sources

Checked 2026-10-08. This is a bounded source investigation, not an assertion of exhaustive literature coverage.

## Exact statement

The OWR report for the April 2017 Discrete Geometry workshop, printed page 1198, problem 4, records Akopyan's question in R^3. It assumes combinatorial equivalence and convexity. The dihedral comparison is weak at one corresponding edge. The report's equality alternative is redundant. The intended objects are full-dimensional bounded polytopes with interior angles in (0,pi). Fix a face-lattice isomorphism before comparing edges; the conclusion is not an unrestricted matching between two multisets of angles. The report was published in 2018, in volume 14 (2017), pages 1135-1205. Its page 1198 was inspected both as extracted text and a rendered image.

Akopyan-Karasev, arXiv:1505.05263v2, Conjecture 5.1, gives the all-dimensional ridge version. It forbids all-strict dominance. It does not itself formulate the stronger implication that weak dominance everywhere forces equality. The paper credits the simplex comparison to Rivin and Lindsey, American Mathematical Monthly 105(7), 671 (1998). Its Conjecture 5.1 and reference list were visually inspected on printed page 5. The current packet's candidate is stronger, and clearly distinguishes that strengthening from the exact original target.

## Analytic dependency used in the candidate

Brendle, *Scalar curvature rigidity of convex polytopes*, Inventiones Mathematicae 235 (2024), 669-708, DOI 10.1007/s00222-023-01229-x, is published. The publisher landing page confirms the bibliographic record. The inspected proof text is the version-pinned author manuscript arXiv:2301.05087v4; the publisher PDF was not retrieved. Section 2 supplies the smooth convex domain, sphere-map homotopy, local boundary condition, positive index, and boundary energy inequality used in PROOF.md. Definitions and proof details of Propositions 2.9, 2.14 and 2.15 were read, and printed pages 11-12 were rendered and visually checked. The manuscript's separate global polytope theorem assumes matching angles; this restriction is not imported into its smooth-domain propositions.

## Recent claims and credit

Bi, *Dihedral Rigidity for Convex Polytopes by Smooth Approximation*, arXiv:2608.06320v1, posted 6 August 2026, develops the smoothing/logarithmic-cutoff strategy used here. Its stated theorem requires a smooth neighborhood metric on one fixed polytope; arbitrary combinatorial equivalence at nonsimple vertices does not automatically supply that hypothesis. Our candidate works directly on the source polytope and constructs target-normal boundary data, so it neither assumes that false smoothness implication nor treats Bi's theorem statement alone as a solution. Its current arXiv page still showed v1; no journal acceptance was verified.

Wang-Xie-Yu, *Gromov's dihedral rigidity conjecture in dimension three*, arXiv:2606.30130v1, posted 29 June 2026, Theorem 1.2 asserts a stronger comparison through nonzero-degree polyhedral maps. Definition 2.8 permits Lipschitz maps smooth away from vertices; Remark 2.9 permits degeneracies. This is a relevant claimed prior resolution route, including nonsimple vertices, and precludes any unqualified novelty claim. The theorem, definitions and selected analytic sections were read; the complete 74-page proof was not independently audited in this investigation. The current arXiv page showed v1 and no journal acceptance was verified. This paper is not a dependency of PROOF.md.

The older Wang-Xie and Wang-Xie-Yu preprints (arXiv:2203.09511v2 and 2112.01510v6) make general claims. Bar-Hanke-Schick, arXiv:2202.05180v2, 15 July 2026, supplies counterexamples to claims in WXY v2 and states that v6 remains under verification. Its complete four-page note was read. This caveat neither proves nor refutes the June 2026 manuscript. Brendle-Wang's published acute-angle result also records a verification caveat about the older program. No blanket 'still open' or 'settled by accepted literature' label is assigned from these materials.
