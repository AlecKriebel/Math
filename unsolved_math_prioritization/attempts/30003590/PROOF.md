# A projective-cone obstruction to openness of the ample-subvariety cone

Public proof-only edition, 10 October 2026. The accompanying [mathematical audit](MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md) accepts the scoped counterexample and general normal codimension-one criterion. This AI-assisted manuscript and audit are unrefereed; acceptance is not external human peer review, journal acceptance, or formal proof-assistant certification.

## Result and scope

**The unrestricted projective-variety formulation is false.** Over \(\mathbb C\), let
\[
X=\{x_0x_3-x_1x_2=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4,
\qquad d=2.
\]
Then \(X\) is a normal projective threefold, and the positive cone generated by its Ottem-ample surfaces is
\[
\mathcal A_2(X)=\mathbb R_{>0}(1,1)\subset N_2(X)\simeq\mathbb R^2.
\]
It is not open in the full numerical cycle space. This remains true if only smooth ample surfaces, or only lci ample surfaces, are allowed as generators.

**This is not a counterexample with smooth ambient variety.** The original Oberwolfach report introduces ample subvarieties using a smooth subvariety of a complex projective manifold on p. 2632. Its Problem 1(1), p. 2633, does not restate the ambient assumptions. Consequently this note disproves the broad projective formulation, but does not claim to resolve the smooth-ambient interpretation of the workshop question. The latter remains unresolved by this attempt. The counterexample has codimension one; it also does not resolve a reformulation that separately requires codimension at least two. No claim of novelty or worldwide current status is made.

## 1. Definitions and credited inputs

For a projective variety \(T\), numerical equivalence of \(k\)-cycles is tested against weight-\(k\) polynomials in Chern classes of vector bundles on \(T\). The resulting real space is \(N_k(T)\). This is the singular-variety definition used by Fulger–Lehmann [FL, §2.1.2], not a quotient obtained by testing only products of Cartier divisors.

For \(0\leq d<\dim T\), set
\[
\mathcal A_d(T)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^m a_j[Y_j]:m\geq1,\ a_j>0,\
Y_j\subset T\text{ an integral Ottem-ample }d\text{-dimensional subvariety}\right\}.
\]
Every class here has positive degree against a polarization, so the origin is excluded. We take neither a closure nor a relative topology.

Ottem [O, Definition 3.1] defines a codimension-\(r\) closed subscheme \(Y\subset T\) to be ample when the exceptional divisor on \(\operatorname{Bl}_Y T\) is \((r-1)\)-ample. For an effective Cartier divisor this is ordinary ampleness. His Corollary 3.5 states that a codimension-one ample subscheme of a normal projective variety is Cartier. This corollary does not require the ambient variety to be smooth. Its proof uses Proposition 3.4: the blow-up has zero-dimensional fibers in codimension one, hence is finite; a finite birational morphism to a normal variety is an isomorphism.

Fulger–Lehmann [FL, Example 2.8] prove the numerical calculation
\[
N_k(C(S))\simeq N_{k-1}(S)\quad(k>0)
\]
for a projective cone. The isomorphism is induced by pulling back to the projective-bundle resolution and pushing forward to the cone. We give the relevant calculation explicitly below. These published inputs supply the obstruction; this note assembles their consequence for the question.

## 2. The variety and its Cartier divisors

Let \(S=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) with its Segre embedding in \(\mathbb P^3\), defined by the line bundle \(L=\mathcal O_S(1,1)\). Then \(X\) is its projective cone. The vertex is
\[
v=[0:0:0:0:1].
\]
The gradient of the defining quadratic is \((x_3,-x_2,-x_1,x_0,0)\), so the singular locus is precisely \(\{v\}\). The quadratic is irreducible, and \(X\) is an integral hypersurface. Its local rings are Cohen–Macaulay; since it is regular in codimension one, Serre's criterion implies that \(X\) is normal. In particular it is also an lci ambient variety, though it is not smooth.

Write
\[
f:Z=\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S\oplus L)\longrightarrow S
\]
using the convention that projectivization parametrizes one-dimensional quotients. Let \(\xi=\mathcal O_Z(1)\). Its five global sections define the blow-down \(\pi:Z\to X\); explicitly they are the constant summand and the four Segre sections. The section \(E\) corresponding to the quotient onto \(\mathcal O_S\) is contracted to \(v\), while the section \(F\) corresponding to the quotient onto \(L\) maps isomorphically to \(S=X\cap\{x_4=0\}\). The map is an isomorphism off \(E\), and
\[
\pi^*\mathcal O_X(1)=\xi,\qquad \xi|_E=\mathcal O_E.
\]

The projective-bundle formula for Picard groups gives
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(Z)=\mathbb Z[\xi]\oplus f^*\operatorname{Pic}(S).
\]
For any line bundle \(M\) on \(X\), write \(\pi^*M=\xi^{\otimes a}\otimes f^*B\). Its restriction to \(E\) is trivial, because \(\pi(E)\) is a point. Hence \(B\) is trivial. Pullback by \(\pi\) is injective on Picard groups: \(\pi_*\mathcal O_Z=\mathcal O_X\) by normality and proper birationality, and the projection formula shows that a line bundle with trivial pullback was already trivial. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal O_X(1)].
\]
An ample line bundle on \(X\) is consequently \(\mathcal O_X(a)\) for an integer \(a>0\), with positivity also detected by restriction to any line on the cone.

## 3. The full numerical surface space

Let \(a,b\in N_1(S)\) be the classes of the two rulings. They are independent, as follows from \(a^2=b^2=0\) and \(a\cdot b=1\). Let \(P_a,P_b\subset X\) be the planes obtained by joining the corresponding ruling lines to the vertex.

The projective-bundle formula on the smooth threefold \(Z\) gives
\[
N_2(Z)=f^*N_1(S)\oplus\mathbb R[E].
\]
Proper numerical pushforward \(\pi_*:N_2(Z)\to N_2(X)\) is surjective: every integral surface on \(X\) has a strict transform mapping birationally onto it. Moreover \(\pi_*[E]=0\), so \([P_a],[P_b]\) span \(N_2(X)\).

They remain independent under the precise singular-variety numerical equivalence. Indeed the inclusion \(i:S\hookrightarrow X\) is an effective Cartier embedding. Its Gysin homomorphism descends to numerical groups [FL, Remark 2.4, citing Fulton, Example 19.2.3], and
\[
i^!:N_2(X)\longrightarrow N_1(S),\qquad
 i^![P_a]=a,\quad i^![P_b]=b.
\]
These intersections occur away from the vertex and each has multiplicity one. Since \(a,b\) are independent, so are \([P_a],[P_b]\). Hence
\[
N_2(X)=\mathbb R[P_a]\oplus\mathbb R[P_b].
\]
This is exactly the \(k=2\) instance of [FL, Example 2.8]. In particular, replacing \(N_2(X)\) by the one-dimensional space of Cartier-divisor classes would change the question.

Let \(h=[S]\) be the class of a hyperplane section. In the above basis,
\[
h=[P_a]+[P_b]=(1,1).
\]
For example, the hyperplane \(x_0=0\) cuts the reduced union of the planes \(\{x_0=x_1=0\}\) and \(\{x_0=x_2=0\}\), one from each ruling. Alternatively apply the isomorphism \(i^!\) and use \(\mathcal O_X(1)|_S=L\).

### 3.1 Independent vector-bundle numerical tests

The following complete alternative separation argument is supplied by the accompanying independent mathematical audit, §4.2. It was already audited and is included as an optional expository addition, not a correction to the valid Gysin argument above. Choose the labels so that a is the ruling {point}×P^1 and b is P^1×{point}.

Write i:S→X for the hyperplane embedding. The two globally generated line bundles M_a=O_S(1,0) and M_b=O_S(0,1) have two sections apiece. Define

\[
0\longrightarrow V_a\longrightarrow O_X^{\oplus2}
\longrightarrow i_*M_a\longrightarrow0,
\]

and similarly V_b. These kernels are genuine rank-two vector bundles on the singular X. Locally on S, trivialize its Cartier equation t and the line bundle M_a. A surjective map R^2→R/(t) can be changed by an invertible basis change into (r,s)↦r mod t. Its kernel is tR⊕R≅R^2 because t is a nonzerodivisor. Away from S the quotient is zero and the kernel is O_X^2. This proves local freeness everywhere, including at the vertex, which lies outside S.

Restrict this sequence to either plane P≅P^2. Its intersection ℓ=P∩S is a line, with t a nonzerodivisor on P. The local resolution by multiplication by t proves Tor_1^X(i_*M_a,O_P)=0. Hence

\[
0\to V_a|_P\to O_P^2\to j_*O_\ell(d)\to0,
\qquad d=\deg(M_a|_\ell),
\]

where j:ℓ→P. On P, the latter quotient has the two-term resolution

\[
0\to O_P(d-1)\to O_P(d)\to j_*O_\ell(d)\to0.
\]

Taking its K-class and Chern class in the ordinary smooth intersection ring of P gives, with H the line class,

\[
c(V_a|_P)=\frac{1+(d-1)H}{1+dH}=1-H+dH^2.
\]

For P_a, d=0; for P_b, d=1. Reversing the two factors of S reverses these degrees. Therefore

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & [P_a]&[P_b]\\\hline
\deg c_2(V_a)&0&1\\
\deg c_2(V_b)&1&0
\end{array}
\]

These are exactly permissible numerical tests on X itself, since V_a,V_b are vector bundles on X. The determinant is −1. Thus P_a and P_b are independent for the full singular-variety numerical equivalence. Combined with the upper bound,

\[
N_2(X)=\mathbf R[P_a]\oplus\mathbf R[P_b].
\]

This explicit calculation is an additional audit proof, not a replacement assumption. It also exposes the invalid alternative of testing only Cartier products: every Cartier class is a multiple of H_X, and H_X^2 has degree one on each plane, so those tests alone see only their sum and lose rank.

## 4. The actual ample-subvariety cone and failure of openness

Let \(Y\subset X\) be any ample surface. Ottem's Corollary 3.5 makes it a Cartier divisor, and Definition 3.1 identifies its ampleness with that of \(\mathcal O_X(Y)\). Section 2 therefore gives
\[
[Y]=a h\qquad\text{for some integer }a>0.
\]
This proves \(\mathcal A_2(X)\subseteq\mathbb R_{>0}h\).

Conversely \(S=X\cap\{x_4=0\}\) is a smooth irreducible effective Cartier divisor with associated line bundle \(\mathcal O_X(1)\). It is ample in the exact Ottem sense. Thus \(h\) is an allowed generator and
\[
\mathcal A_2(X)=\mathbb R_{>0}h.
\]
The same equality holds with only smooth or only lci generators: the upper bound still applies, and \(S\) supplies the lower bound.

For every integer \(m\geq2\), the class
\[
\alpha_m=h+\frac1m([P_a]-[P_b])
       =\left(1+\frac1m,1-\frac1m\right)
\]
is a nonzero effective real surface class, lies outside \(\mathcal A_2(X)\), and converges to \(h\in\mathcal A_2(X)\). Every full-space neighborhood of this nonzero ample class therefore leaves the cone. This proves non-openness. The argument does not involve the origin or a closure. The ray is open in its own span, but that is a different topology. \(\square\)

## 5. A general codimension-one criterion

The mechanism has the following immediate consequence of the same known results. Let \(T\) be a normal projective variety over \(\mathbb C\), of dimension \(n\geq2\), and let
\[
\phi:N^1(T)\longrightarrow N_{n-1}(T),\qquad D\longmapsto D\cap[T].
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal A_{n-1}(T)=\phi(\operatorname{Amp}(T)).
\]
The inclusion from left to right is Ottem's Cartier consequence. In the other direction, a sufficiently divisible positive multiple of any ample Cartier divisor has a very ample linear system with an integral general member, by Bertini. Its class is an allowed generator. The usual real ample cone is generated by ample integral classes, giving equality.

By [FL, Example 2.7], \(\phi\) is injective. Thus \(\mathcal A_{n-1}(T)\) is open in the full \(N_{n-1}(T)\) if and only if \(\phi\) is surjective, equivalently if and only if
\[
\rho(T)=\dim N_{n-1}(T).
\]
If surjective, \(\phi\) is a linear isomorphism and the assertion is the ordinary openness of the ample-divisor cone. If not, the nonempty cone is contained in a proper linear subspace and has empty ambient interior. On a smooth projective variety, \(\phi\) is an isomorphism [FL, Remark 2.9], recovering the usual affirmative divisor case.

The general criterion in §5 concerns all integral ample centers. It does not assert the same equality after demanding smooth centers on an arbitrary singular variety. The smooth-generator statement of §4 is established specifically for the quadric, where a smooth hyperplane supplies the required ray.

## 6. What is not proved for smooth ambient varieties

The singular obstruction disappears for divisors on a smooth ambient variety. No counterexample or general openness proof for smooth ambient \(X\) and higher codimension has been established here.

Ottem's openness in flat lci families [O, Theorem 6.1] does not fill this gap: deformation within a connected flat family preserves numerical class, whereas the problem requires a full neighborhood in the cycle space. Openness of q-ampleness on a fixed blow-up also does not supply ample centers representing perturbations of the original cycle class.

López's \(P_k(X)\) is defined by a complete-intersection class plus a class with empty numerical base locus. His Theorem 1 and its §6 proof concern that cone; Remark 7.5 explicitly distinguishes its relation to the cone of ample subschemes and gives noncontainment in the ample-lci cone. His theorem is therefore not used to identify or open \(\mathcal A_d(X)\). The pliant, basepoint-free and universally pseudoeffective cones in [FL] likewise have different definitions. The full-dimensionality sketch attributed to Ottem in [FL, §6.5] is not by itself an openness proof.

For a precise remaining obligation, fix any real basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) of \(N_d(X)\). Openness of \(\mathcal A_d(X)\) is equivalent to proving, for every allowed ample generator \([Y]\), that there is \(\varepsilon>0\) with
\[
[Y]\pm\varepsilon e_j\in\mathcal A_d(X)\quad(1\leq j\leq s).
\]
The forward implication is immediate. Conversely, convexity then puts a full-dimensional cross-polytope about \([Y]\) in the cone, so every generator is interior. Positive sums of such interior points are interior as well. Establishing full dimensionality or locating a single interior complete-intersection class does not prove this generator-by-generator condition.
