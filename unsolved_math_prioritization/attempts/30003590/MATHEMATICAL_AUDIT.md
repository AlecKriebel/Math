For the normal singular complex projective threefold

\[
X=V(x_0x_3-x_1x_2)\subset\mathbf P^4,

Problem 1(1), printed page 2633, asks about openness in the full numerical cycle space and explicitly motivates the question by higher codimension and the familiar divisor case. It does not supply a fresh singular-ambient convention. Thus the singular codimension-one example refutes a broadened formulation, rather than overcoming the intended smooth setting. The original proof's source-scope warning is retained and strengthened to this unambiguous acceptance limit.

The audited numerical equivalence is the one in [FL, §2.1.2]: a dimension-k cycle is trivial when every degree-k Chern polynomial of vector bundles has degree zero on it. Testing only products of Cartier divisor classes is a strictly weaker test in this example. The target is the positive cone of finite positive real combinations of ample integral subvarieties, without zero, closure, or replacement by relative topology.

[O, §2 and Definition 3.1] defines q-ampleness by eventual vanishing of all coherent-sheaf cohomology in degrees above q, and an ample codimension-r center by (r−1)-ampleness of O(E) on its blow-up. The sign is O(E), not the relatively positive O(−E). Zero-ampleness is ordinary ampleness. For a Cartier center the blow-up is the identity, and E identifies with that effective divisor. Corollary 3.5 explicitly assumes normal projective ambient variety, not smoothness. These statements and their nearby hypotheses were physically inspected on manuscript pages 3, 5, 6.

[FL, Remark 2.4 and Example 2.8], physically inspected on pages 7–8, explicitly permit lci Gysin maps on numerical groups and calculate the projective cone's cycle spaces. The warning about arbitrary flat pullbacks is not an objection here: the bundle projection is smooth and the hyperplane inclusion is an effective Cartier, hence regular, embedding. Example 2.7 states the injectivity of the numerical Cartier-to-Weil map. The detailed original book proofs cited there were not independently inspected; the paper's exact statements were. Section 4 below independently proves the crucial rank-two calculation by actual vector bundles, avoiding the Gysin input entirely.

## 2. The singular quadric and its resolution

Set q=x_0x_3−x_1x_2. Its Hessian has rank four. A reducible homogeneous quadratic over C is a product of two linear forms, whose Hessian has rank at most two. Thus q is irreducible and X is integral of dimension three.

The gradient is (x_3,−x_2,−x_1,x_0,0), so the projective singular locus is the single point v=[0:0:0:0:1]. Hypersurfaces in a smooth ambient variety are local complete intersections and their local rings are Cohen–Macaulay. Therefore X satisfies Serre S2; its only singular point has codimension three, so X satisfies R1. Serre's normality criterion proves that X is normal. None of these statements proves smoothness: the displayed gradient explicitly disproves it.

Let S=P^1×P^1 and L=O_S(1,1). The Segre map identifies S with X∩{x_4=0}. This surface is smooth and integral and does not contain v. Under the quotient convention, let

\[
f:Z=\mathbf P_S(O_S\oplus L)\to S,\qquad \xi=O_Z(1).
\]

The constant section of O_S and four Segre sections of L generate ξ and define a morphism π:Z→X. A point of Z consists of a one-dimensional quotient of O⊕L. On the section E given by the quotient onto O, all four Segre coordinates vanish and the constant coordinate is nonzero, so π(E)=v. On the section F given by the quotient onto L, the constant coordinate vanishes, and π identifies F with S.

Outside E the L-component of the quotient is nonzero. The four Segre coordinates then recover its unique base point in S; the remaining coordinate recovers the quotient. These inverse descriptions are regular on the four usual nonzero-Segre-coordinate charts. Hence π is an isomorphism Z\E→X\{v}. In particular it is proper birational, with π*O_X(1)=ξ. The quotient description also gives ξ|_E=O_S and ξ|_F=L. The normal bundle of E is L^{-1}, consistent with contraction; the other section's normal bundle is L.

This construction verifies the signs and projectivization convention independently. It is not necessary to identify π with a blow-up in order to use it for the calculations below.

## 3. Independent Picard calculation

The projective-bundle formula gives

\[
\operatorname{Pic}(Z)=\mathbf Z[\xi]\oplus f^*\operatorname{Pic}(S),
\qquad \operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbf Z[O(1,0)]\oplus\mathbf Z[O(0,1)].
\]

For any M∈Pic(X), write π*M=ξ^a⊗f*B. Restriction to E is trivial because π|_E is a constant morphism to a point. As ξ|_E is trivial and f|_E is an isomorphism, B is trivial. Thus π*M=ξ^a.

Normality gives π_*O_Z=O_X for this proper birational morphism. The projection formula implies that π* is injective on Picard groups: if π*M is trivial, then M=π_*π*M=π_*O_Z=O_X. Since ξ=π*O_X(1), every M equals O_X(a). There is no torsion or additional Picard-zero part hidden by this argument. Finally O_X(1) has degree one on a ruling line, so its powers are distinct. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbf Z,\quad\text{generated by }O_X(1),
\qquad N^1(X)\cong\mathbf R.
\]

The ample integral line bundles are precisely O_X(a), a>0: positive powers of O_X(1) are ample, while restriction to a line excludes a≤0. This calculation is independent of [FL]'s rank-one assertion for divisor classes on a cone.

## 4. Numerical surface space: two independent routes

Let a be the ruling curve {point}×P^1 on S and b the other ruling P^1×{point}. Their cone surfaces P_a and P_b are planes in X. On S their intersection matrix is [[0,1],[1,0]], so they are numerically independent there.

### 4.1 Upper bound without a singular intersection ring

Z is smooth. Every integral surface on Z is a Cartier divisor, and the Picard calculation above shows that N_2(Z) is spanned by ξ, f*a, f*b. Equivalently it is spanned by E, f*a, f*b, since E=ξ−f*c_1(L). Proper pushforward is defined on numerical cycle groups by the projection formula. Every integral surface in X has a strict transform mapping birationally onto it, so π_*:N_2(Z)→N_2(X) is surjective. The class E pushes to zero, and the other two displayed classes push to P_a and P_b. Consequently dim N_2(X)≤2 and these plane classes span it.

### 4.2 Direct Chern-polynomial separation, independent of Gysin descent

Write i:S→X for the hyperplane embedding. The two globally generated line bundles M_a=O_S(1,0) and M_b=O_S(0,1) have two sections apiece. Define

\[
0\longrightarrow V_a\longrightarrow O_X^{\oplus2}
\longrightarrow i_*M_a\longrightarrow0,
\]

and similarly V_b. These kernels are genuine rank-two vector bundles on the singular X. Locally on S, trivialize its Cartier equation t and the line bundle M_a. A surjective map R^2→R/(t) can be changed by an invertible basis change into (r,s)↦r mod t. Its kernel is tR⊕R≅R^2 because t is a nonzerodivisor. Away from S the quotient is zero and the kernel is O_X^2. This proves local freeness everywhere, including at the vertex, which lies outside S.

Restrict this sequence to either plane P≅P^2. Its intersection ℓ=P∩S is a line, with t a nonzerodivisor on P. The local resolution by multiplication by t proves Tor_1^X(i_*M_a,O_P)=0. Hence

\[
0\to V_a|_P\to O_P^2\to j_*O_\ell(d)\to0,
\qquad d=\deg(M_a|_\ell),
\]

where j:ℓ→P. On P, the latter quotient has the two-term resolution

\[
0\to O_P(d-1)\to O_P(d)\to j_*O_\ell(d)\to0.
\]

Taking its K-class and Chern class in the ordinary smooth intersection ring of P gives, with H the line class,

\[
c(V_a|_P)=\frac{1+(d-1)H}{1+dH}=1-H+dH^2.
\]

For P_a, d=0; for P_b, d=1. Reversing the two factors of S reverses these degrees. Therefore

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & [P_a]&[P_b]\\\hline
\deg c_2(V_a)&0&1\\
\deg c_2(V_b)&1&0
\end{array}
\]

These are exactly permissible numerical tests on X itself, since V_a,V_b are vector bundles on X. The determinant is −1. Thus P_a and P_b are independent for the full singular-variety numerical equivalence. Combined with the upper bound,

\[
N_2(X)=\mathbf R[P_a]\oplus\mathbf R[P_b].
\]

This explicit calculation is an additional audit proof, not a replacement assumption. It also exposes the invalid alternative of testing only Cartier products: every Cartier class is a multiple of H_X, and H_X^2 has degree one on each plane, so those tests alone see only their sum and lose rank.

### 4.3 Check of the submitted Gysin route

The author instead uses i^!:N_2(X)→N_1(S). The inclusion is Cartier even though X is singular; [FL, Remark 2.4] therefore applies. It sends P_a,P_b to a,b with multiplicity one, because the planes meet the hyperplane away from the vertex along reduced lines. Proper compatibility in [FL, Example 2.8] gives the same result. That route is valid. The direct vector-bundle proof above confirms its conclusion without appealing to the cited Gysin theorem.

The hyperplane x_0=0 cuts q=−x_1x_2, hence the reduced union of the two planes x_0=x_1=0 and x_0=x_2=0. Each appears once and belongs to a different ruling. Consequently the hyperplane class h=[S] is [P_a]+[P_b]=(1,1).

## 5. Exact cone and non-openness

For completeness, the codimension-one Cartier implication can be recovered directly. If a center Y of codimension one is Ottem-ample, then O(E) is ample on its blow-up. But O(−E) is relatively ample for that blow-up. On a positive-dimensional projective fiber the restrictions would be mutually inverse ample line bundles, impossible after restricting to an integral curve in the fiber. All fibers therefore have dimension zero. The blow-up is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite; it is integral and birational. Finite birationality onto normal X makes it an isomorphism. Its invertible ideal O(−E) is now the original ideal of Y, so Y is Cartier. This recovers exactly the role of [O, Proposition 3.4 and Corollary 3.5].

Every ample integral surface Y⊂X is therefore an effective ample Cartier divisor. Section 3 implies O_X(Y)=O_X(a) for an integer a>0, and its cycle class is ah. This proves the upper bound for the cone. Conversely S is a smooth integral Cartier hyperplane divisor with ample line bundle O_X(1). Blowing up its invertible ideal leaves X unchanged, so it is ample under the exact definition. Thus h itself is a permitted generator. Both inclusions give A_2(X)=R_{>0}h. The same upper bound and same smooth Cartier generator prove the statement for smooth-only and lci-only generators.

For every integer m≥2,

\[
\alpha_m=(1+1/m,1-1/m)
\]

has strictly positive coefficients in the two effective plane classes, lies off the diagonal, and has squared Euclidean distance 2/m^2 from h. Thus α_m→h while α_m∉A_2(X). The degree against H_X^2 remains two. This is non-openness at a nonzero ample point, using effective perturbations of constant degree. No objection at the origin is involved. The positive diagonal ray is open in its one-dimensional linear span; that is explicitly not the topology in the question.

As a direct non-Cartier control, each ruling plane has a two-generated ideal at the vertex, such as (x_0,x_1) in C[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]/(q) localized at its homogeneous maximal ideal. Its two degree-one generators remain independent modulo the maximal ideal times that ideal, since q has degree two. Nakayama's lemma rules out a principal ideal. These effective surfaces cannot be the ample centers used above.

## 6. General normal codimension-one criterion

Let T be an integral normal projective variety over C, of dimension n≥2, and let

\[
\phi:N^1(T)\to N_{n-1}(T),\qquad D\mapsto D\cap[T].
\]

Use the same positive cone convention and allow all integral ample divisors as generators. The preceding normality argument makes every ample codimension-one center Cartier. Positive combinations of their divisor classes remain in Amp(T), so A_{n-1}(T)⊆φ(Amp(T)).

Conversely, a positive multiple of an ample integral line bundle is very ample. In dimension at least two, Bertini irreducibility over C gives an integral general member of its complete linear system. It is an ample Cartier divisor, and rescaling its class gives the original ample integral class. Every real ample class is a finite positive combination of ample integral classes: one may place the class inside a sufficiently small rational simplex contained in the open ample cone, and clear the denominators of its rational vertices. Thus φ(Amp(T))⊆A_{n-1}(T).

If φ is surjective, any surjective linear map of finite-dimensional real spaces is open, so its image of Amp(T) is open. If φ is not surjective, that nonempty image lies in a proper linear subspace and cannot be open in N_{n-1}(T). This proves the stated iff criterion even without using injectivity.

The additional rank formulation ρ(T)=dim N_{n-1}(T) uses the standard injectivity of φ, verified in [FL, Example 2.7]. That source reduces it to the generalized Hodge-index numerical criterion: triviality of D∩[T] forces D·H^{n−1}=D^2·H^{n−2}=0 and hence the numerical triviality of D. We accept this credited published input; this audit is not a new proof of that general Hodge-index theorem. On a smooth projective variety all divisors are Cartier and the numerical Cartier-to-Weil identification is an isomorphism, as in [FL, Remark 2.9]. In particular the normal criterion agrees with the usual open ample-divisor cone in the smooth case.

This general criterion concerns all integral centers. An extra demand that those centers be smooth on an arbitrary singular T is not silently added to this theorem. The smooth-generator assertion above is established specifically for the quadric, where a smooth hyperplane supplies the needed ray.

## 7. Remaining target and reproducibility

The original smooth higher-codimension issue remains untouched. In a fixed smooth ambient X, a flat deformation through a connected family preserves numerical class; openness of a property in that parameter space supplies no transverse numerical perturbation. Full dimensionality of a cone and a single interior point likewise do not make every ample generator interior.

The author's final generator-by-generator criterion is correct: if, for every ample generator y and each member e_j of a basis, y±εe_j belongs to the cone for some ε>0, their convex hull is a full-dimensional cross-polytope centered at y. Thus each generator is interior, and positive sums remain interior. Establishing this condition, or an actual smooth-ambient counterexample, would be genuine further work.
