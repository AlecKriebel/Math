## Corrected attribution

An earlier bibliographic label called arXiv:1210.7126 a Sharir–Sheffer paper. The source's title page and arXiv record identify **Moria Ben-Ner, André Schulz, and Adam Sheffer**, *On Numbers of Pseudo-Triangulations*. It was submitted to arXiv on 26 October 2012; the retained PDF also prints “November 21, 2021” on its first page. Those are recorded separately rather than forcing a single unsupported document date. Its Section 5, PDF page 14, states the same counting conjecture and distinguishes an upper comparison. None of its upper bounds is used as a lower comparison here.

The Aichholzer–Orden–Santos–Speckmann preprint is version 2 of 14 June 2007; the journal reference is *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* 115 (2008), 254–278. Thus “2007 preprint” and “2008 journal paper” are both appropriate with their contexts.

## Public references

1. TOPP 40, “The Number of Pointed Pseudotriangulations”: https://topp.openproblem.net/p40 . The live entry displays Open and reports verification through ten points. Its displayed revision history ends in 2002, so the page alone is not a comprehensive current literature search.
2. O. Aichholzer, D. Orden, F. Santos, B. Speckmann, “On the Number of Pseudo-Triangulations of Certain Point Sets”: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601747 ; journal DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2007.06.002 . Known family results and the stronger monotonicity conjecture are theirs.
3. M. Ben-Ner, A. Schulz, A. Sheffer, “On Numbers of Pseudo-Triangulations”: https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7126 . Theorem 2.3 gives the opposite-direction factor-three comparison; Section 5 records the target conjecture.
4. G. Rote, F. Santos, I. Streinu, “Pseudo-Triangulations — a Survey”: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612672 . Version 2 is dated 17 October 2007. Theorem 2.7, page 9, is the exact embedding-specific characterization used in the authored sufficient-condition proof; it credits Streinu.
5. D. Randall, G. Rote, F. Santos, J. Snoeyink, “Counting triangulations and pseudo-triangulations of wheels,” CCCG 2001, 149–152: https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/rote/Papers/abstract/Counting%2Btriangulations%2Band%2Bpseudo-triangulations%2Bof%2Bwheels.html . The linked author-hosted PDF is an eight-page version. Corollary 5 on page 3 establishes the known wheel difference. Its notation C_n counts convex n-gon triangulations, so it equals the standard Catalan number with index n-2.
6. TOPP 50: https://topp.openproblem.net/p50 . Its related-results paragraph already records the existence of triangulations with no contained PPT and cites J. O'Rourke's 2002 column. The six-point coordinate certificate in this note does not claim to discover that phenomenon.
