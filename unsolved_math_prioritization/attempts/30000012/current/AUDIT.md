# Independent mathematical and reproducibility audit

## Decision

Accepted as scoped partial progress, with explanatory corrections. The general implication remains unresolved by this packet. None of its examples is a counterexample to the target, and neither conditional lemma supplies the missing global construction. No new proof route was added. No novelty, exhaustive literature-search, or worldwide-openness certificate is given.

The original report, all five mathematical companion files, checker, source metadata, and original test results were reviewed. The originals were preserved byte-for-byte. The corrected copy changes wording and fills a standard field-theoretic justification; it does not alter the mathematical disposition.

## 1. Exact source interface

### OWR and the target

The contribution is in Oberwolfach Report 21/2004, printed pp.1154-1155. The independent check included extracted text and a fresh rendering of printed p.1154. It asks about a compact connected complex manifold Z of dimension n+p, a submanifold Y of dimension p-1 with Griffiths-positive normal bundle, and a covering analytic family of compact n-cycles parametrized by compact normal S. There is no Kähler assumption, and the family is not described as deformations of Y. Compactness of Y is the convention in the surrounding discussion and in the underlying paper, including Theorem 4.3. The report's inclusion of p=1 agrees with BM Remark 0.6(1); n=0 is the positive-normal divisor case.

## 2. Incidence caveat: accepted

For V of dimension five and W of dimension two, a line in V/W lifts to a three-dimensional vector subspace containing W. Projectivization gives a plane in P4 containing the fixed line Y=P(W). The parameter P(V/W) is P2. It is compact, smooth and normal, and the universal linear subspace construction is an analytic cycle family.

A point outside Y determines exactly one parameter, while points on Y lie in every plane. Hence the family covers P4 and the incidence space with Y is S×Y. Its projection to S has one-dimensional fibres, so it is not generically finite. The corrected wording names this map explicitly to avoid confusing it with the family's evaluation map to P4.

The dimensions match n=p=2 and dim Y=1. The normal bundle of a linear P1 in P4 is O(1) direct-summed three times, with the required Griffiths-positive metric. This example defeats only automatic finite incidence for the supplied family. It already has a(P4)=4; in the source framework the all-incident covering situation is an affirmative case. A legitimate replacement family remains logically possible.

## 3. Conics and initial normal data: accepted

The parameter space before removing redundant descriptions is a P5-bundle over the dual P3, hence smooth projective of dimension eight. Its cycle-classification image is compact and irreducible; normalization supplies an effective normal cycle parameter. A smooth conic spans a unique plane and its equation is unique up to scale, so classification is an isomorphism over the smooth-conic locus. The image still has dimension eight. Double-line descriptions are precisely an example of why parameter redundancy must not be ignored.

On the open set of planes H not containing the fixed line Y, the point y=H∩Y is well defined. Vanishing of the quadratic at y is one nonzero linear condition, producing a P4-family in each plane. This gives the irreducible main incidence locus of dimension seven. Planes containing Y form P1, so all conics in those planes have parameter dimension six. Every such conic contains at least one point of Y: its restriction to Y either vanishes identically or is a positive-degree homogeneous polynomial over C. Deform the plane through a chosen such point and deform the quadratic while preserving that incidence. Thus these boundary conics lie in the main closure, and they do not supply another seven-dimensional component.

There is an explicit local check of the first-data calculation. In affine coordinates let Y be the z-axis, write a transverse plane as z=c+u x+v y, and write a conic through its intersection with Y in a chart as

    x+a y+b x²+d xy+e y²=0.

The seven parameters are (c,u,v,a,b,d,e). Its first normal data are (c,[-a:1]); the normal quotient discards the tangential z-coordinate. On this chart the data map has rank two and fibres of dimension five. Point-only data have dimension one and fibres of dimension six. Smooth conics form a nonempty open subset; for example x+y²=0 has a nonsingular homogeneous quadratic. The tangent cone of a smooth conic contributes precisely the same projected tangent line.

Because the plane is transverse to Y, its conic tangent is not contained in TY. General incidence is one point, so all relevant finiteness and nontangency conditions hold. The low-dimensional data still fail to separate the seven-dimensional incidence divisor. This does not contradict the target, since P3 is projective.

## 4. Local Taylor separation and its limitation: accepted

The maximal-rank proof is correct. Among ranks of finite coefficient subcollections, the maximum r exists because ranks are integers between zero and d. Select a finite map attaining r and restrict to a constant-rank neighbourhood. Appending any further coefficient cannot raise rank, so its differential vanishes on the connected local fibres of the selected map. It is constant on each such fibre. If r<d, a fibre contains distinct points, contradicting joint point separation by all coefficients. Therefore r=d.

The corrected proof phrases density invariantly: the top exterior differential of the selected finite map is a holomorphic section nonzero somewhere, and connectedness makes its zero set have empty interior. This proves an open dense full-rank locus. It is a local separation result. Full rank on a noncompact domain alone does not make fibres globally finite, and it says nothing about compactification or meromorphic extension. The corrected statement explicitly preserves both limits.

For effective parameters of distinct reduced irreducible global cycles, a common nonempty open germ forces the same global irreducible analytic support and hence the same cycle. Applying the lemma to graph coefficients is therefore legitimate wherever an analytic graph chart with such germ separation is available. These hypotheses are not asserted automatically at every singular or reducible boundary point.

The local model has incidence a=0, point c, and fixed first tangent; its second derivative is 2b. From P=y-a-bx² one obtains P/P(0,0)=1-y/a+bx²/a. The logarithmic coefficients are b/a at x² and -1/(2a²) at y². Thus the shape variable appears at pole order one, below the maximal order two at total degree two. The calculation supports the stated gap in applying the source's leading-pole separation method. It is not a proof that every possible higher-jet global construction fails.

## 5. Incidence blow-up: accepted

The incidence variety of lines through o in P3 is Bl_o(P3), and its projection to the direction space P2 is a smooth P1-bundle. A line through o has normal O(1)⊕O(1) in P3. Its strict transform is one fibre of that bundle. Restricting the smooth-submersion tangent sequence identifies its normal bundle with the constant two-dimensional tangent space at the parameter point, tensored with O on the fibre. It is O⊕O and cannot be Griffiths positive.

The relevant transformation is therefore modification invariance of algebraic dimension without preservation of the chosen normal-bundle positivity. The example does not rule out other lifts or extra hypotheses. It disproves only the unqualified graph-resolution reduction as written.

## 6. Meromorphic fields on an elliptic K3: accepted

In the cited example NS(X)=Z[F] and F²=0. Every irreducible curve has zero intersection with F. If it dominated the base, that intersection would be the positive degree of its map to P1, a contradiction. Thus every curve is vertical.

For g∈M(X), its polar divisor has finitely many vertical components. Its indeterminacy set is finite on the smooth surface. Remove the corresponding finitely many base values and the singular-fibre values. On each remaining compact smooth elliptic fibre, g is holomorphic and hence constant. Proper connected-fibre meromorphic descent gives g=f*h for h∈M(P1); conversely pullback is injective. Therefore M(X)=f*M(P1), so a(X)=1, whereas both base and generic fibre have algebraic dimension one.

This disproves unqualified addition of base and fibre algebraic dimensions. It does not contradict the target: for a smooth curve C in X, deg N(C/X)=C²=0. The positivity hypothesis needed for the target is absent.

## 7. Boundary growth and finite traces: accepted

If h is holomorphic away from an effective Cartier divisor with local equation σ and satisfies |h|≤C|σ|^(-N), then σ^N h is holomorphic and locally bounded off the divisor. Normality gives holomorphic extension. The resulting local meromorphic fractions agree on overlaps by equality on the dense complement. Polynomial relations also restrict there, so algebraic independence is preserved. On compact irreducible S, p independent meromorphic functions and dim S=p imply a(S)=p.

For the generically finite proper holomorphic incidence evaluation G→Z, normalize G and use Stein factorization G→T→Z. The first map is a modification; the second is finite. A meromorphic function on T satisfies a degree-bounded monic polynomial over M(Z), obtained by symmetric functions of generic fibre values. Finite direct image and common denominators justify their meromorphic extension across branch and pole loci. In characteristic zero every finite generated intermediate extension is simple and has degree bounded by the geometric covering degree. The whole extension is finite, and a(G)=a(Z). Dominant pullback from S now gives a(Z)≥p. The corrected companion spells out this justification.

The crucial starting point is that these are already meromorphic functions on the whole compact space. This field argument cannot be applied to arbitrary holomorphic functions on a divisor complement. The packet does not derive the polynomial boundary bounds, or the required algebraically independent functions with those bounds, from the original target hypotheses.

For z=w^d and h(w)=exp(1/w), sum the absolutely convergent exponential series over the d inverse images. The root-of-unity sum kills precisely those powers whose index is not divisible by d. The resulting Laurent series is

    d Σ(k≥0) z^(-k)/(dk)!.

Every coefficient for k≥1 is nonzero, so there are infinitely many negative powers and the singularity at zero is essential. This is a local obstruction to an automatic trace-extension claim. The existence of the entire infinite tail follows from the formula and factorial nonvanishing, not from testing finitely many coefficients. No target counterexample is asserted.

## 8. Executable validation

The original checker is unchanged. Its exact, negative and all modes were run both normally and with deliberately injected failure under Python, Python -O and Python -OO: 18 combinations, all with the expected status and exit code. An invalid mode is also rejected under all three interpreter modes.

The independent suite performs 2,445 checks per interpreter mode:

- 219 dimension and incidence-evaluation checks
- 9 explicit normal-data and lifted-fibre checks
- 26 field-scope and intersection arithmetic checks
- 406 logarithmic coefficient checks, using an independent formal Euler-derivative/inverse-series oracle
- 1,323 trace checks, using explicit exact rational multiplication matrices at three rational values of z
- 450 finite trace-series coefficient and nonvanishing checks
- 6 documentary source-interface guards and 2 proof-scope guards
- 4 environment checks

Thus 2,433 checks concern finite mathematics, eight are documentary/scope guards, and four concern the environment. This distinction is deliberate. Source guards are not automated verification of the analytic source theorems; the geometry is accepted through the written audit above. No floating-point experiment is used.

Twenty-two named semantic mutations change dimensions, incidence conditions, the normal-versus-tangent data, positivity, algebraic-dimension addition, Taylor signs or pole order, trace degree/exponents/vanishing or tail truncation, source hypotheses, or local/global conclusion scope. Every mutant is rejected for its intended semantic family in all three Python modes: 66 rejections. These are separate from the original checker's intentionally injected failure flag.

Every run used actual real UID 1000 and effective UID 1000. The containing directory and files had no write bits. The suite attempted both opening an existing checker for writing without truncation and exclusive creation of a new probe file; both received PermissionError. Byte snapshots before and after were identical. This is genuine nonroot read-only validation, not a root-run or chmod-only claim.

Reproduction: make the packet directory and its files read-only to the UID/EUID 1000 interpreter, then run `python3 -B run_audit.py`. It prints JSON and requires no network, external library or scholarly-source bytes. A final frozen-byte rerun is recorded separately from the earlier validation snapshot.
