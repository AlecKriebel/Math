## 2. Proof attacks and their resolution

### Group generators versus Lie generators

Let the group generators be g1, g2, g3. The rational Lie subalgebra generated by their logarithms contains the logarithm of every word, by the finite BCH formula and inversion. Since the rational Malcev Lie algebra is the rational span of the group logarithms, those three logarithms generate it. Its nilpotency class is preserved under Malcev completion. Neither torsion in the ordinary abelianization nor a mismatch between integral and rational generator ranks defeats this argument.

If the minimal Lie generator count is two, the author's separate argument is valid. The kernel of the map from the free class-three Lie algebra on two chosen lifts lies in its commutator ideal, because abelianizations have equal dimension. A relation with nonzero degree-two part, bracketed with either free generator, kills both spanning degree-three commutators. The quotient would then have class at most two. Thus a class-three quotient has kernel entirely in the central homogeneous degree-three layer, and the usual positive grading descends. One generator cannot give class three.

### Rational grading and an arbitrary lattice

With a rational positive grading, scaling its degree-i space by q^(-i), q >= 2, is a rational Lie automorphism. Additivity of degrees makes it a bracket homomorphism, and every eigenvalue has modulus less than one. Its exponential is therefore a contracting automorphism alpha of the real Malcev group.

For the original lattice Gamma, the domain H = Gamma intersect alpha^(-1)(Gamma) has finite index. This uses actual commensurability of full subgroups of the same rational Malcev hull, a standard Malcev fact identified as an external input. It does not require alpha to preserve Gamma, and it does not replace Gamma by an easier commensurable lattice in the conclusion.

The independent controls include a rational but nonintegral map in an oblique lattice basis, with matrix [[1/2,1/12],[0,1/4]]. Its full intersection domain in integer coordinates is 2Z x 12Z, of index 24, while its image has index 3. Thus the method explicitly works without an invariant lattice or a surjective virtual endomorphism. This example tests a mechanism, not the universal grading theorem.

### Contracting iterates and the core

For any subgroup K of H with alpha(K) contained in K, every positive iterate of any k in K remains in Gamma and converges to the identity. Discreteness forces those iterates eventually to equal the identity, and injectivity gives k = 1. The argument actually excludes every invariant subgroup, not merely normal ones. It therefore covers the smaller normal core needed for faithful self-similarity. No uniform time over all k is required.

The same argument excludes H = Gamma since Gamma is nontrivial. Hence the finite alphabet has at least two letters. The direction of alpha, the domain intersection, and the invariance inclusion all agree; reversing contraction would invalidate the proof.

### Tree recursion and faithfulness

For left-coset representatives t_a, the equation g t_a = t_b h gives a well-defined section f(h). At any finite level, the first-letter map is a permutation and suffix maps are permutations by induction. Applying the two coset decompositions for g2 and then g1 produces section f(h1 h2) = f(h1)f(h2), proving the group law with the author's left-action convention. The recursion is compatible with deleting final letters and therefore defines rooted-tree automorphisms. All iterated sections remain in the image.

If g is in the action kernel, it fixes the coset H and lies in H. The section at the identity representative is f(g), which is also in the kernel. The preceding core argument now proves faithfulness. The direct proof claims neither boundary freeness nor level transitivity beyond what it establishes; those stronger properties are available separately from Mathieu. A faithful action on the finite-word tree also gives a faithful boundary action, so there is no definition mismatch.
