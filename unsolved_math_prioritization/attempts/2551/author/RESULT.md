# A credited grading argument for Kourovka 21.42

## 1. Exact question and evidence status

The catalogue identity is 2551 / KOU-21.42, rank 768. The October 2026 twenty-first Kourovka Notebook, printed and PDF page 183, attributes the question to **A. Dantas and S. Sidki**. Its class T_{d,c} consists of d-generated torsion-free nilpotent groups of class c. The question is whether T_{3,3} contains a group that is not self-similar. Here self-similar means admitting a faithful state-closed action on a regular rooted tree of some finite degree. There is no fixed degree requirement and no finite-state requirement.

The neighbouring 21.41 supplies that definition. The accompanying 21.42 discussion credits the class-two theorem, the two-generator class-three theorem, and a four-generator class-three non-self-similar example to Berlatto–Santos, arXiv:2509.16947. These bounds identify the exact target; they are not themselves its solution. In that preprint the target reappears as Problem 1 on p.4. The PDF prints the second author's name as Tulio Gentil while the arXiv metadata and Notebook say Tulio Santos; the identifier is the unambiguous reference.

The October Notebook has no solution footnote or status marker on 21.42. That is an observation about the source, not an assertion that no consequence of older work can answer it.

## 2. Earlier results being used

The following are external mathematical inputs, not results claimed by this packet.

**G (positive-gradability).** Three-generated nilpotent Lie algebras of class three in characteristic zero admit positive integer gradings. The attributed source is K. Dekimpe, P. Igodt and H. Pouseele, *Expanding automorphisms and affine structures on nilpotent Lie algebras with few generators*, Communications in Algebra 31 (2003), 5847–5874, DOI [10.1081/AGB-120024857](https://doi.org/10.1081/AGB-120024857).

The 2003 full paper was unavailable in this investigation; no theorem number or full-proof inspection is invented. The needed positive, not merely arbitrary Z-graded, assertion is explicit in K. Dekimpe and J. Deré, *Expanding maps and non-trivial self-covers on infra-nilmanifolds*, [arXiv:1407.8106v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.8106v1), p.13, in the proof of Theorem 6.1. It is also present in the published paper, Topological Methods in Nonlinear Analysis 47 (2016), p.365, DOI [10.12775/TMNA.2016.010](https://doi.org/10.12775/TMNA.2016.010), referring to the 2003 work as reference [9]. The arXiv copy was downloaded in full and that page visually inspected. It explicitly treats rational Lie algebras in the surrounding argument. The university's abstract independently identifies the three-generator/class-three scope; it alone is not used to infer positivity.

**F (field descent, if needed).** A finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a characteristic-zero field has a positive grading if and only if its scalar extension does. This is Cornulier, *Gradings on Lie algebras, systolic growth, and cohopfian properties of nilpotent groups*, Theorem 1.4 / Theorem 3.25; published in Bull. Soc. Math. France 144 (2016), 693–744, DOI [10.24033/bsmf.2725](https://doi.org/10.24033/bsmf.2725). Thus a version of G over the reals or complexes also gives a rational positive grading for the rational Malcev Lie algebra. The inspected author PDF is [here](https://www.normalesup.org/~cornulier/carnotext.pdf). “Contractable” in that source means admitting a positive integer grading.

**M (Malcev correspondence).** A finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group Gamma embeds as a discrete cocompact lattice in a simply connected real nilpotent Lie group N. Its rational Malcev Lie algebra L spans Lie(N), has the same class, and is generated as a rational Lie algebra by the logarithms of any group generating set. Rational automorphisms of L exponentiate to automorphisms of N taking Gamma to a commensurable lattice. These are standard Malcev-theory facts; the rational-hull setup and commensurability statements are used in the inspected Dekimpe–Deré Sections 1–2 and Mathieu's paper.

**S (optional shorter conclusion).** Mathieu, [*Which Nilpotent Groups are Self-Similar?*, arXiv:2101.11291v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11291v1), Theorem 3, proves that a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group admits a free self-similar action with dense orbits when its complex Malcev Lie algebra has a Z-grading with zero degree-zero component. A positive grading has this property. That theorem's statement was visually checked on p.2; it is an external input, not a claimed independent reproof of Mathieu's general theorem.

## 3. Conclusion from the cited theorems

Let Gamma be in T_{3,3}. Let L be its rational Malcev Lie algebra. By M, L is generated by at most three elements and has class three. If its minimal number of generators is three, apply G, and F if required, to obtain a rational positive integer grading

    L = direct sum of L_i for i >= 1,   [L_i,L_j] contained in L_{i+j}.

For completeness, if the minimal Lie generator number is two, its positive grading needs no three-generator convention: the free class-three Lie algebra on x,y has basis x,y,[x,y],[x,[x,y]],[y,[x,y]]. A quotient with two-dimensional abelianization and class three can have no relation with a nonzero degree-two part. Such a relation, bracketed with x and y, would kill both degree-three basis elements, reducing class to at most two. Its defining ideal therefore lies in the homogeneous degree-three part, and the standard degree grading descends. Minimal generator number one is incompatible with class three.

By S, Gamma is freely densely self-similar, hence self-similar in the exact Notebook sense. Alternatively, the direct proof in Section 4 establishes the faithful state-closed tree action without S. Therefore:

**There is no non-self-similar group in T_{3,3}.**

The conclusion is credited as a consequence of earlier results. A first explicit publication of this exact corollary, or historical priority for recognizing it, has not been established here. The original 2003 proof remains an imported theorem, not independently reconstructed. The classification uses only the cited, inspectable theorem chain.

## 4. Direct bridge from a rational positive grading

This section proves the implication from a rational positive grading to the action required in the question. Its only background inputs are M and the elementary exponential/BCH correspondence for nilpotent Lie groups.

### 4.1 A contracting rational automorphism

Fix an integer q >= 2. Define a linear map d on L by d(v)=q^{-i}v for v in L_i. For homogeneous u in L_i and v in L_j,

    [d(u),d(v)] = q^{-(i+j)}[u,v] = d([u,v]).

By bilinearity this holds for arbitrary u,v, and d is invertible. Thus d is a rational Lie algebra automorphism. Let alpha be the corresponding group automorphism of N. All eigenvalues of d are positive numbers strictly below one, so d^n X tends to zero for every X. Since exponential is a homeomorphism, alpha^n(g) tends to the identity for every g in N.

Set H = Gamma intersect alpha^{-1}(Gamma). By M, H has finite index in Gamma. The map f = alpha restricted to H is an injective homomorphism H -> Gamma.

If K is any subgroup of H satisfying f(K) contained in K, then for k in K every alpha^n(k) lies in Gamma. These elements converge to the identity. Discreteness of Gamma implies alpha^n(k)=1 for all sufficiently large n. Since alpha is injective, k=1. Hence K is trivial. In particular there is no nontrivial Gamma-normal f-invariant subgroup of H.

The index m=[Gamma:H] is finite and at least two. If H=Gamma, any nonidentity g in Gamma would have all its contracting iterates in the discrete group Gamma, which the preceding argument rules out.

### 4.2 The rooted-tree action, including its kernel

Choose one representative t_a for each left coset of H, with t_0=1, and let A be the finite set of cosets. Given g in Gamma and a in A, write uniquely

    g t_a = t_b h,  with h in H.

Define the action on a finite word a w recursively by

    rho(g)(a w) = b rho(f(h))(w),

and fix the empty word. On each level this is a permutation: its first-letter action is the left coset permutation, and induction handles the suffixes. The same decomposition, applied to a product g_1 g_2, shows by induction that rho(g_1 g_2)=rho(g_1)rho(g_2). Hence rho is a group homomorphism to the automorphisms of the regular m-ary rooted tree. Every section is again rho of an element of Gamma, by the displayed formula, so its image is state-closed.

Let K be its kernel. It is normal in Gamma. Elements of K fix the first level, so K is contained in H. For g in K, take the coset represented by t_0=1; because g lies in H, the section there is rho(f(g)). It is trivial, so f(g) lies in K. Section 4.1 gives K=1. Thus rho is faithful. No surjectivity of f, uniform tree degree, or assertion about arbitrary lattice compatibility was used.

This completes the required implication for every Gamma individually. It does not assume that an arbitrary chosen integer dilation preserves an arbitrary chosen lattice: the finite-index intersection H is what fixes that issue.

## 5. Exact illustrative class-three group

This example is a reproducible check of the bridge and of a common grading pitfall. It is not the proof of the universal result.

Let L have the ordered rational basis x,y,z,u,v,A,B,C,D,E. Its nonzero brackets, up to antisymmetry, are

    [x,y]=D,  [x,z]=u,  [y,z]=v,
    [x,u]=A, [y,u]=B, [x,v]=B, [y,v]=C,
    [z,u]=D, [z,v]=E.

All of A,B,C,D,E are central, and all other brackets vanish. The only possibly nontrivial Jacobi identity involving three distinct generators is the one for x,y,z: its first two terms are B and -B and the third is zero. Triples involving a commutator-layer vector have zero outer brackets or brackets between commutator-layer vectors. Thus Jacobi holds. The lower central series dimensions are 10,7,5,0. The elements x,y,z generate L.

The positive weights

    (1,2,1,2,3,3,4,5,3,4)

make every displayed bracket homogeneous. The standard attempted weights (1,1,1,2,2,3,3,3,3,3) do not: [x,y]=D would have degrees two and three. One must find an actual Lie algebra grading; the associated lower-central graded algebra cannot simply be substituted for L.

Use the class-three BCH law

    X * Y = X + Y + [X,Y]/2
            + ([X,[X,Y]] + [Y,[Y,X]])/12.

The set Lambda=12 Z^{10} in this basis is closed under BCH and inverse, since all bracket structure constants are integral and the displayed denominators divide 12. It is a discrete cocompact subgroup of the real BCH group.

Let Gamma_0 be the subgroup generated by exp(12x), exp(12y), exp(12z). The logarithms of their pair commutators have leading components 144u and 144v, respectively. Their five corresponding triple commutators have logarithms 1728A, 1728B, 1728C, 1728D, 1728E. Along with the three generators these span L over Q. Malcev theory therefore makes Gamma_0 a full lattice (finite index in Lambda). It is torsion-free, has class exactly three, and needs exactly three group generators because its rational abelianization has dimension three.

The integral dilation delta_2 of the displayed grading maps the three group generators to their powers 2,4,2, so delta_2(Gamma_0) is contained in Gamma_0. Its index is the determinant of delta_2, namely 2^{28}. The inverse map on that image contracts, so Section 4 yields a faithful self-similar action on a tree of that degree. No minimal-degree claim is made.

## 6. Negative controls and boundaries

1. **A nonnegative grading is insufficient for this contraction argument.** On the additive group Z^2, take H=2Z times Z and f(2a,b)=(a,b). The nonzero subgroup 0 times Z is f-invariant and normal, so this datum has a nontrivial core. The group Z^2 is of course self-similar using a different datum.
2. **Real contraction without rationality is insufficient for this lattice datum.** Multiplication by 1/sqrt(2) on R has domain Z intersect sqrt(2)Z={0} if its values must lie in Z. That domain has infinite index. If nonzero integers k,l satisfied k^2=2l^2, the parity of their 2-adic valuations would contradict the equality.
3. **Uniform scalar multiplication is usually not a Lie/group homomorphism.** On a nonabelian Lie algebra, [X/q,Y/q]=[X,Y]/q^2 differs from [X,Y]/q. The grading-dependent powers are essential.
4. **Testing examples does not prove G.** The exact controls verify the displayed ten-dimensional example and the mechanism. The proof of G is a stated literature dependency, and no search over finitely many groups replaces it.
5. **No converse is claimed.** Absence of a positive grading would not alone prove that a group is not self-similar. Mathieu's criterion permits more general gradings, and the original question permits intransitive actions as well.
