# Five substantive attempts

Date: 3 October 2026. Target: Przytycki Problem 1.2, 5300049 / AMR-052-0049.

The five items below are proof approaches, not five literature searches. Supporting source recovery and status checking are recorded separately. None produced a full resolution.

## 1. Empirical-measure transfer

Built invariant empirical limits from the specified orbit and handled the logarithmic singularities by continuous truncation. Proposition 1 proves that every limit has derivative-potential integral at least the original lower exponent. This is stronger than merely selecting a favorable subsequence of derivatives.

Tried to transfer the known accessibility conclusion for invariant measures back to the original point. The exact square-digit orbit for z² defeats that step: it has exponent log 2 but its empirical limit is the point mass at 1, and the starting point lies outside that measure's support. The example is accessible and therefore is explicitly not a counterexample to the original question.

Result: a proved measure-production lemma; no pointwise transfer.

## 2. Expanding times and critical-approach estimates

Used maxima of adjusted partial sums to prove the quantitative record-time estimate in Proposition 2. It gives positive lower density of times at which every suffix derivative product expands exponentially.

Tested whether stronger one-step temperedness or a negligible negative-tail average could be obtained without further dynamical input. Proposition 3 supplies an exact dyadic-spike sequence with lower average 1/2 and a negative term of asymptotic size n/4 at dyadic times. This blocks that scalar shortcut, without asserting that the sequence is a holomorphic orbit.

Reconciled this with the modern source: Theorem 8.1 actually uses positive lower exponent and does not require the extra scalar estimates proposed here. Its surviving basin-side hypothesis, rather than a supposed need for an exponent limit, is the relevant gap.

Result: a proved density estimate and a precise limit on an overly strong numerical inference.

## 3. Local inverse contraction

Constructed a finite-length access by repeatedly applying a contracting inverse branch to a polygonal bridge. Proposition 4 includes all hypotheses needed to keep the curve in U and make its endpoints concatenate; the total length has an explicit geometric-series bound.

For a repelling periodic point, the local inverse exists and contracts after restricting its disk. The proof stops at preservation of the U side and the return of a point to its local connected component. Neither follows merely from the existence of the ambient analytic inverse in the argument given.

Result: a complete sufficient-condition proof; the added component/basin hypotheses remain unproved in general.

## 4. Nested approach domains

Proved the necessary and sufficient nested connected open-set criterion in Proposition 5, including both directions and the endpoint continuity argument.

Tried to obtain these sets by approximating the desired point with accessible points. A simply connected slit-comb domain has accessible points converging to an inaccessible point. Its inaccessibility follows directly from the intermediate value theorem. There is no proposed holomorphic basin dynamics on the comb.

Result: an exact topological characterization and a rigorous rejection of density-to-accessibility inference.

## 5. Coding-tree compactness

Examined a diagonal argument that would use uniform shrinking of edges and compactness of binary itineraries. Proposition 6 constructs an abstract binary tree with edge bound 2/(n+2), every branch convergent, and a nonclosed endpoint set. It gives explicit branches for which symbolic convergence does not imply convergence to the endpoint of the limiting branch.

The source theorem's significant, basin-side telescopes supply information missing from this abstract model. The abstract construction is neither an embedded tree nor a holomorphic coding tree, and makes no claim against that theorem.

Result: a precise counterexample to the bare compactness shortcut; no construction of the necessary telescopes in the original general setting.

## Final position

All five approaches are complete to the endpoints just described. The paper's propositions and examples are the deliverable, with an explicit unresolved gap. No inaccessible positive-lower-exponent point in a qualifying holomorphic basin has been constructed, and no proof that all such points are accessible has been obtained. The completely invariant rational attracting-basin subcase is covered by the cited pointwise literature theorem.
