## Full target, not an isolated extraction

Primary source: Calegari, *Problems in foliations and laminations of 3-manifolds*, arXiv:math/0209081v1, Section7, printedpp13–14. Both target pages were rendered and visually inspected, and surrounding Section7 plus Definitions1.3 and1.9 were read. The question seeks conditions for Haken addition of two normal laminations relative to one triangulation. Its remark emphasizes the unmeasured setting, requires compatible quadrilateral types, and then proposes studying carried laminations modulo monotone equivalence with a suitable topology, possibly star-like or contractible.

The original does not specify a unique addition law, a precise monotone-equivalence relation for this space, or that topology. The question itself does not impose essentiality, coorientation, or transverse measures. Any partial theorem needing those assumptions must say so. A linear measured-weight cone is not a solution of the general unmeasured question. The neighboring taut-ideal-triangulation problem is a separate question and does not impose its hypotheses on Question7.4.

## Credited foundations and boundaries

Brittenham, *Essential laminations and Haken normal form*, PJM168(1995), pp217–219, defines normality by compact disk plaques meeting each tetrahedron edge at most once. Its general normalization theorem may replace the original essential lamination. The companion no-holonomy paper, CAG3(1995), Theorem1 p465, instead isotopes the original lamination. These statements do not assert simultaneous compatible normal forms for a given pair.

Schleimer's primary thesis Section2.6, pp14–16, gives the classical finite normal-surface coordinate/Haken-sum framework and regular exchanges. We credit this framework rather than promote its measured or finite specialization as new.

Hatcher's cited *Measured Lamination Spaces for 3-Manifolds*, author PDF, explicitly describes itself as an unfinished draft. Its pp7–9 explain sector-weight equations and the associated measured construction, including complementary-I-bundle identifications. These measured coordinates should not be assumed to classify arbitrary unmeasured holonomy. Unproved global assertions from that manuscript will not be used as established theorems.
