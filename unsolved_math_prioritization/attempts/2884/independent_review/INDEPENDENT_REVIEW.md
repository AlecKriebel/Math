## 3. Ambient and torus-complement hypotheses

The regular E(2) fiber has product tubular neighborhood, so square zero is
immediate. Removing a small regular-value disk in the base gives a Lefschetz
fibration over a disk containing the singular values. Its total space is built
from F x D^2 by adding the Lefschetz 2-handles. Thus its fundamental group is
that of F modulo the normal subgroup generated by the vanishing cycles.
For the standard (alpha beta)^12 factorization, alpha and beta kill both
fundamental generators of the torus. The complement is consequently simply
connected. This proves the required complement property; the mere fact that
E(2) is simply connected would not have proved it.

The cited monodromy factorization and its regular-fiber use were checked in
Choi–Park–Yun pp.741–742. Their isolated Euler-characteristic typo is not used.
The arithmetic monodromy identity in the checker is only a control of this
known fibration description, not an independent proof that a matrix identity
constructs the required complex elliptic surface.

A connected-sum ball can be chosen disjoint from the fiber neighborhood.
Removing such a ball from a connected 4-manifold does not change pi_1, by
van Kampen on its simply connected S^3 interface. Applying this twice proves
that the fiber complement in X=E(2)#(S^2 x S^2) remains simply connected.
Closedness and smoothness are preserved, as is the fixed fiber framing.
The exterior of the open tubular neighborhood and the complement of the
central torus have the same relevant homotopy type by radial retraction in
normal punctured disks.

## 4. The stabilization is in the original ambient

The key equation is a locality statement before any theorem is applied:

    (E(2)#H)_K is diffeomorphic to E(2)_K#H,  H=S^2 x S^2.

The surgery and connected sum take place in disjoint regions, with identical
peripheral gluing data on the common torus boundary. Their cut-and-paste
pieces therefore identify directly. Baykur's theorem then sends the right
side to E(2)#H. Hence every selected standard surgery in this single fixed
ambient is diffeomorphic to that ambient.

There is no cancellation of H, no inference of unstabilized diffeomorphism,
and no need for the stabilization diffeomorphism to preserve the original
fiber or the connected-sum ball. One allowed fixed surgery pattern for both
knots suffices for the claimed counterexample. The proof does not require
all other possible boundary identifications to give the same diffeomorphism
type. Smooth orientation-compatible gluings are available; the source's
unqualified diffeomorphism conclusion in particular follows.

## 5. The knots meet the prime and nonmirror conditions

The two positive two-braid closures are single-component knots. Their
two-disk/q-band surfaces give genera at most (q-1)/2. Independent Fox/Alexander
calculation from the torus-knot presentation gives

    Delta_T(2,q)(t) = (t^q+1)/(t+1), q odd,

so the degree/genus inequality proves equality for q=3,5. This agrees with
the author's Seifert matrices and polynomials. Their determinants are 3 and5,
which are mirror-invariant, excluding equality even up to mirror.

Primeness is not inferred from irreducible Alexander polynomial alone. For
these full-degree examples, irreducibility forces a putative nontrivial
connected-sum factor to have unit Alexander polynomial; the other factor
already needs the entire genus by the degree bound. The first nontrivial
factor would have positive ordinary genus, contradicting Schubert additivity.
The polynomial irreducibility arguments are valid: negative quadratic
discriminant and the Eisenstein shift of Phi_5, respectively. The author
correctly distinguishes the ordinary-genus theorem recalled in Ozawa's
introduction from Ozawa's new free-genus result. This is a classical input,
not a claim to have consulted Schubert's original 1949 manuscript.
