# Source access and credit

Checked 2026-10-01. Full mathematical text was obtained as described below; publisher previews are not presented as full-text access. PDFs, extracted text and page images remain local reading copies, not repository artifacts. Exact PDF hashes are in source_manifest.json.

1. R. İ. Baykur, R. C. Kirby and D. Ruberman, *K3: A New Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topology*, AMS Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 295, author preliminary version. [Permissioned author PDF](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf), Problem 4.8, p.197. Exact problem and remarks read and visually checked. A fresh download matched the cached full PDF byte-for-byte. This is the modern K3 problem number, not an identification by number with an earlier Kirby-list edition. The imported AIM [workshop report](https://aimath.org/pastworkshops/kirbylistrep.pdf) is a different four-page document and is not the full statement source. No AMS PDF is reposted.

2. R. İ. Baykur, *Dissolving knot surgered 4-manifolds by classical cobordism arguments*. [Full arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04491v3), Theorem 1 p.1, entire proof p.2, and Remark 2 pp.2–3. All three pages read; theorem visually checked. Published in J. Knot Theory Ramifications 27(5) (2018), 1871001, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216518710013). Full typeset publisher PDF was not obtained; the complete revised author manuscript supplies the mathematical access. Earlier stabilization inputs are Akbulut (2002) and Auckly (2003), explicitly credited in this paper.

3. H. Choi, J. Park and K.-H. Yun, *On dissolving knot surgery 4-manifolds under a CP²-connected sum*, Asian J. Math. 23(5) (2019), 735–748. [Full published PDF](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/ajm/2019/0023/0005/AJM-2019-0023-0005-a002.pdf). Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3 p.736; standard longitude gluing p.738; proof Section 3, especially pp.741–742 regular-fiber monodromy/double-node matching. These parts read; theorem page rendered and visually checked. We use the proof's standard (αβ)^(6n) monodromy, not its isolated typographical Euler-characteristic line without the factor n. This theorem is an additional published gluing/stabilization cross-check, with its extra double-node hypothesis kept explicit.

4. R. Fintushel and R. J. Stern, *Knots, links, and 4-manifolds*, Invent. Math. 134 (1998), 363–400. [Full arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9612014v2). Construction and theorem context in introduction pp.1–5 read. The zero-surgery/meridian fiber-sum model and the permitted nonunique boundary choices are relevant; we choose a fixed standard framing, rather than asserting a gluing-independent theorem. A new proof of the Seiberg–Witten formula is unnecessary here and was not attempted.

5. M. Ozawa, *Additivity of free genus of knots*, [full arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811045), introduction p.1. This is used only as an explicit primary-literature locator recalling Schubert's **ordinary** genus additivity, not for its distinct free-genus Theorem 1.1. Schubert's original 1949 text was not obtained. Classical Alexander polynomial multiplication and the degree/genus bound are also stated inputs in our elementary prime-knot check.

6. S. Akbulut, *Knot surgery and Scharlemann manifolds*, [full arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6274). Introduction/Section 1 read as historical corroboration. No argument depends on its unreviewed handle diagrams or its examples with a different torus-complement fundamental group. This is an auxiliary source, not a replacement for Baykur's theorem.
