## 2. Multiplicity and marked groups

Proposition 1 is correct in its stated canonical framing. For an oriented torus in the oriented sphere, the normal Euler number equals its self-intersection, which is zero. An oriented rank-two normal bundle over a torus with zero Euler class is trivial. Alexander duality identifies the exterior's first integral homology with one meridian generator. Changing the two normal framing degrees cancels the two longitude images independently; this is the homologically zero framing of the whole-ambient torus exterior.

Let the last row of the integral gluing matrix be `(u,v,p)`. Mayer–Vietoris presents the surgered first homology by generators `(mu,x,y)` and relation matrix

    [-u  1  0]
    [-v  0  1]
    [ p  0  0].

Add `u` times column two and `v` times column three to column one. Permuting columns to `(x,y,mu)` gives `diag(1,1,p)`. Every operation is unimodular, so the result is cyclic of order `|p|` for nonzero `p`, and infinite cyclic for `p=0`. The connected-piece H0 map is injective and contributes no additional H1 generator. Therefore `p=1` or `p=-1` is necessary for standard S4. This works with either sign convention for the oriented boundary coordinates. Primitivity of the meridian image follows from the gluing being an integral automorphism.

The condition is not asserted sufficient. In particular, it does not prove trivial fundamental group or smooth standardness. The fundamental-group pushout reduces to the stated quotient because the map from the boundary torus group onto the filled neighborhood's group is surjective and its kernel is normally generated by the source meridian. With the surface neighborhood removed, the same quotient carries the images of the surface's peripheral data. “Retained meridians” means retained markings under the quotient, not a guarantee that their images remain nontrivial. A homology computation of the ambient manifold cannot determine those data or a surface isotopy.

The framing warning matters: longitudes killed in the whole-ambient torus exterior need not be killed in the complement of both the torus and the tracked surface. The proof does not interchange these exteriors.

## 3. Hopf-link diagnostic and smooth spinning

Proposition 2 is correct, including the strong smooth endpoint claim. The exterior of the standard Hopf link is a product torus cylinder. The two preferred zero-surgery slopes become transverse primitive meridians on its two ends. Their geometric intersection number is one, giving the ordinary smooth genus-one Heegaard gluing for S3. This identifies the resulting 3-manifold, rather than merely computing its homology. By contrast, doing either zero surgery first is zero surgery on a single unknot, and yields S1 × S2.

For the product spinning construction, the omitted 3-ball and the spin cap have their specified product gluing. The cap kills exactly the extra circle factor in the first piece's fundamental group. Thus spinning preserves the original 3-manifold's group; the intermediate group and H1 are both infinite cyclic. Neither ordering has an admissible first step for the target requirement.

The derivative explicitly identifies the spin of S3 with the boundary of the rounded product B3 × D2, a standard smooth 5-ball. Its boundary is standard smooth S4. In dimension three the punctured output of the displayed S3 identification is B3, so the same argument applies after the double Dehn filling. No four-dimensional Poincaré or Schoenflies conjecture is invoked.

Choose a boundary-parallel arc away from a smaller ball containing the link. Its product spin with the two standard cap disks is an unknotted 2-sphere disjoint from both surgery tori. Thus this is a genuine surface-complement example. Its surface is already unknotted, so it is only a counterexample to the inference “standard final ambient manifold implies acceptable intermediate stages.” It does not obstruct other sequences for that surface, much less disprove KP-4.34.

## 4. Known spun route and pair tracking

The stagewise crossing-circle construction is valid. A crossing change is realized by coefficient ±1 surgery on an unknot in the current classical knot complement; its ambient output is smoothly S3. Choose the next crossing circle in that current pair and spin that step. Each output is then standard smooth S4 and the tracked knot changes as intended. Ending at the classical unknot ends at the standard spun sphere. This is a stagewise use of Larson's known theorem and product construction, not a new arbitrary-surface theorem.

The cited Montesinos theorem, as proved in Larson's Theorem 5.1, recognizes the output for an unknotted surgery torus. It does not prove a proposed torus unknotted or identify its action on another surface. The proof correctly avoids using the integer multiplicity as an ambient recognition theorem.

The precise pair-neutral lemma needs an original boundary identification `iota: boundary N -> boundary E` and an exterior diffeomorphism with restriction `phi composed with inverse iota`. Since the tracked surface is closed and lies in the interior, collars of the surgery boundary can be chosen away from it. Collar straightening gives product form without moving the surface. The exterior inverse and the identity on N then glue smoothly and carry the surface to itself. An isotopic boundary restriction can first be corrected in the collar. If E instead means the exterior of both torus and surface, the extra boundary map must extend over the deleted surface neighborhood preserving its core. Additional marked boundary conditions must also be respected. A homology matrix alone does not supply these extension conditions.

Complement surgery preserves the actual neighborhood of the tracked surface, so its intrinsic type and normal bundle survive. This prevents identifying a single complement surgery with a genus-changing internal stabilization. It does not exclude a more elaborate genus-preserving realization of a combined stabilization, isotopy, and destabilization. Likewise, canceling an external stabilization later does not make its intermediate ambient manifold S4. These are correctly presented as missing constructions, not as obstructions proving a negative answer.
