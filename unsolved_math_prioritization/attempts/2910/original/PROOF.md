# Complement torus surgeries: scope and necessary tests

Problem 2910 / KP-4.34. This is an elementary partial audit, not a solution.

## 1. Category and data

Work in the smooth category. A stage consists of a smooth pair `(X, F)`, a torus `T` disjoint from `F`, a product neighborhood `N = T^2 × D^2` disjoint from `F`, and a boundary gluing `φ: ∂N → ∂(X \ int N)`. The new surface is the unchanged copy of `F` in the exterior. Every individual stage in the target must again have ambient manifold diffeomorphic to the standard `S^4`; the last pair must be standard. A topological locally flat version is a separate question. None of the deductions below solves it.

There is no genus or orientability restriction in the target being audited. Spun 2-knots below are only a subclass. For a nonorientable surface a standard target must also have the appropriate normal Euler number, because complement surgery leaves its tubular neighborhood unchanged.

## 2. The single-torus multiplicity test

**Proposition 1.** Let `T` be any smooth embedded torus in `S^4`. Choose its canonical homologically zero normal framing. Write the image of the glued-in meridian as the primitive slope

`a α + b β + p μ`.

Here `α, β` are the framed torus longitudes and `μ` is the old meridian. If `X_T` is the result, then

`H_1(X_T; Z) = Z / pZ`,

with `Z / 0Z = Z`. Consequently `X_T ≅ S^4` requires `|p| = 1`.

**Proof.** The normal Euler number of `T` is its self-intersection, which is zero because `H_2(S^4; Z)=0`. Its oriented normal 2-plane bundle is therefore trivial. For `E = S^4 \ int N`, Alexander duality gives `H_1(E; Z)=Z`, generated by a meridian. Starting with any framing, its two longitude classes in this group are integer multiples of the meridian. Twisting the normal disk coordinate along either generator of `T` subtracts that multiple. Thus a framing with both longitudes nullhomologous exists.

Use source boundary coordinates `(x,y,m)` for `∂N`. If the bottom row of the gluing matrix in target coordinates `(α,β,μ)` is `(u,v,p)`, the Mayer–Vietoris presentation of `H_1(X_T)` has generators `(μ,x,y)` and relations

`x = u μ`, `y = v μ`, `p μ = 0`.

Indeed the source boundary loops map to `(x,y,0)` in `H_1(N)`, whereas their exterior images are `(u μ,v μ,p μ)`. There is no extra generator from `H_0`, because both pieces and their intersection are connected. Eliminating `x,y` proves the formula. Since `S^4` has zero first homology, its necessary multiplicity is `±1`. ∎

This is a standard calculation, also present in Larson, §§2.1–2.3. It is rederived here to make the framing convention explicit. The canonical framing is for the torus exterior in the **whole ambient sphere**, not for the complement of both the torus and `F`.

The condition `|p|=1` proves only the necessary homology test. It neither proves simple connectivity nor identifies the smooth manifold. With compatible base paths, van Kampen gives

`π_1(X_T) = π_1(E) / <<φ_*(m)>>`.

This follows because `π_1(∂N) → π_1(N)` is surjective with kernel normally generated by `m`. The same argument, with `E_F = X \ (int νF ∪ int N)`, gives the marked surface-exterior group after surgery as `π_1(E_F) / <<φ_*(m)>>`. Meridians of `F` are retained. An ambient homology calculation alone loses that information.

## 3. Why the final ambient manifold is insufficient

**Proposition 2.** There are two complement torus surgeries in `S^4` whose combined result is `S^4`, but performing either component first yields a manifold with first homology `Z`.

**Proof.** Put the standard Hopf link `L_1 ∪ L_2` in the interior of a 3-ball, and give both components coefficient zero relative to their preferred longitudes. The Hopf exterior is `T^2 × I`. Under its product identification, the longitude on either end is the meridian on the other end (up to harmless signs). Filling both zero slopes therefore glues two solid tori so that their meridians have intersection number one. This is the standard genus-one Heegaard description of `S^3`. Filling just one component is zero surgery on an unknot, giving `S^1 × S^2`.

For a closed connected orientable 3-manifold `M`, put

`spin(M) = (M \ int B^3) × S^1 ∪_{S^2 × S^1} S^2 × D^2`.

Start with `spin(S^3)=S^4`. Each product torus `L_i × S^1` has a product tubular neighborhood, and using the 3-dimensional filling map times the identity of `S^1` produces the spin of the corresponding Dehn-filled manifold. Hence the two surgeries together give `spin(S^3)=S^4`.

Van Kampen gives `π_1(spin(M)) = π_1(M)`: the first piece has group `π_1(M) × Z`, the cap is simply connected, and the attaching `S^2 × S^1` kills exactly the second factor. Thus the intermediate manifold has fundamental group, and first homology, `Z`, so it is not `S^4`. Either component is initially an unknot, so either ordering has this defect.

A boundary-parallel arc can be chosen in the initial ball outside a smaller ball containing the Hopf link. Spinning and capping this arc gives an unknotted 2-sphere disjoint from both tori. The example is therefore literally a sequence of surgeries in a surface complement. ∎

The product-surgery construction is the one in Larson, Proposition 3.6. The Hopf-link example only refutes an inference from the final ambient manifold to every intermediate one. It is **not** a counterexample to KP-4.34; its chosen surface is already unknotted, and the target permits choosing a different surgery sequence.

## 4. The valid classical spinning route

Larson's Theorem 3.9 gives the known spun-knot result. Its stagewise application is transparent: choose a classical unknotting sequence and apply the product construction to one crossing circle at a time. Each crossing-circle surgery has coefficient `+1` or `-1` on an unknot and returns a smooth `S^3`; the tracked classical knot changes by that crossing change. Spinning each pair produces standard `S^4` at every stage and ends at the spun unknot. This does not furnish a classical spinning presentation for an arbitrary surface.

For the separate ambient test, Montesinos' theorem, as presented with a proof in Larson's Theorem 5.1, says that multiplicity-one surgery on an unknotted torus in `S^4` returns standard `S^4`. This can certify an explicitly exhibited torus, but is not an existence theorem for an unknotting sequence. Neither the unknottedness of a proposed torus nor its action on the marked surface follows from the integer `p` alone.

## 5. Pair tracking and stabilization do not disappear

**Proposition 3 (pair-neutral gluing).** Identify the original pair as `(E ∪_id N, F)`. Suppose the surgery gluing `φ` extends to a diffeomorphism `h:E→E` that sends `F` to itself. Then `(E ∪_φ N, F)` is diffeomorphic as a pair to the original pair.

**Proof.** On the new exterior use `h^{-1}`, and on `N` use the identity. For `z ∈ ∂N`, the new identification joins `z` to `φ(z)=h(z)`; the two proposed maps send these points to the same point `z` in the old gluing. They therefore fit to a diffeomorphism of pairs. ∎

In particular, an ambient-standardness argument whose exterior extension also fixes the surface does not unknot a previously nonstandard pair. An extension used to recognize the ambient manifold must still be checked for its effect on the surface.

Complement surgery also leaves the surface's intrinsic diffeomorphism type and normal bundle unchanged: its entire tubular neighborhood survives untouched. It therefore cannot literally equal a single internal stabilization that changes the surface's genus. This observation does not exclude replacing a **combined** stabilization–isotopy–destabilization process by a more subtle genus-preserving sequence. Such a replacement is exactly missing. Likewise, an external stabilization changes the ambient manifold; canceling it later does not certify the individual intermediate spheres, as Proposition 2 already illustrates.

## 6. Exact remaining gap

No construction here associates to every embedded surface a sequence of complement tori and slopes that (i) all have standard smooth ambient output, (ii) are tracked through the successive identifications, and (iii) yield a standard final surface. No obstruction ruling out all such sequences is established either. The three approaches stop at these geometric existence and pair-tracking gaps. The full problem remains unresolved by this work. No novelty is claimed for these elementary deductions or the cited results.
