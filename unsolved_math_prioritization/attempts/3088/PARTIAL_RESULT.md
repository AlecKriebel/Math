# Partitioning RP²: restricted rigidity and a finite-search boundary

Problem3088 / OPG-56328. **Unsolved**, with restricted lemmas and a finite coloring certificate. No regularity of the four original parts is assumed in the problem, and none is silently added to the conclusion.

## Exact source

Jonathan Noel's [original MathOverflow post](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/140413/partitioning-the-projective-plane) and [Open Problem Garden statement](https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/partitioning_the_projective_plane) ask whether every part of a four-part partition of RP² must lie in one pair of opposite closed octahedral face cones if every monochromatic triple does. Both were retrieved. The source supplies the equivalent condition

(x·y)(x·z)(y·z) ≥ 0

for every triple of representative nonzero vectors. It is independent of their sign choices. Here octahedral means contained in an orthogonal octant after choosing a lift, not merely contained in an arbitrary simplicial cone. The source already explains why an individual weak class need not be octahedral. No later resolution was verified in this research pass.

## 1. Closure and acute-cone reduction with an explicit hypothesis

**Lemma1.** The closure C of a weakly octahedral set is weakly octahedral.

Proof. For three distinct limiting projective points, take approximating points in disjoint small neighborhoods and choose local continuous unit representatives. Continuity preserves the nonnegative product. Coincident limiting points cause no obstruction: the product then contains a squared inner product and a positive norm. ∎

**Lemma2.** If such a closed set C has nonempty interior in RP², then it has a compact choice of unit representatives A, all pairwise with nonnegative inner product. The closed cone K generated by A is convex, pointed and acute: v·w≥0 for all v,w∈K.

Proof. Choose a unit representative p of an interior point. There is no [x]∈C with p·x=0. Otherwise, for sufficiently small t>0, both [p+tx] and [p−tx] lie in C. Taking x unit, the three pairwise products are t,−t,1−t², whose product is negative. This contradicts Lemma1.

Choose the unique unit representative x of each line in C with p·x>0. This is continuous because the forbidden perpendicular projective line is avoided; compactness gives p·x≥δ>0 uniformly. For two representatives x,y, apply the triple criterion with p. It gives x·y≥0. Degenerate triples give the same inequality directly. Positive linear combinations preserve this property.

The cone is closed: if sums v_n=Σ_j a_nj x_nj converge with a_nj≥0, then δΣ_j a_nj≤p·v_n bounds the coefficients' total mass. Writing each sum as a nonnegative scalar times an element of the compact convex hull of A gives a convergent subsequence representation. Also p·v>0 for every nonzero v in the cone, so it is pointed. ∎

For a fixed lift A and its cone K, containment in an orthogonal positive octant is equivalent to the dual cone K*={b:b·v≥0 for all v∈K} containing an orthonormal basis. This is just the coordinate characterization b_i·v≥0 in that basis.

**Limit:** a finite closed cover has at least one member with interior by Baire category, not necessarily all four. Nor does closure preserve disjointness, and convexification can enlarge overlaps. The lemmas do not turn the arbitrary partition into a face-to-face fan. They do not prove that the four relevant dual cones contain orthonormal bases.

## 2. The conjecture holds for three-plane sign partitions

**Proposition3.** Suppose a partition is obtained by the eight open sign cones of three independent linear forms on R³, pairing opposite cones into four projective parts and assigning boundary points only to parts incident to those points. If each part is weakly octahedral, the three forms define mutually orthogonal planes. Consequently each part is octahedral.

Proof. Let v₁,v₂,v₃ be the columns of the inverse matrix of the three forms. The closure of every sign cone is generated by ε₁v₁,ε₂v₂,ε₃v₃ for its signs ε_i∈{±1}. Every such closed projective cone is weak by Lemma1, since its interior is contained in its assigned part.

If v_i·v_j≠0, choose signs so that the two corresponding unit extreme vectors a,b have a·b=c<0. Their sum z=a+b is nonzero and belongs to the same closed cone. Since v_i,v_j are independent, −1<c<0, and

(a·b)(a·z)(b·z)=c(1+c)²<0.

The three projective points are distinct. This contradicts weakness. Thus v_i·v_j=0 for all i≠j. The inverse columns form an orthogonal frame, hence the form normals do too. Each assigned part is contained in the corresponding opposite closed orthants. ∎

This rules out a nonorthogonal linear deformation of the standard four-part partition. It does not treat curved boundaries, disconnected parts, or nonmeasurable partitions.

## 3. A four-ray witness and the finite search limitation

Consider the four lines with representatives

a=(1,1,0), b=(1,−1,0), c=(1,0,1), d=(1,0,−1).

All pairwise dot products are nonnegative: a·b=c·d=0 and the other four products are1. Therefore every triple satisfies the source criterion. But these four lines cannot be octahedral.

To prove this, suppose representatives could all be made coordinatewise nonnegative in an orthonormal basis. The strictly positive cross products force the choices of signs for a,b,c,d to be all the same. Global negation makes no difference. The orthogonal pair a,b must have disjoint positive-coordinate supports; the same is true for c,d. Each of the four pairs (a,c),(a,d),(b,c),(b,d) has positive inner product, so it requires a coordinate in the intersection of those two supports. These four intersection sets are pairwise disjoint, because changing either a/b or c/d changes to a disjoint support. They require at least four coordinates, impossible in R³. ∎

We asked whether requiring those four lines to have one color already prevents a four-coloring of a finite rational grid. The answer is **no for the tested grid**: `finite_coloring.json` gives a valid four-coloring of all145 projective directions represented by primitive integer triples in [−3,3]³. The four witness lines share color0. Every monochromatic triple has nonnegative dot-product product.

`search_finite.py` searches the three-uniform hypergraph whose edges are exactly negative-product triples. `verify.py` independently regenerates the directions and checks every triple, rather than trusting the search's propagation. This is a counterexample only to a finite-grid analogue. There is no proof that the coloring extends to other rational directions or to all of RP². A compactness argument would require colorings of every finite set with the prescribed witness, not just this one grid.

## Remaining gap and stopping point

Three approach families were explored: closure/acute-cone reduction; rigidity of linear sign fans; exact finite hypergraph search. The first needs new global rigidity for an arbitrary four-part cover, including lower-dimensional and overlap behavior. The second excludes only one construction family. The third neither constructs a global partition nor yields an unsatisfiable finite obstruction. Therefore the original conjecture remains unsolved, proposed3/5 approaches.
