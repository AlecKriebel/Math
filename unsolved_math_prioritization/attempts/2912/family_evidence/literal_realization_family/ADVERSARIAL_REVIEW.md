## 2. Literal question and actual geometric objects

[Kirby, Problem 4.36](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf), printed pp. 219-220, asks whether the homotopy type of a 2-knot complement is determined by its homotopy 2-type. The complete invariant is `(G,A,k)`, where `A=pi_2(X)` is a module with the fundamental-group action and `k` is the first Postnikov class in `H^3(G;A)`. An equivalence consists of a group isomorphism and a compatible semilinear module isomorphism carrying the first class to the second. Neither equality of abstract abelian groups nor equality of `(G,A)` suffices.

[Lomonaco, Section X](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/lomonaco/5knots/Lomonaco-Pacific-Journal-Math.pdf), printed pp. 373-375, explicitly asks about smooth or locally flat PL 2-knots. Theorem 10.1 requires both knots to be quasi-aspherical: both universal-cover third homology groups vanish. Theorem 10.2 separately requires domination by connected 3-dimensional CW complexes and vanishing of both third homology groups. His proof realizes the full algebraic 2-type on 3-dimensional models, then uses QA to obtain the missing homology comparison. Theorem 10.4 includes finitely ended knots in the QA subclass; its converse is not asserted. The candidate accurately retains these assumptions.

The compact exterior of an allowed embedding has boundary `S^2 x S^1`, integral homology of `S^1`, and the homotopy type of a 3-dimensional CW complex. The open complement retracts to that exterior, so the passage does not change the target homotopy type. The question supplies no marking of the boundary, no meridian conjugacy class, no boundary-sphere class, and no relative fundamental-class map. A change of isotopy type or a Gluck reconstruction with homeomorphic exterior cannot refute this space-homotopy question. Arbitrary 3-complexes or homology-circle manifolds cannot serve as counterexamples unless realized as two exteriors in the source's category with equal complete 2-types.

The geometric meridian has infinite order because it generates `H_1(X)=Z`. Thus `pi_1(boundary X)=Z` injects into `G` as its meridional subgroup. Surjectivity of this inclusion forces `G=Z`; it is not an assumption available for general knot groups. Normal generation is necessary for a meridian, but does not alone identify a specified element with the geometric meridian in a given smooth/PL exterior. A group realization, a peripheral-pair realization, realization of a full 2-type, and realization of a pair of equal-2-type exteriors with different homotopy types are distinct requirements. The submission consistently distinguishes them.

## 3. Independent proof audit of the conditional deductions

### The cover homology formula and the degree-one pair criterion

Let `R=Z[G]` and `H=<mu>`. A finite-support element of `R` invariant under the infinite cyclic left action of `H` must vanish: any nonzero coefficient would repeat along an infinite orbit. The `G`-invariants vanish as well. The cohomology sequence of the pair consequently embeds `H^1(X,boundary X;R)` into `H^1(X;R)` with image the restriction kernel. Degree-one local-system cohomology depends only on the fundamental group and its coefficient action; no asphericity of the boundary or exterior is needed in this degree. Equivariant Poincare-Lefschetz duality converts the right cohomology module to the left cover-homology module through inversion. This independently yields the candidate's conjugate kernel formula.

For an oriented pair map of relative degree one, naturality of the homology boundary map gives boundary degree one. On `S^2 x S^1`, the integral generators in cohomology degrees 1 and 2 multiply to the orientation generator, so the induced integers `u,v` satisfy `uv=1`. Therefore each is a unit, and the boundary fundamental-group map is an isomorphism. Combining it with the assumed exterior `pi_1` isomorphism yields an isomorphism of the meridional inclusions and hence their restriction kernels. The cap-product naturality identity and the relative degree-one condition then identify the induced map on third cover homology with an isomorphism. Assumed `pi_2` isomorphism gives the second-cover-homology isomorphism by Hurewicz; degrees 0 and 1 follow from connectedness and simple connectivity. Nothing remains above degree 3. The lifted map is a homology equivalence between simply connected CW spaces, hence a homotopy equivalence, and the exterior map is a homotopy equivalence.

The theorem is sufficient and correct. Its use as a general positive answer would be circular if one inserted the required pair map without proof. The submission does not insert it. Realization of the unmarked full 2-type by an ordinary map on 3-dimensional models is separate from realization with a specified boundary map and degree one. Also the criterion is not claimed necessary; its realization gap is a stopped route, not a proof that the original implication is false.

### The finite amalgam and its geometric qualification

[Hillman's revised monograph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212142v3), Section 14.6, printed p. 277, gives the same group as a known infinite-ended 2-knot group and a satellite example. He writes the presentation without `b^7=1`; iterating conjugation by `a` three times gives `b=b^8`, so the added relation is redundant. The displayed finite subgroup has the explicit affine action on seven points, and the candidate's decomposition as `A *_C B`, with `A=C_7 semidirect C_3`, `C=C_3`, and `B=C_3 semidirect Z` acting by inversion on `C`, follows from the presentations and the injective vertex inclusions of an amalgam.

For the regular coefficient module, positive finite-subgroup cohomology vanishes, and invariants under the infinite vertex vanish. The group-cohomology Mayer-Vietoris segment thus gives `R^C/R^A` as the kernel of restriction to the `B` vertex. Inflation from `B/C=Z` expresses `H^1(B;R)` as the coinvariants of `R^C`. On each regular `B` block, the norm vectors `N_C t^n` form a Laurent basis, so these coinvariants are a copy of `Z`, with no torsion. Restriction to `<t>` sends this copy to the three-orbit group `Z^3` by the primitive diagonal vector. Therefore restriction is injective, and the kernel of restriction from `G` to `<t>` is exactly `R^C/R^A`. This direct integral derivation agrees with the candidate's transfer argument; no code count proves group cohomology.

On each right `A`-coset the quotient is the free abelian group `Z^7 / Z(1,...,1)`, with the primitive diagonal removed, so it has rank 6 and no torsion. Inverting the right `C\A` cosets gives the left `A/C` convention and the displayed actions by doubling and translation. This is a quotient by the diagonal, not an unsupported replacement by the augmentation submodule. It is nonzero. The deduction about an actual exterior's third cover homology remains conditional on the geometric group-pair identification, exactly as the candidate says.

Hillman defines knots to be locally flat embeddings on printed p. 269. His Section 14.7 explains that surgery on a weight element in an appropriate closed knot manifold produces a locally flat knot. Such a result can produce a new peripheral-pair realization in that category, but it does not by itself identify `t` as the meridian of the already chosen exterior, or certify the source's smooth/PL category and a desired full `(G,A,k)`. The displayed presentation paragraph does not itself make that meridional identification. The candidate's geometric caution is warranted; no mandatory repair follows from Hillman's additional surgery observation.

The exact negative-answer gap remains two allowed-category exteriors with equal full triples and an obstruction to homotopy equivalence. This family found no such pair in the submission or in the specifically inspected primary statements. A missing realization hypothesis must not be confused with an actual counterexample.
