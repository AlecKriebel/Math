## Original question

The controlling source is Furuhata–Matsuzoe–Urakawa, *Open Problems in Affine Differential Geometry and Related Topics*, Interdisciplinary Information Sciences 4(2) (1998), 125–127, item 5(a), printed p.126. Its heading and problem page were visually inspected. The questions came from a December 1996 workshop; 1998 is the publication date.

It asks about the natural complex structure on the tangent bundle of a Riemannian-complete Hessian manifold. It separately highlights quotients of a convex domain with no complete straight line by a free properly discontinuous discrete affine group. Neither compactness nor homogeneity is imposed. The current proof covers a finite-facet subclass, not that entire class.

[Original source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/iis/4/2/4_2_125/_pdf/-char/en).

## Existing results and bounded history check

Shimizu's primary 1985 paper was obtained and its full seven-page argument inspected. Its theorem constructs a strictly plurisubharmonic function outside a possible divisor. Compactness yields exhaustion on that complement. Homogeneity removes the divisor, giving the compact locally homogeneous hyperbolic case. It does not settle the printed unrestricted question. This prior result is credited; the finite-facet/logarithm proof is supplied in full in PROOF.md.

[Shimizu, DOI 10.2748/tmj/1178228643](https://doi.org/10.2748/tmj/1178228643).

## Attribution and limits

The new exposition is an elementary deduction using classical Stein criteria, convex separation, affine facet permutation, and a real-lattice quotient. It does not claim priority for any special case. The standard strict-exhaustion criterion is credited to Grauert; the dimension-one conclusion uses the Behnke–Stein theorem. The actual deduction, group-discreteness argument, properness arguments, norm obstruction, and support-envelope calculation are included explicitly.

The present disposition is **unsolved, 5/5 attempts**, meaning this investigation has no full proof or counterexample. It is not a certification that no resolution exists anywhere in the literature.
