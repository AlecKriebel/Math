1. SOURCE AND CONJECTURE AUDIT

The target is Conjecture B in Kristin DeVleming's contribution, joint work with David Stapleton, to Oberwolfach Report 32/2022, printed page 1822. Its content is the Cartier-embedding assertion for smooth abstract prime-degree plane-curve limits into log-terminal Q-Gorenstein plane degenerations. The manuscript's restatement preserves that target.

The JEP paper, printed page 685, gives the equivalent Manetti-surface formulation. Theorems D and E concern degrees five and seven respectively. Theorem 1.10 constructs nonplanar limits in the stated Markov-related degrees as Cartier divisors. These examples are compatible with Conjecture B. Neither these examples nor the low-degree theorems prove its general prime-degree assertion.

Hacking-Prokhorov Corollary 1.2 classifies projective quotient-singularity surfaces smoothable to the plane as Q-Gorenstein deformations of the relevant weighted projective planes. The JEP paper records the corresponding Manetti classification in Theorem 1.4. Its Theorem 1.6 supplies exactly the class-group, Picard-index, and intersection-number facts used in Approach 3. The manuscript is using actual classified degenerations, not an arbitrary surface satisfying K squared = 9.

DeVleming-Singh arXiv:2311.15922v1, Section 2, gives the singularity conventions, delta formula, semigroup generators, and the rational-cuspidal distribution theorem. Section 3.5 gives the threshold formula. Theorem 5.1 and Table 7 leave only characteristic signature (10;11) in degree eleven. This is a statement about the cusp's topological data; the manuscript does not turn it into an unsupported claim of analytic rigidity. The authors' current research page labels this work submitted, and arXiv currently lists only v1.

Borodzik-Livingston Theorem 6.5 was inspected directly in the primary preprint, together with equation (6.1). Its counting interval is half-open, includes zero, and the permitted integers are -1 through d-2. For one cusp the infimum convolution reduces to the single counting function, exactly as required here.

The two 2026 preprints cited for literature status were checked in their stated applications. The weighted-projective paper's Section 6.4 recalls known low-degree cases and constructs hypersurface examples. The wall-crossing survey's Section 5.3 treats specified singularities and boundary constructions. Neither inspected section supplies the missing general prime-degree theorem. A fresh bounded search and both author research pages revealed no contrary resolution. This is not an exhaustive absence certificate. The survey is currently described by its author as to appear in conference proceedings; citing its inspected arXiv version as a preprint remains accurate.

2. APPROACH 1: LIMITING LINEAR SERIES

Accepted: Lemma 2.1, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3, and the degree-eleven numerical list. Locations: manuscript.tex lines 44-98; PDF pages 2-3.

A smooth relative curve over the indicated DVR has a regular total surface. Closing a divisor representing the generic plane polarization therefore extends it to a line bundle. The generic adjunction identity can fail on the total family only by a divisor supported on the special fiber. That fiber is smooth and connected, hence irreducible here, and is principal. The resulting canonical-root identity on the special curve is valid.

The rank-three specialization is also valid: the proper direct image is a finite torsion-free module over the DVR, hence free; multiplication by the uniformizer injects its reduction into special-fiber sections. No assumption that all special-fiber sections lift is needed. Calling the resulting space a three-dimensional net is consistent with possible jumping of h-zero.

Removing the common zero divisor produces a morphism with nondegenerate integral plane image. Its moving degree is m times e. If the original net is base-point-free, primality and e >= 2 force m=1 and e=p. The normalization genus formula then has zero total delta, so the image is smooth and the map is an embedding. If the map after removing a nonzero fixed part were birational, its image degree would be below p and its arithmetic genus below the given genus. Thus m and e must both be at least two, and 1 <= b <= p-4.

For p=5 the unique factorization is (b,m,e)=(1,2,2), giving a double cover of a smooth conic. The conclusion of hyperellipticity is correct and is weaker than the desired ambient embedding.

The eight degree-eleven triples are independently confirmed. For the (1,2,5) case, genus(C)=45 and Riemann-Hurwitz gives deg(Ram)=2*45-2-2*(2h-2)=92-4h. The stated bound h <= 6 is correct for the normalization of a plane quintic. Nonnegativity does not construct such a cover or smoothing.

Remaining gap correctly retained: a limiting net can have a fixed part. It cannot be discarded, and numerical degree factorizations do not build the required surface degeneration.

3. APPROACH 2: HYPERELLIPTIC TEST OBJECTS

Accepted: Proposition 3.1 and its explicit conic net. Locations: manuscript.tex lines 106-144; PDF page 4.

For a Weierstrass point P, the hyperelliptic pencil is O(2P), while the canonical bundle is its (g-1)-st power. Since 2g_p-2=p(p-3), the claimed canonical root follows. The affine hyperelliptic equation has pole orders 2 and 2g+1, so the displayed basis and low-degree h-zero count are correct.

For 0 <= k <= p-3, the dimension difference is floor(k(p-k-3)/2), a nonnegative integer. For k >= p-2, both degree-kp bundles are nonspecial and Riemann-Roch gives equality. These two ranges exhaust every nonnegative k. The proof works for every odd integer p >= 5, not just primes; the independent regression checks include composite odd p.

All sections of O(pP) vanish at least once at P, the largest even pole order is p-1, and a section realizes exactly that order. The constant function gives no common zero away from P. Thus the complete fixed divisor is exactly P. The subspace spanned by 1,x,x squared has fixed divisor (p-4)P and moving degree four, yielding the stated double cover of a conic.

The p=7 negative control is sound: the test curve has genus fifteen and is hyperelliptic, whereas JEP Theorem E forces genuine smooth septic limits to be planar. A smooth plane septic is not hyperelliptic. The packet correctly declines to assert that the chosen test net actually specializes from a plane net.

Remaining gap correctly retained: canonical roots and dimensions alone do not control multiplication maps or polarized deformations. No smoothing or counterexample is supplied.

4. APPROACH 3: CARTIER INDEX AND GENUS

Accepted: Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.2, and Lemma 4.3. Locations: manuscript.tex lines 146-189; PDF pages 5-6.

The local-ring argument in Lemma 4.1 is correct. If a nonzerodivisor cuts a regular one-dimensional quotient, the surface maximal ideal has at most two generators, so the two-dimensional local ring is regular. This justifies applying smooth-surface adjunction near a smooth Cartier curve. The scalar cyclic-quotient example correctly distinguishes a smooth non-Cartier Weil curve.

For H the positive class-group generator, JEP Theorem 1.6 gives H squared = 1/alpha squared and Pic generated by alpha squared H. The unsmoothed local class groups have orders equal to squares of the corresponding Markov entries, so alpha is their product. Positivity and K squared = 9 give K=-3alpha H.

For C=m alpha squared H, ordinary adjunction along its smooth neighborhood yields 2g-2=(m alpha)(m alpha-3). With q=m alpha, subtracting p(p-3) factors as (q-p)(q+p-3); the second factor is positive. Hence q=p and alpha divides p. For prime p, either the surface is the plane or its only nontrivial unsmoothed Markov entry is p, with local class group of order p squared. This is a restriction on an already existing Cartier embedding, not an existence proof.

The Markov descent is valid. Positivity of the other root follows from c*c'=a squared+b squared. The inequality at b puts that root at most b, apart from the root triple handled in the manuscript. Mutating maxima greater than eleven preserves an entry eleven and terminates with maximum eleven.

An independent short check of the resulting finite problem is available: set F_a(b)=b squared-33ab+a squared+121. For a >= 2 and a <= b <= 11, F_a is strictly decreasing and F_a(a)=121-31a squared<0. For a=1 it is decreasing, with F_1(4)=6 and F_1(5)=-18. It consequently has no integer zero in the required range. This verifies the exclusion of eleven without relying on the author's bounded triple enumeration.

Remaining gap correctly retained: eleven not being Markov would force planarity if the conjectured Cartier embedding existed. It does not provide that embedding or establish planarity of arbitrary smooth limits.

5. APPROACH 4: NODAL DIVISORS

Accepted: Theorem 5.1 under exactly its stated assumptions. Locations: manuscript.tex lines 194-215; PDF pages 6-7.

For a proper nodal degeneration over the curve germ, resolving node-thickness singularities after base change subdivides edges. Stabilization then removes unstable rational tails and chains. These operations preserve the first Betti number of the dual multigraph. Genus at least two avoids the exceptional entire unstable genus-zero or genus-one configurations. A smooth stable special fiber therefore forces the starting graph to have no cycles, including self-node loops.

Each component is Cartier because its whole support avoids the ambient singular locus. The rank-one Picard group writes every component as a positive integer multiple of the ample generator. Thus every pair of distinct components intersects positively. Since D is nodal, intersection multiplicity is exactly the number of transverse nodes between those components. A tree with every vertex pair adjacent has at most two vertices. With two vertices, there is one node, forcing alpha=m_1=m_2=1 and two lines in the plane, whose arithmetic genus is zero. This contradicts the family genus. One component and no loops means a smooth irreducible divisor.

The application to a Calabi-Yau limit is sound only when that divisor satisfies the theorem's proper flat-family, reduced-nodal, smooth-locus assumptions and the generic family agrees with the original curve family. Uniqueness of stable reduction then identifies the resulting smooth divisor with the original abstract special curve. No singular boundary divisor is silently equated with the smooth abstract limit.

Remaining gap correctly retained: log canonicity at coefficient 3/p<1 does not imply reducedness, nodality, or avoidance of quotient singularities. All those excluded regimes still require work.

6. APPROACH 5: DEGREE ELEVEN

Accepted: Theorem 6.1, Lemma 6.2, and every row of the elimination table. Locations: manuscript.tex lines 220-294; PDF pages 7-9.

6.1 Imported formulas and hypotheses

For a reduced irreducible branch with characteristic exponents beta_i and strictly dropping gcds e_i, set N_i=beta_i-beta_(i-1). DeVleming-Singh's P_1,Q_1 are m,beta_1, and its later P_i,Q_i are e_(i-1),N_i. This translates the source delta and threshold formulas exactly into the manuscript's equations (1) and (2). The source semigroup recursion similarly gives v_i=(e_(i-2)/e_(i-1))*v_(i-1)+N_i. The characteristic exponents are not being confused with every nonzero Puiseux coefficient.

The curve is reduced and irreducible, its normalization is rational, and it has only one singular point. The degree-genus formula therefore gives delta=45, with conductor 90. The local branch threshold is below one, so the global threshold is this local threshold; smooth points do not lower it.

Borodzik-Livingston applies to precisely this rational cuspidal situation. For one cusp, R(11j+1) must equal (j+1)(j+2)/2. All witnesses use j in {1,2,3,4}, safely inside the allowed range. The strict/weak endpoint convention is correct.

6.2 Exhaustiveness before computation

Since beta_1>m, for m >= 8 the threshold is at most 1/8+1/9=17/72<3/11. Thus the manuscript's restriction 2 <= m <= 7 is valid. In fact m >= 7 is already excluded by 1/7+1/8<3/11; using the weaker bound introduces no gap.

A strict divisor chain starting at 2,3,5,7 has one drop; at 4 or 6 it has at most two. Hence no third characteristic exponent can occur in this threshold regime, regardless of how many ordinary Puiseux terms are present.

For prime m, (m-1)(beta_1-1)=90 gives (2;91), (3;46), no integral m=5 possibility, and (7;16) below threshold. For m=4, the gcd-one case gives (4;31). In the gcd-two case beta_1=b is 2 modulo 4 and exceeds 4; the conductor fixes the positive odd increment N=93-3b. Thus b is exactly 6,10,14,18,22,26,30, and beta_2=93-2b. These seven cases all meet the threshold. For m=6, the threshold requires beta_1 <= 66/7, leaving 7,8,9. The first has the wrong delta; the latter two give (6;8,63) and (6;9,34). Total: twelve, with no omitted gcd chain.

This is a complete mathematical exhaustion independent of a program's output. Each listed characteristic sequence also defines a legitimate local irreducible branch, for example by its finite characteristic parametrization. It is the global rational degree-eleven realization that the next step rules out.

6.4 Degree and Bézout checks

For an irreducible degree-eleven plane curve, a point multiplicity is at most ten: multiplicity eleven would make its degree-eleven defining polynomial homogeneous at that point and hence a union of lines over C. This justifies the author's wider computational multiplicity bound. The main threshold proof is stronger and does not depend on that numerical cutoff.

No invalid rule beta_1 <= 11 is used. After analytic changes of coordinates removing noncharacteristic terms, beta_1 need not equal intersection with the original projective tangent line. Applying a degree-eleven Bézout bound directly to that transformed exponent would incorrectly discard candidates. The packet instead retains the high-exponent cases and rejects them by a valid necessary semigroup identity.

The genus equality uses rationality plus uniqueness of the singularity. It cannot be transferred to a positive-genus normalization, several singularities, a nonreduced divisor, or a curve on a singular surface. The manuscript respects these distinctions.
