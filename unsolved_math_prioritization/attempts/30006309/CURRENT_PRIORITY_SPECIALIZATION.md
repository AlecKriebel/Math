# Independent specialization of the prior Newton formula

This is a written deduction, separate from the finite controls. It addresses precisely the smooth complete embedding scope of the original PR55 CANDIDATE.md, not arbitrary singular or sparse configurations. The notation below uses d for the dimension of Q so that it cannot be confused with Esterov's parameter-space dimension.

Let Q be a d-dimensional Delzant lattice polytope, A=Q∩Z^d, d≥1. Retain the original source's complete very ample embedding and degree≥2. For a regular lower triangulation T, write η_{T,j} for the original massive j-face vector with normalized j-dimensional lattice volume; in particular η_{T,d−1} is the sum over triangulations of boundary facets. Put B=A×Vert(Δ_{d−1}), let π sum the coordinates in each A-column, and denote the ordinary Cayley discriminant by D_B.

The target is

    π Newt(D_B) = conv{dη_{T,d}−η_{T,d−1}: T regular}.

The previously completed GKZ/fan audit by this same agent established, under this smooth full-configuration scope, Newt(D_B)=𝒟(Q×Δ_{d−1}) with the candidate's massive-vector convention. This dependency is explicitly reused here; it is not a second independent examination of the GKZ foundations.

## 1. Literal old theorem and exact parameter choice

The primary mathematical source is Alexander Esterov, *Newton polyhedra of discriminants of projections*, arXiv:0810.4996v3, 31 July 2010, printed pp.1–2,7–9,14,18,21,23,41–46. Numbering here is the 59-page v3 preprint. The journal reference is *Discrete & Computational Geometry*44(2010),96–148, DOI10.1007/s00454-010-9242-7; its publisher records publication on 6 February 2010. The journal text was not acquired or used to assert identical pagination/numbering. The v1 submission in 2008 establishes bibliographic history, not a personal verification that the exact formula was already present in v1.

Theorem5.10(1), p.44, requires: leading coefficients generic in the sense of Definition5.5; analogous finite supports A_i⊂Z^k; and each A_i full-dimensional. Its Newton formula contains the index of the lattice M generated by pairwise differences of A_0+⋯+A_l, signed combinatorial Euler-obstruction coefficients e_{F,A}, and mixed fiber polyhedra with positive exponent tuples of total dim(F)+1. Theorem5.10(2) gives only containment for arbitrary coefficients. Definition5.12, p.45, evaluates the repeated-argument expression as

    Σ_{faces F, dim F≥l} e_{F,A} binom(dim F,l) ∫Δ(F).

Theorem5.13, p.46, reformulates this as the higher mixed fiber polyhedron, under its spanning hypothesis and the preceding generic context. I use the explicitly stated hypotheses of Theorem5.10, rather than silently interpreting the shortened statement of Theorem5.13 as applying to arbitrary coefficients. Definition5.12 includes l≥0. Printed p.2 explicitly supplies the global Laurent-polynomial version of Theorem5.10, so the local toric-germ presentation does not exclude bounded polytopes here.

Choose Esterov's parameters as follows:

    fiber dimension k=d; equation count l+1=d; l=d−1;
    parameter dimension m=|A|; parameter coordinates x=(x_a)_{a∈A};
    A_i=A for all i=0,…,d−1;
    F_i(x,y)=Σ_{a∈A} κ_{i,a} x_a y^a.

The κ_{i,a} are generic nonzero complex constants, independently variable before choosing them. The coefficient function f_{a,i}=κ_{i,a}x_a has Newton polytope {e_a} in R^A. Consequently every equation has exactly the same Newton polytope

    Δ_i=Δ=conv{(e_a,a): a∈A} ⊂ R^A⊕R^d.

This is the required unmixed input. Introducing independent parameter coordinates x_{i,a} instead would give different Δ_i and would not be this specialization.

## 2. Genericity, lattice factors, and the polynomial identity

The identical supports are analogous by Example5.8(1), p.44. Complete Delzant A contains the first primitive lattice point on each edge at a vertex. Those edge directions form a basis of Z^d, so A−A generates Z^d. Holding the other summands fixed shows that M contains A−A; hence M=Z^d and its index is1. The same reasoning within a face gives its full induced face lattice. No factor from a sparse or nonunimodular configuration is discarded. Nonunimodular triangulation cells are still measured by their actual normalized volumes.

The spanning hypothesis in the reformulation Theorem5.13 also holds: differences of points in (dA)×{1} include (A−A,0), generating Z^d×{0}. Subtracting an appropriate such integer vector from any point of (dA)×{1} yields (0,1). Thus this configuration generates Z^d⊕Z.

Here is an explicit proof of Definition5.5, not an assumption that the coefficient family must be generic enough. Choose κ so that every rectangular row/column submatrix has maximal possible rank; nonvanishing of finitely many maximal minors defines a nonempty Zariski open set. Identical Δ_i have identical compatible exposed faces (Definition1.13, p.9). If their common vertex set is I⊂A and a row subset has q elements, the corresponding face equations are

    F_i^Γ=Σ_{a∈I} κ_{i,a} x_a y^a.

For fixed y in the torus, set t_a=x_a y^a. If q≤|I|, the Jacobian in x has full row rank everywhere. If q>|I|, full column rank means that all equations could vanish only when t=0, which is impossible in the parameter torus. Thus zero is a regular value in every required case, including the cases with injective fiber projection specified in Definition5.5.

Definition2.22, p.21, uses the squarefree codimension-one polynomial of the universal system discriminant. Subsection5.1, p.41 and Definition5.1, p.42, define its parameter version by evaluating that polynomial at the coefficient functions. They do not substitute the bifurcation discriminant that also includes degenerations at infinity.

Theorem2.31, p.23, expresses the reduced universal system discriminant as a product of reduced Cayley discriminants for the admissible nonempty row subsets J. In our setting

    codim J = dim(Σ_{j∈J} A)−|J| = d−|J|.

Every proper J has a strictly smaller codimension after adding a row, so it fails the theorem's admissibility inequality. Only the full row set remains. Dehomogenizing the row simplex gives exactly B=A×Vert(Δ_{d−1}). Its affine differences span Z^d⊕Z^{d−1}: the first block comes from A−A and the second from differences between rows at a fixed a. Thus the reduced Cayley discriminant is the ordinary GKZ D_B without the extra power allowed by Esterov's Definition2.7, p.18. For d=1 there is one equation and no row simplex coordinate; the same identity is the ordinary A-discriminant case directly.

In particular the polynomial to which Theorem5.10 applies is

    G_κ(x)=D_B((κ_{i,a}x_a)_{i,a}).

No nondefectiveness of X_A itself is assumed. X_A can be dual defective while the relevant Cayley product has a nonconstant discriminant. The theorem's constant-discriminant convention also avoids using nondefectiveness to justify its formula. The retained degree≥2 source scope is the original Hurwitz setting.

For completeness, generic substitution really produces the desired projected Newton polytope. Write D_B(c)=Σ_β C_β c^β. The coefficient of x^z after substitution is

    Σ_{πβ=z} C_β κ^β.

Its summands have distinct κ-monomials and C_β≠0, so this coefficient polynomial is nonzero. There are finitely many projected exponents. Avoiding their zero sets gives another nonempty Zariski open set. Its intersection with the rank conditions above is nonempty. Choosing κ there gives

    Newt(G_κ)=π Newt(D_B).

This step must use generic row constants. The new cube control demonstrates that κ_{i,a}=1 for every row and column can instead make G_κ identically zero.

## 3. Specializing the face formula, with its signs and units

Proposition1.27, p.14, identifies e_{F,A} with (−1)^{dimF−d} times the geometric Euler obstruction of X_A on the face orbit. Smoothness gives geometric Euler obstruction1. Hence e_{Q,A}=+1 and e_{F,A}=−1 for a facet F.

With l=d−1, no face of dimension less than d−1 contributes. The full-face coefficient is binom(d,d−1)=d; the facet coefficient is binom(d−1,d−1)=1. Equivalently, the positive exponent tuples in Theorem5.10 are d-tuples summing to d+1 for Q (exactly one entry2, hence d choices) and summing to d for a facet (all entries1, one choice). The old formula therefore yields

    N := π Newt(D_B) = dΣ_Q−Σ_{F facet}Σ_F,     Σ_F:=∫Δ(F).

Subtraction has its precise Minkowski meaning: N+∑_{F facet}Σ_F=dΣ_Q. Existence of the polytope N follows from the old discriminant theorem. It is not being assumed from a formal difference of arbitrary convex sets.

Definition1.8, p.7, normalizes the j-dimensional face's integration measure so a fundamental lattice parallelepiped has measure (j+1)!. In the following equation Vol_lattice is the ordinary measure assigning that parallelepiped volume1, before multiplication by j!. On a simplex σ of a lower triangulation of F, its barycentric coordinate λ_a has integral

    ∫_σ λ_a = (j+1)! Vol_lattice(σ)/(j+1)
             = j! Vol_lattice(σ) = normalized j-volume(σ).

The section sends a point of σ to its barycentric combination of the e_a coordinates. Therefore its integral is exactly the face GKZ vector, embedded in the same R^A coordinates. This determines both scale and translation: there is no unspecified affine translation of the fiber polytope. Under a height w the minimum of its linear functional is the integral of the lower envelope, so

    min_{Σ_F}⟨w,z⟩=⟨w,η_{T_F,j}⟩

when w induces a regular triangulation T_F. This also fixes the lower-height/minimum sign.

## 4. Recovering precisely the Hurwitz-vector model

If a generic w induces T on Q, it induces T_F on every boundary facet F. A convex representation of a point of a supporting face uses only points of that face, so taking the restriction is compatible with the lower envelope even when A contains unused points.

Take minima of the established Minkowski equality. Additivity of the minimum under Minkowski sums gives

    min_N⟨w,z⟩
       =d⟨w,η_{T,d}⟩−Σ_{F facet}⟨w,η_{T_F,d−1}⟩
       =⟨w,dη_{T,d}−η_{T,d−1}⟩.

On the open chamber for T this minimum is linear, so its slope is an actual vertex of N. Thus every vector dη_{T,d}−η_{T,d−1} belongs to N, including the vectors for triangulations that omit lattice points. Generic heights are dense. Continuity of support minima and determination of a compact convex polytope by its minima then give

    π Newt(D_B)=conv{dη_{T,d}−η_{T,d−1}: T regular}.

Together with the reused GKZ identification this is exactly π𝒟(Q×Δ_{d−1})=𝓗(Q), the candidate's model comparison. It does not invoke Sano's analytic equality or its K-energy argument as a support identity. The identification of the geometric name “Hurwitz” remains the established source identification; the equality of the two combinatorial models above is derived from the old Newton formula.
