# Forest-indexed consistency varieties: scoped partial results

Problem 30006359 / OWR-14299511-008, queue rank 556.

**Disposition: the general conjecture remains unresolved after five substantive approaches.** This is an AI-assisted, unrefereed research record. It neither claims a counterexample to the authors' intended conjecture nor claims historical novelty. The low-rank classification counts are already reported by Liu and Lu; this packet gives an independently executable algebraic verification for a precisely specified normalized system.

## 1. Source question and scope

Zhengwei Liu's contribution, joint with Fan Lu, to *Subfactors and Applications*, Oberwolfach Report 34/2025, printed pp. 1854–1856, proposes that the forest-indexed pieces of the consistency-equation variety are its irreducible components. The primary report is [DOI 10.4171/OWR/2025/34](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2025/34). The supporting paper is [Lu–Liu, arXiv:2412.17790v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17790v1), especially Proposition 2.1, Theorem 3.8, Lemma 4.5 and Theorems 4.6, 4.9 and 4.10. [Fan Lu's January 2026 slides](https://tsimf.tsinghua.edu.cn/__local/D/AF/4F/D6F859C069AF8BF0346AC84C7EA_C613B5B3_4DA53.pdf), slide 20, still present the assertion as a conjecture.

The report separates algebraic consistency from reflection positivity. We preserve that separation. The calculations below are over C, allow negative or nonreal parameters, and do not classify subfactors or establish positivity. The catalogue's shortened statement is not used as a substitute for these primary sources.

Three distinctions matter:

1. An exact nonzero-support condition defines a locally closed stratum, not usually a closed variety. Its closure is obtained by saturation.
2. Exchange coefficients admit a choice of representation at the incidence-equation stage. The source makes a particular canonical choice. A statement about those choices is different from a statement about all raw exchange-coefficient variables.
3. We normalize the homogeneous dimension and convolution parameters. This does not automatically preserve irreducibility of a variety that also retains a physical circle parameter or an unspecialized gauge.

The proposition in Section 6 concerns exactly the normalized canonical charts defined next. It does not silently promote their classification to the unrestricted source conjecture.

## 2. Explicit normalized algebraic model

Let r be the number of basis labels, with labels 0,...,r−1, and fix an involution i↦bar(i) fixing 0. Write q_0=1, let q_1,...,q_{r−1} be variables, and write C_{ij}^k for normalized convolution coefficients. The source's dimension/convolution variables are homogeneous; formally these normalized variables correspond to q_i=d_i/d_0 and C_{ij}^k=N_{ij}^k/d_0 on d_0≠0.

For each k, let Γ_k be a bipartite graph, with a white copy and a black copy of the labels. Its edge (i,j) represents C_{kj}^i≠0. Edges involving 0 are fixed by the unit and dual rules: C_{0j}^i and C_{j0}^i have support i=j, while C_{kj}^0 has support k=bar(j). All other support bits are invariant under the six reciprocity transformations in (2) below. We consider only families in which every Γ_k is a forest.

For every i,j,k, define canonical vertex coefficients A_{sk}^{ij}, B_{sk}^{ij} as follows.

- If (i,j) is not an edge of Γ_k, all the coefficients are zero.
- If k=0 and (i,j) is an edge, A_{i0}^{ij}=1 and the other coefficients are zero.
- If k≠0 and i=0, set A_{0k}^{ij}=1 and all other coefficients to zero.
- If k≠0 and j=0, set B_{0k}^{ij}=1 and all other coefficients to zero.
- Otherwise remove edge (i,j), and take the connected component containing white vertex i. Give its white vertices coefficient +1 in A, its black vertices coefficient −1 in B, and every other vertex coefficient zero.

These are the canonical conventions of the source, written as a graph algorithm. Define the convolution coefficients by

(1) C_{ij}^k = Σ_s (A_{sk}^{i,bar(j)} + B_{sk}^{i,bar(j)}) q_s.

Let I_Γ be the ideal in C[q_1,...,q_{r−1}] generated by the following equations, including (6b), after substitution of (1):

(2) Reciprocity: C_{kj}^i q_i equals each of
C_{j,bar(i)}^{bar(k)}q_bar(k), C_{bar(i),k}^{bar(j)}q_bar(j),
C_{bar(j),bar(k)}^{bar(i)}q_bar(i), C_{bar(k),i}^j q_j,
and C_{i,bar(j)}^k q_k.

(3) Duality and unit: q_i=q_bar(i), C_{kj}^i=C_{bar(j),bar(k)}^{bar(i)}, and C_{ij}^0=1_{i=bar(j)}q_i.

(4) Dimension: q_i q_j = Σ_s C_{ij}^s q_s.

(5) Associativity: Σ_s C_{ij}^s C_{sk}^l = Σ_s C_{is}^l C_{jk}^s.

(6) For all i,j,k,x,y,z,

C_{zy}^x Σ_s [ A_{sz}^{jk}C_{si}^x + B_{sz}^{jk}C_{si}^y
− A_{s,bar(x)}^{bar(i),j}C_{s,bar(k)}^{bar(y)}
− B_{s,bar(x)}^{bar(i),j}C_{s,bar(k)}^z ] = 0.

We also impose the comparison with the third expansion of W in the proof of Theorem 3.8, source equations (3.12) and (3.16):

(6b) C_{zy}^x Σ_s [ A_{sz}^{jk}C_{si}^x + B_{sz}^{jk}C_{si}^y
− A_{sy}^{k,bar(i)}C_{s,bar(j)}^{bar(z)}
− B_{sy}^{k,bar(i)}C_{s,bar(j)}^{bar(x)} ] = 0.

The incidence equations are

(7) C_{kl}^s (A_{sk}^{ij}+B_{lk}^{ij}−1_{s=i,l=j})=0.

Equation (7) is identically satisfied by the forest construction; the verifier checks the integer identities on every edge. Equation (1) is source equation (3.7), with its conjugate index retained. Source equations (3.5) and (3.6) are identical in the inspected v1 PDF and TeX; (6) records exactly their displayed common expression. We additionally check (6b), taken directly from the proof's third expansion, rather than guessing a correction to the duplicated display. We do not assert that the duplication is itself a mathematical error or that the extra comparison is independent.

Define

h_Γ = (Π_i q_i) (Π_{k,(i,j)∈Γ_k} C_{kj}^i),
S_Γ = V(I_Γ) ∩ D(h_Γ),
J_Γ = I_Γ : h_Γ^∞.

Repeated or constant factors in h_Γ do not matter. On S_Γ the leading factor of (6) and (6b) can be canceled exactly for an edge, and is identically zero for a nonedge. The code uses these canceled equations and the prescribed nonzero factors. Their localized ideal is identical to that of the displayed system.

The circle variable δ is not retained in this model. In the source's unnormalized convention, d_0=1/δ and Σd_i=δ, so δ²=Σq_i. Restoring δ can introduce a double cover. Nothing here says that the cover has the same components as its normalized base. For all nonempty charts found below, Σq_i is not identically zero; excluding its zero locus preserves the stated closures and irreducibility.

## 3. Approach 1: the incidence map and its precise limit

### Lemma 1 (forest criterion and coefficient freedom)

For a finite bipartite graph G with v vertices, e edges and c connected components, including isolated vertices, consider

L: K^vertices → K^edges,  (a,b) ↦ (a_u+b_w)_{uw∈E(G)}

over a field K of characteristic zero. Then rank L=v−c. Consequently, L is onto exactly when G is a forest. When G is a forest, the coefficient solutions representing any prescribed edge vector form an affine space of dimension c.

**Proof.** In each connected component the equations a_u+b_w=0 force a common value t on all white vertices and −t on all black vertices. An isolated vertex contributes its own free coordinate. Thus ker L has dimension c and rank L=v−c. Every connected graph has at least v_component−1 edges, with equality precisely for a tree. Therefore e≥v−c, and surjectivity is equivalent to equality in every component. The dimension assertion follows from the kernel calculation. ∎

For a forest and a selected edge, deleting that edge and using the signed indicator of either resulting component gives an explicit preimage of its coordinate vector. Every internal edge contributes 1−1=0; the selected edge contributes 1. This proves the canonical cut formula used above without a positivity assumption.

Applied to (7), the nonzero coefficient on each support edge can be canceled. Requiring every edge coordinate vector to have a vertex-coefficient representation forces a forest. Thus the graph reduction itself is algebraically justified at this stage. This recovers, in linear-algebra form, the source's cycle obstruction and cut construction; it is not claimed as a new result.

**Limit.** The c-dimensional freedom concerns only the incidence equations. The remaining equations may restrict it. We do not claim that the full consistency variety has this affine space as a free factor, or that canonical exchange coefficients are the only possible raw coefficients.

## 4. Approaches 2 and 3: degeneration, saturation and primeness

### Proposition 2 (rank-two boundary check)

In the normalized rank-two model, bar(1)=1 and q=q_1. The support with C_{11}^1=0 has S={q=1}. The support with C_{11}^1≠0 has

C_{00}^0=1, C_{01}^1=C_{10}^1=1, C_{11}^0=q, C_{11}^1=q−1,

and S=C\{0,1}. Its closure in the q-coordinate is A¹.

**Proof.** The canonical construction gives C_{11}^1=0 for the first support and q−1 for the second. All other coefficients are the ones displayed. In the first case the dimension equation reads q²=q; the nonzero requirement q≠0 leaves q=1. In the second case it reads q²=q+(q−1)q, an identity. Multiplication is the unital algebra with generator t satisfying t²=q+(q−1)t, so associativity is automatic; reciprocity and the finite move equations follow by direct substitution (also checked by the verifier). The support conditions give exactly q≠0,1. ∎

This is a concrete warning about formulations. Exact supports cannot themselves be equated with closed irreducible components in the q,C-coordinate space: the smaller support lies in the larger support's closure. In the full A,B-coordinate space, the two canonical choices are different, and the limiting large-support coefficients need not be the small-support canonical choice. Therefore this calculation is **not** a counterexample to the intended forest-component conjecture.

### Lemma 3 (correct algebraic target)

For any ideal I in C[x_1,...,x_m] and polynomial h, the closure of V(I)∩D(h) is V(I:h^∞). It is nonempty and irreducible precisely when sqrt(I:h^∞) is a proper prime ideal. If several such closures are proposed as the components of a given ambient variety, coverage and pairwise noncontainment must also be proved.

**Proof.** Localization identifies V(I)∩D(h) with the closed locus of I C[x,h^{-1}] in D(h). The contraction of that localized ideal is I:h^∞: an element maps into the ideal exactly when some power of h times it lies in I. Taking radicals gives the vanishing ideal of the closure by the Nullstellensatz. The prime-ideal characterization of irreducible affine closed sets gives the claim. A finite closed irreducible cover consists exactly of the irreducible components after discarding sets contained in others. ∎

The special polynomial structure permits a useful exact elimination procedure. Begin with all affine equations. Solve them over Q. Reduce all remaining equations and every required nonzero form under that affine substitution. Any required form that becomes zero makes the stratum empty. A residual equation can be divided by a required nonzero linear factor whenever exact polynomial division has zero remainder. If it becomes affine, add that equation and repeat. If a nonzero constant appears, the stratum is empty. If all residual equations vanish, the stratum is an affine space minus finitely many hyperplanes.

Each division is by a unit in the prescribed localization, so these are equivalences, not generic-point heuristics. In the successful final case, the affine ideal is prime and none of the nonzero factors vanishes identically on it. Hence it is already saturated by their product and equals J_Γ. In particular, this proves the ideal itself prime, a stronger claim than merely proving its radical prime.

**Limit of the general route.** It is not proved that this process succeeds for arbitrary r. A quadratic residual equation can split while all designated support factors remain nonzero: for example uv=0 on D(u+v) has two components. This is a method-obstruction example, not a fusion-forest example. Counting many equations or bounding their degree by two does not establish primeness. A proof that all actual forest systems avoid this obstruction is missing.

## 5. Approach 4: an all-rank rigid class

### Proposition 4 (nonempty permutation-support charts are points)

Suppose every Γ_k is 1-regular. Then a nonempty normalized canonical chart satisfying (2)–(5) has q_i=1 for every i, and its convolution table is a group table with all nonzero coefficients equal to 1. In particular its saturated ideal is maximal and its closure is irreducible.

**Proof.** The unique output of the product of labels i,j defines an operation i·j. All nonzero coefficients are invertible on the chart. Associativity equates products with singleton nonzero supports, so (i·j)·k=i·(j·k). The unit support makes 0 the identity. Since each Γ_i is a perfect matching, left multiplication by i permutes the labels. Choose j with i·j=0. Associativity gives i·(j·i)=i, and injectivity of left multiplication gives j·i=0. Thus the operation is a group, with the prescribed dual as inverse.

The only output of i·bar(i) is 0. Equations (3) and (4) give q_i²=q_i, because q_bar(i)=q_i and C_{i,bar(i)}^0=q_i. Since q_i is invertible, q_i=1. For any i,j, equation (4) then says the unique nonzero coefficient equals 1. Algebraically the localized ideal contains each q_i−1. If the chart is nonempty, it cannot contain a nonzero constant, so its contraction is precisely the corresponding maximal ideal. ∎

Finite-group planar algebras and their permutation fusion graphs are established examples, explicitly identified in Lu–Liu Example 4.8. This proposition supplies a short rigidity argument; it does not assert novelty or extend that existence theorem to other forests.

## 6. Approach 5: exhaustive exact elimination through rank four

### Proposition 5 (computer-assisted, explicitly normalized scope)

For every involution and every reciprocity-compatible forest support in the model of Section 2 with 2≤r≤4, J_Γ is either the unit ideal or an affine-linear prime ideal. Thus every nonempty S_Γ is an open subset of an affine space, and its closure is irreducible.

The exhaustive results are:

| Rank and duality | Bit patterns | Forest patterns | Empty charts | Nonempty labelled charts | Nonempty label classes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2, both labels fixed | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| 3, all labels fixed | 16 | 13 | 4 | 9 | 5 |
| 3, 1 and 2 exchanged | 4 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 4, all labels fixed | 1024 | 358 | 289 | 69 | 15 |
| 4, 1 and 2 exchanged | 128 | 50 | 41 | 9 | 9 |

Label classes quotient only by permutations fixing 0 and commuting with the chosen involution. There are 426 forest patterns altogether, of which 91 are nonempty. The 7 rank-three and 24 rank-four label classes agree with the algebraic counts in the OWR report. This agreement is a check, not a completeness proof for an uninspected revised system.

**Proof certificate and replay.** The program `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates the orbits of all nonzero-label triples under (2), assigns every bit pattern, inserts the forced unit edges, and rejects exactly those patterns with a graph cycle by a disjoint-set calculation. All involutions on at most three nonunit labels are represented in the table. The canonical coefficient algorithm is the cut construction from Section 2; it directly checks every incidence equation (7). It constructs each remaining polynomial using exact rational arithmetic.

It includes both comparisons obtained from the three W-expansions, including (6b). For every one of the 426 forest cases it then executes the sound elimination procedure proved in Section 4. No numerical sampling, Gröbner-basis decision oracle, unproved primeness test, or positivity sieve is used. Every successful division is verified by the exact polynomial identity dividend=divisor·quotient+remainder. All substitutions are simultaneous. The algorithm would stop with an explicit inconclusive error on an uneliminated nonlinear equation. It encounters none.

The complete per-case receipt is `verification.json`. It includes the support bitstring, involution, affine equations derived at each round, final generators and excluded forms for each nonempty chart, or a certified empty-case obstruction. The replay recomputes the receipt and compares it byte-for-byte. The source of each bit and every polynomial equation is the explicit formula above, not a downloaded classification table. This is a finite algebraic certificate for the bounded proposition; an independent implementation is appropriate for review. ∎

## 7. Exact remaining gap

For arbitrary r we have neither proved primeness of sqrt(J_Γ) for every nonempty forest chart nor produced an actual forest chart where it fails. The elementary unit-division algorithm may encounter a genuinely nonlinear residual system in larger ranks; no general termination-to-affine theorem is supplied. The group-support result does not cover branching forests. The finite enumeration through rank four gives no quantified control of higher ranks.

Furthermore, translating any proposed all-rank answer to the original statement requires a clear choice of ambient coordinates, treatment of canonical coefficient choices versus all raw coefficients, nondegenerate trace/circle conditions, and closure of exact supports. This packet does not use those choices to manufacture a counterexample. The honest disposition remains **unsolved, 5/5**.
