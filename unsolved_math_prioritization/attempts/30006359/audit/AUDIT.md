## Independent computation

The author's implementation was read for review and executed for reproducibility. It was **not imported or called by the independent verifier**. The independent program reads the claimed final per-case outcomes only after reconstructing its own mathematical inputs; it does not use the author's intermediate affine rounds.

Different mechanisms were used where practical:

1. Reciprocity support classes are generated by two permutations of triples, rather than the author's six-transform closure routine.
2. The forced unit/inverse support is inserted as a union of explicit triples.
3. Forest recognition repeatedly deletes leaves. A nonempty remainder is a cycle obstruction. This is independent of the author's disjoint-set test.
4. Canonical coefficients are reconstructed by propagation of the incidence equations from an anchored vertex in a tree. This is independent of removing an edge and collecting one cut component. The special unit conventions are respected. Every edge incidence identity is checked.
5. Convolution coefficients and all remaining equations are independently transcribed into exact integer coefficient vectors, then converted to rational polynomials. Both move comparisons retain their leading support polynomials. No cancellation assumption is needed to construct the ideal.
6. Saturation is computed by exact lexicographic Gröbner elimination of an auxiliary variable from `(I, 1 - t h)`. Required factors can first be reduced modulo I because this leaves the localization unchanged. A factor belonging to I certifies emptiness. Constants and repeated factors are harmless.
7. The eliminated ideal is compared with the claimed affine ideal as a reduced Gröbner basis over Q. Each surviving generator is checked to have degree one. Each required support/dimension factor and the normalized dimension sum are checked to remain nonzero modulo that ideal.
8. Label classes are independently recomputed by the centralizer of the fixed involution in permutations fixing 0. No additional isomorphism classification is inferred.

Exact rational Gröbner arithmetic gives a second independently executable check, not a claim of a certified computer-algebra kernel. The author uses elementary localized elimination instead, reducing correlated algorithmic risk. Since the final nonunit ideals are affine-linear over Q, their extensions to C are prime as well.

## Adversarial checks of the mathematical arguments

### Incidence rank and exchange-coefficient freedom

The kernel of the bipartite unsigned incidence map is exactly one alternating constant per connected component, with isolated vertices included. Rank is therefore v-c, and surjectivity is equivalent to the forest condition. The cut-vector construction is valid over C without positivity. This proves the incidence-level statement, but does not make the kernel a free factor after all higher equations are imposed. The packet explicitly observes that limit.

### Support strata, closures, and actual components

An exact support chart is locally closed. Saturation gives its closure, and irreducibility is controlled by the radical of the saturated ideal. Even prime chart closures must cover an ambient variety and be pairwise noncontained before they can be identified with its components.

The rank-two check is correct: the zero-edge chart gives q=1, while the nonzero-edge chart gives q different from 0 and 1 and has line closure. Thus containment occurs after forgetting canonical exchange-coefficient coordinates. The two fixed canonical A,B choices differ. This example does not refute the authors' intended conjecture in an unspecified full coordinate convention. The packet correctly refuses that conclusion.

### Unit division and equality of saturated ideals

Let P be the affine constraints accumulated by the author's algorithm. Every new affine constraint is derived modulo earlier constraints by division by a unit in the prescribed localization. Inductively, P belongs to the localized original ideal. At a successful termination, substitution shows every original equation belongs to P. Consequently the localized ideals agree. A nonzero product of the excluded forms remains nonzero in the affine domain R/P, so P is already saturated. This proves equality of ideals, not merely agreement at sampled points or equality of radicals.

Simultaneous substitution is essential and is present. Nonzero constants and identically vanishing required factors are correctly treated as emptiness. A residual nonlinear equation would stop the author computation; none survives in the finite range. The independent full-factor saturation check confirms that cancellation did not lose a component.

### Group-support rigidity

For nonempty perfect-matching support, associativity forces associativity of the singleton-output operation: its coefficients are nonzero, so unequal output labels cannot cancel. The forced unit and permutation left translations then give a group. The inverse product and dimension equation give q_i(q_i-1)=0. Localizing at q_i yields q_i=1, and the unique product coefficient is 1. Thus the saturated ideal is maximal. The argument does not extend automatically to branching trees and the packet does not suggest it does.

### Normalization, signs, and excluded degeneracies

The normalized model fixes q_0=1 and excludes every dimension and declared support coefficient from zero. It does not impose positivity. The non-self-dual rank-three chart with q_1=q_2=-1 satisfies the independently reconstructed system; its normalized dimension sum is -1. It cannot be discarded by a unitary or positive-coefficient criterion.

Restoring the circle relation can alter irreducibility. For example, over the rank-two point q_1=1, adjoining the circle equation gives delta squared equal to 2 and hence two complex points. An irreducible normalized chart is therefore not automatically an irreducible full circle-retaining locus. This is already a stated scope boundary, not a defect in Proposition 5. The independent run verifies that the dimension sum is not identically zero on any of the 91 nonempty charts, so excluding its zero locus does preserve these normalized closures.

### Finite exhaustiveness and stopping point

For ranks two through four, the five involution families exhaust involutions up to relabeling fixing 0. The canonical construction is equivariant under those relabelings. The count is therefore exhaustive in exactly the stated normalized model. Neither those counts, overdetermination, nor degree at most two proves higher-rank primeness. The uv example used in the packet is correctly labeled as a general algebraic obstruction, not a forest counterexample.
