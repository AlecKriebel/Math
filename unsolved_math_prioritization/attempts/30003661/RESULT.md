**Proposed original disposition: unsolved, 5/5.** The assigned OWR question asks whether planar pathwise connected choice is strongly Weihrauch equivalent to WKL. The expanded paper's ordinary-equivalence Question7.3 is retained separately. No full positive or negative answer, historical novelty or independent review is claimed by this author summary.

## Retained scope

1. **Planarization obstruction.** A name-level shared-output lemma rules out every continuous diagonal-preserving interpolation flattening of the known twisted cube, plus a broader vertical-singleton-fiber template
2. **Sharp extensional finite capacity.** Four finite choices admit an extensional computable planar path-connected encoding with strong decoding. Five do not, even with continuous extensional preprocessing: negative-name continuity forces monotonicity, and the resulting pair paths would almost embed K5 in the plane. Classical crossing parity is credited
3. **Name-dependent finite coding.** An explicit exclusion-history interval construction strongly handles every fixed finite discrete choice. A fixed rational piecewise-linear simplex decoder works for every output name. The general finite-choice upper bound is known; this construction explains why the extensional obstruction does not settle the unrestricted problem
4. **Computable graph-carrier obstruction.** Every nonempty connected co-c.e. closed subset of a computably parametrized arc or finite embedded graph contains a computable point, nonuniformly. Such output classes cannot encode a computable WKL instance without computable branches, even with ordinary postprocessing
5. **Countable-arc strengthening.** A compact path-connected co-c.e. set covered by countably many computably parametrized arcs has a computable point. A hard instance therefore has a residual subset outside all computable arcs. This is a classical Baire argument and does not effectively select a witness

## Exact remaining gap

The results constrain static/extensional encodings and final carrier geometry, but general strong Weihrauch preprocessing may depend on the negative input name. A full answer still requires an infinite name-dependent planar construction whose limit is path connected and whose every output name uniformly decodes a valid WKL branch, or a separation theorem covering all such constructions. Neither is established.

Negative closed-set information, Cauchy output names, ordinary path connectedness and absence of a supplied path witness are preserved throughout. The old nonuniform computability and Muchnik examples are not promoted to a uniform Weihrauch reduction. Finite illustrations do not replace topological proofs or establish a general separation.
