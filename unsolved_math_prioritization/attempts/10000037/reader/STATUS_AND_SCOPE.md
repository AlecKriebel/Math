# Mathematical status and scope

## Accepted negative conclusion

The canonical construction gives a complete negative example for the explicitly stated context-supported universal full-supercritical uniqueness assertion: there is a simple connected degree-four graph quasi-isometric to Z², with p_c ≤ 9/10, for which iid bond percolation at p = 19/20 has at least two infinite clusters with probability greater than 1/3. It also refutes the stronger universal all-parameter at-most-one assertion. See the [final independent acceptance audit](pass1_audit/INDEPENDENT_ACCEPTANCE.md).

This is a pointwise assertion at one fixed parameter. No behavior at p = p_c is needed to refute either universal assertion. The graph's exact critical probability is not calculated. The proof does not show that there are exactly two infinite clusters, that nonuniqueness is almost sure, or that uniqueness is monotone in p.

The source is an open question, not an incorrect theorem. Its short sentence leaves p implicit; the supercritical formulation is supported by context. No assertion about an author's unstated intended restriction follows from this counterexample.

## Endpoint and supplementary results

The dimension-one endpoint is settled separately: p_c = 1 for all connected bounded-degree graphs quasi-isometric to Z. For each fixed p < 1 there is almost surely no infinite cluster; at p = 1 there is exactly one. Its strictly supercritical parameter range is empty.

The degree-four planar one-ended graph in dimension two is the canonical witness. The geometry report's higher-dimensional subdivision and additive-only gadget constructions are supplementary, with their own stated constants and audited scope. No sharper degree bound is claimed for those extensions.

## Remaining limits

- Historical novelty and publication priority are uncertified. The literature review was bounded, and a negative search is not an originality certificate.
- Acceptance means the included authored mathematical audit accepts the argument. External peer review is not claimed.
- No general positive uniqueness theorem follows for intermediate parameters on other nontransitive graphs.
- High-density uniqueness results in the cited literature are not contradicted merely by nonuniqueness at this one intermediate parameter.
- The finite tests are not formal proofs of infinite survival, separation or nonuniqueness.

Earlier source reports state that they had authenticated no exact-target resolution. Those statements record the source-only stage and do not override the subsequent authored proof and acceptance.
