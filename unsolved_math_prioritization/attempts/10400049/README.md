# Bar-Natan's link-invariant Nullstellensatz: a counterexample

[CANDIDATE.md](CANDIDATE.md) gives an elementary counterexample to the literal arbitrary-ideal assertion over C. For g equal to the sum of squared pairwise linking numbers, the principal ideal I=(g(4g−1)) has nonempty actual zero locus, exactly the algebraically split links. Yet g is outside its radical. The obstruction is proved using a character on the full finite-type invariant algebra, obtained by polynomial restriction to the full-twist family and evaluation at one-half.

The candidate is frozen for independent adversarial review. Proposed classification: **claimed_solved, 1/5**, conditional on that review. No historical-priority claim is made. The separate linking-number-generated ideal example, characteristic-p version and completed-algebra variants are not claimed resolved.

Run `python verify.py` for 8,460 exact algebraic and resolution controls. These supplement the all-order singular-link proof; they are not a finite list of invariant computations offered as a universal proof.
