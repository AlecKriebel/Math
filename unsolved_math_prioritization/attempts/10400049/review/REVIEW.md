## 2. Finite type and actual zero set

For a prescribed collection of singular crossings, each pairwise linking number is an affine function of the binary resolutions. A self-crossing contributes no linking-number change, and an intercomponent crossing changes the appropriate linking number by one. The alternating third difference of the sum of their squares is therefore zero. This verifies order at most two for \(g\), with a degree bound independent of the number of components. The product rule gives order at most four for \(f=4g^2-g\).

Reversing a component orientation changes signs of the incident linking numbers; squaring removes that dependence. Permuting components only permutes the summands. Thus the construction also descends when orientations or component order are forgotten, with the finite-type assertion understood through the usual oriented skein convention.

For actual links, \(g\) is a nonnegative integer, zero exactly on algebraically split links. The other factor \(4g-1\) cannot vanish there. Since the ideal is principal and contains its generator, its common actual-link zero set is exactly \(g^{-1}(0)\). It is nonempty, containing the unlink; when the algebra ranges over all component counts, it includes the knots as well. Hence \(g\) belongs to the ideal of finite-type invariants vanishing on that set.

## 3. Polynomial restriction on the full algebra

The full-twist family is normalized correctly. The closure of the coherently oriented two-strand braid \(\sigma^{2n}\) has two components, and half the signed intercomponent crossing count is \(n\). Thus \(g(L_n)=n^2\).

The discrete-difference argument is valid at every order. Mark one crossing in each of \(r\) disjoint two-crossing blocks. Its two resolutions make that block either \(\sigma^2\) or \(\sigma\sigma^{-1}\), the latter cancelling to the identity. The alternating sum over all resolutions is exactly the \(r\)-th forward difference of \(v(L_n)\). Every resolution has even braid permutation, so closure retains the two components. Additional split components are unchanged. This is a genuine \(r\)-singular link under the source convention.

If \(v\) has order at most \(d\), these differences vanish for \(r=d+1\), for every nonnegative base index \(n\). Characteristic-zero finite-difference calculus therefore gives a polynomial \(P_v\) of degree at most \(d\), with the explicit Newton formula. Uniqueness follows because infinitely many distinct nonnegative integers cannot all be roots of a nonzero polynomial.

This construction applies separately to **every** \(v\in V\). Its degree may depend on \(v\); no finite-dimensional or bounded-order truncation of the whole algebra is being assumed. Linearity, multiplicativity and preservation of constants follow from equality of values at all integer indices and polynomial uniqueness:
\[
P_{vw}(n)=v(L_n)w(L_n)=P_v(n)P_w(n).
\]
Consequently \(v\mapsto P_v\) is a unital homomorphism on the full algebra.

## 4. Radical obstruction, checked independently in two forms

Composing that homomorphism with evaluation at \(1/2\) gives a character with
\[
\chi(g)=1/4,\qquad \chi(g(4g-1))=0.
\]
It kills every ideal multiple, but never kills a positive power of \(g\). Thus \(g\notin\sqrt I\), as claimed.

There is also a direct ideal-membership contradiction which independently checks the same point. If \(g^N=fv\) for an arbitrary finite-type invariant \(v\), restriction to the twist family would give
\[
t^{2N}=t^2(4t^2-1)P_v(t)
\]
as a polynomial identity, since it holds for all nonnegative integers. Substitution of \(t=1/2\) is impossible. This uses polynomial restriction of the arbitrary multiplier \(v\), so it cannot suffer from the error of testing ideal membership only inside \(\mathbb C[g]\).

The character is not evaluation at an actual link, since its value \(1/4\) on \(g\) is not an actual-link value. That is exactly the missing algebraic point responsible for the counterexample. It is not a failure of the classical Nullstellensatz over all algebraic points.

The characteristic-zero field extension and integer-valued-ring observation are valid: Newton denominators are invertible in a characteristic-zero field, while integer-valued finite-type sequences give rational Newton polynomials and hence a ring homomorphism to \(\mathbb Q\). No positive-characteristic conclusion follows or is asserted.

## 5. Essential boundaries of the result

The separate source ideal generated by all pairwise linking numbers is not \(g(4g-1)V\), even though their actual zero sets coincide. This proof does not compute the full vanishing ideal of algebraically split links or decide that separate example.

The absence of a completion restriction matters. For instance, in a completion permitting the formal inverse of \(1-4g\), the factor \(4g-1\) would be a unit and this particular obstruction would disappear. Similarly, a homogeneous-ideal restriction would change the allowable ideal. Those are different formulations, not hidden assumptions of the printed question.

The same reasoning works on fixed-component link categories with at least two components and on the stated algebra of all finite-component links. It does not use knots alone, a transfinite-order invariant, an empty zero set, or an assumption that all characters are links.
