## Scope requirements

The measures are signed Radon measures on punctured space, with finite total variation on sets bounded away from the origin. The source halfspaces H(omega,p)={x:x·omega<p} have closures avoiding the origin exactly when p<0. They are not arbitrary halfspaces whose boundary merely misses the origin while the region contains it.

Rapid decay means every inverse-power bound for the total variation of the tail. Pointwise density decay and merely finite mass at infinity are not interchangeable with this condition. The cone alternative requires one common closed cone Q with Q minus the origin contained in an open linear halfspace. For the convex cone reading this is the usual pointed/proper condition. Two competitors supported in unrelated proper cones do not automatically have their difference supported in one such cone.

Uniqueness from either theorem is class-restricted: take the difference of two measures satisfying the same sufficient class assumptions. Theorems2/4 are not a theorem that every measure belongs to one of these two classes, nor an iff classification of all uniqueness classes. The non-integral homogeneous alternatives in Theorem3 are additional known sufficient results and must preserve their own signed/nonnegative hypotheses.

The Section5 construction with inverse complex powers supplies signed kernel densities in dimension2. Using power3 or higher makes total variation finite outside every ball; the power2 example alone has a logarithmically divergent absolute tail and cannot be used for this finite-away class without further modification. Jordan positive and negative parts can give distinct nonnegative measures with equal avoiding-origin halfspace data when the signed kernel has an absolutely integrable tail.

The source support theorems are credited mathematical inputs. This audit does not assert a new proof of their full distribution theory or of all convergence/regular-variation extensions.
