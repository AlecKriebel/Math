# References and attribution boundaries

This bibliography records verified primary citations and the precise role of each in the manuscript. It does not reproduce third-party source documents. The mathematical proof chapters are authored exposition; the source PDFs remain external. Novelty and priority are unestablished.

## R1  The strict majorant property in arbitrary dimensions

P. T. Gressman, S. Guo, L. B. Pierce, J. Roos and P.-L. Yung, *On the strict majorant property in arbitrary dimensions*, Quarterly Journal of Mathematics 74(1) (2023), 139–161.

- [DOI and journal record](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haac021)
- [Published text at the NSF public-access repository](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10484227)
- [arXiv version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12538v2)

Proposition 4.1, journal p. 151, supplies the dominant-coefficient expansion (arXiv v2 Proposition 10). The circuit specialization, signs, and remainder needed here are written out in Proof 01. Proposition 8.2 and Lemma 8.3, journal p. 159, give affine-independent sufficiency for all positive exponents, even with complex coefficients (arXiv v2 Proposition 13 and Lemma 14). This is the explicit published theorem invoked for that sufficient direction.

Theorem 1.1, pp. 140–141, concerns validity simultaneously for all positive exponents. Theorem 1.2, p. 141, supplies infinitely many intervals of failure for each infinite set. Those quantifiers differ from the prescribed-exponent finiteness theorem in Proof 06. Theorem 1.3 constructs a suitable bad set for each prescribed noneven exponent; its set can depend on the exponent.

## R2  Circle and disk differentiation and bisubharmonic functions

Albert Baernstein II and Robert C. Culverhouse, *Majorization of sequences, sharp vector Khinchin inequalities, and bisubharmonic functions*, Studia Mathematica 152(3) (2002), 231–248.

- [DOI and journal record](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm152-3-3)
- [Primary full text](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/90752)

Theorems 1–3, pp. 232–233, concern moments of sums of independent sphere variables, majorization of squared coefficients, and bisubharmonicity. Lemma 1 and formula (3.2), p. 239, and formula (3.6), p. 240, provide the sphere/ball differentiation mechanism used here. The relevant identities, smoothing arguments, and positivity applications are included in Proofs 01, 03, 04 and 09. The scalar fourth-derivative threshold has established analytic predecessors; it is not presented as a newly discovered calculus fact. The paper is not cited as an affine-circuit or frequency-set classification.

## R3  A methodological predecessor with a stronger property

Stefan Neuwirth, *Metric unconditionality and Fourier analysis*, Studia Mathematica 131(1) (1998), 19–62.

- [Corrected arXiv version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9707211v2)

In arXiv v2, Definition 3.1.1 is on p. 16, Proposition 3.2.1 on p. 18, and Remark 3.3.3 and Computational Lemma 3.3.4 on p. 21. The cited unconditionality statements are in the Banach range 1≤p<∞, with the relevant classification at noneven exponents. The relation/binomial expansions are a methodological predecessor. Real 1-unconditionality in that setting allows complex starting coefficients and requires invariance under real signs. It must not be identified with the one-sided all-positive comparison for real coefficients used in this manuscript. In particular, the at-most-two-elements unconditionality statement does not contradict the positive AP triples here.

## R4  Earlier specific three-term comparisons

Sándor Krenedits, *Three-term idempotent counterexamples in the Hardy–Littlewood majorant problem*, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 388(1) (2012), 136–150.

- [DOI and journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2011.09.053)
- [arXiv version 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0409v3)

Proposition 4 and Remark 5, arXiv pp. 3–4, treat unit-coefficient AP comparisons. Proposition 6, p. 4, treats the support {0,1,3} on (2,4), and Proposition 12, p. 11, treats {0,1,4} on (4,6). These specific unit-coefficient results do not give an arbitrary-amplitude positive classification, and the latter fixed support is not the growing fixed-window {0,2,K} family of Proof 23.

## Limits of the attribution check

The primary-source comparison did not locate the stated prescribed-exponent finiteness bounds or the exact low-exponent classification statements in the inspected sources. This limited negative finding does not establish novelty. In particular, full originals of some early Boas and Bachelis papers were not accessible in that comparison. No priority claim, exhaustive literature claim, or external publication-status claim is made.

Standard background used explicitly in the included arguments includes normalized Haar measure and Fourier uniqueness on compact tori, elementary convexity, distributional differentiation and Weyl's lemma, ordinary differential-equation uniqueness, and elementary complex analysis. The local Green representation, binomial remainder bounds, and Fourier singularity estimate needed beyond that background are included in full.
