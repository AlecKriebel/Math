# Proof 01  Circuit obstructions and positive uncentered blocks

This is a reading edition of an accepted authored proof. Its mathematical scope is unchanged. Source and reading-edition hashes are recorded in the [inventory](../CONTENT_AND_DEPENDENCIES.md). No novelty claim is made.

Public analytic inputs and exact attribution are listed in [References](../REFERENCES.md).

The circuit-obstruction parts of this chapter assume p>0 is not a positive even integer. All coefficient families are real and finitely supported on an arbitrary Gamma subset Z^d. The positive-block statements retain their explicitly stated exponent ranges.

## 1. Circuit obstruction lemma

Let S={n_0,...,n_{s-1}} be a minimally affinely dependent finite set of distinct integer points. Let lambda=(lambda_0,...,lambda_{s-1}) be a primitive integer generator of its one-dimensional affine relation lattice:

sum lambda_j=0, sum lambda_j n_j=0, gcd(lambda_j)=1.

All lambda_j are nonzero, and s>=3. Write K=sum(lambda_j>0)lambda_j=sum(lambda_j<0)(-lambda_j). In particular K>=2.

Fix an index i. Translate n_i to zero, set its coefficient to 1, and let c be lambda with coordinate i omitted. The remaining frequencies have a one-dimensional rational linear relation space generated by c. The vector c is primitive: any integer dividing its entries divides lambda_i=-sum_{j!=i}lambda_j, hence divides every lambda entry. Its positive and negative masses are K and K-|lambda_i|, in either order.

Put q=p/2 and choose sigma_j in {+1,-1} such that product_{j!=i}sigma_j^{|c_j|}=-1. This is possible because c has an odd entry. For t>0 sufficiently small define

P_+(x)=1+t sum_{j!=i} e((n_j-n_i)·x),
P_sigma(x)=1+t sum_{j!=i} sigma_j e((n_j-n_i)·x).

Writing N=sum_{j!=i}|c_j| and r=|c_+|, u=|c_-|, direct uniformly convergent binomial expansion for (s-1)t<1 gives

integral |P_sigma|^{2q} - integral |P_+|^{2q}
= -4 binom(q,r) binom(q,u) [r!/prod(c_+)_j!] [u!/prod(c_-)_j!] t^N + O(t^{N+1}).

For completeness: each integrated monomial has exponent multiindices alpha,beta with alpha-beta=k c, k in Z. Terms k=0 are sign-independent. For k!=0, total degree is at least ||kc||_1>=N. Equality occurs only for k=+1, alpha=c_+,beta=c_-, or k=-1 with the pair reversed. Each has the stated binomial/multinomial coefficient. Their sign factor is -1 for P_sigma versus +1 for P_+. Absolute convergence in a fixed small disk bounds the higher-degree Taylor tail by O(t^{N+1}). This remains valid when r or u is zero, using binom(q,0)=1 and 0!=1.

Consequently a necessary condition for the strict majorant property at q is, for every primitive affine circuit and every i,

binom(q,K) binom(q,K-|lambda_i|) >= 0.          (C)

None of these binomials vanishes because q is positive and nonintegral.

This is only a necessary condition. The fixed witness in [Proof 19](19_uniform_027.md) disproves its sufficiency throughout 4<p<6, in particular at p=5.

## 2. Exact classification for 0<p<2

For every fixed p in (0,2), Gamma satisfies the stated finite-support real majorant property if and only if Gamma is affinely independent.

Sufficiency is the cited published proposition. For necessity, an affinely dependent Gamma contains a finite minimally affinely dependent S. Its primitive lambda has an odd entry. There is an odd lambda_i with 0<|lambda_i|<K:

- If K is even, any side containing an odd entry contains at least two odd entries, since the sum on that side is even. Each such entry has absolute value less than K.
- If K is odd, both sides contain an odd entry. Since s>=3, at least one side has at least two entries. Every entry on that side has absolute value less than K.

For q in (0,1) and integer k>=1, sign binom(q,k)=(-1)^(k-1). The integers K and K-|lambda_i| are positive and have opposite parities. Their binomial product is negative. The explicit finite test polynomials above therefore violate the majorant property.

## 3. Necessary condition for 2<p<4

If Gamma has the strict majorant property for one p in (2,4), every primitive affine circuit has K=2. Thus its circuits can only have patterns (1,-2,1) or (1,1,-1,-1), up to permutation and overall sign.

Proof. For q in (1,2), B(k)=binom(q,k) is positive exactly for k in {0,1,2} and for even integers k>=4, and negative for odd k>=3.

If K is odd and K>=3, each side of lambda has an odd entry. Choose any odd |lambda_i|. K-|lambda_i| is even and nonnegative, so B(K)<0 and B(K-|lambda_i|)>0, contradicting (C).

If K is even and K>=4, primitiveness forces an odd entry |lambda_i|. Then B(K)>0, and K-|lambda_i| is odd. Condition (C) forces K-|lambda_i|=1, so |lambda_i|=K-1. On the same sign side the other entries have total mass 1, hence consist of one entry of absolute value 1. Applying (C) at that entry gives B(K)B(K-1)<0, another contradiction.

The two K=2 circuit patterns follow from nonzero integer coefficients and primitiveness: a side has partition 2 or 1+1; the 2-versus-2 case is nonprimitive and would also identify two distinct frequencies.

The structure of configurations with only mass-two circuits is proved in [Proof 02](02_mass_two_structure.md); [Proof 04](04_radial_joins.md) completes sufficiency for p>=3.

## 4. Separate sign comparison from arbitrary majorants

For p>=1, a sign-comparison property for every nonnegative coefficient vector A implies the full real |a_j|<=A_j property. The function a -> integral |sum a_j e(n_j·x)|^p is convex on the real coefficient space. Every point of the coefficient box prod[-A_j,A_j] is a convex combination of its sign vertices. If all vertices are bounded by the positive vertex, convexity bounds the entire box.

For 0<p<1 that convexity argument is invalid; the sufficiency used in section 2 is the full published affine-independence result instead.

## 5. Positive parallelogram class

Let u,v be rationally linearly independent integer vectors and Gamma={0,u,v,u+v}. Then Gamma has the full real majorant property at every p>=2.

The homomorphism x -> (u·x,v·x) pushes normalized torus Haar measure to normalized Haar measure on T^2. Translation, overall sign and changes of variables t->t+pi, s->s+pi reduce the sign comparison to

integral |a+b e^{it}+c e^{is}+d e^{i(t+s)}|^{2q}
>= integral |a+b e^{it}+c e^{is}-d e^{i(t+s)}|^{2q},

with a,b,c,d>=0 and q>=1. After integrating in s, put

H_q(X,Y)=(2pi)^-1 integral_0^{2pi} |sqrt(X)+sqrt(Y)e^{is}|^{2q} ds.

The two integrands remaining in t are H_q(A+B cos t,C+D cos t) and H_q(A+B cos t,C-D cos t), where A=a^2+b^2, B=2ab, C=c^2+d^2, D=2cd are nonnegative with A>=B, C>=D.

For X>Y>0, H_q=X^q * 2F1(-q,-q;1;Y/X), so differentiation gives

partial_X partial_Y H_q = q^2(q-1) X^{q-2} * 2F1(2-q,1-q;2;Y/X).

Euler's transformation rewrites the last hypergeometric function as

(1-Y/X)^(2q-1) * 2F1(q,q+1;2;Y/X),

which is positive because its latter series has positive coefficients. For q>1, the map (x,y) -> (x^2+y^2)^q is C^2 on R^2, with second derivatives extending continuously as zero at the origin. Square roots are smooth for X,Y>0, so differentiation under the s-integral shows H_q is C^2 throughout the positive quadrant, including X=Y. The mixed derivative positivity off the diagonal therefore extends to the diagonal by continuity; integrating this derivative over arbitrary positive rectangles proves supermodularity. Extend rectangles to the axes by continuity. For q=1, H_1=X+Y is modular. Pairing t values with cos t=z and cos t=-z yields, for z>=0,

H_q(A+Bz,C+Dz)+H_q(A-Bz,C-Dz)
>= H_q(A+Bz,C-Dz)+H_q(A-Bz,C+Dz).

Integrating the symmetric law of cos t proves the sign comparison. Section 4 gives arbitrary majorants. Zero coefficients follow by continuity.

This class is affinely dependent and therefore demonstrates explicitly why the fixed-p>=2 classification cannot simply repeat affine independence.


## 6. Positive two-layer simplex/prism class

For any n>=1 let Gamma={e_i+f_j: i=1,2; j=1,...,n} in Z^{2+n}, with e_i and f_j the corresponding standard basis vectors. Every subset of Gamma has the full real strict majorant property for every p>=2. The same holds for any integer-frequency configuration with the same affine relation lattice, by Haar pushforward. In particular this includes {0,u,v,u+v} with u,v independent. No assertion of novelty or full classification is made.

### Multiradius supermodularity lemma

Define, for s=(s_1,...,s_n) in [0,infinity)^n,

F_p(s)=E |sum_j sqrt(s_j) Z_j|^p,

where Z_j are independent uniform unit complex numbers. For p>=2, F_p is lattice-supermodular:

F_p(x join y)+F_p(x meet y) >= F_p(x)+F_p(y),

where join/meet mean coordinatewise maximum/minimum.

Proof for p>2. On the complex plane, let M_s be convolution with normalized circle measure of radius sqrt(s), and D_s convolution with normalized disk area measure of that radius. For a smooth function g,

partial_s M_s g = (1/4) D_s Delta g.

Indeed, differentiate the circular mean with respect to r=sqrt(s), then use the divergence theorem; the boundary flux is the disk integral of Delta g. The operators all commute. Write f(z)=|z|^p. As a distribution,

Delta^2 f(z)=p^2(p-2)^2 |z|^{p-4} >=0.

This is locally integrable when p>2; distributional differentiation creates no delta term, because both radial boundary fluxes vanish at radius zero. Mollify f by a nonnegative smooth compactly supported approximate identity rho_epsilon. Then Delta^2(f*rho_epsilon)>=0 everywhere. For i!=j and every strictly positive s,

`partial_{s_i} partial_{s_j} [M_{s_1}...M_{s_n}(f*rho_epsilon)](0)`
`= (1/16) [D_{s_i} D_{s_j} product_{k!=i,j} M_{s_k} (Delta^2 f*rho_epsilon)](0) >=0.`

Nonnegative mixed partial derivatives imply the lattice-supermodularity inequality on the positive orthant. Let epsilon tend to zero; convergence is uniform on compact sets of all circle-sum arguments, so the inequality passes to F_p. Extend to zero coordinates by continuity. For p=2, F_2(s)=sum_j s_j is modular, completing the proof.

### Application to the frequency class

The exponential polynomial is sum_j (a_{1j} U_1+a_{2j} U_2) V_j, with independent uniform unit phases U_1,U_2,V_1,...,V_n. Fix nonnegative magnitudes a_j,b_j. For a real sign pattern, absorb each first-row sign into V_j, and factor out U_1. The resulting norm integral is

E_t F_p(A_1+sigma_1 B_1 cos t,...,A_n+sigma_n B_n cos t),

where A_j=a_j^2+b_j^2, B_j=2a_j b_j>=0, and sigma_j in {+1,-1}. The all-positive coefficients correspond to every sigma_j=+1.

For every z>=0, apply lattice supermodularity to x=A+sigma B z and y=A-sigma B z. Their join is A+Bz and meet A-Bz. Therefore

F_p(A+Bz)+F_p(A-Bz) >= F_p(A+sigma Bz)+F_p(A-sigma Bz).

The law of cos t is symmetric under z->-z. Integrating this paired inequality proves sign domination. The p>=1 convexity argument in section 4 gives the full arbitrary-majorant property. Omitting points is equivalent to setting coefficients to zero.

For the affine-lattice transfer, insert an independent uniform common phase. The augmented characters (1,n_j) have annihilator ker_Z[(1,n_j)]. Labelled equality of these affine relation lattices implies equality of the compact-subgroup Haar pushforward measures, by equality of every Fourier coefficient. The absolute value of the polynomial is unchanged by the inserted common phase. This transfer is not needed for the standard-coordinate example itself.


## 7. Cross-polytope without its center

The configuration Gamma={e_j,-e_j:1<=j<=n} also has the full real majorant property for every p>=2. This follows from an exact equality of coefficient-dependent norm functions with the two-layer class of section 6.

For arbitrary real coefficient vectors a,b, define A(Z)=sum_j a_j Z_j and B(Z)=sum_j b_j Z_j for independent uniform unit complex variables Z_j. The cross-polytope polynomial is A(Z)+conjugate(B(Z)). Inserting an independent uniform common rotation W does not change its expectation, because (W Z_j)_j has the same joint law as (Z_j)_j. Thus

E_Z |A(Z)+conjugate(B(Z))|^p
= E_{Z,W} |W A(Z)+conjugate(W) conjugate(B(Z))|^p
= E_{Z,U} |A(Z)+U conjugate(B(Z))|^p
= E_{Z,U} |A(Z)+U B(Z)|^p.

Here U=W^{-2} is uniform, and the last equality follows by conditioning on Z: averaging over U depends only on |A(Z)| and |B(Z)|. The final expression is exactly the norm function of the standard two-layer class with real coefficients a,b. Apply its majorant comparison to conclude. All equalities hold for every p>0; positivity is asserted only for p>=2.

This argument does not include the extra frequency 0 at the center of the cross-polytope. No result for adjoining that center is claimed here.
