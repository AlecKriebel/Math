# Primary-source audit: Hopf-link application

Checked 8 October 2026.

## Inspected sources

1. OWR 8/2020, Rachael Boyd with Corey Bregman, “Embedding spaces of Hopf links,” pp.501–502. The PDF was downloaded, its two-page contribution read, and printed p.502 visually inspected. The quotient, one-edge inclusion, known quaternion computation, and proposed RAAG identification occur in that contribution. “Symmetric automorphism group” is not further defined there. A naming convention cannot remove the no-subgroup obstruction proved in the companion note.
2. Boyd–Bregman, arXiv:2504.21806v2, “The embedding space of a Hopf link,” 18 August 2025. Definitions 2.1–2.3, Theorems A–B, and the Sections 3–4 argument were inspected. The application relies on their smooth theorem; it does not claim a complete independent audit of every parameterised isotopy in Section 3. The round-cover and quaternion endpoint calculations were reconstructed. No journal status beyond the arXiv record is asserted.
3. Damiani–Kamada, “On the group of ring motions of an H-trivial link,” accepted manuscript of the 2019 published paper. Section 2 fixes the round-motion convention; Section 6, especially Theorems 6.4 and 6.6, supplies C4 for labelled round components and Q8 after allowing exchange. Its proof of the nontrivial full-turn element uses the double-linking invariant credited there. This packet does not silently promote that round result to a smooth result without Boyd–Bregman's smooth theorem.
4. Boyd–Bregman, “Embedding spaces of split links,” published version, Advances in Mathematics 470 (2025), 110235. Definitions, Theorem A, Section 4.3's Dahm homomorphism, and Example 4.12 were inspected. The n-unknot/m-Hopf convention and its specialization to one Hopf link agree with the application.
5. Goldsmith, “Motion of links in the 3-sphere,” Mathematica Scandinavica 50 (1982), 167–205. The official bibliographic record was checked. The purported PDF download returned HTML, so this audit makes no claim of fresh full-text verification. Earlier credit is preserved through the explicit citations in the inspected primary papers.

## Separate S^3 discrepancy

The v2 Hopf-link preprint's Theorem C states an S^3-space model RP^2 × RP^2, whose fundamental group is C2 × C2. Yet its introduction also says that the S^3 and R^3 motion groups agree, citing Corollary B of the split-link paper; its R^3 Theorem A gives Q8. These statements cannot all describe identical spaces and isomorphic groups as written. In the published split-link paper, Proposition 4.13 and Corollary B would identify the point-pushing image with Inn(pi_1(complement)); for the Hopf group Z^2 that inner group is trivial. Thus this comparison cannot simply be invoked to identify the two ambient-space cases alongside the stated Theorem C.

This is a localized consistency warning, not a claim here to decide which S^3 theorem or proof needs repair. No step of the single-edge R^3 argument invokes Proposition 4.13, Corollary B, Theorem C, or an ambient-space comparison. The R^3 theorem, round group, and source quotient are matched directly.
