## Setup and dependencies

For a field F, G_F means its absolute Galois group. Throughout generic means t is transcendental. Geometric means G_{Qbar(t)}. No unspecified rational specialization is substituted for either group. Groups are given up to a compatible basis choice. D_5 has order 10, and F_20 means AGL_1(F_5).

The exact imported inputs are: the Tautz--Top--Verberkmoes RM construction; Darmon--Mestre Proposition 2.1 (RM descent), Proposition 3.1(1) (prime-ideal projective residual images for C_1, not C_2), and the infinity inertia trace immediately preceding it; Takei's thesis Proposition 4.1.1 (specified Igusa--Clebsch invariant formulas). Standard background is hyperelliptic two-torsion as even branch subsets, the Weil pairing and self-adjoint real multiplication, Goursat's lemma, and simplicity/automorphism facts for PSL_2 over a prime field. For the last facts, see Kucharczyk's 2015 thesis, Propositions 4.2--4.3, printed p. 42: https://d-nb.info/1077289340/34 . The normal-subgroup assertion for SL_2 follows from projective simplicity and perfectness (which also follows from elementary unipotent commutators for fields of size at least five).

The geometric-to-arithmetic step uses the usual constants exact sequence for a geometrically connected characteristic-zero function field. Full monodromy over C(t) and Qbar(t) agree for this family by invariance of geometric etale fundamental groups under algebraically closed characteristic-zero extension. The congruence-lifting lemma and split-prime independence argument are proved below. Finite matrix enumerations are supplementary only.

## Complete arguments

## Approach 1: explicit two-torsion through the Chebyshev cover

Started after the inherited-attempt gate and primary-source scope check on 2026-10-07. This is a mathematical attempt, not a source-retrieval turn.

Put f(X)=X^5-5X^3+5X and let t be transcendental over Q. Write C_t:y^2=f(x)+t and J_t=Jac(C_t). The elementary identity f(u+u^{-1})=u^5+u^{-5} suggests computing the entire 2-division field rather than inferring it from RM.

Let zeta be a primitive fifth root of unity, take u transcendental, and set t=-(u^5+u^{-5}). The rational map u -> t has degree 10. Thus M=Q(zeta,u) has degree 40 over Q(t), and its 40 automorphisms are specified by zeta -> zeta^a and u -> zeta^b u^e, with a in F_5^*, b in F_5, e in {1,-1}. The five distinct roots of f(X)+t are r_i=zeta^i u+zeta^{-i}u^{-1}. On their labels this automorphism acts by i -> e a i+e b. The kernel has order two, generated by kappa:(zeta,u)->(zeta^{-1},u^{-1}). Therefore the splitting field is M^{<kappa>}, with Galois group AGL_1(F_5), of order 20.

The constant field of M is Q(zeta), so the constant field of the splitting field is Q(zeta+zeta^{-1})=K=Q(sqrt(5)). Consequently the geometric group and the arithmetic group over K(t) both have order 10 and are the dihedral group D_5 acting by i -> +/-i+b. Here D_5 means order 10.

For a smooth odd-degree genus-two hyperelliptic curve, the divisor classes D_i=[(r_i,0)-infinity] generate J[2], with the sole F_2-linear relation sum_i D_i=0. This follows, for example, from div(x-r_i)=2(r_i,0)-2 infinity and div(y)=sum_i(r_i,0)-5 infinity, together with the usual even-subset description of hyperelliptic two-torsion. The root permutation action on this four-dimensional quotient is faithful: D_i are distinct and a permutation fixing the quotient fixes each D_i. Thus the preceding splitting fields are exactly the two-division fields.

The five-cycle has no fixed vectors on F_2^5/<(1,1,1,1,1)>: invariance in the quotient implies, after summing an orbit of length five, invariance of a representative, hence it is constant. In particular J[2](Q(t))=0. The rational Weierstrass section infinity does not supply a rational nonzero two-torsion point or a full level-two basis.

The discriminant is 5^5(t^2-4)^2: f'=5(x^4-3x^2+1), whose four simple critical points have critical values +2 and -2 twice each. Hence the characteristic-zero family is smooth exactly at t != +/-2. Over Z[1/10] its smooth locus excludes t^2-4=0.

Outcome: an exact generic level-two obstruction, with arithmetic F_20 and geometric D_5. Since O_K/2=F_4, the geometric RM-allowed group SL_2(F_4) has order 60; our geometric image has index six. This does not compute higher powers of two or all integral Tate images. No novelty is claimed for this elementary reconstruction.


## Approach 2: residual rigidity and the exceptional prime three

The attempted strategy is to recover the residual image from two unipotent branch generators, retaining the central sign discarded by projectivization.

Use K=Q(sqrt(5)), O=Z[w], w=(sqrt(5)-1)/2, so w^2+w-1=0. The change of parameter s=-t/2 identifies our family with C_1(s) in Darmon--Mestre, not with their different sextic family C_2(s). Their Proposition 2.1 gives the O-action over K and its Galois descent law. Their Proposition 3.1(1) gives, for every prime ideal lambda over a rational prime ell different from 2 and 5, geometric projective image PSL_2(O/lambda), except at ell=3, where it is A_5 inside PSL_2(F_9). These are credited prior results from 2000.

Here is the elementary passage back to linear images. In odd characteristic, a subgroup H of SL_2(k) whose projective image contains an involution contains -I: a lift h of a nontrivial projective involution satisfies h^2=-I. Indeed h^2=I would make h diagonalizable with eigenvalues in {1,-1}; determinant one would then force h=+/-I. Thus H is the entire inverse image of its projective image. At three this has order 120, index six in SL_2(F_9), and is the binary-icosahedral inverse image of A_5. At every other prime ideal covered by the proposition the linear image is SL_2(O/lambda).

For an independent finite control, consider U=[[1,1],[0,1]], V=[[1,0],[-(2+w),1]]. Their product has trace -w and (UV)^5=-I. Exhaustive closure over O/2 and O/3 gives orders 10 and 120 respectively. The latter has element-order counts 1:1, 2:1, 3:20, 4:30, 5:24, 6:20, 10:24. The submitted program verifies every determinant and the product relation. Its finite calculations support, but do not replace, the cited geometric theorem or an integral-lattice identification.

Outcome: ell=3 is a second established exceptional geometric level. Treating every odd unramified prime as maximal would be false. No equality for the complete 3-adic image follows merely from this residual calculation. No rational specialization is substituted for the generic geometric representation.

## Approach 3: split-prime independence and integral lifting

Attempt: upgrade the credited prime-ideal images to the entire geometric ell-adic image, avoiding the invalid inference that two surjective projections must have product image.

Let ell>=7 be prime and R=O tensor Z_ell. Here are the integral-module and polarization details. The rational Betti module is two-dimensional over K (Darmon--Mestre, Section 3, printed p. 308). Betti--etale comparison, compatible with the O-action, therefore identifies V_ell=T_ell(J_t) tensor Q_ell with a free rank-two module over K tensor Q_ell. Since ell is unramified in K, R is a product of discrete valuation rings. Each factor of T_ell is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the corresponding discrete valuation ring, of rank two after passage to its fraction field. Consequently T_ell is free of rank two over R. This argument does not assume that an arbitrary displayed two-generator matrix group has already been identified with the integral Jacobian lattice.

The RM correspondence is Rosati-self-adjoint for the canonical principal polarization: the transpose of the correspondence induced by a fifth root zeta is the one induced by zeta inverse, and these coincide under the quotient involution used in its construction. Thus the perfect alternating Weil form E is R-balanced: E(av,w)=E(v,aw). In an R-basis e_1,e_2, set phi(c)=E(e_1,c e_2). Balancedness and alternation give E(ae_i,be_i)=0 and hence E(v,w)=phi(det(v,w)). The trace pairing of the finite unramified algebra R over Z_ell is perfect, so a unique beta in R satisfies phi(c)=Tr_{R/Z_ell}(beta c). Perfectness of E makes beta a unit in every factor. Therefore E(v,w)=Tr(beta det(v,w)), and an R-linear map preserves E precisely when its R-determinant is one. The geometric image H is consequently a closed subgroup of SL_2(R). All statements are relative to the specified RM action and polarization, up to an R-basis choice.

If ell is inert in K, Turn 2 already gives the full reduction SL_2(F_{ell^2}). If ell splits, the two reductions each equal SL_2(F_ell). To prove joint surjectivity, use Goursat's lemma and the following standard finite-group facts, with ell>=5: the only proper normal subgroups of SL_2(F_ell) are {1} and its scalar center; PSL_2(F_ell) is simple; every automorphism of PSL_2(F_ell) is induced by PGL_2(F_ell). The last statement has no field-automorphism factor because the field is prime.

A proper subdirect product would therefore identify the two projective representations through a PGL_2-conjugacy. Indeed Goursat gives isomorphic nontrivial quotients of the two SL_2 factors. Their normal-subgroup classification leaves either an SL_2 graph or a PSL_2 graph, and both induce a graph after projectivization. There is no additional abelian common quotient, since SL_2(F_ell) is perfect. Such a projective identification preserves trace(g)^2/det(g).

One common inertia element at infinity rules out both graph cases. Crucially, Darmon--Mestre's Section 3, printed p. 308, first fixes the identification of O on the rational Betti module and gives this single characteristic-zero monodromy transformation K-trace -w. Its two prime-ideal reductions are therefore reductions of the same transformation, not independently chosen inertia generators. The two embedded traces are -w and -w', with w+w'=-1 and w-w'=sqrt(5). Hence w^2-(w')^2=-sqrt(5), nonzero modulo ell!=5. This contradicts equality of the projective trace invariants. Reduction modulo ell is the full product.

For completeness the required lifting lemma has an elementary proof. Let R be a finite product of unramified extensions of Z_p, p>=5, and let H be closed in SL_2(R), surjecting modulo p. Write K_n for the kernel of reduction modulo p^n. The map I+p^n X -> X modulo p identifies K_n/K_{n+1} with sl_2(R/pR), additively. All traceless residue matrices occur, by lifting three entries and solving the determinant-one equation for the fourth.

Choose a residue element equal to I+E_12 in just one factor and to I elsewhere, and take any lift h in H. In the selected factor write h=u+pA, with u=I+E_12. Expansion modulo p^2 gives

h^p = u^p + p sum_{j=0}^{p-1} u^j A u^{p-1-j} (mod p^2).

In the correction sum reduced modulo p, u^j=I+jE_12. Every coefficient is a polynomial of degree at most two in j. For p>=5 the sums of 1,j,j^2 over F_p are all zero, so the correction vanishes. Since u^p=I+pE_12, the selected factor of h^p is I+pE_12 modulo p^2. In any other factor h=I+pA, so h^p=I modulo p^2. Thus H intersect K_1 has a first-layer element supported in exactly the chosen factor. This is where full joint residual surjectivity, rather than separate projections, is required.

That first-layer image is an F_p-subspace invariant under the full residue group. In a factor k, conjugating E_12 by diag(a,a^-1), a nonzero, gives a^2 E_12. Every c in k is a difference of two squares, namely ((c+1)/2)^2-((c-1)/2)^2; a zero square simply contributes zero. Hence the span contains k E_12. A Weyl conjugation gives k E_21. Conjugation by [[1,0],[1,1]] then supplies every scalar multiple of diag(1,-1). Therefore it contains all sl_2(k). Since the initial elements have separate factor supports, the first layer is the full direct product.

For n>=1, (I+p^n X)^p=I+p^{n+1}X modulo p^{n+2}. This gives every later layer by induction from the preceding full layer. Starting from the full reduction modulo p, fullness of each kernel layer then gives surjectivity modulo every p^n. Finally compactness and closedness imply H=SL_2(R). The arbitrary-lift step is not valid at p=3: modulo 9, h=[[1,1],[3,4]] has determinant one and reduces to I+E_12, but h^3=[[1,6],[0,1]], rather than I+3E_12. No conclusion at 3 is obtained from this lemma.

Therefore, for the generic geometric representation, for every prime ell>=7,

Im(G_{Qbar(t)} on T_ell J_t) = SL_2(O tensor Z_ell).

The proof uses the credited Darmon--Mestre residual theorem and its infinity inertia trace, the displayed elementary lifting argument, Goursat's lemma, and the stated standard finite-group facts. It does not use finite numerical evidence as an all-primes proof. It does not address 2,3,5, simultaneous independence across different rational primes, or equality after specializing t to a rational number.

Outcome: exact generic geometric local Tate images at every ell>=7. This is a reconstruction from established inputs, with no historical novelty claim.


## Approach 4: recover arithmetic images from determinants and RM descent

Attempt: determine how much the arithmetic generic image adds to the geometric answer, rather than incorrectly treating all RM endomorphisms as Q-rational.

For a prime ell>=7, let T=T_ell(J_t), R=O tensor Z_ell, and E be the Weil pairing after a choice of compatible roots of unity. Define the two concrete groups

G_ell={g in GL_R(T): det_R(g) belongs to the diagonally embedded Z_ell^*},

N_ell={g in GSp_Z_ell(T,E): g R g^{-1}=R}.

The centralizer of R in GSp(T,E) is exactly G_ell. Indeed write E(v,w)=Tr_{R/Z_ell}(beta det(v,w)) as in Turn 3. An R-linear g has E(gv,gw)=Tr(beta det_R(g) det(v,w)); nondegeneracy of the trace pairing shows that this equals m E(v,w) for a scalar m precisely when det_R(g)=m. Thus its symplectic multiplier is its R-determinant.

The O-action is defined over K and Galois conjugation on it is the nontrivial automorphism of K/Q (Darmon--Mestre, Proposition 2.1). Hence the generic arithmetic image over K(t) lies in G_ell, and the image over Q(t) lies in N_ell. The latter normalizer has centralizer G_ell as the kernel of its action on R and quotient of order at most two, since Aut_{Z_ell}(R) has order two.

Over K(t), the determinant is the cyclotomic character chi_ell. Its image is all Z_ell^*. To see this, K ramifies at 5, whereas every subextension of Q(mu_{ell^infinity}) is unramified at 5 for ell !=5. Their intersection is therefore Q. Restricting chi_ell to G_K is surjective, and the constants quotient of G_{K(t)} surjects onto G_K. The geometric image already equals SL_2(R) by Turn 3. A subgroup containing the determinant kernel and surjecting onto the determinant quotient is the whole group. Consequently

Im(G_{K(t)} on T)=G_ell.

The image over Q(t) contains that group and an element conjugating w to w'. Thus its normalizer quotient has order two, and

Im(G_{Q(t)} on T)=N_ell.

These equalities are basis-independent statements relative to the specified RM action and principal polarization, valid for all ell>=7. In the split case G_ell is the set of pairs (A,B) in GL_2(Z_ell)^2 having the same determinant. In the inert case it is GL_2(O_{K,ell}) with determinant in Z_ell^*. Passing to Q permits the nontrivial semilinear component. The ambient unrestricted GL_4 or GSp_4 is not the expected RM image.

Outcome: exact generic arithmetic local Tate images away from 2,3,5, reconstructed from the prior residual theorem plus the preceding lifting proof. This says nothing yet about the full product over primes; a full local projection at every good prime would not on its own rule out adelic entanglement. It also does not assert these equalities for each rational fiber.


## Approach 5: the moduli parameter and the specialization obstruction

Attempt: turn the generic calculations into an answer about fibers over Q by identifying the moduli parameter and testing a smooth exceptional member.

Takei's 2014 thesis, Proposition 4.1.1, computes the Igusa--Clebsch invariants of this same quintic family after the parameter change t=2-4s. In his normalization they become

A=350, B=2500, C=295000-11250t^2, D=3125(t^2-4)^2.

This also agrees with the discriminant in Turn 1. These credited formulas yield a short exact moduli argument. For two fibers with weighted-projectively equal invariants, the nonzero constant A forces the weight-two scaling factor to be one. The weight-six invariant therefore agrees, and t'^2=t^2. Conversely C_t and C_{-t} are isomorphic over Q(i), by (x,y)->(-x,iy). Thus the coarse geometric moduli map is generically two-to-one, and its image has rational parameter t^2. It is a nonconstant curve inside the RM surface of discriminant five. This dimensional statement alone is not a claim that it is a Shimura subvariety or a complete description of every moduli-theoretic level structure.

The smooth fiber t=0 proves that generic images cannot be assigned uniformly to all rational parameters. Its defining polynomial is x(x^4-5x^2+5). Put alpha=sqrt((5+sqrt(5))/2) and beta=sqrt((5-sqrt(5))/2), choosing their real positive values. Since alpha beta=sqrt(5) and sqrt(5)=2alpha^2-5, every root 0,+/-alpha,+/-beta belongs to Q(alpha). The polynomial x^4-5x^2+5 is Eisenstein at 5, so this field has degree four and is the splitting field. The automorphism alpha->beta sends sqrt(5) to -sqrt(5), and hence beta to -alpha. It has order four. Therefore the root group, and by faithful action on hyperelliptic two-torsion the group Im(G_Q on J_0[2]), is C_4. This is strictly smaller than the generic arithmetic F_20. In this fiber [(0,0)-infinity] is a rational nonzero two-torsion point, in contrast to the generic fiber.

Outcome: the all-fibers shortcut fails for a proved reason. The generic local conclusions in Turns 1--4 remain valid, but do not compute any unspecified fiber's complete Tate image. Exact integral images at 2,3,5, cross-prime adelic compatibility, and a parameter-dependent classification of arithmetic fiber images remain unproved in this packet. The broad original problem is therefore unresolved here after five substantive mathematical approaches.
