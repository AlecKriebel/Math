## 1. Original target and source fidelity

The original target is Schröer's joint report with Geiß and Leclerc, Oberwolfach Report 12/2017, printed pages 599–603, Conjecture 4.2 on printed page 602. The ambient algebra in that conjecture is the subalgebra generated by characteristic functions of the free rank-one vertex modules E_i. When the vertex nilpotence length exceeds one, E_i is not an ordinary one-dimensional simple module.

The proof correctly preserves all of the following:

- complex ground field and Euler-characteristic convolution;
- ordinary dimension vector d = D r, versus local-free rank vector r;
- ordinary base-change group G(d), versus H_i-linear automorphism groups in the fixed-loop model;
- E-filtered modules, rather than every locally free module;
- a strict support-dimension inequality;
- membership in the generated convolution subalgebra.

The original displayed OWR inequality omits the word "dim" before its support term; surrounding text, the preceding dimension bound, and GLS IV Conjecture 1.4 confirm the dimensional interpretation used here.

GLS IV version 2 has 50 PDF pages and the version stamp 25 June 2018. Its Section 7.2 theorem is conditional; the author's proof does not assume that conditional conclusion. Section 8.2 uses only the minimal B2 symmetrizer. The extension to every m is therefore something that needs an argument and is actually supplied. Sections 8.3 and 8.4 are also accounted for: the G2 family obstructs an unrestricted dense-orbit extrapolation, while the nonminimal A2 nonmaximal component is compatible with the claimed support ideal.

## 2. General reduction: valid and noncircular

### Generic-value kernel

The upper dimension bound in GLS IV Theorems 1.1/4.1 transfers between the fixed-loop and ordinary-coordinate models as described in Section 2.4. A constructible function has a unique generic value on each irreducible component. A constructible subset of an irreducible component has full dimension exactly when it is dense, and a dense constructible subset contains a dense open subset.

Consequently J_r is exactly the kernel of the generic-value map on the components of dimension T_D(r). Components of smaller dimension need not be absent. A function supported entirely on them can be nonzero, but lies in that kernel. This handles the source's non-equidimensional examples rather than assuming them away.

### Serre-ideal dimension squeeze

The unconditional GLS inputs are the maximal-component count, dim U(n)_r = b_r, and the generic-value surjectivity from Lemma 7.1/Theorem 7.3. The quotient A/I receives a graded surjection from U(n) by the Serre presentation. Once I is shown to vanish generically, the resulting surjection (A/I)_r -> C^{b_r} gives

    b_r <= dim(A/I)_r <= dim U(n)_r = b_r.

Each graded piece is finite dimensional because only finitely many words have a prescribed rank. Thus ker rho_r = I_r. This uses no part of conditional Theorem 7.4 as an input. It also handles rank zero and dimension vectors outside D times the nonnegative integer lattice: the former has zero kernel; in the latter the generated homogeneous piece vanishes.

### Hopf direct-sum argument

The primitive generator coproduct is an unconditional GLS input. Iterated commutators of primitives are primitive, so the two-sided ideal generated by the Serre commutators is a Hopf ideal. Evaluation of the coproduct on M,N equals evaluation of the original function on M direct-sum N.

This identity is justified on words by torus localization on E-flags. Scaling one summand fixes precisely flags split between the summands. A successive quotient is E_i, which is indecomposable; if it splits between the summands, exactly one summand receives it. The resulting shuffle is the primitive-generator coproduct. The relevant free-flag loci are constructible/quasiprojective rather than necessarily complete, but Euler-characteristic localization for an algebraic torus action still applies. Compactly supported Euler characteristic has the same numerical value in this complex-algebraic setting.

The proof does not incorrectly treat evaluation at a module as an algebra character. It evaluates the tensor factors of a coproduct, and at least one factor belongs to I. Thus vanishing of the whole ideal on finitely many detector modules propagates to their arbitrary direct sums. Vanishing only of the individual Serre generators on arbitrary modules would not suffice.

## 3. The all-m rigidity lift

### Kernel-length inequality

For K = ker L_m, the filtration used is K intersect z^j R_m^a, not z^j K. Taking the leading coefficient defines an injective map of the j-th associated quotient into ker L_1. The coefficient of z^j in L_m(v) must vanish, including at j=m-1. Summing the m inequalities gives

    dim_C ker L_m <= m dim_C ker L_1.

This argument does not suppose that every vector of ker L_1 lifts. The independent negative control L_m = multiplication by z has kernel dimension 1 for m=4, whereas m times its reduced kernel dimension is 4. Thus equality is false in general, and the proof uses only the valid direction.

### Combining the bound with Hom–Ext

GLS IV Lemma 2.1 applies to locally free modules, with no missing non-Dynkin hypothesis. The rank vector stays fixed under the displayed lifts; the symmetrized form scales by m. For a rigid reduction M_1, the length bound and nonnegativity of Ext give

    0 <= Ext^1(M_m,M_m)
      = 2 End(M_m) - m(r,r)_{D_0}
      <= m[2 End(M_1) - (r,r)_{D_0}] = 0.

Hence the claimed rigidity and End-dimension equality follow. No arbitrary lifting existence theorem is asserted.

### Hom systems really reduce correctly

In B2, z is represented by epsilon_1 squared at vertex 1 and epsilon_2 at vertex 2. It is a common central action compatible with both arrows. Every module homomorphism is consequently R_m-linear. Once this linearity is imposed, its remaining equations are exactly commutation with t and the two arrows; the vertex-2 loop equation is automatic. Reducing these R_m equations modulo z gives the entire minimal-symmetrizer Hom system, with no lost or additional equations.

This was independently corroborated more strongly than by finite sampling: all 64 polynomial Hom systems for the eight detectors were reconstructed entry by entry over Q[z]. Every nonzero Smith invariant factor is 1. Thus these particular Hom spaces in fact have m times their m=1 dimensions for every m. Independently constructed ordinary complex-coordinate systems agree in 85 selected calculations, including all 64 pairs at m=1, all eight endomorphism systems at m=2 and m=4, and additional ordered pairs at m=4. These checks do not import the author's matrix-system implementation.

## 4. A2: all ranks and all positive symmetrizers

For D=mI, the presentation is the ordinary A2 preprojective algebra tensored with C[t]/(t^m). The four modules E1,E2,P_A,P_B are free constant lifts and are E-filtered. For p>=q, a=p-q, b=q-c, the End dimension of the stated sum is

    m(a^2+b^2+c^2+2bc+ac+ab) = m(p^2+q^2-pq).

The p<q case follows by swapping vertices. The q+1 or min(p,q)+1 sums have different reductions, visible from the rank of A. Their orbits attain the threshold. The PBW count is exactly min(p,q)+1, so the unconditional GLS count exhausts all maximal components. The two Serre degrees exceed the coordinatewise ranks of each of the four detectors; the entire Serre ideal therefore vanishes there. The direct-sum lemma proves I=J in every rank.

### Free-flag and Euler checks

For the generic evaluation formula when p>=q, the intermediate free summand lies between the constant free summands Im A and Ker B. Its choice reduces to the free R-Grassmannian of rank p-a-c in R^{p-q}. Reduction modulo t has ordinary Grassmannian base and affine fibres of dimension

    (m-1)(p-a-c)((p-q)-(p-a-c)).

Its Euler characteristic is therefore binomial(p-q,p-a-c), independently of m. Invalid indices give the empty locus. Reversing the columns gives a triangular matrix with diagonal 1. The author's torus explanation is sound as well: a graded free direct summand with free quotient is generated by its degree-zero part. Freeness of the quotient is crucial to that assertion.

### Nonzero Serre witness

The claimed witness has A(1)=t^{m-1}u and B(v)=t^{m-1}, with B(u)=0. AB=BA=0 for m>=2. The stated E-filtration has free subquotients. A contributing free rank-one summand for the middle word has reduction Cu, so it is uniquely

    R(u+w v),  w in tR.

Every such summand contains A(M_2) and is killed by B. The locus is affine (m-1)-space and has Euler characteristic 1. The two outer words have empty flag loci because A and B are nonzero, yielding Serre value -2.

The three residue constraints on End are independent, giving dimension 5m-3. Independent ordinary-coordinate calculations at m=2,3,5,7 confirm this formula. The ordinary orbit dimension and threshold are respectively 5m^2-5m+3 and 5m^2-3m, with positive deficit 2m-3. At m=2 this gives 13 versus 14, matching the visually inspected GLS IV pages 48–49. No conclusion that this single orbit is the whole support at larger m is needed or made.

## 5. B2: matrices, allowed components, and whole-ideal detection

### Generalized-preprojective relations

From the actual P1–P3 source definitions, C=[[2,-1],[-2,2]] and D=diag(2m,m) give the relations

    t^(2m)=0, z^m=0, t^2 A=A z, z B=B t^2,
    A B t+t A B=0, B A=0.

In the chosen R_m coordinates, t=[[0,z],[1,0]] on each H1 generator. The eight modules satisfy these equations over Q[z], before truncation. For P1, the minus sign in A(b2)=-tv makes the two terms cancel on u and tu. Replacing it by a plus sign fails the exact polynomial relation and is rejected by both implementations. For P2, both AB and BA vanish directly.

The given filtrations for P1 and P2 are stable and have free vertexwise subquotients. The one-arrow modules also have the obvious free vertex-first filtrations. Thus all counted objects lie in nil_E, not merely in the larger locally free variety. This distinction matters: the source's minimal-B2 band modules are locally free but not E-filtered.

### Base modules and compatibility

The source's eight diagrams, six basic maximal rigid modules, and projective modules were visually compared with the displayed matrices. The labels agree up to harmless signs and basis choices. Independently calculated Ext dimensions recover exactly the cycle

    E1 -- T1 -- T3 -- E2 -- T4 -- T2 -- E1,

and both P1 and P2 have zero Ext with all eight detectors. The source's rigidity statement therefore applies to arbitrary multiplicities supported on one cycle edge, with unrestricted projective multiplicities. The all-m lift applies to each such complete direct sum.

Distinct multiplicity vectors remain nonisomorphic after lifting, because reduction yields distinct Krull–Schmidt decompositions. An individual lifted indecomposable cannot split: a decomposition would reduce to a decomposition of its indecomposable reduction, and a summand with zero reduction would be zero by Nakayama. Direct summands of the vertexwise free modules stay free over the local vertex rings.

### Counting and exhaustion

The six nonprojective weights are x,xy,xy^2,y,xy^2,xy. Empty support, singleton support, and one-edge support are disjoint cases; strictly positive multiplicities are used only on the chosen support. This avoids counting a singleton several times through its adjacent edges. The unrestricted projective factors have weights x^2y^2 and xy^2.

The rational identity in the proof is correct. The independent check clears denominators and verifies a zero polynomial in Q[x,y], rather than trusting bounded series agreement. A separate multiplicity enumeration through both rank coordinates 12 agrees with the Kostant-partition counts. Omitting an edge makes the cleared polynomial nonzero and fails the check.

Every resulting orbit has dimension T_D(r). In the E-filtered/locally free ambient convention, its closure is a maximal component. Distinct such orbits cannot share an irreducible closure: each orbit is locally closed and dense, hence contains an open dense subset of its closure. The GLS count gives exactly the same number as the constructed orbits, so no additional maximal component, including a possible family component, can remain. Nonmaximal components are permitted throughout.

This is an all-rank rational-identity argument plus an all-m rigidity argument. It is not a generalization from finitely many tested ranks or symmetrizers.

### Vanishing of the entire ideal on the eight detectors

The two Serre degrees are (2,1) and (1,3). Seven detectors lie below both. At P1, of degree (2,2), only s12*theta2 and theta2*s12 can occur. The homogeneous degree-(0,1) piece is spanned by theta2; no hidden multiplier of another degree is possible in the nonnegative grading.

For s12*theta2, the necessary rank-(2,1) submodule would have its entire vertex-1 space and a proper rank-one vertex-2 space. Surjectivity of B makes this impossible. For theta2*s12, a nonzero E2 submodule would lie in ker A, which is zero. The relevant minors of A and B are units over R_m, so this remains true for every m, including the nonminimal cases. The direct-sum Hopf argument then gives vanishing on every maximal component. The reduction in Section 2 gives I=J.

## 6. Independent Euler-cancellation reconstruction

This audit also reconstructs the source's nonempty-but-zero-Euler-characteristic example, rather than substituting emptiness of all larger flag loci.

Take m=1, H=C[t]/(t^2), and M=P1 direct-sum E1. Use P1 basis u,v and add w for E1. Thus M1=Hu direct-sum Hv direct-sum Hw, M2=Cb1 direct-sum Cb2, with B(u)=b1, B(tu)=b2, A(b1)=v, A(b2)=-tv.

A submodule U with quotient E1 has U2=M2. Its U1 contains Hv=Im A and is of the form Hv direct-sum L, where L is a free direct-summand H-line in Hu direct-sum Hw.

- If the u coefficient of a generator of L is a unit, normalize L=H(u+c w). Then U is isomorphic to P1, and (s12*theta2)(U)=0.
- Otherwise the w coefficient is a unit. Normalize L=H(w+beta*t*u), beta in C. If beta=0, B vanishes on U, and s12 vanishes on every relevant intermediate submodule: the outer-word contribution 2 cancels twice the middle-word Euler characteristic 1.
- If beta is nonzero, B(U1)=Cb2. There is a unique intermediate submodule V of rank (2,1) with E2 quotient, namely V2=Cb2 and V1=U1. On V, A(b2)=-tv and B(w+beta*t*u)=beta*b2. The outer word loci are empty. The free H-lines for the middle word are H(v+c*t*w), c in C, so its Euler characteristic is 1 and s12(V)=-2.

The nonzero integration locus is therefore exactly beta in C*. Its Euler characteristic is 0. Thus (s12*theta2*theta1)(P1 direct-sum E1)=0 although its relevant incidence locus is nonempty. This agrees with the source's Section 8.2.4 and directly validates the cancellation boundary used in the author's explanation.

No finite-field point count was used to infer this complex Euler characteristic.

## 7. Independent full-algebra negative control

Let X1 have M1=H, M2=C, A(1)=t, B=0 at minimal B2. Its End dimension is 2, so its orbit has dimension 3, below the rank-(1,1) threshold 4. Let T4 have M1=H, M2=C^2 and A an isomorphism of complex vector spaces. Its End dimension is 2, so its orbit dimension is 6, equal to the rank-(1,2) threshold 6.

At T4, a submodule isomorphic to X1 with quotient E2 is determined by the unique line A^{-1}(Ct) in M2. Therefore

    (1_X1 * 1_E2)(T4)=1.

This proves failure of the support-ideal assertion in the full invariant constructible-function algebra. It is also independently clear why this is not a generated-algebra counterexample: in rank (1,1), the generated algebra is spanned by theta1*theta2 and theta2*theta1. Both X1 and T2 have their values (1,0), whereas 1_X1 distinguishes X1 from T2. Thus 1_X1 is outside the generated algebra.

The independently computed End dimensions and the unique-line incidence confirm this negative control. The accepted theorem never admits this function as a detector or as an ideal element.

## 8. A1 and disconnected blocks

For A1, every E-filtered module is H^r because extensions by the free module H split. Free complete flags reduce to ordinary complete flags with affine fibres, so their Euler characteristic is r!. Thus theta^r/r! is the unique orbit's characteristic function, the generated homogeneous piece is one dimensional, and J=0.

For disconnected blocks, each module splits canonically and its submodules split blockwise. The generated algebra is the tensor product of the block algebras. Dimension thresholds add, and a product component attains the total threshold exactly when each factor attains its own threshold. Generic-value maps tensor, and over C the kernel of a tensor product of surjections is the sum of tensors containing at least one factor kernel. Cross-block Serre relations are commutators, already zero. This proves precisely the stated block-sum extension with independent positive symmetrizers.

## 9. Findings, limits, and reproducibility

### Blocking findings

None found for the stated theorem. No correction patch is required for acceptance in its declared scope.

### Nonblocking precision

The phrase "orbit closure is a maximal component" should be read with the source's E-filtered/locally free relative-closure convention. An ordinary affine representation-variety closure can contain non-locally-free boundary modules. The generic-value argument and component-count reasoning already operate in the required E-filtered setting, so this wording point does not create a proof gap. The publication proof now states this relative-closure convention explicitly in its Conventions section. This is the only mathematical-expository clarification to the accepted argument.
