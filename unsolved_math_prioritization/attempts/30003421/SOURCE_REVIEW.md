# Source and scope audit

## Original question

The exact contribution is Jan Schröer's joint-work report with Christof Geiß and Bernard Leclerc, “Quivers with relations for generalized Cartan matrices: Crystal graphs,” Oberwolfach Report 12/2017, printed pp. 599–603. Its Conjecture 4.2 is on printed p. 602 (physical PDF p. 12). The contribution and the displayed conjecture were inspected, including a rendered page check.

The relevant algebra is the subalgebra generated by 1_(E_i). The E_i are rank-one free vertex modules over truncated polynomial rings; they need not be ordinary one-dimensional simple modules. The support threshold is the strict inequality

    dim supp(f) < dim G(d) - q_D^C(d/D).

The current proof preserves the ground field C, E-filtered module varieties, dimension/rank conversion d=Dr, strict inequality, and the generated-algebra restriction.

The source text was read through the definitions, dimension and crystal arguments, and full Sections 6–8, with targeted visual checks of the B2 diagrams and original OWR conjecture. In particular:

- Sections 2.2–2.5 distinguish ordinary dimension-vector varieties from the fixed-loop rank-vector version and give the Hopf structure.
- Lemma 2.1 supplies the Hom–Ext identity.
- Theorem 4.1 supplies the upper dimension bound; Proposition 4.4 identifies maximal components with generically crystal components.
- Theorem 5.4 and Theorem 7.2 count maximal components independently of the symmetrizer.
- Corollary 6.5 concerns individual Serre generators. It is not already a proof for the ideal they generate.
- Lemma 7.1 / Theorem 7.3 give unconditional surjectivity of generic-value evaluation.
- Theorem 7.4 is conditional. Its conclusion is not used circularly in the scoped proof; the finite-dimensional argument needed after I is contained in J is reproduced.
- Section 8.2.2 supplies the eight minimal-B2 indecomposable rigid modules and six compatible rigid sets. These source results are credited, not asserted as new discoveries.
- Section 8.2.4 is essential: it displays a C-star cancellation and also an actual failure for the full constructible-function algebra. Its offending characteristic function does not belong to the generated algebra.
- Section 8.3 exhibits a maximal G2 component without a dense orbit, preventing an unqualified generalization of the rigid-orbit method.
- Section 8.4 exhibits a nonmaximal component for A2 with D=2I; this does not conflict with the scoped theorem, which concerns vanishing on maximal components only.

## Relationship of the present argument to the sources

The present A2 proof uses four elementary modules and the GLS maximal-component count. The B2 proof starts from the source's minimal-symmetrizer rigid examples but gives explicit lifts for every m. Its kernel-length argument proves that their rigid sums stay rigid. An exact rational generating-function identity provides the number of such sums in every rank and allows the unconditional GLS count to exhaust all maximal components. A rank check and injective/surjective arrow argument then annihilate the whole Serre ideal on the eight building blocks; its Hopf-ideal structure handles all their direct sums.

No result is asserted for arbitrary C,D, no outside-subalgebra function is used to refute the target, and no Bozec loop-quiver model is substituted. The classical symmetric/minimal theorem remains credited to Lusztig. There is no worldwide novelty or current-openness certification. A bounded web check for the exact GLS support terminology did not produce a replacement proof of the general conjecture; that negative search is not used as mathematical evidence.
