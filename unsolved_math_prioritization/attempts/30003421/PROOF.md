# The generated support ideal in types A1, A2 and B2

## Status and exact scope

Public proof-only edition, 10 October 2026. The accompanying [mathematical audit](MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md) accepts the scoped partial proof. This AI-assisted manuscript and audit are unrefereed; acceptance does not mean external human peer review, journal acceptance or formal proof-assistant certification.

This is a scoped mathematical result for OWR-15217-001 (dataset ID 30003421), not a resolution of its arbitrary-Cartan-matrix conjecture. No claim of novelty is made. The geometric and Hopf-algebra inputs are due to Geiß–Leclerc–Schröer (GLS), and the minimal-B2 rigid modules used below are their explicit examples. The additional arguments here are the rigidity-preserving lift, the all-symmetrizer component exhaustion, and the consequent ideal argument.

**Scoped theorem.** Over the complex numbers, the support subspace in the question is a two-sided ideal in the convolution algebra generated by the rank-one free vertex modules if every connected Cartan block has type A1, A2 or B2, with any positive integral symmetrizer on each block. In particular, this includes

- A2 with C = [[2,-1],[-1,2]] and D = m I, for every integer m >= 1;
- B2 with C = [[2,-1],[-2,2]] and D = diag(2m,m), for every integer m >= 1;
- the vertex-relabelled form of B2.

For these scopes the low-support subspace equals the Serre ideal. Nonminimal symmetrizers are genuinely included; the ideal need not be zero and the E-filtered varieties need not be equidimensional. The theorem does not cover G2, general higher rank, or arbitrary symmetric Cartan matrices with nonminimal symmetrizers.

### Conventions

Write A = M-tilde(Pi(C,D)), generated by theta_i = 1_(E_i), where E_i is free of rank one over H_i = C[epsilon_i]/(epsilon_i^(c_i)). Products are ordered as in GLS: the first factor is evaluated on a submodule and the second on its quotient. A is rank graded; rank r corresponds to dimension d = D r. All supports below are inside nil_E(Pi,d), the E-filtered constructible variety. Every orbit closure and maximal component below is taken relative to this E-filtered, locally free ambient space, as in GLS. We do not take the closure in the full affine representation variety, whose boundary may contain non-locally-free modules.

Set

    T_D(r) = dim G(D r) - q_D^C(r),
    J_r = {f in A_r : dim supp(f) < T_D(r)},
    J = direct sum_r J_r.

The zero function is included. The group in this formula is the product of the ordinary complex general linear groups of dimensions d_i. It is not the product of the H_i-linear automorphism groups. Passing to GLS's fixed-loop/species model subtracts the same orbit-of-loop contribution from both sides; in that model the threshold is dim H(r). We retain the ordinary dimension-vector convention throughout the conclusion.

Let

    s_ij = (ad theta_i)^(1-c_ij)(theta_j),
    I = the two-sided ideal generated by all s_ij, i != j.

## 1. The GLS inputs and the exact reduction

The following are unconditional inputs from GLS IV, arXiv:1702.07570v2.

1. Every E-filtered component has dimension at most T_D(r), and the maximal components have exactly that dimension (Theorem 1.1 / 4.1 and Section 2.4).
2. The number b_r of maximal components is dim U(n(C))_r, independently of D (Theorem 5.4 and Theorem 7.2).
3. The generic-value map

       rho_r : A_r -> C^(b_r)

   is surjective (Lemma 7.1 / Theorem 7.3).
4. A is a cocommutative Hopf algebra with primitive generators theta_i. Its coproduct is evaluation on direct sums:

       (Delta f)(M,N) = f(M direct-sum N).

   See Section 2.5 and the end of Section 7.1. The direct-sum identity can also be checked on words: scale one summand by a one-dimensional torus, localize the Euler characteristic of E-flags to fixed flags, and distribute the successive quotients between the summands.
5. For locally free modules M,N,

       dim Ext^1(M,N) = dim Hom(M,N) + dim Hom(N,M)
                       - (rank M, rank N)_D.

   This is Lemma 2.1, citing GLS I.

By (1) and constructibility, J_r = ker rho_r. Indeed a constructible subset of an irreducible component has its full dimension exactly when it is dense, and a dense constructible subset contains an open dense subset. Components of smaller dimension cannot affect the threshold. No equidimensionality assumption is made.

**Lemma 1 (Serre-ideal reduction).** If I_r is contained in J_r for every r, then I = J, so J is a two-sided ideal.

**Proof.** The generators of A/I satisfy the Serre relations, hence there is a graded surjection U(n(C)) -> A/I. If I is contained in J, rho_r factors to a surjection (A/I)_r -> C^(b_r). Therefore

    b_r <= dim (A/I)_r <= dim U(n(C))_r = b_r.

Both maps have equal finite dimensions, and ker rho_r = I_r. This also recovers GLS's conditional construction in Section 7.2 once its support premise has been established in a particular scope. In particular it does not assume that construction in order to prove the premise. QED.

Each s_ij is primitive, being an iterated commutator of primitive elements. Consequently I is a Hopf ideal:

    Delta(I) is contained in I tensor A + A tensor I.

The corresponding containment holds for any iterated coproduct.

**Lemma 2 (direct-sum detection).** Suppose there is a family T of E-filtered modules such that:

(a) every maximal component has a dense orbit of a direct sum of modules from T;
(b) every f in I vanishes on every module in T.

Then I is contained in J and hence I = J.

**Proof.** Evaluate an iterated coproduct of f in I on a finite tuple from T. Each term has at least one factor in I, whose evaluation is zero by (b). Thus f vanishes on every direct sum in (a). Since f is isomorphism invariant it vanishes on the whole dense orbit, hence generically on every maximal component. Apply Lemma 1. QED.

This reduction is specific to the generated algebra and its Serre Hopf ideal. It is not a statement about all constructible functions.

## 2. A rigidity-preserving lift over truncated polynomial rings

The following elementary length estimate is useful because it is valid for every truncation length, not only tested lengths.

**Lemma 3 (kernel length).** Let R_m = C[z]/(z^m), and let L_m : R_m^a -> R_m^b be a matrix over R_m. Let L_1 be its reduction modulo z. Then

    dim_C ker L_m <= m dim_C ker L_1.

**Proof.** Filter K = ker L_m by K intersection z^j R_m^a. Taking the first nonzero coefficient of an element z^j v gives an injection

    (K intersection z^j R_m^a)/(K intersection z^(j+1) R_m^a)
        -> ker L_1,

for 0 <= j < m. Sum the dimensions. This does not assert that every vector in ker L_1 lifts. QED.

**Lemma 4 (rigidity under an explicit free lift).** Fix C and D_0. Suppose M_m is a locally free Pi(C,mD_0)-module that is free over a common central R_m and whose reduction is M_1. Suppose module homomorphisms are the kernel of an R_m-linear matrix system whose reduction is exactly the system defining End_(Pi(C,D_0))(M_1). If M_1 is rigid, then M_m is rigid and

    dim_C End(M_m) = m dim_C End(M_1).

**Proof.** Lemma 3 bounds End(M_m) above by m End(M_1). The GLS self-Hom–Ext formula and the scaling of the symmetrized form give

    0 <= dim Ext^1(M_m,M_m)
       = 2 dim End(M_m) - m (r,r)_(D_0)
       <= m [2 dim End(M_1) - (r,r)_(D_0)] = 0.

All inequalities are equalities. QED.

The same reasoning applies to an explicitly lifted direct sum, provided its reduction is rigid. It is not a general assertion that arbitrary modules admit free lifts. In the next two sections the lifts are displayed.

A rigid locally free E-filtered module of rank r has End dimension q_D^C(r), so its ordinary G(D r)-orbit has dimension T_D(r). By the GLS upper bound its orbit closure is a maximal component. Distinct such orbits cannot have the same closure: each is open dense in its closure, and two disjoint open dense orbits cannot occur in an irreducible variety.

## 3. Type A2, every symmetrizer

Put R = C[t]/(t^m). For A2 and D=m I, the generalized algebra is the usual A2 preprojective algebra tensored with R. Thus a locally free module is given by R-free vertex modules M_1,M_2 and R-linear maps

    A : M_2 -> M_1,    B : M_1 -> M_2,    AB = BA = 0.

Use the four modules E1, E2, P_A, P_B, where P_A and P_B both have rank (1,1), and their nonzero map is respectively A=1 and B=1. All four have evident E-filtrations.

Fix r=(p,q), k=min(p,q). For c=0,...,k define

    N_c = P_A^c direct-sum P_B^(k-c)
          direct-sum E1^(p-k) direct-sum E2^(q-k).

Only one of the last two exponents can be positive. These are lifts of ordinary rigid A2 modules. More directly, their endomorphism space is the tensor product over C of the ordinary endomorphism space with R, and a direct calculation gives

    dim_C End(N_c) = m (p^2 + q^2 - p q) = q_D^C(r).

For example, if p>=q, writing a=p-q, b=q-c, the ordinary dimension is

    a^2 + c^2 + b^2 + 2bc + ac + ab
      = p^2 + q^2 - p q.

Thus their orbits are maximal, and they are distinct (their reductions have different ranks for A). There are k+1 of them. The positive roots of A2 are (1,0),(0,1),(1,1), so PBW gives dim U(n)_r=k+1. The GLS component count proves that these are all maximal components, for every m.

The two Serre generators have ranks (2,1) and (1,2). No nonzero homogeneous element of the ideal they generate can be evaluated nontrivially on any of E1,E2,P_A,P_B: each of those four modules has coordinatewise rank at most (1,1), and the algebra is nonnegatively graded. Lemma 2 gives I=J.

### A2 evaluation and nontriviality checks

There is an explicit generic-evaluation certificate. If p>=q and 0<=a,c<=q, set

    h_a = theta_1^(p-a)/(p-a)! * theta_2^q/q! * theta_1^a/a!.

On the ordinary module N_c, a contributing intermediate subspace lies between Im A and Ker B. The relevant Grassmannian has Euler characteristic

    h_a(N_c) = binomial(p-q, p-a-c),

where a binomial coefficient outside its usual range is zero. In the reversed column order c=q-b this matrix is triangular with diagonal 1. For R-free constant lifts the same formula follows by the torus t -> lambda t: a fixed free direct-summand flag is exactly the scalar extension of an ordinary flag, and nonfixed orbits have Euler characteristic zero. The case p<q is obtained by interchanging the vertices. This is a check of generic-function detection, not a replacement for the all-rank proof above.

The ideal is nonzero when m>=2. On M_1=R^2 with basis u,v and M_2=R, take

    A(1)=t^(m-1) u,    B(u)=0,    B(v)=t^(m-1).

These maps satisfy AB=BA=0, and the filtration R u, then R u plus M_2, then M gives an E-filtration. The words theta_1^2 theta_2 and theta_2 theta_1^2 vanish because B and A respectively are nonzero. The flag variety for theta_1 theta_2 theta_1 consists precisely of

    U = R(u + z v),    z in tR.

Indeed BU=0 forces the reduction of U to be C u; conversely these U contain A(M_2). This is affine (m-1)-space, so the Serre function has value -2. An endomorphism is a pair (F,g) in M_2(R) plus R with exactly the three residue constraints

    F_21(0)=0,    F_11(0)=g(0),    F_22(0)=g(0).

Thus End has dimension 5m-3. At rank (2,1), dimension (2m,m), the threshold and orbit dimension are respectively

    5m^2 - 3m,    5m^2 - 5m + 3,

with strictly positive deficit 2m-3. For m=2 these are 14 and 13, matching the nonmaximal component in GLS Section 8.4. We do not claim this orbit is the entire support for general m.

## 4. Type B2, every symmetrizer

Here

    C = [[2,-1],[-2,2]],   D_m = diag(2m,m),   R_m=C[z]/(z^m).

Write t=epsilon_1 and epsilon_2=z. For the modules below, t^2=z and the two arrows are R_m-linear. Thus M_1 is free over C[t]/(t^(2m)), M_2 is free over R_m, and the preprojective equations reduce to

    A B t + t A B = 0,    B A = 0,

where A:M_2->M_1 and B:M_1->M_2. The arrow-loop commutation relations follow from t^2=z. On each H_1-free generator u we use the R_m-basis (u,tu), so t has matrix

    [[0,z],[1,0]].

### 4.1 Eight explicit lifts

The following matrices are over R_m, and actually are defined over C[z]. Zero maps not mentioned are zero.

- E1: M_1=H_1 u, M_2=0.
- E2: M_1=0, M_2=R_m b.
- T1: M_1=H_1 u, M_2=R_m b; B(u)=0, B(tu)=b, A=0.
- T2: M_1=H_1 u, M_2=R_m b; A(b)=u, B=0.
- T3: M_1=H_1 u, M_2=R_m b_1 plus R_m b_2; B(u)=b_1, B(tu)=b_2, A=0.
- T4: the same spaces as T3; A(b_1)=u, A(b_2)=tu, B=0.
- P2: the same spaces as T3; A(b_1)=u, A(b_2)=0, B(u)=0, B(tu)=b_2.
- P1: M_1=H_1 u plus H_1 v, M_2=R_m b_1 plus R_m b_2;

      B(u)=b_1, B(tu)=b_2, B(v)=B(tv)=0,
      A(b_1)=v, A(b_2)=-tv.

Their rank vectors are respectively

    (1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (1,1), (1,2), (1,2), (1,2), (2,2).

The sign in P1 matters. On the first H_1 block, AB sends u to v and tu to -tv. Consequently ABt+tAB is zero on both u and tu, including the equation on tu where t^2=z; it is zero on the second block as well. Also BA=0. For P2 both AB and BA are zero. The remaining cases are immediate. These are identities over C[z], valid for all m.

E-filtrations are explicit. When A=0, put all E2 factors first; when B=0, put all E1 factors first. For P2 take R_m b_2, then R_m b_2 plus H_1 u, then P2. For P1 take H_1 v, then H_1 v plus M_2, then P1, refining the middle quotient E2^2 into two E2 factors. All displayed submodules and quotients are vertexwise free.

At m=1 these are the eight modules in GLS Section 8.2.2, with harmless basis/sign choices. The source gives the following six rigid collections (each also includes P1 and P2):

    {E1,T1}, {E1,T2}, {E2,T3}, {E2,T4}, {T1,T3}, {T2,T4}.

All finite direct sums whose nonprojective support is inside one of those pairs are rigid at m=1. This input is credited to GLS; the accompanying audit records independent exact matrix/Hom–Ext checks as supplementary historical verification.

### 4.2 All these rigid sums lift, and exhaust the maximal components

Every homomorphism between our displayed modules is R_m-linear, because z acts as t^2 at vertex 1 and as epsilon_2 at vertex 2. In R_m coordinates it is a pair of matrices F1,F2 satisfying

    F1 t_M = t_N F1,
    F1 A_M = A_N F2,    F2 B_M = B_N F1.

These are an R_m-linear matrix system, and reduction modulo z is exactly the homomorphism system for the minimal-symmetrizer modules. Therefore Lemma 4 proves that the lift of every rigid sum just listed is rigid for every m.

For clarity, the commutation equation at vertex 2 is automatic in R_m coordinates. In ordinary complex coordinates it is F2 epsilon_2 = epsilon_2 F2, as also imposed in the recorded supplementary checks.

Reduction also proves that distinct sums stay nonisomorphic. Each base module is indecomposable and the eight are mutually nonisomorphic; Krull–Schmidt distinguishes their multiplicities. If two lifts were isomorphic, their reductions would be isomorphic. Similarly a decomposition of an individual lift would reduce to a decomposition of its base module; a nonzero summand cannot have zero reduction by Nakayama's lemma.

Here is an all-rank counting certificate. Let x,y record rank coordinates. The six nonprojective vertices lie on the cycle

    E1 — T1 — T3 — E2 — T4 — T2 — E1,

with weights x,xy,xy^2,y,xy^2,xy respectively. If u_v is a weight and h_v=u_v/(1-u_v), the generating function for their allowed multiplicity vectors is

    F = [1 + sum_v h_v + sum_cycle-edges h_v h_w]
        / [(1-x^2 y^2)(1-x y^2)].

The denominator counts the unrestricted P1 and P2 multiplicities. This counts every allowed multiplicity vector once: its nonprojective support is empty, one vertex, or one edge. Direct rational simplification gives the identity

    F = 1 / [(1-x)(1-y)(1-xy)(1-xy^2)].

The right side is the PBW series for B2, whose four positive roots are (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2). Thus the lifted rigid sums give exactly dim U(n(B2))_r distinct maximal orbits at every rank r. The GLS component-count theorem now shows that every maximal component for D_m is the closure of one of these orbits. This conclusion is derived for all m and all ranks; it is not extrapolated from a finite enumeration.

### 4.3 The entire Serre ideal vanishes on the eight detectors

The Serre generator ranks are

    rank s_12=(2,1),    rank s_21=(1,3).

Every displayed module except P1 has first rank at most 1 and second rank at most 2. By nonnegative grading, no element of I can be nonzero on any of these seven modules.

P1 has rank (2,2). The only possible contribution to I_(2,2) is a linear combination of

    s_12 * theta_2,    theta_2 * s_12.

Indeed the second Serre generator already has second rank 3, and the residual degree after s_12 is (0,1), whose generated-algebra piece is C theta_2.

For s_12 * theta_2, a contributing submodule U would have rank (2,1) and P1/U=E2. Hence U_1=M_1 and U_2 is an R_m-free rank-one submodule of M_2. But B:M_1->M_2 is surjective, so B(U_1) cannot be contained in U_2. No such submodule exists.

For theta_2 * s_12, a contributing submodule E2 would be a nonzero rank-one R_m-submodule of M_2 killed by A. But A is injective: its matrix on the v,tv rows is diag(1,-1). Again no such submodule exists.

Thus every element of I vanishes on P1 as well. By the component exhaustion in 4.2 and Lemma 2, I=J for every D_m.

This reasoning also explains why Euler-characteristic cancellation in GLS's larger B2 example is compatible with the result. The Hopf coproduct detects the vanishing on direct sums without asserting that the relevant submodule varieties are empty there; GLS's C-star stratum has Euler characteristic zero.

## 5. Type A1 and block sums

For A1 with D=(m), the only E-filtered module of rank r is H^r. Its orbit has dimension (mr)^2-mr^2, exactly the threshold. Its divided-power characteristic function is theta^r/r!, since the Euler characteristic of the free complete flag variety is r!. Therefore the generated homogeneous piece is one-dimensional and J=0.

For a disconnected Cartan matrix, module categories and their E-filtered varieties split over the connected blocks. The generated algebra is the tensor product of the blockwise generated algebras; products between different blocks are the characteristic functions of direct sums and commute. Dimension thresholds add, and maximal components are products of maximal components. The generic-value map on a fixed multirank is the tensor product of the blockwise surjective generic-value maps. Over C its kernel is the sum of the tensor factors with one blockwise kernel. Consequently a tensor product of the blockwise support-ideal statements proves the theorem for the declared A1/A2/B2 block sums.

## 6. What remains unresolved

No proof or counterexample is supplied for arbitrary symmetrizable C,D. Specifically, the argument would need replacements for both of the following steps outside its stated scope:

1. a family of modules on which the whole Serre ideal can be proved to vanish;
2. proof that their direct sums meet a dense open subset of every maximal component.

It is not enough that the individual Serre generators vanish on generic crystal modules (GLS Corollary 6.5), since multiplying them introduces Euler integration over submodule varieties. Nor is it enough that the bad incidence locus is nonempty or small before projection; a lower-dimensional support can project onto a maximal orbit. The B2 full-algebra example in GLS Section 8.2.4 explicitly shows this danger and its offending characteristic function is outside the generated subalgebra.

The dense-rigid-orbit exhaustion cannot be presumed in general: GLS Section 8.3 exhibits a maximal G2 component containing a dense one-parameter family of orbits and no dense orbit. Therefore the present proof mechanism is genuinely scoped. It does not transport to G2, and it does not establish a uniform support-dimension inequality for all constructible functions.
