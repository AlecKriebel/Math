## Primary target

The complete 436-page author manuscript of *K3: A New Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topology* was available from the [Berkeley author-hosted source](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf), and its contact-topology introduction, Problem 3.42 and surrounding remarks were read. The definitions on printed p.160 and the exact problem on p.162 were rendered and visually checked. A workshop-summary PDF was not used as a substitute.

The section explicitly begins with closed 3-manifolds. It ordinarily assumes coorientability and gives the torsion model with n full rotations on T²×[0,1]. The supremum runs over actual contact embeddings, without restricting them in advance to a single isotopy class. The problem concerns a fixed contact structure, not a bound uniform over all tight structures on the underlying smooth manifold.

The imported landing page was attempted first but was unavailable through the web tool. The full pinned dataset statement agrees with the primary question. Its background's August 2026 triage only records known partial results; no keyed prior research report was present.

## Primary literature checked

1. Vincent Colin, [*Sur la torsion des structures de contact tendues*](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_2001_4_34_2_267_0.pdf), Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. 34 (2001), 267–286. The complete PDF was retrieved. Definitions 1.1–1.3, Theorems 1.4 and 1.7, and the standard rotating torus structures on p.270 were checked. Finite torsion in normal classes has irreducibility and universal-tightness hypotheses. Finiteness of the number of relevant isotopy classes does not by itself bound the torsion in each one.
2. David T. Gay, [*Four-dimensional symplectic cobordisms containing three-handles*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0606402), Geom. Topol. 10 (2006), 1749–1759. The retrieved version is the published version. Corollary 2.2 on p.1751 and its strong-fillability hypothesis in the proof were checked, along with the discussion of arbitrarily large torsion across examples. The artifact does not import strong fillability from tightness.
3. Daniel Bennequin, [*Entrelacements et équations de Pfaff*](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1983__107-108__87_0.pdf), Astérisque 107–108 (1983), 87–161. The full primary PDF was retrieved for attribution; its complete proof was not independently reconstructed. The standard contact-space tightness theorem is used as a classical input, also explicitly recorded by Colin on p.270.
4. Current related work was checked through primary abstracts and source statements, including [Shah–Simone, *Tight contact structures on toroidal plumbed 3-manifolds*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.topol.2025.109602), published 2026, and [Etnyre, *Neighborhoods of transverse knots and destabilizations*](https://etnyre.math.gatech.edu/preprints/nbhds.html), preprint 2025. Their stated results concern adding torsion in particular families or infinitely many distinct torsion-free structures, respectively. Those statements are not a resolution of the fixed-manifold finiteness problem.

This was a bounded search, not proof that no later or unindexed resolution exists. Deep classification and filling theorems were not reproved here. All elementary lemmas asserted in PARTIAL.md have their own written proofs.
