# Five substantive approaches

The budget counts five mathematical approaches, not web searches, tool calls, or local verification runs. Every approach below produced a complete scoped result and an explicit gap. Source recovery and prior-attempt checks are preparatory work.

## 1 Structural and finite quotient reduction

Attempt: exploit characteristic simplicity together with finite automorphism-orbit count to force residual finiteness or triviality.

Completed: the finite residual is characteristic, so failure of residual finiteness means every finite image is trivial. Any candidate is infinite, finite-exponent, perfect, centerless, without proper finite-index subgroups, and has finite width for every nonidentity verbal subgroup. Soluble and globally nilpotent cases are excluded. In the p-power-exponent regime, a pseudofinite candidate is excluded by one first-order sentence.

Failure point: perfect locally finite groups of bounded exponent are known to exist. Finite exponent and local nilpotence do not imply global nilpotence or solubility. Finite element orbits do not, by anything proved here, supply the missing bound or all-tuple orbit finiteness.

## 2 Countable reflection

Attempt: reduce arbitrary-cardinality examples to manageable countable unions without silently losing non-residual-finiteness.

Completed: Theorem 2.2 constructs a countable subgroup preserving characteristic simplicity, an upper bound on the number of element orbits, and absence of all nontrivial finite images. The proof includes a compactness-based finite witness for each element and each finite target group.

Failure point: this reflects an already existing example. It does not construct the first example. An arbitrary countable subgroup would be inadequate; all three witness families in the construction matter.

## 3 Boolean and direct-power constructions

Attempt: use the known characteristic simplicity and large automorphism groups of function groups, and compare their atomic analogues.

Completed: a full proof that pointed Cantor Boolean A5 functions have exactly eight orbits and are characteristically simple. Residual finiteness is witnessed by evaluation. The atomic restricted direct sum has infinitely many orbits detected by centralizer index, even though its spectrum is the same finite set of eight orders.

Failure point: every subgroup of a Cartesian product of finite groups remains residually finite. Merely changing the Boolean partition geometry cannot remove that evaluation obstruction. A quotient would need a separately justified finite-orbit and characteristic-simplicity analysis; no such quotient was produced.

## 4 Dense triangular construction

Attempt: use McLain's dense-order construction to guarantee perfectness and characteristic simplicity and eliminate all finite quotients.

Completed: a self-contained matrix proof of those properties, including extraction of a transvection from an arbitrary nontrivial characteristic subgroup. Regular unipotents give exact p-power orders, proving infinitely many automorphism orbits.

Failure point: long dense chains force unbounded exponent. A finite truncation restores finite exponent but introduces finite-group and characteristic-center obstructions. No bounded-exponent modification preserving the needed automorphisms was obtained.

## 5 Extraspecial and homogeneous bounded-class constructions

Attempt: start with the known few-orbit phenomenon for symplectic central extensions, then try to remove the characteristic center through a homogeneous extension construction.

Completed: the infinite extraspecial exponent-p group has three automorphism orbits and finite residual precisely its order-p center. Its quotient by that center is elementary abelian and residually finite. An explicit finite amalgam shows that unrestricted class-two embeddings cannot be freely amalgamated in class two.

Failure point: the characteristic center cannot be ignored, and quotienting it out destroys the desired non-residual-finiteness. Compatible central-series expansions and higher-class constructions require additional arguments. A bounded global nilpotency class is already excluded by Approach 1. The finite amalgam obstruction does not rule out larger or unbounded class.
