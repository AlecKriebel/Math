# Limits and rejected shortcuts

- The original existence question is unresolved. The five approaches are scoped partial work, not five proofs of the target.
- A countable subgroup can gain finite quotients and lose orbit symmetry. The countable-reflection theorem avoids that by construction; the conclusion does not hold for arbitrary subgroups.
- Finitely many automorphism orbits on elements is not the stated all-tuples hypothesis of omega-categoricity. No unproved promotion between the two is used.
- Local finiteness is not pseudofiniteness. The first-order obstruction is expressly confined to pseudofinite perfect p-power-exponent groups with finite orbit count.
- Finite exponent does not by itself force a locally finite group to be residually finite, soluble, or nilpotent. In particular, the primary archive records perfect locally nilpotent groups of bounded prime exponent.
- The Boolean-function eight-orbit group is residually finite. It is a control on the known side, not an answer to the requested case.
- The McLain group is characteristically simple, not asserted to be simple. Its unbounded element orders prevent it from being a requested example.
- The extraspecial group has a proper characteristic center. Its nontrivial finite residual is precisely that center, not the whole group.
- The class-two amalgam obstruction does not exclude higher-class or unrestricted locally finite amalgams.
- No general reduction of all candidates to p-groups is claimed. The general finite-exponent characteristically-simple classification was not verified in its original source.
- Apps's 1983 original article was identified bibliographically, but the attempted publisher retrieval returned a short unavailable-site HTML page. No original Apps full-text inspection is claimed.
- Published-source assertions are distinguished from independent proofs and primary manuscript status. The Dantas–de Sousa arXiv item is treated as a preprint; no unverified peer-review status is assigned.
- Exact finite controls are proof checks on explicit finite models. They cannot certify the existence, nonexistence, cardinality, characteristic simplicity, or finite residual of an arbitrary infinite group by themselves.
- Search coverage does not establish historical novelty or exclude later or unindexed solutions. No novelty or priority claim is made.
