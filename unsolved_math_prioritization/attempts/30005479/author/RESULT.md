# Verification of a prior affirmative resolution

## Result and attribution

The selected claim is true for every finite loopless matroid, including non-realizable matroids. The exact argument was already publicly presented by Alper Ferudun, *The Amoeba Dimension of an Arbitrary Loopless Matroid*, 30 September 2026, Theorem 1.2. Its author describes the work as an unrefereed, AI-assisted preprint. This investigation independently checks the short deduction against its published mathematical inputs and provides independently written exact tests. No new theorem or priority is claimed.

Public source: https://eulersolve.org/papers/owr-12697711-015/ . The author's metadata lists DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23058144; the DOI archive was not independently accessible in this run, so its bytes were not compared. The downloaded author-site PDF matches its author's advertised SHA-256.

## Exact conventions

Let E be a finite nonempty ground set, M a loopless matroid on E, r its rank function, and d=r(E). Use the full Bergman-fan support F in R^E, including the all-ones line. In the minimum-on-circuits convention, its fine cones are generated by indicators of a maximal chain of flats, with nonnegative coefficients on proper flats and an unrestricted coefficient on E. Simultaneous sign reversal of all cones leaves the dimensions below unchanged.

Define

    A(M) = min over rational subspaces U of [2 dim(U+F) - dim U],
    p(M) = min over partitions P of E of sum over B in P [2 r(B)-1].

Every block is nonempty. The sum U+F is the Minkowski sum, not an intersection or a tropical sum operation. Dimension is real polyhedral/topological dimension, the maximum dimension of the cones, not the dimension of the linear span of the entire support. A rational subspace is a real subspace with a rational basis. The claim is A(M)=p(M).

## Published inputs checked

1. Draisma, Eggleston, Pendavingh, Rau and Yuen, arXiv:2303.13143v3, Theorem 1.3.2, proves for **all loopless matroids** that the partition minimum on each restriction is the rank function p(S) of a matroid M'. This combinatorial theorem does not assume complex realizability. The same paper's Theorem 1.3.1 concerns complex linear-space amoebas and is a different statement.
2. Bernstein, arXiv:2003.10529v4, Theorem 3.5 and Lemmas 3.3–3.4, applies to **all loopless matroids of finite rank**, without representability. With both matroids equal to M, every I for which |J| <= 2r(J)-1 for nonempty J subset I satisfies dim pi_I(F+F)=|I|. His fan has the opposite sign; dimensions are unaffected.

Both theorem statements and their surrounding proofs were read from the original preprints; Bernstein's Theorem 3.5 was also inspected in a rendered PDF page. His linear-space Hadamard-product theorem is an application of this more general matroid theorem, not its hypothesis.

## Complete deduction

Write C_a for the finitely many maximal cones of F and L_a for their linear spans. Put D=dim(F+F). Since a sum of two convex cones has the sum of their linear spans,

    D = max over a,b dim(L_a+L_b),
    dim(F+U) = max over a dim(L_a+U).

For any two vector subspaces L_a,L_b and any real U, the Grassmann identity gives

    dim(L_a+U) + dim(L_b+U)
      = dim(L_a+L_b+U) + dim((L_a+U) intersect (L_b+U))
      >= dim(L_a+L_b) + dim U.

Consequently D <= 2dim(F+U)-dim U for every real U.

Choose a basis I of M', using published input 1. Then |I|=p(E), and any nonempty J subset I has

    |J| = p(J) <= 2r(J)-1,

where the last inequality uses the one-block partition of J. Published input 2 gives dim pi_I(F+F)=|I|. A linear projection cannot increase dimension, hence D>=p(E).

For the reverse bound, choose any partition P={B_1,...,B_k}, and let U_P be the span of the block-indicator vectors. These have disjoint nonempty supports, so dim U_P=k and U_P is rational. Project the span of a maximal flag cone, together with U_P, onto R^{B_i}. The projected generators are indicators of the intersections of that flag's flats with B_i, together with 1_{B_i}. Intersecting a flat with B_i gives a flat of the restriction M|B_i. After deleting empty and repeated intersections, there are at most r(B_i) members in this chain. Thus the i-th coordinate projection has dimension at most r(B_i). The joint space embeds into the direct sum of these projections, so

    dim(F+U_P) <= sum_i r(B_i),
    2dim(F+U_P)-dim U_P <= sum_i (2r(B_i)-1).

Take P attaining the finite partition minimum. Combining the bounds yields

    p(E) <= D <= 2dim(F+U_P)-dim U_P <= p(E).

All are equal. Since D is a lower bound for every real U and U_P is rational, both the real-subspace and rational-subspace minima equal p(E). Every optimal partition supplies a minimizing subspace. This proves the full selected claim, conditional only on the two published theorems whose precise hypotheses were checked above.

## A source-level pitfall checked independently

An equality for **every** partition, stated in DEPRY Section 1.4, is stronger than the upper bound actually needed and can fail. Ferudun explicitly records this issue. For M=U_{2,4} and P={{1,2},{3,4}}, the block cost is 6. The full rank-two fan has maximal spans span(1_E,e_i); adding the two-dimensional block space yields dimension 3, so the objective is 2*3-2=4. The partition minimum is 3, achieved by the one-block partition. There is no contradiction to the theorem: equality with the block cost is guaranteed at optimal partitions by the sandwich argument, not at arbitrary ones.

The example is separately reproduced in the exact test script. No unqualified per-partition identity is used in this packet.

## Hypotheses that must not be conflated

- Tropical dimension of F is d. Amoeba dimension can be larger and is p(E) in the linear-space setting; the theorem does not assert dim F=p(E).
- For a complex linear subspace V not contained in a coordinate hyperplane, the coordinate-projection matroid of V is loopless and F is its tropicalization. The real dimension of Log(V intersect (C*)^E) equals p(E). Non-realizable matroids need no fictitious complex variety for the combinatorial theorem proved above.
- The algebraic matroid of a general nonlinear variety does not by itself determine its amoeba dimension. A subtorus and a linear space can share a uniform coordinate matroid and have different amoeba dimensions. The linear-space hypothesis cannot be dropped in that interpretation.
- In the general irreducible-variety formula, a rational U corresponds to a subtorus and the relevant algebraic quantity is the dimension of the closure of its orbit saturation. It is not just the stabilizer dimension of the original variety. No such algebraic interpretation is needed for arbitrary M here.
- Quotienting out the all-ones line changes dimensions and constants. This proof uses the unquotiented fan throughout.
- Loops are excluded: the finite-coordinate Bergman support and the nonnegative singleton partition cost fail in the intended way when loops are present. For E empty, declaring both minima to be zero extends the equality, but the source problem assumes [n] with positive n.
- The neighboring arbitrary-fan algorithm problem 30005478 is not resolved by this result. No general matroid-free equality A(F)=dim(F+F) is claimed.

## Adversarial checks and limits

`verify_dimensions.py` was written separately from definitions without reading or running third-party source code. It validates rank axioms, enumerates maximal-flat-chain span partitions, computes pair dimensions by bipartite incidence rank, and independently cross-checks sampled ranks with rational Gaussian elimination. It computes partition minima by subset dynamic programming.

All 59 tested instances pass: all 27 labeled loopless matroids on four elements, every U_{r,n} for 1<=r<=n<=7, Fano, dual Fano, Vamos, and a connected free extension of Fano plus two coloops. The last has n=10, rank 5, and common value 8, strictly below min(n,2d-1)=9, so the checks are not confined to trivial optimal partitions. This matroid contains the Fano matroid as a restriction and is not complex-realizable.

The run evaluates 3,717 partitions, 130 rational-subspace samples, and 590 incidence-rank/Gaussian-rank comparisons. Finite experiments cannot exhaust arbitrary matroids or real subspaces; the displayed deduction supplies those quantifiers. External peer review is not asserted. A fresh separate review of this frozen packet remains the publication gate.
