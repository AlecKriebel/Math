# Matroid base weights: exact reductions and proved partial cases

Problem 3064 / OPG-369. Prose edition prepared 2026-10-11.

This AI-assisted manuscript is unrefereed. Acceptance refers to an internal AI mathematical audit, not external human peer review, journal acceptance, or formal proof-assistant certification. This is not a computational reproduction package. No novelty or exhaustive current-openness claim is made.

## Disposition

The full conjecture is **unresolved in this investigation**. No counterexample or complete proof is claimed. The results below are elementary reductions and special-case proofs, with no claim of novelty. Computational checks are bounded evidence only.

The exact target is as follows. Let M be a finite matroid on E, with rank function r, and let w:E→G map to an arbitrary additive abelian group. Write R=r(E), S={sum_{e∈B}w(e): B a basis of M}, and H={h∈G:S+h=S}. The conjectured inequality is

    |S| ≥ |H| (1 − R + Σ_{Q∈G/H} r(w^{-1}(Q))).                 (SS)

Empty fibers have rank zero. Loops and parallel elements are allowed; R=0 is allowed. Neither finiteness nor cyclicity of G is assumed. Since the ground set is finite, S is finite and nonempty.

## 1. Exact stabilizer reduction

H is a subgroup: 0 stabilizes S; translations preserving S are closed under addition and inverse. Fix s0∈S. The map h↦s0+h embeds H into S, so H is finite. S is a disjoint union of H-cosets.

Let π:G→G/H and T=π(S), and give M the labels v=π∘w. Then |S|=|H||T| and T is exactly the set of v-weights of bases. Moreover stab(T)={0}. Indeed, S=π^{-1}(T), so a lift g of any stabilizer of T satisfies g+S=S and hence g∈H. Consequently (SS) is equivalent, instance by instance, to

    |T| ≥ 1 − R + Σ_{q∈G/H} r(v^{-1}(q)), with stab(T)={0}.    (A)

There is no argument here that an arbitrary intermediate sumset in a proof is aperiodic. Section 6 gives an explicit obstruction to that inference.

Deleting loops changes neither S nor any fiber rank. Deleting a coloop changes S by a translation, lowers R by one, and lowers the rank of exactly its weight fiber by one. Thus these operations preserve the truth and the numerical deficit in (SS). Adding a constant c to every label translates S by Rc and permutes its H-coset fibers; (SS) is invariant. These facts justify loop/coloop removal and the normalized finite searches below.

## 2. The arbitrary-group problem reduces exactly to finite groups

Every individual instance can be transferred to a finite abelian group with the same two sides in (SS).

Proof. Let A be the subgroup generated by w(E), a finitely generated abelian group. Since H⊆S−S⊆A, replacing G by A changes nothing. Let W=w(E), and choose a finite set

    F = (S+S−S) ∪ (W+H) ∪ H ∪ {0}.

By the structure theorem write A≅Z^d⊕K, K finite. Choose a positive integer N larger than the range of every integer coordinate attained in F (N=1 is harmless if d=0). Reduction modulo N on the free coordinates, and the identity on K, give a homomorphism q:A→(Z/NZ)^d⊕K that is injective on F. This is immediate: a coordinate difference with absolute value smaller than N cannot be a nonzero multiple of N.

We claim stab(q(S))=q(H). The inclusion q(H)⊆stab(q(S)) is automatic. Conversely, if k stabilizes q(S), choose s1∈S with q(s0)+k=q(s1), and put d0=s1−s0. For every s∈S, q(s+d0) lies in q(S). Both s+d0 and every possible matching element of S lie in S+S−S. Injectivity on F therefore gives s+d0∈S. Translation preserves cardinality, so S+d0=S, whence d0∈H and k=q(d0). Thus the claim holds. Injectivity on S (a subset of S+S−S) and on H gives |q(S)|=|S| and |q(H)|=|H|.

Finally, for a,b∈W, q(a) and q(b) lie in the same q(H)-coset exactly when q(a)=q(b+h) for some h∈H. As a,b+h∈W+H, this is equivalent to a=b+h. Hence the partitions of E into stabilizer-coset fibers are identical before and after q. Their ranks, and both sides of (SS), are identical. ∎

This is a reduction, not a finite algorithm deciding the conjecture: the size of the finite group depends on the instance. In particular it does not reduce arbitrary finite abelian groups to cyclic groups or to the known prime-power cases.

## 3. Torsion-free quotient case

**Theorem.** If G/H is torsion-free, then (SS) holds.

It suffices by Section 1 to prove, for a torsion-free abelian group L and any finite matroid,

    |w(M)| ≥ 1 − r(M) + Σ_{g∈L} r(w^{-1}(g)).                (TF)

Only the finitely generated subgroup spanned by the labels is needed. It is free abelian, so lexicographic order after an identification with Z^d is a translation-invariant total order. If A,B are finite nonempty subsets of an ordered abelian group, listing A={a1<…<am} and B={b1<…<bn} gives the strictly increasing chain

    a1+b1 < a2+b1 < … < am+b1 < am+b2 < … < am+bn.

Thus |A+B|≥|A|+|B|−1. Also a nonempty finite set has no nonzero stabilizer in an ordered group (translate its minimum).

We prove (TF) by induction on rank. At rank zero all elements are loops and the sole basis is empty, so both sides are 1. At positive rank choose a nonloop x. For each g write F_g=w^{-1}(g), set

    A={g:x∈cl_M(F_g)},    N=M/x,    B=w(N).

A is nonempty because w(x)∈A. B is nonempty because every matroid has a basis. If D is a basis of N, then D∪{x} is a basis of M, so x∉cl_M(D). For any a∈A, F_a cannot be contained in cl_M(D), since otherwise x∈cl_M(F_a)⊆cl_M(D). Choose y∈F_a outside cl_M(D); D∪{y} is a basis of M. Therefore A+B⊆w(M).

For every g, the contraction rank formula, including when x∈F_g, gives

    r_N(F_g\{x}) = r_M(F_g∪{x})−1
                  = r_M(F_g)−1_{g∈A}.

The induction hypothesis on N now yields

    |B| ≥ 1−(R−1)+Σ_g r_N(F_g\{x})
        = 2−R+Σ_g r_M(F_g)−|A|.

Using the ordered sumset bound,

    |w(M)| ≥ |A+B| ≥ |A|+|B|−1
             ≥ 1−R+Σ_g r_M(F_g).

This completes the induction and the theorem. ∎

This proof uses the standard contraction rank formula and the elementary finitely generated abelian-group structure theorem. Its contraction mechanism is the same mechanism present in DeVos's exposition of the prime-order Schrijver–Seymour theorem [S3]; no new general matroid theorem is being asserted.

## 4. At most two nonloop label classes after quotient: exact equality

**Theorem.** If at most two H-cosets contain labels of nonloops, then (SS) holds with equality.

Proof. Delete loops and pass to L=G/H as in Section 1. With zero or one label class every basis has the same weight, |T|=1, and the right side of (A) is 1. Suppose instead that labels take exactly two distinct values a,b on a partition E=X⊔Y. Put R=r(E), l=R−r(Y), u=r(X), and d=a−b≠0.

The attainable numbers |B∩X|, as B ranges over bases, are precisely all integers in [l,u]. The endpoints are attained by extending a basis of Y and a basis of X, respectively. Every count lies between these endpoints. To see that no integer is skipped, connect the two endpoint bases by successive basis exchanges: whenever B≠C, choose x∈B\C and, by basis exchange, y∈C\B with B−x+y a basis. Each step decreases |B\C|, so this reaches C. An exchange changes |B∩X| by at most one. The resulting path therefore visits every integer between the endpoint counts.

It follows that

    T = Rb + {kd:l≤k≤u}.

Let m=u−l+1=r(X)+r(Y)−R+1≥1. If d has infinite order, this set has m elements. If d has finite order t and m<t, it again has m elements. If m≥t, then T is an entire coset of the nontrivial cyclic subgroup generated by d, contradicting stab(T)={0}. Thus |T|=m in all allowed cases. This is exactly equality in (A); multiplying by |H| proves the result. ∎

No assertion that an arbitrary proper arithmetic progression always has trivial stabilizer is needed for this argument; aperiodicity is inherited from the exact stabilizer quotient.

### Low rank and corank boundary cases, without label restrictions

If R=1, the bases are the singleton nonloops. In the stabilizer quotient T is precisely the set of labels on nonloops. Each of these |T| label fibers has rank one and all other fibers have rank zero. Thus the right side of (A) is |T|, proving equality. The rank-zero case was already proved above. A matroid consisting only of loops and coloops also gives equality by the deletion reductions of Section 1.

If |E|−R=1, (SS) holds as well. To prove this directly, the dual has rank one, so for a nonempty set C⊆E its bases are exactly the singletons {c}, c∈C. Consequently the bases of M are exactly E\{c}, c∈C. Pass to the stabilizer quotient, write v for its labels and z=Σ_{e∈E}v(e), and put k=|v(C)|. Then T={z−v(c):c∈C}, so |T|=k. For any F⊆E, r(F)=|F|−1 if C⊆F, and r(F)=|F| otherwise: in the latter case choose c∈C\F so that F lies in the basis E\{c}; in the former case exactly one element of C must be omitted. Summing this formula over the label fibers gives Σ_q r(v^{-1}(q))=|E|−1 if k=1 and |E| otherwise. The right side of (A) is therefore 1 if k=1 and 2 if k≥2, which is at most |T|=k. This also covers the possible singleton circuit C, which is a loop. ∎

## 5. A conditional direct-sum closure lemma

If (SS) holds for each of M1,…,Mk for every labeling into every abelian group, then it holds for their direct sum M. This may be used to combine established classes; it does not assert that general connected matroids belong to those classes.

Proof. Quotient the base-weight set of M by its actual stabilizer H. In that quotient write A_i for the base-weight set of M_i, so T=A_1+…+A_k is aperiodic. Every A_i and every partial sum is aperiodic: a period of a summand is a period of T. Apply (SS) to each M_i in this quotient. Their stabilizers are trivial, so

    |A_i| ≥ 1−r(M_i)+Σ_Q r_{M_i}(w^{-1}(Q)∩E(M_i)).

Kneser's theorem [S2, Theorem 1.2], applied successively to the aperiodic partial sums, gives |T|≥Σ_i|A_i|−k+1. Add the displayed bounds and use additivity of ranks in a direct sum to obtain (A). ∎

This section, unlike Sections 1–4 and 6, uses Kneser's theorem as an external mathematical dependency. Its published theorem is invoked, not reproved or independently proof-audited here.

## 6. A concrete obstruction to a tempting induction

Consider the rank-two matroid on {x,y,u,v} whose sole dependent pair is {x,y}. Its bases are xu,xv,yu,yv,uv. Label these elements in C6 by

    w(x)=0, w(y)=3, w(u)=1, w(v)=4.

The five base weights are respectively 1,4,4,1,5. Thus S={1,4,5}, H={0}; the bound in (SS) is 1−2+4=3 and is attained.

Contract x. The element y becomes a loop; u and v are the nonloops of the rank-one contraction. In the notation of Section 3,

    A={0,3},  B={1,4},  A+B={1,4}.

Both B and A+B have stabilizer {0,3}, whereas S is aperiodic. In particular |A+B|=2<|A|+|B|−1=3. The base uv, of weight 5, lies outside the lifted sumset and destroys its period.

This is **not** a counterexample to (SS). It pinpoints the gap in replacing the ordered-group sumset step of Section 3 by an unjustified aperiodic bound. Kneser's correct periodic bound for this intermediate sumset supplies only 2, and the omitted bases require an additional structural argument.

## 7. What remains

After the exact quotient and finite-model reductions, it remains to prove

    |w(M)| ≥ 1−r(M)+Σ_g r(w^{-1}(g))

for arbitrary finite matroids and finite abelian groups when w(M) is aperiodic. The first unresolved situations for the arguments here have torsion and at least three nonloop label classes. Established special cases do cover many such instances; the statement above is a description of the remaining general theorem, not a claim that every individual instance outside Sections 3–4 is unknown.

DeVos–Goddyn–Mohar prove the uniform-with-parallel-extensions case and the group cases stated in their Theorem 1.14 [S2]. The 2025 proximity paper repeats the precise conjecture as Conjecture 1.2 [S4]. Its new proximity results do not imply a full resolution of this cardinality bound as stated. The bounded 2026 literature searches located no complete resolution, which is not an exhaustive openness or novelty certificate.
