# A coarse volume-only bound for finite semigroup holes

**Status:** scoped partial result and source-interpretation hold, awaiting
independent review. This note does not claim the sharp reading of the
Oberwolfach problem has been solved.

**Target:** UnsolvedMath 30000819 / OWR-1595-012, Oberwolfach Report
39/2007, p. 2316, in Christian Haase's contribution *What else is known?*

## 1. Two different assertions

The original report asks whether the highest-hole height can be bounded
"by the normalized volume" and immediately relates this to Eisenbud--Goto
and Herzog's multiplicity question. The dataset paraphrases this as a
bound *in terms of* the normalized volume.

These readings must be kept separate:

- **Volume-only boundedness:** there is some function \(F(V)\), independent
  of the dimension and the chosen subset of lattice generators, bounding
  the highest-hole height
- **Sharp volume bound:** the height is at most \(V\), or an even sharper
  Eisenbud--Goto-type expression

We prove the first with an explicit quartic function by combining classical
toric regularity bounds with elementary support counting. We do not prove
or refute the second. The original wording does not specify an arbitrary
function \(F\), so this coarse theorem must not be silently substituted
for the stronger source question. Its ingredients predate the source;
no historical-priority or new-resolution claim is made.

## 2. Precise algebraic setting

Let \(A=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}\) be a finite set of distinct lattice points,
with \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\) of affine dimension \(d\). Work in the
affine lattice generated by \(A\), and let \(V\) be the normalized volume
of \(P\) there. Put
\[
 a_i=(v_i,1),\quad S=\mathbb N a_1+\cdots+\mathbb N a_n,\quad
 G=\mathbb Z a_1+\cdots+\mathbb Z a_n,
\]
\[
 \overline S=(\mathbb R_{\ge0}S)\cap G,\qquad
 H=\overline S\setminus S.
\]
The height is the final coordinate. Assume that \(H\) is finite, the
semigroup formulation of very ampleness relevant to the source. If it is
nonempty, write \(h=\max\{\deg u:u\in H\}\).

The point set is not required to contain all lattice points of \(P\).
This is essential: replacing \(A\) by \(P\cap\mathbb Z^d\) changes the
semigroup and can remove the problem.

If volume is instead measured in a larger ambient lattice, that volume is
an integer multiple of the intrinsic \(V\); the same upper bound below
still follows because its right side is increasing for integers \(V\ge2\).
If holes are defined against the ambient lattice and are finite, the
ambient and generated lattices on the affine span must already agree.

**Theorem.** If \(H\) is finite and nonempty, then \(V\ge2\) and
\[
 \boxed{\ h\le 2V^2(V-1)^2-2.\ }
\tag{2.1}
\]
If there are no holes, there is no highest-hole height to bound. One can
define it to be \(-1\) for a uniform convention. In particular, \(V=1\)
gives no holes.

The proof uses ordinary standard-graded regularity over \(\mathbb C\).
Since the hole set is combinatorial, this choice of coefficient field
does not restrict the conclusion.

## 3. Finite nonempty holes rule out a free variable

Call the configuration pyramidal at \(a_i\) if \(a_i\) is linearly
independent of the span of all the other homogenized columns. Equivalently,
every vector in the rational relation space \(\ker_{\mathbb Q} A\) has
zero \(i\)-th coordinate. This is a property of the chosen configuration,
not merely of the shape of its convex hull.

**Lemma 3.1.** If \(H\) is finite and nonempty, the configuration is
not pyramidal at any column.

**Proof.** If \(a_i\) is independent of the other columns, let \(S'\)
and \(G'\) be generated by those other columns. Uniqueness of the real
coefficient of \(a_i\) gives direct sums
\[
 S=S'\oplus\mathbb N a_i,\quad
 G=G'\oplus\mathbb Z a_i,\quad
 \overline S=\overline{S'}\oplus\mathbb N a_i.
\]
For the last identity, intersection with the group forces the nonnegative
real coefficient of \(a_i\) to be a nonnegative integer. Consequently,
\[
 H=(\overline{S'}\setminus S')\times\mathbb N.
\]
It is either empty or infinite, contradicting the hypothesis. \(\square\)

This also explains why repeatedly taking a lattice pyramid over an example
with a hole cannot produce eligible fixed-volume counterexamples: it
creates infinitely many holes and destroys very ampleness.

## 4. Bounding the number of generators by volume

Put \(r=d+1\) and \(c=n-r\), the rank of the rational relation space,
equivalently the codimension of the corresponding projective toric variety.
Two classical facts for a homogeneous configuration are used:

1. Every primitive circuit binomial has degree at most \(V\)
2. \(c\le V-1\)

For (1), see Sturmfels [1], inequality (4.4) in the proof of Lemma 4.6.
The degree of the projective toric variety is \(V\) by [1, Theorem 1.4].
For (2), triangulate \(P\) using every point of \(A\) as a vertex.
Let \(t\) be the number of full-dimensional simplices. Their dual
adjacency graph is connected: a generic path between interior points
crosses successive common facets. Order the simplices along a spanning
tree of this graph. The first has \(d+1\) vertices, and each additional
simplex shares a facet with an earlier one and adds at most one new
vertex. Hence \(n\le d+t\). Every lattice simplex has positive integer
normalized volume, so \(V\ge t\ge n-d=c+1\). This is the usual
minimum-degree/lattice-point inequality, with an elementary justification.

Here is an elementary useful consequence which removes the dimension.

**Lemma 4.1.** A nonpyramidal homogeneous configuration satisfies
\[
 n\le2Vc.
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Circuits span the rational kernel. Indeed, if a nonzero
relation is not support-minimal, subtract a multiple of a relation of
smaller support to cancel a chosen coordinate, and induct on the support
size. Choose \(c\) linearly independent circuit relations spanning the
kernel, and scale each to a primitive integer relation.

For a homogeneous primitive circuit \(u\), write \(u=u^+-u^-\).
The two nonnegative integer vectors have disjoint supports and equal
coordinate sums, both equal to the binomial degree. Hence
\[
 |\operatorname{supp}u|
 \le\|u^+\|_1+\|u^-\|_1\le2V.
\]
The union of the supports of a basis contains every coordinate on which
some kernel vector is nonzero. Nonpyramidality says this includes all
\(n\) coordinates. Summing the \(c\) support bounds proves (4.1).
\(\square\)

If \(c=0\), the columns are independent and the semigroup is free and
normal in its generated group, so there are no holes. Under the theorem's
nonempty-hole hypothesis, Lemma 3.1 applies and \(c\ge1\). Combining
(4.1) and the minimum-degree inequality gives
\[
 n\le2V(V-1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 5. Classical toric regularity bound

Let \(R=\mathbb C[S]\), and let
\(I_A\subset Q=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) be its homogeneous toric
ideal, with every \(x_i\) of degree one. Sturmfels [1, Theorem 4.5]
gives
\[
 \operatorname{reg}(I_A)\le nVc.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The theorem concerns arbitrary projective toric ideals, without requiring
projective normality or the use of every lattice point in \(P\).

For clarity about conventions and dependencies, its proof has the following
ingredients. Primitive circuit degrees are at most \(V\); primitive
Graver degrees are at most \(Vc\), by [1, Lemma 4.6]. A reduced toric
Gröbner basis is contained in the Graver basis, so an initial monomial
ideal has generating degrees at most \(Vc\). Every exponent in such a
generator is at most \(Vc\), so every least common multiple appearing in
the Taylor resolution has degree at most \(nVc\). The regularity bound
for the initial ideal, and upper semicontinuity under the Gröbner
degeneration, give (5.1). This is a citation of a classical bound, not a
new regularity estimate claimed here.

The hypotheses do not permit setting the number of generators equal to
\(\#(P\cap L)\); the argument uses the actual \(n=|A|\) throughout.

## 6. From regularity to the last hole

The normalization \(\overline R=\mathbb C[\overline S]\) is a finite
graded \(R\)-module. The quotient
\[
 C=\overline R/R
\]
has a homogeneous vector-space basis indexed by the holes. Under the
finite-hole hypothesis it is a finite-length graded module, and
\(\operatorname{end}C=h\).

Let \(\mathfrak m=R_+\). There are no nonzero zeroth local-cohomology
elements in either \(R\) or \(\overline R\), because they are domains
of positive dimension. Furthermore, \(\overline R\) is a normal affine
semigroup ring and is Cohen--Macaulay (Hochster's theorem). Its dimension
is \(r=d+1\ge2\), since a zero-dimensional polytope gives a one-generator
normal semigroup. The extension of \(\mathfrak m\) to \(\overline R\)
has radical \(\overline R_+\), and thus
\[
 H^0_{\mathfrak m}(\overline R)=H^1_{\mathfrak m}(\overline R)=0.
\]
The long exact local-cohomology sequence of
\(0\to R\to\overline R\to C\to0\) gives
\[
 C\cong H^1_{\mathfrak m}(R).
\tag{6.1}
\]
The standard local-cohomology definition of regularity consequently yields
\[
 \operatorname{reg}(R)\ge h+1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
As \(I_A\ne0\), the standard exact-sequence identity is
\(\operatorname{reg}(I_A)=\operatorname{reg}(R)+1\). It follows that
\[
 h\le\operatorname{reg}(I_A)-2
 \le nVc-2
 \le2V^2c^2-2
 \le2V^2(V-1)^2-2.
\]
This proves (2.1). \(\square\)

The subtract-two indexing is deliberate: the last hole is one less than
the eventual normality threshold, while the ideal's regularity is one
greater than the coordinate ring's regularity. For complete lattice-point
embeddings, this is consistent with the normality/regularity relationship
in Tran [3, Section 4].

## 7. A checkable family and a failed counterexample mechanism

For \(m\ge4\), take \(A_m=\{0,1,m-1,m\}\subset\mathbb Z\).
The normalized volume is \(m\). In degree \(k\), its sumset is exactly
\[
 kA_m=\bigcup_{j=0}^k
 \bigl([j(m-1),j(m-1)+k]\cap\mathbb Z\bigr).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Here \(j\) counts the summands chosen from \(\{m-1,m\}\), and the
remaining binary choices produce every integer offset between zero and
\(k\). These intervals cover \([0,km]\cap\mathbb Z\) exactly when
\(k\ge m-2\). Thus the holes are finite and the highest-hole height is
\(m-3\). This is a consistency family, not a new sharpness claim for
(2.1), and it does not violate the proposed sharper bound \(h\le V\).

Adjoining a pyramid apex preserves the normalized volume but propagates
each hole to all higher degrees. That yields an infinite-hole semigroup,
so it is an invalid attempted counterexample to the source. The verifier
includes this negative control explicitly.

## 8. Exact unresolved issue and disposition

The coarse existential volume-only assertion in the dataset's wording is
proved by the displayed combination of classical facts. The original
source's potential sharp bound \(h\le V\), and the stronger expected
Eisenbud--Goto-type bounds, are not proved or refuted here. Replacing
\(2V^2(V-1)^2-2\) by \(V\) would require a genuinely stronger estimate;
the circuit-support argument and the Taylor-resolution bound do not supply it.

The bounded literature search found classical bounds depending on degree,
codimension, and generator count, and specialized later k-normality bounds.
It did not establish historical priority for this elementary coarse
corollary, nor resolve the sharper formulation. Accordingly the proposed
queue disposition is **partial / source-scope hold**, not claimed solved.
