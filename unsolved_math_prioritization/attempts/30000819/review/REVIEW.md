## Approved theorem and source boundary

For a finite homogeneous configuration of distinct lattice points, let (S) be its degree-one generated affine semigroup, let (G=\mathbb ZS), and define holes as ((\operatorname{cone}S\cap G)\setminus S). If this set is finite and nonempty, its highest degree satisfies

\[
h\le 2V^2(V-1)^2-2,
\]

where (V\ge2) is the normalized volume in the lattice generated by the configuration. The conclusion holds for arbitrary selected generators, rather than only complete lattice-point embeddings.

I visually checked Oberwolfach Report 39/2007, p. 2316. It asks for a bound “by the normalized volume” and connects it to Eisenbud–Goto and Herzog. It does not explicitly introduce an arbitrary bounding function. The dataset's looser formulation permits one, but its interpretation cannot establish the stronger source claim. The candidate appropriately preserves this distinction. [Original report](https://oa.tib.eu/renate/bitstreams/29b4c7a6-7c9e-42dd-8780-60d49fb0675c/download)

The source's p. 2314 connects very ampleness with finite holes. For arbitrary point configurations, the same equivalence with normality of the associated projective variety is explicitly given in Beck–Delgado–Gubeladze–Michałek, Proposition 2.1. Thus the finite-hole hypothesis is appropriate for the intended normal toric setting; it must not be inferred merely from a very ample line bundle on an arbitrary nonnormal projective variety. [Point-configuration reference](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.7422)

## Step-by-step mathematical audit

**Lattices and degree.** Homogenization produces a positive semigroup with a degree-one grading, and translating the affine lattice does not change the semigroup up to graded lattice isomorphism. Sturmfels's Theorem 1.4 uses the lattice affinely generated by the configuration, exactly as the candidate does. In a larger lattice of finite index, the normalized volume is multiplied by that index, not divided by it. Monotonicity of the displayed function for integer volumes at least two preserves the inequality.

If holes instead mean missing points in the larger ambient lattice, a proper generated sublattice produces infinitely many of them. To see this, select an integral point outside the generated group and translate it sufficiently far along an interior semigroup vector. All sufficiently far translates lie in the cone and remain in the excluded coset. The candidate's finite-hole claim about ambient lattice agreement is therefore sound.

**Pyramidal columns.** Independence of one column from the others gives direct decompositions of the group and semigroup. In the normalization, the coefficient of that independent column must be an integer because the point lies in the generated group, and it must be nonnegative because it lies in the cone. A hole in the remaining factor persists after adding every nonnegative multiple of the independent column. Consequently finite, nonempty holes exclude every such column. This concerns the chosen configuration, not just the convex hull's shape.

**Codimension.** A triangulation using every configuration point exists, for example by inserting those points into a triangulation one at a time. The full-dimensional simplices have a connected dual graph. A tree order adds at most one new vertex per additional simplex, so (n\le d+t). Each simplex has positive integral normalized volume in the chosen lattice; hence (t\le V) and (c=n-d-1\le V-1). The newly supplied elementary argument is correct.

**Circuit supports.** Support-minimal rational kernel vectors can be scaled to primitive integer circuits. The induction on support proves that circuits span the full rational kernel. Since each circuit is homogeneous, its positive and negative coefficient sums agree with its binomial degree, so its support size is at most twice that degree. Sturmfels's inequality (4.4) bounds that degree by (V). A basis of (c) circuits covers all coordinates on which any relation is nonzero. The no-pyramid conclusion makes that every coordinate. Thus (n\le2Vc\le2V(V-1)). This is the step which legitimately removes dimension; no ambient-variable count is held fixed without proof.

**Classical regularity bound.** I visually checked Sturmfels, p. 11, Theorem 4.5, Lemma 4.6, and inequalities (4.4)–(4.5), as well as the definition on p. 10. The constant is (n\,\deg(Y_A)\,\operatorname{codim}(Y_A)), and the regularity is the ideal's regularity. No normality or complete lattice-point hypothesis appears. The proof's circuit-to-Graver estimate, Gröbner degeneration, and Taylor-resolution estimate match the candidate. In particular an lcm of generators with individual degree at most (Vc) has total degree at most (nVc). [Sturmfels](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9610018)

**Finite holes and local cohomology.** The normalization is a finite graded module, and its quotient by the original ring has precisely the hole monomials as a vector-space basis. The quotient is finite length with final nonzero degree (h). The irrelevant ideal of the original ring extends to an ideal with radical equal to the normalization's positive ideal: every positive saturated monomial has a positive power in the original semigroup. Hochster's theorem therefore supplies vanishing of the normalization's zeroth and first local cohomology when its dimension is at least two. The original ring, being a positive-dimensional domain, has no zeroth local cohomology. The exact sequence consequently identifies the normalization quotient with (H^1(R)).

Hence (operatorname{reg}(R)\ge h+1). Since the nonempty-hole case has a nonzero toric ideal, (operatorname{reg}(I_A)=\operatorname{reg}(R)+1), giving the correctly indexed (h\le\operatorname{reg}(I_A)-2). Combining the preceding inequalities produces the stated quartic constant without losing either of the two shifts. The normalization need not be generated in degree one; the argument only needs its finite graded-module structure and depth.

**Degenerate cases.** A zero-dimensional configuration has one distinct generator and no holes. When (c=0), the independent generators give a free normal semigroup in their own generated group. When (V=1), the codimension inequality forces (c=0). These cases are correctly excluded by the nonempty-hole hypothesis. The optional convention (h=-1) for an empty hole set does not extend formula (2.1) to (V=1), where that formula's right-hand side is (-2); the theorem itself does not make that extension.
