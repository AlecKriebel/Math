# Dependencies and claim boundary for the marked inverse

The marked inverse is a certificate-search synthesis of existing theorems. The claim is exact termination and correct integral marking under one fixed computable comparison convention. There is no implementation, useful complexity guarantee, worldwide novelty claim, or historical closure claim.

- Deligne, *Variétés abéliennes ordinaires sur un corps fini*, Invent. Math. 8 (1969), 238–243. The complete six-page original was read. Section 3 supplies algebraic descent of the canonical lift; Theorem 7 supplies the equivalence; Section 8 fixes the complex structure from the comparison place. https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/Number6.pdf
- Marseglia, *Computing square-free polarized abelian varieties over finite fields*, arXiv:1805.10223v3. Retained source reused. Sections 3–5 and 8 supply the ideal, polarization and complex-period conventions. The displayed formula on PDF p.16 was visually checked: its bar covers both t and a. The manuscript explicitly converts its Riemann form to the opposite, positive topological first-Chern pairing. https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10223v3
- Katz, *Serre–Tate local moduli*, Theorem 1.2.1 and Theorem 2.1(1),(4). These supply deformation equivalence and the exact homomorphism-lifting condition. The relevant pages were inspected, not the whole 65-page paper. The new CRT argument in the manuscript proves that its finite Frobenius certificate forces the split lift. https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/serretatelocmod.pdf
- Basu–Karisani, *Efficient simplicial replacement of semialgebraic sets*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma (2023). The introduction was inspected for the classical semialgebraic triangulation theorem. The manuscript itself gives finite-witness verification and integral induced-map construction. It does not substitute a rational Betti-number algorithm for integral homology. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/efficient-simplicial-replacement-of-semialgebraic-sets/C32B4A3FA99FAF864D70274612ADA1C2
- Schnell, Lecture 4 on the Appell–Humbert theorem, p.3. The positive Chern-form formula was inspected and used to verify the sign conversion explicitly. https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~cschnell/mat615/lectures/lecture4.pdf
- Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, AV110, Corollary 15.4(b), printed pp.58–59. The retained source was checked for descent of homomorphisms under regular extensions. The manuscript proves that the required finitely generated coefficient extensions are regular. https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/AV110.pdf
- Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud, *Néron Models*, §1.2, Proposition 8, printed p.15. The independent audit retrieved and visually checked this original source: an abelian scheme over the Dedekind base is the Néron model of its generic fiber. This verifies the DVR extension step used in the manuscript; the manuscript also cites the corresponding author-hosted lecture-note statement. https://math.arizona.edu/~cais/scans/BLR-Neron_Models/neron1.pdf

The finite-field model checks, theta existence, and all-characteristic Beauville–Ritzenthaler recognition are inherited from the separately accepted aggregate theorem. Its hypotheses and independent scope remain unchanged. The marked inverse adds the certified label-to-model interface, rather than merely relabeling its unlabeled outputs.

There has been no end-to-end arithmetic/geometric execution of this inverse. The independent acceptance is an argument audit at the stated theoretical scope. Source hashes and archive hashes certify byte identities only.
