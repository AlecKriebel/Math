# Source and scope verification

## Exact source target

The exact original question is Conjecture 5 in Park's Oberwolfach contribution, printed pages 1728–1730. The fixed ambient spaces and the degree-at-most-three ordinary models are part of the statement. The original contribution was reread with the nearby theorems.

The already proved equivalence between a polar cylinder on S and a cylinder on U is separately credited. It does not settle the extension of every automorphism of U.

## Sources actually used

1. Park, *Automorphism groups of the complements of hypersurfaces*, Oberwolfach Report 28/2018, pp.1728–1730. Conjecture 5 fixes the target; Theorems 2, 3, 4 and 6 distinguish the known cylinder, singularity-classification, and connected-group results.
2. Cheltsov–Dubouloz–Park, *Super-rigid affine Fano varieties*, arXiv:1712.09148v1, subsequently Compositio Mathematica 154 (2018), 2462–2484. Sections 1–2 and 4 were inspected where relevant. Theorem 4.1 supplies the credited smooth degree-one theorem; Theorem 4.3 is the cylinder classification; Theorem C and Corollary 4.10 supply the known forward direction.
3. Park, *Ga-Actions on the Complements of Hypersurfaces*, institutional manuscript. Theorem 1.5 and section 3, including Corollary 3.2, were reread. Its full-automorphism statement is a conjecture, not its proved corollary.
4. Cheltsov–Park–Prokhorov–Zaidenberg, *Cylinders in Fano varieties*, EMS Surveys in Mathematical Sciences 8 (2021), 39–105, DOI 10.4171/EMSS/44. Section 4.2, especially Theorems 4.20 and 4.23, Corollary 4.22, and Conjecture 4.24, was reread. It explicitly includes the smooth-cubic problem.
5. Blanc–Poloni–Van Santen, *Complements of hypersurfaces in projective spaces*, JEP 11 (2024), 733–768, DOI 10.5802/jep.264. The Numdam primary PDF was newly retrieved and checked. Printed pp.734, 736, and 737 were visually inspected. The introduction, lifting section, and final singular-cubic section were read. Proposition 2.1 and Remarks 2.2–2.3 support the homogeneous-lifting comparison, not a linearity conclusion. Page 734 still leaves the smooth-cubic extension question unresolved. Proposition 5.22 concerns singular cubics; it does not resolve the smooth case.
