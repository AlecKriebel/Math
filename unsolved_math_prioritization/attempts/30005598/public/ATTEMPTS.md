# Five substantive attempts

Problem: 30005598 / OWR-14297736-004. Disposition: unresolved, 5/5.
These are five distinct mathematical attempts, not five literature lookups or
five numerical samples. The source identification and prior-result check preceded
them and are not counted. Complete derivations are in PROOF.md.

## Attempt 1: Optimize globally defined phase trials

Goal: find a field-uniform trial through gauge optimization, avoiding any
assumption that Omega is simply connected. Center the covariance matrix and
optimize every affine residual field of fixed curl. Completing the square proves
the exact optimum beta^2 det(Sigma)/tr(Sigma) in the constant-modulus quadratic-
phase class. This produces a checkable geometry-dependent initial field interval.
Failure to close: even the optimal member of this class has a quotient/beta
that grows linearly in beta. Thus this class cannot prove the full field range.
The finite checker tests the algebraic completion of squares at 108 rational
parameter combinations; the identity itself is proved symbolically in the text.

## Attempt 2: Restrict the half-plane de Gennes state

Goal: transfer the sharp half-plane eigenfunction to a general bounded domain.
Derive the exact weighted-section defect and recover the credited subgraph sign
mechanism. Then test the actual mechanism on disks tangent to the half-plane
boundary. After normalizing the chord lengths, dominated convergence and an
integration by parts give a strictly positive limiting defect as the radius grows.
Failure to close: the proposed universal trial fails even for a known positive
case. No eigenvalue counterexample follows; the obstruction is to this one trial.

## Attempt 3: Transplant the solved disk case

Goal: use the new disk theorem, affine maps, or disk partitions to prove the
all-domain claim. An exact covariant pullback calculation gives the factor
kappa=(a/b+b/a)/2 for individual radial angular-momentum states on ellipses.
For a noncircle this factor exceeds one, and the established disk asymptotic
shows that the resulting upper estimate cannot close every field range. Form-
domain comparison also shows that Neumann subdivision has the wrong direction,
while zero-extension of unrestricted Neumann states need not lie in H^1.
Failure to close: no loss-free comparison to a disk is established. This step
isolates a quantitative loss that can nonetheless be handled on a finite range.

## Attempt 4: Seek a topological counterexample in thin annuli

Goal: exploit the flux obstruction on a multiply connected domain with the exact
prescribed uniform-field potential. Separate angular modes, trap each radial
energy between a potential minimum and constant-test average, and prove that
only finitely many modes matter uniformly as thickness tends to zero. This
establishes the explicit limit dist(beta R^2/2,Z)^2/R^2. Its ratio to beta is at
most 1/4, strictly below the half-plane constant. An explicit finite-thickness
trial bound is also derived.
Failure to close: these thin circular annuli satisfy the desired bound; they do
not furnish a counterexample. Arbitrarily prescribed Aharonov--Bohm flux would
change the problem and is not used. General topology remains untreated.

## Attempt 5: Certify the finite-field affine margin exactly

Goal: repair the quantitative loss from Attempt 3 where credited disk test
states have sufficient slack. Transfer all 30 exact rational witnesses and real
covering intervals of Lena--Sundqvist. For every endpoint, recompute mass and
energy by two algebraically distinct exact integration formulas. All 60 source
Table 3 values agree, and all 60 new anisotropy-weighted defects are negative.
Convexity in the continuous field parameter upgrades endpoint checks to full
intervals. Together with the constant trial this proves the bound for every
ellipse with a/b<=101/100 and 0<beta ab<=131.
Failure to close: the uniform shape range and scaled field interval are bounded.
There is no argument extending them to all smooth domains or all field strengths.
The numerical lower bound for Theta_0 is a cited published input, not an
independently recomputed spectral certificate. No novelty claim is made.

## Conclusion

The strongest finite certificate is rigorous within its stated scope, conditional
only on the explicitly cited half-line theorem. No full resolution of the original
universal problem has been obtained. The proposed queue disposition is unsolved,
