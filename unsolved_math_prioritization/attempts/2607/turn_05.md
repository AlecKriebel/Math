# Attempt 5: a class-two countermodel to the proposed induction

Date: 2026-10-03 UTC. Fifth and final substantive proof attempt.

## Proposed completion and its missing premise

Attempt 4 puts the derived-word problem, after a finite quotient, inside a class-two group K=G^{(k-1)}. The relevant generating set X consists of individual delta_{k-1}-values, and the covered values are [X,X]. A tempting completion is to assert that normality, generation and power closure of X suffice to make K' finite-by-cyclic whenever [X,X] has a finite cyclic cover.

This attempt tests that assertion by a fully explicit construction. It is false even for a torsion-free, finitely generated class-two group. The example below refutes only the proposed auxiliary statement. No ambient G realizing this X as its delta_{k-1}-value set is constructed, so it does not refute KOU-21.98.

## Explicit group

Let K be the direct product of two integral Heisenberg groups. In coordinates write an element as

  (a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,c_1,c_2) in Z^6.

Multiplication is

  (a,b,c)(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+(a_1 b'_1,a_2 b'_2)),

where a,b each denote their two respective coordinates. Equivalently, in the six-coordinate order above, the first four coordinates add and c_i gains a_i b'_i.

Associativity follows from the bilinearity of the correction a_i b'_i, or directly from multiplication of the upper-unitriangular 3-by-3 matrices in each factor. The inverse has coordinates (-a_i,-b_i,-c_i+a_i b_i). The center and derived subgroup both equal

  Z(K)=K'={(0,0,0,0,c_1,c_2)} isomorphic to Z^2.

Indeed, with [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy, the commutator has central coordinates

  (a_1 b'_1-a'_1 b_1, a_2 b'_2-a'_2 b_2).

Let z_1 and z_2 be the two standard central generators; each is a commutator of the corresponding horizontal basis pair, proving equality with K'.

## The normally invariant power-closed generating set

Let X consist of all elements whose first four coordinates have at most one nonzero entry. The two central coordinates are arbitrary.

1. X is invariant under conjugation by K: conjugation changes only the central coordinates of a class-two group.
2. X is inverse closed: inversion negates the first four coordinates.
3. X is closed under all integer powers: a power multiplies the first four coordinates by the exponent, so at most one stays nonzero; central coordinates remain unrestricted.
4. X generates K, since it contains the four horizontal basis generators and the two central basis generators.

For x,y in X, a nonzero commutator can occur only when their nonzero horizontal coordinates are the two complementary positions in the same Heisenberg factor. Therefore

  {[x,y]: x,y in X} = <z_1> union <z_2>.

The reverse inclusion follows by using a_i=n,b'_i=1 in the desired factor. Thus two cyclic subgroups cover every [X,X]-value, but K'=Z^2 is not finite-by-cyclic: it has no nontrivial finite subgroup and is not cyclic.

This countermodel is stronger than the earlier coordinate-axes example, because it realizes the covered set as commutators of a normal, inverse-closed, power-closed generating set in an actual class-two group.

## Relation to the power-closed-generated series theorem

Fernández-Alcober and Morigi's Theorem B in arXiv:0911.3048 supplies, for a soluble ambient group and an outer commutator word, an abelian normal series of the verbal subgroup with factors generated by values whose powers remain values in those factors. This theorem is credited to them. It does not state that arbitrary products of word values remain word values, or that different cyclic directions admit a common affine progression of values.

The construction above shows why even a strong power-closure assumption on a generating set cannot justify the needed commutator-mixing inference. One must use identities specific to the fact that X is the full value set of a fixed multilinear word on a common ambient group. Merely invoking a PCG-series and replacing products of values by values would be an unsupported step.

There is a second simple obstruction to an over-optimistic counting route. In (Z/pZ)^r, the union of r coordinate cyclic subgroups has 1+r(p-1) elements and generates a group of order p^r. Hence a number-of-values estimate exponential in the value-set size, such as the soluble bound in the conciseness theorem, does not force r<=1 as p grows. This is again a countermodel to a counting inference, not a realization as a multilinear verbal set.
