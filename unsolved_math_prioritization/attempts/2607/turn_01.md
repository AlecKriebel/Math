# Attempt 1: normal cyclic coverings and the finite-by-abelian reduction

Date: 2026-10-03 UTC. This is a substantive proof attempt, not source retrieval.

## Goal

For the set X of values of a multilinear commutator word w, try to deduce that H = <X> is finite-by-cyclic from X being contained in a finite union of cyclic subgroups. The argument below uses no special property of w until its last reduction; it does not claim a resolution.

## Lemma 1: finite generation

Let X be any subset of a group, covered by C_1,...,C_m, with each C_i cyclic. Put D_i = <X intersect C_i>. This is a cyclic subgroup of C_i and lies in H=<X>. The subgroups D_i generate H, so H is generated by at most m elements. Each chosen generator of D_i is itself a finite product of elements of X intersect C_i and their inverses. Thus finitely many elements of X generate H as well. It is not necessary to assume the generators of the original covering subgroups belong to H.

## Lemma 2: a normally invariant finitely cyclic-covered set consists of FC-elements

Assume X is invariant under conjugation by G. An FC-element means an element with a finite conjugacy class in G.

For infinite-order elements define a~b when a^p=b^q for some nonzero integers p,q. This is an equivalence relation: transitivity follows by raising the two equalities to further integer powers. Every infinite cyclic C_i meets at most one equivalence class. Hence the infinite-order elements of X occupy finitely many classes, at most m, permuted by G. Let G_0 be the finite-index kernel of that permutation action.

Fix x in X of infinite order. For g in G_0 there are nonzero integers a,b with (x^g)^a=x^b. The rational number r_x(g)=b/a is well-defined since x has infinite order. Conjugating the power relations shows r_x(gh)=r_x(g)r_x(h), independently of the convention for composing conjugations because Q^* is abelian.

For every covering subgroup C_i=<c_i> whose elements meet the class of x, there are nonzero integers a_i,b_i with c_i^{a_i}=x^{b_i}. If y=c_i^k is commensurable with x, its rational ratio relative to x equals k b_i/a_i. The denominators of all these ratios therefore divide one fixed positive integer D (use the product of the |a_i|). For every n in Z the element x^{g^n} belongs to X. Consequently r_x(g)^n lies in D^{-1} Z for every integer n.

If a nonzero rational r has all positive and negative powers in D^{-1}Z, then r is 1 or -1: in a reduced expression r=u/v, any prime divisor of u or v creates unbounded denominators in one direction. Thus r_x(g)=+/-1 for all g in G_0.

Within each infinite cyclic C_i, at most two elements have ratio +1 or -1 relative to x, since k b_i/a_i determines k. Therefore x has at most 2m conjugates under G_0, and finitely many under G. No unique-root property of G is assumed here; uniqueness is used only inside each cyclic covering subgroup.

If x has finite order d, all its conjugates have order d. A cyclic group has at most d elements of order dividing d, so the finite cyclic cover again permits only finitely many conjugates. This includes finite-order values even when some covering groups are infinite.

This proves the lemma.

## Corollary: finite torsion kernel and a lattice quotient

By Lemma 1 choose finitely many elements x_1,...,x_s of X generating H. By Lemma 2, each C_G(x_i) has finite index. Their intersection equals C_G(H) and has finite index in G. In particular Z(H)=H intersect C_G(H) has finite index in H. Schur's theorem gives H' finite.

The abelian group H/H' is finitely generated. Its torsion subgroup is finite. Let T be its full preimage in H. Then T is finite, characteristic in H, and H/T is free abelian of finite rank r. It is exactly the set of finite-order elements of H: an element of finite order maps to torsion, and every element of the finite group T has finite order. In the verbal setting H is characteristic in G, so T is normal in G. Moreover G acts through a finite group on H/T, since C_G(H) has finite index.

The target is now precisely r <= 1. A finite kernel followed by a free abelian group of rank at most one is finite-by-cyclic. Conversely, if H is finite-by-cyclic, the free abelian quotient H/T has rank at most one.

If all w-values have finite order then X lies in T, hence H=T is finite. This establishes that special case without conciseness machinery.

## Reduction of the ambient group

Choose finitely many w-values generating H and all group elements occurring in the tuples producing them. They generate a finitely generated subgroup K of G with w(K)=H. Intersecting C_i with K preserves the covering hypothesis. Thus any counterexample has a finitely generated ambient counterexample with exactly the same verbal subgroup.

After quotienting by T, the verbal subgroup is Z^r. If h is the height of the binary tree for w, every value of the derived word delta_h is a w-value. This follows by induction on the two subtrees: a deeper derived value is a value of every shallower derived word, and disjoint variables permit the two subtree substitutions. Hence G^{(h)} <= w(G). When w(G) is abelian, G^{(h+1)}=1. The quotient ambient group is therefore solvable of derived length at most h+1.

## Outcome and exact gap

Proved: the original question reduces to a finitely generated solvable group with free abelian verbal subgroup Z^r, finite conjugation action on that subgroup, and a finite union of rational lines containing all values. The required assertion is r <= 1. These structural reductions do not show that two value directions can be combined into a third. The set of coordinate axes in Z^2 is a normal, cyclic-covered generating set of rank two, so normality and finite generation alone cannot finish the proof. Its failure to be shown to be a multilinear verbal set is exactly the relevant distinction.

Best-guess progress toward the original question: 20%, a heuristic research estimate rather than a probability of correctness or novelty. Novelty of these reductions is not claimed.
