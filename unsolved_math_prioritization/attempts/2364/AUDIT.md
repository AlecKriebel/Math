## The mathematical reduction is complete

For N>0, both factors of a signed integer factorization have the same sign. Replacing a negative pair by its absolute values preserves the absolute difference. Thus positive factor pairs suffice. For d>=0, membership in D(N) is equivalent to d²+4N being a square: the forward implication follows by taking the factor sum; the reverse gives factors (x-d)/2 and (x+d)/2. Since x²=d²+4N>d², x>d; congruence modulo 4 gives x and d the same parity. Both factors are therefore positive integers. When d=0, this says exactly that N is a square.

Fix integers 0<=a<b and let H=b²-a²>0. If N has both differences, take positive roots x²=a²+4N and y²=b²+4N. Then y>x>0 and the unique pair u=y-x, v=y+x satisfies uv=H and 0<u<v. Consequently all possibilities are found among the positive divisors u<=isqrt(H), with v=H/u. There is no independent bound on N or on a third difference.

The report's Q=(v-u)²-4a² filter is sufficient as well as necessary. If Q>0 and 16 divides Q, then v-u is even, so u and v have the same parity and x=(v-u)/2, y=(v+u)/2 are integers. The congruence x²-a²=Q/4≡0 mod 4 gives x≡a mod 2. Also y²-b²=x²-a², so y≡b mod 2. With u<=v and Q>0, x>a; the resulting N=Q/16 is positive. The endpoint u=v has x=0 and Q=-4a²<=0, and is always discarded. The endpoint x=a gives N=0 and is also discarded. No negative, fractional or zero N passes.

The map from a retained smaller divisor u to N is injective: H/u-u is strictly decreasing for positive u, and its retained values are positive. Squaring on the positive half-line preserves this strict ordering. This also establishes that duplicate rows are not legitimate multiplicities.

My independent enumeration uses the explicit conditions that u+v is even, x>a, y>b, x-a is even, and y-b is even, then constructs N=((x-a)/2)((x+a)/2). It agrees exactly with the Q filter. Thus the verification does not merely repeat the report's combined divisibility test.

For each generated N, trial division independently computes its prime factorization, stopping only when the trial divisor squared exceeds the remaining cofactor. At that point any remaining cofactor is prime: a composite would have had a smaller prime divisor. All divisors are then generated by their complete exponent choices. Every D(N) is enumerated as N/r-r for r dividing N with r²<=N. The inclusive endpoint is essential. There is no downloaded factorization routine, probabilistic primality test, floating-point arithmetic, or preset prime cutoff.

## Rational rectangle equivalence and the fixed-scale limit

The reported equivalence for fixed k is sound. A factor-difference configuration supplies rational X={0,4N₁,...,4Nₖ} and Y={d₁²,...,dₖ²}, of sizes k+1 and k, with all sums squares. Conversely, for rational X,Y with all sums rational squares, choose x₀=min X and a positive integer L clearing the denominators of all square roots. For any y, L²(x-x₀) is the difference of the two integer squares L²(x+y) and L²(x₀+y), so Nₓ=L²(x-x₀) is integral and positive when x>x₀. The values dᵧ=2Lsqrt(x₀+y) are nonnegative integers and distinct, because the nonnegative square root is injective. The displayed square identity then yields the desired factor pairs, including any zero difference.

The fixed-pair obstruction cannot be promoted to an unrestricted rescaling theorem. Scaling known factor pairs by a positive integer t sends (N,d) to (t^2 N,td), but membership of td in D(M) alone does not force t^2 to divide M. Thus a hypothetical configuration with scaled differences cannot automatically be divided back to the audited integer scale. The acceptance concerns only the exact S and T identified in the source. It excludes neither arbitrary scaled copies nor configurations whose common differences meet each of S and T in at most one element.
