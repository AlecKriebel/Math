# Graph-square projection calculation (working authored notes)

Assume G is infinite, connected, simple, locally finite, vertex-transitive of degree d. Let H=G², E=E(G), F=E(H)\E. Use one unoriented edge coordinate with sign changes under orientation reversals. Z is the closed subspace of square-summable gradients of finite-energy functions, equivalently the orthogonal complement of finite cycles. The FUSF is the determinantal process of projection P_Z.

Choose the average over all length-two paths between each added pair. This defines an equivariant bounded map L on Z by L(df)(xy)=f(y)-f(x). Boundedness follows from E_G(f) <= E_H(f) <= (4d-3)E_G(f): there are at most 2(d-1) length-two paths containing each old edge, and each path difference has square at most twice its two edge squares. Every square-gradient is (z,Lz), so Z_H is the graph of L.

Writing A=(I_Z+L*L)^(-1), the projection onto Z_H in Z ⊕ l²(F) is [[A,AL*],[LA,LAL*]], and it vanishes on Z-perp in the old-edge factor. Let tau_E(S)=one half the sum of diagonal entries over old edges incident to the root and similarly tau_F. Then

b_F(H)-b_F(G) = tau_F(LAL*) - tau_E(L*LA),

where operators on Z are extended by zero to l²(E). The formula is exact and uses no trace property. (The -1 terms cancel.) For unimodular common transitive actions, the edge trace cyclicity forces zero. Without unimodularity, replacing the right side by zero is precisely an unproved step, not a consequence of the bounded isomorphism of Dirichlet spaces.

Zero-invariance: the equivalent energies give identical Dirichlet vector space modulo constants and identical closed subspace D0 generated by finite-support functions. Thus D/D0 vanishes for G iff for H. Via Royden decomposition this is equivalent to no nonconstant harmonic finite-energy functions, hence FUSF=WUSF. WUSF has degree expectation 2 on all transitive graphs (not only unimodular). Positivity/transitivity of the harmonic projection shows b_F=0 iff that projection vanishes. Therefore b_F(G)=0 iff b_F(G²)=0, including nonunimodular graphs.
