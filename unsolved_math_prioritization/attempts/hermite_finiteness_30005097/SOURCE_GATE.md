# Row 1889 source gate: problem 30005097

## Disposition

The degree-unrestricted fixed-field question has a known negative answer. Do not present its quartic counterexamples as new. The degree-at-least-five conjecture is a different, narrower target and is not settled by this gate. Source inspection is not a new mathematical attempt.

## Original source and missing restriction

Jan-Hendrik Evertse's contribution, “Hermite equivalence of polynomials,” in Oberwolfach Report 21/2022 occupies printed pages 1153–1155. Its question at page 1154 uses PI(K), the primitive irreducible integer polynomials with a root generating the fixed number field K. The surrounding text explicitly records quartic counterexamples. Thus the unrestricted question should not be treated as an unanswered universal conjecture. [Report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46958?nt=1).

## Established negative answer and intended conjecture

Bhargava–Evertse–Győry–Remete–Swaminathan, “Hermite equivalence of polynomials,” Conjecture 4.2, assumes degree at least five. Section 5.1 and Theorem 5.1 establish infinitely many split Hermite classes inside certain fixed quartic fields, not merely across varying fields. These are fields generated by irreducible S4 polynomials (X²-r)²-X-s. The construction supplies monic minimal-polynomial pairs in a common field, equal generated orders, distinct orders along an infinite subsequence, and failure of GL2(Z)-equivalence. This directly refutes the degree-unrestricted formulation. The theorem and construction are credited literature, not an authored discovery. [Versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02932v3).

## Later progress

Evertse, “Orders with few rational monogenizations,” Theorem 1.2, establishes the desired finiteness for degree at least five with 5-transitive normal closure. For every quartic field it establishes finiteness of classes splitting into more than two GL2(Z)-classes, not finiteness for the threshold more than one. This improves the original report's weaker GL2(Q)-class result and includes nonmonic polynomials. [Versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01552v2).

The 2025 revision of Evertse–Győry's survey repeats this result as Corollary 10.16 and explicitly distinguishes the conjecture without 5-transitivity. Problem 6 asks the stronger order-theoretic finiteness question for all fields of degree at least five. No unrestricted positive resolution was located in this bounded search. [Survey v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02627v2).

Akhtari–Shumaker's August 2026 preprint concerns monogenic quartic orders and exceptional orders outside types I and II. Its Theorem 1.3 removes the S4 restriction in that classification. It does not assert the degree-at-least-five Hermite conjecture or invalidate the known quartic counterexamples. Only its abstract, introductory context and theorem statement were inspected; no proof audit is claimed. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02983v1).
