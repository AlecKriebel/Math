For a finite set of n distinct planar points, n≥2, let r(p) be the number of distinct positive distances from p to the other points, σ(P) the number of different integers among the r(p), and g(n)=max σ(P). The target is g(n)/n→1. Since 1≤r(p)≤n−1, the source's n−o(n) construction question is equivalent to this formulation. Root directly inspected the complete rendered 1995 manuscript pages14–15: page14 states distinct planar points and credits the two-pin product observation to Erdős and Saldanha; page15 asks for the number of distinct pinned-count values and proposes n−o(n). Its earlier use of g(n) for unit-distance multiplicity is a separate parameter. The full 1997 cited article, Erdős–Fishburn and Csizmadia–Ismailescu proofs, and 2026 external preprint proofs are not recertified. Their reported bounds are not premises of the elementary arguments below, nor a certified best-known bound.

For independent translations of nonempty finite blocks P_i, admissibility means disjoint coordinates, mutually different external distances at every pin, and external distances distinct from all internal distances. Every forbidden cross-block coincidence or distance equality is the zero set of a proper polynomial. For two comparison points in the same external block, subtraction leaves a nonconstant affine coefficient from their nonzero difference. For points in different comparison blocks, at least one block is external to the pin; varying it alone leaves a nonzero quadratic term. Internal/internal equalities are deliberately allowed. A finite union of proper closed polynomial zero sets has empty interior, so admissible translations form an open dense set in the full independent translation space. Restricted directions need not have this property; the statement does not impose them.

At a pin of block size m_i, the union count is n−m_i+r_i. Consequently its deficit (n−1)−r_union equals (m_i−1)−r_i exactly. The union deficit spectrum is the set union of seed deficit spectra, not their sum and not a multiset. For maximal seed size M≥2, deficits lie in {0,…,M−2}, so at most M−1 values occur; singleton seeds have deficit0 and the all-singleton case has one value. Repetitions cannot amplify the spectrum; singleton padding adds at most one value. Iterating at every admissible finite hierarchy level gives the leaf-spectrum union. Thus M(n)=o(n) cannot produce a linear spectrum. The unit-square union of two unit segments is a correct nongeneric counterexample to extending this identity without admissibility; it says nothing against controlled nongeneric constructions for the original target.

A positive distance circle meets a supporting line in at most two points. At a pin on a positive-radius supporting circle, its distance circle has a different center and meets the support in at most two points. Hence ceil((n−1)/2)≤r≤n−1 and σ≤ceil(n/2). Equally spaced line points attain it with r_i=max(i,n−1−i). A circular arc whose total angular span is below π has chord lengths strictly increasing in the positive index difference and attains the same counts. Whole-circle folding is excluded. If m=n−t≥2 points lie exactly on one such support, each supported pin still has at least a=ceil((m−1)/2) distances. Their full counts occupy at most n−a integers; t exceptional pins add at most t, giving σ≤min(n−1,n+t−ceil((n−t−1)/2)). In particular t=o(n) yields (1/2+o(1))n. An exceptional center pin is included in t. Approximate geometric closeness does not satisfy the exact-support hypothesis.

For distinct pins p,q, each other point lies on one of r(p)r(q) pairs of distance circles, each having at most two intersections. Thus n−2≤2r(p)r(q). For s≥2 different count values a_1<…<a_s≤n−1, a_j≤n−s+j−1. With h=n−s, choosing the first two representatives gives n−2≤2h(h+1), hence h≥(sqrt(2n−3)−1)/2. For s=1, h=n−1 satisfies the same inequality directly; n=2 is harmless. This classical sublinear defect is compatible with g(n)/n→1. The restricted half barrier does not become a universal half barrier.
