# An effective criterion for the prescribed block-code extension problem

## Status and target

Candidate complete effective characterization of Boyle's Problem 16.3, subject to independent audit. No claim of novelty or of a previously published symbolic-dynamics resolution is made. The decisive external theorem is credited explicitly below; its general proof is not reproved here.

The input is a nonempty, two-sided mixing sofic shift T over a finite alphabet A and a specified surjective sliding block map f:T→T. The question asks whether f has an extension F:T′→T for some shift of finite type T′ containing T. Inclusion is non-strict: the original says “containing.” This is an extension of the given map, not an embedding or conjugacy problem and not a search for some unrelated self-map of T. No fixed point assumption is imposed in Problem 16.3.

We obtain an explicit finite, computable test. It also applies when mixing is weakened to irreducibility. It does not decide the separate existential-over-f question or the stable-cellular-automaton-limit-set characterization.

## Data and notation

L(T) is the language of all finite words appearing in T, including the empty word. A presentation of T is a finite labelled graph; trim it to vertices/edges on bi-infinite paths. All vertices serve as initial and final states, so this graph recognizes L(T). Its subset automaton is an effective complete DFA.

Write f with memory a and anticipation b and put ell=a+b+1. Complete its local rule arbitrarily on the ell-words outside L(T). This defines a full-shift block map F:A^Z→A^Z extending f. For a finite word w of length at least ell, F_*(w) is the word of length |w|-ell+1 obtained by applying that local rule to its consecutive ell-windows, in order. Define F_*(w)=epsilon for |w|<ell.

Define the bad-word language

B = { w in A* : F_*(w) is not in L(T) }.

B is regular and is a two-sided ideal: if w is bad, then uwv is bad for every u,v. Indeed F_*(w) is a factor of F_*(uwv), and L(T) is factorial. An effective DFA for B stores the last at most ell-1 input symbols and the current state of the complete DFA recognizing L(T), feeding that DFA an output letter whenever another ell-window is available. It accepts precisely when the output DFA rejects. This proves regularity and gives a concrete finite construction, including short words.

For n≥2 define the canonical n-block approximation

T[n] = { x in A^Z : every length-n block of x belongs to L_n(T) }.

It is an SFT containing T. Its graph has vertices L_(n-1)(T) and edges L_n(T), with the usual overlaps. Irreducibility of T makes this graph strongly connected: any two vertex words occur with a connecting word in a point of T. Hence T[n] is irreducible. Also L_n(T[n])=L_n(T). Every finite word of length at least n whose n-factors lie in L_n(T) extends to a bi-infinite path in this graph. Shorter allowed words have the same extension property.

## The finite criterion

Construct any finite monoid M and morphism alpha:A*→M recognizing both regular languages L(T) and B. For example, take the transition monoid of the disjoint union of complete DFAs for the two languages. It is effective: enumerate the finite transformations generated by the letters and the identity. Set

k = 4(|M|+1),   N = max(2, ell, k).

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent:

1. The specified f has a block-map extension into T from some SFT containing T.
2. L(T) and B can be separated by a locally testable language.
3. L(T[N]) has empty intersection with B.
4. F(T[N])=T.

Thus the answer is yes exactly when a single finite-automaton intersection test in (3) is empty. On yes, T[N] with the originally chosen completed local rule is a witness. On no, no choice of larger block radius and no different completion of the same map on T can work.

### External input

Place, van Rooijen and Zeitoun, *On Separation by Locally Testable and Locally Threshold Testable Languages*, Logical Methods in Computer Science 10(3:24), 2014, Theorem 4.2: if two regular languages recognized by a common finite monoid M admit a locally testable separator, they admit one in LT[k] for k=4(|M|+1). Their LT[k] equivalence records the set of k-profiles. A profile consists of the at-most-floor(k/2) letters preceding a position and the at-most-ceil(k/2) letters beginning there. Only presence, not multiplicity, is recorded. [Published article](https://lmcs.episciences.org/1163/pdf), pp. 4–8; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2168/LMCS-10(3:24)2014).

This published finite-bound theorem is the only non-elementary imported ingredient. We use neither an unproved compactness stabilization assertion nor a bounded-search extrapolation.

## Proof

### 1. Passing to canonical approximations and fixing the local rule

Suppose G:S→T extends f, with S an SFT containing T. If the ambient alphabet is larger, replace S by S intersect A^Z; this remains an SFT containing T. There exists q such that T[q] is contained in S: choose q at least the lengths of a finite forbidden list for S. Every forbidden word for S is absent from T, so a point all of whose q-blocks occur in T cannot contain one.

Although F and G may use different radii, their local rules agree on T. Enlarge their windows to a common finite interval J. The output rules agree on every J-word of T. Choose n large enough for both the inclusion T[n]⊆S and the window J. Every J-word in a point of T[n] occurs in T, so F=G on T[n]. Consequently F(T[n])⊆T.

Conversely, if F(T[n])⊆T, its restriction is the required extension. This proves that allowing different completions or larger radii does not change the existence question.

For every n, F(T[n])⊆T if and only if L(T[n])∩B is empty. In one direction, a bad word appearing in a point has a forbidden output block. In the other, if F(x) is not in T, it contains a finite word outside L(T); the corresponding input interval is a bad word in L(T[n]). Finally, since f(T)=T and T⊆T[n], inclusion automatically gives F(T[n])=T. This is the only use of surjectivity.

### 2. A witness SFT gives an LT separator

For sufficiently large n as above, L(T[n]) contains L(T) and avoids B. This language is locally testable: words of length at least n are accepted precisely when all their n-factors belong to L_n(T); the finitely many shorter words are accepted precisely when they belong to L(T). The extension assertion here uses the strong connectivity of the n-block graph. Finite languages and the property of excluding a specified finite list of factors are locally testable, so their combination is locally testable. Thus (1) implies (2).

### 3. Saturation lemma: an LT[k] separator forces a canonical witness

Assume C in LT[k] contains L(T) and avoids B, and let n≥max(2,k). Suppose for contradiction that a bad word w appears in T[n]. Choose a word U in L(T), of length at least n+k, containing every word of L_n(T). Such U exists by concatenating the finitely many n-words with connecting words, using irreducibility.

Because T[n] is irreducible, there are words c,d such that

W = U c w d U  belongs to L(T[n]).

Because B is a two-sided ideal and contains w, W belongs to B. Every n-factor of W is in L_n(T), and the occurrence of U guarantees that its n-factor set is exactly L_n(T).

Independently, irreducibility of T gives a connecting word e with

V = U e U  belonging to L(T).

V likewise contains every n-word and no other n-word. Therefore V and W have the same set of k-letter factors, namely L_k(T), since k≤n and every k-word in T extends to an n-word. Both start and end with U, so their prefixes and suffixes of length k-1 coincide.

These facts imply equality of their k-profile sets under the published convention: interior profiles are obtained by splitting each length-k factor after floor(k/2) letters; profiles truncated at the left or right endpoint are determined by the identical prefixes or suffixes. Since both words have length at least 2k, there is no position truncated at both ends. Thus V and W are LT[k]-equivalent.

As V is in L(T)⊆C, W is also in C. This contradicts W∈B and C∩B=empty. We conclude L(T[n])∩B=empty for every n≥max(2,k).

Applying the external theorem to (2) supplies exactly such an LT[k] separator for k=4(|M|+1). Taking n=N proves (2) implies (3). Section 1 proves (3) equivalent to (4), and (4) implies (1). The proof is complete.

### 4. Why the test is finite and exact

Build the finite overlap graph for T[N]. Label an edge by its newly appended symbol and use all vertices as initial/final states. Its finite-path language is L(T[N]). Form its product with the DFA for B. The intersection is nonempty exactly when a B-accepting product state is reachable from some initial graph vertex and the initial B-state. Graph reachability decides this without any word-length cutoff. The monoid and overlap graphs may be very large; no practical complexity improvement is claimed.

The code accompanying this proof exercises this exact intersection method for small examples. It does not compute the enormous universal bound for arbitrary inputs, reprove the external theorem, or infer the theorem from finite examples.

## Independent elementary controls

### Automorphisms

If f:T→T is injective as well as onto, it is a conjugacy and its inverse g is a block map. If F maps an SFT neighborhood into T, choose a canonical T[n] inside that neighborhood and large enough that g composed with F equals the identity there (the equality is local and holds on T). Then every x in T[n] satisfies x=g(F(x))∈T. Hence T=T[n] is SFT. Conversely, if T is SFT, choose T′=T. Thus an automorphism has the required extension exactly when T is SFT.

This does not say that every surjective self-code is injective. It rules out incorrectly using identity as a universal positive example.

### An infinite negative family

Let E be the binary even shift, presented by edges p--0→p, p--1→q, q--1→p. It is mixing because the graph is strongly connected and has a loop; its label factor is mixing. It is not SFT: its forbidden words 0 1^(2j+1) 0 have unbounded length, while all proper factors of each such word belong to L(E). For any n choose an odd m with m+2>n. The word 0 1^m 0 is n-locally E-admissible and extends to E[n], but not to E. Consequently the identity map of E has no such extension. The padding construction above explicitly produces, for every k, an allowed word and a forbidden word with the same k-profile set. Periodic input witnesses (0 1^m)^infinity give the same obstruction whenever their shortest forbidden block exceeds n.

### Positive and hypothesis controls

Every SFT T admits T′=T for every self-code f. If onto is dropped, a constant map to a fixed point of a non-SFT T extends from the full shift; its image is only that fixed point, so equality with T would be false. Code tests include this control. The theorem's existence argument itself needs irreducibility, regularity and a prescribed block map; onto is needed to identify the output with all of T.

## Attribution and remaining review boundary

Boyle supplies the 2008 problem; Ballier's published work identifies this property as steadiness and treats important closing/continuing classes. Those results do not by themselves identify every prescribed map. Place–van Rooijen–Zeitoun supply the decisive LT separation bound. The reduction from the present extension question to that bound is proved here and requires fresh adversarial checking. Bounded literature searches located no source explicitly making this reduction; absence from these searches is not evidence of novelty. If the reduction withstands review, it gives a complete effective answer to Problem 16.3, without claiming a classification of the different problem of which shifts admit some steady self-map.
