## Proposition by proposition audit

### Proposition 1 and general capping

PASS. [Manolescu-Neithalath](https://web.stanford.edu/~cm5/lasagna.pdf), Proposition 2.1, gives a natural surjection for a 3-handle attachment and an isomorphism for a 4-handle attachment in the empty-link setting used here. Their Proposition 1.6 gives the punctured-closed identification. The proof uses transversality to avoid the cocore for surfaces, and the one-parameter avoidance needed for injectivity in the 4-handle case. These are imported geometric results, not consequences of the finite checker.

Filling the particular spherical boundary of a punctured closed manifold is therefore safe. A compact trace with arbitrary boundary does not acquire a closed invariant merely by invoking this statement. General closure can add other handles, and surjectivity alone cannot preserve a difference. The two projections onto Q give an exact algebraic counterexample to that inference.

The semisimple cap example is also correct: for A=Qe plus Qf, balanced tensoring retains only pairs in the same idempotent sector. Both (Q,0) and (Q,Q) become (Q,0) after tensoring with cap (Q,0). This is an exact, nonfaithful functor with a nonzero cap. No realizability as an actual lasagna cap is claimed. [Blackwell-Krushkal-Luo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05861v1), Theorem 3.8 and Remark 3.10, provide the relevant categorical balanced-tensor gluing framework, not a theorem that tensoring by an arbitrary cap reflects isomorphism. The packet's distinction is valid.

### Proposition 2 and comparison under all markings

PASS. For a surjection p:V->W, a linear functional lambda factors through p exactly when it vanishes on ker(p). The candidate descended value on p(v) is independent of representatives precisely under that condition. A nonzero value of lambda(v) then forces p(v) to be nonzero. This remains valid without finite dimensionality.

The proposition is conditional on actual capping maps, kernels, and grading transport; none is produced for a candidate. A fixed cap map need not be surjective merely because a more elaborate gluing sum is surjective. The packet does not make that inference. When homology degrees merge, one must push the grading forward and combine the components having the same image. It is correct to warn that source degrees cannot simply be retained as distinct target degrees.

The unmarked comparison criterion is sufficient: an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism would induce a homology isomorphism preserving the intersection form and the invariant's graded dimensions. Excluding equality for every allowable isometry therefore excludes such a diffeomorphism. Excluding only one chosen marking does not. For non-simply-connected manifolds, torsion and any additional structure carried by the homology transport cannot silently be discarded; the proposition's full H_2 transport formulation accommodates this.

### Proposition 3 and the positive sphere obstruction

PASS. The smooth tubular neighborhood of an oriented sphere of square n is the disk bundle with Euler number n, equivalently the n-framed unknot trace. The unknot's maximal Thurston-Bennequin number is -1, so the Ren-Willis v3 Theorem 1.4 condition becomes n>=1. This is exactly strict positivity for integral n. The zero-square case is excluded.

Ren-Willis Example 3.2 states this specialization. Its preceding theorem proof passes from the embedded trace to the ambient manifold by gluing and proves cable support escapes every fixed total degree. This is a genuine all-tail argument in the source, unlike an arbitrary finite cable computation. The theorem and its Ng-bound/cabling foundations remain credited inputs.

The projective line in CP^2 has square +1. The diagonal in S^2 times S^2 has square +2. Connected-sum balls can be chosen disjoint from those spheres, so both vanishings follow. Neither a positive lattice vector nor a merely locally flat topological sphere satisfies the smooth hypothesis. Reversing the ambient orientation reverses the square. The author's conclusion is not that b2+>0 alone forces vanishing.

### Proposition 4 and the closed conditional construction

PASS, including the orientation-free supplement. Here is the full hypothesis audit.

1. Closedness and simple connectivity imply that H_2(X;Z) is torsion free and that its intersection pairing is an integral unimodular form. Poincare duality and the universal coefficient theorem justify these properties; compactness alone would not.
2. Write p=b2+(X)>0 and m=b2-(X). A negative blowup adds a <-1> summand, so the new form has indices (p,m+1). It is indefinite because both indices are positive, and odd because the exceptional generator has square -1. These facts hold whether Q_X was initially even or odd, definite or indefinite.
3. The standard classification of odd indefinite unimodular integral forms identifies it with p copies of <+1> and m+1 copies of <-1>. The topological comparison is then supplied by Freedman's classification in its applicable closed, simply-connected, almost-smooth regime. Both smooth manifolds have zero Kirby-Siebenmann invariant. The matching oriented forms and obstruction therefore give the needed orientation-preserving homeomorphism. The [original Freedman paper](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/1982_The%20topology%20of%20four-dimensional%20manifolds.pdf), Theorem 1.5 and its odd-indefinite discussion, were inspected; its deep classification proof is imported.
4. The connected-sum formula is indeed available over a field, including Q, for interior connected sums. MN Theorem 1.4 is the boundary-sum formula; Corollary 7.3 combines it with puncturing and 4-handle filling to obtain the interior formula. Its construction uses neck cutting and checks isotopy independence. It is not restricted here to both summands being 2-handlebodies.
5. Ren-Willis Proposition 1.14 gives nonzero ordinary rational module for CPbar^2. Its proof reduces to explicit surviving cable classes and the formulas in Section 6.9; the nonzero ordinary conclusion does not follow merely from the existence of the unfiltered Lee basis. In particular the zero homology class supplies a nonzero rational piece.
6. Over Q, tensoring two nonzero vector spaces is nonzero: choose nonzero vectors and linear functionals evaluating to one. Thus S(X) nonzero gives S(X#CPbar^2) nonzero. This argument would be false for arbitrary nonzero modules over an arbitrary ring, so the coefficient restriction matters.
7. The standard comparison has a positive projective-plane summand because p>0. It contains a smooth +1 sphere and has zero module by Proposition 3. Zero versus nonzero distinguishes the modules regardless of any homology reindexing. It excludes every orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.
8. For r negative blowups, both signatures equal sigma(X)-r. The rule r=1 except r=2 when sigma(X)=1 makes this integer nonzero in every case. The forms remain odd and indefinite and the tensor product remains nonzero. Any orientation-reversing diffeomorphism between the two manifolds would force their signatures to be opposite. Equal nonzero signatures rule this out. A single blowup would not give this signature obstruction when sigma(X)=1, which is why the supplement is needed for the stated argument.

This route is already explicitly credited to Ren-Willis Section 6.12. The orientation safeguard is a sound elementary supplement, without a novelty claim. No step constructs or proves the existence of an X satisfying the crucial closed nonvanishing premise. The proposition is a sufficient route, not a necessary characterization of every possible answer to the target.

### Proposition 5 and what the Lee structure forgets

PASS. Ren-Willis Theorem 4.1 supplies canonical generators indexed, for empty link, by ordered pairs of integral second-homology classes. Mapping (a,b) to (f_*a,f_*b) under an oriented homeomorphism gives a bijection of those indices and therefore a linear isomorphism of the unfiltered modules. This is an algebraically defined basis transport, not an asserted smooth cobordism map for a nonsmooth homeomorphism.

The degrees are checked with the source conventions: homological degree -2a.b; homology degree a+b; and, with no boundary components, mod-4 quantum degrees -(a+b)^2 and -(a+b)^2-2 for the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations. An oriented homeomorphism preserves all those expressions. When a=b, the antisymmetric vector vanishes; this does not create an extra generator. Torsion in H_2 is not a license to collapse distinct basis labels. The independent tests retain a torsion coordinate explicitly.

The integer filtration is not determined by those degree expressions. A nonzero space with F_q=V for every integer q has all nonzero elements at filtration value minus infinity and has zero associated graded in every degree. It is exhaustive but nonseparated. This exactly blocks the proposed inference from a Lee basis to ordinary-module nonvanishing. No such inference is made in the author proof.

### Proposition 6 and the filtered two-handlebody test

PASS. A finite filtration degree q gives an element of F_q/F_(q-1) that is nonzero. The source's increasing-filtration convention is correctly used. If the original witness is not homogeneous, its finite decomposition into homological and homology-class components has at least one component with a nonzero associated-graded class; otherwise the whole vector would lie in F_(q-1). This elementary clarification fills in the direct application to a tri-graded comparison and does not change the proposition.

Ren-Willis Theorem 1.11 then forces a nonzero ordinary rational component for the 2-handlebody. The source proves the inequality by lifting independent associated-graded Lee classes to leading Khovanov cocycles and showing that any later death would lower the Lee filtration, a contradiction. It does not construct a lasagna spectral sequence. The author correctly respects its two-handlebody hypothesis. With boundary S^3 and only the 4-handle remaining, MN transfers nonvanishing to the closed X. If 3-handles remain, survival would require additional information. A finite upper bound on filtration or an s-invariant does not exclude minus infinity; a finite lower bound and a finite representative do.

### Proposition 7 and direct-limit death

PASS. In a directed system of vector spaces, equality of two representatives in the colimit means equality at some common later stage. Applying this to a representative and zero gives the eventual-death criterion. No injectivity assumption is present or needed.

For fixed finite-dimensional V_i, the kernels of the composites from V_i increase. Their union has a finite basis; choose a common stage killing all its basis vectors. That single stage realizes the union. The proof gives existence of a last kernel increase, not a computable bound derived from a prefix. In particular, observing equal kernels for many consecutive stages does not exclude a later increase. If V_i were infinite dimensional, even the finite-stage realization could fail. Both limitations are correctly scoped.

### Proposition 8 and finite-prefix ambiguity

PASS. At every visible stage take Q with identity maps. Identity maps forever give colimit Q. Starting with the first unseen map and using zero maps forever gives colimit zero because every representative eventually dies. These systems have the same prescribed finite prefix. Thus an arbitrary finite prefix alone cannot decide the colimit.

The word "forever" matters. One zero transition followed by identities would kill the old representatives but leave a Q from later stages, so it would not be a zero-limit counterexample. The author uses zero maps at all later stages and avoids this trap. The examples do not claim to arise as actual cable systems.

A compatible family of functionals defined for the whole tail, nonzero on the chosen representative, is a valid sufficient certificate of survival. The source formula uses rational symmetrization, the appropriate cable shifts, and dotted-annulus maps. The diagonal in the finite product of nonnegative integer index sets is cofinal. Rational invariants and coinvariants are related by averaging; the packet does not extend this unqualifiedly to characteristic dividing a symmetric-group order. A finite list of successful stages supplies none of the missing all-tail compatibility.

### Proposition 9 and stabilization

PASS. The smooth positive diagonal sphere proves S(X#(S^2 times S^2))=0; the field-valued connected-sum formula gives the same result by tensoring with zero. Equality after this operation cannot be cancelled. The algebraic comparison of Q and Q^2 after tensoring with zero is valid.

The nonzero countably infinite-dimensional factor example is also valid for ungraded vector spaces. The map from the basis labelled (epsilon,n) to the basis labelled 2n+epsilon has inverse quotient and remainder upon division by two. This proves W is isomorphic to Q^2 tensor W while Q and Q^2 are not isomorphic. Finite numerical samples are only checks of that formula, not the proof of its infinite extent.

The warning about graded local finiteness is justified. Ren-Willis Proposition 6.17 invokes the additional finiteness in Proposition 6.16 for CPbar^2-stably standard homotopy spheres. The packet neither substitutes bare nonvanishing for that finiteness nor incorrectly cancels the vanishing S^2 times S^2 factor. Proposition 6.17 is cited as a restricted literature fact; none of the ten propositions relies on re-proving its general graded cancellation step.

### Proposition 10 and null-homologous Gluck twists

PASS with the stated imported-source boundary. RSSWZ Theorem 7.1 applies to ordinary Khovanov skein lasagna modules over Q, although the paper also develops a different one-dimensional-input theory. It supplies inverse maps for the two Gluck twists and records the H_2 identification and class-dependent bidegree shift. The author does not confuse the two input theories.

For zero integral sphere class, every intersection a.[S] is zero. Both components of the shift vanish, and Corollary 7.2 supplies the graded isomorphism. The source theorem proof handles the odd-intersection case by showing the corresponding local filling is zero; the half-integer expression for an odd intersection therefore does not establish nonzero fractional-graded sectors. The packet's finite half-integer arithmetic checks are abstract scope controls, not existence claims about such sectors.

In S^4 every sphere is integrally null-homologous. The ball calculation and 4-handle invariance give S(S^4)=Q in the ordinary rational theory, so the asserted Gluck-twist consequence follows. This does not prove that any such twist is exotic, does not extend the theorem to integral coefficients or every N, and does not prohibit detection outside this particular family.

The inspected source proof uses the local belt-link classes, the auxiliary Lemma 7.4, general position, neck cutting, and inverse-map relations. Its full 88-page functoriality, foam, and prior link-homology machinery has not been independently reconstructed. This is an explicit dependency of the restricted exclusion, not something validated by either finite program.

## Disposition and remaining obligations

The appropriate disposition is **unsolved, 5/5 approaches**, with no new-discovery or global-openness claim. The missing obligations remain exactly substantive:

1. An actual closed smooth-structure comparison requires a topologically equivalent pair and a complete invariant distinction under allowable reindexings.
2. The conditional positive-b2+ route requires an actual closed simply-connected nonvanishing example.
3. Closing boundary examples requires a cap-survival theorem or calculation; a 4-handle theorem or gluing formula alone is insufficient.
4. The Lee route requires a finite integer-filtration witness in a setting where the ordinary comparison and final handle maps apply.
5. A cable route requires full-tail survival information. Stabilization and the null-homologous Gluck family supply the stated obstructions to particular searches, not a general negative answer.

The minor clarifications about homogeneous filtration witnesses, source-level Gluck wording, and vanishing odd-intersection sectors are documented above. None changes the frozen conclusions. Publication as a draft investigation should retain LIMITATIONS.md and the exact source-version boundary. The accepted status of Ren-Willis must not be represented as inspection of its later accepted body.
