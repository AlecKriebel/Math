## Conventions that remain essential

1. Nets are nontrivial, irreducible, diffeomorphism-covariant, and finite-index in the strong-additive, split setting specified in PROOFS.md. Complete rationality supplies finite DHR data; it does not make the full soliton category a fusion category.
2. All Hilbert spaces are separable. This is a load-bearing hypothesis for the absorbing-object theorem, as Henriques Remark 4.17 explains.
3. Soliton actions are normal on all allowed cut-circle intervals, including intervals that terminate at either side of the cut. Restricting only to relatively compact intervals would change the comparison problem.
4. The audited equivalences preserve complex-linear *-structure and have unitary monoidal constraints. The Henriques-Penneys theorem provides the stronger bi-involutive, positive equivalence in its own scope. This audit does not silently claim that every arbitrary monoidal functor is normal or preserves all that structure.
5. A net isomorphism is an isomorphism of the covariant net, not a collection of unrelated abstract isomorphisms of local factors. An abstract tensor equivalence need not preserve a chosen embedding into Bim(R) or be induced by a fixed unitary on vacuum spaces.

## Route 1 and the center obstruction

### Necessary invariant

Lemma 1.1 correctly transports central half-braidings through a unitary strong monoidal equivalence. The two tensor constraints are in the correct order; the monoidal quasi-inverse and counit extend the definition from essential-image objects to every target object. Naturality, the half-braiding hexagon, and unit conditions transport. Henriques Theorem A then supplies the required braided equivalence of representation categories. No converse follows.

### Holomorphic negative control

Lemma 1.2 is valid for the stated nontrivial finite-index holomorphic net H. Henriques Remark 1.2 gives the separable Hilbert completion of the finite DHR category. Holomorphicity therefore gives Rep(H) equivalent to Hilb, and Theorem A gives Z(T(H)) equivalent to Hilb. Naturality against maps from the tensor unit forces any Hilb half-braiding to be the ordinary flip, giving Z(Hilb) equivalent to Hilb. Henriques-Penneys 1511.05226 Theorem A, applied to finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, supplies Hilb as a bicommutant category.

The inequivalence argument uses an actual endomorphism algebra, not an invariant of a chosen embedding. Henriques Section 4.3 identifies End(Omega) with the local algebra on the unused triangle edge; it is a type III factor. A fully faithful *-equivalence to Hilb would give a unital *-isomorphism onto B(K) for a nonzero Hilbert space K. A rank-one projection in B(K) would pull back to a nonzero minimal projection. A minimal projection is finite, while a type III factor has no nonzero finite projections. Contradiction.

This argument does not require an equivalence to preserve the name or construction of Omega, its chosen spatial representation, or its absorbing structure. Its image may be any nonzero object; its endomorphism algebra still must be preserved. Nor does the contradiction require assuming normality of the putative *-isomorphism. Thus this control is stronger than a failure of embedding-preserving equivalence. It refutes unrestricted center-only reconstruction inside bicommutant categories; it does not refute the original cross-net conjecture.

**Residual dependency:** a genuine center-to-soliton lifting or independent construction of a full tensor equivalence for a candidate pair. No such theorem is supplied.

## Route 2 and holomorphic tensor powers

Kawahigashi-Longo Example 2.8 explicitly constructs a holomorphic central-charge-8 net. The source's expected identification with a level-one loop-group net is not used as a proved assertion. This separation is correct.

For a finite tensor product, the two-interval inclusions tensor and their finite Jones indices multiply. Tensor products preserve the relevant split and strong-additivity properties. Thus the powers remain completely rational with mu-index 1, so each full locally normal representation category is equivalent to Hilb. Finite-index center theorems then identify their centers.

The central charge is additive: the Virasoro generators on distinct tensor factors commute, so the central coefficients add to 8m. A covariant vacuum-preserving net isomorphism intertwines the projective conformal action and hence its central-extension class. Equivalently, after the vacuum/Mobius normalization, the Virasoro central term is invariant. Consequently different positive integers m give nonisomorphic covariant conformal nets. The argument does not infer nonisomorphism from an unproved VOA comparison or from the local-factor isomorphism class.

The E8 matrix is independently verified by signed permutation determinants, with Bareiss cross-checks: leading minors 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 1. Symmetric integral entries and even diagonal make the lattice even; positive leading minors prove positive definiteness; determinant 1 proves unimodularity. Direct sums have rank 8m and determinant 1. These checks support the finite lattice sanity check, not the construction of a conformal net or equivalence of solitons. Dong-Xu Corollary 3.19 supplies the relevant lattice-net theorem.

**Residual dependency:** an essentially surjective fully faithful unitary monoidal comparison between T(H_m) and T(H_n). Equal centers do not provide it. No formula for the full soliton category of a tensor-product net is proved. The packet properly rejects T(H) equivalent to Hilb and rejects a universal stabilization assertion that includes the trivial net; that boundary counterexample does not decide nontrivial-net stabilization.

## Route 3 and Morita transfer

Henriques-Penneys 2004.08271 Theorem B/Theorem 6.8 applies to fully faithful representations of Morita-equivalent unitary fusion categories in hyperfinite factors that are both type II or both type III_1. Its equivalence respects bi-involution and positivity. It is not a theorem classifying arbitrary net soliton categories by DHR data.

The finite model is sound. The one-simple-object Hilbert-space module category for Vec_G is indecomposable. Its module endofunctors are determined by a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with coherent unitary G-action; module natural transformations are intertwining maps. This gives the standard Morita equivalence to Rep(G), with the harmless tensor-order convention resolved by the symmetric structure on Rep(G). For S3, the three displayed characters are independently reconstructed from all six permutations and are orthonormal. Squared dimensions sum to six. Vec_S3 has six simple objects and Rep(S3) has three, ruling out tensor equivalence while leaving Morita equivalence intact.

Composition of independently established identifications T(A) equivalent to C' and T(B) equivalent to D' with the cited theorem would work. No such identifications are established. Substituting Rep_f(A) for C merely because it is a center would assume the missing step. Substituting T(A) for a fusion category violates the theorem's hypothesis. The five-route packet does neither.

The inspected Henriques-Penneys-Tener preprint treats finite-depth objects in the fusion-commutant class. Its broader statement appears as a conjecture. Even a finite-depth-object classification would not by itself provide a functor on every object and morphism of an arbitrary full soliton category, with all required tensor constraints.

**Residual dependency:** realize the full soliton categories as the specific commutant categories covered by the theorem, and verify the ambient representation and factor hypotheses. The source theorem alone does not discharge this dependency.

## Route 4 and the normal extension predicate

The conditional criterion in Proposition 4.1 is valid. This section makes explicit the operator actions and tensor coherence implicit in its short proof. It is an audit expansion, not the construction of a map between the proposed nets.

### Exact predicate and morphisms

Fix, for each net, the parametrizations used in Henriques Section 4.1. They identify A(I34) with R_A and A(I12) with R_A^op, including the orientation reversal. A normal R_A-R_A bimodule K then carries the prescribed commuting representations sigma34 and sigma12. Restrict sigma12 to A(I2) and sigma34 to A(I3). Define P_A(K) to mean that there exists a **normal unital** representation pi23 of A(I23) whose restrictions are exactly these two actions.

It is insufficient to know that the two restricted actions are separately normal or that their ranges commute. The condition concerns extension to the specified joined-interval von Neumann algebra in the given representation. It is also insufficient to identify abstract factors or merely count possible actions.

Strong additivity makes such an extension unique when it exists. Henriques Lemma 4.2, through its gluing lemma, supplies the remaining allowed interval actions and the essential-image characterization. Lemma 4.1 makes this image full: a bimodule intertwiner commutes with the half-interval actions and, by strong additivity and normality, with every allowed interval action. Lemma 4.4 supplies closure under Connes fusion. These are explicit external inputs; none is encoded by the arithmetic checker.

### Transport of both actions

For a normal *-isomorphism theta: R_A to R_B, define F_theta on the same Hilbert space by

    lambda_F(s) = lambda_K(theta^{-1}(s)),
    rho_F(s^op) = rho_K((theta^{-1}(s))^op).

Both actions are normal and commute. On intertwiners the functor is the identical bounded operator. Transport along theta^{-1} is its inverse. The predicate comparison must then be checked using the **B** parametrizations and **B** interval inclusions on this transported bimodule; no compatibility with the A interval subalgebras is automatic. The quantifier is every separable bimodule K, in both directions:

    P_A(K) if and only if P_B(F_theta(K)).

Checking only vacuum, irreducible, finite-index, finite-depth, or a selected family of solitons would not establish this hypothesis. Since every B bimodule is a transported A bimodule, the biconditional gives essential surjectivity onto the full predicate-defined image.

### Connes fusion and naturality

Let U_theta: L2(R_A) to L2(R_B) be the standard-form unitary implementing theta on both the left and right actions. In the bounded-operator model of Connes fusion, a right-bounded vector is T in Hom_{-R_A}(L2(R_A), K). It is transported to T U_theta^{-1} in Hom_{-R_B}(L2(R_B), F_theta(K)). Define the comparison

    J_{K,L}: F_theta(K) fusion_{R_B} F_theta(L) -> F_theta(K fusion_{R_A} L)
    J_{K,L}((T U_theta^{-1}) tensor eta) = T tensor eta.

This respects balanced relations. If lambda_A(a) = S* T, then

    (S U_theta^{-1})* (T U_theta^{-1}) = U_theta lambda_A(a) U_theta^{-1}
                                                   = lambda_B(theta(a)).

The transported action on F_theta(L) sends theta(a) to the original action of a. Hence the defining relative tensor-product inner products agree exactly. The dense algebraic comparison descends through the null space and extends isometrically to the completions; applying the inverse transport proves it is onto. It intertwines the outside left action on K and right action on L.

For bimodule maps f: K to K' and g: L to L', both naturality paths send the displayed elementary tensor to (fT) tensor g(eta), so the comparison is natural. Take unit comparison L2(R_B) to F_theta(L2(R_A)) to be U_theta^{-1}; the unit equations are the standard left/right multiplication identifications. The associator for Connes fusion is characterized by the bounded multilinear tensor model. On this dense model, both composite comparisons in the associativity square perform the same change of standard-form variables on each factor. They agree, hence agree on the completion. Together with the unit equations this gives the monoidal coherence, including the pentagon. Adjoints and unitary constraints are preserved.

The predicate hypothesis allows restriction of this equivalence and its tensor constraints to the full soliton images. Thus Proposition 4.1 gives a sufficient spatial transport strategy. Failure to find theta is not a theorem against abstract equivalences lacking this chosen-embedding compatibility. The proposition's stated conclusion is unitary tensor equivalence; one should not replace it by a stronger positive bicommutant equivalence without checking the corresponding additional structures.

### Normality of equivalences

There is no hidden need to allow singular endomorphism-algebra maps in the packet's *-equivalence arguments. A full faithful complex-linear *-functor induces unital *-isomorphisms on endomorphism von Neumann algebras. Such an isomorphism is an order isomorphism, hence preserves suprema of bounded increasing positive nets and is normal. For a Hom(X,Y) space, use its off-diagonal corner in End(X direct-sum Y). The functor preserves a finite orthogonal biproduct, and the induced linking-algebra isomorphism is normal; restricting to the corner gives normality on Hom(X,Y). This applies to the W*-categories here. It does not assert that an arbitrary non-full *-functor is normal.

### Same factor is insufficient

The A1 lattice discriminant is 2; the E8 discriminant is 1. Dong-Xu gives two versus one irreducible DHR sectors. Both local-factor families are hyperfinite type III_1 in the stated setting. If a factor isomorphism automatically preserved the full predicate, Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 1.1 would force their representation categories to be equivalent, contradicting the different simple counts. Thus the abstract factor isomorphism class cannot replace the predicate hypothesis. This argument uses the standard local-factor structure theorem as an external input; lattice determinants alone do not prove it.

**Residual dependency:** exhibit theta and prove the universal, two-sided predicate condition for a nonisomorphic candidate pair. No such theta is constructed.

## Route 5 and proper solitons

The cut diffeomorphisms can be built as stated: a positive smooth derivative with prescribed nonzero endpoint values, constant near the endpoints, is normalized by its integral without changing their ratio. Integrating yields a cut-interval diffeomorphism with all required one-sided derivatives. An allowed interval can have at most one endpoint at the cut; its restricted map extends to a smooth circle diffeomorphism. Compatible normal actions follow from local covariance and unitary conjugation. Irreducibility follows from the vacuum net's punctured-circle irreducibility because the map permutes the allowed intervals.

Del Vecchio-Iovieno-Tanimoto Section 2.2 explicitly includes half-lines. Proposition 3.5 gives irreducible index-one solitons. Theorem 3.6 proves properness for unequal one-sided derivative ratio and equivalence exactly when ratios agree. The packet correctly relies on the theorem's modular argument rather than purporting to derive inequivalence from abstract type III factor structure. The assumptions are diffeomorphism covariance and the source's nontrivial net setting; the packet's finite-index strong-additive setting is within the intended standard covariant setting. The trivial net must not be included.

Ratios different from 1 already form an uncountable set, so there are uncountably many proper simple soliton classes. Allowing ratio 1 adds a vacuum-equivalent class and does not weaken the conclusion. A separable Hilbert completion of a fusion category with n simple classes still has exactly n simple classes: its objects have multiplicity spaces, and a nonzero multiplicity space larger than one dimension gives a nontrivial endomorphism projection. An equivalence preserves endomorphism algebras and simple isomorphism classes. Hence no such completion can be the full T(A). This does not contradict the existence of an absorbing object with a type III endomorphism algebra.

The Z/2 associator control is also correct. The exponent identity verifies all 16 pentagon instances, and normalization holds whenever one argument is zero. A monoidal equivalence must induce the identity automorphism of Z/2. After normalizing its unit constraint, its scalar 2-cochain has beta(0,a)=beta(a,0)=1. At (1,1,1), its coboundary is 1 for every possible nonzero beta(1,1), while omega is -1. Thus equal simple labels and fusion multiplication do not imply tensor equivalence. The independent checker reconstructs the coboundary cancellation formally; it does not rely on testing finitely many candidate phases.

**Residual dependencies:** essential surjectivity onto all solitons, identification of every Hom space, and coherent unitary monoidal constraints. Geometric labels alone give none of these. The finite Z/2 example is a logical negative control, not an assertion of that particular obstruction in the conformal-net candidates.
