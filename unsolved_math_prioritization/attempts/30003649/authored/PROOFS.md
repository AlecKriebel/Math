## From the finite certificate to the rational obstruction

Work in F = Q(α), where α is the positive real root of t³ − 2. Eisenstein at 2 makes 1, α, α² a rational basis. The checker verifies an orthogonal real 7-by-7 matrix O, a symmetric Q with trace zero, and the decomposition O = B₀ + αB₁ + α²B₂ with rational Bₖ. Let Cᵢ be the 7-by-3 matrix whose columns are column i of these three Bₖ, and write v = (1,α,α²)ᵀ. Thus uᵢ = Cᵢv is column i of O.

Each checked Cᵢ has rank three. The ray R uᵢ contains no nonzero rational vector: if uᵢ = s w for a nonzero rational vector w, selecting a nonzero coordinate of w gives s ∈ F. Comparing its three basis coefficients makes every column of Cᵢ a scalar multiple of w, contradicting rank three.

The checker also verifies Hᵢ = CᵢᵀQCᵢ, Hᵢv = 0, and positivity of its first leading principal entry and leading 2-by-2 determinant. These imply Hᵢ is positive semidefinite of rank two. Indeed, write its leading block as D, its final column above the diagonal as b, and v = (w,α²). The kernel identity gives b = −Dw/α² and the remaining entry wᵀDw/α⁴. Its quadratic form on (z,t) is therefore (z−tw/α²)ᵀD(z−tw/α²), with kernel precisely Rv.

The rational triangle W has first row (1,1,1), and the checked positive vector λ satisfies Wλ = v. Define Gᵢ as the cone generated by the three columns of CᵢW, and K as the sum of these seven cones. Each uᵢ belongs to Gᵢ. A checked rational functional is strictly positive on all 21 generators, so K is pointed. It is full dimensional because it contains the orthonormal basis u₁,…,u₇.

The quadratic form q(x) = xᵀQx is nonnegative on each Gᵢ. Its zeros there are exactly the nonnegative uᵢ-ray: injectivity of Cᵢ reduces the statement to Hᵢ, and the first coordinate of a nonzero conic combination of the columns of W is positive. For i ≠ j, all nine checked generator pairings between Gᵢ and Gⱼ are strictly positive. Consequently xᵢᵀQxⱼ > 0 for any nonzero xᵢ ∈ Gᵢ and xⱼ ∈ Gⱼ. Expanding q(Σxᵢ) shows it vanishes only if at most one summand is nonzero, and that summand lies on its distinguished ray. In particular q has no nonzero rational zero in K.

Every facet of the full-dimensional, finitely generated, seven-dimensional cone K contains six linearly independent input generators. Enumerating all six-element subsets therefore captures every facet normal. The independently written nullspace routine uses checked integer elimination and verifies every resulting null vector against its input. A normal is retained exactly when it has a nonnegative orientation on all generators. The resulting 444 primitive rows, checked against the public R, give K = {x : Rx ≥ 0}. Completeness follows from the standard finite-polyhedral halfspace representation; a facet omitted by the enumeration would still have six independent input generators and hence would have been enumerated. The separately checked rank of R is seven.

Set A = RRᵀ. Because RO ≥ 0 and OOᵀ = I, A is completely positive. Suppose there were a rational nonnegative C of any finite width with CCᵀ = A. For y ∈ ker Rᵀ, the identity 0 = yᵀAy = Σⱼ(yᵀcⱼ)² puts every column cⱼ in range R. The rational left inverse L = (RᵀR)⁻¹Rᵀ therefore gives X = LC with RX = C, XXᵀ = I and rational columns xⱼ in K. Then

0 = trace Q = trace(QXXᵀ) = Σⱼq(xⱼ).

Every term is nonnegative, hence every xⱼ is a rational zero of q and must vanish. This contradicts XXᵀ = I. The argument uses no upper bound on factor width.

Since rank A = 7 < 444, a nonzero z ∈ ker A exists. For every ε > 0, zᵀ(A−εzzᵀ)z < 0; hence A−εzzᵀ is not completely positive. Together with A ∈ CP, this proves boundary membership. The seven-column real factor proves cp-rank at most seven, and ordinary rank proves the reverse inequality. Strict positivity of every A entry was checked exactly. It also follows from the facet incidences: each facet has exactly six generator zeros, so a row of RO can have at most two zero coordinates among seven. Every pair of rows has overlapping positive support.

These arguments establish the complete rational counterexample from the predicates recomputed by verify_certificate.py. The mathematical construction is existing work; the checker and this verification presentation assert no novelty.

## Complete order-two integral existence proof

The existence theorem is due to Laffey and Šmigoc. Here is a self-contained descent proof without a factor-width bound. Let A have rows (a,b),(b,c), with nonnegative integers a,b,c and ac ≥ b². Complete positivity implies these hypotheses, and the argument proves the stronger assertion for every such doubly nonnegative A.

If a ≥ b and c ≥ b, take a−b copies of (1,0)ᵀ, b copies of (1,1)ᵀ, and c−b copies of (0,1)ᵀ. Their outer products sum to A.

Otherwise suppose a < b. Necessarily a > 0. Define

A′ = [[a,b−a],[b−a,c+a−2b]], U = [[1,0],[1,1]].

Then A = UA′Uᵀ. Congruence preserves positive semidefiniteness. Also b−a > 0 and c+a−2b ≥ b²/a+a−2b = (b−a)²/a > 0, so A′ is entrywise nonnegative and integral. Its trace is a+c+a−2b, smaller than trace A by 2b−a > 0. If instead c < b, interchange the coordinates and apply the same step.

Strong induction on the nonnegative integral trace now gives an integral nonnegative factor A′ = DDᵀ. The nonnegative integral matrix UD factors A. The zero matrix is the empty-column base case, or may use one zero column. Thus every input terminates at the diagonally dominant case. No denominator, scaling, nonsingularity or finite width bound has been assumed. integer2_controls.py implements this proof using three compressed integer multiplicities, each explicitly expandable into repeated columns.

## Why the integral dimension restriction matters

Let M have rows (2,3,1), (3,5,3), (1,3,5). It equals BBᵀ for

B = (1/2) [[2,2,0,0],[3,3,1,1],[1,1,3,3]].

This rational factor is checked exactly. Suppose an integral nonnegative C factors M. Its first row has squared length two, hence precisely two entries equal to one and every other entry zero. The first-second inner product forces the corresponding two entries x,y of row two to sum to three. The second diagonal gives x²+y² ≤ 5, which forces (x,y) = (1,2) or (2,1); their squares already sum to five, so every other entry of row two is zero. The first-third inner product makes the corresponding row-three entries sum to one. Their product with row two is therefore at most two, contradicting M₂₃ = 3. This excludes every finite width. The example is historical, not new.

## Rational weights and scaling

For positive integers p,q, p/q is the sum of pq copies of the rational square (1/q)². Hence a term (p/q)xxᵀ with rational nonnegative x can be replaced by pq identical columns x/q. This proves that finite rational nonnegative weighted rank-one decompositions and rational nonnegative Gram factorizations are equivalent, with no width restriction.

For any positive rational t, A has a rational nonnegative Gram factor if and only if tA does: apply the preceding expansion to weights t and 1/t in the two directions. In particular an integral factor for any positive rational multiple of Holden's A would give a rational factor for A, which is impossible.

More generally, A has a rational nonnegative Gram factor if and only if d²A has an integral nonnegative Gram factor for some positive integer d. Clear all factor denominators in one direction and divide by d in the other. Existence of an integral factor for any positive integer multiple is also equivalent: the reverse direction uses rational weight 1/m, and the forward direction uses d². This is different from an integral factor for A itself, as M shows.

Finally, inability to use a square or minimal-width rational factor is not an obstruction to the original problem. The scalar [2] has real cp-rank one but rational factor [1,1]. Every rational positive scalar has some rational Gram factor by the same rational-weight expansion.
