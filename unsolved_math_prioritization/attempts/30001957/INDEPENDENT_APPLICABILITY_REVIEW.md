## Substantive claim audit

### Original target and “other”: PASS

The exact source is OWR03/2012, Open Problems, Problem 1, printed p.125, PDF index 48. Its displayed known example is a perturbation on T² of the matrix with rows (2,2) and (2,3). It gives negative inverse-SRB and positive forward-SRB production, then requests further folded hyperbolic examples. The certificate takes the allowed negative alternative and a proper repeller in a different ambient phase space. It does not recycle that displayed toral example as its answer. [OWR primary source](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46375?nt=1)

### Concrete 2010 family and hypotheses: PASS

The certificate copies Example 4, pp.817–818, with a change of parameter name only. I visually checked the matrix with rows (2,1) and (2,2), the toral sine and cosine-squared terms, and both degree-two homogeneous coordinates. Example 3 uses a different matrix; the certificate correctly uses Example 4's matrix.

Definition 1, p.803, makes transitivity and critical-set avoidance part of the repeller setting. Proposition 3, p.805, preserves connected hyperbolic repellers and their within-repeller preimage counts under sufficiently small perturbations. Example 4, p.818, explicitly supplies stable and unstable directions, the perturbed geometry, and the inverse measure. Theorems 2–3, pp.807–808 and p.814, together with Corollary 1, establish the stable-potential equilibrium measure, its inverse physical interpretation, and stable conditional absolute continuity. The certificate's unspecified sufficiently small positive parameter is appropriate; no numerical threshold is falsely claimed. [2010 primary paper](https://imar.ro/~mihailes/Physical-JSP.pdf)

### 2013 specialization, degree, measure, and strict sign: PASS

The Example on pp.886–887 specializes exactly with k=2, ε=t, and g=g_t. Corollary 3, p.887, states strict negative production for its inverse SRB measure. The small-perturbation hypothesis is retained. The degree is k|det A|=4, counting preimages inside the repeller. Definition 1, p.883, and Proposition 1(a), p.884, identify folding entropy as log d and production as folding entropy minus the ambient log-Jacobian integral. Thus the certificate's log4 formula has the correct measure, determinant, degree, and sign. The cited example is explicitly non-Anosov and fractal, with both stable and unstable directions. [2013 primary paper](https://www.imar.ro/~purice/Inst/2013/EMihailescu/JSP-Entprod2013-final.pdf)

### Printed caveats: PASS

Visual inspection confirms the degree-k/degree-two homogeneous-coordinate mismatch on p.887. The quadratic specialization avoids it without claiming a source correction. The missing “not” and determinant/logarithmic-determinant issue in the separate Corollary 2 proof are correctly disclosed; that toral argument is not the certificate's invoked sign theorem. This review checks application of published statements, not a reconstruction or independent validation of all their foundational proofs.

### Chronology and added content: PASS

The 2010 article already contains the geometry and inverse measures. Its complete extracted text has no occurrence of “production”; inspection of its example and theorem statements finds no strict entropy-production conclusion for the family.

The OWR-cited 2011 preprint's Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 concern toral maps. Corollary 4(b), pp.14–15, is precisely the signed toral example repeated in the January 2012 problem. Its general formulas do not themselves identify the certificate's strict sign. [2011 primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.2342v1)

The later strict sign is supplied by the 2013 article, published online December 22, 2012. Consequently the certified answer pairs previously published geometry with the subsequently published sign theorem. It does not claim that the geometry was new in 2013. This suffices for “other” relative to the signed toral example actually presented in OWR. Version 2 makes this essential distinction explicit.
