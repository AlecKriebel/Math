# Substantive turn 3: a precise obstruction to preserving the local quotient globally

**Partial, unreviewed; original target unresolved.** Date: 2026-10-01. This turn tests whether the relative genus-two example from turn 2 can be completed to a positive identity relation while preserving the same pointwise-invariant Heisenberg quotient. The answer to that restricted completion strategy is no.

## 1. Why a positive completion changes the problem

The local example has one positive separating twist as total monodromy. An inverse twist would close it algebraically, but would be negative and hence would leave the source target. Replacing an inverse by a positive word is not enough: a putative global section can choose its boundary class at the cut, and the new factors have additional allowable point-pushing moves. The relative boundary value v^2 cannot simply be imposed on every possible section of the completed fibration.

The following result proves that the special invariant quotient used in turn 2 cannot survive an essential positive completion in genus two.

## 2. Restricted global no-go theorem

Let rho be the genus-two integral Heisenberg quotient from turn 2:

    rho(a)=X, rho(b)=Y, rho(c)=Y, rho(d)=X,
    [a,b][c,d]=1, [X,Y]=Z.

There is no nonempty factorization of the identity into positive twists about essential curves in the closed genus-two surface such that **each** chosen pointed twist action phi_i satisfies rho composed with phi_i = rho.

### Proof

Use the ordered homology basis (a,b,c,d), with a intersect b and c intersect d positively once. The induced map on abelianizations is

    p(A,B,C,D) = (A+D, B+C).

Its kernel L is generated by a-d and b-c. These two vectors have intersection zero, since

    (a-d) dot (b-c) = (a dot b) + (d dot c) = 1-1 = 0.

Thus L is a Lagrangian plane in H_1(S;R).

If phi_i is a twist about a nonseparating curve of homology v_i, the Picard–Lefschetz formula gives

    p(phi_i(x)-x) = plus/minus (x dot v_i) p(v_i).

The hypothesis rho phi_i = rho implies p phi_i = p. Because v_i is primitive, some integral x intersects it once, and therefore p(v_i)=0. For a separating curve v_i=0 already. Hence every v_i lies in L.

Let Omega be the intersection matrix in this basis. With one fixed convention for a positive twist, its matrix is

    T_i = I - v_i v_i^t Omega.

The opposite uniform sign convention gives the same conclusion. Since L is isotropic, all products of two correction terms vanish. Therefore

    product_i T_i = I - (sum_i v_i v_i^t) Omega.

The closed-surface identity relation forces this matrix to be I. Since Omega is invertible, sum_i v_i v_i^t=0. This sum is positive semidefinite over R, so every v_i=0. Every essential vanishing cycle would therefore be separating.

The established no-Torelli-fibration theorem excludes a nontrivial relatively minimal positive Lefschetz fibration over the sphere with only separating vanishing cycles. Apply [Ivan Smith, *Lefschetz fibrations and the Hodge bundle*](https://doi.org/10.2140/gt.1999.3.211), Geometry & Topology 3 (1999), Theorem 6.2, pp. 231–232. The correspondence with positive identity factorizations gives the contradiction. Inessential twist factors, if allowed, are identities and may be discarded; they cannot supply a nontrivial completion of the local separating twist. QED.

This argument excludes only the specified pointwise-invariant quotient strategy. It does not exclude a quotient on which the twists act nontrivially, a different quotient, a higher-genus construction, or a positive solution of the original lifting question.

## 3. Exact global test case recovered

The complete publisher's online-first copy of [Baykur–Hamada, *Lefschetz fibrations with arbitrary signature*](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1326), subsequently JEMS 26 (2024), 2837–2895, was recovered. The accessed PDF uses its own pages 1–59, so the following are PDF/online-first page numbers, not the final journal pagination.

- Proposition 8, pp. 10–12, identifies the order of the abelianized point-pushing obstruction modulo the vanishing-cycle lattice with divisibility of the fiber class. This credits and contextualizes the first-stage quantity in turn 1.
- Theorem 14, pp. 33–38, gives the actual 48-twist genus-nine positive identity relation, equation (15), with curves in Figure 23. The total space has H_1 = Z^7 plus Z/4 plus Z/2.
- Equation (14), p. 33, gives a pointed lift with one explicit point-pushing insertion P_alpha. Its two contributing loops are depicted in Figure 22. In the displayed factorization, write the relation as U P_alpha V = 1. Cyclically moving U yields the positive word VU = P_alpha^(-1) in the pointed group. Thus the residual is determined by the actual global construction, not chosen freely as in the relative example.
- The authors explicitly calculate that the abelian class of this residual lies in the vanishing-cycle lattice, giving a primitive fiber/pseudosection. This does not prove a section. Their introduction explicitly says they have not identified a section of that example.

Only the relevant passages and figures have been inspected; based fundamental-group words for all 48 curves have not been reconstructed. The long homology table cannot substitute for those nonabelian words. In particular, no finite-quotient exclusion of all curve lifts has been calculated for this fibration.
