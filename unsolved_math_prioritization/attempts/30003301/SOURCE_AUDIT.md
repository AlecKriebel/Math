# Exact-source checkpoint

## Target

Numeric ID 30003301, source key OWR-15177-019, current queue rank 229. In a closed oriented genus-g surface, a prescribed ordered positive Dehn-twist factorization of the identity is given. Can each curve be isotoped, after forgetting one marked point, so that the same number and order of right twists multiply to the identity in the once-pointed mapping class group?

The exact primary source is Mustafa Korkmaz's **Question 13**, section 14 of the problem session, printed p. **3189**, PDF page **41**, of [Surface Bundles, OWR 56/2016](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46660), DOI 10.4171/OWR/2016/56. The workshop took place 4–10 December 2016. The imported report's title uses 2017; the report itself is 56/2016 and volume 13 (2016), pp. 3149–3195. The statement was read in the full PDF and visually verified.

The puncture is a marked point, not a boundary circle fixed pointwise. Each curve can be moved independently through an isotopy of the closed surface that need not fix the marked point. The question does not ask for one homomorphic splitting of the forgetful map, nor for lifts chosen uniformly for all relations. Arbitrary lifts of twists are insufficient: the lifted factors must still be individual right Dehn twists about the respective closed-surface isotopy classes.

## Earlier and current primary sources

Korkmaz's chapter *Problems on homomorphisms of mapping class groups* in [Problems on Mapping Class Groups and Related Topics](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf), Problem 2.8, printed pp. 88–89, PDF pp. 95–96, gives the same problem with explicit right-handed twists and genus g at least two. It identifies the geometric question as existence of a section for every Lefschetz fibration over the sphere. The genus restriction is explicit there but not repeated in the brief OWR statement. The substantive unresolved investigation will concern g at least two; low-genus and empty/trivial factors must be treated separately rather than silently discarded.

The 2026 author-hosted preliminary [K3 problem list](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/websites.umass.edu/dist/b/22144/files/2026/04/K3-problem-list-watermarked.pdf), Problem 2.22, pp. 103–104, again states the section question and expressly gives the equivalent positive-factorization formulation. It distinguishes mixed-sign/achiral counterexamples and notes a genus-9 Baykur–Hamada fibration as a possible test case. That is evidence of the current state of this literature, not a proof of a universal impossibility or a guarantee that no later solution exists.

Recent [Gompf, arXiv:2506.18066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18066), concerns achiral and other singular fibrations without sections. Its abstract was checked; its full proof has not yet been used. Mixed positive and negative twists cannot settle the positive source target. Likewise, known surface-bundle nonsection examples over a positive-genus base do not immediately provide sphere-base positive relations of the required kind.
