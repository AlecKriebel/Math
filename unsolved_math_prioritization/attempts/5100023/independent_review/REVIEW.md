## 3. Chord signs, perimeter and first variation

With the caustic on the left, the origin lies strictly to the left of each chord, so $\det(A,B)>0$. The eccentric-angle difference can therefore be chosen in $(0,\pi)$, giving $0<\delta<\pi/2$ and positive $U=\cos\delta$, $V=\sin\delta$.

The chord equation and the support function of the inner ellipse give

$$U^2=1-\lambda D^2,\qquad V^2=\lambda D^2,
\qquad D^2=C^2/a^2+S^2/b^2.$$

Since $\sqrt{a^2S^2+b^2C^2}=abD$, the positive chord length is exactly $2ab\sqrt\lambda D^2$. Summing and using

$$D^2=\frac1{b^2}-\frac{c^2C^2}{a^2b^2}$$

gives the displayed mean $H=N^{-1}\sum C_i^2$, with the stated normalization of $L$ and $N$.

For the mixed sum, the first-variation argument is correctly applied **before** imposing caustic constraints on the comparison deformation. The derivative of polygon perimeter at a vertex is the incoming unit direction minus the outgoing unit direction; the billiard reflection law makes it normal to the outer ellipse. Hence every tangent vertex deformation has zero first variation, even for a self-intersecting star orbit.

Under the simultaneous eccentric-angle shift, each $\delta_i$ is fixed and each $s_i$ increases at unit speed. Differentiating the unconstrained chord-length expression and only then substituting its original caustic identity gives

$$\frac{d\ell_i}{d\varepsilon}
=\frac{2\sqrt\lambda\,c^2}{ab}S_iC_i.$$

All division factors are nonzero in the noncircular, nondegenerate setting, so $\sum S_iC_i=0$. There is no unproved symmetry assumption on the distribution of chord directions. The same stationarity applied to the actual smooth Poncelet family proves $dL/dt=0$. The oriented family is parameterized by the connected outer circle, so this constant is global; reversal of orientation gives the same perimeter.

## 4. Focal pair identity and denominator control

The antipedal line at $A$ is characterized by

$$(Q-A)\cdot(A-f)=0.$$

Writing $q=Q-f$, $A=m-v$ and $B=m+v$ yields the two equations in the candidate by adding and subtracting the original equations. Both coordinates in this system are absolute geometric quantities; the final addition of the focus is essential.

I independently solved the two line equations in **absolute coordinates**, added the solution for $(-A,-B)$, cleared denominators, and reduced each residual modulo

$$C^2+S^2=1,\qquad U^2+V^2=1,\qquad h^2=a^2-b^2,$$

with $\lambda=V^2/D^2$. Both residuals vanish identically. This verifies the complete vector identity

$$Q_f(A,B)+Q_f(-A,-B)
=\left(-2h+\frac{2hKC^2}{a^2(b^2-\lambda)},
\frac{2hKSC}{ab(b^2-\lambda)}\right).$$

The denominator analysis is also sound, rather than merely formal. Both foci lie strictly inside the inner ellipse because $c^2<a^2-\lambda$ when $\lambda<b^2$. A supporting tangent chord cannot contain a focus or the origin. More explicitly,

$$\det(A-f,B-f)=2bV(aU-hC),$$

and the opposite-edge determinant has $aU+hC$. Their product is positive because

$$U^2-\frac{h^2C^2}{a^2}=(b^2-\lambda)D^2>0.$$

Thus all the line intersections used in the proof are finite and unique. No limiting uniform bound as $\lambda\uparrow b^2$ is asserted.

Finally, summing the opposite-edge identity over **all** edges counts each antipedal vertex twice. Dividing by $2N$, substituting the two proven means, and retaining $h=\pm c$ gives exactly the claimed focal centroid. The signs and the factor of two check out.
