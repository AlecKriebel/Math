# A torsion-rank obstruction to the prescribed Alexander realization conjecture

Complete counterexample candidate to Ohtsuki–Turaev Conjecture 12.26, pending separate adversarial review. Two substantive approaches. No novelty or human peer-review claim.

## 1. Exact target and proposed counterexample

The primary statement is V. Turaev's Conjecture12.26 in Ohtsuki's *Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds*, Geometry & Topology Monographs4 (2002), printed p.542, PDF p.170: https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf . It asks whether a prescribed pair (H,Delta), with H a finitely generated abelian group of rank one, is realized by the first integral homology and Alexander polynomial of a closed connected oriented 3-manifold precisely when Delta is reciprocal up to an even power of t and Delta(1)=plus or minus the order of Tors H.

Crucially, the stated polynomial lies in Z[H/Tors H]=Z[t,t^(-1)]. It is the ordinary Alexander polynomial of the infinite cyclic cover associated to the primitive free-abelianization map. It is not a refined torsion retaining finite-group variables in Z[H], and no primitive-part normalization dividing away integer content is made. As usual, its unit ambiguity is plus or minus t^k.

Consider

H = Z direct-sum (Z/2)^3,
Delta(t) = t + 6 + t^(-1).

This polynomial satisfies Delta(t)=Delta(t^(-1)) and Delta(1)=8=|Tors H|. We prove that no closed connected oriented 3-manifold realizes this pair. More generally, for every prime p the pair

H_p=Z direct-sum (Z/p)^3,
Delta_p(t)=t+(p^3-2)+t^(-1)

has the same obstruction.

The obstruction is the following necessary condition, in which the zero polynomial is understood to have infinite vanishing order:

ord_(t=1)(Delta_M mod p) >= dim_(F_p)(Tors H_1(M;Z) tensor F_p).

Sections2–4 establish this condition from an integral square presentation, including finite torsion of the cut manifold and the exact specialization. No localization or cancellation of a (t-1) factor is used.

## 2. Topological square-presentation lemma

Let M be a closed connected oriented 3-manifold with H_1(M;Z)=Z direct-sum T, T finite. Let phi:pi_1(M)->Z be the primitive projection and let M_tilde be its infinite cyclic cover. Set Lambda=Z[t,t^(-1)].

**Lemma.** There is a square matrix A(t) over Lambda such that

H_1(M_tilde;Z) = coker A(t) as a Lambda-module,
coker A(1) = T as an abelian group,
and det A(t) represents Delta_M(t) up to a unit of Lambda.

**Proof, step 1: a connected cut.** Choose a connected oriented embedded nonseparating surface F representing the Poincaré dual of phi; write its genus as g. The standard integral codimension-one realization theorem supplies such a connected representative for a primitive class. It can be obtained from a regular level of a map to S^1 representing phi, using oriented surface surgeries to connect components; primitiveness is the hypothesis ensuring a connected nonseparating representative. Equivalently this is the usual connected Seifert-surface construction for a primitive class in a closed 3-manifold. This standard topological fact, rather than the corresponding assertion for arbitrary divisible classes, is used here.

Let X be M cut along a product collar of F. Then X is connected and compact with two boundary components F_+ and F_-, each of genus g. Let i_+,i_-:F->X use the same orientation inherited from F. The two image classes [F_+] and [F_-] in H_2(X;Q) are equal, because the oriented boundary of X is F_+ minus F_-. Hence i_+-i_- is zero on H_2(F;Q).

The Mayer–Vietoris sequence for re-gluing the two boundary components contains

H_2(F;Q) --(i_+-i_-)--> H_2(X;Q) --> H_2(M;Q).

The first map is zero, so the second is injective. Poincaré duality and b_1(M)=1 give b_2(M)=1, hence b_2(X)<=1. On the other hand, the pair sequence for (X,boundary X) shows b_2(X)>=1: H_3(X,boundary X;Q)=Q maps into H_2(boundary X;Q)=Q^2 with rank one, generated by its oriented boundary, and the quotient of dimension one injects into H_2(X;Q). Thus b_2(X)=1.

Doubling X along its boundary gives a closed oriented three-manifold of Euler characteristic zero, so chi(X)=chi(boundary X)/2=2-2g. Since b_0(X)=1 and b_3(X)=0, it follows that b_1(X)=2g. Therefore the full integral group has the form

H_1(X;Z)=Z^(2g) direct-sum (direct-sum_{j=1}^r Z/d_j),

with finite d_j>=2. This step identifies the free rank but does not discard the finite summand.

**Step 2: integral cyclic-cover sequence.** The cover M_tilde is assembled from copies X_k, k in Z, by gluing the positive boundary of one copy to the negative boundary of the next. The integral Mayer–Vietoris sequence for this locally finite union is the direct-sum sequence, equivalently the Lambda-module sequence

H_1(F;Z) tensor Lambda --(i_+-t i_-)--> H_1(X;Z) tensor Lambda
 --> H_1(M_tilde;Z) --> H_0(F;Z) tensor Lambda --(1-t)--> H_0(X;Z) tensor Lambda.

Both F and X are connected, so the last map is multiplication by 1-t on Lambda and is injective. Consequently the Alexander module is exactly the cokernel of the first displayed map. The inverse choice of deck generator merely replaces t by t^(-1), which does not affect the obstruction.

Choose a basis for H_1(F;Z)=Z^(2g) and generators for the displayed decomposition of H_1(X;Z). Present H_1(X;Z) tensor Lambda using 2g+r generators and r relations d_j e_(2g+j)=0. Lift the images of each of the 2g surface generators under i_+ and i_- to integral vectors in these generators. Adjoin the 2g columns given by their differences i_+-t i_-. Together with the r torsion columns, this gives a (2g+r)-by-(2g+r) matrix A(t). It presents the entire Alexander module integrally. A different choice of lift differs by a torsion column and does not change the presented module. There is no free generator from H_0, because 1-t was injective. Every finite torsion relation of H_1(X) has been retained.

**Step 3: exact specialization.** Evaluating this matrix at t=1 gives the abelian group

coker A(1) = H_1(X;Z) / (i_+-i_-)H_1(F;Z).

The ordinary integral re-gluing sequence is

H_1(F;Z) --(i_+-i_-)--> H_1(X;Z) --> H_1(M;Z)
 --> H_0(F;Z)=Z --0--> H_0(X;Z)=Z.

The connecting map H_1(M;Z)->Z is intersection with F, namely phi up to sign. It is surjective and its kernel is exactly T. Exactness therefore identifies coker A(1) with T, not just with a subgroup or a rational approximation to T.

Because T is finite, det A(1) is nonzero (with the empty matrix convention det=1). Therefore det A(t) is not identically zero. The square presentation has full rank over Q(t), so the Alexander module has no free Lambda summand. Its zeroth Fitting ideal is generated by det A(t), and its order over the Laurent polynomial UFD is the same determinant up to a unit. This is the ordinary integral Alexander polynomial. Any presentation stabilization by identity blocks only multiplies the determinant by a unit; no (t-1) factor is divided out. This proves all assertions. QED.

The cut construction and rational rank computation are also treated in Alcaraz, *On the Characterization Problem of Alexander Polynomials of Closed 3-Manifolds*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2042v1 , Section4.2. The proof above gives the needed integral presentation and specialization directly. We do not rely on a rational Seifert matrix, or on a theorem prescribing only the polynomial and first Betti number, to prescribe the full torsion group. For the Alexander-module/order convention, see Massuyeau, *An introduction to the abelian Reidemeister torsion of three-dimensional manifolds*, https://www.numdam.org/article/AMBP_2011__18_1_61_0.pdf , the section on the Alexander polynomial and Milnor torsion. A complete readable author preprint is also available at https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/massu.pdf , Section3.2 and equation(3.2); the downloaded Numdam PDF was malformed, so the preprint was used for full-text verification.

## 3. Algebraic vanishing-order lemma

**Lemma.** Let A(t) be any N-by-N integral Laurent matrix with finite coker A(1)=T. For a prime p, put r_p=dim_(F_p)(T tensor F_p). Then (t-1)^(r_p) divides det A(t) modulo p in F_p[t,t^(-1)].

**Proof.** Reduction and right exactness of tensor products identify coker(A(1) mod p) with T tensor F_p. Thus A(1) mod p has rank N-r_p. Choose constant invertible row and column matrices over F_p bringing it to diag(I_(N-r_p),0). Apply the same constant operations to A(t) modulo p. All entries in the last r_p rows then vanish at t=1, so each is divisible by t-1 in the Laurent ring. Factor one t-1 from each of these rows in the determinant. The row and column operations only multiply the determinant by nonzero constants. This proves the divisibility, also when the reduced determinant is zero. QED.

Combining this lemma with Section2 gives the stated necessary condition for every closed connected oriented M with b_1=1. Multiplication of Delta by plus or minus t^k leaves its order at t=1 unchanged, as does replacing t by t^(-1). Thus Alexander normalization and deck orientation cannot remove the obstruction.

## 4. Contradiction for the prescribed pairs

For T=(Z/p)^3, r_p=3. The symmetric Laurent polynomial Delta_p has Delta_p(1)=p^3, as required by the conjecture. But

t Delta_p(t) = t^2+(p^3-2)t+1 = (t-1)^2+p^3 t.

Modulo p this is exactly (t-1)^2, a nonzero polynomial of order two at t=1. Since t is a unit there, Delta_p modulo p also has order exactly two. Section3 requires order at least three. This is impossible.

In particular H=Z direct-sum (Z/2)^3 and Delta=t+6+t^(-1) meet both displayed conditions in Conjecture12.26 and are not realizable. This refutes their sufficiency for the literal prescribed-pair conjecture, while leaving their necessity and the already-known cyclic-torsion cases intact.

## 5. What is and is not checked

The proof concerns ordinary integral homology, the full free-abelian Alexander polynomial in Z[H/Tors H], and closed connected oriented three-manifolds. It imposes no irreducibility, hyperbolicity, fiberedness, knot-surgery, or smooth-versus-PL restriction beyond the standard three-manifold category. It does not address refined torsion in Z[H] or a problem where only |T|, rather than its isomorphism type, is prescribed.

The source audit has not established priority for the obstruction or counterexample. The algebraic checker tests the determinant-rank divisibility lemma over finite fields, the exact candidate polynomials and finite torsion ranks. Those computations do not certify the topological lemma; its full argument is in Section2 and must be independently reviewed before any claimed-result publication.
