# Author turn 5: supplying derived Hopficity through a finite central residue

## 1. A derived-subgroup theorem

**Theorem A.** Let G be a finitely generated Hopfian group for which Q=G/Z(G) is perfect and Hopfian. Put D=[G,G], C=Z(D), and A=G/D. Let m be the exponent of the torsion subgroup of the finitely generated abelian group A, with m=1 when A is torsion-free. If C/mC is finite, then D is Hopfian.

Here C is written additively where divisibility is discussed. It need not be finite or finitely generated. The finite-residue condition holds, for example, if C is finitely generated or if m=1. The hypothesis on Q is only perfectness and Hopficity in this theorem; the semisimple central quotient is imposed separately when applying the wreath-product results.

This is a sufficient condition. We do not claim that a derived subgroup of every finitely generated Hopfian group is Hopfian.

## 2. The central extension and its epimorphism

Write Z=Z(G). Since Q is perfect, G=D Z. Exactly as in turn 4, a finite generating set of G gives a finite generating set of D, and

    D=[D,D],  C=D intersect Z,  D/C=Q,  Z/C=A.                 (1)

Let psi:D->D be any epimorphism. It sends C into C, because it is surjective and C is the center. Hence it induces an epimorphism of Q, which is an automorphism by assumption. Consequently

    ker(psi) <= C,               psi(C)=C.                   (2)

The second equality deserves verification: if c in C, take d with psi(d)=c. The injectivity of the induced map on D/C forces d in C. Thus psi is onto on C, not merely center-preserving.

The induced endomorphism of the finite group C/mC is onto and therefore an automorphism. Choose N>=1 so that its N-th power is the identity. Write rho=psi^N|_C. Then

    rho(c)-c belongs to mC for every c in C.                  (3)

For m=1 the quotient is zero and N=1 is allowed. No finite generation of C is used in this step.

## 3. Extending that power across the center of G

Choose a decomposition of A into cyclic torsion summands of orders n_1,...,n_s and a free abelian part. Each n_i divides m. Choose lifts z_i in Z of the torsion generators and lifts w_j of the free generators. Put c_i=n_i z_i in C.

The abelian group Z is generated by C and these lifts, with the internal relations of C and exactly the additional relations

    n_i z_i=c_i.                                             (4)

To justify this presentation, reduce any proposed relation modulo C in the direct-sum presentation of A. The free coefficients must vanish and each torsion coefficient must be divisible by n_i; substitution of (4) reduces it to a relation already in C. In particular no splitting of Z->A is assumed.

By (3), rho(c_i)-c_i lies in mC and thus in n_i C. Choose b_i in C such that

    n_i b_i=rho(c_i)-c_i.                                    (5)

Define a homomorphism theta:Z->Z by

    theta|_C=rho,    theta(z_i)=z_i+b_i,    theta(w_j)=w_j.

Relations (4) are preserved, since n_i theta(z_i)=c_i+n_i b_i=rho(c_i). This defines theta on all of Z. It is onto: it induces the identity on Z/C=A and maps C onto C by (2). More explicitly, given z in Z, choose a preimage of its class under the induced identity, and correct the remaining C-error using surjectivity on C.

The maps psi^N:D->D and theta:Z->Z agree on C=D intersect Z. Their images commute because theta has values in Z. Therefore they define a homomorphism

    Theta:G=D Z -> G,         Theta(dz)=psi^N(d) theta(z).     (6)

Well-definedness follows directly: if dz=d'z', then d'^(-1)d=z'z^(-1) lies in C, where the two maps agree. Both restrictions are onto, so Theta is onto G. Hopficity of G makes Theta injective. In particular psi^N is injective on D, hence psi is injective. This proves Theorem A.

This is a finite-power extension argument. It does not say that psi itself extends across Z, nor that arbitrary central endomorphisms extend without preserving the relations (4).

## 4. Consequences for regular wreath products

**Theorem B.** Let G,H be finitely generated Hopfian groups, suppose G/Z(G) is a nontrivial finite product of nonabelian simple groups, and retain D,C,A,m as above. If C/mC is finite and A wr H is Hopfian, then G wr H is Hopfian.

Indeed Q is perfect and Hopfian by turn 2; Theorem A makes D Hopfian. Turn 4, Theorem A now applies.

Two concrete sufficient subcases are:

1. A is torsion-free. Then m=1, so the residue condition is automatic. The credited Bradford--Fournier-Facio Corollary 1.6 gives Hopficity of A wr H for every finitely generated Hopfian H. Thus Theorem B yields a positive result with no finiteness condition on the center.
2. C/mC is finite and H is sofic and Hopfian. The same credited corollary supplies the abelian-wreath hypothesis, so Theorem B applies. Finitely generated C is one sufficient way to satisfy its residue hypothesis.

More precisely one can substitute the prime-by-prime matrix direct-finiteness conditions from the credited Theorem 1.5 for the assumption on A wr H. For a nonsplit G no necessity of those conditions is claimed. The exact equivalence was established only for the split bases A times P in turn 4.

The case A=0 recovers the perfect-base central-quotient theorem of turn 3. These results concern a restricted family of central quotients; they do not settle the original question for arbitrary finitely generated Hopfian bases.

## 5. Why a power cannot always be extended by the same argument

Here is an explicit abelian extension showing the role of the finite-residue condition. This is an obstruction to an attempted proof step, not an example of a non-Hopfian regular wreath product.

Let

    C=direct-sum_(j>=0) Z e_j,
    sigma(e_0)=0,  sigma(e_(j+1))=e_j,

and fix an integer m>=2. The map sigma:C->C is onto and noninjective. Adjoin z with m z=e_0. The resulting abelian group is

    Z_tilde=Z z direct-sum direct-sum_(j>=1) Z e_j,

where C embeds by e_0=mz and by the other indicated basis elements. The quotient Z_tilde/C is cyclic of order m. For every N>=1,

    sigma^N(e_0)-e_0=-e_0 is not in mC.

Thus equation (5) cannot hold for an extension inducing the identity on Z_tilde/C. In fact no extension of sigma^N to Z_tilde can be onto at all: an extension theta would satisfy

    m theta(z)=theta(e_0)=sigma^N(e_0)=0.

Since Z_tilde is torsion-free, theta(z)=0. The other generators have images prescribed in C, so its entire image is contained in C and misses the nontrivial quotient. Such extensions do exist (send z to 0 and e_j to sigma^N(e_j)), but they are never onto.

Here C/mC is infinite. Surjectivity of its induced shift is not enough to make that shift invertible or periodic. This precisely invalidates a blind replacement of the finite-residue argument by surjectivity alone. We do not construct G with this C as Z(G'), do not assert G is Hopfian, and do not promote the abelian example to a counterexample to the original problem.

## 6. Exact controls and final scope

The checker verifies central relation lifting for unimodular integer maps and their finite residue orders, including cases where the chosen power is not the identity on C. It also checks noninjective surjective multiplication on finite fragments of Q/Z, where mC=C, and the explicit shift obstruction on basis vectors. Finite fragments are labeled as identity checks, not finite models proving surjectivity of the infinite shift. The infinite statements are proved above.

Five genuine author turns are complete. The original Kourovka 21.22 question remains unresolved. The central-quotient positive results, the basic-map theorems, and the faithful semisimple permutation-action result do not control arbitrary non-basic epimorphisms. The regular abelian-base problem already contains the credited stable-finiteness obstruction, and turn 4 carries that obstruction into nonabelian split bases. The 2026 non-Hopfian permutational construction has a nonfree action and does not answer the regular question. No sixth author search is undertaken, and no novelty claim is made for the partial results.
