# Author turn 1: centerless bases and base-preserving epimorphisms

## 1. Result and the remaining distinction

Write W=G^(H) semidirect H for the standard restricted wreath product, with left coordinate translation. A map is **basic** if it sends the restricted base B=G^(H) into B, as in Bradford--Fournier-Facio Definition2.1.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finitely generated centerless Hopfian group and H a Hopfian group. Every basic surjective homomorphism Phi:W->W is an automorphism.

In particular, for the original finitely generated two-factor problem with centerless G, any non-Hopfian counterexample must have a genuinely non-basic self-epimorphism. The theorem does not assert that every epimorphism is basic. The source's nonabelian free-base question is not thereby solved.

The proof retains the quotient automorphism and the inner cocycle. It neither abelianizes G nor invokes an unproved direct-finiteness property. The finite-propagation inverse is explicit below. No historical novelty claim is made.

## 2. Quotient automorphism and surjectivity on the base

Assume Phi(B)<=B. It induces a surjective self-map alpha of H. Since H is Hopfian, alpha is an automorphism. Put F=Phi|_B, and write

    Phi(1,h)=(b(h),alpha(h)),

where b(h) belongs to B. If w belongs to B, surjectivity supplies Phi(f,h)=w; projecting to H gives alpha(h)=1, hence h=1. Thus F is onto B. The same argument gives ker(Phi)<=B. This is the basic semidirect-product reduction of the credited Lemma2.14, with the Hopfian quotient hypothesis explicit.

The multiplication rule gives the cocycle identity

    b(hk)=b(h) (alpha(h).b(k)),

and compatibility with conjugation gives

    F(h.f)=b(h) (alpha(h).F(f)) b(h)^(-1).                       (1)

All products and conjugations on the right are pointwise in B. No assertion that this cocycle is trivial or a coboundary is used.

## 3. Local coordinate maps and commuting normal factors

Let i_h:G->B denote the lamp copy at coordinate h. For s in H define

    psi_s(g)=F(i_1(g))(s),  A_s=psi_s(G)<=G.

Each psi_s is a homomorphism. Because G is finitely generated, F(i_1(G)) is generated by finitely many finitely supported functions. The union S of their supports is finite, and psi_s is trivial for s outside S. Remove the trivial A_s from S. If G is trivial the whole theorem reduces to H being Hopfian, so assume G nontrivial and S nonempty.

The subgroup F(i_1(G)) is normal in B: i_1(G) is normal in the source base and F is onto. Projecting to coordinate s shows A_s is normal in G.

For any h and y, equation (1) gives

    F(i_h(g))(y)
      = b(h)(y) psi_(alpha(h)^(-1)y)(g) b(h)(y)^(-1).             (2)

In particular its nontrivial coordinate images are supported in alpha(h)S. At a fixed output coordinate y, the input coordinate corresponding to s in S is uniquely

    h=alpha^(-1)(y s^(-1)).                                    (3)

By normality of A_s, the image at that coordinate is exactly A_s, despite the inner conjugation in (2). Distinct input lamp groups commute elementwise, so the A_s are pairwise commuting. Surjectivity of F shows that their product is all of G.

They in fact form an internal direct product. If a product of elements a_s in A_s is one, each a_s is the inverse of a product in the other factors; hence a_s commutes with its own factor, and also with every other factor. It is central in G, so it is one because G is centerless. Thus multiplication gives an isomorphism

    product_(s in S) A_s  --> G.                               (4)

Centerlessness is used precisely here. Commuting normal images alone do not have trivial intersections for an arbitrary base.

## 4. Reconstructing one surjective endomorphism of G

Define

    Theta:G->G,   Theta(g)=product_(s in S) psi_s(g).             (5)

The factors commute, so this is a homomorphism. It is surjective, a point requiring more than individual surjectivity of the psi_s onto their images.

Choose arbitrary a_s in A_s for all s in S. Define w in B by w(s)=a_s for s in S and w(y)=1 otherwise. Choose f in B with F(f)=w. At output coordinate s, the A_s-component of F(f)(s) comes only from input coordinate 1: equation (3) gives alpha^(-1)(s s^(-1))=1. Since b(1)=1, this component is psi_s(f(1)). Every other input coordinate contributes to a different direct factor of (4). Therefore

    psi_s(f(1))=a_s for all s in S.

So the map g->(psi_s(g))_s is onto the full product, and Theta is surjective. Hopficity of G now makes Theta an automorphism. In particular the intersection of the kernels of all psi_s is trivial.

If F(f)=1, fix any input coordinate h. At output coordinate alpha(h)s, its contribution to the A_s factor is the inner conjugate in (2) of psi_s(f(h)); no other input coordinate contributes to that factor. Consequently psi_s(f(h))=1 for every s. Injectivity of Theta gives f(h)=1. This holds for every h, so F is injective. Since ker(Phi)<=B, Phi is injective and the theorem follows.

## 5. Explicit finite-support inverse on the base

Let pr_s:G->A_s be the projection from (4). For w in B define

    f(h)=Theta^(-1)( product_(s in S)
       b(h)(alpha(h)s)^(-1)
       pr_s(w(alpha(h)s))
       b(h)(alpha(h)s) ).                                      (6)

Each conjugated factor still belongs to A_s, by normality. Formula (2) and uniqueness of the input coordinate for each output factor show that this is exactly F^(-1)(w).

It is finitely supported: f(h) can be nontrivial only if alpha(h)s belongs to supp(w) for some s. Hence

    supp(F^(-1)(w)) subset alpha^(-1)( supp(w) S^(-1) ),
    |supp(F^(-1)(w))| <= |S| |supp(w)|.                          (7)

Similarly supp(F(f)) is contained in alpha(supp(f))S. The inner cocycle changes values but not these support containments. Formula (7) is valid for nonabelian H; the order y s^(-1), rather than s^(-1)y, is essential in (3).

## 6. A sufficient basicity criterion and its limited scope

If every homomorphism G->H is trivial, then every endomorphism of W is basic: compose each lamp restriction with the quotient W->H. That composition is trivial on each lamp, and the lamps generate B. Combining this observation with the theorem gives:

**Corollary.** If G is finitely generated, centerless and Hopfian, H is Hopfian, and Hom(G,H) contains only the trivial homomorphism, then G wr H is Hopfian.

This is a sufficient condition, not a necessary one. Known incompatibility/just-non-property examples in the source are credited rather than reclaimed. In the unrestricted original problem, G can map nontrivially into H, and a self-epimorphism can potentially mix lamp images into the quotient. Neither centerlessness nor Hopficity alone has been shown here to prevent that.

For bases with a center, the direct-factor separation in (4) also fails; the source's abelian stable-finiteness obstruction remains intact. Thus no conclusion about all two-Hopfian-factor wreath products follows from this turn.

## 7. Controls and next route

The exact checker constructs split-factor lamp maps over cyclic and nonabelian acting groups, includes a nontrivial inner coboundary cocycle and a nonidentity quotient automorphism, and checks the inverse, support containment and covariance formulas. These finite examples control orientation and factor bookkeeping, not the infinite Hopficity theorem.

One substantive author turn is used. The original remains unresolved. The next route must address non-basic image structure, or supply a different mechanism for bases with a center, rather than assume automatic basicity from the abelian theorem.
