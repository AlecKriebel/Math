# Author turn 4: derived-subgroup reduction and the surviving abelian obstruction

## 1. Scope and statement

All wreath products below are restricted wreath products for the regular left action. Put B=G^(H). A homomorphism is basic when it sends B into B.

**Theorem A.** Let G and H be finitely generated Hopfian groups. Write Z=Z(G), D=[G,G], A=G/D, and Q=G/Z. Assume Q is a nontrivial finite product of nonabelian simple groups. If D is Hopfian and A wr H is Hopfian, then G wr H is Hopfian.

The separate assumption on D is essential to the scope of this statement: we do not infer Hopficity of an arbitrary derived subgroup from Hopficity of G. The theorem also does not assert that all the abelian wreath products in its hypothesis are known to be Hopfian. Their obstruction is the credited stable-finiteness problem.

## 2. The derived subgroup is finitely generated and perfect

Since Q is perfect, G=D Z. Choose a finite generating set g_i=d_i z_i with d_i in D and z_i in Z, and let L be generated by the d_i. Then G=L Z. Hence

    D=[G,G]=[L Z,L Z]=[L,L] <= L <= D.

Thus D=L is finitely generated, and the displayed equality also gives D=[D,D]. Further,

    Z(D)=D intersect Z(G),   D/Z(D) is isomorphic to G/Z(G).

For the first equality, an element centralizing D automatically centralizes Z, and G=D Z. These are elementary consequences of the perfect central quotient, not general properties of derived subgroups.

## 3. A twisted-base criterion without a cocycle hypothesis

We isolate exactly what the perfect-base proof of turn 3 uses. Let D be finitely generated, perfect and Hopfian. Suppose F:D^(H)->D^(H) is a surjective homomorphism, alpha is an automorphism of H, and, for each h in H, there is c_h in D^(H), with c_1=1, such that

    F(h.f)=c_h (alpha(h).F(f)) c_h^(-1).                       (1)

Then F is injective. No cocycle law for the c_h is required.

Indeed set psi_s(d)=F(i_1(d))(s). Finite generation gives a finite support set S. Surjectivity and the normality of each input coordinate subgroup make K_s=psi_s(D) normal in D. Distinct input coordinates commute. For any output y, the input corresponding to offset s is uniquely h=alpha^(-1)(y s^(-1)); formula (1) therefore implies that the K_s commute pairwise and generate D, as in turn 1. Inner conjugation preserves each K_s.

The product Theta=product_s psi_s is a homomorphism. Since F is onto, it preserves the center of the base. Passing to D/Z(D), which is centerless because D is perfect, permits exactly the finite prescribed-output proof of turn 1: the quotient K_s form an internal direct product, and one prescribes the K_s-component at output s for every s in S. Contributions from every other input have trivial K_s-component. The induced bar(Theta) is therefore onto. Consequently Theta(D) Z(D)=D. Perfectness gives Theta(D)=D, and Hopficity makes Theta an automorphism.

The maps p_s=psi_s Theta^(-1) now satisfy product_s p_s=id. The argument in turn 3, Section 4 shows that each p_s is an actual projection: it is equivariant under inner automorphisms, so p_s(K_t) is central for s!=t, and it is trivial there because K_t is perfect. Thus D is the internal direct product of the K_s. A kernel element of F has every unique-coordinate component psi_s(f(h)) trivial; injectivity of Theta forces f=1.

All occurrences of c_h in this proof are the individual conjugations in (1). We have not asserted that arbitrary chosen lifts c_h form a cocycle or define a homomorphism of a semidirect product.

## 4. Proof of Theorem A

Let Phi:G wr H->G wr H be onto. The invariant-central-base lemma, credited to Bradford--Fournier-Facio Lemma 5.7 and proved in turn 3, gives

    Phi(Z^(H)) <= Z^(H).

The induced epimorphism of Q wr H is an automorphism and is basic by turn 2. The inverse image of Q^(H) in G wr H is B, so Phi is basic. Since H is Hopfian, its induced endomorphism alpha is an automorphism, F=Phi|_B is onto B, and ker(Phi)<=B (the credited Lemma 2.14, or the direct quotient argument).

Surjectivity gives

    F(D^(H))=F([B,B])=[B,B]=D^(H).                            (2)

The induced F_A:A^(H)->A^(H) is onto and alpha-semilinear. Inner conjugations vanish after abelianization, so

    (a,h) |-> (F_A(a),alpha(h))

is an epimorphism of A wr H. Its assumed Hopficity makes F_A injective. Thus ker(F)<=D^(H).

Write Phi(1,h)=(b(h),alpha(h)). Because G=D Z, choose, coordinate by coordinate, b(h)=d(h) z(h), with d(h) in D^(H) and z(h) in Z^(H); choose both equal to 1 for h=1. Finite support is retained. Conjugation by b(h) on D^(H) equals conjugation by d(h). The restriction in (2) consequently satisfies (1) with c_h=d(h). The criterion in Section 3 applies and makes that restriction injective. Since ker(F)<=D^(H), F and hence Phi are injective. This proves Theorem A.

The choices d(h) need not satisfy a cocycle law. Requiring such a law would create an unjustified splitting assumption; the twisted-base criterion avoids it.

## 5. An exact reduction for a split semisimple base

Let A be a finitely generated abelian group, P a nontrivial finite product of finitely generated nonabelian simple groups, and H a finitely generated Hopfian group. Then

    (A times P) wr H is Hopfian  <=>  A wr H is Hopfian.        (3)

For the reverse implication use Theorem A with G=A times P: its derived subgroup is P, its center is A, and P is Hopfian by the finite simple-factor argument of turn 2. The base G is itself Hopfian: its characteristic derived subgroup is P; an epimorphism restricts onto P and induces an epimorphism of A, so both are injective.

For the forward implication, suppose A wr H is not Hopfian. H cannot be finite: in that case A wr H is a finitely generated virtually abelian, residually finite group and is Hopfian. For infinite H, Bradford--Fournier-Facio Proposition 2.6 and Lemma 2.14, with the Hopficity of H explicitly retained, show that a noninjective epimorphism is basic and induces an automorphism alpha of H. Its base restriction is onto and alpha-semilinear. Discard its abelian base cocycle and compose with the coordinate relabeling induced by alpha^(-1). This gives an onto, noninjective H-equivariant homomorphism T:A^(H)->A^(H). The map

    (a,p,h) |-> (T(a),p,h)

is then a noninjective epimorphism of (A times P) wr H. This proves (3). No assertion about necessity of Hopficity of H outside the standing hypotheses is made.

## 6. The known matrix obstruction survives a nonabelian factor

Write

    A = A_0 direct-sum direct-sum_(p,e) (Z/p^e Z)^(m_(p,e)),

where A_0 is free abelian and only finitely many m_(p,e) are nonzero. The credited Bradford--Fournier-Facio Theorem 1.5, combined with (3), says that (A times P) wr H is Hopfian precisely when, for each torsion prime p, the ring

    M_(max_e m_(p,e))(F_p[H])

is directly finite. There is no additional condition from the free abelian summand. This is a transport of the known criterion, not a proof of direct finiteness. Even fixing P=A_5 leaves this entire obstruction inside a family of nonabelian bases.

For clarity, the matrix orientation in a hypothetical counterexample is as follows. Identify the base of A=(F_p)^d with row vectors R^d, R=F_p[H], with H acting by left multiplication on coefficients. If XY=I but YX!=I in M_d(R), right multiplication T_Y:v->vY is onto because T_Y(T_X(v))=vXY=v. A nonzero row of I-YX belongs to its kernel, since (I-YX)Y=0. Right multiplication commutes with the left H-action, so T_Y extends as above to a noninjective wreath-product epimorphism. No such matrices over one of these group rings are constructed here.

## 7. Exact controls and fourth-turn status

The accompanying checker exhausts F_2[S_3] scalar products and all pairs of 2-by-2 matrices over F_2[C_2], checking the right-multiplication orientation, finite-ring direct finiteness and left-action equivariance. Finite-ring checks illustrate identities only; they do not certify the infinite-group-ring conjecture or replace the group-theoretic proof.

Four substantive author turns are now complete. The original question remains unresolved. The new reduction handles the stated semisimple central quotient when D and A wr H are Hopfian; the remaining fifth turn will examine when the derived-subgroup hypothesis can actually be supplied, without assuming that subgroups inherit Hopficity.
