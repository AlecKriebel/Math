## Proved partial statements

1. For a finitely generated centerless Hopfian base G and Hopfian H, every basic epimorphism of G wr H is invertible. The proof retains a general inner cocycle, reconstructs a collapsed base automorphism from simultaneous prescribed outputs, and gives a finite-propagation inverse. If Hom(G,H) is trivial, basicity is automatic and this yields full Hopficity.
2. If G is a finite product of finitely generated nonabelian simple groups and H is Hopfian, every epimorphism of G wr_X H is basic and injective for any faithful transitive action on X. The proof counts intrinsic simple-factor conjugacy orbits. The regular action is included. Faithfulness cannot simply be removed from the basicity assertion.
3. The basic-map theorem extends to finitely generated perfect Hopfian bases with arbitrary centers. Full Hopficity follows if G/Z(G) is a finite product of nonabelian simple groups. This avoids assuming separately that Z(G) wr H is Hopfian. The credited invariant-central-base lemma is retained with a complete proof.
4. For finitely generated Hopfian G,H with semisimple central quotient as above, it suffices that G' and G_ab wr H are Hopfian. A twisted-base criterion avoids requiring chosen central lifts to form a cocycle. For G=A times P with A finitely generated abelian and P a nontrivial finite product of finitely generated nonabelian simple groups, the exact equivalence is: G wr H is Hopfian if and only if A wr H is Hopfian. Thus the credited prime-by-prime group-ring matrix obstruction survives in nonabelian bases.
5. A separate central-extension theorem supplies derived Hopficity: if G is finitely generated Hopfian, G/Z(G) is perfect and Hopfian, C=Z(G'), and C/mC is finite for m the exponent of torsion in G_ab, then G' is Hopfian. A power of each epimorphism of G' extends to an epimorphism of G by respecting a presentation of its center. Combining this with turn 4 gives the stated wreath-product theorem. In particular, a torsion-free abelianization removes the central-residue condition and invokes the known torsion-free abelian wreath result. A shift example shows why surjectivity on an infinite C/mC cannot replace finite periodicity; it is only a failed-lifting example, not an original group counterexample.
