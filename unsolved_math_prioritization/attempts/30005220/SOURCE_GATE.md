## Relevant primary literature

1. G. Navarro and P. H. Tiep, *The fields of values of characters of degree not divisible by p*, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 9 (2021), e2, 1–28. [DOI 10.1017/fmp.2021.1](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2021.1). The exact problem is Conjecture C. Lemmas 7.1–7.2 supply the field inclusion and odd-prime reformulation; Theorem 7.3 proves the normal-Sylow case. Their results for global fields of odd-degree characters do not by themselves resolve Sylow restriction fields.
2. I. M. Isaacs and G. Navarro, *Primes and conductors of characters*, Journal of Algebra 653 (2024), 42–53. [DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2024.04.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2024.04.022). Proves the p-solvable case. This result was already announced in Navarro's original report. Publisher abstract and subsequent primary papers were checked; the complete article was not independently re-proved here.
3. N. Hung and M. Schaeffer Fry, *The p-rationality of height-zero characters*, Nagoya Mathematical Journal 261 (2026), e7, 1–22. [DOI 10.1017/nmj.2025.10077](https://doi.org/10.1017/nmj.2025.10077). Conjecture B is the nondegenerate field formulation of the target. Theorem D resolves prime-degree characters. Lemmas 4.2–4.3 and Theorem 4.4 give the induction mechanism used in Attempt 4. The separate defect-normalizer conjecture must not be confused with the stronger Sylow-restriction statement.
4. Nguyen N. Hung, *Problems on the conductor of finite group characters*, [arXiv:2512.18042v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18042v3), 26 May 2026. Section 5 retains the general target as Conjecture 5.2 and identifies the stronger modular-level assertion in Question 5.3. Its equality formulation is used here only when a≥1; the original index statement handles a=0 without any ambiguity.
5. Markus Linckelmann, *On the p-part of the conductor of a generalised character*, [arXiv:2604.01351v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01351v1), 1 April 2026. Theorem 1.1 detects c(χ)_p from generalized decomposition numbers. It does not identify the field of the Sylow restriction. Attempt 5 isolates the precise cancellation obstacle.

## Claim boundary

The general target has not been proved or disproved in this work. Source recovery and elementary special-case derivations do not constitute a new solution. The exact negative examples concern explicitly reducible characters; none qualifies for the original irreducible-character hypothesis. No first-result or historical-priority claim is made.
