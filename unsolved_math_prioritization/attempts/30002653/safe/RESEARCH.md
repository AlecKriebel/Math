# Perfect-cone Prym indeterminacy: five retained approaches

## Outcome and exact target

**Unsolved in full after five substantive approach families.** This note proves a restricted infinite-family statement, gives exact obstruction certificates, and identifies the missing general step. It does not claim a new all-genus theorem, a counterexample to the target, or an exhaustive literature determination of present openness. Novelty of the restricted results has not been established.

Use the dimension convention
\[
 P_g^{\mathrm{Perf}}:\overline R_{g+1}\dashrightarrow\overline A_g^{\mathrm{Perf}},\qquad g\geq1.
\]
The base curve has arithmetic genus \(g+1\), its connected admissible double cover has genus \(2g+1\), and the principally polarized Prym has dimension \(g\). Work over \(\mathbb C\). The source is the compactification by admissible double covers of stable curves; the target is the **first Voronoi/perfect-cone** toroidal compactification. The question is
\[
 \operatorname{Ind}(P_g^{\mathrm{Perf}})=\overline{FS}_2\cup\overline{FS}_3.
\tag{T}
\]
Here \(FS_n\) has two smooth irreducible covering components, each invariant under the involution, meeting in \(2n\) nodes exchanged in pairs. The base has \(n\) joining nodes. The bars are essential: they include further degenerations. Nodes fixed by the involution are permitted in an admissible cover only when the local branches are not interchanged.

The primary OWR statement is Hulek's Question 3, printed p. 2189 in OWR 39/2014 [S1]. Its closure bars were visually checked. The same target is Question 7.4 of [S2], printed p. 692. These papers supply the extension criterion used below: on
\(L=H^1(\widetilde\Gamma,\mathbb Z)^-\), take primitive nonzero coedges \(\ell_e\), with the normalization specified in [S2, (5.1)]. Extension to the perfect-cone target is equivalent to existence of a positive definite quadratic form \(Q\) on \(L_{\mathbb R}\) with
\[
 Q(\ell_e)=1,\qquad Q(x)\geq1\quad(0\ne x\in L). \tag{P}
\]
The monodromy cone is generated by \(\ell_e^2\). For a lifted edge \(\widetilde e\), the primitive coedge is \(\widetilde e^*-\iota\widetilde e^*\), except when \(\iota\widetilde e^*=-\widetilde e^*\) in cohomology, when it is \(\widetilde e^*\). Fixed-edge coedges vanish. One must not replace this lattice by the integral anti-invariant homology lattice or omit this primitive normalization.

The smooth Deligne-Mumford source stack has normal-crossing boundary; its coarse space can have quotient singularities. For these locally liftable period maps the stack/coarse extension tests agree [S2, notation, §3.1, §2.3, Theorem 5.6]. Smoothness of the stack does not eliminate the indeterminacy.

## Prior results and limits of the literature check

[S2, Theorem 7.1] locates any further indeterminacy inside the higher Friedman-Smith boundary and in codimension at least six. It proves (T) for target dimensions at most three, but does not assert the dimension-four case globally. The second-Voronoi answer instead uses all \(\overline{FS}_n\), \(n\geq2\); the central-cone test adds integrality of \(Q\). Neither is interchangeable with (T).

Frinak's 2018 thesis [S3, Theorem 1.0.1, printed pp. 2-3] reports the equality for \(\overline R_5\dashrightarrow\overline A_4^{\mathrm{Perf}}\) outside a locus of codimension at least ten. The thesis' later summary changes genus indexing inconsistently; this note does not promote it to an all-genus theorem or a complete genus-five result. Its complete source code/enumeration was not independently replayed here.

Zakharov's July 20, 2026 preprint [S4] resolves \(\overline R_4\dashrightarrow\overline A_3\) by a toroidal modification, using tropical trigonal constructions. The target is dimension three, where the relevant standard fans coincide. This is a resolution problem, and does not settle (T) in higher dimension. Its introduction also describes the limited scope of [S3]. Searches through October 5, 2026 found no complete prior resolution of (T); this is a bounded search result, not a claim that none exists.

## Approach 1: half-integral cut lattices

### Mechanism and retained theorem

Try to construct the required metric directly from the graph involution. This succeeds when every vertex of the covering dual graph is fixed by the involution. Equivalently, every irreducible covering component is preserved, although the involution on that component need not be the identity.

**Restricted theorem.** For an admissible double cover with every vertex of its dual graph fixed, (T) holds at that cover. This includes arbitrary genus, multigraphs, loops, and nodes fixed branchwise. The assertion concerns only this graph class.

Here is a complete proof, reducing the geometric conclusion to the cited criterion (P) and the definition of Friedman-Smith degeneration.

**Step 1: contract fixed edges.** Let \(F\) be the subgraph of edges fixed by the involution. There are no edge inversions in an admissible involution. Contract every connected component of \(F\). The induced exact sequence of integral graph homology has kernel \(H_1(F,\mathbb Z)\), on which the involution is the identity. Write
\[
 H_1(\widetilde\Gamma,\mathbb Z)^{[-]}
   =\tfrac12(1-\iota)H_1(\widetilde\Gamma,\mathbb Z).
\]
The contraction induces an isomorphism on these projected lattices. Indeed, it is onto because graph contraction is onto on integral homology; its kernel has both invariant and anti-invariant real class, hence is zero. This argument uses the projected lattice, so it does not silently discard an index-two effect. Dually it identifies the anti-invariant integral cohomology lattices and all surviving coedges. Fixed coedges are zero and irrelevant to (P).

A partition defining an FS degeneration cannot cross a fixed edge, since joining nodes must occur in exchanged pairs. Conversely, a connected partition after contraction lifts to connected invariant subgraphs before contraction. Thus contraction preserves membership in each \(\overline{FS}_n\).

After contraction, the graph is obtained from a connected multigraph \(G=(V,E)\) by replacing every edge with an exchanged pair, leaving its vertices fixed. Loops are allowed.

**Step 2: compute the integral lattice, not just its real span.** Orient each pair compatibly and write \(t_e=[e_+^*-e_-^*]\). These \(m=|E|\) classes are a real basis of the anti-invariant cohomology: the coboundaries have equal coefficients on each pair, while anti-cochains have opposite coefficients. Let \(B^T\) be the integral coboundary matrix of \(G\). An integral cochain representative with pair coefficients \((a_e,b_e)\) is anti-invariant in cohomology precisely when
\[
 (a_e+b_e)_{e\in E}=B^T f\quad\text{for some }f\in\mathbb Z^V.
\]
Its real anti-invariant coordinates are \((a_e-b_e)/2\). Consequently
\[
 L=\mathbb Z^E+\tfrac12\mathcal C(G),\qquad
 \mathcal C(G)=\operatorname{im}(B^T:\mathbb F_2^V\to\mathbb F_2^E), \tag{1}
\]
where the binary cut code is lifted coordinatewise into \(\{0,1\}^E\). To verify the converse explicitly, if \(d\in\mathbb Z^E\) and \(d\equiv B^Tf\pmod2\), choose
\(a=(B^Tf+d)/2\) and \(b=(B^Tf-d)/2\). They are integral and represent \(d/2\). This proves equality of lattices.

**Step 3: normalize bridges.** A singleton \(\{e\}\) is a cut-code word if and only if \(e\) is a bridge. Formula (1) therefore says that \(t_e/2\) is integral exactly for bridges. For a bridge, \([e_+^*+e_-^*]=0\), so the primitive coedge is \(t_e/2\). Every other coedge is \(t_e\). Let \(J\) be the bridges. Because the singleton words of \(J\) belong to the cut code, it splits into their coordinate code and a code \(\mathcal C_0\) supported off \(J\). In primitive coordinates,
\[
 L=\mathbb Z^J\oplus(\mathbb Z^{E\setminus J}+\tfrac12\mathcal C_0). \tag{2}
\]
There are no weight-one words in \(\mathcal C_0\).

**Step 4: solve a general binary-code metric problem.** For any binary linear code \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^r\) without weight-one words, consider
\(L_{\mathcal C}=\mathbb Z^r+\tfrac12\mathcal C\) with prescribed coedges the coordinate unit vectors. A form satisfying (P) exists if and only if every nonzero word has weight at least four.

For sufficiency take \(Q(x)=\sum x_i^2\). A nonzero integral vector has norm at least one. A vector in a nonzero parity coset has at least \(w\geq4\) half-integral coordinates, so has norm at least \(w/4\geq1\). The coordinate vectors have norm one and the form is positive definite.

For necessity let a nonzero codeword have support \(S\) and weight \(w<4\). All \(2^w\) vectors
\(v_s=\frac12\sum_{i\in S}s_i e_i\), \(s_i\in\{-1,1\}\), belong to the same lattice coset and are nonzero. Averaging their quadratic values cancels mixed terms:
\[
 2^{-w}\sum_s Q(v_s)=\tfrac14\sum_{i\in S}Q(e_i)=w/4<1.
\]
This contradicts the required lower bound. This obstruction applies to **every** quadratic form, not just the diagonal trial metric.

Apply the result to (2), with the unit metric also on bridge factors.

**Step 5: identify the geometric obstruction.** A nonzero word of \(\mathcal C_0\) is the cut of a vertex subset and contains no bridge. If its size is two or three, both shores must be connected. Otherwise a disconnected shore has at least two nonempty connected components, each contributing at least two crossing edges: a component with one crossing edge would make that edge a bridge. This would require at least four edges in the cut. Thus every weight-two or weight-three word is a bond, meaning a cut with connected shores.

For the doubled graph a bond of size \(n\) gives two connected invariant covering subgraphs meeting in \(2n\) exchanged edges, precisely a degeneration in \(\overline{FS}_n\). Conversely such an invariant connected partition gives a bond of size \(n\). A bond of size at least two cannot contain a bridge: the bridge alone is a smaller separating cut. Hence the size-two and size-three bonds are exactly the obstructing words in \(\mathcal C_0\). This proves the restricted theorem. ∎

### Examples, normalization check, and exact gap

For a graph with two vertices and \(n\) parallel edges, (1) is
\(L=\mathbb Z^n+\mathbb Z(\frac12,\ldots,\frac12)\). It gives the usual FS obstruction at \(n=2,3\), extension at \(n\geq4\), and, after primitive normalization, extension at \(n=1\). This agrees with [S2, Theorem 6.4], so the argument is not presented as a new discovery of that known fact.

For \(G=K_5\), every nonzero cut has size four or six; the metric exists in torus rank ten. One may realize the graph by five genus-one base components with connected étale double covers and paired gluings. The base genus is eleven and the Prym dimension is ten. This is a genuinely higher-rank family example, but no claim is made that it was previously unknown.

**Gap:** for graphs with exchanged vertices, the coedges are generally dependent and the simple coordinate cut-code model (1) no longer applies. Removing fixed edges does not eliminate exchanged vertices. The restricted theorem does not prove that every cover outside the two small FS closures belongs to this graph class.

## Approach 2: gluing along invariant cut vertices

### Mechanism and complete gluing lemma

Try to decompose arbitrary involution graphs into pieces already handled by the cut-code argument or by a Jacobian-type metric.

**Lemma.** Suppose invariant connected graphs \(\Gamma_1\) and \(\Gamma_2\) meet in exactly one fixed vertex and have disjoint edges. Then their anti-invariant cohomology lattices split integrally,
\(L=L_1\oplus L_2\), and the nonzero primitive coedges are the coedges of the two summands. Criterion (P) holds for the union if and only if it holds for both pieces.

**Proof.** Every graph cycle in the union is a sum of cycles in the pieces: a closed walk crossing the common vertex can be cut there into closed walks in the separate pieces. Therefore integral homology, its invariant parts, and its projected anti-invariant lattices split. Dualizing gives the stated cohomology splitting and also preserves primitive normalization. If forms \(Q_i\) work, set \(Q=Q_1\oplus Q_2\). A nonzero lattice vector has a nonzero component with norm at least one; the other component contributes nonnegatively. Prescribed coedges have norm one. Conversely restriction of a working form to either summand remains positive definite and meets all required inequalities. ∎

This fills in the elementary perfect-cone part of [S2, Lemma D.3 and Remark D.4]. The same proof works for finitely many such pieces.

An additional useful building block is a pair of isomorphic graphs interchanged by the involution and meeting at one fixed vertex. Its anti-invariant cohomology is \(\{(a,-a):a\in H^1(H,\mathbb Z)\}\), and its primitive coedges identify with ordinary graph coedges on \(H\). The cographic/Jacobian extension theorem of [S2, Theorem 5.1] supplies (P). This is a use of a published theorem, not an independent proof of that theorem.

### Why overlapping gluing fails without extra information

Even when two coedge systems share a lattice basis and each individually admits a metric, a shared metric need not exist. In \(\mathbb Z^2\), put \(u=(1,0)\), \(v=(0,1)\). The systems
\(\{u,v,u+v\}\) and \(\{u,v,u-v\}\) admit respectively
\[
 Q_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the first, \(Q_+(a,b)=a^2-ab+b^2\) is a positive integer at least one for every nonzero integral pair; the same holds with the sign reversed. But prescribing all four vectors to have norm one contradicts
\[
 Q(u+v)+Q(u-v)=2Q(u)+2Q(v)=4.
\]
This is an obstruction to an unrestricted gluing argument, **not** an asserted admissible-cover counterexample to (T).

**Gap:** no decomposition into invariant one-vertex wedges has been proved for general covers outside \(\overline{FS}_2\cup\overline{FS}_3\). Involution-preserving blocks joined at multiple vertices can couple both lattice and metric constraints. This route stops at that missing structural theorem.

## Approach 3: regular matroids and induction on faces

### Mechanism, exact test, and obstruction to the route

Try to strengthen the hypothesis outside the two forbidden closures to the unimodularity condition for second-Voronoi extension, then transfer to the perfect-cone fan. That strengthening is false already for generic \(FS_4\).

For \(n\geq2\), a standard integral coordinate model for the FS coedges is
\[
 a=2e_1-e_2-\cdots-e_n,\quad e_2,\ldots,e_n. \tag{3}
\]
Their determinant has absolute value two, so they form a real basis but not an integral lattice basis. This fails the regular-matroid extension criterion. Nevertheless Approach 1 supplies (P) for every \(n\geq4\). Thus a strategy that tries to prove all remaining coedge systems unimodular cannot solve the correct problem.

Every proper subset of (3) is primitive and can be extended to an integral basis. If \(a\) is omitted, this is immediate. If \(e_j\), \(j\geq2\), is omitted, adjoin \(e_1\); expansion along the missing coordinate gives determinant of absolute value one. Smaller subsets inherit the same extension property. Consequently all proper ray faces satisfy the elementary basis metric test even for \(n=2,3\), although their full cones fail (P).

The trial form in the integral basis (3) has matrix
\[
 Q_n=\begin{pmatrix}
 n/4&1/2&\cdots&1/2\\
 1/2&1&&0\\
 \vdots&&\ddots&\\
 1/2&0&&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its Schur complement is \(1/4\), so it is positive definite with determinant \(1/4\). It is the pullback of the Euclidean metric by the lattice basis \(\frac12(1,\ldots,1),e_2,\ldots,e_n\); hence its lattice minimum is \(\min(1,n/4)\). This checks the difference between determinant two and actual perfect-cone obstruction in all ranks.

**Gap:** extension on all proper faces does not imply extension at the original stratum, and failure of unimodularity need not imply failure for the perfect-cone target. An induction needs compatibility information on the full cone beyond either test.

## Approach 4: exact convex certificates and finite graph screening

### Mechanism and a finite sufficient certificate

Try to turn (P) into a verifiable metric-feasibility calculation without confusing a finite search with the all-genus theorem.

For a proposed rational symmetric \(r\times r\) matrix \(Q\), require a rational \(\lambda>0\) such that \(Q-\lambda I\) is positive definite, checked by exact Sylvester minors. Put \(B=\lfloor\sqrt{1/\lambda}\rfloor\). Check
\[
 z^TQz\geq1\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb Z^r\setminus\{0\},\ |z_i|\leq B,
\]
and check all prescribed coedge equalities. This finite test proves (P): outside the box one coordinate has magnitude at least \(B+1\), so
\(Q(z)>\lambda\|z\|_2^2\geq\lambda(B+1)^2>1\).
This is a **sufficient certificate for a supplied form**, not a complete decision procedure for finding one. Failure of one form or one lower-bound choice does not prove nonextension.

For genuine negative certificates, sign averaging gives an exact linear-functional contradiction. In the integral FS basis, the sign vectors
\[
 w_s=\big(s_1,(s_2-s_1)/2,\ldots,(s_n-s_1)/2\big)
\]
are integral, and
\[
 2^{-n}\sum_s w_sw_s^T=\tfrac14\left(aa^T+\sum_{i=2}^ne_ie_i^T\right).
\]
For \(n=2,3\), applying any purported metric to this identity gives average \(n/4<1\). This proves infeasibility for all forms, with no floating-point optimizer.

The accompanying standard-library verifier checks these identities, finite positive certificates (including the rank-four FS metric), and every connected labelled simple graph on at most five vertices in the all-vertices-fixed class. It compares the normalized cut-code obstruction with directly enumerated size-two/three bonds. It also tests multigraph, loop, and bridge fixtures. Graph counts and exact assertion totals are recorded in `verification_results.json`.

**Gap:** the enumeration does not cover all admissible involutions, all higher-genus graphs, or all degenerations. Even an exhaustive fixed-genus computation would not establish the uniform statement. No bound reducing the unresolved general class to finitely many tested graphs has been proved.

## Approach 5: tropical resolutions and descent

### Mechanism and exact failure of the shortcut

The 2026 tropical trigonal construction suggests resolving the monodromy map chamber by chamber, then trying to show the modification is unnecessary outside the two small FS closures. The descent step is the difficulty; resolution alone cannot settle it.

Already the rank-two FS cone demonstrates the distinction. Set
\[
 A=(2x-y)^2,\quad B=y^2,\quad R=(A+B)/2=x^2+(x-y)^2.
\]
The two cones \(\langle B,R\rangle\) and \(\langle A,R\rangle\) subdivide \(\langle A,B\rangle\). The first lies in
\(C_1=\langle x^2,y^2,(x-y)^2\rangle\); the second lies in
\(C_2=\langle(2x-y)^2,x^2,(x-y)^2\rangle\).
The forms
\[
 H_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&3/2\\3/2&3\end{pmatrix}
\]
show that these two containing cones are perfect-cone cones: for \(H_1\), the lattice minimum is one with exactly the three displayed primitive rays. The unimodular substitution \((p,q)=(a+b,b)\) sends \(H_2(a,b)=a^2+3ab+3b^2\) to \(p^2+pq+q^2\), so its minimal vectors are exactly the three displayed vectors for \(C_2\). The finite verifier checks both metrics and the rank-one matrix identity for \(R\).

Yet the original FS cone admits no containing perfect cone, by the rank-two averaging obstruction. Thus even an explicit toroidal resolution into valid pieces does not prove that a period map was defined at the original point. This rederives, in rank two, the familiar subdivision in [S2, Appendix A.4].

**Gap:** a higher-genus construction would need to prove that, away from the two forbidden closures, every original monodromy cone lies in one target cone, or prove a valid descent theorem forcing constancy on every exceptional fiber. No such general statement has been proved here. The base-genus-four result [S4] is not that statement.

## Final boundary of the result

The strongest new work retained here is the complete cut-code derivation and restricted all-vertices-fixed theorem, together with exact gluing and certificate lemmas. It is not labeled novel. The unresolved part of (T) includes admissible involution graphs with genuinely exchanged vertices whose coedge and metric constraints cannot be reduced to the proved cases. A valid future advance must control that class, or produce an admissible counterexample outside \(\overline{FS}_2\cup\overline{FS}_3\), or verify a complete published resolution.

No numerical experiment, raw graph count, source abstract, or renamed compactification is used as a substitute for that missing step.
