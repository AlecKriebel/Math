# Primary sources and exact locators

1. M. Gromov, *101 Questions, Problems and Conjectures around Scalar Curvature*, October 1, 2017, author-hosted PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/101-problemsOct1-2017.pdf . Read the complete surrounding subsection on Lipschitz maps, printed pp.71–72, and visually inspected [?73][c] on p.72. Euclidean balls are defined in the preceding paragraph. The next [?74] scalar-curvature hypothesis belongs to a different question and is not imported here. The full cocycle-evaluation context matters because a ball is not a cycle.

2. A. Berdnikov, L. Guth and F. Manin, *Degrees of maps and multiscale geometry*, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 12 (2024), e2, DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2023.33 . Final publisher PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1EFE7F7236FF1C1F92B3A68BF7A3C737/S2050508623000331a.pdf/degrees_of_maps_and_multiscale_geometry.pdf . Published January18,2024; received July28,2022, revised September6,2023, accepted December16,2023. Theorem2.3 is on printed p.5 and its ball proof is Section2.3.1 on p.24. The general proof is Section2.3, pp.18–24, using Lemmas2.19–2.21 and the Fourier tools of Section2.1. These full proof sections were read. The theorem applies to every Lipschitz map of a ball and does not require formality, simple connectivity, a boundary condition or one globally compatible family.

3. Full author/NSF repository copy of the same work: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10562505 . This was the initially recovered complete text. It has the same Theorem2.3 and ball proof. The final publisher version was subsequently downloaded, read and pinned separately; the source assessment is based on that final version. Preprint identifier: https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12347 . The author publication page https://www.math.utoronto.ca/manin/papers.html independently lists the final 2024 article. Neither the repository's crawl date nor the older generated dataset status is a publication date or evidence of continued openness.

The application uses only elementary connected-sum cohomology, dilation, orientation reflection and the de Rham/Stokes boundary correction in addition to the published theorem. No claim of original theorem discovery or a new independent Fourier proof is made. No arbitrary unbounded singular-cochain convention is substituted for the original controlled geometric evaluation.

Full PDFs, extracted texts, rendered pages and imported records are reading inputs excluded from every public checkpoint. The publisher PDF includes a download footer; none of that file is redistributed. Hashes and URLs in source_manifest.json identify the exact reading copies without copying their content.
