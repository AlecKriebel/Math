## 1. Findings and correction scope

### F1. The small-site test family must include every etale object

The earlier Corollary 6.1 chooses an arbitrary point of the punctured representation and then invokes conservativity for the small equivariant Nisnevich site. That jump needs justification: the conservative point family must also cover points of arbitrary equivariant etale `U -> V minus {0}`. It is insufficient to cite the big-site point theorem and silently replace its quantifier over all objects by just the base object.

The distinction is genuine for small Nisnevich sites in general. For the small site of a field F and a nontrivial finite separable extension L/F, the free abelian Nisnevich sheaf generated by Spec L evaluates to zero on Spec F, but is nonzero on Spec L. The one base-object point does not detect that sheaf. This is a different issue from testing only closed points of a variety; both quantifier restrictions must be avoided.

The needed repair is elementary. Given any equivariant etale U over the punctured representation and any point u of U, choose a geometric point above u. Its inertia subgroup embeds in the stabilizer of the nonzero image vector. Such a stabilizer is 1 or a transposition subgroup C2. If inertia is 1, the free-germ theorem applies on the invariant free open of U. If inertia is C2, its normality in the set-theoretic stabilizer H, together with `N_S3(C2)=C2`, forces H=C2. The subgroup theorem applies. Thus every required orbit-henselian test is connective, which proves the full small-site sheaf assertion.

The replacement proof is present in APPROACH_1.md, Corollary 6.1, not merely an instruction to repair it later. The same correction is made in Section 6 of the companion `FREE_LOCUS_PERFECT_FIELD.md`. The neighboring sentence is clarified to say that any still-uncontrolled small-site germ lies over the origin. It does not assert that one evaluation at the base origin is a conservative test of the entire fiber.

Other stalk quantifiers were checked. The dimension argument is on the whole small site of its test scheme; the subgroup and free-germ results quantify over arbitrary smooth objects and their points. They do not need the same correction. The warnings about closed-point detection and nonsplit free atlases remain valid.

### F2. Two precision edits

1. The continuity paragraph now says finite-type smooth models with affine transition maps, with charts and descent for nonaffine objects. An arbitrary nonaffine object is not literally an inverse limit of affine schemes.
2. The localized map `A -> B_n` in the coefficient-field construction is called essentially etale. `B` is finite etale over A, but localization at a prime is not to be confused with a finite-presentation assertion. Unchanged-residue henselization invariance is exactly the property used, so the construction is unaffected.

## 3. Independent audit of the localization and dimension chain

The spectral argument is sound and does not assume the desired connectivity theorem.

First, multiplication by a trivially acted-on algebraic simplex preserves ordinary equivariant Nisnevich distinguished squares. In spectra, geometric realization preserves finite colimits and therefore finite limits. Thus it preserves the excision squares and the empty-object condition. The complete, regular, locally dimension-bounded cd-structure identifies this with Nisnevich descent. The usual singular-construction contraction supplies A1-invariance and an A1-local equivalence from E. Consequently the singular object is the desired localization. This stable argument would not follow merely by replacing spectra with arbitrary spaces and asserting realization preserves all pullbacks.

Second, continuity must preserve local connectivity before the small-site cohomological dimension is used. Here is an explicit check of the interface. A negative section in the filtered-colimit extension is represented at a finite smooth model. Since the original negative homotopy sheaf is zero, a Nisnevich cover kills that section. Pullback of the finite cover to the limit remains a Nisnevich cover and kills the represented section. Etale objects of finite presentation and finite diagrams descend to a finite stage; the finite group action descends with its finitely many compatibility relations. This argument applies to every object of the limit's small site. It proves the required local vanishing there, not just at a chosen point of its base scheme.

The cohomological bound is then applied to the actual noetherian scheme `T x Delta^n`, of dimension `d+n`. It is not taken from the often larger dimensions of finite-type approximations. The equivariant density argument only uses the noetherian etale site and invariant opens; finite orbits preserve the required specialization-length bound. BKRS supplies an alternate quotient-stack formulation of the same estimate. Finite local homotopy dimension justifies the spectral Postnikov/descent lower bound `E(T x Delta^n) >= -d-n`.

Finally, the stable skeletal argument has the correct signs. Each proper degenerate term in the nth latching object is at least `-d-n+1` connective. Colimits preserve that lower bound. The cofiber of its map to A_n is at least `-d-n` connective; the nth skeletal attachment suspends it n times and is at least `-d` connective. Starting with A_0 and taking the filtered union proves `|A| >= -d`. No convergence of an unbounded diagonal spectral sequence is being assumed.

This proves the stated general dimension estimate and in particular connectivity on zero-dimensional essentially smooth field orbits with arbitrary compatible residue-field action. It does not identify such an orbit with a positive-dimensional henselian germ. For the standard S3 origin the retained bound is only `>= -2`.

## 4. Independent audit of subgroup restriction

For `j(U)=G x_H U` and `r(X)=X` with restricted H-action, scheme induction is left adjoint to restriction. The induced presheaf functor `j^*E(U)=E(jU)` has right adjoint `F -> (X -> F(rX))`. The direction can be checked on representables: restricting the representable of X gives the representable of rX.

Every equivariant etale object over `G x_H U` is induced from its identity-coset component. This identifies covers and the residue-field/set-stabilizer conditions. Hence restriction commutes with local homotopy sheafification and is t-exact. Restriction of a G-cover to H is again a cover because stabilizers intersect with H. Thus both explicit presheaf functors preserve sheaves.

Both commute with product by the trivial interval. The right adjoint preserves local objects, while the left restriction also preserves local objects. Their adjunction therefore proves that restriction carries localization to localization; alternatively the established singular formula gives the comparison directly. No equivariant connectivity assertion is needed in this step.

At a point x with set stabilizer H, the selected orbit in the etale induced map gives the henselian identity `X^h_Gx = G x_H (X|H)^h_x`. It retains the semilinear H-action on the residue field. Morel handles H=1 and Bachmann handles H=C2. This does not apply to a point merely because its geometric inertia is trivial: its set stabilizer can be larger. The separate free-germ argument is needed precisely there.

## 5. Independent audit of the free-germ proof

All four steps work with the stated separably-generated residue hypothesis.

1. Let A be the local ring of the smooth quotient at y and F its residue field. A separating transcendence basis of F/k can be lifted to A. Every nonzero polynomial in the lifts has nonzero residue and is a unit, giving an actual rational subfield K0 inside A. Their differentials are independent because their images form a basis of `Omega_F/k`. The differential criterion makes the corresponding map to affine space smooth near y, so A is essentially smooth over K0.
2. The algebra `B=A tensor_K0 F` is finite etale over A. Reduction and multiplication define its selected prime with residue F. Its localization is essentially smooth over F and essentially etale over A with unchanged residue. Thus its henselization is A^h and supplies both a compatible embedded copy of F and a smooth local F-model. This is materially stronger than assuming a formal coefficient field in a completion.
3. Equivariantly Nisnevich locally, a free orbit admits an affine induced neighborhood, and its finite etale quotient Q is a smooth scheme. Over `S=O^h_Q,y` the torsor is a finite etale algebra with G-action. The coefficient field lets the residual F-torsor P be pulled back to S. Full faithfulness of the residue equivalence identifies this pullback with the original torsor, equivariantly. It does not split P into a trivial G-torsor.
4. Restriction along the separably generated field extension is t-exact: smooth finite-presentation F-objects and finite equivariant diagrams descend to smooth k-models; the same cover-killing argument from Section 3 applies. It commutes with localization by the singular formula and filtered-colimit interchange. For fixed P, every equivariant etale object over `P x_F U` descends to an ordinary etale object over U. The descended separated etale algebraic space is a scheme. One can check the Nisnevich topology by descending equivariant splitting sequences: invariant closed strata and equivariant sections descend along the torsor, and ordinary splitting sequences pull back. Thus this restriction commutes with homotopy sheafification and is t-exact, without assuming a false split-free atlas. It also commutes with the trivial simplicial interval.

Apply ordinary stable connectivity over F to this ordinary connective sheaf, and evaluate at the essentially smooth F-henselian local ring S constructed above. Such evaluation is a genuine Nisnevich point and is connective. The comparisons identify its value with the original free orbit-henselian test.

Perfectness of k ensures every quotient residue F is separably generated, so all free germs over an infinite perfect field are covered. Over an imperfect field this construction is accepted only at the stipulated separably generated residue germs. The argument does not claim otherwise.

## 6. Presentation obstruction and examples

The tangent/codimension proposition is correct. At a fixed rational point, etale passage identifies tangent representations. A smooth map of relative dimension r gives an invariant tangent kernel of dimension r. Quasi-finiteness on the support gives `d-c <= d-r`, so `1 <= r <= c`. Tameness supplies a direct-summand interpretation but is not used to reverse the necessary condition.

A 3-cycle has no nonzero fixed vector, excluding an invariant line because every one-dimensional S3 character is trivial on 3-cycles. The invariant quadratic is a divisor through the origin, so the tangent obstruction excludes the specified positive-relative-dimension smooth finite-support presentation there. It says nothing about whether negative localized homotopy actually exists.

The quotient by C3 is singular at the origin: invariant monomials are generated by `u^3,v^3,uv` with relation `AB=C^3`. Its embedding dimension is three and dimension two. The scalar contraction of V also does not extend to the henselian local scheme: the unit `1-x` would pull back to the nonunit `1-tx`. Both warnings correctly exclude particular shortcuts, not all conceivable approaches.

The blow-up free-abelian cokernel example vanishes on field tests and is nonzero at the local surface identity germ, because that morphism has no lift to the blow-up. Evaluation at a Nisnevich-local object is exact. Its scalar-homotopy restriction shows it is not A1-invariant, so it is not a counterexample to the desired theorem. The square-cover example correctly disproves closed-point conservativity. No stage of the corrected proof uses either invalid detection principle.
