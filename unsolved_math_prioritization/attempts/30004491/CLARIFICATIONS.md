# Exposition accompanying the unchanged freezes

These two clarifications are already justified in Sections 5 and 6 of the independent audit. They do not change the frozen proofs, disposition, or mathematical scope.

## Invariant tangent generator in the compositum

Set K=C(x,y)=C(a,b), with a=(x+1)/(x-1), b=(y+1)/(y-1), and sigma(a,b)=(-a,-b). The fixed field is F=C(s,r), s=a^2 and r=b/a. The derivation D=t x partial_x-y partial_y is anti-invariant under sigma; it need not preserve F. In (a,b) coordinates,

D = t(1-a^2)/2 partial_a - (1-b^2)/2 partial_b.

The product delta=aD is invariant and restricts to a nonzero rational tangent derivation on F. Given any finite extension E/F carrying a rational first integral h, form the compositum L=EK in an algebraic closure of F. The extension of delta to L is unique in characteristic zero and agrees with a times the extension of D. Thus delta(h)=0 implies D(h)=0. The constant-field lemma for the finite extension L/K forces h in C. This is the scalar-generator justification for the author's finite-cover argument; it does not assume D itself preserves the quotient field.

## Birational general leaves and exceptional labels

Resolve the rational surface models to a common smooth surface and factor the birational morphisms into point blowups. On the common open locus they are isomorphisms. A regular leaf not contained in a removed algebraic curve meets that curve in a closed discrete analytic subset of its intrinsic Riemann surface. Removing such a subset leaves the surface connected: a compact path meets only finitely many such points, and local detours avoid each. Contracted invariant curves are exceptional leaves, singular centers do not join regular leaves, and a regular center belongs to just one regular leaf. The modifications therefore do not join distinct general leaves or split their equivalence classes on the common open locus. This does not identify exceptional global leaf spaces.

The finitely many leaves through the quotient fixed points can be nonalgebraic and Zariski dense. They cannot silently be excluded by claiming they lie in a proper algebraic subset. Instead, each has only a countable set of labels on a local transversal, because the torus labels are taken modulo Lambda=2 pi i(Z+t Z). Removing finitely many such leaves therefore removes at most countably many transverse labels. For each k>0, the condition for the k-th quotient iterate to fix a remaining leaf is

(lambda^k-1) ell in Lambda or (lambda^k+1) ell in Lambda,

where lambda=(3+sqrt(5))/2>1. This is another countable set, so it cannot contain a transverse open disk even after the exceptional labels are excluded. The argument proves the required failure of general leaf-fixing without any unjustified Zariski-open exclusion.

## Scope remains unchanged

The example is virtually transversely additive and is a counterexample only to the specified stronger conclusions (additivity downstairs and rational integrability after finite covers). The arbitrary singular dominant-rational conjecture remains unsolved. These clarifications make no novelty claim.
