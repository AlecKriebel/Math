# Five substantive approaches

These are five distinct routes examined during this attempt. Literature verification and prior-attempt retrieval are not additional turns. None resolves the full target.

## 1. Differentiate the symmetry group

Normalize a rational conormal form by contraction with a transverse infinitesimal symmetry. Section 1 of PROOFS gives a complete Cartan-formula proof, including rational poles. This succeeds whenever such a rational vector field exists, in particular for a suitable connected algebraic-group action. It reproduces a standard mechanism rather than a new theorem of transverse dynamics.

The obstruction is the missing passage from one discrete rational map to a transverse infinitesimal symmetry. The monomial map in Section 3 has unbounded exponent matrices, and mere infinitude gives no bounded family from which to differentiate. Infinite leafwise translation on a product is a separate exact negative control against using ambient infinitude.

## 2. Force algebraic integrability or reduce the algebraic part

A rational first integral immediately supplies a closed rational form; that proof is in Section 2. The published algebraic-part reduction preserves the transverse problem, and its surface case is already known. The attempted stronger route was to derive a rational first integral after passing to a finite cover.

Sections 2–3 rule out that route as a general strategy: irrational logarithmic examples with infinite transverse action have differential constant field C, unchanged in every finite algebraic extension. Thus virtual additivity does not imply virtual algebraic integrability, even in dimension two. This control settles the proposed stronger intermediate claim negatively, without settling the original conjecture.

## 3. Descend a closed form from a finite cover

The attempted route was to average a closed defining form over the deck group. Section 4 constructs a double-cover model in which the deck involution sends beta to -beta. Its trace is zero; more strongly, any other closed rational defining form upstairs is a nonzero constant multiple, so no closed defining form descends.

The exact invariant-field model supplies smooth projective X, a rational degree-two cover, a birational f with infinite transverse action, and a rational alpha whose integrating factor is sqrt(s)^(-1). This verifies the necessity of retaining the virtual conclusion. A new cover must be constructed in the unsolved case; arbitrary averaging or unproved lifting of f cannot do it.

## 4. Restrict to orbit closures and slice down to a surface

The source reduction was checked with its hypotheses rather than extrapolated. On the purely transcendental quotient, one can pass to general orbit closures; the low-dimensional cases are established by the source. The extension theorem used there requires a genuine algebraic fibration and the appropriate transverse structure on a very general fiber, with no rational first integral for that restriction. A general complete-intersection surface is not an invariant orbit closure.

A simple exact control: on (C*)^3, q(x,y,z)=(x^2,y^3,z^5) has Zariski-dense forward orbit at (2,3,5). To see density, a polynomial vanishing on all iterates would give a finite sum c_(a,b,c) 2^(a 2^n) 3^(b 3^n) 5^(c 5^n)=0. Order nonzero terms lexicographically by (c,b,a), divide by the largest term, and let n grow; every other ratio tends to zero, contradiction. Its preserved closed form dz/z already satisfies the desired conclusion. Yet the surface x+y+z=1 is not preserved: (1/3,1/3,1/3) lies on it and maps to a point whose coordinate sum is 37/243, not 1. Surface restriction cannot be invoked merely because an ambient foliation exists.

This control is not a difficult-case counterexample. The unresolved case remains a purely transcendental foliation on an orbit closure of dimension at least three, without an established transverse structure. The known surface theorem has no identified input there.

## 5. Use the pullback cocycle, positivity, or transverse jets

Section 5 derives the exact pullback-covariance equation and its gauge ambiguity. If a flat affine connection has already been found and its transported connection differs, a closed defining form follows. In general neither flatness nor algebraicity of an integrating factor follows from the covariance equation.

The published transversely projective result would finish the task if such a structure were constructed; it cannot supply its own missing hypothesis. The Pic(X)=Z result uses a regular map of degree >1, and the adjoint-general-type result uses explicit birational, singularity, and positivity hypotheses. No argument here verifies those hypotheses for arbitrary f. The 2025 jet-instability and regular negative-transverse-curvature results add other sufficient assumptions, which likewise were not deduced from the target's dynamics.

No new general theorem emerges. Exact algebra, examples, or unsuccessful route changes are not evidence that the conjecture has been disproved. Final author disposition: **unsolved, five of five approaches used**.
