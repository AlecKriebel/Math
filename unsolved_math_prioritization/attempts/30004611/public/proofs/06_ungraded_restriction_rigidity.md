# Ungraded lattice-sheaf restriction maps: complete classification and epimorphism rigidity

14 October 2026 UTC. Row 1775 / 30004611, final shared substantive pass 5.
Authored mathematical component.

## 1. Result and scope

On the fixed common nonzero-flat poset used in the accepted earlier passes,
allow **every S-linear sheaf morphism**, with no degree-preservation condition
and no naturality across different arrangements. Then:

1. Every morphism from the normal-annihilating sheaf E to the Ziegler
   restriction i_*Z is A pi, where A is a polynomial matrix preserving the
   individual multiplicity lattices defined below. Such a sheaf epimorphism
   exists if and only if canonical pi is an epimorphism.
2. Every morphism D -> i_*T to the ordinary restriction is A rho + w nu,
   where A is a polynomial matrix preserving the individual ordinary
   hyperplane lattices and w is an arbitrary polynomial logarithmic
   derivation of the restricted arrangement. Such a sheaf epimorphism exists
   if and only if canonical rho is an epimorphism.

Thus the two canonical defects in the earlier four-plane and seven-plane
examples cannot be removed by choosing ungraded arrows on this poset. This
rules out short exact sequences with these source and quotient sheaves,
regardless of their proposed kernels, whether graded or ungraded. It does
not rule out arbitrary S-linear epimorphisms at an isolated ordinary stalk:
sheaf compatibility is essential to the ordinary theorem. It does not
resolve the broad constructive source question, change the named terms, or
identify cohomology on a new comparison space. No retained-v3 freeness
criterion is used.

## 2. Setup and the polynomial lattice algebras

Let A be a finite central arrangement in V over a field K, let H in A be
marked by a fixed equation alpha, and assume dim V=ell>=3. Put
S=Sym(V*), R=S/(alpha). Essentiality is allowed but is not needed in the
proofs here. Let P be the nonzero geometric flats ordered by inclusion, let
Q={X in P:X subset H}, and let i:Q->P be the inclusion. We use the previously
fixed covariant functors

    D_X = D(A_X),
    E_X = {theta in D_X : theta(alpha)=0},
    T_X = D(B_X),
    Z_X = D(B_X,m),          X in Q,

where B=A^H and m is its Ziegler multiplicity. The target sheaves are zero
off Q. All their maps toward their H-stalk are the actual inclusions. Write

    rho:D -> i_*T,
    pi:E -> i_*Z

for canonical reduction. We also use pi_X for its same reduced image inside
T_X, since Z_X is a submodule of T_X. For X in Q define

    nu_X(theta) = theta(alpha)/alpha mod alpha.

Fix coordinates on H and identify Der_K(R)=R tensor_K H. For every restricted
hyperplane K0 in B choose an equation beta=beta_K0, a vector n with beta(n)=1,
and the multiplicity lattice

    L_K0^(d) = R beta^d n + R tensor_K K0
             = {delta in Der_K(R): delta(beta) in beta^d R}.

Its definition is independent of the chosen n. Write

    A_d = {A in End_R(Der_K(R)) :
                  A L_K0^(d(K0)) subset L_K0^(d(K0))
                  for every K0 in B}.

We denote by A_1 the ordinary case d(K0)=1 and by A_m the actual Ziegler
multiplicities. These are algebras of **polynomial matrices**, not just
constant endomorphisms of H. Their condition can equivalently be tested as

    beta(A v) in beta^d R for every constant vector v in K0.       (2.1)

Indeed L_K0^(d) is generated by those tangent constant vectors and beta^d n;
the latter's image automatically satisfies the required divisibility.
For every X in Q,

    T_X = intersection_(K0 containing X) L_K0^(1),
    Z_X = intersection_(K0 containing X) L_K0^(m(K0)).             (2.2)

Consequently A_1 preserves every T_X and A_m preserves every Z_X.

## 3. Classification of all ungraded maps E -> i_*Z

### Theorem 3.1

    Hom_S-sheaves(E,i_*Z) = {A pi : A in A_m},                    (3.1)

with unique A.

Proof. Choose coordinates z=alpha,t on V. At H one has E_H=S tensor_K H and
Z_H=R tensor_K H. Any S-linear map is uniquely reduction followed by a
polynomial matrix A over R. Naturality of every arrow X->H and injectivity
of Z_X->Z_H force all other components to be the restrictions of this map.

Each K0 in B is a geometric flat of A of dimension ell-2, so it is a
nonzero flat retained in P. At this flat choose t=beta_K0 and coordinates u
on K0. The members of A_K0 other than H have equations t+c_j z, with
q=product_j(t+c_j z) and m(K0) factors. Direct tangency gives

    E_K0 = S q partial_t + sum_u S partial_u.

Hence reduction maps E_K0 **onto** L_K0^(m(K0)). The required K0-stalk
condition is exactly A L_K0^(m(K0)) subset L_K0^(m(K0)). These conditions
are necessary. They are sufficient by (2.2), because pi_X(E_X) already lies
in Z_X and A preserves Z_X. Maps off Q are zero and impose no additional
conditions. This proves the classification and uniqueness. QED.

### Theorem 3.2: ungraded Ziegler epimorphism rigidity

A sheaf epimorphism E -> i_*Z exists if and only if canonical pi is an
epimorphism.

Proof. An epimorphism A pi is onto at H. At H pi is reduction of a free
rank-(ell-1) module, so the square polynomial matrix A is onto over R and
therefore det A is a unit. In particular A belongs to GL_(ell-1)(R).

It remains to show that A^-1 preserves the same lattices; this is not being
assumed merely because A preserves their intersection. For each K0 use the
basis of L_K0^(m(K0)) consisting of beta^m n and constant tangent vectors,
and let P_K0 be its basis matrix in Der(R). It is invertible over Frac(R).
Preservation of this individual lattice says

    C_K0 = P_K0^-1 A P_K0 has entries in R.

Its determinant is det A, a unit. The adjugate formula therefore gives
C_K0^-1 in Mat_(ell-1)(R), proving A^-1 preserves this lattice. It preserves
every Z_X by (2.2). Thus A is a sheaf automorphism of i_*Z, and

    coker(A pi) is isomorphic to coker(pi).

The asserted equivalence follows. QED.

No Smith normal form over a multivariate polynomial ring is needed.

## 4. Classification of all ungraded maps D -> i_*T

### Theorem 4.1

Every S-linear sheaf map D -> i_*T has the unique form

    Phi_(A,w) = A rho + w nu,      A in A_1, w in D(B).           (4.1)

Here w nu means scalar multiplication of the polynomial vector field w by
nu(theta). There is no bound on the polynomial degrees of A or w.

Proof. At H,

    D_H = S z partial_z direct-sum (S tensor_K H),
    T_H = R tensor_K H.

An arbitrary S-linear map is specified by a polynomial matrix A on the
tangential basis and a polynomial vector field w as the image of
z partial_z. Thus it has form (4.1) before the other stalks are imposed.
The targets' inclusions into T_H force the remaining maps uniquely.

At K0 use the same coordinates as in Section 3. The elementary rank-two
basis is

    D_K0 = S(z partial_z+t partial_t)
                 direct-sum S q partial_t
                 direct-sum sum_u S partial_u.

The constant tangent directions force beta(A v) in beta R for every v in
K0, equivalently A in A_1. Applied to z partial_z+t partial_t, tangency of
its image then says beta(w) in beta R: the contribution beta(A(t partial_t))
already contains t=beta. For all K0 these conditions say w in D(B).
Conversely A in A_1 preserves every T_X and w lies in every T_X, so (4.1)
defines the required sheaf map. This proves necessity, sufficiency, and
uniqueness. QED.

Although a complement to H was used in the proof, the formula uses intrinsic
rho and nu. Changing the complement changes z partial_z by z times a
constant tangent derivation, whose image in any alpha-annihilated target is
zero, so the parameters have the same intrinsic meaning.

For an ordinary epimorphism, its H-stalk is the rectangular map [A w].
It is incorrect to conclude from its surjectivity that det A is a unit.
The next argument avoids that issue entirely.

## 5. Euler independence lemma

We use coefficient grading only as a proof tool for the fixed arrangement
modules. We impose **no grading condition on Phi, A, or w**.

Fix X in Q, and write

    T=T_X,
    M=pi_X(E_X) subset T,
    m_R=the homogeneous maximal ideal of R,
    e=the Euler derivation epsilon_H in T.

Both T and M are finitely generated graded R-modules.

### Lemma 5.1

If e belongs to M+m_R T, then M=T.

Proof. Let U be the intersection of the restricted hyperplanes in B_X.
For the current flat-localization setup U=X. Split H=H' direct-sum U,
use t-coordinates on H', u-coordinates on U, and a normal coordinate
z=alpha. The arrangement B_X is essential in H' and has no conditions on
u-directions. Its constant logarithmic derivations are precisely U. Thus
T_0=U, and the H'-component of every element in (m_R T)_1 is zero.

Every hyperplane in A_X other than H has an equation

    beta_i(t)+c_i z,                                         (5.1)

where beta_i is nonzero; repetition of proportional beta_i is permitted at
this stage. Rescale equations to use the same beta_i within any restriction
fiber. These equations have no u-coefficients because their hyperplanes
contain U=X.

If H'=0 then A_X={H}, and reduction E_X->T is already onto. Otherwise take
the homogeneous degree-one part of e in M+m_R T. It gives an element of
M_1 whose H'-component is exactly sum t_j partial_(t_j). Since M is the
image of a degree-preserving map, this element has a preimage eta in
(E_X)_1. The H'-coefficient vector of eta must therefore be

    t+z v,       v in H' constant:                            (5.2)

any linear u-term or other linear t-term would survive at z=0 and contradict
the specified H'-component. Its U-coefficient vector is irrelevant to the
equations (5.1), and eta(z)=0 by definition.

Tangency to (5.1) now requires the linear polynomial

    beta_i(t)+z beta_i(v)

to be divisible by beta_i(t)+c_i z. Comparison of their nonzero t-parts
makes the constant quotient exactly 1. Consequently

    beta_i(v)=c_i for every i.                                (5.3)

In particular, distinct original hyperplanes cannot have the same
restricted hyperplane: within such a fiber (5.3) makes their equations
identical. More importantly, putting t'=t+zv changes every equation (5.1)
to beta_i(t').

Given any delta in T, substitute t+zv for t in each of its polynomial
coefficients, retain the t- and u-derivative basis, and put its z-component
equal to zero. This is a derivation eta_delta in E_X: every required
beta_i-component is divisible by beta_i(t+zv), and there is no condition
from H beyond the zero z-component. At z=0 it restricts to delta. Thus
E_X->T is onto and M=T. QED.

This is a direct coordinate argument over every field. It does not use
freeness, characteristic-zero Euler division, or a linearization theorem.
In contrapositive form, if M is proper, then the class of e in T/m_R T is
linearly independent of the image of M.

## 6. Ungraded ordinary epimorphism rigidity

### Theorem 6.1

A sheaf epimorphism D -> i_*T exists if and only if canonical rho is an
epimorphism.

Proof. The reverse implication takes Phi=rho. For the forward implication,
let Phi=A rho+w nu be any sheaf epimorphism, classified by Theorem 4.1.
Suppose canonical rho fails to be onto at some X in Q. Use the notation of
Section 5 and set

    Vbar=T/m_R T,
    Mbar=(M+m_R T)/m_R T,
    ebar=e mod m_R T.

The exact S-module splitting

    D_X=S epsilon_V direct-sum E_X                            (6.1)

holds because epsilon_V(alpha)=alpha. Hence canonical image is

    im rho_X = M+R e.                                        (6.2)

The graded quotient T/(M+R e) is nonzero by assumption. Graded Nakayama
therefore implies

    dim_K(Mbar+K ebar) < dim_K Vbar.                          (6.3)

Here graded Nakayama applies only to these fixed graded modules and their
canonical image, not to Phi. Since M is proper, Lemma 5.1 gives

    dim_K(Mbar+K ebar)=dim_K Mbar+1.                           (6.4)

The polynomial matrix A preserves T by (2.2), so it induces a K-linear
endomorphism abar of Vbar. From (6.1), the image of Phi_X modulo m_R T is
contained in, and in fact equals,

    abar(Mbar)+K(abar(ebar)+wbar).                             (6.5)

Therefore its dimension is at most dim_K Mbar+1, which is strictly less
than dim_K Vbar by (6.3)-(6.4). It cannot be onto even on this fiber, whereas
surjectivity of Phi_X would force fiber surjectivity. This contradiction
proves the theorem. QED.

The proof neither assumes A is invertible nor that A preserves M. Indeed
both assumptions would be unjustified for an arbitrary ordinary map.
It also does not infer surjectivity of a degree-zero component of Phi.

A quantitative corollary is useful. Whenever canonical rho_X is not onto,
any sheaf map Phi satisfies

    dim_K((coker Phi_X) tensor_R K)
        >= dim_K((coker rho_X) tensor_R K) > 0,               (6.6)

Both cokernels are R-modules because their targets are; tensor_R K means
the homogeneous-maximal-ideal fiber. This follows from the same dimension
calculation and right exactness of tensor product, even though coker Phi_X
itself need not be graded. Equivalently, one may first factor either map
through the R-module D_X/alpha D_X before taking its fiber.

## 7. Consequences for the retained explicit defects

### 7.1 Ordinary seven-plane example

Over a field of characteristic different from 2, take the seven equations

    z, x, y, x-z, y-z, x-y+z, x-y-z

in three variables, mark z, and promote by the separate equation w=0 in
four variables. The nonzero w-axis X remains in the common poset. Pass 4
proved at X that canonical ordinary reduction has cokernel

    K[x,y,w]/(x+y,xy)(-2),

with the free partial_w summand mapping onto. In particular rho_X is not
onto. Theorem 6.1 therefore proves that **no ungraded S-linear sheaf
epimorphism D -> i_*T exists** for this promoted arrangement. Any short
exact sequence ending in these two named sheaves is impossible, regardless
of the first term, its grading, or the proposed arrows.

This is deliberately a sheaf statement. An arbitrary S-linear map from the
isolated D_X to T_X need not arise from an ambient polynomial A at H and
need not induce the endomorphism abar used in (6.5). No claim that all such
isolated-stalk epimorphisms are impossible follows from this argument.

### 7.2 Normal-annihilating four-plane example

For the four equations x,y,z,x+y+z, mark z and again promote by w=0. At the
retained w-axis, the earlier passes proved that canonical pi has nonzero
cokernel K[w](-1), over every field. Theorem 3.2 proves that **no ungraded
S-linear sheaf epimorphism E -> i_*Z exists**. Thus no short exact sequence
ending in these named source and quotient sheaves can be obtained merely
by altering arrows or kernel terms.

Here the Ziegler multiplicities are all one. This does not rule out the
ordinary D/deletion sequence, which has a different source and is exact
in this four-plane example.

## 8. Optional direct D-to-Z classification

For completeness the same proof also classifies direct maps, without any
new assertion of epimorphism rigidity for this different pair of terms:

    Hom_S-sheaves(D,i_*Z)
        = {(A,h): A in A_m, h in D(B,m)}.                     (8.1)

For theta in D_X, put f=theta(alpha)/alpha. The map belonging to (A,h) is

    theta |-> A pi_X(theta-f epsilon_V)+(f mod alpha) h.      (8.2)

To see necessity, restrict the map to E and apply Theorem 3.1. The image h
of epsilon_V must lie in every Z_K0 and thus in D(B,m); the rank-two flats
are all present. Conversely these conditions make (8.2) land in every Z_X.
The splitting (6.1) makes the parameters unique. This classification allows
arbitrary polynomial h and should not be confused with the graded scalar
family or with the E-to-Z theorem.

## 9. Exact scope of the completed gap

4, and 6 close that gap for the fixed common poset and the named two source-
quotient pairs. The resulting impossibility statements have no hidden
requirement of degree preservation or cross-arrangement naturality.
