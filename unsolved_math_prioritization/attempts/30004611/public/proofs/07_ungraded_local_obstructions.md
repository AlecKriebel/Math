# Ungraded fixed-term local obstructions and a genuine stalk epimorphism

14 October 2026 UTC. Row 1775 / 30004611, final shared substantive pass 5.
Authored component, not an independent acceptance report. These are statements about the
accepted concrete terms, not a solution or impossibility theorem for the
broad source Problem 6.3. No freeness criterion from retained v3 is used.

## 1. Results and exact distinction

After forgetting every grading:

1. The promoted four-plane Ziegler example admits no S-linear epimorphism
   E_X -> Z_X at its bad stalk, even with no sheaf compatibility imposed.
2. The promoted seven-plane ordinary example DOES admit S-linear stalk
   epimorphisms D_X -> T_X. An explicit one is given below. However, no
   exact sequence 0 -> F_X -> D_X -> T_X -> 0 exists with its actual deletion
   module F_X and arbitrary maps. An Ext-length obstruction proves this.
3. In both cases a rank-one locally free tensor twist on the proposed
   kernel cannot change the obstruction, since it trivializes at the
   polynomial prime used in the proof. Its transition maps are irrelevant.
4. The pass-4 rank-two Hilbert obstruction to the direct D/F/Z shape does
   NOT survive forgetting grading at an isolated stalk: all three terms
   there are abstractly free over S or R, and an ungraded exact sequence
   with those terms exists. This makes no claim about compatibility of
   such choices across a lattice.

The seven-plane stalk epimorphism does not contradict sheaf-map rigidity:
an arbitrary S-linear homomorphism on one nonfree module need not extend
from the polynomial coefficient matrix forced at the top H of the poset.
The separate fixed-poset classification component addresses that constraint.

## 2. Four-plane Ziegler target: no epimorphism even at the stalk

Let S=K[x,y,z,w], R=S/(z), and mark z in the arrangement with equations
x,y,z,x+y+z,w. At the retained w-axis X, the plane w=0 drops out. Write
u=x+y+z. The accepted pass-3 presentation gives, as ungraded modules,

    E_X = S partial_w direct-sum coker[S --(u,-y,x)--> S^3].

Its Ziegler restriction has multiplicity one and is the free R-module

    Z_X = R epsilon direct-sum R eta direct-sum R partial_w,
    epsilon=x partial_x+y partial_y,  eta=x(x+y) partial_x.

Modulo z, the column (x+y,-y,x) is equivalent by constant invertible row
operations to (0,x,y). The row operation replacing the first coordinate by
first+second-third kills its first entry. Hence

    E_X/zE_X is isomorphic to R^2 direct-sum (x,y)R.             (2.1)

For clarity, coker[R --(x,y)--> R^2] is isomorphic to (x,y), using
(a,b) -> -ya+xb; the kernel is exactly R(x,y) because x,y are coprime.
This proves in particular that (2.1) is torsion-free of rank three.
At q=(x,y) in R, with residue field K(w), it has four minimal generators,
whereas a free rank-three R_q-module has three.

Every S-linear map E_X -> Z_X factors through E_X/zE_X. If it were onto,
it would split because Z_X is R-free. Its kernel would be torsion-free of
rank zero, since both modules have R-rank three, so that kernel would be
zero. This would identify the nonfree module (2.1) with R^3, a contradiction.
This argument works over every field, including characteristic two.

In particular, an arbitrary sequence 0 -> L tensor E_X -> E_X -> Z_X -> 0
is impossible for every rank-one locally free L, and indeed for every
proposed kernel whatsoever.

## 3. Seven-plane presentations, with completeness verified

Assume char K != 2. In S0=K[x,y,z] take the equations

    z, x, y, x-z, y-z, x-y+z, x-y-z,

with z marked, and put

    p=x(x-z), q=y(y-z), r=(x-y)^2-z^2,
    b=3x-y-z, c=y-x.

The normal-z submodule E0 has the following tangent pair generators,
where (A,B) means A partial_x+B partial_y:

    h1=(p(-x+3y-z), q(3x-y-z)),
    h2=(-pq,-pq),
    h3=(-pr,0).

They satisfy

    p h1+b h2+c h3=0.                                         (3.1)

Their full relation module is generated by this column. Indeed h2,h3 are
independent over the fraction field, so every relation is a rational
multiple of (p,b,c). Its entries have greatest common divisor one: the
independent linear forms b,c are coprime. In a UFD a rational multiple of a
primitive polynomial vector can have all polynomial entries only when
that rational multiplier is polynomial.

Thus the submodule they generate has Hilbert numerator  t^3+2t^4-t^5.
The exact accepted pass-4 calculation establishes this same Hilbert series
for E0 over every field of characteristic different from two. Inclusion
of these homogeneous submodules with equal Hilbert series proves equality.
This use of grading verifies a presentation; the obstruction below does
not assume any proposed arrow preserves grading.

The Euler splitting D(A7)=S0 epsilon_V direct-sum E0 therefore gives

    0 -> S0 --(0,p,b,c)--> S0^4 -> D(A7) -> 0                 (3.2)

after forgetting shifts.

For the deletion A7 minus {z=0}, instead split off the Euler derivation
using its x coefficient. Its normal-x part consists of pairs (B,C), meaning
B partial_y+C partial_z, satisfying

    y divides B,  x-z divides C,
    (y-z)(x-y+z) divides B-C,  x-y-z divides B+C.

Put

    a=-3xy+xz+3y^2+z^2,
    d=y(x-y-z), e=z-x-y.

Three explicit tangent pairs are

    f1=(-y(x-z)(x-y-z), -y(x-z)(x-y-z)),
    f2=(y(-2x^2+3xy+3xz-y^2-3z^2), (x-z)a),
    f3=(y(y-z)r, 0).

They satisfy

    a f1+d f2+e f3=0.                                         (3.3)

Tangency is a direct divisibility check. For f2 the useful identities are

    B-C=(y-z)(x-y+z)(x+y-z),
    B+C=-(x-y-z)(5xy-xz-y^2-2yz-z^2).

The same fraction-field argument proves that (3.3) generates all relations:
f1,f3 are independent, and (a,d,e) is primitive because e is linear while
d modulo e equals -2y^2, which is nonzero in the stated characteristics.
The generated normal-x submodule consequently has numerator
2t^3+t^4-t^5. This equals the independently established pass-4 deletion
Hilbert series after subtracting the Euler summand. Hence these three
pairs generate the whole normal-x module. We obtain the exact presentation

    0 -> S0 --(0,a,d,e)--> S0^4 -> D(A7 minus H) -> 0.          (3.4)

All identities and primitiveness arguments are valid over every field
of characteristic different from two; no characteristic-zero extrapolation
from numerical ranks is being made.

Promote by the separate equation w=0, set S=S0[w], and retain the w-axis X.
At X, D_X and F_X are obtained from (3.2) and (3.4) by scalar extension and
one extra free generator partial_w. Thus their presentations are the same
columns with one additional zero entry.

## 4. Ext length rules out every exact ordinary extension

Apply Hom_S(-,S) to the two free presentations. It gives

    Ext^1_S(D_X,S) is isomorphic to S/(p,b,c),
    Ext^1_S(F_X,S) is isomorphic to S/(a,d,e).

The exact ideal identities are

    (p,b,c) = (y-x,z-2x,x^2),
    (a,d,e) = (z-x-y,x^2,y^2).                               (4.1)

For the first, eliminate y=x and z=2x; p becomes -x^2. For the second,
eliminate z=x+y; a becomes 2x^2+4y^2 and d becomes -2y^2. The assumed
invertibility of 2 proves both identities directly.

At the prime P=(x,y,z) of S, the residue field is K(w). Formula (4.1)
therefore gives finite lengths

    length_{S_P} Ext^1_S(D_X,S)_P = 2,
    length_{S_P} Ext^1_S(F_X,S)_P = 4.                        (4.2)

The ordinary restriction of the seven-plane factor has equations x,y,x-y;
its module is free with basis epsilon=x partial_x+y partial_y and
eta=x(x-y) partial_x. Thus after promotion

    T_X is isomorphic to (S/(z))^3

as an ungraded S-module. In particular pd_S T_X=1. Any proposed exact
sequence with arbitrary ungraded S-linear maps

    0 -> F_X -> D_X -> T_X -> 0

would, upon applying Hom(-,S) and localizing, give a surjection

    Ext^1_S(D_X,S)_P -> Ext^1_S(F_X,S)_P,

because Ext^2_S(T_X,S)=0. This contradicts (4.2). This is a map-independent,
ungraded obstruction using the actual deletion term, not a Hilbert-series
comparison of the proposed sequence.

The same proof works with L tensor F_X as kernel whenever L_P is a free
rank-one S_P-module. In particular every stalkwise rank-one locally free
sheaf twist is excluded at this one point, without assumptions on its
transition maps. Local freeness may be interpreted on Spec S; only
triviality after localization at P is used.

## 5. Nevertheless an arbitrary ordinary stalk epimorphism exists

Reducing (3.2) modulo z gives an Euler free summand and the relation column

    (x^2,3x-y,y-x).

Subtract x/2 times its second and third coordinates from its first.
The column becomes (0,3x-y,y-x). Since these last two linear forms span
(x,y), the same elementary cokernel calculation as in Section 2 gives

    D_X/zD_X is isomorphic to R^3 direct-sum (x,y)R.            (5.1)

Here two free summands come from epsilon_V and partial_w; the third comes
from the zero coordinate of the transformed column. Thus projection onto
R^3 proves the claimed epimorphism to T_X.

An explicit S-linear epimorphism is specified on generators by

    epsilon_V -> epsilon,
    partial_w -> partial_w,
    h1 -> eta,
    h2 -> -(x/2) eta,
    h3 -> -(x/2) eta.

The only relation maps to

    [x^2-(x/2)(3x-y)-(x/2)(y-x)] eta=0,

and the target basis is visibly in the image. The map is not degree zero:
h1 has coefficient degree three while eta has coefficient degree two.
Section 4 proves that its kernel, and the kernel of every other surjection
with this target, cannot be isomorphic to F_X. This is precisely the
remaining local freedom that a graded Hilbert obstruction alone misses.

## 6. Direct D/deletion/Z at a rank-two stalk

At a nonzero rank-two flat K with restriction multiplicity m>=2, the
accepted elementary rank-two basis calculations give abstract ungraded
isomorphisms

    D_K isomorphic to S^ell,
    F_K isomorphic to S^ell,
    Z_K isomorphic to (S/(alpha))^(ell-1).

Consequently there always exists an ungraded exact sequence with those
three abstract modules: under any chosen such identifications, take

    F_K -> D_K : diag(1,alpha,...,alpha),
    D_K -> Z_K : reduce the last ell-1 coordinates modulo alpha.

The first map is injective and its image is exactly the second kernel.
This works over every field. It shows why the pass-4 rank-two discrepancy
(t^m-t)/(1-t)^(ell-1) is genuinely a degree-preserving obstruction, rather
than an ungraded isomorphism invariant. It does not show that the chosen
identifications glue to a sheaf sequence with the actual transition maps,
or supply a universal direct D/F/Z construction. Those are separate
compatibility questions, outside this local calculation.

## 7. Verification and scope

The accompanying check_local_obstructions.py verifies the two full
polynomial relation identities, all required tangency divisibilities,
both ideal identities by mutual Groebner reduction, and the explicit
ordinary epimorphism's relation. It also checks the relevant quotient
lengths from standard monomial bases. Its output is RESULTS.json.

The presentation-completeness argument uses exact Hilbert series already
proved in accepted pass 4, solely to identify the displayed finite
presentations. The nonexistence conclusions use torsion-freeness and
minimal generators, or local Ext lengths; they apply to all S-linear maps
without any grading hypothesis.

The four- and seven-plane source examples remain credited to Abe–Kawanoue,
arXiv:2406.00305v2, Example 4.3, as in the accepted passes. The calculations
here are bounded refinements of these fixed examples. They neither
classify arbitrary replacement terms/comparison spaces nor claim the
broad source short-exact-sequence problem solved.
