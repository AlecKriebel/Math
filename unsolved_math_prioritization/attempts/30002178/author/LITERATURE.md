# Primary literature checked, 2026-10-05

No full resolution was verified in the primary sources inspected. This is a bounded search conclusion, not proof of global openness. The distinction between a lower bound for every nonzero sum and an upper construction, exceptional-set statement, or average is essential.

1. **Thang Le, “On homology growth of finite covering,” in Low-Dimensional Topology and Number Theory, Oberwolfach Report 42/2012.** Conjecture 7, printed p.2574, is the target with fixed number of summands and all sufficiently large common conductors. Publisher PDF and rendered page checked. [Publisher](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/12014), [PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46413). The workshop was in 2012; the report's publisher page gives publication in 2013.

2. **Jincheng Zhuang, Qi Cheng, Jiejing Wen, “Solution counts and sums of roots of unity,” Journal of Number Theory 240 (2022), 551–561.** Introduction/Problem 1 and the finite-field counting method inspected in an author-hosted PDF. It states the same polynomial-lower-bound question, and distinguishes repeated-root and distinct-root variants. Its counting identities do not supply the missing uniform polynomial estimate. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022314X22000518), [Author PDF](https://qcheng2023.github.io/paper/sum_roots.pdf).

3. **Ben Barber, “Small sums of five roots of unity,” arXiv:2104.15057v2, revised 16 February 2023.** Introduction, Proposition 3, and main result inspected. The five-term results are upper constructions, including O(N^{-4/3}) for all N and a stronger subsequence. Proposition 3 treats m<=4 and corrects an older three-term formula. None proves the missing lower bound for m=5. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.15057v2).

4. **P. Habegger, “The Norm of Gaussian Periods,” arXiv:1611.07287v1; Q. J. Math. 69 (2018), 153–182.** Theorem 3 counts exceptionally small Galois conjugates for each prime p: after choosing c,lambda depending on the number of terms and epsilon, the number of bad t is at most c p^epsilon. A small exceptional set may still contain the particular embedding being bounded. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07287v1).

5. **P. Habegger, “Diophantine Approximations on Definable Sets,” Selecta Math. (N.S.) 24 (2018), 1633–1675, DOI 10.1007/s00029-017-0378-7.** Theorem 7 in arXiv:1608.04547v1 bounds the count of bad prime orders up to T by c T^epsilon for fixed nonzero coefficients. This is distinct from item 4: it allows a sparse exceptional set of primes rather than removing it. It is not an all-prime or all-conductor theorem. The theorem statement and proof location in Section 8 were inspected; the full imported o-minimal machinery was not independently reproved. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04547v1).

6. **Vesselin Dimitrov and Philipp Habegger, “Galois orbits of torsion points near atoral sets,” Algebra & Number Theory 18 (2024), 1945–2001.** Theorem 1.1 in arXiv:1909.06051v2 gives a power-saving estimate for the Galois average of log|P| under an essentially-atoral hypothesis and a strictness parameter. It does not bound each individual value by a fixed power of the conductor. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06051v2), [Publisher PDF](https://msp.org/ant/2024/18-11/ant-v18-n11-p01-s.pdf).

7. **Ido Nahshon and Amir Shpilka, “A new bound on cofactors of sparse polynomials,” Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 14 (2026), e52, published 31 March 2026.** Lemma 3.14 produces a suitable prime in a controlled interval, depending on the polynomial, with a lower bound at its nontrivial roots. The existential choice of prime is not a bound at every prescribed conductor. Publisher full text checked at Section 3.3. [Publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/new-bound-on-cofactors-of-sparse-polynomials/2CADDB77D2E5CBFB7B803CCCAC49D2FD).

8. **Gunther Cornelissen, David Hokken, Berend Ringeling, “The asymptotic Mahler measure of Gaussian periods,” arXiv:2507.09303v3, revised 19 June 2026.** Abstract and introduction checked. It concerns Mahler measures/heights and selected Gaussian periods. The individual complex modulus of every nonzero fixed-length sum is a different quantity. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09303v3).

9. **Alexandros Kalogirou, “Small sums of roots of unity,” arXiv:2607.06098v1, submitted 7 July 2026.** Introduction and Theorems 1–2 checked. Its results are upper bounds, including a seven-term O(N^{-3/2}) construction and exponents increasing with the number of terms. They do not settle the lower-bound target. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06098v1).

Older Myerson and Konyagin–Lev results were encountered through the later primary papers above; their original full texts were not independently inspected here. Attribution to those older results is secondary, and no claim is made to a complete historical bibliography. Publication status is recorded only as verified above; an arXiv listing alone is not evidence of journal acceptance.
