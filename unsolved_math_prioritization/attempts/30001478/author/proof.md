# A projective line quotient of R(alpha,beta)

## Result

For the six-relation algebra in OWR-4335-003 / problem 30001478, both stated conjectures are false. There is a nonzero proper homogeneous completely prime ideal

\[
 I=(x_3-x_1,\ x_4-x_2)\subsetneq R_+
\]

whose quotient is a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring of the projective line. The map is unital, degree-preserving, surjective, and has an infinite-dimensional image of GK-dimension 2. This is a complete algebraic counterexample to the stated assertions, not a classification of all primes or of surface-dimensional quotients. This note is unrefereed; publication priority is not claimed.

## The precise algebra

Let k be an uncountable algebraically closed field and let alpha,beta in k be algebraically independent over its prime subfield. Give every x_i degree one and put R=k<x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4>/(f_1,...,f_6), with

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=x_1(\alpha x_1-x_3)+x_3(x_1-\alpha x_3),\\
f_2&=x_1(\alpha x_2-x_4)+x_3(x_2-\alpha x_4),\\
f_3&=x_2(\alpha x_1-x_3)+x_4(x_1-\alpha x_3),\\
f_4&=x_2(\alpha x_2-x_4)+x_4(x_2-\alpha x_4),\\
f_5&=x_1(\beta x_1-x_2)+x_4(x_1-\beta x_2),\\
f_6&=x_1(\beta x_3-x_4)+x_4(x_3-\beta x_4).
\end{aligned}
\]

These relations and hypotheses are given in [1, Theorem 3, p.1361]; the two conjectural assertions follow on p.1362. The published article [2] repeats the assertions on p.1198 and gives the presentation on p.1202, Theorem 3.5(2), with c=(theta-1)/(theta+1), d=(rho-1)/(rho+1) in place of alpha,beta. We work directly from the displayed presentation, without importing any field-characteristic assumption from that parametrization.

## 1 The explicit quotient

After imposing x_3=x_1 and x_4=x_2, write X for x_1 and Y for x_2. The first four relations vanish identically in the free algebra k<X,Y>. Each of the last two becomes

\[
 Q=\beta X^2-XY+YX-\beta Y^2.
\]

Consequently, the presentation of the quotient is exactly

\[
 R/I\cong A:=k\langle X,Y\rangle/(Q).
\]

No extra relation is asserted or needed: this is the ordinary substitution of the two imposed linear relations in a free-algebra presentation.

## 2 An explicit twisted homogeneous coordinate ring

Algebraic independence gives beta^2 != 1. Define the degree-preserving polynomial-ring automorphism theta of S=k[s,t] by

\[
 \theta(s)=s+\beta t,\qquad \theta(t)=\beta s+t.
\]

Its matrix has determinant 1-beta^2, which is nonzero in every allowed characteristic. On the graded vector space S, define multiplication for homogeneous f of degree m by

\[
 f\star g=f\,\theta^m(g).
\]

Call this graded algebra B. Its multiplication is associative, since both (f star g) star h and f star (g star h) equal f theta^m(g) theta^{m+n}(h) when deg(g)=n. The identity is 1.

Let sigma be the automorphism of P^1_k whose pullback on homogeneous coordinate forms is theta, so in homogeneous coordinates it is [s:t] -> [s+beta t:beta s+t]. Using this chosen lift to O(1), the section ring B(P^1,O(1),sigma), with multiplication f sigma^{m*}(g), is precisely B: its degree n part is H^0(P^1,O(n))=S_n. Thus B is a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring of a positive-dimensional projective variety. The line bundle O(1) is sigma-ample, since all its sigma-pullbacks are O(1) and the successive tensor products are O(n).

The algebra B is generated by s,t. Indeed, if degree n is generated, the span of products B_n star B_1 is S_n theta^n(S_1)=S_n S_1=S_{n+1}. It is a domain: if nonzero elements have highest homogeneous components f_m and g_n, their product has highest component f_m theta^m(g_n), which is nonzero in the ordinary polynomial ring.

Send X to s and Y to t. In B the image of Q is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\beta s(s+\beta t)-s(\beta s+t)
       +t(s+\beta t)-\beta t(\beta s+t)\\
 &=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

This gives a surjective graded unital homomorphism A -> B.

## 3 The map is an isomorphism

To identify the kernel in R exactly, set U=X-Y and V=Y in the free algebra. This invertible linear change does not divide by 2. The relation becomes

\[
 \beta U^2+(\beta-1)UV+(\beta+1)VU=0.
\]

Since beta+1 != 0, it allows rewriting VU as a k-linear combination of UV and U^2. With fixed word length and lexicographic order V>U, both replacements decrease the word. Repeated rewriting therefore terminates. All irreducible words have the form U^i V^j. In degree n there are n+1 such words, so dim_k A_n <= n+1.

The surjection A -> B and dim_k B_n=dim_k S_n=n+1 give the reverse inequality in every degree. Hence A -> B is an isomorphism. This dimension argument needs only spanning, not an assumption that the rewriting system is confluent.

We have proved

\[
 R/(x_3-x_1,x_4-x_2)\cong B(P^1_k,O(1),\sigma).
\]

In particular H_{R/I}(z)=(1-z)^(-2) and GKdim(R/I)=2.

## 4 The ideal refutes the prime assertion

The defining ideal (f_1,...,f_6) of R is generated by homogeneous degree-two elements, so it has no degree-one component. Thus R_1 has basis x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4, and x_3-x_1 is nonzero. Therefore I is nonzero. It is homogeneous and two-sided by its definition. Since R/I is the domain B, I is completely prime, and hence is an ordinary two-sided prime and a graded prime.

Moreover I is contained in R_+, while x_1 is not in I because its image is the nonzero section s. Hence 0 != I != R_+. This disproves the first assertion. The quotient map to B is surjective and nontrivial in positive degrees, disproving the second assertion.

## Scope and boundary checks

- No specialization of alpha or beta is used. The stronger theorem holds over every field for arbitrary alpha and beta with beta^2 != 1, including characteristic 2.
- When char(k) != 2, U'=X+Y and V'=X-Y diagonalize the relation to (beta+1)U'V'+(beta-1)V'U'=0. This is the familiar quantum-plane form, but the proof above does not rely on that change of variables.
- This is a projective-line quotient, not just a map to k or a finite-dimensional quotient. It remains nonzero in arbitrarily high degrees, and its section dimensions grow as n+1.
- Independently, sending all x_i to z gives a graded surjection R -> k[z] for every alpha,beta and every characteristic. This simpler test is not the basis for the positive-dimensional result above.
- A twisted homogeneous coordinate ring is generally noncommutative. We use its twisted multiplication explicitly; we do not replace the problem by one about ordinary commutative coordinate rings.
- The construction does not embed R in B, identify their graded quotient rings, or produce a dominant/surface-dimensional image. It does not contradict a theorem excluding such embeddings or birational realizations. The source's stated universal map and prime claims, however, do not contain those restrictions.
- The proof uses neither noetherianity of R nor the classification of birational dynamics. Such results are unnecessary for this quotient.
