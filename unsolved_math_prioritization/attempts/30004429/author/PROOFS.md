# Retained mathematics and exact boundaries

## 1. Governing setup and quantifiers

Let S = {−1,+1}, let Ω̃ = S^(Z²), and give S the discrete topology and Ω̃ the product topology. The zero-field nearest-neighbor Ising measure in a finite box V with plus boundary assigns weight proportional to

    exp(β sum_{unordered nearest-neighbor edges meeting V} σ_i σ_j),

where spins outside V equal +1. Its weak limit is μ⁺_β. Fix finite β > βc = log(1+√2)/2. The Schonmann projection ν = ν⁺_β is the law of X_i = σ_(i,0) under μ⁺_β on Ω = S^Z. The time evolution in the one-dimensional interpretation is the left shift; no extra dynamics is part of the question.

A one-sided transition function g(a | x⁻) conditions on all coordinates i < 0. A two-sided singleton conditional q(η) conditions X_0 on all i ≠ 0, where η belongs to E = S^(Z\{0}). Let ρ be ν's marginal on E. Both spaces carry their product topology and Borel σ-algebra. Cylinder neighborhoods of η fix η_i for 0 < |i| ≤ n.

A full two-sided specification is a family γ_Λ of proper probability kernels for finite Λ ⊂ Z, measurable in the exterior, consistent under conditioning on nested finite volumes, and satisfying νγ_Λ = ν. The almost-Gibbs formulation asks for such a version with continuity at ν-almost every configuration for every finite Λ and local observable. Bethuelsen–Conache Definition 2.1 formulates the question using a positive normalized singleton kernel γ(η,a), continuous at ν-almost every configuration and equal to the singleton conditional almost surely. The reduction below proves an exact criterion for this singleton formulation, the formulation used in that primary paper. It does not assert that arbitrary independently chosen singleton versions automatically constitute a globally consistent specification.

The quantifier on a version is existential. One cannot require an arbitrary version to have a null discontinuity set. At a continuity point, the neighboring configurations range over the entire product space, not merely over some selected typical set. In particular, a null configuration can still be a topologically important limit point.

The source question is a family question in the phase-transition regime; it does not single out a numerical β. We prove neither an affirmative nor negative answer for that regime or its sufficiently-low-temperature subregime. A different phase mixture, a finite strip, a zero-temperature limit or a decimated marginal is a different problem.

## 2. Finite energy and support

Put cβ = 1/(1+exp(8β)). Under any nearest-neighbor zero-field Ising Gibbs state, the conditional probability that a spin is +1 given all other spins is

    1 / (1 + exp(−2β sum_{its four neighbors} σ_j)).

It belongs to [cβ,1−cβ]. Taking a conditional expectation proves the same bound when conditioning on any σ-algebra generated by other spins, including an arbitrary subset of line spins. Thus ν has singleton finite energy, and every finite conditional q_n below lies in [cβ,1−cβ]. The infinite conditional does too almost surely.

For any prescribed finite string at k distinct line sites, apply the chain rule and this bound successively. Its probability is at least cβ^k and at most (1−cβ)^k. Consequently ν and ρ have full support, and every individual configuration in either infinite space has probability zero. These statements are for finite β. They would fail as stated at zero temperature.

## 3. A version-invariant essential-oscillation theorem

The next theorem is elementary and applies to any binary process whose exterior marginal has full support. It is not claimed as new.

Let J_n = {i ∈ Z: 0 < |i| ≤ n}, and C_n(η) = {ζ ∈ E: ζ|J_n = η|J_n}. Define the uniquely specified finite conditional

    q_n(η) = ν(X_0 = +1, X|J_n = η|J_n) / ρ(C_n(η)).

The denominator is positive by full support. Let q be any measurable version of ν(X_0=+1 | X_i, i≠0). For each n set

    L_n(η) = ess inf_{ζ∈C_n(η), with respect to ρ} q(ζ),
    U_n(η) = ess sup_{ζ∈C_n(η), with respect to ρ} q(ζ),
    B_n(η) = U_n(η) − L_n(η),
    B_∞(η) = lim_{n→∞} B_n(η).

These quantities are unchanged if q is modified on a ρ-null set. They are measurable cylinder functions for each finite n. Because the cylinders are nested, L_n increases, U_n decreases, and B_n decreases.

**Theorem 1.** There is a version of q continuous at ρ-almost every exterior configuration if and only if ρ(B_∞=0)=1. More precisely,

    q*(η) = limsup_{m→∞} q_m(η)

is a version of q whose continuity set is exactly {B_∞=0}. In addition,

    L_n(η) = inf {q_m(ζ): m≥n, ζ∈C_n(η)},
    U_n(η) = sup {q_m(ζ): m≥n, ζ∈C_n(η)}.              (1)

All these claims concern the actual full-support exterior measure; they are not statements about points outside its support.

**Proof.** The conditional-expectation martingale theorem gives q_m → q almost surely. For a fixed cylinder C_n and m≥n, q_m on any m-cylinder contained in C_n is the average of q on that m-cylinder. Thus every such q_m lies between the essential infimum and supremum of q on C_n. This gives one inequality in each identity (1).

For the opposite inequality for U_n, let u be that essential supremum. For each ε>0, the set C_n ∩ {q>u−ε} has positive measure (the endpoint cases are harmless). On almost every point of that set, q_m converges to q, so for some m≥n one of its m-cylinder values exceeds u−2ε. Hence the supremum in (1) is at least u−2ε. Let ε↓0. The infimum identity follows in the same way.

The function q* is defined everywhere since 0≤q_m≤1. It equals q almost surely by martingale convergence. Fix k. Formula (1) implies that for every η and every m≥k,

    L_k(η) ≤ q_m(η) ≤ U_k(η).

The same bound holds for q*(η). If ζ∈C_k(η), then L_k(ζ)=L_k(η) and U_k(ζ)=U_k(η), so both q*(ζ) and q*(η) lie in the same interval of length B_k(η). If B_∞(η)=0, choose k with B_k(η)<ε; this proves continuity of q* at η.

Conversely, suppose any version r of q is continuous at η. For each ε>0, there is a cylinder C_n(η) on which all values of r differ from r(η) by less than ε. As r=q almost surely, B_n(η)≤2ε. Hence B_∞(η)=0. Applying this also to r=q* proves the exact continuity-set claim. ∎

For the Schonmann projection, q* lies in [cβ,1−cβ] everywhere by §2. The functions γ(η,+1)=q*(η) and γ(η,−1)=1−q*(η) are therefore everywhere positive and normalized. Theorem 1 supplies exactly the version requirement in the cited singleton definition.

A point with B_∞>0 is an essential discontinuity: no null-set modification can make any version continuous there. At each such point some ε>0 survives every cylinder, with positive-measure subsets carrying separated conditional values. This is stronger than merely arranging two exceptional sequences by hand.

## 4. Attraction reduces the extrema to monochromatic annuli

For n≤m define A_(n,m)=J_m\J_n and

    q_(n,m)^s(η) = ν(X_0=+1 | X|J_n=η|J_n, X|A_(n,m)≡s),  s∈{−1,+1}.

For m=n this is q_n. These are conditionals on finite, positive-probability events; no value at an infinite null boundary is being assigned.

**Theorem 2.** For the Schonmann projection,

    U_n(η) = lim_{m→∞} q_(n,m)^+(η),
    L_n(η) = lim_{m→∞} q_(n,m)^−(η).                 (2)

The first limit is increasing in m, the second decreasing. Consequently the exact singleton almost-Gibbs target is

    lim_{n→∞} lim_{m→∞} [q_(n,m)^+(η) − q_(n,m)^−(η)] = 0
    for ρ-almost every η.                            (3)

**Proof.** In a finite two-dimensional box with plus boundary, ferromagnetic monotonicity implies that the probability of the increasing event {σ_(0,0)=+1}, conditional on a prescribed collection of other spins, increases when any prescribed spin changes from minus to plus. This is the standard attractive Ising comparison, and remains valid with other pinned spins of either sign. Passing to the plus-phase limit preserves the comparison: every finite pinning event has positive limiting probability by §2, so the ratios of joint cylinder probabilities converge. This gives coordinatewise monotonicity of q_m on J_m.

For a fixed n,m and fixed inner string, the smallest q_m among all assignments on A_(n,m) is therefore q_(n,m)^− and the largest is q_(n,m)^+. When the annulus is enlarged, conditioning all newly exposed sites plus can only raise its conditional probability: the old conditional is an average over those new assignments, and all-plus is the largest. All-minus similarly can only lower it. The supremum/infimum over m in (1) are thus exactly the monotone limits (2). Theorem 1 yields (3). ∎

The unresolved step is (3), not the existence of its limits. If finite-box numerics are used, their order is first the two-dimensional plus-volume limit at fixed n,m, then m→∞, then n→∞. Interchanging these limits or fixing m/n without a uniform error bound does not prove (3).

One possible sufficient probabilistic estimate would be, for every rational ε>0,

    ρ(B_n>ε) → 0.                                   (4)

Indeed B_n↓B_∞, and {B_∞>ε}⊂{B_n>ε} for every n; (4) forces B_∞=0 almost surely. We have not proved (4). Summable bounds would also suffice but are stronger than necessary.

## 5. What a one-sided g-function can and cannot transfer

Assume a stationary binary process has a continuous positive normalized g-function. Compactness gives a uniform positive lower bound. For a past x⁻ and finite future y_1,…,y_m, define

    W_m(a) = g(a|x⁻) product_{j=1}^m g(y_j | x⁻ a y_1…y_(j−1)),
    Q_m = W_m(+1)/(W_m(+1)+W_m(−1)).

The chain rule shows that Q_m is a version of the conditional of X_0 given the entire past and the first m future coordinates. This identity holds for almost every past simultaneously for all finite words; positivity ensures that forcing either central symbol or a finite future word does not create a zero conditional denominator.

Its log odds are

    log(Q_m/(1−Q_m))
      = log(g(+1|x⁻)/g(−1|x⁻))
        + sum_{j=1}^m log[ g(y_j|x⁻ +1 y_1…y_(j−1))
                          /g(y_j|x⁻ −1 y_1…y_(j−1)) ].           (5)

Let v_k be the maximal variation of log g(a|·) between pasts agreeing on their last k symbols, maximized over a. The jth summand in (5) is bounded in absolute value by v_(j−1).

**Proposition 3.** If sum_{k≥0} v_k < ∞, then the singleton two-sided conditional has a continuous everywhere positive normalized version.

**Proof.** Every finite expression in (5) is continuous on E. The summable majorant gives uniform convergence to a finite continuous log-odds function. Applying the logistic function gives a continuous positive normalized kernel. The conditional martingale theorem identifies its almost-sure value with the two-sided conditional. ∎

This familiar sufficient condition cannot settle the target: Bethuelsen–Conache Proposition 3.2 rules out summable g-variations for the non-Gibbsian Schonmann marginal in the phase-transition regime. Since g is uniformly non-null, variation of g and of log g are comparable up to constants. The 2024 regular-g theorem does not supply the summability hypothesis.

A potentially weaker sufficient route is to control the tails of (5) locally uniformly near almost every η: for every ε>0, find a cylinder U containing η and K such that all m≥K and all ζ∈U have the tail from K+1 through m bounded by ε. Finite sums are continuous, so such local uniform Cauchy bounds yield continuity of the limiting conditional at η. No such two-sided local-uniform estimate is proved here. Almost-sure convergence along one fixed η, or square summability of perturbations along typical histories, does not on its own supply uniformity over U.

## 6. Exact controls against invalid shortcuts

### 6.1 An arbitrary version can be discontinuous everywhere

Let ν be the fair iid measure on S^Z. Let D be the set of configurations with only finitely many minus spins. It is measurable, countable, shift invariant, invariant under finite modifications, dense, and ν-null. Its complement is also dense.

Define p(η)=1/4 on D and p(η)=1/2 off D; membership in D is determined by the exterior of any finite Λ. Define γ_Λ by keeping the exterior fixed and replacing the spins in Λ independently, each with plus probability p(η).

These kernels form a proper specification. To check consistency for Λ⊂Δ, observe that modifying finitely many spins does not change D or p. Both resampling operations therefore use the same Bernoulli parameter, and resampling Λ after resampling Δ leaves the independent product law on Δ unchanged. Properness and exterior measurability are immediate. Since ν(D)=0, these kernels agree ν-almost surely with the fair iid specification, so ν is consistent with γ.

However γ_{0}(X_0=+1|·) takes the two separated values 1/4 and 1/2 on two dense sets, and is discontinuous at every point. The standard constant version is continuous everywhere. Thus the nullity of the discontinuity set of an arbitrarily chosen specification is not a property of the measure. Essential oscillation is zero everywhere here.

### 6.2 Martingale convergence and full support do not imply almost Gibbsianness

Let ν = (1/2)Bernoulli(1/4)^Z + (1/2)Bernoulli(3/4)^Z. It is stationary, has full support and uniform finite energy in [1/4,3/4]. The exterior empirical frequency identifies its latent parameter Θ∈{1/4,3/4} almost surely by the strong law. Thus q=Θ almost surely.

Inside every finite cylinder, both latent components still have positive conditional probability. Hence its essential conditional range is exactly [1/4,3/4]. Consequently B_n=1/2 for every n and every exterior configuration, and B_∞=1/2 everywhere. No version can be continuous anywhere. Nevertheless q_n→q almost surely, and each q_n is a continuous cylinder function. This is a counterexample to the proposed general martingale shortcut, **not** a counterexample for the extremal Ising projection: this mixture is nonergodic and does not have the required one-sided mixing.

For N observed spins of which k are plus, its exact finite conditional is

    q_N = [(1/4)(1/4)^k(3/4)^(N−k) + (3/4)(3/4)^k(1/4)^(N−k)]
          /[(1/4)^k(3/4)^(N−k) + (3/4)^k(1/4)^(N−k)].

Appended arbitrarily long all-plus and all-minus annuli force this quantity towards 3/4 and 1/4, respectively, whatever fixed central exterior block was prescribed.

### 6.3 Rare additional conditioning can amplify dependence even in an FKG family

Consider the same two Bernoulli parameters but give the high parameter prior mass λ_k=1/(1+3^k). Let X,Y and k evidence coordinates be conditionally iid given Θ. The unconditional covariance of X,Y is λ_k(1−λ_k)/4, tending to zero as k→∞. Conditional on all k evidence coordinates being plus, Bayes' formula gives equal posterior masses, and the covariance is exactly 1/16.

This family has the finite-dimensional FKG lattice condition. To check it, let w(r) be the probability of an individual string with r plus symbols out of N. It is a positive sum of two exponentials in r, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives w(r+1)^2≤w(r)w(r+2). This is the elementary two-coordinate lattice inequality at fixed other coordinates; chaining these inequalities gives log-supermodularity on the binary cube. The example demonstrates that attraction by itself does not allow an unconditional small-dependence estimate to be reused unchanged after rare evidence. It does not disprove any specific Ising estimate or produce a single φ-mixing counterexample.

## 7. Native summable long-range interactions are a different, elementary case

Let J_j=J_(−j) be pair couplings on Z with sum_{j≠0}|J_j|<∞ and fixed finite β. The canonical singleton specification of the original interaction is

    γ_0(+1|η) = 1/(1+exp(−2β sum_{j≠0} J_j η_j)).

If η and ζ agree for 0<|j|≤n, their fields differ by at most 2 sum_{|j|>n}|J_j|. The logistic derivative with respect to the field is at most β/2. Therefore

    |γ_0(+1|η)−γ_0(+1|ζ)| ≤ β sum_{|j|>n}|J_j| → 0.        (6)

For J_j=|j|^(−α), α>1, this is at most 2β n^(1−α)/(α−1). Changing finitely many near-neighbor couplings has no effect on the conclusion. For any finite Λ, the boundary-dependent Hamiltonian is a uniformly convergent sum, since its absolute bound is |Λ| sum_j|J_j|. All finite partition functions are finite positive sums, so the corresponding kernels are continuous everywhere. Coexistence of Gibbs phases does not alter this canonical specification.

Thus the original Dyson model has no canonical two-sided discontinuities at any finite β when α>1. This does not say that every version is continuous (see §6.1), nor that its one-sided transition kernel is continuous, nor that a projection or decimation is Gibbsian. The catalog's unqualified long-range extension cannot be assigned one of those meanings without additional specification. No long-range extension of the unresolved Schonmann statement is claimed.

## 8. Final gap

No argument above proves (3) or produces a positive-ρ set where its left-hand side stays positive. The exact unresolved quantity is a two-sided annulus influence after a typical central line segment is pinned. Later one-sided continuity, an isolated bad boundary, finite-volume experiments, and a null-set modification of a conditional version do not determine that quantity.
