# Literature boundary, checked 2026-10-05

The governing question is in Kevin Hutchinson's contribution to
[Oberwolfach Report 52/2016](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2016/52),
pp. 2958-2959. The subscript-zero group there is the kernel of the map to
indecomposable K3. The source also specifies the places and half-integral
coefficients. The imported kernel of localization to field homology is a
different group. The original report's eventual surjectivity assertion is
retained as an input, not independently proved for every global field here.

[The third homology of SL2(Q)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11650v4)
calculates the field group and its odd residue summands. The v4 abstract says
a gap in an earlier proof of Theorem 3.11 was repaired. This pass used v4,
not an earlier version. Its Theorem 4.3 is an imported theorem in the rational
reduction, not a new result. It does not by itself establish eventual
injectivity of the finite-S localization maps.

[The homology of SL2 of discrete valuation rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11159v2)
gives a right-exact localization sequence, with residue-field size conditions.
The inspected v2 introduction expressly leaves general left-hand injectivity
undetermined. The sequence alone is insufficient to remove the finite-S kernel.
The inspected preprint differs in layout and numbering from the 2022 published
article; this packet uses the preprint's numbered statements precisely.

[Bloch Groups of Rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04996), v3 of 10 February
2026, gives an explicit small-ring calculation used here: Proposition 8.22,
Lemma 8.24 and Proposition 8.25 account for both the group and the maps for
Z[1/2]. The calculation is credited. Its underlying third homology is credited
there to [Adem and Naffah](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9503230). The original
Adem-Naffah cohomology computation was also inspected at pp. 7-8.

[The norm-Euclidean quadratic imaginary case](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07819)
extends the field analysis. In inspected v1, Proposition 6.10 is a statement
after taking unit coinvariants for m=1,2,3,7,11. Corollary 6.12 removes that
operation for m=2,3,7,11, not for m=1. The full journal article was not
successfully opened in this pass. No unqualified Q(i) theorem or finite-S
consequence is silently inferred from the abstract.

[A refined scissors congruence group and the third homology of SL2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08872v2)
studies Bloch-Wigner and relative-homology sequences under ring hypotheses.
[Remarks on refined scissors congruence group and the third homology of SL2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08890v2),
revised 7 September 2026, relaxes hypotheses and gives examples for certain
Z[1/m]. Its Theorem 7.13 and Corollary 7.14 were inspected. These sequences
still involve the ring's refined Bloch group; they do not themselves calculate
the localization kernel needed in the present question. No general eventual
finite-S resolution was identified in these inspected results.

The pass additionally checked the introductions of
[The third homology of SL2 of local rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.5010)
and [The low dimensional homology groups of the elementary group of degree two](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17632v3),
including the latter's semilocal hypothesis in Theorem 6.3. A ring of
S-integers for finite S is not automatically a semilocal ring. Importing such
theorems without their hypotheses would be invalid.

This was a targeted current literature pass. Absence of a discovered resolution
is not a comprehensive proof of open status. Several scholarly results were
used or screened at their statements and relevant proof passages, not re-proved
from every original foundation. The version, hash, inspection scope and
retrieval limitations of each inspected PDF are in SOURCE_VERIFICATION.json.
