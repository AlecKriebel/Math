### Approach 3: rational localization reduction: PASS

[The third homology of SL2(Q), v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11650v4), Section 4 and Theorem 4.3, identify N_Q with the direct sum of valuation-character residue groups. The direct-sum claim is made on N_Q, not on all A_Q. The source explicitly distinguishes these domains. The action of a prime on its own residue summand is −1, on the others +1, and the sign class acts trivially. The v4 repair notice was checked; an earlier version was not substituted.

[The homology of SL2 of discrete valuation rings, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11159v2), Theorem 2.1, is right-exact and does not assert injectivity at the left. For a residue field of order p^d its size hypothesis is d(p−1)>6. For Z_(p), d=1, so all primes outside S are eligible once S contains 2,3,5,7. No completeness hypothesis is being silently imposed on Z_(p).

The normalization is correct. By Proposition 1.3 the finite-field module has trivial unit-squareclass action after inverting two. Lemma 2.2 gives rho_0(a+<p>b)=a+b and rho_p(a+<p>b)=b. On the augmentation image of N_Q the first value is zero. Valuation-character evaluation is a−b=−2b, giving s_p=−2 Delta_p on N_Q. Section 2.7 identifies Delta_p with the composite used in the exact sequence. These identifications are compatible with the refined-module comparison in the rational-field paper. Since −2 is a unit in R, the kernels agree.

For p outside S, O_S -> Q factors through Z_(p), hence Delta_p j_S=0. For x in N_S the field residue vector is therefore supported inside S. Injectivity of the full field specialization implies pi_S(x)=0 exactly when j_S(x)=0. This proves ker(pi_S)=L_S. Combining with the primary's eventual surjectivity gives the claimed equivalence with eventual injectivity of j_S on A_S. The proof does not use the right-exact sequence as though it began with zero.

### Approach 5: finite squareclass characters: PASS

For rational S-integers, sign and prime valuations establish the asserted independent squareclasses.
