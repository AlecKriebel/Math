## What the inspected papers establish

- Koberda–Mangahas–Taylor (2017), DOI 10.1090/tran/6933: the loxodromic support/centralizer characterization in its stated connected-graph setting, and stability/quasiconvexity of finitely generated purely loxodromic subgroups. Corollary 1.5 retains an admissibility hypothesis on the mapping-class embedding. None of these statements constructs the universal type-preserving embedding asked for here. Relevant sections inspected: 1.2–1.4 and 2.2–2.4.
- Kim–Koberda (2015), DOI 10.2140/gt.2015.19.3289, arXiv:1312.6465v4: an embedding of an arbitrary RAAG into an anti-tree RAAG, constructed via a finite subtree of the opposite graph's universal cover. The diagonal generator formula and injectivity proof in Section 4 were inspected. They do not assert preservation of all loxodromic elements. Proposition 4.1 of the authored report identifies the exact failure for cycle complements.
- Runnels (2021), DOI 10.1007/s10711-021-00615-0: Theorems 2–3 provide effective high-power RAAG generation and the pseudo-Anosov-on-support conclusion. The published version, including Section 4.3, was retrieved from the NSF public-access repository. This support theorem is not present in the available arXiv:2004.13585v1, so the earlier arXiv file is not used as evidence for that theorem.
- Oh–Park, arXiv:2608.09535v2, 18 August 2026, *From algebraic orthogonality to RAAG embedding obstructions in hierarchically hyperbolic groups*: its introduction, factorization results, and questions concern intermediate RAAGs and expanded-core-graph embedding obstructions for specified HHG structures. The retrieved manuscript does not supply the all-loxodromic-to-pseudo-Anosov conclusion needed here. Its factorization theorem must not be upgraded to type preservation. The proof of that manuscript was not independently audited.

## A significant partial-family announcement, not a verified full-text theorem here

An indexed primary excerpt from Ian Runnels's May 2021 University of Virginia thesis, *Right-Angled Artin Groups in Mapping Class Groups*, page 46, announces a Theorem 11 for trees of diameter at least three, anti-trees, and anti-cycles. It describes type-preserving quasi-isometric embeddings for those classes in forthcoming work.

Primary thesis URL: https://libraetd.lib.virginia.edu/downloads/6d56zx52j?filename=1_Runnels_Ian_2021_PHD.pdf

The excerpt was found repeatedly in primary-domain search results, and the institution's defense announcement corroborates the thesis title and date: https://math.virginia.edu/2021/04/Runnels/ . The complete PDF download returned HTTP 403 through both the document fetch and direct retrieval routes. Consequently this packet treats Theorem 11 as a located author announcement whose full proof was **not inspected**. No PDF hash is claimed for the blocked thesis, and none of the authored proofs depends on it.

This matters to novelty: neither the free-group affirmative example nor the existence of special-family embeddings should be advertised as a newly solved general question. It also prevents interpreting the cycle-cover obstruction as a counterexample to direct type-preserving embeddings of anti-cycles.

## Normalization hazards

1. A set injection is not the intended homomorphism.
2. Loxodromic depends on the action unless the intrinsic RAAG classification is specified; do not use a disconnected extension graph without qualification.
3. A singleton isolated vertex in Γ is a legitimate loxodromic support intrinsically. Closed domination handles it; total domination alone does not.
4. Pseudo-Anosov on a proper support is still reducible on the whole surface.
5. Quasi-isometric embeddings need not preserve loxodromic type.
6. Claims about convex cocompactness require the hypotheses actually present in the invoked theorem, including finite generation and admissibility where stated.
7. Boundary-fixing mapping class groups carry boundary twists. Centralizer arguments cannot silently import boundaryless conventions.
8. Failure of a support model, covering construction, or free-quotient detector is not a counterexample to arbitrary embeddings.
