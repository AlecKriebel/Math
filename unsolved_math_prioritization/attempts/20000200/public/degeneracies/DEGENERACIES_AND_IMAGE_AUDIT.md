# Degeneracies, signs, and actual-image reconstruction

Shared pass 1/5, row 1793, fixed complex `SE(2,C)` pair `(u,v)=(κ²,κ_s)`.

This is an authored audit of the adopted contract, including independent checks of the imported homogeneous-line result. That line classification is prior partial work, not a new result of this pass. No claim about the full AIM question or mathematical priority is made.

## Findings

1. Every one-dimensional integral target with `u` constant, or with `v=0`, is impossible.
2. For the stipulated degree-greater-than-one sources, the point-signature boundary is exactly `(a,0)` with `a∈C*`. Such a source is a complex circle. `(0,0)` occurs only for nonisotropic lines, outside the source universe.
3. Exact differentials satisfying the audited Frenet identities reconstruct an actual integral affine plane curve, with the correct fixed signature image. No assumption that the supplied parameter curve is already the normalization is needed for this assertion.
4. The true signature degree must be calculated on the field generated by the reconstructed coordinates. For the preferred field `L=K(R,h)` with `h²=R/(4uv²)`, field generation follows automatically once both prescribed differentials are exact.
5. The imported homogeneous-line signs and allowable slopes check out. The underlying monomial source is birational when the exponents are coprime; its signature degree is `|a+b|`. Its affine image means Zariski closure when the parametrization omits a boundary point.

## 1. Conventions and rational formulas

Let `X` be an integral plane curve, `C_X` the smooth projective normalization of its projective closure, and `E=C(C_X)`. The coordinates are

\[
z=x+iy,\qquad w=x-iy,\qquad x=(z+w)/2,\qquad y=(z-w)/(2i).
\]

Over `C`, `z` and `w` are independent isotropic coordinates; they are not constrained to be complex conjugates. For a local parameter `t`, put

\[
g=x'^2+y'^2=z'w',\qquad
\Delta=x'y''-y'x''.
\]

The standard rational invariants are

\[
u_X=\frac{\Delta^2}{g^3},\qquad
v_X=\frac{g\Delta'-\frac32\Delta g'}{g^3}.
\tag{1}
\]

On a graph `x=t`, these specialize to

\[
u_X=\frac{(y'')^2}{(1+(y')^2)^3},\qquad
v_X=\frac{(1+(y')^2)y'''-3y'(y'')^2}{(1+(y')^2)^3},
\]

agreeing with Kogan–Ruddy–Vinzant, equations (20)–(21) and Table 1. Their Example 2.39 states that this ordered pair is classifying for `SE(2,C)` and that no irreducible degree-greater-than-one plane curve is exceptional for it.

When the metric is nonzero, define

\[
R=\frac{dz}{dw},\qquad \alpha=\frac{dR}{R},\qquad \beta=dz\,dw.
\]

Since `z''w'-z'w''=2iΔ`,

\[
\alpha=\frac{2i\Delta}{g}\,dt=2i\kappa\,ds,\qquad
\beta=ds^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, when `α≠0`, there are parameter-free formulas in `E`:

\[
\boxed{u_X=-\frac{\alpha^2}{4\beta},\qquad
v_X=i\frac{du_X}{\alpha}.}
\tag{3}
\]

These identities are particularly useful for reconstruction because they do not require a global arc-length parameter or a square root of `u`.

Choosing the opposite local square root of the metric replaces both `ds` and `κ` by their negatives; `κ²` and `dκ/ds` are unchanged. Reparametrization, including a ramified covering away from its branch points, preserves both rational expressions (1). An ambient reflection interchanges `z,w`, changes `α` to `−α`, and sends `(u,v)` to `(u,−v)`; that is not an allowed change of the fixed target coordinates. In contrast, a special-Euclidean transformation has the form

\[
(z,w)\longmapsto(\lambda z+a,\lambda^{-1}w+b),\quad \lambda\in\mathbb C^*,
\]

and preserves `α,β,u,v`.

## 2. Metric and curvature degeneracies

### 2.1 Generic isotropy forces a line

On the one-dimensional function field, `dz·dw=0` implies `dz=0` or `dw=0`. Characteristic zero and constant field `C` imply respectively `z∈C` or `w∈C`. Thus the plane curve is an isotropic line. In particular, the generic-nonzero-metric hypothesis is automatic for an integral plane curve of degree greater than one, although retaining it in the contract is harmless.

### 2.2 Zero curvature forces a line

If the metric is nonzero and `u_X=0`, then `Δ=0`, so `dR/R=0`. Hence `R=A∈C*` and `d(z-Aw)=0`. The plane curve is contained in the line `z-Aw=B`, and irreducibility and dimension one make it that line. Thus `u_X` cannot vanish identically for an allowed source.

### 2.3 Constant first invariant and zero second invariant

One can adjoin a square root of the metric to the function field and write `ds` for the resulting nonzero meromorphic differential. With `κ=Δ/g^{3/2}` one has

\[
du_X=2\kappa v_X\,ds.
\tag{4}
\]

If `u_X` is a nonzero constant, then `κ²=u_X` forces `κ` to be constant on a connected component of this finite cover, and hence `v_X=0`. If `u_X=0`, the preceding paragraph gives a line and again `v_X=0`. Conversely `v_X=0` gives `dκ=0` and hence constant `u_X`.

Every integral one-dimensional closed subset of `A²_(u,v)` with `u` constant is a vertical line; every such subset with `v=0` is the horizontal line `v=0`. Their rational signature image would be a point by the preceding argument, a contradiction. These exhaust all degeneracies excluded by the open-stratum condition `uvdu≠0`: on a characteristic-zero function field, `du=0` is equivalent to constant `u`.

## 3. Point-signature boundary

The only point signatures are `(a,0)`. If `a=0`, the source is a nonisotropic line and is excluded by degree. If `a≠0`, choose `k∈C*` with `k²=a`. On the metric double cover, use the derivation `d/ds` and unit tangent `T=(x_s,y_s)`. The signed Frenet equations give

\[
T_s=kJT,\qquad J(p,q)=(-q,p).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\left((x,y)+\frac1k JT\right)=0.
\]

The resulting center `(x_0,y_0)` consists of constants, and

\[
(x-x_0)^2+(y-y_0)^2=1/k^2=1/a.
\]

This conic is irreducible over `C` because its right side is nonzero; its degree is two. The integral source is therefore exactly this circle. Conversely each such circle has the required point signature, independently of orientation or local square-root choice. A convenient isotropic parametrization is `z=t`, `w=1/(at)` after translation of the center to zero.

## 4. Reconstruction into an actual source

Let `S` be an integral one-dimensional target with `u,v,du` generically nonzero. Let `D` be its smooth projective normalization and `K=C(D)=C(u,v)`. Suppose a finite extension `L/K` with constant field `C` carries functions `z,w` satisfying

\[
\frac{d(dz/dw)}{dz/dw}=i\frac{du}{v},\qquad
dz\,dw=\frac{du^2}{4uv^2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define `E=C(z,w)⊂L` and let `X` be the reduced Zariski closure of the image of `(z,w)` in the isotropic affine plane. Equivalently, its ideal is the kernel of `C[Z,W]→L`, `Z↦z`, `W↦w`. The metric identity implies that both `z,w` are nonconstant. Thus `E` has transcendence degree one, the ideal is prime of height one, and `X` is an integral affine plane curve with function field `E`.

In characteristic zero, `Ω¹_E→Ω¹_L` is injective for the finite separable extension `L/E`. The functions `R=dz/dw`, the differential `α=dR/R`, and the quadratic differential `β=dz·dw` are all defined over `E`. Equations (3) and (5) therefore give, inside `L`,

\[
u_X=-\frac{(i\,du/v)^2}{4\,du^2/(4uv^2)}=u,
\qquad
v_X=i\frac{du}{i\,du/v}=v.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, `K=C(u,v)⊂E`; the candidate target functions are genuine rational invariants of the plane image, not merely functions on a covering parameter curve. The inclusion `K⊂E` defines a finite surjective map from the projective normalization of `X` to `D`. Its rational signature has Zariski closure exactly `S`, since the induced coordinate-field map is precisely the inclusion of `C(u,v)`.

Moreover `β≠0` makes the source metric generically nonzero. Also `α=i du/v≠0` gives nonconstant tangent ratio, so `X` is not a line and has degree greater than one. Singular affine points, singular points at infinity, poles of the coordinates, and ramification of the parameter curve do not affect this generic algebraic argument.

### True degree versus parameter-cover degree

The actual signature degree is

\[
\deg(C_X\to D)=[E:K],
\]

whereas a supplied covering parameter curve has degree `[L:K]=[L:E][E:K]`. These are equal only when `E=L`. The symmetry-count theorem of Kogan–Ruddy–Vinzant applies to the actual source degree `[E:K]`; one must not apply it to an unnecessarily repeated parametrization.

### Field generation for the usual radical construction

Suppose specifically that

\[
L=K(R,h),\qquad dR/R=i\,du/v,\qquad h^2=R/(4uv^2),
\]

and the two tested differentials are

\[
dz=h\,du,\qquad dw=(h/R)\,du.
\tag{7}
\]

If both are exact in `L`, equations (5) hold. Equation (6) first proves `K⊂E`. Then

\[
R=dz/dw\in E,\qquad h=dz/du\in E,
\]

so `L=K(R,h)⊂E`. The reverse inclusion is by definition, hence `E=L`. This proves that the exact primitives generate the entire preferred cover. It also certifies the reconstruction degree without a separate birationality hypothesis or an additional search over primitive choices.

## 5. Imported homogeneous-line result: checked, not counted as new

For `L_c: v=cu`, the boundary argument excludes `c=0`. If `c≠0`, write `r=i/c`. Then

\[
\eta=i\,du/v=r\,du/u.
\]

If a source exists, the norm of its tangent ratio gives `nη=dQ/Q` for some positive integer `n`, so the residue at `u=0` forces `r∈Q*`.

If `r=1`, the tangent ratio on the source is `R=A u`; the metric relation makes `dz` a nonzero constant multiple of `du/u`. This cannot be exact because any zero or pole of the nonconstant meromorphic `u` gives a nonzero residue. If `r=−1`, the same obstruction applies to `dw`. Thus `c=±i` are impossible even after further finite covers.

Conversely choose coprime integers `a,b` with

\[
ab(a+b)(a-b)\ne0,\qquad r=-\frac{a-b}{a+b},
\]

and take `z=t^a`, `w=t^b`. This defines an actual integral affine source by Zariski closure. Bézout's identity gives `C(t^a,t^b)=C(t)`, including when either exponent is negative. Put `N=a+b`, `D=a-b`. Direct calculation gives

\[
dz\,dw=ab\,t^{N-2}dt^2,\qquad R=(a/b)t^D,
\]

\[
u=-\frac{D^2}{4ab}t^{-N},\qquad
v=-\frac{ND}{4iab}t^{-N},\qquad
\frac vu=-i\frac ND=\frac{i}{r}=c.
\tag{8}
\]

All the signs agree with (1)–(3) and with the convention in Kogan–Ruddy–Vinzant. The actual source is nonisotropic and nonline, and its signature degree is `|N|`.

For completeness, with `a_+=max(a,0)`, `a_-=max(−a,0)`, and similarly for `b`, an irreducible equation of the closure is

\[
z^{b_+}w^{a_-}-z^{b_-}w^{a_+}=0.
\]

Its degree is `max(b_++a_-,b_-+a_+)`, which is greater than one under the displayed restrictions. When both exponents have the same sign, the image of `G_m` can omit the origin; it is the closure, rather than the raw constructible image, that is the source in the adopted contract.

Every `r=A/B∈Q*\{±1}` in lowest terms, `B>0`, is obtained by setting

\[
g_0=\gcd(|B-A|,|B+A|),\quad a=(B-A)/g_0,\quad b=(B+A)/g_0.
\]

Here `g_0∈{1,2}`; it is two exactly when `A,B` are both odd. The source signature degree is `2B/g_0`, not always the denominator `B` of the logarithmic residue. This is an explicit check on the extra quadratic extension in the preferred radical construction.

Thus the prior classification remains

\[
\boxed{L_c\text{ realizable}\iff c\in i\mathbb Q^*\setminus\{i,-i\}.}
\]

The log-aesthetic literature identifies the real curvature law `κ_s=cκ²` with reciprocal curvature affine in arc length, and the corresponding nonconstant real family with logarithmic spirals. It does not supply the above complex algebraic classification in the passages inspected. Therefore this check supports the interpretation and signs, not any novelty claim.
