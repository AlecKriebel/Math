## Two elementary permanence lemmas

**Lemma E (finite presentations in extensions).** If 1 -> N -> P -> Q -> 1
is an exact sequence of pro-p groups and N and Q are finitely presented, so
is P.

Here is a direct proof to make the input precise. Choose finite pro-p
presentations for N and Q, and choose in P lifts x_j of Q's generators.
Adjoin the generators of N and these x_j. Impose N's relators; the finitely
many conjugation relations specifying x_j n_i x_j^-1 in N; and for each
relator of Q a relation saying its lifted value is its actual value in N.
All right-hand sides may be pro-p words; finite pro-p presentability does
not require finite abstract words. The copy of N in the presented group P'
is a quotient of the original N. The natural map P' -> P restricts to the
identity on N, so this copy is in fact isomorphic to N. The specified
conjugations are automorphisms of N, as they came from P; their relations
make that copy normal. The quotient P'/N has exactly the chosen
presentation for Q. A kernel element of P' -> P consequently lies in N,
where the map is injective. Thus P' -> P is an isomorphism.

**Lemma Q (quotients by finitely generated normal subgroups).** If C is
coherent and N is a finitely generated closed normal subgroup, then C/N is
coherent. Indeed, for finitely generated closed L <= C/N its full preimage
M <= C is generated topologically by generators of N and finitely many
lifts of generators of L. Hence M is finitely presented. Kill the finite
chosen generating set of N in that presentation; its closed normal closure
inside M is exactly N, so the resulting finite pro-p presentation gives L.

A third immediate consequence is that a pro-p group with an open coherent
subgroup is coherent. For finitely generated K in that group, the open
subgroup K0 in the coherent subgroup is finitely generated by pro-p
Schreier. It is finitely presented; its core in K is again open and finitely
presented by Reidemeister–Schreier. Lemma E applied to this core and the
finite quotient gives finite presentability of K. One can also use that
finite presentability is invariant under passage to finite-index open
subgroups and overgroups, but the argument identifies the needed facts.

## Coherence for actions with procyclic edge stabilizers

Let K be finitely generated and act on a pro-p tree T, with all vertex
stabilizers coherent and all edge stabilizers procyclic. If the action has a
global fixed vertex then K itself lies in a coherent stabilizer and is
finitely presented. Otherwise Chatzidakis–Zalesskii, Theorem 6.8, supplies
the finite subgroup decomposition needed by Attempt 1. Its vertex groups
are subgroups of the original stabilizers; hence they are coherent, and
Attempt 1 makes them finitely generated and finishes the proof.

For caution about a nonfaithful action, let N be its kernel. If there is an
edge, N lies in its procyclic stabilizer, so is finitely generated and
finitely presented. Divide by N. Vertex stabilizers of the faithful action
are quotients C/N of the former ones and are coherent by Lemma Q. Edge
stabilizers remain procyclic. The same argument proves K/N finitely
presented, and Lemma E recovers K. A tree with no edge already gives the
global-fixed-vertex case. Thus no silent faithfulness assumption is needed.

## Virtual procyclic edge theorem

Suppose G = G1 amalg_H G2 is the proper amalgam in the target and H has an
open procyclic subgroup C. By the relative topology on the embedded H,
there is an open normal subgroup U of G with U intersect H <= C.

Let K <= G be finitely generated and put K0 = K intersect U. This is open
in K and finitely generated. In the standard pro-p tree its edge
stabilizers have the form K0 intersect gHg^-1. Since U is normal,

K0 intersect gHg^-1 <= U intersect gHg^-1
                         = g(U intersect H)g^-1 <= gCg^-1.

Each is procyclic. Its vertex stabilizers are closed subgroups of conjugates
of Gi and therefore coherent. The action criterion just proved makes K0
finitely presented. Finite-index permanence makes K finitely presented.
Since K was arbitrary, G is coherent.

If H is finite, choose C = 1, which is open in H. This includes the
finite-amalgamation case without assuming that the factors or G are
finitely generated. If H is procyclic, take C = H. If H = 1, the proof
recovers coherence of free pro-p products of coherent groups.

## Why this does not prove the full target

For H = Z_p^2, every open subgroup has rank two, so the above choice of U
cannot force U intersect H to be procyclic. The general polycyclic edge has
finite subgroup-generation bounds, but Theorem 6.8 requires procyclic
stabilizers, not just uniformly bounded rank. Replacing its hypothesis by
finite rank would reintroduce the unresolved accessibility issue.
