## Coherence through a polycyclic kernel

Call a pro-p group polycyclic here if it has a finite closed subnormal series
with procyclic factors. Every closed subgroup of such a group has such a
series: intersect the series with the subgroup, and identify each factor
with a closed subgroup of the corresponding procyclic factor. Finite cyclic
p-groups have a one-generator, one-relator pro-p presentation; Z_p is free
pro-p of rank one. Induction using Lemma E from Attempt 2 therefore proves
that every closed subgroup of a polycyclic pro-p group is finitely generated
and finitely presented.

**Kernel lifting lemma.** Suppose N is a closed normal polycyclic subgroup
of a pro-p group P, and P/N is coherent. Then P is coherent.

For any finitely generated closed K <= P, its image L in P/N is closed by
compactness and finitely generated by images of its generators. It is
finitely presented by coherence. The kernel K intersect N is polycyclic,
hence finitely presented by the preceding paragraph. Lemma E applied to
1 -> K intersect N -> K -> L -> 1 proves K finitely presented.

The kernel need not be central and K intersect N need not be open in N.
This distinction matters: intersecting K with an arbitrary finitely
generated normal subgroup would not ensure finite generation. Polycyclicity
is used exactly to make every closed intersection finitely presented.

## Quotient of the amalgam

Suppose N <= H is closed and normal in each Gi. Its normalizer in G is
closed and contains both factors, so N is normal in G. Then

G/N = (G1/N) amalg_(H/N) (G2/N).

To check the universal property, maps from Gi/N agreeing on H/N lift to
maps from Gi agreeing on H and annihilating N. Their unique extension from
G annihilates N and therefore factors through G/N. The converse is immediate.
Properness survives: if an element of Gi maps into N under Gi -> G, it
already belongs to N, because Gi embeds in G. Thus Gi/N embeds in G/N.

The quotient factors Gi/N are coherent by Lemma Q of Attempt 2, since N
is finitely generated. Thus any applicable coherence theorem for this
quotient amalgam lifts through N to G.

## Concrete consequences

1. If H/N is virtually procyclic, Attempt 2 proves G/N coherent, and the
   kernel lifting lemma proves G coherent.
2. If N is open in H, then H/N is finite, so (1) applies. This includes the
   case that H itself is normal in both Gi, by taking N=H.
3. If H/N is malnormal in one of Gi/N, and is analytic, the prior
   Castellano–Zalesskii Corollary 3.14 proves G/N coherent. It lifts as above.
   In particular this recovers the existing malnormal case by setting N=1.
   This is an application of credited prior work, not an independent proof
   of its accessibility theorem.

The central-amalgam situation H <= Z(G1) intersect Z(G2) lies in case (2).
It is a genuine higher-rank special case: H may be Z_p^r for arbitrary
finite r, so it is not covered merely by virtually procyclic amalgamation.

## Try the largest possible kernel

The obvious candidate is C = core_G(H) = intersection_(g in G) gHg^-1.
It is closed, normal in G and a closed subgroup of H, hence polycyclic.
The argument gives an exact reduction to the core-free amalgam

(G1/C) amalg_(H/C) (G2/C).

However, nothing proved here makes H/C virtually procyclic or malnormal in
a factor. For example take H=Z_p^2 and Gi=H amalg_1 Z_p. The factors are
coherent by Attempt 2, and their H free factors have trivial normal core
in the full free product. Thus H/C still has rank two. This particular
example is already coherent (indeed malnormality settles it), but it shows
that the desired small quotient does not follow from polycyclicity alone.
The example is not offered as a counterexample to coherence.

## Status of the attempted proof

This reduction separates harmless common normal polycyclic directions from
the remaining core-free subgroup-action difficulty. The full question
requires handling a core-free higher-rank edge with no available
malnormal/acylindrical condition. This step is still missing; replacing it
by a general finite-rank accessibility assertion would be circular.
