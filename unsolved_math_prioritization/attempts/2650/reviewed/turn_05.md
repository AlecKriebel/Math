## Try a limit of the long-chain examples

Attempt 4 supplies coherent two-generator P_n with arbitrarily long reduced
graphs. Could the limiting group be finitely generated but not finitely
presented and occur as a closed subgroup of a fixed target amalgam?

Let F be the free pro-p group on x,y, and let R_n be the closed normal
subgroup generated by

[x^(p^i), y^(p^(n-i))], 0<=i<=n.

Then P_n=F/R_n. Every relator for R_(n+1) follows from those for R_n: if
0<=i<=n, take a p-th power of the y-entry in the corresponding commuting
pair, and for i=n+1 take a p-th power of the x-entry for i=n. Hence
R_(n+1) <= R_n, giving surjective transition maps P_(n+1) -> P_n.

For any continuous map F -> A to a finite p-group A, choose e>=1 such that
every element of A has p^e-th power equal to 1. If n>=2e-1, then for every
i between 0 and n either i>=e or n-i>=e. At least one entry in every
relator defining R_n is therefore the identity in A. Thus R_n is contained
in the kernel of this finite quotient map for all sufficiently large n.

The intersection of all open normal subgroups of F is trivial. The
preceding fact consequently proves intersection_n R_n=1. The canonical
map F -> inverse_limit_n F/R_n is onto by compactness: compatible cosets
form a descending family of nonempty closed subsets of F. It is injective
by the trivial intersection. Therefore

inverse_limit_n P_n = F,

the free pro-p group of rank two. This limit is coherent. The unbounded
finite diagrams do not accumulate into an incoherent group along these
natural transition maps. Nor has an embedding of some other inverse limit
into one fixed polycyclic-edge amalgam been supplied.

## Try an infinite independent family of relators instead

A broader attempt uses any set S of pairs of nonnegative integers and the
pro-p presentation

P(S)=<x,y | [x^(p^a), y^(p^b)]=1 for all (a,b) in S>.

This cannot make P(S) itself a finitely generated, infinitely related
counterexample. If (a,b) <= (a',b') coordinatewise, the relation at (a,b)
implies that at (a',b'), because positive integer powers of commuting
elements commute.

The set M of coordinatewise minimal pairs of S is finite. To prove this
in two dimensions without importing a compactness assumption, distinct
minimal pairs cannot share the same first coordinate. Listed in increasing
first coordinate, their second coordinates must be strictly decreasing,
otherwise a later pair would dominate an earlier one. There is no infinite
strictly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers. Also each s in S
has a minimal predecessor: the finite rectangle below s contains a minimal
member of S, which is then globally minimal. Thus every s dominates a
member of M.

It follows that the closed normal subgroup generated by all relators from
S equals that generated by the finite subfamily from M. The inclusion from
M to S is immediate, and the opposite inclusion follows from the
commuting-power implication just proved. Hence P(S) is finitely presented.
The empty set gives F and is included.

This is a no-go theorem for this precise form of infinite relation set. It
does not prove coherence of every P(S), since a finitely presented group
can still have finitely generated non-finitely-presented closed subgroups.
It also does not address arbitrary relators or arbitrary polycyclic-edge
amalgams. Those distinctions prevent the finite-frontier argument from
being advertised as a solution to Problem 21.141.

## Auxiliary exact checks

power_commutator_frontier.py examines all 65,536 subsets of the
4-by-4 exponent grid. For each it checks that its minimal frontier is an
antichain, dominates every selected pair and has the same upward closure.
It also checks the exponent-cover condition for e=1,...,64 and the
combinatorial lower-diagonal witness at n=2e-2. All checks passed. The
all-S and inverse-limit results rest on the written proof, not these
finite computations.
