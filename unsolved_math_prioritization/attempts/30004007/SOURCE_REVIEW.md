# Source and attribution assessment

## Exact original scope

The original question is László Végh's contribution “Open Problem: Linearly representable functions” in *Combinatorial Optimization*, Oberwolfach Report 50/2018, DOI 10.4171/owr/2018/50, printed p. 3018 (PDF p. 50): https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46772 . Both the extracted text and the actual page image were inspected. It permits arbitrary subspaces over a field F, without an equal-dimension condition, and asks for an exact nonnegative linear combination of linear-matroid ranks over that field. The representation supplies normalization at ∅. The four-element example meets every hypothesis.

## Relevant earlier counterexample

Shaddin Dughmi, Tim Roughgarden and Qiqi Yan, *From Convex Optimization to Randomized Mechanisms: Toward Optimal Combinatorial Auctions*, full author manuscript dated 16 April 2011, https://theory.stanford.edu/~shaddin/papers/convex-current.pdf ; stable manuscript record https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0040v3 .

Definition 2.1 allows nonnegative real coefficients. Section D.2, printed p. 22 / PDF p. 23, gives exactly the budget-2 function with one weight-2 element and three weight-1 elements, and establishes that it is not a matroid rank sum using Claim 4.5. Its labels 1,2,3,4 correspond to b,c,d,a here. The claim and proof, definition and example were read; title page and PDF pages 11 and 23 were also visually inspected. The author webpage confirms a later merged JACM article, but this packet relies on the inspected 2011 author manuscript, not an uninspected final journal text.

This edition supplies the explicit all-field plane/three-line realization, elementary rank-slack proof, integer separator and full independent mathematical audit. The counterexample and its non-MRS property are credited to the earlier authors. Its application answers the exact printed OWR question; the source does not itself discuss that later question. The separation proof is complete without relying on the manuscript's status. No novelty claim is made.
