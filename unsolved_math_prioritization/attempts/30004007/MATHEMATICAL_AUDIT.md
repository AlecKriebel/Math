# Independent mathematical audit: representable polymatroids and the matroid-rank cone

Problem 30004007 / OWR-16633-025. Audit date: 10 October 2026.

Public proof-only edition. This AI-assisted audit is unrefereed; acceptance does not mean external human peer review, journal acceptance or formal proof-assistant certification.

## Verdict and credit

The proposed universal assertion is false, over every field. A four-element representable polymatroid is outside the cone generated by **all ordinary matroid rank functions on the same four-element ground set**, hence also outside the smaller cone generated by linear-matroid ranks over the given field. The argument allows arbitrary nonnegative real coefficients.

This is a source-backed negative resolution, not a novelty claim. Dughmi, Roughgarden and Yan already give the budget-2 example below and prove that it is not a matroid rank sum, in Section D.2 of their 2011 full paper, *From Convex Optimization to Randomized Mechanisms: Toward Optimal Combinatorial Auctions*. Their Definition 2.1 permits nonnegative real weights; their Claim 4.5 gives a discrete-Hessian obstruction. The explicit representation below supplies the connection to the Oberwolfach question. We do not claim that those authors explicitly discussed the later Oberwolfach problem, or that the displayed representation or dual example is new.

This report independently verifies both the credited budget-2 example and the proposed budget-3 example. ## 1. Exact source compatibility

The original question is László Végh's contribution headed “Open Problem: Linearly representable functions,” printed page 3018 of Oberwolfach Report 50/2018, PDF page 50. The full contribution was inspected as a rendered image and as extracted text; the live EMS primary PDF was also opened.

The source allows one arbitrary linear subspace T_v of F^n for each v in V, and defines the rank on S as the dimension of the span of the union of its subspaces. It imposes **no common-dimension requirement** on those subspaces. The requested summands are linear-matroid rank functions over the same field. Their domains must be the same 2^V for the asserted functional equality to be meaningful. Neither a ground-set expansion, approximation nor integer-only coefficient condition occurs in the question. Although its brief definition of a polymatroid omits an explicit normalization clause, the subspace representation itself forces f(empty)=0.

Our counterexamples have V={a,b,c,d}; all subspaces are in F^2 or F^3, and are defined over the original arbitrary field F. Both have singleton dimensions (2,1,1,1). Their integer-valuedness, normalization and monotonicity are immediate from dimensions. Submodularity follows from the dimension formula: if U_S is the span of the subspaces indexed by S, then U_(S intersect T) is contained in U_S intersect U_T, whereas U_(S union T)=U_S+U_T.

## 2. Credited budget-2 example: direct all-field representation

Put B={b,c,d}. In F^2 choose

- T_a=F^2;
- T_b=span(e_1);
- T_c=span(e_2);
- T_d=span(e_1+e_2).

The three lines are distinct over every field, including the two-element field. The determinants of their pairwise generator matrices are 1, 1 and -1 up to ordering, and these are nonzero in every field. Thus every pair of different lines spans F^2. It follows that the rank function is exactly

    g(S)=min(2, 2*1_{a in S}+|S intersect B|).

In particular g(a)=2, each g(i)=1 for i in B, and g(S)=2 whenever S contains a or at least two members of B. No division by 2, field extension, generic choice or large-field hypothesis is used.

### Independent elementary non-decomposition proof

Suppose g=sum_j lambda_j r_j, with lambda_j>0 and each r_j an ordinary matroid rank on V. Zero coefficients may be discarded. For each i in B, the monotonicity slack r_j(ai)-r_j(a) is nonnegative. Its weighted sum is g(ai)-g(a)=0, so it vanishes in every retained summand. For each distinct i,k in B, the subadditivity slack r_j(i)+r_j(k)-r_j(ik) is nonnegative, and its weighted sum also vanishes.

Fix a retained summand r. If r(a)=0, the first group of equalities and monotonicity force all r(i)=0. If r(a)=1 and r(i)=1, then r(ai)=1, so i is parallel to a. No two members i,k of B can both be nonloops: two nonloops parallel to a would be parallel to one another, giving r(ik)=1, whereas the second group of equalities would give r(ik)=2. Hence in every retained summand

    r(b)+r(c)+r(d) <= r(a).

Taking the weighted sum would give 3<=2, a contradiction. This is a proof against arbitrary ordinary matroids; representability is never required of the summands.

### Independent check of the author's global separator

For any normalized set function h define the 4 by 4 matrix

    H_h(u,v)=h({u,v})-h(u)-h(v),

with the same formula when u=v. For an ordinary matroid rank r, a loop produces a zero row and column. Among nonloops, H_r(u,v)=-1 exactly when u and v belong to the same parallel class; otherwise the entry is zero. Therefore, for any real vector x,

    -x^T H_r x = sum_C (sum_{v in C} x_v)^2 >= 0,

where C ranges over the nonloop parallel classes. This proves the required Hessian fact directly at the empty set, without a numerical eigenvalue computation or reliance on an unproved external lemma.

With x=(-1,1,1,1), in order (a,b,c,d), the resulting linear functional is

    L(h)=-5h(a)+3[h(b)+h(c)+h(d)]
         +2[h(ab)+h(ac)+h(ad)]-2[h(bc)+h(bd)+h(cd)].

It is nonnegative on every ordinary matroid rank and hence on every nonnegative real combination. For the represented g, it equals -10+9+12-12=-1. This separately certifies the conclusion and excludes the closure of the rank cone as well.

## 3. Proposed budget-3 example

In F^3 choose

    T_a={(x,y,z): x+y+z=0},
    T_b=span(e_1), T_c=span(e_2), T_d=span(e_3).

The linear functional (x,y,z) -> x+y+z is nonzero, since its value on e_1 is 1. Hence its kernel has dimension 2 over every field. Each coordinate line meets the kernel only in zero, because the functional restricts to the identity on that coordinate. Consequently T_a plus any coordinate line is F^3. The coordinate lines are independent. Equivalently, (1,-1,0),(1,0,-1) form a basis of T_a, valid also in characteristic 2.

The final author packet uses the equivalent realization U_a=span(e_1,e_2), U_b=span(e_3), U_c=span(e_1+e_3), U_d=span(e_2+e_3). The invertible coordinate map (x,y,z) -> (y,z,x+y+z) carries the preceding arrangement to that arrangement over every field. Its matrix has determinant 1. Thus the following audit covers the exact realization in the final author proof as well.

The rank function is exactly

    f(S)=min(3, 2*1_{a in S}+|S intersect B|).

Its singleton ranks are 2,1,1,1; its three pairs ai have rank 3; its three pairs within B have rank 2; its four triples and V have rank 3.

### Rigorous zero-slack proof

Suppose f=sum_j lambda_j r_j with positive real coefficients and ordinary matroid ranks. For each i in B, both

    r_j(a)+r_j(i)-r_j(ai) >= 0,
    r_j(V)-r_j(ai) >= 0

are valid slacks with weighted sum zero. In addition use the **two separate** nonnegative slacks

    r_j(b)+r_j(c)+r_j(d)-r_j(B) >= 0,
    r_j(V)-r_j(B) >= 0.

Each weighted sum is zero. Thus every retained summand r satisfies

    r(ai)=r(a)+r(i)=r(V) for all i in B,
    r(V)=r(b)+r(c)+r(d).

The first group makes r(b)=r(c)=r(d)=t. The second then makes r(V)=3t, and r(a)=2t. Singleton ranks of an ordinary matroid belong to {0,1}, so t cannot be 1. Thus t=0, r(a)=0 and r(V)=0, forcing the entire rank function to be zero. All retained summands would be zero, contradicting f(a)=2.

**Necessary precision:** the unsplit expression r(b)+r(c)+r(d)-r(V) is not nonnegative for all matroids. A matroid having only a as a nonloop makes it -1. The split through r(B) above is essential if invoking the general zero-slack principle. The final proof has been checked for this correction.

### Independent check of the author's secondary separator

Let E(h) be the sum of the eight correctly split slacks just displayed. Direct expansion gives

    E(h)=3h(a)+2 sum_i h(i)-2 sum_i h(ai)+4h(V)-2h(B).

For any ordinary matroid rank r, E(r) is a nonnegative integer, and the zero-slack argument shows E(r)=0 only for the zero rank. A nonzero rank thus has E(r)>=1, while r(V)<=4. Hence K(r)=4E(r)-r(V)>=0 for every matroid rank, including zero. On the represented f, E(f)=0 and f(V)=3, so K(f)=-3. This verifies the author's second universal separator without enumeration.

## 4. Dual relationship and coefficient conventions

For a normalized rank function h define

    D(h)(S)=h(V minus S)+sum_{v in S}h(v)-h(V).

Direct substitution on all sixteen subsets gives D(f)=g and D(g)=f. For a matroid rank r, D(r) is the rank of the dual of the restriction to its nonloops, extended by making its original loops loops again. This follows from the ordinary dual rank formula on the nonloop set. Thus D maps every ordinary matroid rank to an ordinary matroid rank and is linear in h. It also preserves the corresponding cone. The two independent counterexamples are therefore consistent under this loop-preserving duality; no assertion that this operation is involutive on every matroid is needed.

The conventional meaning of a linear combination is a finite sum. All proofs above cover every such combination with arbitrary nonnegative real coefficients; the coefficients are not assumed integral, rational, or to sum to 1. The zero-slack inference uses only nonnegativity and positivity of each retained coefficient.

Allowing a countably or arbitrarily indexed nonnegative sum with finite coordinate values would not help. There are only finitely many distinct ordinary matroid rank functions on a fixed finite ground set. Group terms by their rank function; every nonzero type has total rank at least 1, so finiteness of the V-coordinate forces its grouped coefficient to be finite. This reduces to a finite conic combination after discarding zero ranks. Alternatively the displayed separating functional, continuous in the sixteen rank coordinates, excludes limits too.

## 5. Optional sharpness: minimum ground-set size and ambient dimension

The author's claim that four elements are necessary is correct even for all real-valued normalized monotone submodular functions, without representability. Here is an independent verification of the full argument.

On {1,2,3}, write H=h(123). Set p_i=H-h({1,2,3} minus {i}), q_ij=h(ik)+h(jk)-h(k)-H for the remaining element k, and t=h(1)+h(2)+h(3)-h(12)-h(13)-h(23)+H. Monotonicity gives p_i>=0; submodularity applied to ik and jk gives q_ij>=0. Algebra gives q_ij+t=h(i)+h(j)-h(ij)>=0 by subadditivity.

Let u_A(S)=1 when S meets the nonempty set A and 0 otherwise. This is the rank-one matroid with nonloops A, representable over every field. Direct evaluation on all subsets gives the identity

    h=sum_i p_i u_{i}+sum_{i<j} q_ij u_{ij}+t u_{123}.

When t>=0 this is the desired decomposition. The rank function r_2(S)=min(2,|S|) of U_(2,3) has coordinates p_i=0, q_ij=1, t=-1 in this identity. Thus when t<0, with k=-t, subtracting k*r_2 leaves the nonnegative coefficients p_i and q_ij-k=q_ij+t, and zero triple coefficient. The resulting decomposition is

    h=sum_i p_i u_i+sum_{i<j}(q_ij-k)u_{ij}+k*r_2.

The matroid U_(2,3) is represented over every field by (1,0),(0,1),(1,1). Hence both cases provide sums of same-field linear ranks. Smaller ground sets follow by adding loops and restricting. The independent checker also verified both linear identities on a symbolic coordinate basis. This supplementary check agrees with the exact algebraic identities proved above over the real numbers, rather than extrapolations from sampled polymatroids.

Ambient dimension two is also minimal: in a space of dimension at most one, each representing subspace is either zero or the whole one-dimensional space. Its dimension function is itself a rank-one matroid rank, or zero. The credited counterexample is represented in dimension two, attaining this bound. No equal-dimension hypothesis is introduced.

## 6. Historical independent exact computation and controls

The following records supplementary computations performed during the independent audit. The programs, raw outputs and generated certificates are omitted from this proof-only edition. The audit checker uses only Python's standard library, with explicit exceptions rather than optimization-removable assertions. It imports no author checker. It enumerates all 32768 set families containing the empty set, retains the 167 hereditary families, and tests the exchange axiom. Exactly 68 labeled matroids on four elements result. Their rank functions independently pass the normalized, integer, monotone, submodular and cardinality-bound rank axioms.

The checker confirms:

- both complete sixteen-coordinate rank tables and all polymatroid axioms;
- exact subspace ranks over Q and GF(2), GF(3), GF(5), GF(7), GF(11), GF(101);
- that only the zero matroid satisfies the eight correctly split tight constraints for f;
- that all five matroids on the tight face for g satisfy sum_i r(i)<=r(a);
- the author's Hessian witness at every one of the 68 matroid generators;
- loop-preserving duality on all 68 ranks and the exact f/g dual relation;
- a second independent integral separator for g, namely sum_i h(ai)+sum_i h(i)-2h(a)-sum_{i<k}h(ik), which is nonnegative on all 68 ranks and equals -1 on g;
- positive controls including the zero, free, rank-one uniform, rank-two uniform and single-nonloop matroids;
- rejection of intentionally damaged singleton/pair ranks and a negative control exposing the invalid unsplit slack.

For completeness, the second supplementary separator also has a universal argument. Write J(h)=sum_i h(ai)+sum_i h(i)-2h(a)-sum_{i<j}h(ij). If r(a)=0, then J(r)=2 sum_i r(i)-sum_{i<j}r(ij)>=0 by subadditivity. If r(a)=1, let m be the number of nonloops among b,c,d, p the number among them parallel to a, and q the number of parallel nonloop pairs among them. Then sum_i r(ai)=3+m-p and sum_{i<j}r(ij)=2m-q, so J(r)=1-p+q. Since q>=p(p-1)/2 and p is one of 0,1,2,3, this is nonnegative in every case. This separator is supplementary; the principal proof does not depend on it.
