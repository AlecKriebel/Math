## Verified mathematics and boundary cases

**Representation count.** Finite abelian \(A=H_1(Y)\) has \(|A|\) unitary characters. Diagonal abelian SU(2) representations are identified by inversion, central characters give point orbits, and noncentral characters give S2 orbits. Finitely many disjoint compact orbits are the representation set's components. If \(c=|A[2]|\), ordinary homology has total complex dimension \(c+2(|A|-c)/2=|A|\). This includes \(A=0\), \(A\) of exponent2 and noncyclic groups. Counting this ordinary homology is insufficient to control Floer perturbations at degenerate components.

**Twisted cohomology and covers.** The adjoint system of a diagonal representation is a trivial real line plus the realification of the rank-one system with character \(\chi^2\). Because base first real cohomology vanishes, its real first-cohomology dimension is twice the complex dimension of the square-character system. Central characters square to1 and contribute zero. Fourier idempotents split a regular cyclic cover's complex cochains into local-system cochains, so cover first cohomology is their direct sum. The relevant cover is \(\ker\chi^2\); an arbitrary quotient character must have a square-character lift before it can be used as this cover. Quantification over all characters makes all relevant-cover Betti vanishings equivalent to all adjoint vanishings. Positive cohomology in one cover does not necessarily reside in its particular primitive eigensummand; a power representation may be needed. The formulation avoids that hidden pointwise quantifier error.
