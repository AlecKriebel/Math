# Sources, normalization, and literature scope

Checked 7 October 2026. Negative searches are bounded checks, not a proof that no further literature exists.

## Primary question and bibliographic correction

The originating discussion is Ihsan Topaloglu, **Nonlocal variational problems on polygons**, joint work with Marco Bonacini and Riccardo Cristoferi, in *Calculus of Variations*, Oberwolfach Report 37/2022, pp. 2171–2174. The classification paragraph is on p. 2172. The report DOI is [10.4171/OWR/2022/37](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2022/37); the [publisher's report PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46973) was inspected.

The cited paper [DOI 10.1016/j.na.2020.112223](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.na.2020.112223) is **Marco Bonacini, Riccardo Cristoferi, and Ihsan Topaloglu**, *Minimality of polytopes in a nonlocal anisotropic isoperimetric problem*, *Nonlinear Analysis* 205 (2021), article 112223. Attribution to Fusco–Julin–Morini is incorrect. Bibliography was cross-checked against the [University of Trento repository record](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/285006) and the authors' sites.

## Exact scope

- The model is initially defined for finite-perimeter sets in \(\mathbb R^d\), with \(0<\alpha<d\), fixed volume 1, and energy \(P_\psi+\gamma\iint|x-y|^{-\alpha}\).
- The planar classification discussion concerns convex polygons, side translations preserving their normal directions, and exact preservation of area. It does not require stationarity under arbitrary smooth variations or under rotating a side.
- The main planar result has \(d=2\), \(0<\alpha<2\). A higher-dimensional sufficient theorem is also present. A planar result alone must not be called a classification of all higher-dimensional anisotropies.
- The source assumes \(\psi\) convex, positively one-homogeneous, strictly positive on the sphere, and crystalline. It does **not** assume evenness. Every convex polygon containing 0 is allowed as the Wulff shape of its support function.
- Unit-area normalization is harmless for the qualitative small-γ property. For a fixed volume \(m\), the effective coefficient is \(\gamma m^{(d+1-\alpha)/d}\).
- The exponent is fixed; “sufficiently small” need not mean a uniform threshold across all exponents, shapes, or numbers of facets.

## Primary results used

**BCT 2021.** Theorem 2.5 proves small-γ uniqueness for angle-bisector-reflection-symmetric planar polygons; Remark 2.6 gives the equal-side-average potential equations as necessary conditions. Its proof first establishes existence, small quasiminimality, and the fixed-normal reduction, before using symmetry. Theorem 5.3 supplies a higher-dimensional facet-transitive sufficient class. The general classification is not supplied. Both an early author manuscript and the repository's refereed author manuscript were inspected; the latter retains the classification discussion.

- [Refereed author manuscript](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835198-3dd2-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Bonacini%20-%20Cristoferi%20-%20Topaloglu%2C%20Minimality%20of%20polytopes%20in%20a%20nonlocal%20anisotropic%20isoperimetric%20problem.pdf)
- [Author-hosted manuscript](https://ihsantopaloglu.github.io/papers/BoCrTo20.pdf)
- [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03628)

**BCT 2022.** *Riesz-type inequalities and overdetermined problems for triangles and quadrilaterals*, *Journal of Geometric Analysis* 32, article 48, [DOI 10.1007/s12220-021-00737-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12220-021-00737-7). Remark 4.1 explicitly says that every triangle is sliding-stationary. Theorem 1.2 characterizes the equilateral triangle using tilting, and the square using sliding **and** tilting. The larger variation class is essential; these results do not classify the crystalline question's sliding-only critical shapes.

- [Post-peer-review manuscript](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/5058/BonCriTop22.pdf)
- [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.06657v3)

**Figalli–Maggi 2011.** *On the shape of liquid drops and crystals in the small mass regime*, *Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis* 201, 143–207, [DOI 10.1007/s00205-010-0383-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-010-0383-x). The retrieved [author manuscript](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~afigalli/papers-pdf/On-the-shape-of-liquid-drops-and-crystals-in-the-small-mass-regime.pdf) labels the planar crystalline rigidity result Theorem 3.7. BCT cites the journal numbering as Theorem 7. The result concerns sufficiently small perimeter quasiminimizers, not only an arbitrary chosen external-potential model. This distinction allows the nonlocal Lipschitz estimate to supply its hypothesis.

**Existence and quantitative stability dependencies.** The proof uses the small-parameter existence result of Choksi–Neumayer–Topaloglu, *Anisotropic liquid drop models*, *Advances in Calculus of Variations* 15 (2022), 109–131, Theorem 3.1, whose statement and proof were checked in the [author manuscript](https://ihsantopaloglu.github.io/papers/ChNeTo.pdf). It covers every \(0<\alpha<d\) and does not impose evenness. Quantitative Wulff stability is BCT equation (2.6), due to Figalli–Maggi–Pratelli. Their full original proofs were not rederived here. These dependencies are explicit rather than counted as authored solutions.

## Later-literature check

The 2025 published article by Bonacini–Cristoferi–Topaloglu, *Alexandrov's Soap Bubble Theorem for Polygons*, *American Mathematical Monthly* 132, 666–675, [DOI 10.1080/00029890.2025.2500881](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.2025.2500881), was inspected in its [author manuscript](https://ihsantopaloglu.github.io/papers/BoCrTo24_2.pdf). It concerns a perimeter theorem and reviews nonlocal sliding-and-tilting problems. It does not supply the crystalline sliding-only classification. Its discussion of more general kernels cannot be substituted for the negative-power Riesz kernel here.

The current publication lists of [Ihsan Topaloglu](https://ihsantopaloglu.github.io/) and [Riccardo Cristoferi](https://www.math.ru.nl/~cristoferi/publications.html) were checked, along with targeted searches for the exact 2021 title and combinations of “crystalline”, “Wulff”, “Riesz”, “sliding”, “fixed normals”, “stationary”, and “classification”. No exact published resolution of the classification was located. This is a dated, bounded literature finding, not a claim of exhaustive coverage or proof of novelty.
