# Two obstructions to a five-generator principal-matrix characterization

## Status and exact scope

The original manuscript was accepted as a bounded partial result by the accompanying independent mathematical audit. This proof-only edition omits optional computational claims and adds the separately identified [proof supplement](PROOF_SUPPLEMENT.md). The original audit predates that supplement; the separate [supplement audit](SUPPLEMENT_AUDIT.md) now accepts its exact corrected bytes. The frozen supplement’s pending-review sentence records its submission state and is superseded by that report. This AI-assisted manuscript and audit are unrefereed, with no claim of external human peer review, journal acceptance, or formal proof-assistant certification.

This is a **partial result**, not a general characterization of Gorenstein monomial curves in five variables. It proves (1) an information-loss obstruction even when *all* principal matrices are supplied, on the full class including complete intersections, and (2) a structural non-Gorenstein theorem for the direct five-dimensional cyclic-zero extension of the familiar four-generator matrix pattern. The intended classification of Gorenstein **non-complete-intersection** semigroups remains unresolved here. Novelty is not asserted.

Let S = <a₁,…,a₅>, where the positive integers aᵢ minimally generate S and gcd(a₁,…,a₅)=1. This is a collective gcd condition. No pairwise-coprimality assumption is made. A genuine principal matrix A has Aᵢᵢ=−cᵢ, Aᵢⱼ≥0 for i≠j, Aa=0, and cᵢ the *least* positive integer for which cᵢaᵢ belongs to the semigroup generated by the other four generators. Let P(a) be the complete set of such matrices, with the given generator ordering.

We use the standard equivalence: k[S] is Gorenstein if and only if S is symmetric, equivalently S has one pseudo-Frobenius number. A pseudo-Frobenius number is an integer f outside S such that f+aᵢ belongs to S for every i. The equivalence is field-independent. The known four-generator criterion, complete-intersection results, and gluing results are credited in §7.

## 1. Complete principal-matrix data can lose the Gorenstein property

**Theorem 1.** Put

- a = (32,48,40,21,49),
- b = (40,60,50,27,63), and

    A = [−3  2  0  0  0]
        [ 3 −2  0  0  0]
        [ 1  1 −2  0  0]
        [ 0  0  0 −7  3]
        [ 0  0  0  7 −3].

Both a and b are primitive, minimally generating five-tuples, and

    P(a) = P(b) = {A}.

The matrix has rank 3. Nevertheless, k[<a>] has Cohen–Macaulay type 7, while k[<b>] is a complete intersection and is Gorenstein.

### Proof of principal-matrix uniqueness

Both tuples have the form (4d,6d,5d,3e,7e), with (d,e)=(8,7) and (10,9), respectively. Their critical multipliers are (3,2,2,7,3). At those multiples, the complete lists of expressions through the *other* generators are exactly

    3a₁ = 2a₂,     2a₂ = 3a₁,     2a₃ = a₁+a₂,
    7a₄ = 3a₅,     3a₅ = 7a₄,

and identically with b. There are no expressions at smaller positive multiples.

Here is an elementary modular verification that the two blocks do not introduce additional expressions.

For (d,e)=(8,7), any positive contribution from the last two generators to a target divisible by 8 is at least 112: its unscaled value is a positive multiple of 8 in <3,7>, whose least such value is 16. The first three critical targets are 96,96,80, so they have no cross-block expression. Conversely, a contribution from the first three generators to a target divisible by 7 is 8 times a positive multiple of 7 in <4,5,6>. Up to 147 the only possibility is 112. For a target 21r with 1≤r≤7, an expression excluding 21 would require 21r=112+49v, hence 3r=16+7v; none has v≥0. For targets 49 and 98 the contribution 112 is too large; at 147 the remainder 35 is not generated by 21. Thus the last two critical relations and their uniqueness follow.

For (d,e)=(10,9), the only possible positive cross-block contribution below 120 from the last block to a target divisible by 10 is 90, since the smallest positive multiple of 10 in <3,7> is 10. The remainder at the targets 100 and 120 is respectively 10 and 30, neither generated by 40,60,50. Lower candidate multiples give no expression. In the opposite direction, contributions below 189 divisible by 9 from the first block are 90 and 180. For a target 27r with 1≤r≤7, excluding 27 requires 3r=10k+7v with k∈{1,2}; for k=1 the only possible r in this range is 1 and gives v=−1, and for k=2 it is 2 and gives v=−2. For 63r with 1≤r≤3, excluding 63 requires 7r=10k+3u; direct reduction modulo 3 excludes both k=1 and k=2 in the allowable ranges. The pure pair expressions first occur at 189.

Inside <4,6,5>, the three critical relations displayed above are unique by inspection of 12,12,10, and their smaller candidate multiples. Inside <3,7>, coprimality gives the unique pure pair relation. Thus all five critical relations are unique for both tuples. Each multiplier is at least 2, proving minimal generation. The collective gcds are 1. The two diagonal blocks of A have ranks 2 and 1, so rank A=3.

### Proof of the opposite Gorenstein outcomes

The second semigroup is the gluing

    <b> = 10<4,6,5> + 9<3,7>.

Indeed gcd(10,9)=1, 10=3+7 belongs to <3,7>, and 9=4+5 belongs to <4,6,5>. Neither gluing coefficient is a generator of the relevant factor. Moreover <4,6,5> itself is the gluing 2<2,3>+5<1>, with 5=2+3. The known complete-intersection gluing theorem therefore applies. In the displayed variable order, a defining regular sequence is

    x₁³−x₂²,  x₃²−x₁x₂,  x₄⁷−x₅³,  x₁x₃−x₄x₅.

For the first semigroup, its Apéry set with respect to 21, listed in residue order 0,…,20, is

    (0,64,128,129,88,89,48,49,113,72,136,
     32,96,97,98,120,121,80,81,40,104).

Its Frobenius number is 136−21=115. Both 7 and 108=115−7 are gaps, so it is not symmetric. Its full pseudo-Frobenius set is

    {77,92,99,100,107,108,115}.

The complete residue-by-residue membership witnesses and Bellman inequalities proving the first semigroup's displayed Apéry and pseudo-Frobenius claims are supplied in [PROOF_SUPPLEMENT.md §1](PROOF_SUPPLEMENT.md). The second semigroup's Gorenstein property follows from the complete-intersection gluing argument above. ∎

**Corollary 1 (quantifier obstruction).** There is no Boolean function of a single principal matrix that decides the Gorenstein property correctly for every minimal primitive five-tuple and *every* principal-matrix choice. More strongly, there is no Boolean function of the complete set P(a) that decides it on this full class. In particular, merely switching from “for every principal matrix” to “there exists a principal matrix satisfying a fixed matrix-only condition” cannot repair the problem on this class: both examples have the same singleton set of choices.

This is an obstruction to using principal-matrix data *alone*. It does not forbid a criterion using the generators or additional scale data. Since the Gorenstein member of the pair is a complete intersection, it also does not settle the original problem's principal non-complete-intersection focus. There is no rank-4 choice for either of these two semigroups, so a rule that silently requires a rank-4 principal matrix is not universally available.

## 2. A saturated row-lattice lemma

Let a be any primitive positive n-tuple and put

    L(a) = {z∈Zⁿ : z·a=0}.

For an integer matrix A, write L_A for the integer lattice generated by its rows.

**Lemma 2.** Suppose A has rank n−1, Aa=0, and the gcd of all maximal minors of some n−1 independent rows is 1. Then L_A=L(a).

**Proof.** Let B consist of those rows. Every z∈L(a) has a unique rational expression z=λB. For any nonsingular (n−1)×(n−1) submatrix Bⱼ obtained by omitting column j, Cramer's rule says det(Bⱼ)λ is integral. An integer linear combination of these determinants equals 1, so λ is integral. Thus the rows of B already span L(a). ∎

If A additionally has column sums zero and the entries of one nonzero adjugate column have gcd 1, this hypothesis holds. Indeed the left nullspace is spanned by (1,…,1), so all columns of adj(A) are equal. Their entries are the signed maximal minors for deleting the corresponding row.

For any rank-(n−1) principal matrix, even without the gcd-minor-one hypothesis,

    saturation(L_A) = (span_Q L_A) ∩ Zⁿ = L(a).

Consequently this saturated lattice is invariant under changing among rank-(n−1) principal matrices of the same ordered tuple. We do not advertise this identity as a Gorenstein classification.

## 3. Cyclic orders produce pseudo-Frobenius numbers

**Lemma 3.** Let A be an integer matrix with nonnegative off-diagonal entries, column sums zero, Aa=0 for a positive primitive a, and L_A=L(a). For a linear ordering p of {1,…,n}, define

    v(p)ⱼ = (sum of Aᵢⱼ over vertices i occurring after j in p) − 1,
    f(p) = v(p)·a.

Then f(p) is not in S=<a>. If every consecutive pair j,i in the *cyclic* ordering p satisfies Aᵢⱼ>0, including the wraparound pair, then f(p) is a pseudo-Frobenius number.

**Proof of the gap assertion.** If f(p)=w·a for w≥0 integral, saturation gives v(p)−w=λA for an integral row vector λ. Choose j to be the last vertex in p among those attaining minᵢλᵢ. For every vertex i after j, λᵢ−λⱼ≥1. Column balance gives

    (λA)ⱼ = Σᵢ≠ⱼ (λᵢ−λⱼ)Aᵢⱼ
           ≥ Σᵢ after j Aᵢⱼ = v(p)ⱼ+1.

But (λA)ⱼ=v(p)ⱼ−wⱼ≤v(p)ⱼ, a contradiction.

**Proof of the pseudo-Frobenius assertion.** Moving the first vertex i of an ordering to the end changes v(p) by precisely row i of A. Thus cyclic rotation leaves f(p) unchanged. To show f(p)+aⱼ∈S, rotate p so that j is last. Its j-th coordinate is −1, while every other coordinate is nonnegative: the next vertex in the ordering supplies at least one positive summand. Adding the j-th unit vector therefore gives a nonnegative factorization of f(p)+aⱼ. This works for all j. ∎

For a minimally generated numerical semigroup of embedding dimension at least 2, these pseudo-Frobenius numbers are nonnegative in the usual convention. A negative one would have to equal minus the multiplicity, after which every other generator could be reduced by the multiplicity inside S, contradicting minimality.

## 4. The five-dimensional cyclic-zero pattern is non-Gorenstein

**Theorem 4 (structural obstruction).** Let A be a genuine 5×5 principal matrix for a minimally generated numerical semigroup S. Suppose:

1. every column sums to zero;
2. rank A=4 and the entries in an adjugate column have gcd 1;
3. the off-diagonal zeros are exactly A₁₂=A₂₃=A₃₄=A₄₅=A₅₁=0; every other off-diagonal entry is strictly positive.

Then S has at least two pseudo-Frobenius numbers. In particular k[S] is not Gorenstein.

**Proof.** Lemma 2 applies. Consider the two cyclic orders

    p=(2,4,5,3,1),       q=(4,2,5,3,1).

Every required reverse consecutive entry Aᵢⱼ in these two cycles is positive under hypothesis 3. Lemma 3 gives pseudo-Frobenius numbers f(p),f(q). The orders differ only by interchanging their first two vertices, so

    f(p)−f(q) = A₄₂ a₂ − A₂₄ a₄.

If these numbers were equal, this would be a relation expressing A₄₂ copies of a₂ through a₄ alone. However, column balance and the positive entries A₃₂,A₅₂ give

    0 < A₄₂ < A₃₂+A₄₂+A₅₂ = c₂.

This contradicts the defining criticality of the second row. Hence f(p)≠f(q), proving the theorem. ∎

This is a direct structural matrix obstruction, not an algorithm that reconstructs the generators and tests symmetry. By Dey–Srinivasan's Theorem 26, a pseudo-principal matrix satisfying these sign, column-balance, primitive-adjugate, and one-zero-per-column hypotheses is already genuine principal. Their theorem supplies principality, not the conclusion about the Gorenstein property.

**Quantification and invariance.** Theorem 4 applies to *any* genuine principal matrix satisfying its hypotheses. The existence of even one such matrix is a sufficient certificate that the underlying semigroup is not Gorenstein. The conclusion is independent of all other choices, because its two explicitly constructed integers are pseudo-Frobenius numbers of that same S. We do not assert that every principal matrix of a non-Gorenstein semigroup has this pattern, or that the pattern itself is unchanged by a row-choice change. Alternative rank-3 choices would not invalidate the certificate. For alternative rank-4 choices the saturated row lattice is the same by §2.

## 5. A concrete full-rank example

The matrix

    B = [−4  0  1  1  1]
        [ 1 −5  0  1  1]
        [ 1  2 −4  0  1]
        [ 2  2  2 −3  0]
        [ 0  1  1  1 −3]

is a genuine principal matrix for a=(89,72,85,164,107). It has rank 4, all column sums zero, exactly the five cyclic off-diagonal zeros, and every adjugate column equals the primitive vector a. The critical multipliers are (4,5,4,3,3).

The two vectors from Theorem 4 are

    v(p)=(−1,4,0,1,1),       v(q)=(−1,2,0,2,1),

giving the distinct pseudo-Frobenius numbers 470 and 490. The five-variable cyclic-zero extension of the four-variable criterion therefore fails even with genuine critical rows and primitive adjugate, not merely because a pseudo-principal matrix was mistaken for a principal one. ## 7. Sources, credit, and the remaining gap

The originating question is Srinivasan's joint-work report with Gimenez, “Gorenstein Monomial Curves,” in OWR 57/2016, printed pp. 3227–3230: https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2016/57 . Its five-generator question appears on printed p. 3229 in the discussion of non-complete intersections. It also records a decomposable case. We do not count those known cases as a new result.

Gimenez–Srinivasan, “Structure of some numerical semigroup rings,” Banach Center Publications 121 (2020), 53–61, https://doi.org/10.4064/bc121-5 , reviews the four-generator Bresinsky criterion and its principality strengthening in §3, and gives the gluing resolution and type-product results in Theorem 15 and Corollary 17. Those existing results justify the complete-intersection/gluing use in Theorem 1. Their Question 11 concerning type from maximal-rank principal data is not answered generally here.

Dey–Srinivasan, “Principal Matrices of Numerical Semigroups,” arXiv:2012.15464v3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15464 , explicitly demonstrates rank nonuniqueness in Example 2, classifies rank-3 five-generator block forms in Theorem 22, and proves the sufficient principality result numbered Theorem 26 in v3 (called Proposition 26 in its introduction). These results do not by themselves classify Gorenstein semigroups. The report's initial maximal-rank simplification must not be silently imposed on all genuine principal matrices.

**Sharp remaining task.** On the non-CI five-generator scope, obtain a necessary-and-sufficient structural condition that handles genuine choices and proves the required choice invariance. In particular, Theorem 1 does not provide a Gorenstein *non-CI* member of its indistinguishable pair, and Theorem 4 excludes only one explicit full-rank pattern. It does not settle other strongly connected rank-4 patterns, unsaturated row lattices, rank-4 block forms, or the residual rank-3 possibilities. Any use of extra scale/generator information must be stated. Reconstructing the tuple and applying a standard symmetry test is not the missing structural classification. No global novelty or current-openness claim is made.
