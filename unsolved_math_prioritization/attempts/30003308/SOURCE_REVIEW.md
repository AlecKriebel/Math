## Originating question

Srinivasan (joint work with Gimenez), “Gorenstein Monomial Curves,” OWR 57/2016, printed pp. 3227–3230, was read in full, rather than stopping at the extracted question. The relevant question lies in the non-complete-intersection discussion on p. 3229. The adjacent generator-count question is a different problem. The four-generator theorem and decomposable five-generator result are prior work, not contributions of this attempt.

The original report's informal maximal-rank recovery discussion must be read in light of later explicit rank-deficient examples. “Relatively prime” means collective gcd 1, not pairwise coprime. No step in the authored result strengthens that hypothesis.

Public source: https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2016/57

## Later primary papers

- Gimenez–Srinivasan, “Structure of some numerical semigroup rings,” Banach Center Publications 121 (2020), 53–61: all substantive sections read. Its maximal-rank type question remains distinct from a structural five-generator classification. The gluing type-product and complete-intersection statements used in Theorem 1 are credited to its established results and earlier Delorme/Herzog theory. Public source: https://doi.org/10.4064/bc121-5
- Dey–Srinivasan, “Principal Matrices of Numerical Semigroups,” arXiv:2012.15464v3, 18 June 2021: full paper, examples, appendix, and references read. Example 2 supplies the rank-choice control; Theorem 22 supplies rank-3 block context; the principality theorem is numbered Theorem 26 in the main text although its introduction calls it Proposition 26. None of these is misrepresented as a Gorenstein classification. Public source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15464 ; later chapter DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-91986-3_9

The report and later paper contain typographic slips in isolated formulas; the authored definitions use genuine least-positive critical multipliers and correct signs. The actual displayed examples used here are checked independently.

## Literature and claim boundary

Additional bounded web searches were used to look for closely related matrix/Gorenstein work. They do not establish novelty or an exhaustive current-openness result. Later family-specific papers found in those searches are not relied on for a general theorem. The authored mathematical results follow from the proofs and explicit certificates, not absence of a search hit.

The cyclic-order gap argument is proved from first principles; no claim is made that this lemma or the resulting special-case obstruction is new to the literature. The complete principal-data obstruction is likewise presented as a proved scope correction, not a novelty claim.

## Negative scope checks

- A pseudo-principal relation is not accepted without criticality.
- Rank 4 is not assumed for every genuine principal matrix.
- A conclusion about one selected matrix is not promoted to an invariant matrix-pattern characterization.
