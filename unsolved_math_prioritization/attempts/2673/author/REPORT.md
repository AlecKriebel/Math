# Universal nonabelian SU(2) surgery slopes: partial result and obstruction audit

**Problem:** KP-1.14 (record 2673). **Assessment:** partial progress; the full classification is not solved. **Research date:** 2026-10-08. Five mathematical approaches are recorded below. Source recovery, implementation, and tests are not counted as approaches. No novelty is asserted for consequences of existing theorems.

## 1. Scope and conventions

Let U be the set of nonzero rational numbers r such that every nontrivial knot K in S³ has a homomorphism π₁(S³_r(K)) → SU(2) with nonabelian image. The original question asks for U. The exact source is the new **K3 — A New Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topology**, Problem 1.14, printed page 24, scribed by John Baldwin [K3]. The formerly cited AIM workshop URL currently gives a four-page report, not the volume containing the question.

Knots are the ordinary smooth/tame knots for which the exterior has torus boundary. There is no restriction to prime or hyperbolic knots. Nor is irreducibility of the filled manifold assumed. Write r=p/q in lowest terms, q>0. With positively linked meridian μ and zero-linking Seifert longitude λ, filling kills μᵖλᑫ. The coefficient ∞ is not a rational number, and coefficient 0 is excluded by the question. Reversing the knot's orientation changes both peripheral generators and leaves the unoriented slope unchanged. Mirroring gives S³_r(mirror K) ≅ −S³_−r(K), so U=−U.

Abelianization of the exterior is generated by μ, with λ=0. The surgery relation therefore gives H₁(S³_p/q(K);Z)=Z/|p|. Every abelian representation factors through this finite cyclic group. Thus “abelian image” and “cyclic image” agree here. A representation into SU(2) is reducible over C iff its image is abelian: an invariant complex line has invariant orthogonal complement, giving simultaneous diagonalization; conversely commuting unitary matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable. Nontrivial image alone is insufficient when |p|>1. Neither a nonabelian abstract fundamental group nor an irreducible SL(2,C) representation by itself establishes the desired conclusion.

## 2. Results established in this audit

The principal authored corollary, using published mathematical ingredients, is:

> **Theorem A.** If p,q>0 are coprime, p=4ℓᵉ for an odd prime ℓ and integer e≥1, and p/q<7, then ±p/q belong to U.

Examples include 12/5, 12/7, 20/3, 28/5, and 36/7. This does not assert that these cases were unknown; it supplies a fully specified deduction beyond the prime-power-numerator range recorded in the original problem notes.

Published inputs also give 0<|r|≤2; prime-power-numerator slopes 0<|r|<5; and power-of-two-numerator slopes 0<|r|<7. The same nondegeneracy argument proves the <5 assertion for twice an odd prime power. References and dependencies are below.

The exact right-trefoil obstruction is

E={6} ∪ {6−1/n,6+1/n : n≥1}.

Every element of E∪(−E) is outside U. More generally, [SZ, Proposition 4.3] classifies all nontrivial SU(2)-abelian torus-knot surgeries: for coprime a,b with |a|,|b|>1 the slopes are ab+1/n, n∈Z\{0}, together with ab if |a|=2 or |b|=2. These furnish further exclusions, not a complete classification for arbitrary knots.

## 3. Imported mathematical inputs

The following are used as external theorems, not independently certified by our finite tests.

- **[KM]** Kronheimer–Mrowka, Theorem 1: every nontrivial knot has the required representation for |r|≤2.
- **[BS]** Baldwin–Sivek, Proposition 9.1 and Theorem 1.15: if Δ_K(ζ²)≠0 for every pth root ζ and positive p/q surgery is SU(2)-abelian, that surgery is an instanton L-space; K is fibered and strongly quasipositive, and 2g(K)−1≤p/q. We use the standard normalization Δ_K(1)=1. The genus-one possibility is the positive trefoil. Their Lemma 9.5 records the binary-dihedral argument attributed to Klassen.
- **[BLSY]** Baldwin–Li–Sivek–Ye, proof of Theorem 6.2: an instanton L-space knot of genus 2 has Δ=t²−t+1−t⁻¹+t⁻²; in genus 3 the alternatives are t³−t²+t−1+t⁻¹−t⁻²+t⁻³ and t³−t²+1−t⁻²+t⁻³. Their argument applies the Klassen representation count: det(K)>1 gives a nonabelian binary-dihedral representation. Theorem 6.2 itself proves the power-of-two numerator range below 7.
- **[FRW]** Farber–Reinoso–Wang, Corollary 1.4: the positive cinquefoil is the only genus-two instanton L-space knot. Corollary 1.5 supplies the prime-power range below 5.
- **[SZ]** Sivek–Zentner, Proposition 4.3: the torus-knot classification stated above.

The nondegeneracy hypothesis cannot be omitted from [BS]. “Instanton L-space” in these steps is not silently replaced by a Heegaard Floer L-space.

## 4. Approach 1: exact trefoil character calculation

This independently checks an infinite obstruction family and all slope signs/endpoints for the right trefoil. It recovers a known special case of [SZ].

Use G=⟨x,y | x²=y³⟩, h=x²=y³, μ=xy⁻¹, λ=hμ⁻⁶. These are the standard positive-trefoil peripheral generators. The element h is central. In a nonabelian SU(2) image its image must be ±I: the centralizer of a noncentral element of SU(2) is an abelian maximal torus. If h maps to I, x²=I makes x scalar, leaving an abelian image. Thus h maps to −I. Then x is a unit imaginary quaternion; y has angle π/3 unless y=−I, which again gives an abelian image.

After conjugation take x=i and y=1/2+(√3/2)v for a unit imaginary quaternion v. Nonabelian image is equivalent to v≠±i. The real part of μ=xy⁻¹ is (√3/2)⟨i,v⟩. Consequently its angle α takes exactly the open interval (π/6,5π/6), and every angle in that interval is realized. The filling equation is

(p−6q)α+qπ ∈ 2πZ.

Put d=|p−6q|. For d=0, coprimality gives (p,q)=(6,1), so the equation fails. For d=1 it would require α to be an integral multiple of π, also impossible. For d≥2 we seek k≡q mod 2 in the interval (d/6,5d/6), then take α=kπ/d. If d=2, coprimality forces q odd and k=1 works. If d=3, choose k=1 for odd q and k=2 for even q. If d≥4, the open interval has length 2d/3>2, so contains an integer in either parity class. This proves that the trefoil surgery has a nonabelian SU(2) representation exactly when |p−6q|≥2.

This proof works for all integral p and q>0 in lowest terms, not just a bounded slope interval. Passing to the mirror supplies the negative exclusions for U. It also verifies that the reducible manifold at slope 6 is a legitimate obstruction: “reducible representation” and “reducible manifold” are different notions.

**Remaining obstruction:** eliminating torus-knot counterexamples does not establish the universal property for other knots.

## 5. Approach 2: nondegeneracy, cyclotomic orders, and low genus

Let N=p/gcd(p,2). The squared pth roots of unity are exactly the Nth roots. If Δ_K has a primitive dth root as a zero, multiply by a power of t to get an integral polynomial. Its divisibility by the monic irreducible polynomial Φ_d remains integral, by Gauss's lemma. Since Δ_K(1)=1, this is impossible for d=1 or for d a prime power: Φ₁(1)=0, and Φ_ℓʲ(1)=ℓ.

Thus N=1 or a prime power guarantees nondegeneracy for every knot. This is exactly the arithmetic class “p is a prime power, or twice a prime power,” including p=1,2. Li–Ye explicitly note this extension, so we do not claim it as a new observation.

For r<5, [BS] forces a hypothetical SU(2)-abelian knot into genus at most 2. The genus-one case is the positive trefoil; the genus-two case is the positive cinquefoil by [FRW]. Section 4 and [SZ] exclude both below 5. This proves the asserted <5 range.

For a general numerator, a hypothetical exceptional knot in this interval must instead have a cyclotomic divisor Φ_d with d|N and d neither 1 nor a prime power. This is an exact necessary obstruction to this route. It gives deg Δ_K≥φ(d), hence g(K)≥φ(d)/2, but it does **not** allow the genus upper bound from [BS], whose hypothesis has now failed.

## 6. Approach 3: binary-dihedral descent and proof of Theorem A

First record the descent independently. Let D={exp(iθ)}∪{exp(iθ)j}⊂SU(2). A nonabelian representation of a knot group into D sends the normally generating meridian outside the circle subgroup; otherwise the full image would lie in that abelian normal subgroup. Every element outside that circle has square −1, hence fourth power 1. The Seifert longitude lies in the second commutator subgroup of the knot group. Because D has trivial second commutator subgroup, λ maps to 1. Consequently the representation factors through every surgery with 4|p, with unchanged nonabelian image. By the imported Klassen existence statement, SU(2)-abelian surgery with 4|p therefore forces det(K)=|Δ_K(−1)|=1.

Now suppose p=4ℓᵉ with ℓ odd prime. Then N=2ℓᵉ. Its divisors other than 1 and prime powers have precisely the form 2ℓʲ, 1≤j≤e. But

Φ_2ℓʲ(−1)=Φ_ℓʲ(1)=ℓ.

If any of these divided Δ_K, then ℓ would divide Δ_K(−1), contradicting determinant one. Thus a hypothetical SU(2)-abelian surgery is nondegenerate. For p/q<7, [BS] now implies 1≤g(K)≤3. The imported genus-one classification and genus-two/three polynomial restrictions give determinant 3,5,7, or 3, respectively. All contradict determinant one. This proves Theorem A. Mirroring proves its negative-slope version.

No machine computation is needed for the universal quantifiers in this proof. A finite evaluation check of the three displayed low-genus polynomials is only an arithmetic safeguard.

A useful more general conclusion is: if 4|p, 0<p/q<7, and the surgery is SU(2)-abelian, then det(K)=1 and Δ_K has a primitive dth root with d|p/2 and Φ_d(1)=Φ_d(−1)=1. Neither prime-power d nor twice-odd-prime-power d can occur.

**Remaining obstruction:** integers d such as 12 and 15 satisfy both cyclotomic evaluations equal to 1. The determinant argument does not remove them. For example Φ₁₂=t⁴−t²+1 and Φ₁₅=t⁸−t⁷+t⁵−t⁴+t³−t+1 survive these necessary algebraic checks. This is not a construction of an exceptional surgery.

## 7. Approach 4: characteristic-two Floer improvement, with source-status limits

The newer preprints were inspected rather than inferred from the older problem notes.

**[LY25]**, Theorem 1.6, establishes for 2<r<6 and numerator prime power or twice prime power that only the right trefoil at r=6−1/n can be SU(2)-abelian. Its arXiv page has v1, and the author's page calls it submitted. It is credited preprint progress, not our proof.

**[GME25]**, Theorem 1.3, provides integers M,R divisible by 4, R≥|M|, with mod-2 dimension qR+|p−qM| except at r=M with trivial bundle, where the dimension is R+2. Its Corollary 1.6 and the comparison with the complex theory imply that a positive nondegenerate SU(2)-abelian surgery has r>M≥2g−1 and M>0. The paper and author website disclose dependence on foundational work, including the still-forthcoming small-dg-module book. A public author draft of the indefinite-cobordism work is now linked. The foundational proofs are not audited here.

**Deduction conditional on these imported preprint results:** if 0<r≤8 is nondegenerate and SU(2)-abelian, M is a positive multiple of 4 strictly smaller than r. Hence M=4, and 2g−1≤4 gives g≤2. Published small-genus classification then leaves the trefoil or cinquefoil. The latter has no SU(2)-abelian slope below 9. Therefore the only possibilities through 8 are the trefoil slopes E.

This extends the universal range through 8, excluding E, whenever nondegeneracy is automatic. It also extends Theorem A to 0<r≤8 under the same preprint dependencies: the determinant-one argument forces nondegeneracy, and the surviving trefoil has determinant 3.

The integer bound is obtained directly by rounding M≥2g−1 up to a multiple of 4. Some displayed floor symbols in the preprint disagree with its abstract and piecewise expression; no floor transcription is used in this deduction. Neither preprint resolves all numerators or arbitrarily large slopes.

## 8. Approach 5: test the density/compactness shortcut

A dense set of admissible slopes does not establish admissibility at its rational limit. There is a concrete obstruction inside the exact trefoil calculation. Take odd n≥3 and r_n=(6n+2)/n. These fractions are reduced, |p−6q|=2, and Section 4 supplies nonabelian representations. Yet r_n→6, and slope 6 has no nonabelian representation for the trefoil. Conversely 6+1/n is SU(2)-abelian for every n and also tends to 6.

The obstruction is not just that irreducible representations can converge to reducibles. Here the group relations being imposed change with p and q; convergence of p/q does not turn μᵖλᑫ=1 into the filling relation of the limiting rational slope. For r_n one may take the same α=π/2; it satisfies each varying surgery equation, while at r=6 the equation becomes π≡0 mod 2π, which fails. Thus even a fixed irreducible representation along the sequence does not repair this limiting argument.

**Outcome:** the proposed closure principle is false already for a fixed trefoil. This blocks extending the prime-power or 4ℓᵉ classes solely by density.

## 9. Exact remaining task and validation boundary

The full set U is not determined. The published-only results here leave, among many others, the universal questions at r=15/4 and r=24/5 unresolved by these methods. Their respective cyclotomic obstructions d=15 and d=12 survive. No SU(2)-abelian surgery at either slope has been constructed, and no assertion that no other literature settles these individual examples is made. Bounded searches are not a novelty or comprehensive open-status certificate.

The accompanying dependency-free Python checker verifies rational arithmetic, cyclotomic evaluations and divisor classes, low-genus determinants, exact trefoil congruence witnesses, separation of proven positive classes from imported torus obstructions, and the density counterexample sequence. Its bounded enumerations cannot certify Floer theorems, completeness of source searches, or universal knot statements. It rejects unknown input fields and malformed/nonreduced slopes. Integrity verification requires an externally supplied manifest hash. Normal and optimized Python runs, hostile-input controls, read-only execution, and external hash pins are reported separately.
