## 3. Independent mathematical reconstruction

### 3.1 Model and measurable ambient space

Let ν be counting measure on the integers plus twice counting measure on the half-integers, and μ=λ₁⊗ν on R². Take the full phase space Ω=R/Z with its Borel sigma-field, action θ_(a,b)u=u+b modulo 1, measure-valued map ξ(u)=θ_(0,u)μ and rooted law Q=δ₀. An additional X may be constant with trivial action. This supplies the complete orbit, not merely two root states on which an action could be undefined.

Every compact set meets only finitely many supporting lines over a bounded horizontal interval. Thus ξ is nonzero, locally finite and Radon. Every singleton has zero measure; more generally an atomless Lebesgue measure on each of countably many disjoint lines has no positive-measure atom. Diffuseness therefore holds in the measure-theoretic sense, despite singularity with respect to planar Haar measure. The root is in the support. Infinite total mass is allowed.

Translation of compactly supported continuous test functions shows that u↦ξ(u) is vaguely continuous. The density-1 line locations distinguish phases modulo 1, so this map is injective. Compactness of the circle and Hausdorffness of the Radon-measure space make it a homeomorphism onto its compact orbit. The evaluation sigma-field and vague Borel sigma-field agree for locally finite Radon measures here. In particular σ(ξ)=B(Ω); no unobserved random coordinate is smuggled in. The action is jointly continuous and ξ is flow-adapted with the stated sign convention.

The stabilizer of every state is R×Z. All horizontal translations act trivially on the state, even though they translate spatial points. This is exactly the rigidity used next; it is permitted by the cited problem.

### 3.2 Universal allocation rigidity, including collisions

For an arbitrary admissible allocation, pointwise covariance gives τ_u(s)=s+π(θ_su), where π is a Borel displacement on the entire circle. Write π(0)=(a₀,b₀) and π(1/2)=(a₁,b₁). At state 0, every density-1 source line is sent by (x,n)↦(x+a₀,n+b₀), while every density-2 source line is sent by (x,n+1/2)↦(x+a₁,n+1/2+b₁).

These formulas follow from the stabilizer; they are not an assumed class of maps. Horizontal scaling, nonuniform splitting along a line, and using different integer-index rules are forbidden by pointwise covariance. Off-support map values may still be arbitrary and need not be injective.

A rectangle with horizontal width one detects the target vertical measure. Since horizontal translations preserve length, preservation implies equality of two unit-periodic vertical measures. Restricting to one half-open period, then taking residues modulo one, gives the finite identity

    δ_[b₀] + 2δ_[1/2+b₁] = δ₀ + 2δ_[1/2].

This restriction-before-quotient step is essential; pushing the entire infinite measure to the circle would produce infinite atoms and would not justify the argument.

There are only three possibilities after excluding positive mass at any off-support residue: both source families collide; they swap the two targets; or they retain their targets. Collision gives mass 3 at one target and zero at the other. Swapping gives target weights (2,1), not (1,2). Only target retention preserves mass. No alternate preimage multiplicities are available: within each source family, consecutive lines have consecutive images because the translation is fixed. In particular the single weight-1 family cannot provide two different preimages of one target line. Thus b₀ and b₁ are integers without any injectivity assumption.

Consequently τ₀(0) lies in R×Z and θ_(τ₀(0))0=0. The complete rooted law, including constant X, is fixed under every admissible preserving allocation. Preservation at the one Q-supported state already suffices. Requiring preservation at every state only narrows the class and causes no difficulty.

### 3.3 Whole-orbit and full canonical-space checks

For any phase u, each supported source point s has θ_su either 0 or 1/2. Hence the same two integral transverse displacements preserve ξ(u) on every phase, not merely almost surely under Q. Identity is one such globally defined preserving allocation; fixed horizontal and integral vertical translations give others. A Borel π with integer vertical components at 0 and 1/2, arbitrary Borel values elsewhere, also defines a genuine allocation on the entire orbit.

If a formulation insists on the ambient space being all locally finite Radon measures, the circle orbit is a compact Borel invariant subset. Any Borel displacement on it can be extended by zero outside it. Translations never move a measure from outside this orbit into it. The induced allocation is identity outside, is measurable and pointwise covariant everywhere, and preserves all measures whenever it preserves the orbit. Conversely any full-space allocation restricts to the orbit class already handled. Thus using Ω=R/Z neither conceals a global-definition obstruction nor omits an allowed test.

### 3.4 No exceptional-root escape

Although the origin is a μ-null singleton, changing π(0) changes the allocation of every point on every density-1 line. Each unit segment on such a line has positive μ-mass. The value at the root is therefore constrained by the preservation equation. Independently modifying that singleton while retaining all other map values breaks pointwise horizontal covariance. A theory identifying allocations only up to unspecified spatial null sets would require a separate origin convention; that altered problem is not certified here.

### 3.5 Direct joint mass-stationarity failure

Use C=[0,1)², U uniform on C, and V chosen from the normalized restriction of μ to C−U. The window has horizontal length one and contains precisely one line of each type, including the harmless half-open endpoint cases. Its mass is exactly 3. The chosen V lies on a density-1 line with probability 1/3 and a density-2 line with probability 2/3.

The measurable test H(η)=η([0,1)×{0}) equals 1 under Q. After θ_V it equals 2 with probability 2/3. Thus even the first marginal changes. This disproves the required joint identity (θ_Vω,U+V) =d (ω,U); it does not replace that identity with an insufficient marginal-only definition. Constant X introduces no additional condition. The proof is independent of any Palm uniqueness assertion.

For consistency, uniform phase P on the circle is stationary and ergodic for the full R² action. Its intensity is 3 and its normalized Palm law has phase weights (1/3,2/3). This agrees with the calculation. Merely differing from a chosen Palm law would not itself be a substitute for the preceding direct test, but no such substitution occurs.

### 3.6 Boundary controls

The model is two-dimensional, singular, and has a continuous invariant direction. It does not contradict one-dimensional diffuse results or positive-density/no-invariant-direction theorems. An independent stationary field can remove the stabilizer obstruction; it is excluded by the question's premise.

The model is not invariant under all preserving Markov kernels. At the two phase types, the stochastic matrix with rows (0,1) and (1/2,1/2) preserves the weights (1,2) and moves the light root to the heavy type. Realize its transitions by the corresponding vertical displacements and use the identity off support; the same covariance formula produces a global preserving kernel. This explicit control prevents misreporting the negative answer as a counterexample to the neighboring all-Markov question.
