Audit date: 2026-10-15 UTC. Core verdict frozen at 11:36:33 UTC before reading any other priority-family output. Scope: the candidate's exact diffuse negative result under measurable, pointwise-covariant, original-pair allocation tests. This audit makes no firstness or worldwide-current-openness claim.

## Decision

**The full negative diffuse conclusion is a complete elementary consequence of existing results. It should not be promoted as a new mathematical resolution.** Last–Thorisson's 2009 Example 7.1 supplies a non-mass-stationary law invariant under all preserving allocations. Their product proposition, already in arXiv:1405.7566v1 on 2014-05-29 and retained in v2 and the 2015 publication, preserves and reflects mass-stationarity after adjoining Lebesgue measure. Pointwise covariance along the added trivial coordinate also preserves and reflects the allocation-invariance property, by the short calculation below. Consequently the old atomic example gives the entire claimed diffuse counterexample on R², with constant X and a continuous stabilizer, without the candidate's periodic construction.

I did not locate the full diffuse negative conclusion as an explicit statement in the primary bodies actually inspected. That is a narrower bibliographic observation; it does **not** create a novelty gap when the complete implication is elementary. The exact deterministic periodic realization with rational weights (1,2) has unestablished historical priority in this audit. Its specificity does not rescue a discovery claim for the general negative conclusion.

Mathematical validity and explanatory usefulness are separate. A concise credited note can still be useful: state the general product allocation equivalence, identify the old Poisson counterexample as sufficient, and present the periodic example as an elementary illustration. Whether such a note warrants publication is an editorial decision, not established mathematical novelty.

## Exact audited claim and conventions

Let A(Q;X,ξ) mean that for every measurable displacement π determined by the original pair, whose allocation

    τ_y(s) = s + π(θ_s y),       y=(X,ξ),

preserves ξ, the rooted pair shifted by π has law Q again. Covariance is pointwise in states and locations. No injectivity assumption is made. Mass-stationarity means the full joint identity with the independent uniform window point U and conditionally mass-chosen displacement V. Only testing the marginal shifted measure is not substituted for this definition.

The candidate asserts existence on R² of nonzero, singular, diffuse, locally finite ξ with constant X, with the origin in its support and with continuous translation stabilizer, such that A holds and full mass-stationarity fails. It uses Lebesgue measure on horizontal lines of two alternating densities. Its current addendum already disclaims novelty of the unequal-mass mechanism, product lift, and negative result. This audit concerns the stronger priority question of whether those credits correspond to a *complete implication*, rather than just related ingredients. They do.

## Checkable allocation-transfer calculation

This calculation is the auditor's derivation of the exact logical bridge, not a claim that the cited papers state the bridge verbatim.

Take a measurable base pair y=(X,ξ) on R^d. Extend the action to R^(d+1) by θ_(t,v)y=θ_t y, and put

    J(y) = (X, ξ⊗λ₁).

In particular the new coordinate acts trivially on the *entire observable state*, not only on the random measure. Consider any pointwise-covariant allocation T on these lifted states. Covariance under (0,v) gives

    T_y(s,u+v) = T_y(s,u)+(0,v).

Therefore there are measurable functions a_y:R^d→R^d and b_y:R^d→R such that

    T_y(s,u) = (a_y(s), u+b_y(s)).                       (A)

The functions are obtained by evaluating T at (s,0) and projecting. Covariance in the original coordinates implies

    a_(θ_t y)(s−t) = a_y(s)−t,

so a is a base allocation. It is generated by the displacement α(y)=a_y(0). This does not presume a measurable choice of origins, a fundamental domain, a bijection, or a free action.

For Borel B⊂R^d and an interval I⊂R of positive finite length, Tonelli and translation invariance of λ₁ give

    (T_y)_*(ξ_y⊗λ₁)(B×I)
      = ∫ 1{a_y(s)∈B} [∫1{u+b_y(s)∈I}du] ξ_y(ds)
      = |I| ξ_y(a_y^−1(B)).                            (B)

Thus T preserves ξ⊗λ₁ if and only if a preserves ξ. Necessity follows immediately from (B). For sufficiency, (B) establishes equality on a determining class of rectangles; both measures are σ-finite because the target is locally finite, and Tonelli extends the equality. No cancellation of infinities or pushforward of an infinite measure onto a compact quotient occurs. The calculation allows collisions and arbitrary measurable b.

At the root, the vertical part of T_y(0,0) has no effect on J(y):

    θ_(a_y(0),b_y(0))J(y) = J(θ_(a_y(0))y).             (C)

If A holds for the base pair, the projected preserving allocation a and (C) show A for the lifted pair. This is the direction needed for the counterexample.

For the reverse direction, let a be any measurable preserving base allocation. Define

    T_y(s,u) = (a_y(s),u).

It is measurable, pointwise covariant, and preserving by (B). Lifted allocation invariance then gives the equality in law of J(θ_(a_y(0))y) and J(y). The base pair can be recovered measurably: for the measure component, evaluate

    P(η)(B) = η(B×[0,1))

on product states, so P(ξ⊗λ₁)=ξ; keep the X coordinate. Hence this equality reflects back to the base pair. We have proved

    A(Q;X,ξ)  ⇔  A(J_*Q;X,ξ⊗λ₁).                     (D)

J is a measurable equivariant injection on the usual random-measure state space; the displayed cylinder evaluation supplies its measurable inverse on its image. Thus (D) is about the original *observable pairs*. It neither adds independent randomness nor secretly gives allocations a larger state than the problem permits. A test defined on the product image can simply be pulled back by J. If a convention requires a rule on a larger ambient canonical space, use the identity allocation off the invariant Borel product-state image.

The preservation calculation is pointwise at each state where preservation is required. It therefore also accommodates a Q-a.e. preservation convention with pointwise covariance. It does not assert validity for conventions that only specify a spatial-a.e. allocation class without a covariant representative at the root.

The published product proposition gives

    MS(Q;X,ξ) ⇔ MS(J_*Q;X,ξ⊗λ₁).                       (E)

Equations (D) and (E) transfer any allocation-only counterexample from dimension d to a diffuse one in dimension d+1. The source's Section9 formula (23) in v2, or (22) in v1, is consistent with precisely this reduction: substituting (A) into its u-average makes that kernel the Dirac kernel at a_y(s). Thus the prior paper already places the calculation very close to the relevant formalism, although it does not explicitly announce the corollary here.

## Applying the old counterexample without hidden state

Use the 2009 Example 7.1 on R. Under Q, let N₁ be a Palm version of a rate-one Poisson process and N₂ an independent stationary rate-one Poisson process, with irrational c>0, and define ζ=N₁+cN₂. The source establishes allocation invariance on its full two-process state Ω, and non-mass-stationarity of ζ. Take X constant and consider ζ alone.

Every ζ-measurable allocation is also Ω-measurable by composition. A ζ-preserving allocation therefore belongs to the class already covered by the source, and invariance of Ω implies invariance of its ζ factor. No reverse factor inference is needed for this direction. The failure of mass-stationarity is observable from ζ itself: under Q the atom at the origin has mass1 a.s.; the defining mass sampling chooses a c-atom with positive probability. Hence forgetting the two-process labels cannot remove the failure.

In fact, on the full-probability invariant set of disjoint simple Poisson configurations, the atom weights1 and c recover the two labels from ζ. This can be implemented measurably on the standard Borel point-measure space, but the stronger recovery assertion is unnecessary to pass invariance to the factor. The cylinder projection P then recovers ζ from ζ⊗λ₁.

Apply (D) and (E) to get μ=ζ⊗λ₁ on R². This is nonzero, locally finite, and diffuse: each individual point has zero λ₁ mass, while bounded rectangles have finite mass. It is singular because its support is a countable union of parallel lines. Translation along the added Lebesgue coordinate fixes the entire canonical μ-state. A constant X is legitimate. The origin belongs to the support because N₁ has an atom at0. The resulting μ passes every original-pair measurable pointwise-covariant preserving-allocation test and is not mass-stationary.

One can also see non-mass-stationarity directly after the lift, rather than relying only on a latent-state version of (E): for a fixed unit segment I in the Lebesgue coordinate, the statistic μ({0}×I) records the base root atom's weight. It equals1 under Q and equals c with positive probability after the required mass-chosen shift. This is a marginal failure and therefore a full joint failure. It uses the same observable witness as the source, not extra randomness in the test class.

If preservation is only required Q-a.e., the same conclusion still applies. The event E that a given pointwise-covariant rule preserves the flow-adapted measure is measurable (test a countable determining class) and shift-invariant. Replace the rule by the identity outside E. The replacement is globally preserving, remains pointwise covariant, and agrees with the original rule Q-a.s. The source's global preserving-rule assertion therefore covers this convention as well.

This establishes every existential feature of the audited theorem. It does not claim that the source explicitly chose this μ.

## Irrational weights, periodicity, and the candidate's rational weights

The source's irrational c rules out arbitrary splitting and recombination of atomic weights in a general Poisson configuration. It should not be silently replaced by2 in that same argument: a weight2 target could in principle receive two distinct weight1 atoms.

The candidate's (1,2) example has a different simplification. Pointwise covariance forces one complete line family per unit period to be translated to one complete line family per unit period. Its finite per-period mass identity has only two source atoms, of weights1 and2. A collision gives weight3; swapping gives incorrect weights; thus each family stays in its own residue. This is not a new way to defeat the abstract Poisson result, but a periodic realization in which irrationality is unnecessary. The finite quotient calculation in the candidate compares per-period measures. Directly pushing the entire infinite line measure onto the torus would instead produce infinite atoms and would not justify the argument.

The general product transfer above does not depend on any quotient or periodicity. It works equally for the aperiodic Poisson base and for the candidate's periodic base. Consequently potential historical priority of this exact periodic formula is a separate question from priority of the full diffuse negative conclusion. The former remains unknown here; the latter is already a complete prior-result consequence.

## Adversarial checks on the implication

1. **Could the new diffuse coordinate itself permit splitting?** Not under the audited state and covariance. The entire state is invariant in that coordinate, forcing (A). A background varying vertically would invalidate this restriction, but the original question excludes such added information.
2. **Does a projection lose the counterexample witness?** No. The base root weight is measurable from the measure and from a unit slice of its lift. Thus failure survives canonical projection.
3. **Is every lifted test covered?** Yes: projection of an arbitrary lifted rule gives a measurable base rule, with preservation proved by (B). It is not enough to check only lifted versions of selected base rules; (A) checks the full class.
4. **Does the reverse test transfer require hidden state?** No: J and P are explicit measurable observable maps. A base rule can be read from the lifted measure using P.
5. **Is bijectivity assumed?** No. Neither (A) nor (B) uses it. The Poisson irrational-weight mechanism and the candidate's finite periodic mechanism address their respective noninjective possibilities.
6. **Does marginal invariance replace joint mass-stationarity?** No. (E) concerns the full defining joint identity, and the observable root-weight change independently defeats even a marginal, which is sufficient to defeat the joint identity.
7. **Does the argument generalize beyond its assumptions?** The full claim is existential on R². It does not resolve a diffuse R theorem, a free/no-invariant-direction version, an absolutely-continuous positive-density version, or the neighboring Markovian-kernel question.
8. **Is a missing explicit sentence enough for novelty?** No. Here the central theorem follows by covariance, one Tonelli calculation, and two already available results. There is no remaining substantive mathematical step whose resolution could justify calling the whole conclusion new.
