## Exact claim and source contract

Let

\[
\nu=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\delta_n+2\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\delta_{n+1/2},
\qquad \mu=\lambda_1\otimes\nu.
\]

Use the canonical shift convention \(\theta_t\eta(B)=\eta(B+t)\). The state is phase \(u\in\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), with \(\theta_{(a,b)}u=u+b\pmod1\), \(\xi(u)=\theta_{(0,u)}\mu\), and law \(Q=\delta_0\). The extra random element \(X\) is constant. The claim is that this diffuse, singular random measure satisfies invariance of its rooted law under **every measurable pair-dependent pointwise covariant preserving allocation**, but fails mass-stationarity. This refutes the unrestricted implication of Thorisson's Problem 4.1 / Last–Thorisson's Problem 7.5 already on \(\mathbb R^2\).

Thorisson §4, pp. 5–6, defines allocations through \(s\mapsto s+\pi(s(X,\xi))\) and asks the background-free diffuse question. Last–Thorisson (2009), Example 3.4, explicitly has covariance for every state and every pair of translations. Definition 6.1 and Remark 6.2 require, for each admissible relatively compact \(C\),

\[
(\theta_V\omega,U+V)\stackrel d=(\omega,U),
\]

where \(U\) is uniform on \(C\), independent of the original state, and conditional on that state and \(U\), \(V\) has the normalized random-measure restriction to \(C-U\). A discrepancy of the first component already disproves this joint identity. None of these operative source statements assumes a free action, an everywhere positive density, absence of invariant directions, or full support.

## Measure and state realization

Each compact planar rectangle meets finitely many supporting horizontal lines in finite-length segments. Thus \(\mu_u\) is nonzero, locally finite and Radon, and is sigma-finite despite infinite total mass. Every point has zero mass. More strongly, for any finite-positive-mass Borel set, its first-coordinate marginal is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\lambda_1\), so that set can be split in its first coordinate; no positive-mass Borel atom is hidden in the supporting lines. Diffuseness here does not imply planar absolute continuity. At the supported root phases \(u=0,1/2\), the origin lies in the measure support.

The torus flow is jointly Borel and the measure map is flow adapted, with the correct positive sign on phase: shifting the origin upward by \(b\) changes phase from \(u\) to \(u+b\). Translation is continuous in the vague topology, and unequal line densities make the phase map injective. The compact-to-Hausdorff argument in the candidate therefore identifies the phase sigma-algebra with that generated by the measure.

An independent explicit check of the measurable inverse is available. For a measure on this orbit, set

\[
F(\eta)=\int_{[0,1)^2}e^{2\pi i y}\,\eta(dx\,dy).
\]

This is a Borel evaluation integral, and \(F(\mu_u)=-e^{-2\pi i u}\). Its argument determines \(u\) measurably. Thus there is no hidden random mark or extra sigma-algebra in the torus representation. The coordinate swap relative to the product order in the 2015 source is immaterial and is explicit here.

## Universal allocation premise

Write the arbitrary measurable displacement at phases 0 and 1/2 as \(\pi(0)=(a_0,b_0)\), \(\pi(1/2)=(a_1,b_1)\). By the exact source covariance,

\[
\tau_0(x,n)=(x+a_0,n+b_0),\qquad
\tau_0(x,n+1/2)=(x+a_1,n+1/2+b_1).
\]

In particular, the displacement cannot depend on the first coordinate along a source line, and the unit-period stabilizer prevents different choices on different lines of the same family. This is forced by the original-state dependence and covariance, not an assumed injectivity, continuity, or differentiability of the allocation.

Equality of pushforward and original measure, tested on finite rectangles of horizontal length one and a single transverse period, gives

\[
\delta_{[b_0]}+2\delta_{[1/2+b_1]}=
\delta_0+2\delta_{1/2}.
\]

This finite per-period identity is justified directly from the periodic measures. It does **not** push an infinite measure onto a compact quotient, which would give infinite masses. Off-support residues are impossible; a collision would give mass 3 at one target; a swap gives the wrong unequal weights. Hence \(b_0,b_1\in\mathbb Z\), and the rooted displacement lies in the stabilizer \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z\). The rooted state therefore remains phase 0 for every preserving allocation.

Potential noninjectivity has been allowed throughout. There is one light line family per period, so the heavy family cannot be replaced by two independently chosen light families. Preservation only at the sole \(Q\)-supported state already gives the result; requiring preservation everywhere only narrows the class. Identity is preserving, as are horizontal or integer vertical translations, so the premise is not vacuous.

The root is a mass-null singleton, but cannot be modified independently under pointwise covariance: changing \(\pi(0)\) changes the allocation on each entire light line, and unit segments on those lines have positive mass. A different convention that allows arbitrary changes at the root modulo spatial null sets without selecting a pointwise covariant representative is outside the stated claim. The actual primary definition uses the pointwise convention.

## Direct complete conditional sampler and discrepancy

Take \(C=[0,1)^2\). It is relatively compact, of Haar mass one, and its boundary has planar Haar mass zero. Write \(U=(U_1,U_2)\) uniform in \(C\). Its translated square intersects exactly one light line and one heavy line, each in horizontal length one. Therefore

\[
\mu(C-U)=1+2=3.
\]

This is in fact true also at the half-open endpoint phases; boundary values can safely be ignored but introduce no zero denominator.

Here is an explicit Borel realization of the required conditional law, independent of any purported Palm uniqueness theorem. Independently of \(U\), choose \(S\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1)\) and \(J\in\{0,1\}\) with probabilities \(1/3,2/3\). Put

\[
V_1=S-U_1,
\qquad
V_2=\begin{cases}
0,&J=0,\\
1/2,&J=1,\ U_2<1/2,\\
-1/2,&J=1,\ U_2\ge1/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Given \(U\), the light segment has normalized mass 1/3, the heavy segment mass 2/3, and the horizontal position is uniform on the required unit interval. This is exactly the normalized restriction in the definition. The choices of \(S,J\) exist only to realize the prescribed resampling experiment; they are **not** extra stationary information admitted to the allocation premise.

The new state is phase \(V_2\pmod1\). Its law is

\[
Q_*=(1/3)\delta_0+(2/3)\delta_{1/2},
\]

whereas the original state law is \(Q=\delta_0\). The evaluation event

\[
\{\eta([0,1)\times\{0\})=2\}
\]

is Borel and has probabilities 2/3 after resampling and 0 originally. A null-set change of the conditional kernel on exceptional \(U\) values cannot eliminate this positive discrepancy. Thus the first marginal, and therefore the required joint mass-stationarity identity, fails.

For completeness, the entire joint law after this square experiment is \(Q_*\otimes\mathrm{Unif}(C)\): \(U_1+V_1=S\), while \(U_2+V_2\) is either \(U_2\) or its rotation by 1/2 modulo one, and is uniform in either case, independently of \(J\). This is a direct joint-law verification rather than a marginal-only replacement of the definition.

For the other supported initial root phase \(1/2\), the roles of which family sits at height zero reverse, but mass sampling again yields the same \(Q_*\). Thus neither deterministic root phase is mass-stationary; the two-state reset matrix has both rows \((1/3,2/3)\). In particular, the failure is not an accidental choice of the notation for a root phase.

## Palm consistency and established product mechanism

Under uniform phase \(P=\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb T)\), the model is stationary. For any nonnegative measurable \(f\), direct integration gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb T}\int_{[0,1)^2}f(\theta_tu)\,\mu_u(dt)\,du
=f(0)+2f(1/2).
\]

The intensity is 3, so its normalized Palm phase law is exactly \(Q_*\). This confirms the resampling computation, but the proof of failure does not infer non-mass-stationarity merely from differing from one selected Palm law.

There is also an independent finite Mecke-identity falsification. Equation (2.7) of Last–Thorisson (2009) is necessary for a nonzero rooted law to be any stationary Palm measure. Let \(H(\eta)=\eta([0,1)\times\{0\})\), let \(B=[-1,0)\times[-3/4,-1/4)\), and define

\[
g(\omega,s)=1\{H(\xi(\omega))=2\}\,1_B(s).
\]

Under \(Q=\delta_0\), the right side of (2.7), \(E_Q\int g(\omega,s)\xi(ds)\), is zero because the root density is 1. Its left side, \(E_Q\int g(\theta_s\omega,-s)\xi(ds)\), is 2: the reflected rectangle picks exactly a length-one segment of the heavy line at height 1/2, and re-rooting there has root density 2. Both integrals are finite. This excludes being any stationary Palm measure, independently of selecting the uniform-phase model, and agrees with the direct mass-stationarity failure. It is an alternate check of the same frozen mechanism, not a new proof-search route or a priority claim.

I read the complete body of Last–Thorisson (2015), §8 Proposition 1 and Theorem 8, including the preceding reduction lemma and subsequent explanation of the kernel classes. Proposition 1 proves that adjoining a Lebesgue coordinate preserves and reflects mass-stationarity. This is established prior work and must remain credited.

An elementary structural check makes the connection still more explicit. Start with any base flow \(\theta_s\) and measure \(\zeta\). Lift to \(G\times\mathbb R\) by \(\widehat\theta_{(s,v)}=\theta_s\) and \(\widehat\zeta=\zeta\otimes\lambda_1\). A pair-dependent displacement \(\pi=(a,b)\) yields

\[
\widehat\tau(s,v)=
\bigl(s+a(\theta_s\omega),\ v+b(\theta_s\omega)\bigr).
\]

For finite-measure Borel rectangles, Tonelli and translation invariance give

\[
(\widehat\tau_*\widehat\zeta)(A\times B)
=\lambda_1(B)\,(\rho_*\zeta)(A),
\quad\rho(s)=s+a(\theta_s\omega).
\]

Consequently lifted preservation is equivalent to base preservation, and the root shift on the original state is exactly the base root shift. Conversely each base allocation lifts with \(b=0\). Background-free preserving-allocation invariance therefore transfers in this product setting. Combined with the cited mass-stationarity product proposition, this transfers allocation-versus-mass-stationarity separation from a base atomic example to a diffuse example. This derivation explains the periodic candidate and is not asserted to be original.

Both directions also preserve the class of observed-state measurable maps; this is not an illicit enlargement of the state. Define the measurable embedding \(E(\zeta)=\zeta\otimes\lambda_1\), and the measurable projection \(D(\eta)(A)=\eta(A\times[0,1))\). The latter is locally finite when \(\eta\) is locally finite, and measurability follows from the evaluation sigma-algebras. Product-kernel measurability makes \(E\) measurable, while \(D E\zeta=\zeta\). Thus \((X,\zeta)\) and \((X,E\zeta)\) generate exactly the same observed information. For a lifted displacement, compose with \(E\) and project to its base coordinate to obtain the measurable base displacement. For a base displacement \(a(X,\zeta)\), the formula \((a(X,D\eta),0)\) gives a measurable lifted displacement on the canonical measure space. The lifted flow acts trivially on the new coordinate of \(X\), so it supplies no extra mark or stationary randomness. The rectangle identity proves preservation in both directions, either pointwise or almost surely in the original state; a monotone-class argument extends rectangle equality to all Borel sets. On the product image, the intertwining of \(E\), \(D\), and root shifts then makes rooted-law invariance equivalent in the two observed state spaces.

The 2009 Example 7.1 already supplies an unequal-atomic-mass obstruction. The negative conclusion is therefore an elementary mathematical consequence of that known atomic separation, the explicit allocation-transfer identity just checked, and the 2015 mass-stationarity product proposition. This establishes a derivation from prior ingredients; it does not establish when or by whom the combined consequence was first observed or published. **This audit is not a novelty certificate.** The existing credit addendum correctly warns against presenting the product method or negative result as newly discovered. \[
\lambda_1\bigl([0,H)\cap[-y,H-y)\cap[c-y,d-y)\bigr)
\]

for every relevant supporting line \(y=n+r_j-u\), weighted by \(w_j/[H^2(a+b)]\). This calculates the joint probability of output phase \(r_j\) and output coordinate \(U_2+V_2\in[c,d)\).
