The Lebesgue-product construction is established prior work. Last and Thorisson, *Construction and Characterisation of Stationary and Mass-Stationary Random Measures on R^d*, arXiv:1405.7566v2, Section 8, Proposition 1, proves that adjoining a Lebesgue coordinate preserves and reflects mass-stationarity. Its Theorem 8 characterizes the resulting diffuse lift using independent stationary backgrounds. [Primary manuscript, printed pp. 14–15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.7566v2).

The frozen argument gives an explicit periodic realization, with a complete proof that every background-free preserving allocation fixes its rooted state. The product-lift method itself is not an original contribution of this candidate. No novelty, historical priority, or exhaustive literature-clearance claim is made.

The unequal-mass obstruction is also established prior work: Last–Thorisson (2009), Example 7.1, and the related authored atomic `(1,2)` class-separation control in [PR 284, TURN_3.md at the cited commit](https://github.com/AlecKriebel/Math/blob/7d244eed5d7ddd89d5c540aecfa72490b7b30073/unsolved_math_prioritization/attempts/30001080/TURN_3.md). The atomic, diffuse, background-randomized, and Cox-projected test classes must remain distinguished.

This is a credit/presentation correction, not a mathematical repair. Descriptions of the candidate should carry this attribution and should avoid describing either the product-lift method or the negative result as newly discovered.
