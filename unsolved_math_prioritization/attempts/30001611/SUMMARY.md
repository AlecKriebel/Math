# Scope and status

Problem: 30001611 / OWR-4531-003, bicyclic-operator inverse-growth threshold.

Result: internally accepted rigorous partial; the requested counterexample or universal cyclicity theorem is not established.

The AI-assisted mathematics and independent internal audit are unrefereed; no external human peer review or formal proof-assistant certification is claimed.

Proved here:

- The square-root-weight Hilbert shift satisfies every hypothesis, with forward norm 1, inverse norm exp(√n), a bicyclic vector, and empty adjoint point spectrum.
- A second explicit Banach model has a bicyclic vector z−1, forward norm 1, inverse logarithmic norm asymptotic to √n, and adjoint point spectrum exactly the unit circle minus {1}.
- Explicit bounded dual functionals separate z−1 from the cutoff ideal. Its distance is at least 15/8192 in the stated norm; the defect quotient is infinite-dimensional.
- The defect quotient itself is cyclic. A proper cutoff ideal therefore does not establish the desired noncyclicity.

The mathematical argument is self-contained apart from standard Banach-space, Banach-algebra, Fourier, and complex-analysis results explicitly identified in the proof. The endpoint ideal phenomenon is classical and is credited to Atzmon's 1980 discussion. No novelty or complete-resolution claim is made. The 2007 primary note was read in full; the original report's complete relevant contribution was inspected. The longer 2008 article was bibliographically verified but its full text was not retrieved.

The computational checks are consistency tests for the authored formulas and norm inequalities. They are not certificates of cyclicity or noncyclicity, nor substitutes for the infinite-dimensional proofs.
