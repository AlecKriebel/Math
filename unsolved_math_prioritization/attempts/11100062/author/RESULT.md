# Reduced powers on loops of finite complexes: scoped results

Problem 11100062 (AMR-110-0062), Hovey's unstable-homotopy item 3.
Research cutoff: 6 October 2026. Status: **the original question is unresolved by this investigation**. Five substantive approaches were examined. No new general proof, fixed-space counterexample, or priority claim is asserted.

## 1. Exact target and conventions

For every fixed simply connected finite CW complex X, is there a number p0(X) such that, for every odd prime p > p0(X), every integer t > 0, and every degree n >= 0,

    P^t : H^n(ΩX; F_p) -> H^{n+2t(p-1)}(ΩX; F_p)

is zero?

This is the complete mathematical question recovered from [Hovey, item 3](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~tlawson/hovey/unstable.html), rather than an inference from the truncated catalogue title. The normalized complete source entry agrees exactly with the authenticated catalogue statement. Only positive reduced powers are intended: P^0 is the identity. The Bockstein is not included. The space X is fixed before the prime varies; the bound must work in all loop-cohomology degrees at once. No replacement by rationalization, p-completion, a stable loop functor, or a finite-dimensional truncation is made.

## 2. Literature disposition

The rationally elliptic case has a classical affirmative answer through McGibbon–Wilkerson's large-prime loop decomposition. The hypotheses include finite-dimensional rational homotopy, not merely finite-dimensional X. The original 1986 PDF was inaccessible; its theorem was checked in Anick's 1992 Theorem 6 and against Stanton's 2025 discussion. [Anick 1992](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/anick1.pdf)

Anick's cup-power theorem, as stated and extended by Menichi, gives x^p=0 for x of positive degree when X is r-connected and dim X <= rp. For a fixed finite simply connected X, r=1 suffices at large primes. That result does not assert the vanishing of all P^t. [Menichi, page 1 and concluding question](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0101221)

Stanton's published 2025 paper obtains the target for a family including moment-angle complexes indexed by two-dimensional simplicial complexes. A newer preprint must also be included: Stanton–Vylegzhanin, arXiv:2506.15573v3, revised 14 January 2026, gives a large-prime decomposition for every finite moment-angle complex. Its Proposition 4.19 supplies the sharper reduced-power consequence in Section 4 below. This is a preprint, and its general polyhedral-product results retain hypotheses on the input spaces. They do not settle arbitrary X. [Stanton 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0013091525000203); [Stanton–Vylegzhanin v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15573v3)

A 2025 thesis explicitly presents the broad Steenrod-action question as open. Only its indexed repository text, not its inaccessible complete PDF, was inspected. This is bounded status evidence, not proof that no later solution exists. [Stanton thesis](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/503922/)

## 3. Homology-generator criterion and sharp suspension case

**Proposition 3.1.** Let Z be a connected homotopy-associative H-space with a unit and finite-dimensional mod-p homology in each degree, p odd. If the Pontryagin algebra H_*(Z; F_p) is generated by homogeneous elements of degree strictly below 2p, then every positive reduced power vanishes on H^*(Z; F_p).

**Proof.** Write P_*^t for the linear dual of P^t. If z has degree q, then P_*^t z has degree k=q-2t(p-1). A potentially nonzero pairing with a cohomology class a in degree k requires 2t <= k by instability. Thus a nonzero P_*^t on degree q requires q >= 2pt. Every generator with q<2p is therefore annihilated by every P_*^t with t>0. Naturality for the H-space multiplication and the external Cartan formula give

    P_*^t(xy) = sum_{i+j=t} (P_*^i x)(P_*^j y).

For t>0, at least one factor has positive index. Induction on word length proves vanishing on every monomial in the generators, hence on all homology. Degreewise duality proves the claim. This reasoning imposes no torsion-free integral-homology hypothesis. □

**Corollary 3.2.** If Y is a connected finite CW complex of dimension d, then every positive P^t vanishes on H^*(ΩΣY; F_p) whenever d<2p.

**Proof.** The Bott–Samelson theorem identifies H_*(ΩΣY; F_p), as a Pontryagin algebra, with the tensor algebra on reduced H_*(Y; F_p). Its algebra generators have degrees at most d; apply Proposition 3.1. The theorem and its natural tensor generators are classical; see the explicit use in [Büscher–Hebestreit–Röndigs–Stelzer, proof of Proposition 4.3, page 143](https://msp.org/agt/2013/13-1/agt-v13-n1-p05-s.pdf). □

The bound is sharp for this dimension-only criterion. At any odd prime p take Y=CP^p, with x in H^2(Y; F_p). Then P^1x=x^p is nonzero. The James unit j:Y->ΩΣY induces an injection on homology onto tensor length one, hence a surjection on cohomology. Choose a in H^2(ΩΣY; F_p) with j^*a=x. Naturality gives j^*P^1a=x^p != 0. Thus nonvanishing occurs when d=2p.

These results also pass to homotopy retracts: if Z is a retract of W and the powers vanish on W, the induced split injection H^*(Z)->H^*(W) and naturality force vanishing on Z. A p-local retract is sufficient for mod-p cohomology in the usual nilpotent finite-type setting. Finite products preserve vanishing by the Künneth and Cartan formulas.

## 4. An explicit moment-angle consequence of current literature

Let K be a finite simplicial complex on m vertices, without ghost vertices. Write Z_K=(D^2,S^1)^K for its moment-angle complex and DJ(K)=(CP^∞,*)^K for its Davis–Januszkiewicz space.

**Corollary 4.1 (credited consequence).** For every odd prime p>=m and every t>0, P^t vanishes on H^*(ΩZ_K; F_p). The same conclusion holds for ΩDJ(K).

**Proof.** Proposition 4.19 of Stanton–Vylegzhanin v3 says that H_*(ΩDJ(K); k), for any field k, has algebra generators in degrees <2m. These lie below 2p when p>=m, so Proposition 3.1 applies. Their Theorem 6.4 gives the space-level splitting

    ΩDJ(K) ≃ (S^1)^m × ΩZ_K.

Thus ΩZ_K is a homotopy retract, and the conclusion transfers. □

Only the published-in-preprint generator theorem and splitting are used. No torsion-prime exclusion is needed for this reduced-power consequence. This is not a claim that the integral loop decomposition or the Bockstein vanishes at this bound, and no novelty or optimality of the bound p>=m is claimed. Although DJ(K) is not finite, it is used solely as an auxiliary space; Z_K is finite. The general Hovey question is not reduced to this family.

## 5. A degree-dependent bound for every finite X

**Proposition 5.1.** Let X be an r-connected finite CW complex of dimension d, with r>=1 and d>=r+1. Let N>=0 and p be odd. Put w=d-r-1. If

    2(p-1) > w floor(N/r),

then P^t vanishes on H^n(ΩX; F_p) for every 0<=n<=N and every t>0.

**Proof.** Use the convergent cohomological Eilenberg–Moore filtration F_s for the based loop space, with F_0=F_p in degree zero. It is preserved by Steenrod operations. Its associated-graded term of length s is a subquotient of the reduced bar term of length s. The filtration and Steenrod compatibility are standard; an explicit formulation appears in Menichi's proof of Theorem A, page 2.

Each bar factor has degree between r+1 and d, and passage to total degree subtracts s. Consequently the length-s associated graded has support between rs and (d-1)s. Therefore F_s has no degree greater than (d-1)s, and all of H^n(ΩX; F_p) lies in F_floor(n/r). These statements use strong convergence and the fact that only finitely many filtration lengths can occur in a fixed degree; they do not assume the spectral sequence collapses.

For a class a of degree n>0, set s=floor(n/r). Since P^t a stays in F_s but has degree n+2t(p-1), it is zero whenever

    2t(p-1) > (d-1)s-n.

Write n=rs+u with 0<=u<r. Then the right side equals ws-u <= w floor(N/r), giving the assertion. Degree zero is killed by instability. □

The quantifiers here are weaker than the target: for every N there is a prime bound depending on X and N. If w>0, the displayed sufficient bound grows with N. Taking N to infinity does not give a uniform prime bound. In the special case w=0 the proposition does give vanishing in all degrees for every odd p.

## 6. A loop-space example separating cup powers from reduced powers

**Proposition 6.1.** For every odd prime p there is a finite 3-connected complex X_p of dimension 2p+2 for which all positive-degree pth cup powers in H^*(ΩX_p; F_p) are zero, but P^1 on H^3(ΩX_p; F_p) is nonzero.

**Proof.** Set X_p=Σ^2 CP^p and Y_p=ΣCP^p. The connectivity and dimension are as stated. Because 2p+2<=3p, Anick's theorem applies with r=3 and gives the cup-power vanishing.

Let x generate H^2(CP^p; F_p). Stability and the top-power axiom give

    P^1(σx) = σ(P^1x) = σ(x^p) != 0

in H^{2p+1}(Y_p; F_p). The James unit j:Y_p->ΩΣY_p=ΩX_p induces a homology injection, hence j^*:H^3(ΩX_p; F_p)->H^3(Y_p; F_p) is surjective. Lift σx to a. Then j^*(P^1a)=σ(x^p), so P^1a is nonzero. □

This example lies inside Anick's connectivity-dimension range, so upgrading his conclusion to all reduced powers would be false even in that range. The space varies with p; it does not contradict Hovey's fixed-X assertion. For a fixed member of this family, Corollary 3.2 supplies vanishing at larger primes.

## 7. What is still missing

The finite number of cells of X does not bound the degrees of all algebra generators of H_*(ΩX; F_p). A finite Adams–Hilton chain model is not a proof of bounded generation of its homology algebra. The moment-angle generator theorem has a special algebraic input and cannot be asserted for arbitrary X.

Nor does a trivial action on an Eilenberg–Moore associated graded imply a trivial action on the abutment: a filtration-preserving map may lower filtration and still be nonzero. When w>0, the support inequalities allow arbitrarily long filtration. Neither argument removes the dependency on loop degree.

Finally, it is false that looping an arbitrary finite simply connected complex eliminates integral p-torsion at all sufficiently large primes. Anick constructed a four-dimensional finite simply connected complex whose loop homology has torsion of every order. This blocks a blanket torsion-free rational-decomposition argument; it is not a counterexample about reduced powers. [Anick 1986](https://msp.org/pjm/1986/123-2/pjm-v123-n2-p01-s.pdf)

The precise remaining task is still to prove or disprove the existence of p0(X) for every fixed finite simply connected X, uniformly over all input degrees and all positive reduced powers. The arguments above are classical consequences and explicit diagnostic examples, with no assertion of new priority.
