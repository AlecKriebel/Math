## 2. Approach 1: hyperfocal descent and nilpotent targets

**Lemma 1.1: accepted.** For each subgroup, the induced automorphism-group homomorphism sends prime-to-p elements to prime-to-p elements or identity. It therefore sends the subgroup generated by such elements into the corresponding subgroup. Applying the Sylow homomorphism to hyperfocal generators gives the required inclusion. This argument handles noninjective homomorphisms.

**Proposition 1.2: accepted.** The natural Sylow map is surjective on fundamental groups with hyperfocal kernel. A homomorphism into a finite p-group factors through that quotient precisely under the displayed kernel condition. Maps from a connected CW homotopy type into a K(T,1) are classified by fundamental-group homomorphisms modulo conjugacy. This proves both existence and the stated unbased uniqueness; it does not claim a unique actual map or a preferred homotopy.

For a nilpotent target, all its local automorphism groups are p-groups, so its hyperfocal subgroup is trivial. The hyperfocal lemma gives the required kernel condition. Identification of the target with its Sylow classifying space is used compatibly with the Sylow map.

The cohomological calculation is valid: restriction and pullback commute through the fusion-preservation square. Pullback preserves products and Steenrod operations. The stable-elements theorem makes the resulting cohomology map necessary for an extension, but the packet correctly refrains from treating it as sufficient to realize a space-level map.

## 3. Approach 2: coprime homotopy fixed points

### Lemma 2.1

**Accepted.** There is no missing assumption that the action fixes a basepoint or that the fundamental group acts trivially on higher homotopy.

1. For the homotopy-orbit fibration, connectedness of the fiber and asphericity of BH give an extension of H by the finite p-group pi_1(Y). Its middle group is finite.
2. The coprime extension splits. In the special case actually needed, this can also be checked without invoking the full Schur–Zassenhaus theorem: induct on the order of the normal p-group N, take the characteristic nontrivial elementary abelian subgroup Omega_1 Z(N), split the quotient extension by induction, and split the resulting abelian extension using vanishing of H^2(H;Omega_1 Z(N)). Multiplication by |H| is invertible on that coefficient group.
3. A chosen splitting gives the first relative Postnikov section and the applicable H-actions on all higher homotopy modules. Positive-degree group cohomology vanishes for every such action, since averaging is available when |H| is invertible. Triviality of the action is unnecessary.
4. At the n-th higher stage the obstruction is in H^{n+1}(H;pi_n(Y)), for n at least 2. These groups vanish.
5. Use a fibrant relative Postnikov tower and choose compatible lifts, rather than merely unrelated sections of individual truncations. Connected spaces have convergent Postnikov towers; the same fiberwise argument over BH identifies the original fibration with the homotopy limit. A compatible section therefore gives a section of the original fibration. Infinite dimension of BH does not invalidate this argument.

### Proposition 2.2

**Accepted.** The substantive hypotheses are correctly matched.

- Fusion preservation keeps the component of theta' Bphi invariant under strict precomposition by H.
- The centralizer mapping-space theorem permits a noninjective homomorphism. Its fully centralized-image hypothesis is met by replacing the image by a fusion-conjugate; the resulting map stays in the same mapping component.
- That component has the homotopy type of a p-local finite-group classifying space. Its fundamental group is a finite p-group. The simply connected cover described in BLO Proposition 1.12 is p-complete. Its higher homotopy groups are uniquely divisible by integers prime to p, as required by Lemma 2.1. No assertion that the image is centric is used here.
- An equivariant map EH to the mapping component evaluates to a map from the homotopy orbit of BS. Its restriction has the prescribed homotopy class. The homotopy orbit is B(S semidirect H), and completion preserves the restriction relation by naturality.
- Identification with the initially chosen linking-system classifying space requires the established finite-group comparison and compatible uniqueness of linking systems. This is an explicit external dependency, not a newly proved arbitrary-homomorphism lifting theorem.

For the automorphism-controlled reformulation, saturation makes Inn(S) a normal Sylow p-subgroup of Aut_F(S). A complement H gives exactly the displayed normal-Sylow model when all fusion morphisms extend. For abelian S, all subgroups are fully centralized, and the saturation extension subgroup for any morphism into S is all of S. Its extension is an injective endomorphism of finite S and hence an automorphism.

The elementary centric-image cross-check is also valid: in the abelian case the only source centric object is S; the pullback automorphism-group extension has abelian p-kernel T and coprime quotient H; splitting and axiom (C) give the displayed semidirect-product homomorphism. ## 5. Approach 4: centric-image criterion

**Proposition 4.1: accepted. The coefficient system and obstruction degree are correct.** The following supplies the naturality detail behind the concise written proof; it is explanatory, not a correction.

Let alpha:P to Q be a source morphism, T=phi(P), U=phi(Q), and beta=Phi(alpha):T to U. All of T, U, and beta(T) are target-centric. If z belongs to Z(U), it centralizes beta(T), so target-centricity implies

    z in C_{S'}(beta(T)) = Z(beta(T)).

Thus beta^{-1}(z) is defined and belongs to Z(T). This proves the coefficient map is well defined. Replacing alpha by an inner postcomposition in Q replaces beta by the corresponding inner postcomposition in U; it fixes z, so the coefficient map is independent of representatives. Composition gives contravariant functoriality.

For identification with mapping-space pi_1, use the multiplication map from BZ(T) times BP supplied by BLO Theorem 4.4(c). Choose a linking-system lift lambda of beta. It gives the path comparing the two objectwise maps. Axiom (C) gives

    lambda delta_T(beta^{-1}(z)) = delta_U(z) lambda.

Consequently precomposition by alpha, together with that path, takes z to beta^{-1}(z) on fundamental groups. A different connecting path changes transport by an inner automorphism of Z(T), which is trivial because that group is abelian. The strict homotopy-colimit diagram therefore has the claimed coefficient system Z_phi, canonically up to the harmless identifications just described.

For a strict diagram D over the source centric orbit category, let M(P) be the desired component of Map(D(P),X'). Compatibility makes M a contravariant diagram of connected 1-types. Selecting object points and arrow paths leaves, for a composable pair, a loop in M(P). Its class is a degree-2 category cochain. Associativity yields the degree-3 cocycle equation; changing arrow paths changes the defect by the coboundary of a degree-1 cochain. Thus vanishing of lim^2 Z_phi removes this defect. Extensions across higher simplices have obstructions in pi_{n-1} M(P), for n at least 3, and these vanish. Completion then gives the desired map on X with its Sylow restriction, since S is among the centric objects.

The degree is **2**, not the degree-3 existence obstruction for constructing a linking system from nothing. A source linking system and its strict diagram already exist. Nor can Z_phi be replaced by Z_F without justification. The center-functor vanishing theorem verified in Oliver is a theorem about Z_F. Pullback along a general phi changes the coefficient functor.

The two warnings are valid: inclusion of a proper C_p factor in C_p times C_p is an existing classifying-space map whose image is noncentric, and the carry cocycle represents a nonzero degree-2 class even though its coefficients are abelian. Neither is an actual fusion-system counterexample. The latter was independently checked with unnormalized cochains, not the author's normalized matrices.

## 6. Approach 5: diagonal wreath retraction

**Proposition 5.1: accepted.** A purported left inverse after completion gives, by precomposition with completion and passage to pi_1, a homomorphism from the finite wreath product to C_p splitting the diagonal. Conjugacy of the coordinate factors forces the same scalar on all of them. The diagonal therefore has scalar n times that common scalar, which is zero when p divides n. Because C_p is abelian, the unbased homotopy relation still gives the exact required identity on C_p.

The value on the symmetric-group factor cannot alter this conclusion. For n prime to p, the invariant sum divided by n is indeed a homomorphism giving a retraction. The packet handles n=1 and m=0 separately from the claimed failure for n at least 2 divisible by p.

This rules out a general postcomposition-retraction strategy, not the original extension. The statement remains compatible with the identity map of BC_p existing before wreathing. No unstable conclusion is imported from the stable spectrum theorem.
