# Primary sources and bounded literature review

Checked 7 October 2026. No general solution is established by this source pass; this is not certification that the literature contains none. Publication/crawl dates in search results were not treated as mathematical publication dates.

## Exact question

**Assaf Libman, joint with Natàlia Castellana, “Wreath products and representations of p-local finite groups,” in Topologie, Oberwolfach Report 43/2008**, printed pp. 2466–2468, especially Definition 6 and Theorem 7 on p. 2468. [Report DOI](https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2008/43). [Official PDF](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/3087/OWR_2008_43.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).

The report asks the unstable extension question after defining subgroup-level fusion preservation. Its adjacent theorem supplies an extension only after diagonal passage to a wreath-product target with p^m factors. The question does not assume injectivity or preservation of centric objects. The PDF was obtained and the target section inspected. The corpus labels the report Topologie (2009), but the report identifies itself as 43/2008.

## Foundational results used

**[BLO] Carles Broto, Ran Levi, Bob Oliver, “The homotopy theory of fusion systems,” Journal of the American Mathematical Society 16 (2003), 779–856.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-03-00434-X). [Full PDF](https://web.mat.bham.ac.uk/C.W.Parker/Fusion/BLO.pdf).

Inspected: Definitions 1.1–1.8; Proposition 1.12 (fundamental group and p-goodness); Proposition 2.2 (centric homotopy-colimit model); Theorem 4.4(c) and Corollary 4.5 (mapping components and classification); Theorem 5.8 (stable elements); Theorem 6.3 (centralizer mapping-space theorem); the obstruction discussion in Section 8. Theorem 6.3 requires a fully centralized image, achieved by F-conjugating a given image before invoking it. No claim that arbitrary images are centric is made.

**[BCGLO] Carles Broto, Natàlia Castellana, Jesper Grodal, Ran Levi, Bob Oliver, “Extensions of p-local finite groups,” Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 359 (2007), 3791–3858.** [Full author-hosted PDF](https://web.math.ku.dk/~jg/papers/extensions.of.p-local.finite.groups.pdf).

Theorem B, proved as Theorem 2.5, is the hyperfocal subgroup theorem, including compatibility with the canonical map from S. It supplies the external pi_1 identification used in Approach 1.

**Kári Ragnarsson, “Classifying spectra of saturated fusion systems,” Algebraic & Geometric Topology 6 (2006), 195–252.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2006.6.195). [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502092).

Definition 7.4 and Theorem 7.9 give fusion-preserving morphisms and functorial **classifying spectra**. This is credited prior work, not an unstable extension theorem. No desuspension follows just from this statement.

**Andrew Chermak, “Fusion systems and localities,” Acta Mathematica 211 (2013), 47–139; Bob Oliver, “Existence and uniqueness of linking systems: Chermak's proof via obstruction theory”; George Glauberman and Justin Lynd, later classification-free proof.** Oliver's Theorem A and introductory discussion were inspected in the arXiv full text. Their established uniqueness/lifting consequence is used for fusion isomorphisms only. This pass did not re-prove or independently inspect every proof of these deep theorems. Their current accepted role and distinction from arbitrary homomorphism functoriality are also stated in the inspected Henke paper below. Direct citation information: [Chermak DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11511-013-0099-5), [Oliver arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6479). The specific compatible linking-system uniqueness formulation is the external dependency.

## Related later work and a withdrawal trap

**Ellen Henke, “Extensions of homomorphisms between localities,” Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 9 (2021), e63.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2021.57). [Inspected arXiv v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12626).

Inspected the introduction, Theorems A.1/A.2/B/C, and Lemma 3.1. The main automorphism comparisons concern two localities/linking systems associated to the same saturated fusion system. The extension lemma begins with an existing locality homomorphism and additional local data. These statements were not read as producing a lift of every homomorphism of underlying Sylow groups. The paper records the established general linking-system existence/uniqueness theorem and the later removal of CFSG dependence.

**Lluís Puig, “Existence, uniqueness and functoriality of the perfect locality over a Frobenius P-category,” arXiv:1207.0066v6 (2015), published Algebra Colloquium 23 (2016), 541–622.** [Record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0066), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0066).

The inspected Introduction 1.11–1.12 restricts the stated functorial conclusion to quotient perfect localities. This is not cited as the target unstable theorem. Puig subsequently published a correction to partial-perfect-locality uniqueness: [arXiv:1706.04349](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04349). The correction adds an extendability condition in that partial setting. Its abstract and record were inspected; this packet makes no independent global verification claim for the corrected theory.

**Lluís Puig, “Note on the universality and the functoriality of the perfect F-locality,” arXiv:1605.06657.** [Current primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06657).

The v1 abstract advertises stronger functoriality. The current record says the paper was withdrawn on 6 March 2020 and gives the reason “A fatal error in the proof.” The current PDF endpoint returned 404 because the current version is withdrawn. This paper is **not** a theorem dependency or a credited resolution. The separate 2020 paper [arXiv:2003.03567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03567) advertises a construction of perfect selfcentralizing localities; its abstract alone does not settle the extension question.

## Known wreath result and access limitation

**Natàlia Castellana and Assaf Libman, “Wreath products and representations of p-local finite groups,” Advances in Mathematics 221 (2009), 1302–1344.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2009.02.011), [institutional record](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/wreath-products-and-representations-of-p-local-finite-groups/).

The journal and institutional metadata were checked. The institutional accepted-manuscript PDF link was discovered but returned a Cloudflare challenge/403; it was not read. We do not claim full inspection of the 2009 article. The exact wreath-product extension used in the discussion is already stated in the inspected original 2008 report. The book “Fusion Systems in Algebra and Topology,” by Aschbacher–Kessar–Oliver, also describes that restricted conclusion on printed p. 222; this page was read through the [author-hosted PDF](https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~bobol/ako.pdf) in the web reader. A separate local download was incomplete and is not treated as a verified local PDF.

## Additional 2025–2026 scope checks

**George Glauberman and Justin Lynd, “Cohomology on the centric orbit category of a fusion system,” Fundamenta Mathematicae 269 (2025), 187–199.** [Publisher record](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/fundamenta-mathematicae/all/269/2/115877/cohomology-on-the-centric-orbit-category-of-a-fusion-system), [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05361). Theorem 1.1 and the introduction were inspected. Their vanishing theorem is for H^j(−;F_p), j≤p−2; this is not the arbitrary pulled-back center functor Z_phi of Approach 4.

**Marco Praderio Bova, “Computing higher limits over the fusion orbit category via amalgams” (2026)** and **“An inductive approach to the Diaz-Park sharpness conjecture” (2026)**: [January preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14983), [April preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21161). The current primary records and abstracts were inspected, not their full proofs. They advertise cohomological sharpness results for specified classes, and are not used as a general unstable-extension theorem or as a vanishing theorem for Z_phi. No claim that these papers are irrelevant in every possible way is made.
