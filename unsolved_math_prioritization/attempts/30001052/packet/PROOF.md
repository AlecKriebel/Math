# The unstable fusion-preserving extension question: five approaches

Problem 30001052 / OWR-2089-013, rank 966. Author research record, 7 October 2026.

**Disposition: unresolved after five substantive approaches.** This note proves special cases and limitations of proposed methods. It gives neither a general extension nor a counterexample to the original question. None of the deductions is claimed novel. The foundational results listed in SOURCES.md remain external dependencies. Computer controls are small exact checks, not a formalization of the homotopy theory.

## 0. Exact target and conventions

Fix a prime p. A p-local finite group is (S,F,L), with S a finite p-group, F a **saturated** fusion system on S, and L an associated **centric** linking system. Write X=|L|^wedge_p and theta:BS→X for the canonical map. Use primes for a second triple. The requested conclusion is a map f:X→X' satisfying

    f theta ≃ theta' Bphi.

Here phi:S→S' is a group homomorphism, not necessarily injective, satisfying the report's Definition 6: for every alpha:P→Q in F there is alpha':phi(P)→phi(Q) in F' with alpha' phi|P=phi|Q alpha. This is subgroup-level fusion preservation, not merely preservation of conjugacy classes of individual elements. Homotopies and maps are unbased unless expressly stated; basepoints can be chosen when discussing fundamental groups.

The induced alpha' is unique because phi:P→phi(P) is onto. Denote it Phi(alpha). Uniqueness proves compatibility with identities and composition, so Phi is a functor F→F'. Moreover K=ker(phi) is strongly F-closed: if x∈K∩P and alpha:P→S is a fusion morphism then phi(alpha(x))=Phi(alpha)(1)=1. These observations do **not** construct a linking-system functor.

The report's Theorem 7 changes the target to a wreath-product classifying space. Ragnarsson's theorem gives a stable map. Neither assertion supplies the map f above. The precise question and this distinction are present on printed page 2468 of the original report.

## 1. First approach: detect an obstruction on the first Postnikov stage

The first attempted obstruction was failure of phi to descend to fundamental groups. In fact it always descends, and the argument gives a complete affirmative answer for nilpotent targets.

For a saturated system F, write

    hyp(F)=<x^{-1} alpha(x): x∈P≤S, alpha∈O^p(Aut_F(P))>.

For a finite group A, O^p(A) is the subgroup generated by elements of order prime to p. The hyperfocal theorem [BCGLO, Theorem B/2.5] identifies pi_1(X) with S/hyp(F), compatibly with theta.

### Lemma 1.1. Hyperfocal naturality

For every phi as above,

    phi(hyp(F)) ≤ hyp(F').

**Proof.** For each P, the map alpha↦Phi(alpha) is a group homomorphism Aut_F(P)→Aut_F'(phi(P)). The image of an element of order prime to p again has order prime to p (or is the identity). Therefore this homomorphism takes O^p(Aut_F(P)) into O^p(Aut_F'(phi(P))). Apply phi to each displayed generator of hyp(F). The resulting element is a displayed generator of hyp(F') or a product of them. This proves the inclusion. ∎

Thus there is always a well-defined homomorphism

    bar(phi): S/hyp(F) → S'/hyp(F').

This eliminates a fundamental-group obstruction without assuming existence of f.

### Proposition 1.2. Nilpotent targets

If F'=F_{S'}(S'), so X'≃BS' compatibly with its Sylow map, then every fusion-preserving phi extends. More generally a homomorphism a:S→T to any finite p-group T extends to a map X→BT if and only if hyp(F)≤ker(a). When an extension exists its unbased homotopy class is uniquely determined by a up to T-conjugacy.

**Proof.** If a extends, its map on pi_1 factors through the quotient S→S/hyp(F), proving necessity. Conversely let bar(a):S/hyp(F)→T be the factor. Compose the first Postnikov map X→B pi_1(X) with B bar(a). Its restriction along theta induces a on pi_1, and maps BS→BT, with BT a K(T,1), are classified by homomorphisms up to conjugacy. Hence the restriction is homotopic to Ba. Maps from any connected CW homotopy type X to BT are likewise classified by homomorphisms pi_1(X)→T up to conjugacy. Surjectivity S→pi_1(X) proves the asserted uniqueness.

Finally hyp(F_{S'}(S'))=1, since each Aut_{S'}(P) is a p-group. Apply Lemma 1.1 to fusion-preserving phi. ∎

### Cohomological continuation of this attempt

Ordinary mod-p cohomology also supplies no obstruction at the level of stable elements. If u∈H*(BS';F_p) is F'-stable, then for alpha:P→Q,

    alpha* res_Q(phi*u)
      = (phi|P)* Phi(alpha)* res_phi(Q)(u)
      = (phi|P)* res_phi(P)(u)
      = res_P(phi*u).

Thus phi* restricts to a ring homomorphism H*(F';F_p)→H*(F;F_p), commuting with Steenrod operations. Under [BLO, Theorem 5.8] these are the cohomology rings of X' and X. If an extension exists its induced map must be this one, since theta* is injective.

**Stopping point for approach 1.** Descent of pi_1 and of the unstable cohomology algebra does not realize a map of spaces. For a non-aspherical target one still needs lifts through all target Postnikov stages, with the requisite action and k-invariant compatibility and the prescribed Sylow restriction. No automatic vanishing of these obstructions has been proved here. This is a reduction, not a general affirmative argument.

## 2. Second approach: construct coherent extensions using a coprime action

A separate gluing attempt succeeds whenever the source fusion is controlled by automorphisms of S. It avoids requiring the image of S to be centric in F'.

### Lemma 2.1. Coprime homotopy-fixed-point existence

Let H be a finite group of order prime to p acting on a connected space Y of CW homotopy type. Suppose pi_1(Y) is a finite p-group and multiplication by |H| is an automorphism of every pi_n(Y), n≥2. Then Y^{hH} is nonempty.

**Proof.** A homotopy fixed point is a section of Y→E H×_H Y→BH. Since BH is aspherical and Y connected, the fundamental-group sequence is

    1→pi_1(Y)→pi_1(E H×_H Y)→H→1.

The middle group is finite and its kernel is a normal Hall p-subgroup. Schur–Zassenhaus provides a splitting. Hence there is a section at the relative first Postnikov stage.

Build a section successively up the relative Postnikov tower. Once a section through stage n−1 has been chosen, its obstruction at stage n (n≥2) lies in H^{n+1}(H;pi_n(Y)), with the H-action specified by the chosen fundamental-group splitting. These groups vanish: group cohomology in positive degrees is annihilated by |H|, whereas multiplication by |H| is invertible on the coefficient module and consequently on its cochain complex and cohomology. Thus compatible sections can be chosen at all stages. Use the tower as a tower of fibrations; lifting a section includes the needed compatibility, and the inverse limit/homotopy limit of the compatible sections is a section of the original fibration. The relative Postnikov tower converges because its fibers are the convergent Postnikov tower of the connected fiber Y. ∎

### Proposition 2.2. Source with a normal Sylow model

Suppose F=F_S(S⋊H), where H≤Aut(S) has order prime to p. Then every fusion-preserving phi:S→S' extends to X→X'. Consequently the answer is affirmative whenever every F-morphism extends to an element of Aut_F(S). In particular it is affirmative for every source with **abelian S**.

**Proof.** Let h=theta' Bphi:BS→X'. Let Y be its component in Map(BS,X'). The strict action of H on BS gives a strict action on the mapping space by precomposition (use h^{-1} in the left-action convention). Fusion preservation and [BLO, Corollary 4.5] imply that this action preserves Y.

By [BLO, Theorem 6.3], after replacing the image phi(S) by a fully centralized F'-conjugate if needed, Y is homotopy equivalent to the classifying space of its centralizer p-local finite group. In particular pi_1(Y) is a finite p-group. Its simply connected covering space is p-complete, as in the proof of [BLO, Proposition 1.12]; hence multiplication by any integer prime to p is an isomorphism on its higher homotopy groups. The same holds for pi_n(Y), n≥2. Lemma 2.1 applies.

An H-equivariant map E H→Y, followed by evaluation, gives a map

    E H×_H BS ≃ B(S⋊H) → X'

whose restriction to BS is in the component h. Complete this map at p. Since BS and X' are p-complete, naturality of completion gives the desired restriction relation on B(S⋊H)^wedge_p. The finite-group classifying-space theorem and uniqueness of the associated centric linking system identify this source with X over BS. These last identification statements are established results, not re-proved here.

For the second assertion, saturation implies Inn(S) is a normal Sylow p-subgroup of Aut_F(S). Choose a p'-complement H by Schur–Zassenhaus. If all F-morphisms extend to Aut_F(S), then F=F_S(S⋊H); no claim about arbitrary constrained systems is made.

If S is abelian, every subgroup is fully centralized in F. For alpha:P→S, the saturation extension subgroup N_alpha is all S, since all conjugations by S are trivial. The extension axiom therefore extends alpha to an injective endomorphism of finite S, which is an automorphism. This verifies the hypothesis. ∎

### Algebraic cross-check when the image is centric

If S is abelian and T=phi(S) is F'-centric, one can replace the homotopy-fixed-point proof by an elementary extension-of-groups construction. The source linking system has just one object S and automorphism group S⋊H. Pull back

    1→Z(T)=T→Aut_L'(T)→Aut_F'(T)→1

along the homomorphism H→Aut_F'(T) induced by phi. Its kernel is the p-group T and its quotient is H, so it splits. A complement gives lifts u_h, and (s,h)↦delta_T(phi(s))u_h is a homomorphism S⋊H→Aut_L'(T), by linking-system axiom (C). Its nerve, included in |L'| and completed, is the required map. This agrees in scope with the centric special case of Proposition 2.2.

**Stopping point for approach 2.** For a general saturated system, fusion need not be generated by automorphisms of S. Extra automorphisms of proper essential subgroups cannot be handled as an action of one p'-group on Map(BS,X'). The averaging argument above has not been extended to their full orbit-category coherence problem. In particular “constrained source” is not substituted for the displayed normal-Sylow hypothesis.

## 3. Third approach: search for a small nontrivial finite-group counterexample

We examined a genuinely non-abelian Sylow group S=D8, with r^4=s^2=1 and srs=r^{-1}. The three systems are those induced by D8, Sigma_4 and A_6. We identify S inside Sigma_4 by r=(1 2 3 4), s=(1 3), and embed Sigma_4 in A_6 by multiplying odd permutations by (5 6). This fixes compatible Sylow inclusions. All groups here are finite, so their fusion systems are saturated.

The exact calculation in check_fusion.py enumerates all ten subgroups of D8, all conjugations from those subgroups into D8 in each ambient group, and all 36 endomorphisms of D8. Testing maps P→S suffices: a fusion map P→Q is the corresponding map P→S with restricted codomain, and a target conjugator realizing its equation automatically has image in phi(Q).

For each ordered pair (source,target), the numbers of fusion-preserving homomorphisms are:

    source D8:      36 to D8, 36 to Sigma_4, 36 to A_6
    source Sigma_4:  6 to D8, 10 to Sigma_4, 14 to A_6
    source A_6:      1 to D8,  1 to Sigma_4,  9 to A_6.

These are counts of homomorphisms, not conjugacy classes of maps. Every one extends, for the following reasons.

1. A D8 source has nilpotent fusion, so X≃BD8 and theta' Bphi itself is an extension.
2. Constant homomorphisms extend by constant maps.
3. The five nonconstant noninjective homomorphisms from Sigma_4 fusion factor through the sign quotient D8/V→C2, where V={1,r^2,rs,r^3s} is its normal Klein four subgroup. Compose B(Sigma_4)^wedge_2→BC2, induced by sign, with the map BC2→X' determined by the chosen involution. This realizes exactly these five choices in each target.
4. The four injective maps from Sigma_4 fusion to itself are fusion isomorphisms. All eight D8 automorphisms preserve A_6 fusion, so the eight injective maps from A_6 fusion to itself are also fusion isomorphisms. The established lifting theorem for fusion isomorphisms gives equivalences of p-local finite-group classifying spaces with their specified Sylow maps.
5. The eight injective maps from Sigma_4 fusion to A_6 fusion are the fixed parity-twist group inclusion followed on S by one of those eight target fusion automorphisms. Compose the map of finite-group classifying spaces with the equivalence from item 4.

For completeness, lifting a fusion isomorphism here follows from the existing uniqueness theorem: transport the target centric linking system back along the fusion isomorphism, use uniqueness of the source centric linking system in the sense compatible with the distinguished transporter maps, and take nerves and p-completion. This is an invocation of the Chermak/Oliver linking-system theorem (with the later Glauberman–Lynd CFSG-free proof available), not an asserted functoriality theorem for arbitrary homomorphisms.

### Why the noninjective cases collapse

Every nontrivial normal subgroup of D8 contains z=r^2. A kernel of a fusion-preserving homomorphism is strongly closed. In Sigma_4 fusion, z is fused with the other two nonidentity elements of V; hence a nontrivial kernel contains V. The resulting image has order at most two. In A_6 all five involutions of D8 are fused, and they generate D8. A nontrivial kernel is therefore all D8. The exact checker confirms these strongly closed subgroup inventories, and also computes hyp(D8 fusion)=1, hyp(Sigma_4 fusion)=V, and hyp(A_6 fusion)=D8.

The injective cases, including exclusion of A_6→Sigma_4, are verified against the complete morphism tables, not just element conjugacy. Every retained homomorphism is present explicitly in FUSION_CHECKS.json and is assigned one of the mechanisms above. For example A_6→Sigma_4 cannot preserve fusion: the former fuses all involutions, while the latter does not.

**Stopping point for approach 3.** These nine pairs give no counterexample. We did not classify every saturated fusion system on D8, and the calculation cannot exclude larger Sylow groups, other targets, or exotic systems. This is a bounded complete calculation on named systems, not an exhaustive search for the original problem.

## 4. Fourth approach: lift locally and remove the composition defect

The next constructive attempt was to choose lifts on the source centric orbit category and kill their failure of coherence. It gives a precise sufficient condition but no proof that it always holds.

Assume for this subsection that **phi(P) is F'-centric for every F-centric P**. Then Phi induces a functor

    bar(Phi): O^c(F) → O^c(F').

Indeed composition with an inner automorphism of Q becomes composition with the corresponding inner automorphism of phi(Q). Define the abelian coefficient functor

    Z_phi(P)=Z(phi(P)),

and, for alpha:P→Q, define its contravariant map by applying Phi(alpha)^{-1} to Z(phi(Q))⊆Phi(alpha)(phi(P)). This inclusion holds because Phi(alpha)(phi(P)) is F'-centric: an element centralizing phi(Q) centralizes this subgroup and hence belongs to its center. The resulting map is independent of the orbit-category representative. Equivalently Z_phi=bar(Phi)^*(Z_F').

### Proposition 4.1. A conditional centric-image criterion

Under the preceding centric-image hypothesis, if

    lim^2_{O^c(F)} Z_phi = 0,

then phi extends.

**Proof.** Use the strict homotopy-colimit model D:O^c(F)→Top from [BLO, Proposition 2.2], with D(P)≃BP and hocolim D≃|L|. The desired objectwise maps h_P:BP→X' are theta' B(phi|P). Fusion preservation makes their homotopy classes compatible with every morphism. The mapping-space component at h_P is BZ(phi(P)) by [BLO, Theorem 4.4(c)]. In particular it is a connected 1-type with abelian fundamental group and no higher homotopy.

Choose representative maps on objects and paths of maps along each arrow, with identities normalized. For a composable pair of arrows, traversing the chosen paths in the two possible ways gives a loop in the mapping component at the source, hence an element of Z_phi(P). The associativity comparison for a composable triple is the category-cochain cocycle equation. Thus the failure to fill the two-dimensional simplices is a 2-cocycle with coefficients Z_phi. Changing the arrow paths by loops changes that cocycle by a coboundary. The assumed vanishing allows a choice with zero defect, hence extensions over all two-dimensional simplices. All higher obstructions lie in pi_n of the mapping components for n≥2 and vanish. The resulting coherent family is a map hocolim D→X' with the required restrictions. Complete it at p as in Proposition 2.2. ∎

This argument specifies the obstruction degree and coefficient system. It does **not** identify Z_phi with the ordinary source center functor Z_F. For an isomorphism of fusion systems they identify; for an arbitrary phi the values are Z(phi(P)), not Z(P). The standard linking-system uniqueness vanishing theorem for Z_F therefore cannot simply be applied with a changed coefficient functor.

### Two concrete tests of invalid shortcuts

First, even an actual injective group homomorphism need not preserve centric objects: include C_p as a proper factor of C_p×C_p with both fusion systems nilpotent. The only source centric object is C_p; its image has centralizer the whole target and is not centric. The map of classifying spaces certainly exists. Thus failure of this subsection's hypothesis is a failure of this method, not a negative answer to the original question.

Second, the fact that all mapping components are abelian 1-types does not by itself make the 2-cocycle vanish. As a small model, for the one-object category C_p with trivial coefficient F_p, the normalized cocycle

    c(i,j)=floor((i+j)/p) mod p,   0≤i,j<p,

is not a coboundary. It is the carry cocycle for C_{p^2}→C_p. Direct calculation gives delta c=0. If c=delta b with b(0)=0, summing c(i,1)=b(1)−b(i+1)+b(i) over i∈C_p would give 1=p b(1)=0 in F_p, a contradiction. The controls check p=2,3,5,7. This toy category is **not** claimed to be the centric orbit category of a counterexample; it only demonstrates the extra vanishing assertion that a general argument must justify.

**Stopping point for approach 4.** We have neither proved centric-image coverage nor proved vanishing of lim^2 Z_phi for arbitrary phi, and no nonzero obstruction for an actual pair of saturated systems has been constructed. For noncentric images the mapping components are generally full centralizer classifying spaces, with possible nontrivial higher homotopy, so reducing every case to this single center-valued 2-cocycle would itself be invalid.

## 5. Fifth approach: destabilize the wreath-product extension

The known wreath-product theorem provides an extension after composing the target Sylow map with the diagonal inclusion into a target with n=p^m copies. A tempting final step is a retraction from that new target to the old one. Such a retraction is unavailable even in the easiest nontrivial case.

### Proposition 5.1. A diagonal wreath inclusion need not split

Let n≥2 and p|n. Set W=(C_p)^n⋊Sigma_n, where Sigma_n permutes the coordinates, and let d:C_p→W be the diagonal inclusion into the base subgroup. The map

    Bd:BC_p→BW^wedge_p

has no homotopy left inverse.

**Proof.** If r:BW^wedge_p→BC_p were a left inverse, composing r with the completion map BW→BW^wedge_p would give a map BW→BC_p whose fundamental-group homomorphism a:W→C_p satisfies a d=id. Since the target is abelian, the restrictions of a to the n coordinate C_p factors are equal, as those factors are conjugate in W. Write that common homomorphism as multiplication by t∈F_p. The diagonal element x is sent to nt x=0, contradicting a d=id. This argument works irrespective of the value of a on Sigma_n, because d lies in the base subgroup. ∎

This is an obstruction to a universal **postcomposition retraction**, not to the original extension. For instance phi=id_{C_p} has the identity extension before wreathing. Nor does Proposition 5.1 rule out some specially constructed map X→X' which never factors through the wreath target.

The arithmetic is essential. If p does not divide n, the homomorphism (x_1,...,x_n;sigma)↦n^{-1}sum_i x_i to C_p is Sigma_n-invariant and is a genuine retraction on the diagonal. At n=p^m with m>0, division by n is impossible in F_p. The exact controls enumerate invariant linear forms for p=2,3,5 and n=p, and check the prime-to-p control n=p−1 (including n=1 for p=2).

The stable splitting of classifying spectra does not change this conclusion. A stable map in a p-completed Burnside module is not automatically a map of spaces. The available Ragnarsson result is credited as a stable theorem only; no desuspension criterion is proved here.

**Stopping point for approach 5.** The existing wreath theorem cannot be converted into a general unstable answer by assuming the diagonal admits a retraction. A different destabilization construction or a realized nonzero obstruction would be needed.

## 6. Final scope

The five approaches above are mathematical attempts, not five sources, five audits, or five packaging operations. They establish:

- universal hyperfocal descent and the affirmative nilpotent-target case;
- affirmative normal-Sylow/coprime-action sources, including abelian Sylow groups;
- complete affirmative checks for all nine named D8/Sigma_4/A_6 fusion-system pairs;
- a sufficient centric-image/lim^2-vanishing criterion with its unproved hypotheses exposed;
- nonexistence of a general diagonal wreath retraction, even for C_p.

They do not resolve the unrestricted question. The current-source pass is bounded and does not certify that no solution exists anywhere in the literature. In particular, an enticing stronger functoriality preprint was withdrawn for a fatal proof error; its old abstract is not used as a theorem. This packet is authored, source-free, unrefereed research documentation with extensive AI assistance. Independent mathematical review is pending at this freeze.
