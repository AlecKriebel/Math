## Route 1: order extremality

For gamma1 <= gamma2, the domain of the latter infimum is contained in that of the former; hence E is nondecreasing. The index zeta=gamma proves E <= r, including extended-real values. Every nondecreasing h <= r obeys h(gamma) <= h(zeta) <= r(zeta) for every zeta >= gamma; taking an infimum proves h <= E. This establishes the greatest minorant, not the least possible risk of a predictor.

Idempotence follows because E itself is a nondecreasing minorant. Order preservation follows by taking infima of pointwise ordered functions. If two finite profiles differ by at most delta, their tail infima differ by at most delta. The packet correctly uses infimum: without lower semicontinuity even adjoining infinity does not ensure attainment. For instance r(zeta)=1+1/zeta at finite zeta and r(infinity)=2 has unattained tail infimum 1. Thus no hidden minimum or minimizer is needed for Proposition 1.

## Route 2: selection quantifiers

Use a training sigma-field F_n that includes all candidate-building randomness, and a larger sigma-field G_n generated by training, independent validation, and any independent selector randomization. The fresh test pair must be independent of G_n. The selected index is G_n-measurable and the returned predictor is exactly one F_n-measurable candidate. Then its conditional risk given G_n equals Q_(n,J_n). This is the precise reading of the packet's selector and validation statements.

Pathwise, every candidate has Q_j >= d_j - Delta >= d_n - Delta. This simultaneously covers every allowed selector, without a union bound over selection rules. If d_n -> E and Delta -> 0 in probability, the probability of risk less than E-epsilon tends to zero. For empirical minimization, including arbitrary ties,

Q_selected <= min_j Q_j + 2e <= d_n + Delta + 2e.

The lower and upper bounds prove convergence in probability to E. The proof does not require candidates to be independent. Conditional Hoeffding needs independent validation observations and a uniform bound on their realized losses; 2K exp(-2vt^2/B^2) is correct, and log(2K)/v -> 0 suffices. The independent validation set must be included in the total sample accounting when mapping the theorem to a specific gamma.

The theorem is restrictive by design. Grid approximation d_n -> E is a separate hypothesis. No theorem about a new refit follows. A randomized selection has a convex combination of candidate risks when randomness is integrated out, and obeys the same lower bound. Averaging predictions instead can beat all candidate risks. The exact control with deterministic response zero and predictions -1,+1 distinguishes these operations: random selection has risk 1, averaging has risk 0.

The audit also checks sharpness of the finite bound. Taking d=(1,1+2e), Q=d+Delta, and estimated risks (Q_1+e,Q_2-e) gives a tie and attains the upper endpoint d_n+Delta+2e. Thus the coefficient two is not merely a loose algebraic accident under these assumptions.

## Route 3: pointwise limits versus simultaneous realized control

For each fixed dimension, all observations and the future pair have the stated common distribution. Dimension is used only to supply unused coordinates, so the construction is coherent across p. For constant predictions a=0 and a=1, fair response Y in {-1,1} gives risks 1 and 2, respectively. All candidate losses are at most 4. Squared loss is not globally bounded on arbitrary real arguments; the bound is on the candidates and model here, which suffices. Clipping squared loss at 4 would preserve the entire construction if a globally bounded loss were desired.

The base learner is permutation-invariant because the fractional sum is symmetric. This does not assert that the overall prefix wrapper is permutation-invariant. For S_m=(U_1+...+U_m) modulo one, conditioning on all preceding U_i leaves S_m uniform, since adding a fresh uniform variable modulo one preserves uniform measure. Consequently S_1,...,S_N are jointly independent uniforms. The inverse torus map is U_1=S_1 and U_m=(S_m-S_(m-1)) modulo one, corroborating the argument.

Thus the risk-1 event for fit m has probability 1/m. For every deterministic m,p -> infinity, its probability vanishes. In fact the individual probabilities vanish uniformly for m >= ceil(sqrt(N)); nevertheless the probability of at least one realized exception is

1 - product_(m=a)^N (1-1/m) = 1 - (a-1)/N -> 1.

This distinction is the central quantifier issue: sup_m P(exception_m) -> 0 does not imply P(union_m exception_m) -> 0. The selected constant is observable, so no unknown distribution or risk oracle is used by the stated selector. The direct selector's risk is always 1 or 2 and tends to 1 in probability and expectation.

For validation, loss(1)-loss(0)=1-2Ybar. Counting ties as failures is conservative, and P(Ybar >= 1/2) <= exp(-v/8) is the correct Hoeffding bound for labels of range two. With v=ceil(sqrt(N)), this and the exact no-hit probability imply the stated limit. The p=floor(gamma(N+v)) convention correctly includes validation. Restriction to m in [ceil(N/2),N] leaves a limiting hit probability 1/2, so the lower-bound failure does not require unbounded aspect ratios, although risk then need not have a deterministic limit.

### Discriminating source-grid check

This is a counterexample for the specified dense collection, not for every choice of subsample-size grid. On the manuscript's square-root grid choose total n=k^2, validation k, training n_tr=k^2-k, spacing k, and xi=1,...,k-3. Candidate sizes are 2k,3k,...,(k-2)k. For this same learner,

P(any risk-1 candidate) <= sum_(j=2)^(k-2) 1/(jk) = (H_(k-2)-1)/k -> 0.

The union bound works for arbitrary dependence between random subsamples. Therefore every candidate has risk 2 with probability tending to one on this grid; uniform control is restored. This confirms, rather than challenges, the packet's warning not to infer a contradiction to the formal source result.

## Route 4: full data-conditional Gaussian limit

For a fitted coefficient b and an independent isotropic test covariate, full conditional prediction risk is 1+||b-beta||^2. For p=2m, write A=||P beta||^2, B=||eta||^2, C=beta^T eta, and ||beta||^2=4. Orthogonality of (I-P)beta to the row space gives exactly

Q_t = 5 + (t^2-2t)A + t^2 B + 2t(t-1)C.

No independence between A, B, and C is required to conclude convergence from their individual limits.

Rotational invariance gives A/4 ~ Beta(m/2,m/2), so E[A]=2 and Var(A)=4/(m+1). The noise norm has distribution B=V/Z with independent V~chi-square(m) and Z~chi-square(m+1). This follows by separating the noise radius and direction, rotating the independent Wishart matrix, and applying its diagonal inverse Schur complement. Hence B -> 1 in probability. For m>3 its moments are

E[B]=m/(m-1),
Var(B)=2m(2m-1)/((m-1)^2(m-3)).

Rotational invariance of X^T(XX^T)^(-2)X, followed by the inverse-Wishart mean, yields E[C]=0 and E[C^2]=2/(m-1). Thus C -> 0 in probability. Combining these limits gives

Q_t -> 3+2(t-1)^2+t^2 = 11/3+3(t-2/3)^2.

At t=2/3 the limit is 11/3, and its finite expectation is 11/3+4/(9(m-1)) for m>1. It is below 4 for m>=3. The argument does not infer a conditional limit from expectation alone.

For the unshrunk fit, the same calculation gives r(zeta)=1+4(1-1/zeta)+1/(zeta-1) when zeta>1. In the underparameterized regime the error-noise norm has the analogous chi-square ratio with numerator degrees p and denominator degrees m-p+1, yielding 1/(1-zeta). At zeta=1, the numerator degrees are asymptotic to m and the denominator degrees are o(m), so the noise norm diverges in probability; bounded signal terms cannot cancel it. At zeta=infinity with m->infinity, the projection fraction and noise norm vanish, giving risk 5. This checks the endpoints as well as the interior.

The identity r(zeta)-4=(zeta-2)^2/(zeta(zeta-1)) proves E(1)=4. Its derivative above one is (zeta-2)(3zeta-2)/(zeta^2(zeta-1)^2), and the underparameterized branch reaches 4 at gamma=3/4. These facts verify the packet's complete piecewise envelope. Shrinkage is a new predictor, outside the single-candidate selector class. The fixed comparison at gamma=1 does not by itself claim the shrunk family has a globally monotone risk curve.

## Route 5: profile insufficiency and minimax

The same constant-one learner has risk 2 under both displayed response models. The squared-loss conditional mean is the unique Bayes optimum up to null sets: prediction zero in the fair +/-1 model has risk 1; prediction one in the shifted, variance-two model has risk 2. Features and training cannot lower the latter irreducible variance. This is a valid demonstration that a scalar base-risk curve does not determine statistical optimality.

For the small-noise modification Y=+/-epsilon, the base risk is 1+epsilon^2 and the Bayes risk is epsilon^2. Taking singleton model families, or any explicitly specified families with the same relevant comparison, rules out a universal constant factor inferred solely from the existence of a profile. It does not refute a constant depending on SNR.

The fixed-p minimax monotonicity proof is valid provided the parameter family does not change with sample size and admissible rules can discard observations. For gamma1<gamma2, floor(p/gamma1) >= floor(p/gamma2); taking existing limits preserves the inequality. This establishes monotonicity of a properly defined minimax value, not equality to the envelope of a prescribed base learner.

The packet correctly separates three additional obstructions. Convergence in probability need not imply convergence in mean (n with probability 1/n and zero otherwise is a nonnegative control). Pointwise parameter convergence is not uniform parameter convergence. Finally the displayed two-by-two matrix has max-min 1 and min-max 2. Neither adaptation nor exchange of minimization and maximization is supplied by the scalar profile.
