## 3. This is an explicit move system

The state space is the disjoint union of even tagged braid-word sets, modulo the stated braid relations. The strand tag is necessary and is retained. The empty words on two and four strands have different ordinary closures.

Every displayed scheme is a finite algebraic replacement with specified word parameters and explicit index bounds. Given those parameters, its two endpoints can be written down and checked without deciding link equivalence, solving a braid word problem, or finding a path through odd-strand braids.

In particular:

- BC and T require their preceding blocks to use indices at most \(N-2\).
- BR and BL require unshifted blocks to use indices at most \(N-3\), with \(N\ge4\).
- The shifts in L and BL raise only the block indices. The fixed index-one crossings and the final retained positive crossing stay in their stated places.
- D adds two strands in one replacement. The intermediate strand count is not a state of that replacement.
- All rules are reversible; a reverse rule has the same even endpoints.

These conditions are syntactic on words in an even ambient group. A block fixing an outer strand may of course be identified with a smaller braid in a proof, but such an identification is not a move-applicability oracle or a new allowed state.

The support restrictions are mathematically essential. Without the restriction in T, at \(N=2\) one could use the prefix \(\sigma_1^2\) and change \(\sigma_1^3\) to \(\sigma_1\), incorrectly identifying a trefoil closure with an unknot. The actual \(W_2(0)\) restriction forbids this. Merely checking the number of closure components would not detect that error.

There are four or eight **schemes**, with arbitrary finite word blocks, in the usual sense in which conjugation itself is an algebraic scheme. This is not a finite list of individual edges, nor does the candidate claim that. C and BC may additionally be generated by single-letter conjugations, in the appropriate reverse order for the letters of a conjugator.

## 4. Soundness

| C | Conjugation |

The index bounds guarantee every step invoked in these verifications is legitimate. In particular, T is not an arbitrary crossing change.

The auxiliary odd braids in these soundness arguments are objects in a proof about the prior Markov theorem. They do not become states of the new move relation. The relation itself has already been defined independently by the explicit even-endpoint patterns. Using a known theorem to verify those patterns is not circular.

It follows that any finite chain of the candidate's allowed moves preserves the relevant oriented ordinary closure.

## 5. Completeness

The padding map \(P\) fixes every even tagged state and adds one positive right stabilization to every odd state. It preserves closure and sends every positive strand count to an even one.

The edge-by-edge conversion is exhaustive:

| Unrestricted edge | Source strand parity | Image |
|---|---|---|
| Conjugation | even / odd | C / BC |
| Right stabilization | even / odd | D / T |
| Right virtual exchange | even / odd total count | R / BR |
| Left virtual exchange | even / odd total count | L / BL |
| Braid relation | either | Same relation in the retained prefix |

For stabilization, parity refers to the smaller strand count. For exchange, it refers to the total strand count of the exchange. This distinction is correctly maintained in the candidate.

For an odd exchange count \(m\), the underlying unshifted blocks use indices at most \(m-2\). Padding makes \(N=m+1\), so the bound becomes \(N-3\), exactly as required by BR and BL. Their final positive crossing has index \(m=N-1\). There is no off-by-one error in the left shift.

At the word level, an unrestricted Markov chain can include braid relations to reconcile successive choices of word representatives. Padding an odd-level relation simply retains the terminal positive crossing while the relation acts within the prefix. Hence there is no well-definedness problem caused by different words for the same braid.

Applying \(P\) to a complete unrestricted chain therefore produces a chain of the explicit even schemes. Its original even endpoints are fixed **exactly**, including strand tags. Repeated vertices may be removed. Reversibility covers every destabilization and reverse exchange.

This proves injectivity of the closure map on the even-word quotient. Alexander's theorem followed by one application of \(P\) proves surjectivity. In the classical proof the unrestricted chain uses no virtual letters or exchanges, so the four classical schemes suffice independently of the virtual theorem.

This argument is stronger than defining a relation by the existence of an arbitrary odd-intermediate path: each prior generating edge is converted to one already specified, syntactically testable scheme.

## 6. Boundary and scope checks

The one-strand trivial braid pads to the two-strand word \(\sigma_1\), giving an even representative of the unknot. T at \(N=2\) has an empty preceding block, as required. R and L at \(N=2\) reduce to cancellations. The smallest buffered exchange has \(N=4\), because a genuine odd-level exchange starts at three strands.

The zero-strand empty link can be isolated without adding a move from zero to a positive count. No positive-strand ordinary closure is empty.

If an input Markov certificate has maximal strand count \(M\), the padded certificate has maximal count at most \(2\lceil M/2\rceil\). This follows directly from the monotone rounding map on strand counts. It is a certificate-conversion bound, not an algorithmic bound for finding a certificate.

Neither a uniformly bounded-support geometric move system nor a framed, transverse, or plat theorem follows from this argument. These limitations are explicit in the candidate. The literal source item does not impose those stronger demands.
