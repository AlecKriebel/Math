# A natural realization of quandle cohomology

## Result and attribution

**Target:** UnsolvedMath 10400107 / AMR-103-0107, Ohtsuki Problem 5.11,
proposed by C. Rourke and B. Sanderson. The answer is affirmative.

The correct existing construction is the quandle specialization of the
Ishikawa–Tanaka space in §6.1 of *Quandle colorings vs. biquandle colorings*,
Topology and its Applications **345** (2024), 108832,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.topol.2024.108832).
The inspected [author preprint, arXiv:1912.12917v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12917v2),
pp.17–18, already contains the construction and its CW statement.
This is a verification of a known affirmative answer, not a new solution.

Here we give the quandle specialization, an elementary normal-form verification,
and the full natural **cochain** comparison. In particular, we do not infer a
natural cohomology isomorphism merely from abstract isomorphisms of homology groups.

## 1. Precise scope and conventions

A quandle is a set X with an operation a*b such that a*a=a, each right
translation R_b:a↦a*b is bijective, and (a*b)*c=(a*c)*(b*c).
There is no finiteness or connectedness hypothesis. Give X the discrete topology.
Let A be any abelian group, with constant coefficients throughout.

For n≥0 let C_n^R(X)=Z[X^n], where X^0 has one element. On a tuple x=(x_1,…,x_n)
set

- d_i^0 x=(x_1,…,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},…,x_n);
- d_i^1 x=(x_1*x_i,…,x_{i-1}*x_i,x_{i+1},…,x_n).

Put ∂x=Σ_{i=1}^n (−1)^i(d_i^0 x−d_i^1 x). For n=1 this is zero.
This sign agrees with the standard orientation of [0,1]^n. The cubical face
identities, and hence ∂²=0, follow from right self-distributivity.

Let D_n be the subgroup generated by tuples with x_i=x_{i+1} for some i;
D_0=D_1=0. This is a chain subcomplex. Indeed, for such an equal pair a,a,
faces away from the pair remain degenerate. The two deletion faces at the pair
are equal and occur with opposite signs; so do the two action faces, using a*a=a.
Thus C_n^Q(X)=C_n^R(X)/D_n is a free abelian group on the nondegenerate tuples
ND_n(X)={x∈X^n : x_i≠x_{i+1} for every i}.
The quandle cochains are Hom_Z(C_n^Q(X),A), with differential δφ=φ∂.

The theorem below gives H^n(B^QX;A)≅H_Q^n(X;A) for all n≥0 under this
unaugmented C_0=Z convention. If quandle cohomology is only defined in positive
degrees, the assertion is precisely the same for n≥1. A convention setting
C_0=0 matches reduced cohomology in degree zero; this convention change is not
an obstruction to the question. The construction also works for the empty set
if it is admitted as a quandle, yielding a point and zero positive cohomology.

## 2. The space

Start with the disjoint union U_X=⨿_{n≥0} [0,1]^n×X^n. Write its points as
(t_1,…,t_n;x_1,…,x_n), or as words ((t_1,x_1),…,(t_n,x_n)).
Take the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by:

1. If t_i=0, delete the i-th coordinate and the i-th label.
2. If t_i=1, delete that coordinate and label and replace every earlier label
   x_j (j<i) by x_j*x_i. Later labels are unchanged.
3. If x_i=x_{i+1}, redistribute t_i,t_{i+1} arbitrarily within [0,1] while
   preserving their sum; all other coordinates and labels stay fixed.

Call the result B^QX. Rules 1–2 alone define the rack space BX.
The quotient is exactly the quandle specialization of Ishikawa–Tanaka §6.1:
the second biquandle operation becomes a*b=a, so only labels before i change
in Rule 2. The construction uses no selected base element or ordering on X.

For clarity, Rule 3 identifies equal-sum fibers inside a degenerate square. It
does **not** collapse the whole degenerate square to a point or collapse a
subcomplex. This distinction preserves its boundary loop.

## 3. Verification of the CW structure

The published CW assertion in Ishikawa–Tanaka §6.1 is the topological input:
there is one n-cell for every element of ND_n(X), and the map q:BX→B^QX is
cellular, sending a degenerate n-cell to the (n−1)-skeleton. The following
normal-form argument checks that assertion directly for quandles.

### 3.1 Shortening rules

The following operations reduce the word length and are consequences of Rules
1–3:

- erase a coordinate 0;
- erase a coordinate 1, applying R_x to its prefix if its label is x;
- merge an adjacent equal-label pair (s,a),(t,a): write s+t=k+u with
  k=floor(s+t) and 0≤u<1; apply R_a^k to the entire prefix, and replace the
  pair by (u,a) if u>0, or by nothing if u=0.

Here k may be 0,1,2. For s+t≤1 the equal-sum rule pushes one coordinate to 0;
for s+t≥1 it pushes one to 1 and Rule 2 applies. The case s+t=2 consists of
erasing two coordinates 1. Idempotence R_a(a)=a makes the described formulas
consistent. Every shortening sequence terminates.

### 3.2 Confluence

Every right translation is a quandle automorphism. For any such automorphism S,

    S R_a^k = R_{S(a)}^k S.                         (1)

In particular R_b R_a=R_{a*b} R_b. These identities resolve all local choices:

- Erasing a zero commutes with another shortening, except at an adjacent merge,
  where the merge with zero simply gives the unchanged other coordinate.
- Two erasures of 1 commute after the labels are adjusted: for positions i<j,
  the two transformations of the prefix agree by
  R_{x_i*x_j} R_{x_j}=R_{x_j} R_{x_i}.
- An erasure of 1 and a merge in disjoint positions commute by (1); the right-hand
  operation acts uniformly on the labels of the left-hand operation. If they
  overlap, the merge has one summand 1 and gives the same carry and residual.
- Two merges in disjoint positions commute by (1), including carries with k=2.
  If they overlap, the three adjacent labels are all a. Both orders give carry
  floor(s+t+u), the same residual fractional part, and the same power of R_a
  on the prefix. Every remaining a is fixed by those powers.

These cases cover all overlaps of the length-one erasures and length-two merges.
Thus every two immediate reductions have a common further reduction. Termination
and induction on length give a unique terminal word.

A terminal word has all coordinates strictly between 0 and 1 and unequal
adjacent labels. Conversely such a word admits no shortening. The normal form
is preserved by each generating equivalence: this is immediate for Rules 1–2,
and for Rule 3 both pairs merge using the same sum. Therefore two terminal
words represent the same point exactly when they are identical.

### 3.3 Topology, not only a bijection

It follows that the open cubes (0,1)^n×ND_n(X) are pairwise disjoint in B^QX
and cover it. On a closed cube the normalization is piecewise affine in its
coordinates: merging adds coordinates, and carrying is split at integral sums.
There are only finitely many shortening patterns, as each reduces length and
begins with a word of fixed finite length. On each resulting closed polyhedral
piece the quotient map factors through a closed nondegenerate cube of dimension
at most n. Formulas on common faces agree by confluence. Only finitely many
labels can occur in these reductions of one fixed cube, because there are
finitely many patterns, each applying finitely many right translations.

For a nondegenerate n-tuple, no interior point shortens. Its boundary maps into
the lower-dimensional cubes by Rules 1–2 and subsequent normalization. A
degenerate n-cube factors piecewise through dimensions at most n−1 by merging
an equal pair. Consequently one can construct B^QX inductively by attaching just
the closed nondegenerate n-cubes along those boundary maps. No additional
interior identifications are introduced at higher dimensions, by uniqueness of
normal form. Each attaching map meets finitely many cells, as above.

The quotient topology from all closed cubes is the weak topology from the
nondegenerate cubes: the additional degenerate-cube maps factor continuously
through a finite closed cover into the lower skeleta. Thus both constructions
have the same final topology. This proves the stated CW structure and the
cellularity of q. It also verifies the restriction of each characteristic map
to its open cube is a homeomorphism onto that open cell. The argument is local
to finite words and applies to arbitrary cardinality of X.

## 4. Cellular chain and cochain identification

Orient every cube by (t_1,…,t_n). The cellular chain complex of the rack space
is C_*^R(X): its two i-faces have the labels d_i^0x,d_i^1x and the indicated
cubical signs. The cellular map q sends the generator x to its equally oriented
nondegenerate cell if x∈ND_n, and to 0 if x is degenerate.
Its induced chain map is therefore precisely the quotient map

    C_*^R(X) → C_*^R(X)/D_*.

This is a surjection in each degree. Since cellular maps commute with boundary,
the cellular differential on B^QX is forced to be the differential induced by
∂ on C_*^Q(X). Hence there is an actual chain isomorphism, not only a homology
isomorphism,

    C_*^cell(B^QX;Z) ≅ C_*^Q(X).

Apply Hom_Z(−,A). For infinite X this means all functions on the indicated basis
(the relevant products of A), not just finitely supported functions. We get the
cochain isomorphism

    C_cell^*(B^QX;A) ≅ Hom_Z(C_*^Q(X),A).

Cellular cohomology of a CW complex computes its singular cohomology. It follows
for every abelian group A and every n≥0 that

    H^n(B^QX;A) ≅ H_Q^n(X;A).                     (2)

There is no universal-coefficient splitting or torsion restriction in this argument.

## 5. Naturality

If f:X→X′ is a quandle homomorphism, apply f to every label and leave all
coordinates unchanged. The quandle identity f(a*b)=f(a)*f(b) respects Rule 2;
equality preservation respects Rule 3. The map descends continuously to B^Qf,
respects identities and composition, and is cellular. If f identifies adjacent
labels, it maps the corresponding cell to a lower skeleton; this causes no
exception to functoriality.

On cellular chains B^Qf sends a tuple to its coordinatewise f-image modulo
degenerate tuples. This is exactly the usual quandle chain map. Pulling cochains
back and using the natural cellular-to-singular cohomology comparison proves
that (2) is contravariantly natural in X. It is covariantly natural in the
coefficient homomorphism A→A′ as well. This is the requested natural space.

## 6. Boundaries and falsification controls

- **Singleton:** for X={a}, every n-tuple with n≥2 is degenerate. The corrected
  CW structure has a vertex and one loop, so B^QX≅S^1; H_Q^1(X;A)=A and
  H_Q^n(X;A)=0 for n≥2.
- **Disconnected/trivial quandles:** no connectedness was used. For the trivial
  quandle on m elements, ∂=0 and there are m(m−1)^{n−1} n-cells for n≥1,
  matching the normalized complex in every degree.
- **Non-injective homomorphisms:** cellular collapse is required for naturality;
  assuming that every cell maps to a cell of the same dimension would be false.
- **Original quotient warning:** a degenerate square (a,a) has ordinary edges
  labeled a. The degeneracies are not a CW subcomplex of BX. Indeed, the
  subcomplex generated by all degenerate cells is all of BX when X is nonempty:
  every nonempty tuple (x_1,…,x_n) is a face of
  (x_1,…,x_n,x_n), and the vertex is a face of an edge. Collapsing that
  subcomplex gives a point, already wrong for the singleton.
- **Literal Nosaka cone:** the domain in Nosaka Definition 2.1 is a disjoint
  union of contractible cubes. Its positive homology is zero. The cofiber exact
  sequence therefore preserves H_n(BX) for n≥2. For the singleton, every rack
  differential is zero, so H_2(BX)=Z while H_2^Q(X)=0. That literal cone cannot
  establish the target. The historical error and a second unsuitable quotient
  are explicitly discussed in Ishikawa–Tanaka Remark 6.1. The corrected space
  above is the one used here.

## 7. Conclusion and limits

The full, ordinary quandle-cohomology target is realized in all degrees with
arbitrary constant abelian coefficients and naturally in quandle homomorphisms.
No finite search or special case is being substituted for it. The recommendation
is **already_solved**, credited to the corrected existing construction. The
remaining task is independent audit of this verification, not an unresolved
mathematical gap in the stated target.

The supplied exact controls check the boundary, degeneracies, naturality, and
finite rewriting instances. They cannot by themselves prove an infinite CW
assertion; Sections 3–5 and the cited primary construction supply that argument.
