# Independent acceptance audit: a periodic two-row Lefschetz chain

Date: 13 October 2026 UTC.

## Verdict and exact scope

**ACCEPTED.** The candidate gives a valid counterexample to each of the weak and strong eventual Lefschetz dichotomies in the ordinary standard-graded, arbitrary-field, arbitrary-fixed-row formulation of Tim Romer's question. Fix any prime p and the single infinite field K = F_p(t). Use c = 2 for every n. For every n >= 2, both WLP and SLP hold exactly when p does not divide n-1. Both truth values occur at arbitrarily large n within that same field and that same compatible invariant chain.

The exceptional initial quotient at n = 1 has both properties. The candidate properly restricts its residue-class classification to n >= 2. No mathematical correction is needed. This counterexample gives no negative answer to a separately imposed characteristic-zero or c = 1 restriction. In characteristic zero the displayed chain has both properties for every n.

## 1. Ideal, grading, properness, and chain compatibility

Let R_n = K[x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n], with each variable of degree one. Define I_n using all the following generator types:

1. Every monomial of degree three.
2. x_i x_j when i != j.
3. y_i y_j for every i,j, including i = j.
4. x_i y_i for every i.
5. x_i y_j - x_i y_k when i,j,k are pairwise distinct.

Every generator is homogeneous of degree two or three. In particular I_n has zero components in degrees zero and one, so it is proper and A_n is a standard graded algebra with A_n,0 = K and dim A_n,1 = 2n. The inclusion of all degree-three monomials implies (R_n)_+^3 is contained in I_n, hence A_n is finite-dimensional and Artinian.

Each displayed condition is preserved by any permutation of column indices. Each generator at n is a generator at n+1; its translate by any permutation of n+1 columns is again of the same allowed type. Ideals generated by these translates are therefore contained in I_(n+1). This verifies the full source chain condition, not merely invariance of each isolated ideal. The definition also makes sense at n = 1 and n = 2: types requiring too many distinct indices are empty, as they should be.

Multiples of degree-two generators and combinations with degree-three generators cannot produce new degree-two relations. In a homogeneous ideal generated in degrees at least two, its degree-two part is exactly the K-linear span of its quadratic generators. Thus there are no hidden higher-degree ideal consequences affecting the degree-two basis below.

## 2. Degree-two quotient, over every field

For n >= 2 the surviving monomials before the difference relations are imposed are x_i^2 and x_i y_j with i != j. No difference relation involves any x_i^2. For a fixed i the off-diagonal monomials form a vector space on n-1 symbols. Its subspace generated by pairwise differences is the kernel of the coefficient-sum map to K: choosing any one reference symbol gives n-2 independent differences, each with a coefficient 1 in its own distinct non-reference coordinate. This argument works over every characteristic, including characteristic two and when p divides n-1.

Relations with different fixed x-index i involve disjoint monomial sets. Hence A_n,2 has the independent basis

u_i = x_i^2, and v_i = x_i y_j for any j != i, for 1 <= i <= n.

The Hilbert function is (1,2n,2n), followed by zeros. In particular each v_i is a nonzero class even when p divides n-1. The vector space quotient does not identify the sum of off-diagonal symbols with their common individual class; confusing these two would create a false characteristic-dependent dimension claim.

One may also construct a graded algebra directly on K, the stated degree-one basis, and the 2n degree-two basis elements, declaring the above products and all positive-degree products with degree-two elements to vanish. Associativity is automatic since all products of three positive-degree elements vanish. The generator relations hold in this algebra, proving independence without reliance on a Groebner basis or an unverified orbit calculation.

At n = 1 there are no v_i. The basis is 1,x_1,y_1,x_1^2 and the Hilbert function is (1,2,1).

## 3. Every linear form and the determinant

For ell = sum_i(a_i x_i + b_i y_i), put B = sum_i b_i. Directly reducing products gives

- ell*x_j = a_j u_j + (B-b_j)v_j;
- ell*y_j = sum_(i != j) a_i v_i.

With domain basis (x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n) and target basis (u_1,...,u_n,v_1,...,v_n), the degree-one multiplication map has the block matrix

M(ell) = [[D(a), 0], [D(B-b), D(a)(J-I)]].

The block-triangular determinant is

det M(ell) = product_i(a_i^2) * det(J-I)
            = (-1)^(n-1)(n-1) product_i(a_i^2).

The latter identity is an integer identity, so no diagonalization or division by n is being used in positive characteristic. For example, apply det(I-u v^T) = 1-v^T u with both u and v equal to the all-ones vector, then account for the factor (-1)^n.

Since both graded pieces have dimension 2n, maximal rank here is equivalent to this determinant being nonzero. Therefore when p divides n-1 every possible linear form fails WLP, over K and after any field extension. When p does not divide n-1 the degree-one map is an isomorphism exactly for the open condition product_i a_i != 0. This is a computation of the full linear-form family, rather than an unjustified specialization to a symmetric form.

The generic rank is exactly 2n-1 in the bad class: the all-ones x-coefficient witness gives the block matrix diag(I,J-I), and J-I has kernel precisely the all-ones line when p divides n-1. Indeed (J-I)w = 0 implies w is constant, and a constant vector is in the kernel exactly when (n-1) times that constant is zero. In the good class the generic rank is 2n.

## 4. Independent obstruction and all strong Lefschetz maps

There is a particularly direct failure certificate in the bad class. The degree-one element z = sum_j y_j is nonzero, because A_n,1 has the unreduced variable basis. For every i,

y_i z = 0, and x_i z = (n-1)v_i.

Thus if p divides n-1, z is a degree-one socle element annihilated by every linear form. Equal dimensions in degrees one and two then force failure of injectivity and of maximal rank for every ell. This proves the failure result independently of the determinant.

For the good class choose ell = sum_i x_i. The map A_n,1 -> A_n,2 is diag(I,J-I), hence invertible. The map A_n,0 -> A_n,1 is injective because ell != 0. All degree-one multiplication maps with target degree at least three have zero target and hence maximal rank. This proves WLP.

For SLP the only additional map with nonzero source and target is multiplication by ell^2 from A_n,0 to A_n,2. Its image of 1 is sum_i u_i, since all mixed x_i x_j vanish. This sum is nonzero in every characteristic, including two: the u_i are linearly independent and each coefficient is 1. Every map by a higher power has zero target, and powers starting from a positive degree also have zero target except the already verified degree-one map. Therefore the same ell is strong Lefschetz.

Conversely SLP includes the first-power maps and implies WLP. There can be no SLP successes in the bad class. At n = 1, ell = x_1 has nonzero maps A_0 -> A_1 and A_0 -> A_2, and a surjective map A_1 -> A_2, so both properties hold there as well.

## 5. Fixed-field quantifiers and infinite successes/failures

Fix a prime p once and for all, choose K = F_p(t) once and for all, and use the above generator rule for every n. The indices 1+kp for k >= 1 all fail both properties. The indices 2+kp for k >= 0 all have both properties. These are two unbounded sets in a single chain; no field or row count is changed as n grows. In particular p = 2 gives successes at every even n >= 2 and failures at every odd n >= 3.

The good witness is defined over the prime field, while failure applies to every linear form over every extension. The conclusion consequently also holds over finite fields if WLP/SLP are defined through the existence of a base-field linear form. No finite-field convention is needed for the counterexample to the usual infinite-field formulation.

The source's two eventual dichotomies are both refuted, separately. Neither can be recovered by interpreting the slash between SLP and WLP differently, since both properties have identical truth values in this example.

## 6. Independent computation and its limits

The companion independent verifier builds the complete quadratic monomial lattice and the full family of pairwise-difference generators. It does not import the producer's quotient routine. It verifies a quotient projection against every relation, then uses integer Smith normal form, raw monomial multiplication, symbolic determinants, and exact prime-field rank computations. A normal, non-optimized Python execution completed successfully.

- Seven integer relation-lattice cases, n = 1,...,7: every nonzero Smith invariant is 1 and the quotient dimension is 1 at n = 1 or 2n at n >= 2. There is no torsion in these degree-two integer quotients.
- Five symbolic determinants, n = 2,...,6: each matches the stated polynomial exactly, including its sign.
- Sixty-five modular witness cases, n = 1,...,13 and p in {2,3,5,7,11}: the all-x linear form has the predicted rank and nonzero square. The tests include bad congruence classes in every listed characteristic.
- Every column injection n -> n+1 was tested for n = 1,...,4, totaling 152 injection maps: all source quadratic generators vanish under the verified target quotient projection. Degree-three preservation is automatic from degree.

These finite tests provide independent implementation checks. The acceptance verdict rests on the all-n algebraic proof above, not on extrapolation from their range. No repository write, publication, external contact, or execution of source-paper code was part of this audit.
