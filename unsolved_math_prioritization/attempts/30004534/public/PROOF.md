# A two-row periodic Lefschetz chain in positive characteristic

## Statement

Fix a field K of positive characteristic p. In particular, K may be the infinite field F_p(t). Set

R_n = K[x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n],

with all variables of degree one, and let Sym(n) permute subscripts simultaneously. Define I_n as the homogeneous ideal generated by:

1. every degree-three monomial;
2. x_i x_j for distinct i,j;
3. y_i y_j for all i,j, including i=j;
4. x_i y_i for all i;
5. x_i y_j - x_i y_k for pairwise distinct i,j,k.

For every n >= 2, R_n/I_n has WLP and SLP if and only if p does not divide n-1. Hence both properties hold and fail infinitely often on this single compatible invariant ideal chain. The proposed eventual dichotomy is false in the unrestricted-characteristic, arbitrary-fixed-row formulation. This construction takes c=2. It does not settle the c=1 or characteristic-zero variants.

## Validity of the chain

The listed generators are homogeneous, their collection is unchanged by every simultaneous permutation of column indices, and the quotient is Artinian because I_n contains the cube of the irrelevant maximal ideal. Every displayed generator at n remains a generator at n+1; every Sym(n+1)-translate is also among the displayed generators at n+1. Thus

Sym(n+1) * (I_n R_(n+1)) is contained in I_(n+1).

No ideal is chosen independently as n varies. The construction uses a fixed finite collection of local generator types of column support at most three. For n=1 the pairwise/triple-distinct conditions simply contribute no generators.

## Graded vector spaces

There are no generators of degree zero or one, so A_n,0=K and A_n,1 has basis x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n.

For n >= 2 the degree-two quotient has basis

u_i = x_i^2,   v_i = x_i y_j for any j != i,   1 <= i <= n.

Indeed, all other monomials have been killed except the x_i^2 and the off-diagonal x_i y_j. For each fixed i, the latter span a vector space on n-1 symbols modulo all pairwise differences, which is one-dimensional over every field. Relations never join distinct i, nor involve x_i^2. Therefore no characteristic-dependent dimension assertion is involved. The Hilbert function is exactly (1,2n,2n), and all degrees >=3 vanish.

## Multiplication by every linear form

Write an arbitrary linear form as

ell = sum_i (a_i x_i + b_i y_i),   B = sum_i b_i.

The relations give

ell*x_j = a_j u_j + (B-b_j) v_j,
ell*y_j = sum_(i != j) a_i v_i.

Using ordered bases (x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n) and (u_1,...,u_n,v_1,...,v_n), the multiplication matrix is

M(ell) = [ D(a)          0          ]
         [ D(B-b)       D(a)(J-I)  ],

where D denotes a diagonal matrix and J is the all-ones n-by-n matrix. Consequently

det M(ell) = (-1)^(n-1) (n-1) product_i a_i^2.

The identity det(J-I)=(-1)^(n-1)(n-1) is an integer polynomial identity and remains valid in every characteristic. For example, it follows from the rank-one determinant identity det(I-u v^T)=1-v^T u.

Because the two graded pieces have equal dimension, maximal rank here means invertibility. Thus no linear form can be weak Lefschetz when p divides n-1. This is a computation for every linear form, not a replacement of a generic form with an invariant one.

An additional direct obstruction in the bad congruence class is the nonzero degree-one element z_n=sum_j y_j. It satisfies y_i z_n=0 and x_i z_n=(n-1)v_i=0. Thus z_n is a nonzero degree-one socle element, preventing injectivity of every multiplication A_n,1 -> A_n,2.

When p does not divide n-1, choose the explicit K-rational form ell=sum_i x_i. Its matrix is block diagonal with blocks I and J-I, hence invertible. Multiplication A_n,0 -> A_n,1 is injective and A_n,2 -> A_n,3=0 is automatically maximal rank. Therefore ell is weak Lefschetz.

Moreover ell^2=sum_i u_i is nonzero, including in characteristic two. The only additional possibly nonzero map required for SLP is multiplication by ell^2 from A_n,0 to A_n,2, and this map is injective. All higher target degrees vanish. Thus this same ell is strong Lefschetz. Conversely SLP implies WLP by definition.

## Infinite alternation

For every fixed prime p, the infinitely many integers n=1+kp (k>=1) fail both properties. The infinitely many integers n=2+kp (k>=0) have both properties. In characteristic two the sequence for n>=2 is exactly: both hold at even n and both fail at odd n.

At n=1, the quotient has basis 1,x_1,y_1,x_1^2 and x_1 is strong Lefschetz; this finite initial exception has no effect on the conclusion.

## Scope

The example works over any field of positive characteristic, even under a requirement that the Lefschetz form be defined over the base field. Taking K=F_p(t) meets the infinite-field generic-element convention directly. For c>2, one may include every variable in the additional rows among the linear generators; the same quotient and conclusions result. For characteristic zero this particular chain has both properties for every n, and no conclusion about the general characteristic-zero question is asserted.
