## 1. Reference solution, normalization, and physical boundary

Take Ω=(−L,L)^{d−k}×(0,L)^k with 1≤k≤d and R(T)<L. The radial full-space profile is even in each coordinate. Restricting it to k positive-coordinate half-spaces divides mass and every radial integral by 2^k. Choosing full mass 2^k therefore gives mass one on Ω, at every time. The outer coordinate faces lie outside the closed support by the strict R(T)<L condition.

On x_j=0 the pressure derivative ∂_j q₀=−βx_j/t vanishes, even though the density has a positive interior trace on part of that face. The full-space distributional porous-medium solution restricts to a no-flux solution on the orthant, either by even reflection or by the vanishing normal flux and the interior PDE. Corners cause no extra boundary measure: the full-space flux is defined on both sides, has zero normal component on each reflecting face, and intersections of faces have lower boundary dimension.

The spherical free boundary meets each coordinate-zero face orthogonally. The scaling X(t,a)=(t/t₀)^βa preserves the orthant and pushes forward its initial reference measure. In particular this example has permanent symmetric reflecting contact; it is not an off-center Barenblatt profile artificially continued after a wall collision.

## 2. The radial cutoff produces a domain-preserving flow

Fix R(T)<R₁<R₂<L and the same radial signed cutoff as in A2. A smooth radial q, quadratic near zero, has u(t,x)=f(t,|x|²)x for a smooth bounded coefficient on its compact support. Fix t while taking the auxiliary variation, and let Φ_s be the autonomous flow generated by v=u(t,·).

For each coordinate, along this flow,

x_j(s)=x_j(0) exp(∫₀ˢ f(t,|x(σ)|²)dσ).

Thus every coordinate-zero hyperplane is invariant, and each coordinate retains its sign for finite s. Since v vanishes for |x|≥R₂, it vanishes on an open neighborhood of every nonzero physical face. A trajectory cannot cross one of those faces: the vector field is zero nearby, and uniqueness of the full-space ODE, in either time direction, prevents crossing. Therefore Φ_s(Ω)=Ω. This holds at all corners as well as on smooth portions of the faces.

The proof uses the exact flow Φ_s. It does not rely on the generally false assertion that Id+sv preserves an arbitrary curved boundary merely because v is tangent there.

## 3. The proximal variation has no unproved wall-pressure condition

For any finite-energy minimizer Y with reconstructed density r, Φ_s∘Y is admissible for both signs of small s. Its quadratic-cost derivative is ε^(−1)〈Y−X_n,v(Y)〉. The density change of variables gives the internal-energy derivative −∫Ωrᵐ div v, because d/ds det DΦ_s at zero is div v. Bounds on the flow Jacobian and r∈Lᵐ justify dominated convergence.

Stationarity therefore proves candidate (2.1), without integration by parts, regardless of whether r or rᵐ has a nonzero wall trace. This is exactly the only admissibility input from A2 that the wall-contact geometry changes.

## 4. Every other reference identity is unchanged

The scalar cutoff q equals q₀ inside B_{R₁}, is a negative constant outside B_{R₂}, and stays uniformly negative in the annulus. Its residual vanishes in the first and last regions and is bounded by C(−q) in the annulus, as in A2. The restriction to Ω preserves that bound. All required global derivatives remain bounded.

The pressure integral P(t)=∫Ωρᵐ has scaling P(t)=P(t₀)(t/t₀)^(−λ), and div u=dβ/t throughout the positive-density region. Thus P'=−(m−1)∫Ωρᵐ div u. Differentiation of the C¹ pressure power also gives P'=∫Ωρq_t. The orthant restriction changes neither coefficient.
