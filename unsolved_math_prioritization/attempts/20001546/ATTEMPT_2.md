# Attempt 2: bounded thickening and a scalable ball family

## Why this route could matter

The failure in Attempt 1 is local. A graph can fail exact Hellyness while its
Helly hull is only a bounded distance away. One natural repair is to take a
power Q^k, joining vertices whose Q-distance is at most k. These powers still
have free proper cocompact A-actions. We therefore test this repair and seek
large ball families which could obstruct a uniformly bounded Helly completion.

## An exact all-scale pairwise-intersecting family

Retain p=ab and d=b^-1 a b=a b a^-1. Put z=p^3. In the rank-two braid subgroup,
z is central; conjugation by p cycles a,b,d. From the braid relation,

    a^-1 p=b,
    a^-2 p^2=ba,
    a^-3 p^3=bad.

Since a commutes with z, for every m>=0,

    a^-m p^m = (bad)^floor(m/3) times the first (m mod 3) letters of bad.

Thus the right side is always a word of length m in S. For each integer n>=1,
the radius-n balls centered at 1, a^(2n), and p^(2n) intersect pairwise:

- the first two contain a^n;
- the first and third contain p^n;
- the second and third contain a^(2n) times the first n letters of the
  displayed word of length 2n for a^(-2n) p^(2n).

This family is exact for all n, without a search or a word-problem assumption.

## Complete certificates at n=1,2,3

The supplied script verifies, using the homomorphism of Attempt 1, that for
n=1,2,3 the image candidates in the first ball that can belong to the second
ball consist only of the image of a^n, and those that can belong to the third
consist only of the image of p^n. These images are distinct. Therefore the
three balls have no common vertex in A, regardless of whether the homomorphism
is injective. The image balls have sizes 19, 211, and 2089.

It follows that Q^n is not Helly for n=1,2,3: radius-one balls of Q^n are exactly
radius-n balls of Q. This is a complete exclusion of each of these three
models, not an extrapolation from finite sampling. See checks_turn2.json.

## What a coarse obstruction would require

The same argument for arbitrary n would need a uniform normal-form description
of the intersections, or a separating metric functional. The finite certificates
do not provide that statement. Worse, even showing empty intersections at the
original radius n does not establish a growing defect. To refute coarse
Hellyness one must prove that the least common enlargement needed tends to
infinity. It is not enough to have increasingly large examples repaired by
adding 1 to every radius.

A suggestive submodel is the rank-two subgroup generated by a and z. With the
rank-two dual-simple word metric, its stable norm has the triangular form
max(|u|, |v|, |u+v|), after using v for the central exponent measured in p-units.
Such a norm admits homothetic ball obstructions. This is only motivation here:
we have NOT shown that its distances and ball intersections are preserved in
Q, or that possible centers outside the subgroup can be retracted without
increasing distances. No conclusion about coarse Hellyness is based on it.

**Normalization and source clarification.** In this motivational rank-two
calculation the symmetric simple-generating set is D={a,b,d,p} and its inverses,
and an element is a^u z^k, with z=p^3 and v=3k. Thus the expression means
max(|u|,|3k|,|u+3k|). The standard simple-generator length formula is

    |g|_D = max(sup(g),0) - min(inf(g),0).

For the rank-two dual normal form of a^u z^k, inf(g)=3k+min(u,0) and
sup(g)=3k+max(u,0), yielding that expression. The general length formula is
recorded in E.-K. Lee and S. J. Lee, *Translation numbers in a Garside group
are rational with uniformly bounded denominators*, Section 4,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0604061v2. This clarifies the intrinsic rank-two
normalization only; no statement about ambient Q distances, outside centers,
or an unbounded coarse-Helly defect follows from it.

The exact remaining bridge is a uniform metric/intersection theorem for this
parabolic submodel inside Q. Attempt 3 switches to the complementary approach:
can the already-Helly extended Deligne model acquire a proper action by a more
general line-fiber modification?
