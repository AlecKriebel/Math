## Geometry independently checked

1. The quadric Q is nonsingular by its nondegenerate diagonal equation. The hyperplane section H is a smooth quadric surface. Dependence of the two gradients defining C forces at most one of its four coordinates to be nonzero; H then forces all four to vanish. Hence C is smooth. Connectedness also follows from the complete-intersection Koszul resolution on P^3; adjunction gives trivial canonical bundle and genus one. Thus the chosen center meets the family 2.23(a) hypotheses precisely.

2. The blowup V is smooth. The ideal I_C(2) is generated by q and u times the five homogeneous coordinates. Its blowup graph is a closed subvariety of Q×P^5, with hyperplane classes h and 2h−F. Consequently 3h−F is ample. The smooth codimension-two blowup formula identifies it with −K_V.

3. The same argument works for Q_0 and Z. The center avoids the unique cone vertex o, so the blowup is unchanged near that vertex. The local equation is a nondegenerate quadratic hypersurface in four variables: an ordinary threefold double point. Elsewhere the ambient variety and center are smooth. Thus Z is normal, klt, Gorenstein and singular; its anticanonical divisor is ample. The strict transform E≅H is smooth because C is Cartier on H.

4. The family with equation t²u²+Σx_i²=0 and blowup center C×A^1 is the specified degeneration. Under its generic-fiber trivialization u_original=t u_family. Central vanishing of u_original has order one, matching ord_E, with the sign used in Miao–Wang. Their §3.1 gives the special degeneration and the corresponding graded anticanonical ring.

5. The fan normals of S have determinant one consecutively, and all facets are at distance one from the origin. This is the smooth Fano Hirzebruch surface F_1. D is its prime toric boundary divisor for the ray (0,1). E×S and V×D are smooth and transverse.

The irrational-ratio proof is valid for every p,q>0. Hermite–Lindemann makes b transcendental because its equation would otherwise make exp(2b) algebraic; b²=2 cannot satisfy that equation. Thus a/b is irrational without an appeal to an unproved algebraic independence conjecture.

## Valuation and the full real degeneration

The Gauss formula on K(V)(z,w) extends the valuation a ord_E, assigns value zero to z and value b to w, and is multiplicative. Its center is the generic point of E×D. Distinct powers of the residue-transcendental z cannot cancel. It agrees with the SNC monomial valuation of weights (a,b). Its group is aZ+bZ and its discrepancy is a+b. Irrationality of the ratio gives rational rank two, excluding every positive real multiple of every prime-divisor valuation.

In anticanonical degree m, write a section using the independent toric character lines of B_m. On the (i,j) line, the filtration is the ord_E filtration on A_m with combined weight ak+b(j+m). Irrationality makes different (k,j) pairs distinguishable. Taking leading coefficients therefore gives, compatibly with multiplication,

gr_v R(X) ≅ (gr_ordE R(V)) # R(S) ≅ R(Z) # R(S).

This is a genuine ring identity, not only a Hilbert-function identity. Character multiplication handles coincident i-independent weights; the coefficient graded ring is a domain. The equal-degree product is the section ring of the exterior tensor product of the ample anticanonical line bundles. After a common Veronese it is finitely generated and has Proj Z×S.

Finite generation here is the standard finite generation of a real filtration, as in Miao–Wang Definition 2.1 and BLXZ §2.2: finite generation of its associated graded algebra. One can also lift finitely many homogeneous graded generators. Successive subtraction of leading forms terminates in each R_m because each degree has only finitely many jumps, proving generation by finitely many weighted sections. No assertion that the dense nonnegative subgroup aZ+bZ itself is a finitely generated semigroup is needed.

The normalized degree-m weight is

ak+b(j+m)−m(a+b)=a(k−m)+bj.

This is exactly the sum of the canonical first-factor generator and the toric generator. The associated central anticanonical ring and klt Fano central fiber establish specialness. Alternatively, positive rational first-factor weights correspond to base changes of the ordinary special E-degeneration, combined with a toric product action. Real refinement preserves this central ring. There is no unjustified replacement of a real test configuration by one integral one-parameter subgroup.

## Stability, singular product metric and global minimization

The precise forward bridge is Miao–Wang Theorem 3.2 → weighted K-polystability of (Z,aξ_E) → BLXZ Corollary 5.9 → reduced uniform weighted Ding stability → Han–Li generalized YTD / BLXZ Theorem 4.9 → weak Kähler–Ricci soliton. The original v2 citation omitted the explicit Corollary 5.9 bridge; v4 repairs it. For the reverse soliton-to-polystability implication and the stationary second step, BLXZ Theorem 1.3 and its proof are now explicitly cited.

Wang–Zhu Theorem 1.1 provides the smooth toric soliton on S with the already verified vector. The product assertion is justified directly, rather than by claiming tensorization of minimizers. On the regular locus, Ricci forms and Lie derivatives split. The product potential is the sum of the pulled-back potentials. The sole top-degree term in its Monge–Ampère expansion is binomial(5,3) times the product of factor measures; Hamiltonians add. Boundedness of the weak soliton potential follows also from Han–Li generalized YTD, Corollary 31, and is preserved by adding the smooth factor. Bedford–Taylor products of locally bounded potentials put no mass on the singular analytic locus, and the klt right-hand measure does not charge it either. Thus the equality on the regular locus extends across {o}×S. The product is indeed a weak Kähler–Ricci soliton in the required singular sense.

BLXZ's stability/existence equivalence applies to the actual induced vector field, including its torus data. Han–Li optimal-degeneration Theorem 5.3 then applies to the constructed finite-discrepancy valuation and special real test configuration. The theorem characterizes global minimizers over Val(X); its proof and Theorem 1.5 identify the full filtration infimum as well. It is not restricted to toric, product-type, or rank-one valuations. Uniqueness identifies the constructed valuation and central fiber with the canonical first step. The product soliton is already K-polystable, so the second step preserves its variety.

Finally, X is smooth and Z×S is singular along {o}×S. This independently proves genuine nontriviality.
