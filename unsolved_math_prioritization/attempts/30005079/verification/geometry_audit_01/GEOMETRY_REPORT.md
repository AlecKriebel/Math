## 1. Source and central geometry: direct verification

Write `f=Σx_i²`, `q=Σ(i+1)x_i²`, with four x-coordinates. On `H={f=0}⊂P³`, dependent gradients for `C={f=q=0}` would imply `(i+1−λ)x_i=0` for every i. Distinct coefficients allow at most one nonzero coordinate, contradicting f=0. Thus C is smooth. On `H≅P¹×P¹`, it is a divisor of type (2,2). The sequence `0→O_H(−2,−2)→O_H→O_C→0`, together with the elementary product cohomology calculation `H¹(O_H(−2,−2))=0`, gives `H⁰(O_C)=C`; hence C is connected. Adjunction gives `K_C=O_C`, so it has genus one. This verifies the hypotheses for family 2.23(a), rather than assuming the family label.

The blowup `V=Bl_C(Q)` is smooth. Let h be the pulled back hyperplane and F its exceptional divisor. Locally a smooth codimension-two center has regular parameters u,q. In the blowup chart u=sq, the identity `du∧dq=q ds∧dq` proves the discrepancy-one formula, hence `−K_V=3h−F`. The ideal sheaf `I_C(2)` is globally generated by q and u times the five projective coordinates. The graph construction embeds the blowup in `Q×P^N`, with second hyperplane class `2h−F`. Consequently `3h−F` restricts the ample external product `O_Q(1)⊠O(1)` and is ample.

The identical argument applies to `Z=Bl_C(Q₀)`, where `Q₀={f=0}⊂P⁴` is the cone. The vertex o is absent from C because C lies in u=0. In the chart u=1, the vertex equation is the nondegenerate four-variable quadratic equation f=0. Blowing up its origin resolves it: the exceptional divisor is the smooth quadric surface, and adjunction from the blowup of A⁴ gives discrepancy `3−2=1`. The resolution is smooth and its exceptional divisor smooth. This proves the node is terminal, in particular klt, and Gorenstein. The blowup along C changes nothing near it, and is smooth elsewhere. Thus Z is normal, klt, Gorenstein, Fano and singular. Its only singularity is that node.

For the surface, the four successive primitive rays `(1,0),(0,1),(−1,1),(0,−1)` have determinant one. Their cones cover R², and each affine toric chart is A². The polygon in the candidate has each facet at anticanonical distance one and normal fan equal to this fan. It therefore defines the smooth projective Fano surface F₁; D is a smooth toric boundary divisor. The product X=V×S is smooth projective, with ample anticanonical external product. In particular it is a smooth Fano fivefold.

Taking the product of the displayed node resolution with S is a log resolution of Z×S with the same discrepancy +1. Hence the central product is klt Gorenstein Fano. Its singular locus is `{o}×S`, while X is smooth. They cannot be isomorphic. This distinguishes the **first** underlying variety and rules out a merely product-type example with unchanged central variety.

## 2. Flatness and the actual special family

Let `Qcal={t²u²+f=0}⊂P⁴×A¹` and blow it up along `C×A¹`. Over t≠0, the coordinate replacement `u↦tu` identifies every fiber with Q and fixes C, so the generic blowup fiber is V.

The hypersurface is flat over A¹: its homogeneous coordinate algebra has no C[t]-torsion (the quadratic polynomial is primitive and irreducible over C(t)); the same holds on its projective affine charts. Equivalently, before projectivization it is monic in one x-variable. At the center it is smooth over A¹, since the gradient of f along the smooth H is nonzero. The center is relatively smooth codimension two; u,q are relative regular parameters there. The two usual blowup charts are the relative charts of the blowup of a smooth surface-coordinate plane at its origin. They are smooth over A¹. Away from the center the blowup is the original flat family. This proves flatness everywhere, and those same charts prove that its t=0 fiber is scheme-theoretically `Bl_C(Q₀)`, without assuming arbitrary blowups commute with base change.

There is also a direct check of the ordinary special test-configuration hypotheses. Near the unique singular point of the total space, its equation is `t²+Σx_i²=0` in A⁵. Blowup at the origin resolves it with discrepancy `4−2=2`. The central divisor t=0 has exceptional multiplicity one, so the discrepancy for the pair is `2−1=1`. The exceptional quadric threefold and strict central divisor meet transversely in a smooth quadric surface. Away from this point the family is smooth over the base, including after the blowup along C. Thus the total pair with its central divisor is plt. Relative adjunction gives the anticanonical class `3h−F`, relatively ample by the same graph argument. No hidden nonnormal or non-Q-Gorenstein central model is needed.

For section-ring base change, one can avoid a bare appeal to a vanishing theorem. The complete-intersection ideal `(u,q)^m` has generators `u^(m−i)q^i`, 0≤i≤m, of degrees m+i and consecutive syzygies of degrees m+i+2. At degree 3m, all twists occurring are nonnegative for m≥1. The ambient quadric exact sequence in projective space gives vanishing of their higher cohomology. The resulting exact sequence proves base-change compatibility of the anticanonical sections. The ordinary degeneration therefore gives the full anticanonical associated graded ring claimed in the note (or any common sufficiently divisible Veronese).

## 4. Product valuation, graded ring and normalization

At the generic point of E×D the local regular parameters are h,w, with residue field K(E)(z). The monomial valuation of weights a,b is well-defined and has finite discrepancy a+b. A basis of the first factor adapted to ord_E, tensored with toric character lines of the second, is adapted to this valuation. For fixed degree m its weights are

`a k + b(j+m)`.

The leading coefficient of a first-factor basis element lies in K(E), and distinct horizontal toric powers z^i are independent over that field. Consequently no extra cancellation can change the multiplicative associated-graded algebra. If a/b is irrational, different (k,j) additionally have distinct weights, exactly as the candidate says. The unweighted degree-graded algebra is the Segre product

`gr_v R(X) ≅ gr_ordE R(V) # R(S)`.

A Segre product of positively graded finitely generated algebras is finitely generated: lift finite homogeneous generators, take the semigroup of nonnegative exponent vectors satisfying equality of total degrees, and use the finite generation of this rational polyhedral semigroup. Its Proj is the product with the external product polarization. This verifies `Proj gr_v R(X)=Z×S` with its anticanonical polarization.

There is an explicit geometric realization if one wants to make the real-filtration terminology more concrete. Take the ordinary E family, product with the product test configuration of S induced by its torus action, over A¹×A¹. This is a flat torus-equivariant two-parameter family with central variety Z×S. The real direction (a,b) refines the E degeneration by that torus action. Equivalently, use a finite anticanonical embedding adapted to both gradings and its real diagonal weights. This realizes the required special real degeneration and avoids treating an arbitrary soliton degeneration as automatically canonical.

The canonical normalization is independently checkable. On the first factor, a local anticanonical frame has the derivative weight opposite to the normal coordinate h; the raw order k becomes k−m in degree m. This is precisely MW's shift on PDF p.11. On the toric factor, the section indexed by (i,j) has raw divisor order j+m along D; the canonical anticanonical character weight is j. Subtracting m(a+b) from the combined raw weight gives

`a(k−m)+bj`.

Thus the induced vector is the prescribed sum `(a ξ_E,b(0,1))`; no extra character twist survives and no sign reversal is needed in the induced grading.

## 5. Exact product soliton proof and convention clarification

The polygon has x-fiber `[−1,y+1]`; hence its width is 2+y and the x-average is y/2. Equation (B) therefore makes both weighted moment components vanish. Strict positive definiteness of the covariance Hessian gives uniqueness. WZ's original equation (2.16) uses `exp(+c·p)`; the HL/MW convention uses `exp(−ξ·p)`. Accordingly c=−ξ. HL-opt explicitly records this sign convention in Remark 2.45 and gives

`e^φ (dd^c φ)^n = e^{θ_φ(ξ)}`  (110),

`Ric(dd^c φ)−dd^c φ = −dd^c θ_φ(ξ)`  (111).

The candidate's displayed Ricci product identity is correct and does **not** state a contrary plus-sign equation. The following optional clarification would make the convention and weak extension explicit without changing the result:

> Use the HL convention (110). For factor potentials φ_Z, φ_S, take φ=p_Z*φ_Z+p_S*φ_S and θ=θ_Z+θ_S. With factorial-normalized Monge–Ampère measures, `MA₅(φ)=MA₃(φ_Z)⊗MA₂(φ_S)`. The factor weak soliton equations multiply, since their canonical volume measures and the functions exp(θ) multiply. After a constant normalization this gives the weak soliton equation on Z×S for the sum vector field. Thus the central pair is weighted K-semistable in exactly the convention of the optimality theorem.

To check the weak assertion, the S-potential is smooth and bounded. On the resolution of Z times S, the product potential has finite energy whenever the Z-potential does; the energy integrals reduce by Fubini to the factor integrals plus bounded terms. The nonpluripolar Monge–Ampère product is the indicated tensor product and gives no mass to the singular locus. Its equality with the canonical volume measure on the regular locus consequently extends to the whole product. If one uses the bounded representative from HL-opt's definition, this is also the ordinary bounded-potential product calculation. The singular set has not been ignored.

MW supplies weighted polystability of (Z,aξ_E); BLXZ supplies its weak soliton. WZ and the moment equation supply the soliton of S with the translated sign convention. The displayed product calculation supplies the soliton of the central product. The singular existence/stability theorem supplies the weighted semistability needed by HL-opt Theorem 5.3.
