## Strongest retained results

### Turn 1: transport and an all-n subclass

An action-cocycle transports through every set-action conjugacy, but can lose two-coordinate locality or uniformity across positions/strand counts. A diagonal gauge changes the coefficients again; one failed transport does not exclude other intertwiners. The exact classical-action centralizer criterion records this missing freedom.

For commuting permutations f,g,h, the pair r(x,y)=(f(y),g(x)), s(x,y)=(h(y),h^(-1)(x)) has an explicit all-n local rack-derived realization. Simultaneous h-invariance of scalar weights is sufficient for local transport to its twist-second reduction. These are subclasses, not the general answer.

### Turn 2: a nonmonomial intertwiner matters

On F_p for any odd prime, r(x,y)=(2x+y,−x), s(x,y)=(−y,−x) and its dihedral rack-derived pair r′(x,y)=(y,−x+2y), q=s have equivalent complex linearizations for every n. Their color matrices are inverse transposes, so the standard finite Fourier transform is an explicit intertwiner.

At p=3 the usual guitar map is nonlocal, and no compatible local target has an isomorphic set action at n=3. Yet the complex linear representations are equivalent. This is a concrete warning against promoting either of those failures to a linear counterexample.

### Turn 3: a genuine characteristic-three negative theorem

For the same three-color source pair, every linearization over a field of characteristic three is inequivalent at n=3 to every compatible local rack-derived target on the same X. An exhaustive certificate covers all 9! bijections q, without assuming nondegeneracy; only q_c(x,y)=(c−y,c−x) survive. A specified nine-word group-algebra norm has rank zero in the source and rank two in every target. The rank argument is valid under arbitrary linear conjugacy.

This is a negative answer to a field-uniform reading of Q2. It is not a negative answer over C, where Turn 2 proves the opposite for this family. Since the short source leaves the field unspecified and Q1 remains unresolved in any case, the bundle is not promoted to solved.

### Turn 4: complete local scalar classification in one test case

For that three-color source pair and its actual twist-second violin reduction, every normalized scalar whole-crossing cocycle is a tensor-diagonal coboundary. Explicit 16-by16 minors of determinant −1 certify both classifications over every coefficient field and every abelian coefficient group, with no overlooked torsion or logarithm assumption. Thus this candidate family does not supply weighted relevance in that specific model.

This does not classify arbitrary welded pairs, unnormalized braid weights, or general nonabelian strandwise cocycles.

### Turn 5: virtual cocycles need welded descent

For the ordered strand-weight model from the cited Farinati–Garcia Galofre paper, a direct calculation supplies the two additional welded identities Wg and Wf. The published two-color general-s infinite-cyclic virtual example fails Wf. It descends exactly after imposing a²=1, and the surviving family transfers to a twist-second pair by a color-only relabeling for all n.

A separate published twist-second example with coefficient group Free(a,b)×Z passes the welded relation without forcing ab=ba. Hence the preceding obstruction does not imply all welded cocycles become abelian or trivial.
