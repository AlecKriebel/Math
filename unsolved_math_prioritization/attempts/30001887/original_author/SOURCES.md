# Source verification and limits

Checked 2026-10-05 UTC. Public-source PDFs and extracts are deliberately absent from this packet. `source_verification.json` records byte counts and SHA-256 hashes of the privately inspected downloads. Dates below come from the documents or publisher/arXiv pages, not search-engine recency labels.

1. **Dömötör Pálvölgyi, joint work with János Pach and Géza Tóth, “New results on decomposability of geometric coverings.”** Contribution in *Discrete Geometry*, Oberwolfach Report 44/2011, printed pp. 2511–2514. [Publisher record](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/11136), [primary PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46358?nt=1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2011/44). The workshop was in September 2011; EMS records publication on 11 April 2012. Inspected the relevant text on pp. 2512–2514 and visually checked pp. 2513–2514. These pages establish the whole-plane, fixed-translate definition and both questions. The polygon bounds were already positive results there; its Theorem C(iv) has a qualification by the earlier wedge criterion and cites unpublished work, so it is not used here to assert every polygon case is resolved. Neighboring homothetic-triangle statements are expressly about another family.

2. **János Pach, Dömötör Pálvölgyi, Géza Tóth, “Survey on Decomposition of Multiple Coverings.”** [Public preprint, dated 24 July 2013](https://real.mtak.hu/15290/1/surveyfinal.pdf); bibliographic references identify the 2014 book chapter in *Geometry, Intuitive, Discrete, and Convex*, volume 24. Inspected Definitions 1.1 and 2.3, Section 2.3, and Section 6. Problem 6.6 is the same whole-plane finiteness implication for all k. Section 2.3 distinguishes total-cover decomposability and gives the far-half-plane device; Section 6 states the finite-family r(k-1)+1 bound. Those observations inform Approaches 4 and 5. This older question list is not evidence by itself of present-day openness.

3. **Gábor Damásdi, Balázs Keszegh, János Pach, Dömötör Pálvölgyi, Géza Tóth, “Coloring Geometric Hypergraphs: A Survey.”** [arXiv:2512.09509v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09509v1), submitted 10 December 2025. Inspected the definitions and Conjecture 2.1 in Section 2.1, the discussion of the 2024 obstruction, and Conjectures 9.1–9.2. The hereditary-hypergraph finiteness and linear-growth statements are still presented as conjectures in this version. They concern hereditary finite incidence families and cannot automatically be identified with unrestricted whole-plane covering by an arbitrary P. The present search did not find a verified later full resolution.

4. **Dömötör Pálvölgyi, “Note on Polychromatic Coloring of Hereditary Hypergraph Families.”** *Graphs and Combinatorics* 40 (2024), article 131, published 22 November 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-024-02836-y), [public publisher-form PDF](https://d-nb.info/1357048173/34), [preprint record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04603). Inspected the statement and proof discussion of Theorem 3. It gives a finite hereditary family with m_2=3 and m_3=6, refuting a particular proposed sharp inequality. This is not m_3=infinity and is not a whole-plane fixed-shape counterexample. Its source edge list is not reproduced in this packet and was not independently recomputed here.

5. **Dömötör Pálvölgyi, “Note on polychromatic coloring of hereditary hypergraph families II.”** [arXiv:2604.24412v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24412v1), submitted 27 April 2026. Inspected the title/abstract, Theorem 2, and complement-construction Lemma 3 on pp. 1–3; the later random construction and appended finite certificates were not independently audited. Its stated result, for every h>=4, is a finite (2h-1)-uniform obstruction to three polychromatic colors whose h-heavy restricted subhypergraphs are two-colorable. Since h changes with the example, this is not a fixed finite m_2 with infinite m_3. The public arXiv comments identify it as AI-drafted and author-checked; only public preprint status, not journal acceptance, was verified.

## Search scope and identification limits

Searches included the exact problem ID/title/source code, the m_2-to-m_3 implication, multiple-cover linear thresholds, whole-plane versus total decomposability, the 2025 survey, and 2024/2026 hereditary-family results. Search result excerpts were used to locate primary sources, not as substitutes for the primary mathematical statements above. This bounded search supports “no verified prior resolution found”; it does not prove that no resolution exists.
