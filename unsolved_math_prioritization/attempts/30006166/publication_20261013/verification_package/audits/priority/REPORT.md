## Exact target and historical source

The target is a dense G_delta subset of the Polish space of **all** continuous actions of the countable discrete restricted group W = Z wr Z on Cantor space, every member of which has an **entire** hyperfinite Borel orbit relation. “All” specifies the ambient action space. The stronger auxiliary Borel theorem assumes an equivariant profinite height; it is not a theorem about every Borel W-action. A conull or phase-space comeager restriction, an almost-finite action, or a generic result in a minimal/pmp subspace does not replace the target.

Sumun Iyer's contribution, joint with Forte Shinko, “Generic actions of countable groups,” in *Set Theory*, Oberwolfach Report 2/2025, printed pp.114–115, explicitly poses this target as Question 3 on p.115. The definition immediately preceding it separates Borel hyperfiniteness from measure hyperfiniteness. The full relevant two pages and the rendered question page were freshly inspected. This establishes a historically posed open question; it is not a certification that the question remains open worldwide on the audit date. [Official report, DOI 10.4171/owr/2025/2](https://content.ems.press/assets/public/full-texts/serials/owr/22/1/14299082/online/10.4171-owr-2025-2.pdf).

## Material generic-factor predecessors

**Doucha–Melleray–Tsankov provides an all-actions theorem from which the height ingredient follows by specialization.** Proposition 3.2 puts all Cantor G-actions in an admissible Boolean-algebra coding. Definition A.3 and Proposition A.7 make existentially closed models a dense G_delta. In finite Boolean variables, the old fibers C_i and new fibers D_j require a nonempty partition, a(D_j)=D_j, t(D_j)=D_(j+1 mod n), and C_i equal to the union of D_j with j=i mod m. The fiber-product extension realizes these finite constraints; generic existential closure realizes them inside the action. Iteration yields the compatible factorial height. This is the auditors' explicit specialization, not a theorem quoted verbatim about W or Zhat. Proposition 2.2 supplies the associated factor-perturbation mechanism, and Proposition 3.2's proof uses fiber-product amalgamation. The source's separately named universal-profinite-action result, Theorem 6.5, concerns minimal-pmp finitely generated free-group actions. It does not resolve the requested W theorem. [DMT v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05474v1), [current author manuscript](https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~tsankov/papers/comeager-cantor-actions.pdf).

**Doucha supplies earlier all-group extension perturbation.** Proposition 2.12(3), statement pp.9–10 and complete proof p.11 in the inspected v3, realizes a symbolic extension over a refining clopen partition while keeping an action in a prescribed finite neighborhood. Its atomwise Cantor homeomorphism argument materially precedes the frozen perturbation proof. No explicit generic W-height or hyperfiniteness theorem is claimed from this source. [Doucha v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08145v3).

**Akin–Glasner–Weiss supplies the earlier conditional-lift syntax and the Z-action factor.** Theorem 3.3, equation (3.7), manuscript pp.14–15, uses the complement of the old-factor condition union the compatible finer-factor conditions, then a countable intersection over continuous finite maps. The preceding paragraph on p.14 gives the universal adding-machine factor for dense-type homeomorphisms. This is a single-homeomorphism result; genericity cannot simply be pulled back from Homeo(Cantor) to Act(W) without an additional argument. It therefore precedes a mechanism, rather than the entire W theorem. [AGW published paper](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-08-04450-4), [inspected author manuscript](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~glasner/papers/fat.pdf).

The lifting proof should be described as an **explicit elementary specialization of these existing extension/genericity principles**, with predecessor citations. Its retained standalone proof remains useful for checking the exact factor compatibility.

## Harder GMRS plus generic LCL composition

The additional composition check is complete. [The detailed addendum](broader_resolution/QUANTITATIVE_CHAIN_ADDENDUM.md) records each arrow and the exact formulas. GMRS's proved condition is two-parameter Borel control `dim_B(s,C s^a)=O(s^b)` with `a>=1` and `ab<=1/4`, or a specified cross-scale recurrence. Its assertion that single-variable subexponential Borel dimension growth implies hyperfiniteness is **Conjecture F(2)**. Polynomial color counts alone do not constitute the proved hypothesis.

The older polynomial W result uses a multiplicity/Lebesgue-number quantity. The stronger Dranishnikov–Sapir v4 Theorem 6.12/Corollary 6.13, pp.19–20, advertises O(s) colors at O(s^3) diameter for W=B_1. Davila's 2020 dissertation, Chapter 4 pp.28–30, identifies an extension-proof error and supplies a replacement whose formulas yield the same pair for W. This audit records the repair as the source's claim rather than independently validating it. **Even granting a lossless ordinary-to-generic-continuous-to-Borel LCL transfer**, that pair is `(a,b)=(3,1)`, product 3, outside the required 1/4. No threshold-fitting bound or verification of the general recurrence was identified. This excludes an established immediate implication from the inspected estimates; it does not prove that better bounds or different metric constructions are impossible. [Dranishnikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405239), [DS v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3868v4), [Davila dissertation](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/05/64/17/00001/Davila_T.pdf).
