# A profinite-height candidate for generic wreath-product hyperfiniteness

## Disposition and exact target

This is a complete affirmative candidate for problem 30006166 / OWR-14299082-012, stopped after three substantive approaches. Independent adversarial review is pending. It is AI-assisted, unrefereed work; no external human verification, formal proof-assistant verification, historical novelty, or priority is claimed. Finite computations below check identities and constructions, not the infinite theorem.

The primary target is Question 3 in Sumun Iyer's contribution, joint with Forte Shinko, to *Set Theory*, Oberwolfach Report 2/2025, printed p.115, DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2025/2. It asks whether generic continuous actions of the restricted wreath product of the integers by the integers on Cantor space are hyperfinite. Here generic means a comeager subset of the Polish space of all continuous actions. Hyperfinite means that the entire orbit equivalence relation, on the entire Cantor space, is an increasing union of finite Borel equivalence relations. It does not mean merely hyperfinite on a conull or comeager subset of the phase space. A single comeager conjugacy class is neither assumed nor established.

Write X for the Cantor space, N=direct_sum_{i in Z} Z, and W=N semidirect Z. Let a_i be the standard lamp at i, a=a_0, and t the shift, with t a_i t^{-1}=a_{i+1}. Thus W is the countable discrete group generated by a,t with all conjugate lamps commuting. Its elements can be written (f,k), with f finitely supported and k integer; multiplication is (f,k)(g,l)=(f+shift_k(g),k+l). Let chi:W->Z be chi(f,k)=k. Unrestricted products and Polish wreath products are not involved.

Let A=Act(W,X) be the closed subspace of Homeo(X)^W consisting of homomorphisms, with the product of compact-open topologies. For Cantor homeomorphisms this is uniform convergence of maps and inverses. It is Polish and Baire. The generators a,t determine every action, but neighborhoods may constrain any finite list of group elements.

## Main theorem (candidate)

There is a dense G_delta subset R of A such that every alpha in R has hyperfinite orbit relation E_alpha.

The proof factors into two statements:

1. Generically, a continuous W-action has a continuous equivariant map to the profinite integers Zhat, on which W acts by addition through chi.
2. Every Borel W-action on a standard Borel space that admits a Borel equivariant map to Zhat has hyperfinite orbit relation.

In particular, (2) does not require the action to be free, minimal, or probability-measure preserving.

## Published inputs and their precise use

The principal input is Conley, Jackson, Marks, Seward, and Tucker-Drob, *Borel asymptotic dimension and hyperfinite equivalence relations*, Duke Math. J. 172 (2023), 3175-3226, DOI https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2022-0100; author manuscript https://math.berkeley.edu/~marks/papers/polycyclic_v13.pdf.

We use its Corollary 5.5: actions of groups of uniformly bounded local polynomial growth have finite Borel asymptotic dimension. Each particular Z^m satisfies that hypothesis; no bound uniform in m is claimed. We use the colored-cover characterization in Lemma 3.1 and the Borel version of Lemma 2.2: for each finite set of group moves one has a fixed finite number of Borel colors whose monochromatic components have uniformly bounded sizes. For a bounded-degree graph, uniform component-size bounds also give diameter bounds.

We use its Theorem 7.3: for proper Borel extended metrics rho_n, if sup{rho_{n+1}(x,y):rho_n(x,y)<r} is finite for every n and finite r, and each rho_n has finite Borel asymptotic dimension, then the union of their finite-distance relations is hyperfinite. Our metrics are graph metrics of increasing bounded-degree Borel graphs, so rho_{n+1}<=rho_n on every pair at finite rho_n-distance. This checks the compatibility condition explicitly; we do not invoke an unrestricted union theorem for hyperfinite relations.

Finally, its Corollary 7.5 applies to countable groups of local polynomial growth, including the countable abelian group N, and gives hyperfiniteness of every Borel N-action. Alternatively this last input is the credited Gao-Jackson theorem for countable abelian group actions.

For background and the baseline not covering W, see Iyer-Shinko, *Asymptotic dimension and hyperfiniteness of generic Cantor actions*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03078. Their locally finite-asymptotic-dimension hypothesis fails for W. Their Theorem 18 can alternatively supply generic finite-dimensional control on the lamp subgroups, but it is not necessary for our main proof.

## 1. Generic compatible finite-height factors

For a positive integer m and continuous c:X->Z/m, let D(c,m) consist of actions alpha such that

c(alpha(a)x)=c(x), and c(alpha(t)x)=c(x)+1 mod m, for every x.

These equalities imply c(alpha(g)x)=c(x)+chi(g) mod m for all g in W. The set D(c,m) is clopen in A. To see openness, the fibers of c form a finite clopen partition: a sufficiently small uniform perturbation of a homeomorphism leaves all its output c-labels unchanged, simultaneously for every input x. Apply this to a and t. For closedness, a failure at a particular point remains a failure under a sufficiently small perturbation. Equivalently the label-valued maps depend continuously, in the uniform discrete topology, on each homeomorphism.

There are countably many continuous maps from X to each finite discrete space. Indeed X has a countable clopen basis, every clopen subset is a finite union of basic clopens by compactness, and a map to a finite set is determined by finitely many clopen fibers.

For m|n and such a c, put

U(c;m,n)=(A minus D(c,m)) union union{D(d,n): d:X->Z/n continuous and d mod m=c}.

It is open. We prove it dense by a relative lifting construction.

### Relative lifting lemma

Fix alpha in D(c,m) and any neighborhood V of alpha in A. There are beta in V and a continuous d:X->Z/n such that beta belongs to D(d,n) and d mod m=c.

Proof. Form the compact zero-dimensional space

Y={(x,j) in X times Z/n: c(x)=j mod m}.

For every x there are n/m choices of j; Y is nonempty and has no isolated points. Let W act on Y by

g*(x,j)=(alpha(g)x,j+chi(g) mod n).

This is well-defined by alpha in D(c,m), and it is a continuous action, not an approximate representation. Denote it by gamma.

We explain the perturbation with full neighborhood control. Choose a finite symmetric list F of group elements sufficient to control V; including inverses handles the topology on homeomorphisms. Choose a finite clopen partition Q of X with sufficiently small mesh for the required uniform bounds. Let P be a common refinement of Q, all alpha(g)^{-1}Q for g in F, and the fibers of c. Every nonempty atom P is a Cantor space, and so is

Y_P={(x,j) in Y:x in P},

which is a finite disjoint union of copies of P. Choose a homeomorphism h_P:P->Y_P, and combine these into a homeomorphism h:X->Y. Set beta(g)=h^{-1} gamma(g) h.

For x in X, the first coordinate u of h(x) lies in the same P-atom as x. Hence alpha(g)u and alpha(g)x lie in the same Q-atom for every g in F. By construction h^{-1}(alpha(g)u,j+chi(g)) is in the same P-atom as alpha(g)u, hence the same Q-atom. Thus beta(g)x and alpha(g)x have the required uniform closeness for all g in F. The same applies to inverses because F is symmetric. Therefore beta belongs to V.

Define d(x) to be the second coordinate of h(x). It is continuous, and d(beta(g)x)=d(x)+chi(g) mod n. Moreover d(x) mod m=c(u)=c(x), since P refines the c-fibers. This proves the lemma.

If alpha is outside D(c,m), it already belongs to U(c;m,n). If it is inside, the lemma places a member of U in every neighborhood. Thus U is dense.

Let R be the countable intersection of U(c;m,n) over all positive m|n and all continuous c:X->Z/m. R is dense G_delta. For alpha in R, start with c_1=0. Recursively lift c_{n!} to c_{(n+1)!}, using alpha's membership in the corresponding U and the fact that it belongs to D(c_{n!},n!). These maps are compatible, so they define a continuous map

p:X->Zhat=lim_inverse Z/n!.

It satisfies p(alpha(g)x)=p(x)+chi(g). It is onto: its image is a nonempty compact subset invariant under addition and subtraction of 1, and the integers are dense in Zhat. No selection of p that is Borel or continuous as a function of alpha is needed; the theorem asserts existence for each alpha in R.

## 2. Finite-width slab graphs

Now let W act Borelly on any standard Borel space X and let p:X->Zhat be Borel and equivariant as above. Fix m>=1. Let r_m(x) in {0,...,m-1} be the residue of p(x) mod m. Put B_m={x:r_m(x)=0}. It is Borel and N-invariant.

Every x has a unique representation x=t^r z with r=r_m(x) and z=t^{-r}x in B_m. Thus X is Borel isomorphic to B_m times {0,...,m-1}. This uniqueness concerns the prescribed residue r; it does not assume freeness.

Define an undirected Borel graph G_m on X by:

- include the lamp edges {x,ax} (and hence their inverses);
- include the shift edge {x,tx} precisely when r_m(x)<m-1.

Ignore loops. The graph has degree at most four, so its path metric rho_m is a proper Borel extended metric with countable finite-distance classes.

In the coordinates x=t^r z, its edges become

(z,r) -- (a_{-r}z,r), and (z,r) -- (z,r+1) for r<m-1.

The first identity follows from t^{-r} a t^r=a_{-r}. The second follows because an uncut shift does not cross the residue boundary. At the cut boundary no corresponding edge is included. Define

K_m=<a_0,a_{-1},...,a_{-(m-1)}>, isomorphic to Z^m.

It acts Borelly on B_m. Its orbit relation need not have finite classes, but its Borel asymptotic dimension is finite by CJMST Corollary 5.5.

### Finite-dimensional slab lemma

The graph metric rho_m has finite Borel asymptotic dimension.

Proof. Fix R>=1, and let S be the finite set of K_m-elements of word length at most ceil(R) in the displayed m lamp generators and their inverses. The cited finite-dimensional characterization gives a finite number D_m+1 of Borel colors on B_m, independent of R, with every monochromatic S-component of size bounded by a finite number C_R.

Color (z,r) by the color of z. A path of length at most R in G_m changes the base coordinate z by a K_m-word with at most ceil(R) lamp letters: vertical moves leave z unchanged and each lamp move is one of the indicated generators or its inverse. Consequently a monochromatic rho_m-distance-at-most-R chain projects to a monochromatic S-chain in B_m. Its base coordinates lie in one such component, so the whole chain component has at most m*C_R points. As it is connected by steps of length at most R, its rho_m-diameter is at most R*(m*C_R-1) when it is nonempty. These Borel colorings verify finite Borel asymptotic dimension. No dimension bound uniform over different m is used.

## 3. Compatible graph exhaustion outside the exceptional orbit

Take m_n=n! for n>=1. Since m_n divides m_{n+1}, a residue equal to m_{n+1}-1 reduces to m_n-1. Therefore every shift edge permitted in G_{m_n} is permitted in G_{m_{n+1}}. Lamp edges never change. Thus the graphs increase and their metrics satisfy rho_{m_{n+1}}<=rho_{m_n} on finite-distance pairs. In particular the exact compatibility hypothesis of CJMST Theorem 7.3 holds, with bound r for pairs at previous distance <r.

Let Z be embedded in Zhat in the usual way, and put X_exc=p^{-1}(Z), X_reg=X minus X_exc. Both sets are Borel and W-invariant. Restrict all slab graphs to X_reg. Invariance ensures that no path used above leaves this set.

For x in X_reg, there is an n with r_{n!}(x)!=n!-1. Otherwise p(x) is congruent to -1 modulo every factorial, which says p(x)=-1 in Zhat, contrary to x in X_reg. Once a shift edge appears, it stays, by graph monotonicity. Thus every generator edge of the W-action on X_reg belongs to some slab graph. Any finite generator word uses only finitely many edges; all occur together in a sufficiently late slab. Therefore

E_W restricted to X_reg = union_n E_{rho_{n!} restricted to X_reg}.

By the slab lemma and the verified metric compatibility, CJMST Theorem 7.3 makes this relation hyperfinite. This is an application of the compatible finite-dimensional metric theorem, not the unresolved general union problem.

## 4. Explicit treatment of the exceptional preimage

For x in X_exc there is a unique integer h(x) with p(x)=h(x). The map h is Borel, since its fibers p^{-1}({k}) are Borel and there are countably many k. Let B=p^{-1}({0}) and q(x)=t^{-h(x)}x in B. The map q is Borel and N preserves B.

We claim

x E_W y if and only if q(x) E_N q(y).

If y=gx, then h(y)=h(x)+chi(g), and t^{-h(y)}g t^{h(x)} has zero chi, hence lies in N. This proves one implication. Conversely, q(y)=f q(x) for f in N implies y=t^{h(y)} f t^{-h(x)}x, proving the other.

CJMST Corollary 7.5 gives an increasing sequence F_j of finite Borel equivalence relations on B with union E_N restricted to B, since N has local polynomial growth. To avoid the invalid step of pulling finite relations through an infinite-to-one q without a cutoff, define H_j on X_exc by

x H_j y iff x=y, or [|h(x)|<=j, |h(y)|<=j, and q(x) F_j q(y)].

Each H_j is a Borel equivalence relation. Within the band it is a pullback of F_j; outside the band points are singleton classes. For each z in B and integer k there is exactly one x with h(x)=k and q(x)=z, namely x=t^k z. Consequently every nonsingleton H_j-class has at most (2j+1) times the size of the relevant finite F_j-class. Thus H_j is finite. The relations increase because the bands and F_j increase. The displayed equivalence proves union_j H_j=E_W restricted to X_exc.

Choose finite increasing exhaustions on X_reg and X_exc and take their disjoint union stage by stage. Since both pieces are invariant, this gives finite Borel equivalence relations increasing to E_W on all of X. This completes statement (2), and statement (1) proves the main theorem.

## 5. Failure modes checked

- Amenability alone is not used to infer Borel hyperfiniteness. Measure hyperfiniteness does not settle this question.
- W is not locally of finite asymptotic dimension. It is two-generated and contains Z^d for every d. Lamps supported in {0,...,d-1} satisfy the word-length bounds ||f||_1<=|(f,0)|_{a,t}<=||f||_1+2(d-1), so these are undistorted finite-rank subgroups.
- A Borel integer-height map on the entire compact action would contradict existence of an invariant probability measure: it would push it to a translation-invariant probability on Z. The proof uses a compact profinite height and reserves integer height for its exceptional preimage; it does not assume that exceptional set is empty, countable, null for every measure, or negligible in category.
- Global finite shift-period actions do not prove genericity: each condition t^m=id is closed and has empty interior because free actions are dense. Our finite height maps are factors, not finite-order conditions on the original action.
- The graph G_m generally has infinite components. It is not claimed to be a finite equivalence relation. Finite Borel asymptotic dimension plus the cited compatible-union theorem supplies the required finite exhaustion.
- The closed-form grid cut has a persistent edge at height -1 to 0. The full exceptional W-invariant preimage p^{-1}(Z) is explicitly treated rather than discarded.
- Compactness prevents a free finitely generated infinite-group Cantor action from being exhausted by nested finite relations whose generator-edge membership sets are all open: an increasing open cover for each generator would stabilize at a common finite stage. Our proof requires only Borel finite relations, so it makes no such unjustified topological assertion.
