# Author approach 1: classify the lifting obstruction

Aim: test whether a quotient-valued invariant can be obtained by projecting an unquotiented one. This is a mathematical lifting analysis, not a count of the source lookup that located Wouters's result.

## A precise obstruction

Let B be an abelian group, D a subgroup, Q=B/D, and f:S→Q a homomorphism. Form

    E_f={(s,b) in S×B : f(s)=b+D}.

There is an exact sequence 0→D→E_f→S→0, with maps d↦(0,d) and (s,b)↦s. A homomorphic lift g:S→B of f exists if and only if this exact sequence splits. Indeed a splitting has the form s↦(s,g(s)); conversely any lift gives such a splitting. In the usual language this is the vanishing of the pullback extension class f*[B] in Ext^1_Z(S,D). No computation of this class is supplied for a general central simple algebra.

For s∈S killed by n, choose b∈B lifting f(s). Then nb∈D and

    theta_n(f,s)=nb mod nD in D/nD

is independent of the lift: replacing b by b+d changes nb by nd. A homomorphic lift must make every theta_n zero. For a cyclic source generated by s of order n, this condition is also sufficient: if nb=nd, then b-d is n-torsion and defines a lift. For a direct sum of cyclic groups, impose the condition on each cyclic generator and combine the lifts. This statement does not assume that arbitrarily chosen pointwise lifts are automatically additive or natural in F.

Consequently, to disprove a natural lifting of cohomological invariants it suffices to find one field and one element with theta_n nonzero.

## Concrete application: the known Platonov–Suslin–Wouters example

Take k=Q_5, F=k((x))((y)), and

    A=(2,x)_2 tensor (5,y)_2.

Here (a,b)_2 is the quaternion algebra with generators u²=a, v²=b, uv=-vu. The quadratic extensions k(sqrt(2)) and k(sqrt(5)) are respectively unramified and ramified, hence linearly disjoint. Platonov's theorem, as stated and used in Wouters §2.2(b), makes A a division algebra of index 4 and gives SK_1(A)≅Z/2. This is an imported theorem, not a new construction. Its exponent is 2.

The two successive Laurent-series residues, followed by the local invariant map, identify

    B=H^4(F,Q/Z(3))≅Br(k)≅Q/Z.

This is the residue splitting used in Wouters §2.2(c); the unramified higher-degree terms over k vanish because cd(k)=2. The subgroup D=[A] cup K_2^M(F) is killed by 2. It is nonzero: cup with {5,y}, then take the two residues. The contribution from (2,x) yields the nonzero quaternion class (2,5) over k; the repeated-symbol contribution from (5,y) vanishes mod 2 since -1 is a square in Q_5. The class (2,5) is nonzero because 5 has odd valuation and cannot be a norm from the unramified quadratic extension k(sqrt(2)). Thus under the residue normalization,

    D={0,1/2} inside Q/Z.

Suslin's injectivity theorem for biquaternions, identified with sigma_1^1 by Kahn §7.C/Corollary 7.5, says sigma_1^1:SK_1(A)→B/D is injective. The unique nonzero element s of the source therefore maps to the unique element of order 2 in B/D, namely 1/4+D (the alternative 3/4 has the same coset).

Now theta_2(sigma_1^1,s)=1/2 in D/2D=D, which is nonzero. Hence sigma_1^1 has no homomorphic lift to B. In particular,

    sigma_1^1 != q_1 c_A.

Equivalently, every map Z/2→Q/Z lands in B[2]=D, so its projection to B/D is zero, whereas sigma_1^1 is nonzero. This is Wouters's Proposition 4.2, with an explicit choice of the local field and parameters and with the integral/finite/Q/Z coefficient passage spelled out. No novelty is claimed. The finite-coefficient picture is Z/2→(Z/4)/(2Z/4), with every Z/2→Z/4 killed by the quotient projection.

## Outcome and gap

The naive universal equality of all three constructions after mere quotient projection is false. This does not determine beta_1(s): both possibilities in the order-two target remain consistent with this argument. It also does not classify comparisons using coefficient reduction or multiplication. Those distinctions are essential to the original, open-ended request.
