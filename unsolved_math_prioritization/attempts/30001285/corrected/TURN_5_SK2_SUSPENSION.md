# Author approach 5: products, suspension, and the normalization barrier

Aim: use the relation between SK_1 and SK_2 to constrain a comparison, while keeping the extra field hypothesis and the signs visible.

Assume throughout that F contains an algebraically closed subfield and ind(A) is prime to char(F). The same is true of F(t). Only the two families beta_i and sigma_1^i are under consideration. No third SK_2 invariant is postulated.

## An unconditional K-theoretic suspension

Write P_a(x)=x·{a} for the **right** K_1(F)-module product, where x∈SK_1(A). Compatibility with reduced norms gives P_a(x)∈SK_2(A). Let partial_K be the localization residue normalized by

    partial_K({t}·x)=x

for x extended from F. The graded interchange rule in K-theory is x·{t}=-{t}·x, so

    partial_K(P_t(x))=-x.

Consequently P_t:SK_1(A)→SK_2(A_{F(t)}) is injective, with left inverse -partial_K. Equivalently the left-product suspension {t}·x has left inverse partial_K. This recovers the direct-summand observation in Kahn's introduction, without silently changing product order.

## A precise, conditional comparison lemma

Let f_i,g_i:SK_i(A_L)→Q_{i,1}(L), i=1,2, be pairs of natural homomorphisms over extensions L/F. Impose the following **explicit common normalization hypothesis**:

    f_2(P_a(x))=f_1(x) cup {a},
    g_2(P_a(x))=g_1(x) cup {a}.                     (H)

Here the products on the right are the ordinary right cup-products in Q/Z-valued Galois cohomology; they descend to the quotient because the Milnor denominator is stable under multiplication by {a}. Put delta_i=f_i-g_i. Subtraction gives

    delta_2(P_a(x))=delta_1(x) cup {a}.             (1)

The cohomological residue, normalized with the uniformizer first, descends to

    partial_H:Q_{2,1}(F(t))→Q_{1,1}(F).

Indeed [A] is unramified, and residues send [A] cup K_3^M into [A] cup K_2^M, with a possible overall sign that does not change the denominator subgroup. If b is extended from Q_{1,1}(F), its cohomological degree is four, and graded commutativity gives

    partial_H(b cup {t})=partial_H({t} cup b)=b.

Applying this to (1) yields

    partial_H(delta_2(P_t(x)))=delta_1(x).          (2)

Thus, **under (H)**:

* equality of f_1 and g_1 implies equality of f_2 and g_2 on the subgroup generated by the products P_a(x);
* equality of f_2 and g_2 over F(t) implies equality of f_1 and g_1 over F;
* a nonzero difference on SK_1 produces a nonzero difference on the suspended element in SK_2 over F(t).

These assertions are proved by (1)–(2), not by an assumption that products generate all SK_2. They also do not assert that partial_H and the selected partial_K form an unsigned naturality square: the right suspension has the signs stated above.

## Why applying (H) requires care

Kahn Proposition 7.7 proves product compatibility for the geometric family in the product conventions of that paper; Proposition 7.8 proves residue compatibility in its residue conventions. Kahn–Levine §6.9 supplies a module spectral sequence for beta. These are strong structural inputs, but the passage between integral étale-motivic and Q/Z targets is a degree-one connecting map, so arbitrary left/right replacements are unsafe.

For the usual cohomological connecting-map convention, if alpha has degree one and b is Q/Z-valued, then

    delta(alpha cup b)=-alpha cup delta(b),
    delta(b cup alpha)=delta(b) cup alpha.

The recovered draft had written an unsigned formula with left products in both K-theory and Q/Z cohomology without establishing that its coefficient and residue identifications implemented that convention. This freeze does not certify that unverified formula. The formal comparison lemma above has the fully stated hypothesis (H); a common normalization of beta and sigma satisfying (H) is a remaining verification task here. We do not change a literature invariant by a sign and then silently call it the original invariant.

Even after this normalization task, the decomposable subgroup need not exhaust SK_2. Kahn's introduction recalls the Calmès symbol, whose image is invisible to residues. That observation is used only to reject an unjustified generation step, not to assert a nonzero beta-versus-sigma difference.

## Outcome and gap

The K-theory direct summand and the conditional comparison lemma are rigorous. The common sign/edge normalization for applying (H) to beta_i and sigma_1^i is not certified by this packet, and the indecomposable part is uncontrolled. The Q_5 example from approach 1 compares sigma_1 with a projection of c_A, not beta with sigma_1; it also lacks the algebraically closed-subfield hypothesis. It cannot furnish an SK_2 counterexample here.
