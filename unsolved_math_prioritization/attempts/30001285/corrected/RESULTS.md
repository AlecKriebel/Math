# Results and unresolved comparison

Problem 30001285 / OWR-3481-002, rank 972. Author disposition: **unsolved**, after five substantive approaches. This is a partial mathematical investigation, not a new resolution or a claim of historical priority.

## Established partial conclusions

1. **The universal projection identity is false.** For A=(2,x)_2 tensor (5,y)_2 over Q_5((x))((y)), sigma_1^1 cannot be the projection of any homomorphism SK_1(A)→H^4(F,Q/Z(3)). The obstruction is a nonsplit pullback extension, detected by theta_2=1/2∈D/2D. This reconstructs the known example in Wouters Proposition 4.2, with an explicit local-field choice. It is not a new counterexample to the whole source-level question.
2. **A different comparison is known.** Kahn Theorem E gives c_A=sigma_2^1 for exp(A)=2<ind(A), with no denominator. In the explicit example, multiplication by 2 carries sigma_1^1 to c_A although quotient projection of c_A is zero. The named coefficient arrow is essential.
3. **Lifting and multiplier tests are exact.** A homomorphic lift is equivalent to splitting the pullback extension. For cyclic sources the theta_n obstruction is also sufficient. If eD=0, the kernel of mu_e:B/D→B is B[e]/D. Hence scalar agreement in the universal unquotiented invariant group may leave a nonzero quotient-valued difference.
4. **Prime-to-index descent can detect equality.** Restrictions to finite extensions with degrees jointly coprime to the source annihilator detect a common-target difference, by restriction–corestriction and Bezout. Splitting fields do not meet this test in general. Their vanishing supplies no general comparison.
5. **Generic evaluation gives a precise missing class.** For G=SL_1(A), K=F(G), and eta the generic element, beta_1=sigma_1^1 as field-valued invariants if and only if their difference vanishes on eta in Q_(1,1)(K). This applies Merkurjev's generic-evaluation theorem to the bounded-torsion quotient cycle module. The class has not been computed.
6. **The K-theory suspension is injective.** SK_1(A) embeds as a summand of SK_2(A_(F(t))). Approach 5 proves a conditional transfer of comparisons for two pairs of product-compatible invariants with an explicitly shared normalization. The required common sign/edge convention for applying that lemma to beta and sigma is not certified in this packet, and the indecomposable SK_2 part remains uncontrolled.
7. **Some equality cases are vacuous.** If the index is square-free, SK_1(A)=SK_2(A)=0 by the Wang and Kahn–Levine/Merkurjev–Suslin results recalled as Theorems 1 and 6 in Kahn's introduction, using the now-established norm-residue theorem. All defined maps from these groups are zero. For SK_2 we continue to impose the field hypothesis of the source report when discussing its displayed constructions. No nonvacuous general comparison with beta is proved here.

## What is still missing

The central missing calculation is beta_1 versus sigma_1^1. More generally the full comparison among spectral-sequence maps, geometric maps for different r, and the SL_1 construction remains unsettled by this investigation. The work neither supplies a filtered comparison morphism nor computes the generic difference class. It does not classify all equality cases or prove general injectivity, and it does not assert a third SL_1-based SK_2 map.

The source's open-ended request is not equivalent to the stronger, false assertion that all maps agree after ordinary quotient projection. A known negative answer to that single assertion is useful partial information, not a resolution of the requested comparison.
