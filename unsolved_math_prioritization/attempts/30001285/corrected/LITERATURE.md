## Sources that change the disposition

- Bruno Kahn, *SK1 and SK2 of division algebras*, in [Oberwolfach Report 31/2009](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2009/31), pp. 1754–1755: fixes the field, division degree, r-dependent quotient targets and the open-ended comparison question. The report requires an algebraically closed subfield for the SK_2 display. The integral Brauer degree is three. Page 1754 was visually inspected and both pages were inspected through extracted text.
- Bruno Kahn, [*Cohomological Approaches to SK1 and SK2 of Central Simple Algebras*](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/27499?nt=1), Documenta Mathematica, Extra Volume Suslin (2010), pp. 317–369: the introduction distinguishes the three constructions; §§7.B–7.D define and refine the geometric maps; §7.C identifies the r=1 and degree-four classical cases; Theorem 10.7 gives the universal SL_1 invariant; Remark 10.8 and Lemma 10.9 limit normalization under extension; Theorem E gives the exponent-two comparison; Remark 10.12, footnote 4, credits Wouters's negative projection answer. The relevant sections were inspected in extracted text; printed p. 356 was visually inspected.
- Bruno Kahn and Marc Levine, [*Motives of Azumaya algebras*](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bruno.kahn/preprints/Azumaya-final.pdf), J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 9(3) (2010), 481–599, §6.9: fixes the spectral-sequence indexing, its module structure, the Brauer-valued d_2, and the extra d_3 issue for SK_2. Those computations identify targets; they do not by themselves compare the invariant values. The section was inspected in extracted text.
- Tim Wouters, [*Comparing invariants of SK1*, arXiv:1003.1654v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.1654v2), 18 March 2010: §2.2 gives the Platonov and residue models; §4.1 distinguishes multiplier and projection; Proposition 4.2 proves nonsurjectivity of projection on invariants. The relevant sections were inspected in extracted text and PDF p. 18 was visually inspected. This preprint's numbering is used throughout. The later journal article is [Algebras and Representation Theory 16 (2013), 729–745](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10468-011-9327-x); the final full text was not inspected.

## Source precision and limits

The negative projection theorem and the exponent-two positive comparison are credited to the literature. The finite-group controls in this packet test the written algebraic lemmas and coefficient models; they do not independently establish Platonov's theorem, Suslin/Rost injectivity, the norm-residue theorem, cycle-module generic evaluation, or spectral-sequence naturality. The common product normalization discussed in approach 5 remains an explicit gap rather than an attributed theorem of either paper.
