# Credited literal correction, not the quantitative resolution

Let r_A(x) count distinct finite subsets of the infinite logarithmic random set A whose sum is x. For fixed nonnegative integer x, every such subset is contained in A intersect [1,x], so r_A(x)<=2^x<infinity. There is one empty representation of zero; negative integers have none.

Ford–Green–Koukoulopoulos's published lower bounds, for example Theorem2 combined with Lemma2.1, imply that the finite-prefix maximum M(D) tends to infinity in probability. This already gives the literal conclusion without independence between different D. For fixed integer K, the event {sup_D M(D)<K} is contained in {M(D)<K} for every D, so its probability is at most a quantity tending to zero. A countable intersection over K shows sup_D M(D)=infinity almost surely. Every finite subset lies in some prefix, hence sup_x r_A(x)=sup_D M(D)=infinity almost surely.

Thus the collection of finite values r_A(x) is unbounded almost surely, and has no finite attained maximum. This is an elementary consequence of credited published results, not a new full solution to the intended finite-prefix growth question. It does not identify the rate of M(D), its leading logarithmic exponent or its limiting fluctuations.
