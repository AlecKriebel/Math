# 30003533: scoped partial results; original question unresolved

Recommended queue status: **unsolved, 5/5**.

The exact source is Valery Smyshlyaev's contribution in *Material Theories*, Oberwolfach Report 33/2017, pp.2083–2086; the question is on p.2085. Despite the broad workshop title, the citation is correct. The operator is the standard direct combined-field operator (1/2)I+D′_k−ikηS_k on complex L² of the boundary, for a fixed positive coupling factor η. It is not the modified star-combined operator.

Five substantive mathematical approaches are developed in PROOFS.md. Their retained conclusions are:

1. A variable positive normal boundary multiplier still inherits any negative curvature; an explicit smooth strictly-star-shaped planar example has curvature −8/3 at a specified point.
2. Smooth three-dimensional boundary deformation changes the unitarily identified operator by at most Ct(1+k²); this transfers coercivity only on a shrinking sufficient deformation scale.
3. An exact 2×2 example has uniform invertibility and a uniformly equivalent coercive metric but zero numerical-range distance in the original norm. A quantitative near-scalar transfer criterion identifies the missing estimate.
4. A nilpotent contractive boundary-interaction model has a sharp finite-escape gap, with a polynomial bound if the number of steps grows polynomially. Acyclicity without contraction is insufficient.
5. An exact block/tail criterion can certify coercivity for compact perturbations of half the identity. Compactness and positive fixed-frequency gaps do not impose polynomial frequency dependence.

No route proves high-frequency coercivity for an arbitrary fixed smooth nonconvex star-shaped obstacle. No smooth counterexample to the allowed polynomial-loss formulation is obtained. Matrix models are logical controls, not scattering-domain counterexamples; geometric lemmas and finite checks do not certify the missing PDE estimates.

The known smooth nontrapping counterexample only forces an O(k^−1) upper bound along a frequency sequence. The known star-shaped open-book counterexamples have nonsmooth polyhedral boundaries. Neither is promoted to a solution of the smooth polynomial-loss question. These results belong to their cited authors.

The source's displayed liminf rate has a free k on its right side and its phrase “strongly nontrapping” is not defined. The packet explicitly records these defects, uses the preceding prose to formulate a polynomial lower-bound interpretation, and separately identifies the smooth strictly-star-shaped core class. It does not claim that the source specifies uniform constants over all domains or all positive coupling factors.
