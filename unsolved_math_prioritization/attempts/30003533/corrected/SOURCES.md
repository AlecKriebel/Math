# Primary sources and qualification

Checked 8 October 2026. The complete retrieval/inspection manifest is SOURCE_METADATA.json. Downloaded PDFs, extracted source text and page images are excluded from this packet. Downloading a paper is not represented as verifying all its proofs.

1. Valery Smyshlyaev, “On coercivity of boundary integral functionals in high-frequency scattering,” pp.2083–2086 in [Material Theories, OWR 33/2017](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2017/33), published 2018. [Official PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46698?nt=1). Equation (2), p.2084, gives the direct operator with coupling kη; (3) gives L² modulus coercivity. The question is on p.2085. Its informal geometric class and malformed liminf display are recorded explicitly in PROOFS.md. The operator is not redefined from a later schematic kernel line.

2. E. A. Spence, I. V. Kamotski and V. P. Smyshlyaev, [Coercivity of combined boundary integral equations in high-frequency scattering](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.21543), CPAM 68 (2015), 1587–1639. The [author preprint](https://people.bath.ac.uk/eas25/SpKaSm13.pdf), dated 5 February 2014, was inspected. Theorem 1.2 assumes convexity, strictly positive curvature, C³ boundary and piecewise analyticity. For sufficiently large positive coupling proportional to k, its real-part coercivity approaches 1/2. The constant-normal-multiplier obstruction on nonconvex domains is already Lemma 5.1. Our variable-weight boundary differentiation is an elementary refinement/reconstruction, not a novelty claim. The abstract's broad “smooth convex” wording must not erase the theorem's explicit hypotheses.

3. E. A. Spence, S. N. Chandler-Wilde, I. G. Graham and V. P. Smyshlyaev, [A new frequency-uniform coercive boundary integral equation for acoustic scattering](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.20378), CPAM 64 (2011), 1384–1415. Credited through the inspected OWR and 2015 discussions: the positive star-shaped result uses a modified star-combined operator. This investigation did not download/re-audit the complete 2011 article, and it does not transfer its conclusion to the standard operator by name similarity.

4. S. N. Chandler-Wilde, E. A. Spence, A. Gibbs and V. P. Smyshlyaev, [High-frequency bounds for the Helmholtz equation under parabolic trapping and applications in numerical analysis](https://doi.org/10.1137/18M1234916), SIAM J. Math. Anal. 52 (2020), 845–893. [arXiv version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08415); [v5 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08415v5). The 2017 version's Remark 6.6 becomes Section 6.3.2 in v5. It constructs smooth nontrapping obstacles with numerical-range distance at most C/k along k=mπ/a, for coupling of order k. It explicitly leaves fixed-frequency coercivity possible. Thus it refutes a uniform lower bound on that larger class, not a polynomial lower bound with exponent at least one on the same sequence. The nontrapping definition uses a uniform exit time for generalized billiard rays, not just absence of one closed orbit. Inverse bounds are stronger than mere invertibility but weaker than coercivity.

5. S. N. Chandler-Wilde and E. A. Spence, [Coercivity, essential norms, and the Galerkin method for second-kind integral equations on polyhedral and Lipschitz domains](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-021-01256-x), Numer. Math. 150 (2022), 299–371. Corollary 1.6 supplies star-shaped open-book polyhedra in three dimensions for which the standard Helmholtz operator is not even coercive plus compact, at any k>0 and η>0. The boundaries are Lipschitz polyhedral. Smoothing them does not preserve an unproved high-frequency zero-gap statement automatically. The [2023 correction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-023-01357-9) repairs localization statements and related passages; its full listed corrections were read. Corollary 1.6 is not withdrawn. We cite this credited theorem, not a fresh proof of its polyhedral construction.

6. J. Galkowski and E. A. Spence, [Numerical analysis of the high-frequency Helmholtz equation using semiclassical analysis](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492925100275), Acta Numerica 35 (2026), 1–171. The published PDF was downloaded and searched, and the introductory BEM comparison was inspected. The survey's coercive modified volume forms, Galerkin quasioptimality results and pollution estimates do not, by themselves, assert the requested numerical-range bound for the unmodified boundary operator. This targeted check is not an exhaustive review or a proof that no newer solution exists.

7. Current publication listings of [Euan Spence](https://euanspence.github.io/papers2.html) and [Jeffrey Galkowski](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucahalk/research.html) were checked for closely related current work. A 2024 coercive second-kind result concerns Laplace's equation and new formulations; it is not silently used as a high-frequency standard Helmholtz theorem.

Standard elementary operator facts used in the authored proofs include the Schur test, spectral square roots of bounded positive operators, and the Toeplitz–Hausdorff theorem. The nilpotent-contraction bound is a classical operator-theoretic phenomenon; its needed version is fully reconstructed in Proposition 4.1. These are not claimed as new results.
