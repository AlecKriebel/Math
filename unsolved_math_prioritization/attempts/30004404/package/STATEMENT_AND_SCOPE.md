# Source and quantifier audit

## Original source

The exact primary source is Kimihiko Motegi's contribution with Tetsuya Ito and Masakazu Teragaito, “Dehn fillings on knot groups,” in Low-dimensional Topology, Oberwolfach Report 8/2020, pp.491–493, DOI 10.4171/owr/2020/8. The workshop report bears year 2020; the complete PDF was produced in 2021. The numbered website identifier OWR-17471-010 is a catalogue label, not a printed question number.

Printed p.491 identifies a slope with p/q∈Q∪{∞} in the standard meridian–longitude coordinates, and identifies its normal closure with the kernel of the filling homomorphism. Printed p.493 asks, for a hyperbolic K without finite surgery, for a rank-two free H whose intersection with the union of all rational slope normal closures is trivial. The equation and question were visually checked in the primary PDF. No slope except ∞ is omitted; no “all but finitely many” qualification appears.

## Consequences of the literal quantifiers

The assertion is universally quantified over eligible K. To refute it, it suffices to produce one eligible K for which every H≅F₂ fails. Our witness K is 4₁. For every such H, the explicit second-derived word in a free basis belongs to N₀∩H and is nontrivial. One does not need a single ambient word working for all choices of H.

The union condition is equivalent to injectivity of each separate filling restriction. It is much stronger than avoiding the intersection of all kernels; joint injectivity into the product of fillings does not suffice. Nor is it enough that two generators individually survive.

“Finite surgery” concerns finite fundamental group. The zero filling has an infinite group Z²⋊Z and is therefore not a finite surgery despite being solvable. Its presence does not violate the hypothesis. Meridional filling is the slope ∞, which the source excludes; zero is longitude filling and remains part of the target.

## 2026 comparison

The definition in §7.1 of arXiv:2604.01697v1 again requires all nonidentity elements to survive all nonmeridional fillings. Its Question 7.1 is phrased for a hyperbolic knot and is preceded by a statement that no hyperbolic examples are known. This does not remove zero filling from the definition. A universal positive reading is ruled out by the present example. The separate problem of finding even one hyperbolic knot with a persistent F₂ is not resolved by a negative example.
