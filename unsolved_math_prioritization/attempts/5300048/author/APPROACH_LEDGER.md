# Five mathematical approaches and the remaining gaps

This accounting concerns mathematical work. Source retrieval, problem matching,
recovery, checking, packaging and external review are not additional approaches.
The five approaches below are all present in `PROOF.md`; each develops an
argument or explicit construction and identifies its remaining obstruction.

## Approach one: realize a neutral inaccessible point globally

Work: classify boundary multipliers; transfer inaccessibility through nested
compacta; test the direct complement-of-hedgehog construction.

Authored deductions: Lemmas 1–3. An attracting or linearizable indifferent
boundary point contradicts attraction inside U. Inaccessibility persists on
passing to smaller complementary domains. A univalent invariant local map on
an empty-interior compactum cannot extend to a degree-at-least-two proper
self-map of its full spherical complement.

Imported input: Biswas's local inaccessible hedgehog, version 4, Theorem 1.2.
The red-dwarf route supplies a conditional conclusion, while the separate
existence theorem supplies solar examples.

Gap: no admissible polynomial/rational realization of the needed inaccessible
periodic point. The local compactum must fit a global filled Julia set or
another domain whose extended proper map meets all hypotheses.

## Approach two: exclude competing inverse-basin components

Work: count preimage components by rational-map degree, and convert boundary
separation to preservation by the contracting inverse return branch.

Authored deduction: Lemma 4. If a repelling return point is on no competing
component boundary, its inverse branch locally preserves U.

Imported conclusion: Corollary 5 applies Przytycki's Theorem A, with every
good-point hypothesis verified in the proof. It is not a new proof of that
accessibility theorem.

Gap: competing components may share the same periodic boundary point. Their
finiteness alone does not separate them. The argument does not address neutral
points or arbitrary local-holomorphic extensions outside the rational case.

## Approach three: control intrinsic connecting curves

Work: derive a hyperbolic-ball diameter bound and concatenate geodesics joining
a sequence approaching the requested boundary point.

Authored deductions: Lemma 6, Proposition 7, Corollary 8. Accessibility follows
from exp(2R_n)|z_n-q| tending to zero; geometric ambient decay tolerates a
specified linear growth rate of the intrinsic steps. The disk monomial is a
positive control. Schwarz–Pick's direction along a backward orbit is explicit.

Imported input: the classical univalent growth theorem and Koebe quarter theorem.

Gap: an appropriate orbit in the designated basin and an intrinsic upper bound
have not been obtained for an arbitrary periodic point.

## Approach four: find a periodic prime-end address

Work: construct the nonempty compact forward-invariant cluster-address set;
then test whether expansion alone forces a periodic address.

Authored deductions: Lemma 9 and Proposition 10. Irrational mechanical words
produce a compact invariant aperiodic set for doubling, so those circle-dynamic
properties alone do not suffice.

Gap: no additional analytic/planar property forces a periodic address for the
specified q. Even a periodic address whose impression contains q need not have
q as its radial landing point. The doubling example is not claimed to be the
address set of a holomorphic basin.

## Approach five: transport z² into an inaccessible domain

Work: prove the slit comb is simply connected and has an inaccessible boundary
point; construct its proper attracting degree-two map by conformal transport;
then examine whether such a map can extend across the closure.

Authored deductions: Lemma 11 and Proposition 12. Properness and continuity
force boundary preservation. Baire's theorem and real-analytic uniqueness send
each slit interval into one supporting line. Infinitely many accumulating
vertical source slits then make the derivative real-valued or imaginary-valued
on an open set. Holomorphic openness and continuation force an affine map.

Gap: the degree-two transport fails the holomorphic-extension hypothesis.
Also, without such an extension the displayed inaccessible boundary point is
not asserted to be a periodic point of that transported map. No dynamical
counterexample to the original question has been constructed.
