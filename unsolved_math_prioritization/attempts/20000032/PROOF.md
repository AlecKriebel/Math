# A compact-generation obstruction for a-inverted real Artin–Tate spectra

## Outcome and scope

This is a structural partial result for AIM Problem 3.2, corpus ID 20000032 / AIM-ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY-0032. It does not identify the intrinsic deformation ring or stack requested by the problem. The proof below is an explicit consequence of Burklund–Hahn–Senger (BHS), rather than a claimed new solution or a claimed high-novelty theorem.

**Theorem.** Both the BHS `i2` a-inverted Artin–Tate category and its derived 2-completion require infinitely many compact generators. A countable set suffices, but no finite set does. Consequently, neither category is equivalent, even without a monoidal compatibility requirement, to the category of modules over any ordinary associative ring spectrum. In particular, an affine spectral-scheme model or a one-object unfiltered spectral-algebra model cannot answer the problem.

“Ordinary” here means a ring object in spectra. Modules over an algebra in filtered spectra, graded spectra, or a many-object spectral category are not excluded. The BHS filtered model remains valid.

## 1. Exact categories and notation

Let C₀ = SH(R)^AT, the presentably symmetric monoidal stable category of Artin–Tate R-motivic spectra. Write 1₂ for the derived 2-completion of its unit. Following BHS's conventions (arXiv v2, p.16), put

C = Mod_{C₀}(1₂) = SH(R)^AT_i2.

This is a category of modules over the completed unit, with relative tensor product. It is not the full subcategory of derived 2-complete objects. In C, the free 1₂-module trigraded spheres S₂^{r,q,w} are compact, dualizable generators. Their ordinary suspension coordinate is r.

The two maps in use are

a : S₂^{0,-1,0} → 1₂,     ta : S₂^{0,0,-1} → 1₂.

They are different maps. The first comes from the Artin orbit cofiber sequence; the second is the BHS deformation parameter. Define

A = C[a⁻¹] = Mod_C(1₂[a⁻¹]).

This is the smashing symmetric monoidal localization at a. Define

T_w = S₂^{0,0,w}[a⁻¹],     B = Cta[a⁻¹] = cofib(T_{-1} → T_0).

The symbol Map denotes an ordinary mapping spectrum, rather than a C₂-enriched mapping object. Thus

π_k Map_A(T_w, X) = [S₂^{k,0,w}[a⁻¹], X].

Finally define D = A₂^∧ to be the derived 2-completion of A: the full subcategory right-orthogonal to all objects Z on which multiplication by 2 is invertible. Its reflector is denoted L₂. Its colimits and tensor products are obtained by applying L₂ to those in A. This order of construction is part of the definition. No assertion that a-inversion commutes with derived 2-completion is needed.

## 2. Inputs from BHS

Only the following mathematical inputs are used.

1. The trigraded spheres compactly generate C₀ (BHS, pp.5–6), and BHS's i2 convention gives the compact free module spheres in C (p.16).
2. BHS Corollary 7.1 (p.53) gives B the structure of a nonzero commutative algebra and computes its trigraded homotopy groups. At weight zero the computation includes π_{0,0,0}B = F₂.
3. BHS Theorem 10.1(8) (p.62) states, already after a-inversion, that π_{p,q,w}B vanishes unless p ≥ 0 and w ≥ 0. We need only the implication w < 0 ⇒ π_{p,q,w}B = 0 for all p,q.

The value π_{0,0,0}B = F₂ can also be read directly from Corollary 7.1: at w = 0, the normalized BP cobar complex has no positive cohomological-degree term of internal degree zero, since its augmentation-ideal generators have strictly positive internal degree. Its degree-zero term is F₂. In the remaining polynomial factor π_*Φ^{C₂}HZ₂ = F₂[y], |y| = 2, only y⁰ contributes to p = 0.

The motivic comparison and Ext formula are inputs from BHS, not independently reconstructed motivic calculations. The argument from these inputs to the obstruction is given in full below. The current journal version has the same conventions at journal p.1643 (PDF p.20), the same Corollary 7.1 at journal p.1688 (PDF p.65), and the same negative-weight vanishing as Theorem 10.1(viii) at journal p.1699 (PDF p.76).

## 3. Compact generators after a-inversion

The a-local objects in C are those for which a acts invertibly. Since a is a map between invertible objects, the subcategory of a-local objects is closed under colimits computed in C. Hence the inclusion A → C preserves filtered colimits. By adjunction, the images in A of the compact spheres of C remain compact. They generate A because the inclusion is fully faithful and conservative.

In A, a identifies the q-shift with the identity. Therefore

{T_w : w ∈ Z}

is a countable set of compact generators, with all ordinary suspensions understood.

We record the finite-support fact used below. If a stable presentable category is compactly generated by a set G of compact objects, its compact objects are the thick closure of G. Each particular object in that thick closure uses only finitely many members of G: finite sums, cofibers, suspensions and retracts cannot introduce infinitely many generators. One justification is to identify the category with the Ind-completion of the idempotent-complete stable subcategory generated by G. Compactness makes an object a retract of a finite-stage object in this Ind-presentation. This fact concerns ordinary thick closure, not closure under arbitrary tensor products.

Thus, for every P ∈ A^ω, there is a finite F ⊂ Z with

P ∈ thick{T_w : w ∈ F}.

## 4. The orthogonality witness

For any integer N, put B(N) = T_N ⊗ B. It is nonzero because T_N is invertible and B is nonzero. For w < N and every integer k,

π_k Map_A(T_w, B(N))
 = [S₂^{k,0,w-N}[a⁻¹], B]
 = π_{k,0,w-N}B
 = 0.

The last equality is exactly the negative-weight vanishing in §2. The index is w−N, not N−w. A spectrum whose homotopy groups all vanish is zero, so

Map_A(T_w, B(N)) ≃ 0 whenever w < N.                       (1)

Now let P ∈ A^ω and choose finite F as in §3. Choose N > max(F), taking any N if F is empty. The class of objects Y satisfying Map_A(Y, B(N)) ≃ 0 is closed under finite sums, cofibers, suspensions and retracts. Equation (1) therefore implies

Map_A(P, B(N)) ≃ 0,     B(N) ≠ 0.                        (2)

A generator cannot have a nonzero object in its right orthogonal. Thus no compact P generates A. A finite collection of compact objects has compact direct sum, so no finite collection generates A either. The countable collection in §3 proves that the minimum cardinality of a compact generating set is exactly ℵ₀.

The witnesses can themselves be chosen compact in A: B is the cofiber of a map between compact invertibles, and tensoring by T_N preserves compactness. This stronger observation is not needed for the completed-category argument.

## 5. Derived 2-completion: compact Moore generators

We give details because the completed unit need not be compact in D. Set

H_w = cofib(2 : T_w → T_w) = (S/2) ⊗ T_w.

### Lemma 5.1: H_w is already derived 2-complete

In any stable category, if M is the cofiber of 2 : X → X, then 4·id_M = 0 in the homotopy category. Indeed, in the triangle X →₂ X →ᵢ M →ᵟ ΣX, the equality (2·id_M)i = i·2 = 0 shows that 2·id_M factors through δ. Since (Σ2)δ = 0, a second multiplication by 2 makes that factorization zero.

If Z is 2-invertible, multiplication by 2 acts invertibly on Map(Z,M), using the source. Multiplication by 4 acts trivially, using the target. It follows that Map(Z,M) is zero. This proves the claimed completeness of H_w.

### Lemma 5.2: {H_w} compactly generates D

A completion unit X → L₂X is a mod-2 equivalence: its cofiber is 2-invertible, so tensoring it with S/2 gives an equivalence. It follows that

Map_A(H_w, X) → Map_A(H_w, L₂X)

is an equivalence. One may see this by writing H_w = (S/2)⊗T_w and using duality of the finite Moore spectrum, whose dual is Σ⁻¹(S/2).

For a filtered diagram {X_i} in D, therefore,

Map_D(H_w, colim_D X_i)
 ≃ Map_A(H_w, L₂ colim_A X_i)
 ≃ Map_A(H_w, colim_A X_i)
 ≃ colim_i Map_A(H_w, X_i).

The last equivalence uses compactness of H_w in A. Thus H_w is compact in D.

If all Map_D(H_w,Y) vanish, the cofiber sequence defining H_w shows that multiplication by 2 is an equivalence on every Map_A(T_w,Y). Since the T_w generate A, 2:Y→Y is an equivalence. A complete object which is also 2-invertible is zero. This proves generation.

### Lemma 5.3: B(N) is already complete and nonzero in D

B is a commutative algebra and π₀ Map_A(T₀,B) = F₂. The element 2 times its unit is null. Multiplication by this element on the B-module B is the scalar map 2·id_B, so 2·id_B is null. The same holds for B(N). The argument of Lemma 5.1 now shows that B(N) is complete. Since D is a full subcategory, B(N) remains nonzero there.

### Conclusion for D

Every compact P ∈ D lies in thick{H_w : w ∈ F} for some finite F. Choose N > max(F). Equation (1), followed by the exact mapping sequence for H_w, gives

Map_D(H_w, B(N)) ≃ 0 for all w ∈ F.

Consequently Map_D(P,B(N)) ≃ 0, with B(N) nonzero by Lemma 5.3. Thus D has no compact generator and no finite set of compact generators. Lemma 5.2 provides a countable set, so again the minimum cardinality is ℵ₀.

## 6. Obstruction to ordinary module models

For an associative ring spectrum R, the category Mod_R has the compact generator R: mapping out of R is the underlying-spectrum functor, which preserves filtered colimits and detects zero objects. An equivalence of presentable stable categories preserves colimits, compactness and generation. The preceding theorems therefore rule out

A ≃ Mod_R   and   D ≃ Mod_R

for every such R. This argument needs no monoidal compatibility. It also excludes module categories over spectral categories with finitely many objects, since the finite direct sum of their representable modules is a compact generator.

A spectral affine scheme has quasi-coherent category Mod_R for its commutative ring spectrum R, so that particular kind of geometric recognition is excluded. No claim about all stacks or non-affine geometric models is made.

## 7. What remains unresolved

The theorem does not reconstruct the filtered coefficient ring Φ^{C₂}R_•, compute its higher operations, identify a familiar stack, or resolve the comparison functors at the algebraic special fiber. It does not classify a-inverted motives of arbitrary real varieties. It does not address the literal integral category by arithmetic gluing.

The BHS equivalence with modules over Φ^{C₂}R_• in filtered spectra is compatible with this obstruction: filtered spectra themselves need infinitely many compact weight generators. Treating the filtered ring as an unnamed coefficient algebra is a valid presentation but not the sought intrinsic recognition.

No novelty claim is certified. The argument is a short formal consequence of published BHS calculations; its value here is an explicit, checked restriction on possible answers and a careful separation of i2-linear and genuinely completed categories.
