## Proved scoped results, pending that review

1. **First-moment process-law classification.** Under X log X, the finite-dimensional smoothing transform has exactly one integrable fixed law with all coordinate means1; arbitrary integrable mean-one seeds converge to it in first Wasserstein distance. The original sequence converges in expected local L1-function norm. General finite-mean fixed laws are classified by their coordinate mean profile.
2. **Functional sufficient theorem.** Local X log(e+X)^(2+epsilon) moments, for any interval-dependent epsilon>0, imply almost-sure locally uniform and expected-supremum convergence to a càdlàg process. No variance, moment above one, or increment Hölder assumption is imposed. A preliminary p>1 theorem and a bounded clustered-jump example outside the source's increment hypothesis are preserved.
3. **Exact X log X endpoint controls.** Active-family envelopes vanish uniformly, actual consecutive supremum increments are almost-sure square summable, and scalar expected temporal maxima tend to zero uniformly in the parameter. The latter keeps the parameter supremum outside expectation. Uniform integrability and first-moment equicontinuity follow.
4. **Exact endpoint jump alignment.** All generations' jumps have a common square-summable genealogical envelope almost surely, and their jump-size vectors converge in l2. All macroscopic jump locations and sizes therefore stabilize. Cumulative small-jump oscillations against the normalizing drift remain uncontrolled.
5. **Geometric joint-law partial.** A rational continued-fraction enclosure computes the true bivariate Laplace transform with geometric error. A third mixed moment excludes an independent-increment/subordinator candidate that matches every marginal and the covariance kernel. This is not a proof of non-Markovianity or a simple representation of the entire process.
