The primary statement was text-extracted and visually checked on the original page. It concerns finite groups; X contains only groups of odd order and is closed under subgroups, quotients, and extensions. The normal subgroup N is arbitrary, and H is maximal by inclusion among X-subgroups of G. The target is maximality of H∩N within N. In particular, neither H nor N is assumed to be a Hall subgroup, and G is not assumed solvable.

The October source leaves 21.57 unstarred. This is separate from neighboring problems and from all older-edition numbering. ## Sources and their precise roles

1. W. Guo, D. O. Revin, E. P. Vdovin, [The reduction theorem for relatively maximal subgroups, arXiv:1808.10107v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10107v2), revised 2020. The PDF was inspected. Sections 1.1, 1.2, and 1.4 supply the complete-class framework, normal/subnormal submaximal terminology, the credited Wielandt–Hartley result, and the known PGL₂(7) even-prime example. The current v2 title differs from the earlier v1 title. Quotient theorems and stronger claims are not silently imported beyond their hypotheses.
2. W. Guo and D. O. Revin, [Classification and properties of the pi-submaximal subgroups in minimal nonsolvable groups](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13373-017-0112-y), Bulletin of Mathematical Sciences 8 (2018), 325–351. The primary full text was inspected. Theorem 1.1 and Tables 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 identify the odd-pi minimal-simple cases as pi-maximal. This classification is credited, not independently reproduced.
3. X. Zhang, L. Su, D. O. Revin, [An example of a relatively maximal nonpronormal subgroup of odd order in a finite simple group](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/smj7876), Sibirsk. Mat. Zh. 65 (2024), 596–600; English version Siberian Mathematical Journal 65 (2024), 644–647, DOI 10.1134/S0037446624030133. The primary PDF was read through the web tool. It establishes an adjacent nonpronormality phenomenon; it does not give the normal-intersection counterexample required here.

Hall's theorem for solvable groups and the Feit–Thompson odd-order theorem are standard inputs stated explicitly where used. The general proof steps in these notes are mathematical arguments, not consequences of the small finite computations.
