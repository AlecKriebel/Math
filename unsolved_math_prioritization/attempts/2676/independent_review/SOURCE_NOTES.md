# Independent primary-source audit: KP-1.17 / 2676

Checked 2026-10-01. This review uses the source files bound by the author's ALL_SOURCE_INPUTS.json and separately checks the cited statements. Full source PDFs, images and reading copies are not portable review artifacts.

- **Exact problem:** K3 preliminary 2026 edition, printed p.26, Problem1.17 and all five remarks. The rendered page was inspected. The question is about alternating and nonalternating **links** sharing a branched double cover. Greene's arXiv1103.0487 v1 pp.3–4 calls it Conjecture1.3; K3 identifies the published Conjecture1.4. The 1997 Kirby numbering is different. K3 Remark3 already excludes the Seifert common-cover sector; this is credited background.
- **Finite actions:** Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorems E and H, printed p.1129, give geometric realization for finite actions on elliptic and closed hyperbolic three-manifolds. The argument only geometrizes each involution separately, then places it in the fixed isometry group by Mostow rigidity. It does not apply a finite-action theorem to an unproved-finite group generated by the original actions.
- **Seifert branching:** Mecchia–Reni (2002), printed pp.429–430, explicitly states spherical uniqueness and the nonspherical alternatives for fiber-preserving versus fiber-reversing involutions. It also states that Montesinos descriptions sharing the Seifert cover are related by mutation. K3 independently records the resulting alternation exclusion.
- **Prime reduction:** Hedden–Ni, arXiv0906.4771, Proposition5.1 and its proof on pp.15–16, give the split and connected-sum formulas, irreducibility for nonsplit prime links, and absence of an S1×S2 summand for nonsplit links. Those are all-links statements. The prime-factor matching argument uses three-manifold prime decomposition, not a presumed unique factorization of arbitrary links.
- **Definite surfaces:** Greene, *Alternating links and definite surfaces*, Theorem1.1 and Corollary1.4, give exactly the opposite-definite-spanning-surface criterion and its amphichiral consequence. An arbitrary definite filling is not among those hypotheses. Ozsváth–Szabó's absolute-grading paper gives the correction-term cobordism and negative-definite inequalities used for the homology-ball example. The source and proof both preserve its non-simply-connected and reducible limitations.
- **BGH Seifert edition:** arXiv2402.15914v1, Theorem1.1, printed p.3, applies to prime Seifert links not ADE up to orientation; no strong-quasipositivity hypothesis is used. Its Proposition2.1 identifies the finite ADE groups. The actual v1 printed p.8 image does contain the odd-D homology-order entry discussed by the author. The review independently gets order4 from the Cartan determinant and binary-dihedral abelianization. Nothing is asserted about an inaccessible later publisher table.
- **BGH toroidal edition:** arXiv2106.14378v5, 13 April2026, Theorem2.13 printed p.10 and proof pp.58–59, explicitly excludes L-spaces for the double branched cover of a prime toroidal link in an integer homology sphere with irreducible exterior. Degree2 is a proved assertion. The all-degree conjecture is unnecessary. Branch-exterior toroidality is distinct from toroidality of the closed cover.
- **L-space gluing:** Hanselman–Rasmussen–Watson's complete author manuscript, Theorem1.14, requires incompressible torus boundaries and an orientation-reversing gluing, and uses the union of the **interiors** of the L-space slope intervals. The meridian is a closed-interval endpoint for a right trefoil. Ozsváth–Szabó, AGT11 (2011), Proposition9.6, equation40, printed p.51, verifies the rank formula for positive and negative nonzero numerators; zero and meridional filling are treated separately. HRW Theorem7.20 supplies the already-known small toroidal L-space context.
- **Equivariant JSJ:** Gordon–Luecke, AGT6 (2006), Section2, printed p.2060, explicitly recalls an invariant JSJ decomposition for the covering involution of a prime knot. That recalled statement has no unknotting-number-one assumption, even though other theorems in that paper do. Its discussion of side-exchanging tori reinforces why the author's separate piece-exchange exclusion is needed. The original Meeks–Scott paper was not directly recovered; the exact consequence is stated in this complete primary paper.
- **Local conjugacy:** Sakuma (1985), printed pp.176 and185, was visually checked in the full scan. His equivalence is by an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the pair, and Proposition3.1(1) gives uniqueness for a torus knot's strong inversion. Hirasawa–Hiura–Sakuma, arXiv2206.02097v2, Proposition2.1(2) and its preceding definition, corroborates the same convention. This does not give uniqueness on a general hyperbolic or satellite piece.
- **Pillowcase kernel:** Farb–Margalit, *A Primer on Mapping Class Groups*, Proposition2.7 and proof, printed pp.56–57, was independently read in the accessible full book transcription at https://doczz.net/doc/519948/a-primer-on-mapping-class-groups--pms. It defines the homology-lift map to PSL(2,Z) and proves that its kernel is the four-element half-translation subgroup. The kernel elements are exactly the identity and three double-transposition mutation maps. No local publisher PDF is claimed.
- **Mutation and lens branching:** The precise Menasco mutation consequence and Hodgson–Rubinstein lens-space consequence are explicitly stated in Greene's complete 2011 primary paper, and mutation is independently cited in the exact K3 source. The original full Menasco1984 and Hodgson–Rubinstein1985 papers were not directly recovered in this review. The available Menasco2019 item is an expository article, not the 1984 original. These access distinctions are not replaced by claims of having read those originals.

Primary URLs for the exact editions and all 22 local PDF hashes are retained in ALL_SOURCE_INPUTS.json and HASH_CHECK.json. The review checks theorem applications, rather than reproving the deep external classification and Floer theorems. No later full resolution of the original question is certified by this audit.
