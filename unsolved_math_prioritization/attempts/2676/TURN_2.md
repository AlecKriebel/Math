# Turn 2: homological compatibility does not supply equivariant spanning surfaces

**2676 / KP-1.17. Scoped partial analysis, author turn 2/5. The original mixed-pair question remains unresolved.**

## 1. The exact surface theorem and the proposed transfer

Greene's 2017 Theorem 1.1 characterizes nonsplit alternating links through a pair of embedded spanning surfaces whose Gordon–Litherland pairings are definite with opposite signs. Its general statement assumes a mod-two homology sphere and irreducible complement; in S^3 the complement of a nonsplit link is irreducible. The checkerboard surfaces of an alternating diagram supply the forward direction. Howie's independent 2017 theorem uses spanning surfaces with a sharp Euler-characteristic/boundary-slope condition. For links his meridian markings matter, and his convention excludes the unknot from the term alternating. Here the original K3/Greene convention is retained; the unknot is harmless and was handled separately in Turn 1.

A pair of definite **fillings of the three-manifold** does not itself supply spanning surfaces for an arbitrarily chosen quotient branch link. The following sections make two limits of such an inference explicit: the first-homology action cannot distinguish the deck involutions, and even strong nonequivariant filling data can occur for a manifold with no alternating branch presentation at all.

## 2. Every double-cover deck involution acts as minus identity on H1

**Proposition 1.** Let Y be the double cover of S^3 branched over any nonempty tame link L, and let tau be its deck involution. Then

    tau_* = -id on H1(Y; Z).

There is no knot, nonsplit, finite-homology or odd-torsion restriction.

**Proof.** Take a Wirtinger presentation of the link group using meridian generators m_0,...,m_s. Form the orbifold group

    Gamma = pi1(S^3 minus L) / normal-closure(m_i^2 for all i).

The map sending every meridian to the nontrivial element of C2 descends to Gamma. Its kernel N is pi1(Y): start with the unbranched index-two cover of the exterior and fill along the lifted meridian squares. This is the same quotient by their normal closure, including the conjugates arising from the two cosets.

Set t=m_0, so t^2=1 in Gamma, and use the coset representatives {1,t}. Reidemeister–Schreier rewriting shows that N is generated by a_i=m_i*t and their inverses; equivalently, every even meridian word is a product of these elements after inserting t*t between successive letters. On these generators conjugation by t gives

    t*a_i*t = t*m_i = a_i^(-1).

The action on N itself need not invert every product, because inversion reverses order in a nonabelian group. After abelianization the displayed identities give multiplication by -1 on every generator and hence on all of N_ab=H1(Y;Z). Conjugation by t represents the deck action up to an inner automorphism, which has no effect after abelianization. ∎

For example, the result includes the double cover of a split unlink, whose first homology is free, and even-order torsion, on which -id may act trivially on some elements. The proof does not divide by two or infer an integral statement from rational transfer.

**Corollary 2.** After identifying a common cover Y, every one of its branching involutions has the same action on H1(Y;Z). Thus their actions on ordinary first homology alone cannot distinguish an alternating branch set from a non-alternating one.

This does not assert that every finer equivariant invariant agrees. In particular an affine action on a Spin^c torsor, chain-level Floer data, fixed-locus placement or the quotient's meridian data may contain additional information.

## 3. The obvious definite-lattice compatibility condition is automatic

Let F be a connected spanning surface embedded in S^3, pushed a little into B^4 while keeping its boundary fixed, and let X(F) be the double cover of B^4 branched over that push-in. A disk-with-bands decomposition of F gives a handle decomposition of X(F) with one zero-handle and b1(F) two-handles. In particular X(F) is simply connected, H2(X(F);Z) is free of rank b1(F), and its intersection pairing is the Gordon–Litherland pairing, with consistent boundary orientation conventions.

If this form Q is nonsingular, its discriminant group is H1(boundary X(F);Z). The lattice automorphism -I preserves every symmetric bilinear form Q and induces -id on the discriminant group. Proposition 1 says that this is exactly the first-homology action of **every** possible new branching involution on the same boundary. Therefore the requirement that the boundary action lift to an isometry of the intersection lattice places no obstruction at this first, unmarked homology level: -I always provides such an algebraic lift.

For the natural deck transformation of X(F), the action on H2 is also -I. One can see this without guessing a basis action. Its fixed surface has Euler characteristic 1-b1(F). The finite-order Lefschetz formula gives that same number as the alternating sum of traces. Since X(F) has homology only in degrees zero and two, the trace on H2 is -b1(F). An involution on this free group has eigenvalues ±1 over Q, so it must be -I.

An algebraic lift to -I is not an extension by a diffeomorphism, much less an extension by an involution with prescribed quotient and fixed surface. No realization theorem of that kind has been obtained here.

## 4. A precise sufficient equivariant certificate

For a nonsplit link L with deck involution tau on Y=Sigma(L), the following is sufficient and, allowing the fillings to vary, necessary for alternation:

- there are two smooth double branched covers X_+→B^4 and X_-→B^4 whose boundary covering pairs identify with (Y,tau)→(S^3,L)
- each branch surface is, relative to its boundary, isotopic to the push-in of a connected embedded spanning surface F_+ or F_- in S^3
- the intersection forms of X_+ and X_- are respectively positive and negative definite

Sufficiency follows from the Gordon–Litherland/intersection-form identification and Greene's surface theorem. Necessity follows from the checkerboard surfaces of a connected alternating diagram. This is a classical-consequence reformulation that records the required geometric data, not a new characterization theorem.

For the original common-cover problem, suppose another branch presentation is already alternating and supplies its checkerboard fillings. The unresolved step is producing such certificates for the **new** involution. A homeomorphism of the common boundary does not automatically extend to either chosen filling. Even an extension would still need quotient B^4 and a branch surface that is a push-in from the boundary. A general smooth or ribbon surface in B^4 is not interchangeable with an embedded spanning surface in S^3.

## 5. A concrete obstruction to the weaker nonequivariant criterion

This example is built from the classical one already noted by Greene in the 2011 lattices paper, p.4, when explaining why ordinary graded Floer homology is not enough to detect alternation.

Let J=T(3,5), let H=Sigma(J) be the Poincare homology sphere, and put

    K=J # mirror(J),        Y=H # (-H)=Sigma(K).

The identity Sigma(T(3,5))=H is also explicitly recorded in McCoy–Zentner, Example 3 of their full author manuscript for the 2023 proper-rational-tangle paper. H has nontrivial fundamental group. For instance its standard presentation

    <x,y,z | x^2=y^3=z^5=xyz>

has trivial abelianization, while mapping to the (2,3,5) rotation group gives a nontrivial quotient A5. The abelianized relation matrix has determinant -1. Thus Y is an integral homology sphere and pi1(Y) is the nontrivial free product pi1(H)*pi1(H), so Y is not S^3.

By Turn 1 Proposition 5, **Y has no alternating branch presentation**. In particular K is non-alternating. The determinant computation can also be seen from the standard torus-knot Alexander polynomial formula: at t=-1, Delta_(3,5) has absolute value one, and the connected sum has determinant one.

Nevertheless Y bounds definite four-manifolds of either sign with trivial first homology and arbitrary positive second Betti number. Let P=H minus an open three-ball and let W=P×[0,1], with corners rounded. It is an integral homology ball with boundary H#(-H), and pi1(W)=pi1(H). For r>=1 form the interior connected sums

    X_+(r)=W # r CP^2,       X_-(r)=W # r (-CP^2).

They have the same boundary Y, H1=0, and forms +I_r and -I_r. Their nontrivial fundamental groups are unchanged. Thus they cannot be any X(F) arising from the push-in of a connected S^3 spanning surface, since every such X(F) is simply connected.

Even the basic correction-term equalities add nothing for this example. The unique boundary Spin^c structure has d(Y)=0 by homology-cobordism invariance, or by additivity and orientation reversal. On X_-(r), the characteristic vectors with all coordinates ±1 have square -r, so

    c1(s)^2+b2(X_-(r))=0=4d(Y).

Each restricts to the unique boundary Spin^c structure. The same calculation applies to -X_+(r) with boundary -Y. These statements use Ozsvath–Szabo's Theorem 1.2 and the negative-definite inequality in their absolutely graded Floer paper; they do not impose a new convention on what other authors mean by a sharp filling. Greene's cited example also has the same absolutely graded hat Floer group as S^3.

This is **not** a counterexample to KP-1.17: its cover has no alternating branch link. It refutes the weakened strategy that uses only two-sided definite fillings, trivial H1, their diagonal lattices and these correction-term equalities. It does not refute a criterion with additional simple-connectivity, irreducibility or genuine branch-surface hypotheses. In particular the example's cover is reducible and the exhibited fillings are not simply connected. Those limitations are essential.

There is an additional surface-level check: K is amphichiral. If it had any definite spanning surface, reflection and the ambient equivalence would produce a definite surface of the opposite sign. Greene's Corollary 1.4 would then force K to be alternating, contradicting the determinant-one argument. Thus this K has no definite spanning surface at all despite the nonequivariant definite fillings above.

## 6. Stabilization does not repair definiteness automatically

A further attempted transfer would first stabilize or modify surfaces to make them equivariant, then try to recover definiteness. Here is a precise elementary warning, rather than an assertion about all equivariant constructions.

Let F be a connected spanning surface for a link of nonzero determinant, and attach an orientable tube in its interior, leaving a collar of the boundary unchanged. The rank of its Gordon–Litherland form increases by two, and its boundary Euler number is unchanged. The Gordon–Litherland signature formula therefore keeps the signature unchanged. Since the link determinant is nonzero, the form remains nonsingular. Its positive and negative indices consequently each increase by one.

In particular, a definite surface becomes indefinite after this tube stabilization. At the level of forms, stabilization by a hyperbolic plane has exactly this effect. Adding only orthogonal summands of the formerly desired definite sign cannot remove the newly created opposite-sign direction. An actual compression or another genuinely geometric operation would be required. No theorem guaranteeing such operations compatibly with an arbitrary second deck involution has been proved here.

## 7. Status after turn 2

The new proved point is an integral, all-links deck-action formula and its consequence that the first-homology lattice-action test is automatic. The surface theorem yields an exact geometric certificate requirement. The Poincare-double example demonstrates the failure of a weaker nonequivariant filling shortcut, with all hypotheses and limitations stated, and tube stabilization identifies an additional concrete difficulty.

The original question is still open in this attempt. Three substantive turns remain. A full affirmative answer needs an actual mixed pair with certified quotient links; a full negative answer must recover alternation across arbitrary branch involutions and across the nonhyperbolic cases. Neither has been obtained. Classical surface, branched-cover, and Floer inputs are credited; no novelty claim is made.
