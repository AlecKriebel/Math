# Five bounded approaches

All five were performed on 2026-10-05. Source retrieval and bookkeeping are not additional proof attempts.

1. **Connected sum and concordance reduction.** Use odd rank, mirror duality and reduced Künneth multiplicativity. Complete for rectangular ribbon knots, and recovers the credited equivalence between the ribbon assertion and ordinary smooth-concordance invariance using the stable-ribbon lemma. Stops because a general ribbon knot is not known to have tensor-square homology. No universal result.

2. **Ribbon splitting, determinant and lifted-Lee normal form.** Derive the exact identity r_F≡1+2E mod 4 for slice knots and char(F)≠2, where E counts even X-exponents. Produce an explicit three-generator formal countermodel to the insufficient splitting/determinant argument. Recovers the known extortion-order-one family. Stops at the missing geometric evenness of E; the countermodel is not a knot.

3. **Change coefficients and use torsion.** Prove r_Fp=r_Q+2t_p and construct the formal integral one-torsion-factor negative control. This explains precisely why rational or large-prime observations do not settle every requested field. No general ribbon parity theorem for t_p found.

4. **Local ribbon-singularity induction.** Check the primary non-locality obstruction and independently calculate the actual knot 6_2 rank 11 over four fields. A merely local simplification pattern also occurs on a nonslice knot. Stops unless the full ribbon disk participates in the induction. No new topological counterexample.

5. **Certified examples and recent-results gate.** Implement the integral reduced cube and exact field ranks from explicit small braid witnesses. Check six examples in four fields, with crossing-order and basepoint changes. Inspect the 2025 Dunfield–Gong census, 2025 Schütz torsion paper and 2026 Lobb concordance-minimum paper for a resolution or an applicable extension. They do not supply a universal parity theorem in the inspected material. Stop instead of converting a finite list or rank inequality into a congruence proof.
