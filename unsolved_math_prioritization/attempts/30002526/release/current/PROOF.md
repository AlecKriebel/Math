# Five approaches to eliminating Whitney umbrellas

## 0. Exact question and scope

For every finitely presented group G, does there exist an irreducible, reduced, two-dimensional complex projective variety W such that pi_1(W) is isomorphic to G and every analytic local germ of W is smooth, an ordinary double crossing xy = 0, or an ordinary triple crossing xyz = 0? The fundamental group is the ordinary topological fundamental group of the complex points.

An irreducible algebraic variety may have a reducible analytic germ. Thus “irreducible with normal crossings” is not contradictory. Requiring *simple* normal crossings globally would change the question: an irreducible simple-normal-crossing variety is smooth.

Theorem 1.2 of Kapovich [K] gives the desired surface if Whitney umbrellas are also admitted, and gives NC-only for the stated hyperbolic 3-manifold group subclass. Kapovich–Kollár [KK, Theorem 2] permits arbitrary G with simple normal crossings but allows reducibility. Neither theorem supplies the conjunction in the question. OWR [O, p. 542] explicitly asks to remove the umbrellas. See `SOURCES.md` for the 2016 announcement caveat.

This document records **route obstructions**, not a counterexample to the question. It establishes no new general realization theorem.

## 1. Change the orbifold quotient or discard its torsion

### Mechanism

Kapovich's construction begins with a group Gamma acting on hyperbolic 3-space and recovers G as Gamma modulo the normal closure of its point stabilizers. Passing to a torsion-free finite-index subgroup removes the relevant quotient singularities. Could that alone yield G with only NC singularities?

### Exact local computation

Let A = C[x,y,z]/(xy), and let theta(x,y,z) = (y,x,-z). Put

    s = x+y, d = x-y, a = d^2, b = dz, c = z^2.

In the coordinates (s,d,z), A = C[s,d,z]/(d^2-s^2), and theta fixes s and negates d,z. Invariants of the polynomial ring are generated by s,a,b,c with the single relation b^2 = ac. Indeed an invariant monomial has the d- and z-exponents of the same parity: both even use a,c; both odd use b,a,c. The normal forms with b-exponent at most one also prove that this is the entire relation ideal. Averaging over the order-two group is exact in characteristic zero. Passing to the theta-invariant relation a=s^2 therefore gives

    A^theta = C[s,b,c]/(b^2-cs^2).

This is the pinch point. In the original polynomial ring the correct identity is

    [z(x-y)]^2 - z^2(x+y)^2 = -4z^2xy.

The program checks this identity, its reduction modulo xy, and invariance of the three generators. This independently establishes the local model in [K, Lemma 4.12].

If theta instead interchanges x,y and fixes z, its invariant ring is C[s,z], which is smooth. But the fixed locus then includes the entire double curve; this is a different action, not a permitted alteration of the original action without further construction.

### Why torsion removal is not a proof

A covering need not preserve the fundamental group; an additional group-preserving argument is required. The local real model makes the lost relation visible: R^3/{v~−v} is the cone on RP^2, hence contractible; deleting its cone point leaves RP^2 times a positive interval, with pi_1 = Z/2. Filling the fixed point kills that order-two loop. Restoring a free action or removing the fixed point cannot simply be assumed to retain the filled quotient's group. This example is a local control, not itself a global projective counterexample.

Moreover a torsion-free action on contractible hyperbolic 3-space has the acting group as its quotient's fundamental group. Arbitrary finitely presented groups cannot all be such torsion-free groups: G=Z/2 already rules out that universal shortcut, despite finite groups being admissible in the original projective question.

**Exact gap:** construct a replacement action/gluing that retains all required stabilizer-killing relations, has the required global projectivity and transitivity, and introduces no non-NC germs. Merely taking the torsion-free subgroup supplies none of these group-identification steps.

## 2. Birationally repair each pinch point

### Mechanism and calculation

Use the local equation F(u,v,w)=v^2-wu^2. Blow up the origin. In the w-chart set u=wa, v=wb. Dividing by the exceptional factor w^2 gives

    b^2-wa^2 = 0.

This is exactly the same pinch-point equation. Blowing up the new pinch point again has an identical chart. Thus repeated blowups at these pinch points do not terminate in NC-only germs.

This is not just a failure of one chart choice. Bierstone–Milman [BM, Question 1.2 and Example 1.7] give the obstruction to a proper birational modification that is an isomorphism over the NC locus. The two analytic branches along the punctured double curve interchange around the pinch point. An isomorphism there preserves that monodromy. We use their result only with that preservation hypothesis; it does not prohibit all modifications or all alternative global realizations.

### Broader-center alternative

Blow up the double-line ideal (u,v). In the u-chart put v=ut. The strict transform is t^2-w=0, a smooth surface with coordinates (u,t). In the v-chart put u=vr. The equation is 1-wr^2=0, which is smooth because r is nonzero and the derivative with respect to w is −r^2. This modification removes the pinch by changing the whole double curve, including its NC points.

The corresponding normalization map is

    (u,t) -> (u,ut,t^2).

It is finite (t satisfies t^2=w) and birational (t=v/u where u is nonzero). Its smooth source is normal, so it is the normalization. Along the double curve it identifies t and −t. The conductor ideal in C[u,t] is (u): elements with a factor u belong to the subring C[u,ut,t^2] after multiplication by any polynomial, whereas a nonzero polynomial in t alone cannot have both itself and t times itself even in t. Thus the conductor map is the branched double cover t -> t^2.

**Exact gap:** prove preservation of the global fundamental group after modifying the double curves, or compensate exactly for every lost gluing relation. Local smoothness is insufficient. Section 5 gives an explicit projective example where normalization changes pi_1.

## 3. Unramify the conductor by a double cover

### Mechanism

The normalization describes the pinch as ramification of a two-sheeted conductor. Adjoin a square root of w to split those sheets.

### Exact calculation

After the base change w=t^2 the surface has equation

    v^2-t^2u^2 = (v-tu)(v+tu)=0.

Each factor defines a smooth sheet. At the origin their differentials are both dv, so their normal covectors have rank one. Their tangent planes coincide, rather than meeting transversely. Thus this base change does **not** directly produce a normal-crossing surface at the origin. Its normalization separates the two sheets, changing connectedness locally. Away from u=t=0 the ordinary intersections may be transverse; this does not repair the origin.

The map of conductor parameters t -> t^2 is ramified at zero. Calling it an étale double cover at the pinch would be false. An actual étale neighborhood cannot make a non-NC analytic germ NC, since the analytic germ is unchanged by an étale local isomorphism over C.

**Exact gap:** find global covering and descent data whose resulting surface is irreducible, projective, NC-only and has precisely G. The local factorization supplies neither transverse intersection nor a pi_1-preserving descent.

## 4. Deform or smooth away the unwanted singularities

### Mechanism

Try to deform the pinch-point equation, retaining enough crossings to preserve the fundamental group.

### Exact local test

For epsilon nonzero, v^2-wu^2=epsilon is smooth: a singular point would have v=0 and u=0 by the v- and w-derivatives, forcing epsilon=0. Therefore eliminating the local singularity by smoothing is algebraically easy. Its topology, and its compatibility with a projective global deformation, are separate requirements.

Here is an explicit warning against unproved pi_1-invariance of smoothing. Consider

    C_e : Y^2 Z = X^3 + X^2 Z + e Z^3  in P^2.

C_0 is an irreducible nodal cubic. For e nonzero and e not equal to −4/27, C_e is a smooth plane cubic: in the affine chart Z=1, repeated roots of x^3+x^2+e would require x=0 or x=−2/3, yielding e=0 or e=−4/27; the point at infinity is smooth. Consequently

    pi_1(C_0 x P^1) = Z,
    pi_1(C_e x P^1) = Z^2  for smooth C_e.

The first identity is proved explicitly in Section 5; the second is the topology of a genus-one smooth plane cubic and the simply connected factor P^1. This example involves an NC singularity rather than a pinch, so it refutes a blanket smoothing-invariance rule, not every specially engineered pinch smoothing.

Also, a strategy ending with a *smooth* projective variety for every G cannot work: Hodge decomposition gives b_1=2h^{1,0}, whereas G=Z has b_1=1. That is an obstruction to complete smoothing, not to NC-only realization.

**Exact gap:** construct a global deformation that smooths only the unwanted germs, retains the right crossings, and prove that its vanishing-cycle/gluing changes leave pi_1 equal to G.

## 5. Rebuild the realization using one normalization and NC self-gluing

### Mechanism

Avoid orbifold pinches entirely. Start from a connected smooth projective normalization and identify suitable divisors in pairs, with ordinary triple intersections encoding relators. Irreducibility of the normalization would ensure irreducibility of its finite image. The issue is whether arbitrary presentations admit compatible projective NC gluing data.

### Fully verified boundary case G=Z

Take C=C_0 above. Its affine equation is y^2=x^2(x+1). It is irreducible because x+1 is not a square in C(x). Its only singular point is (0,0), with nondegenerate quadratic part y^2-x^2, hence an ordinary node. The point [0:1:0] at infinity is smooth because the Z-derivative of the homogeneous equation is nonzero there.

An explicit normalization is

    [S:T] -> [T(S^2−T^2) : S(S^2−T^2) : T^3].

These homogeneous cubics have no common zero. Substitution verifies the defining cubic equation. In the chart T=1, the parameter s gives x=s^2−1 and y=s(s^2−1); the map is one-to-one except that s=1 and s=−1 are identified to the node. It is finite and birational, with source P^1, and hence is the normalization. Topologically C is S^2 with two distinct points identified. This quotient has homotopy type S^2 wedge S^1 (equivalently attach an interval between the points and contract that interval). Therefore pi_1(C)=Z.

Set W=C x P^1. It is an irreducible projective surface. Every singular germ is an ordinary node times a smooth curve, analytically ab=0 in C^3; there are no Whitney umbrellas. Since P^1 is simply connected, pi_1(W)=Z. Its normalization P^1 x P^1 has trivial fundamental group. This proves at once that (i) odd b_1 does not obstruct the original NC target and (ii) normalization cannot be presumed pi_1-preserving even for an irreducible projective NC surface.

### Why the general gluing route stops

Pairwise self-identifications supply loops. General presentations additionally require prescribed two-dimensional attaching relations. To use triple-point gluing, one must actually produce the conductor curves, compatible identifications and cocycle conditions, check every resulting completed local ring is a coordinate-crossing ring, and exhibit an ample line bundle descending to the quotient. One must then compute the resulting fundamental group, rather than infer it from a chosen abstract presentation complex.

The reducible SNC theorem [KK] does not supply that construction on a *single connected normalization*. Conversely, merely quotienting a reducible SNC model to make its components equivalent can reintroduce the branch-switching action of Section 1. We obtained no general geometric realization of the required relators.

**Exact gap:** arbitrary-presentation NC self-gluing on a connected normalization, with projectivity, local-ring checks and a complete van Kampen calculation. This is the principal residual construction problem, not a solved lemma.

## 6. Result and adversarial limits

All five approaches have precise stopping points. The strongest checked conclusions are the invariant-ring calculation, the self-reproducing pinch blowup chart, the conductor/branched-base-change calculations, and the explicit irreducible NC projective surface with group Z and non-group-preserving normalization/smoothing controls. These are elementary or known facts, with no novelty claim.

They do not imply that Whitney umbrellas are unavoidable for every realization of any particular group. No group is excluded from the question. No arbitrary-group NC-only construction has been established here. The conservative status is **unsolved in this investigation**, with an unresolved stronger-announcement literature caveat.
