# Original-source normalization

The official OWR 7/2006 contribution by Anja Kohl, pp. 414–417, defines arbitrary natural-number lists and requires absolute label differences at least d for adjacent vertices and at least s at distance two. It minimizes the common list cardinality k, not label span. Theorem 6 on p. 416 assumes n>=3. Its Conjecture 2 has floor(3d(1−1/n))+1. The pinned catalog incorrectly writes ceiling.

The error is material: for d=1, P4 squared has list chromatic number 3 (greedy along the path, and its first three vertices form a triangle), whereas the ceiling expression gives 4. This is only a transcription counterexample and cannot be reported as resolving the genuine floor conjecture.

The dissertation of the same author (2006), printed pp. 100–102, retains the upper-bound conjecture and proves special cases, common-extremum lists and consecutive lists. Those restrictions cannot be silently extended to arbitrary lists. For n=1 the number is 1; for n=2 it is d+1, consistent with excluding n=2 from the original target. The all-trees (2,1) equality is a separate adjacent record.

The current literature gate did not find a full resolution. Hutchinson–Moore's precoloring-extension paper and recent distance-consistent labelings use different problems. This is not a novelty certification.
