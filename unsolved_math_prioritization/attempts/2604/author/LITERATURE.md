## Recognition criteria

Peter J. Cameron and Natalia V. Maslova, [Criterion of unrecognizability of a finite group by its Gruenberg–Kegel graph](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26585/1/Cameron_2021_Criterion_of_unrecognizability_JAlgebra_AAM.pdf), J. Algebra 607 (2022), 186–213, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2021.12.005. Theorem 1.3 gives necessary conditions for finite labelled recognisability: almost simplicity, no universal vertex, and a coclique of size at least three. Theorem 1.2 relates infinite labelled nonrecognisability to a competitor with nontrivial solvable radical. Statements and relevant proof passages in Section 3 were inspected. These criteria do not license assuming away radicals when attempting a new recognition proof. Proposition 1 of this packet is reproved directly and does not depend on their classification argument.

Ilya B. Gorshkov and Natalia V. Maslova, [Finite almost simple groups whose Gruenberg–Kegel graphs coincide with Gruenberg–Kegel graphs of solvable groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01402), arXiv v2 (2016), published Algebra and Logic 57 (2018), 115–129. Theorems 1–2 identify the no-three-coclique obstruction and explicit families. The English theorem statements, the Russian corresponding statements, and the constructive Lemma 3 were inspected. The list includes the exceptional nonsimple extensions with socle A_6. This is a cited result, not an independently reproduced CFSG-based classification.

## Later unlabelled results do not settle 21.95

Mingzhu Chen, Natalia V. Maslova and Marianna R. Zinov’eva, [On characterization of groups by isomorphism type of Gruenberg–Kegel graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14703), v1, 20 April 2025. The Main Theorem establishes unlabelled recognisability for certain exceptional simple groups, and Problem 2 restates precisely the nonsimple existence question. The introduction and theorem/problem statements were checked in both the public HTML and downloaded PDF. No later revision is listed on the inspected abstract page. The theorem's complete proof was not audited here.

Melissa Lee and Tomasz Popiel, [Recognisability of the sporadic groups by the isomorphism types of their prime graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10113), v3, 13 June 2024; [journal version](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jgth-2024-0150/html), J. Group Theory 29 (2026), 507–543. Theorem 1.1 concerns eight sporadic simple groups. It is not a classification of their nonsimple outer extensions. The introduction also corrects a previous labelled-recognisability claim for Ru. Sections 2, 3.4 and selected Section 4 reductions were inspected; their computational modular-representation arguments were not replayed. This cautions against treating a bibliography's older positive claims as independently verified solutions.

## Promising labelled candidates that fail after erasing labels

Masoumeh Sajjadi, [On the recognizability of some projective general linear groups by the prime graph](https://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/151645), Comment. Math. Univ. Carolin. 63 (2022), 443–458, DOI 10.14712/1213-7243.2023.009. Theorem 3.5 proves labelled recognisability for PGL_2(13²) and PGL_2(17²). The introduction, Lemma 3.1, the theorem statement, and the final identification arguments were inspected. This packet independently disproves unlabelled recognisability for these two groups by explicit collisions with PGL_2(181) and PGL_2(29). These collisions do not contradict Sajjadi's theorem: the prime labels differ. No independent audit of all imported tables and modular characters is claimed.

## Sporadic extension screening

Behrooz Khosravi, [On the prime graphs of the automorphism groups of sporadic simple groups](https://am-brno.math.muni.cz/09-2/am1714.pdf), Archivum Math. (Brno) 45 (2009), 83–94. The start of Section 3 reports that M_12, He, Fi_22 and HN have the same labelled graphs as their proper automorphism groups; Proposition 2 therefore excludes those extensions. The displayed equality on page 88 gives Γ(Aut(McL))=Γ(C_2 × Aut(McL)), excluding Aut(McL) as well. Theorem statements 3.2–3.5 constrain possible quotients for other outer extensions. Such necessary quotient descriptions are not existence constructions, so they are not used to declare all the remaining extensions nonrecognisable. These five exclusions are literature-dependent; the sporadic class data was not independently regenerated.

## Search boundary

Exact question, problem number, unlabelled/isomorphism-type recognition, outer-extension, symmetric-group and PGL_2 searches were made. No verified later solution was located. Spectrum recognition, prime graph plus group order, and the integral-group-ring “Prime Graph Question” were treated as distinct problems. No priority or novelty assertion follows from these searches.
