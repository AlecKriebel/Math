# Limits and rejected shortcuts

1. No proof or odd-characteristic counterexample to general settledness was found. The five approach families end with an explicit missing step, except for the stated special cases.
2. Characteristic two is a literal scope counterexample. It does not resolve the intended odd-characteristic question, whose source context divides by 2 and whose subsequent formulations explicitly require odd q.
3. Source normalization matters. Types are labels of monic irreducible factors; scalar multiplication can change them. Nonmonic f is handled only by the explicit scalar conjugacy fixing 0.
4. The fixed-critical-point family is special. Translation conjugacy moves the rooted target and cannot turn its theorem into a theorem for all quadratics.
5. The checked finite stable masses are lower bounds for the limiting mass, not estimates equipped with error bars. Their complements are not certified positive limiting masses.
6. Proposition 4 has an unproved uniform-depth hypothesis. Existence of a possible path in a proposed type graph is not evidence that every actual factor realizes it.
7. Published missing-transition restrictions obstruct the original one-step factorization mechanism. They are not a refutation of every refined Markov model, and the finite witness alone is not a statistical refutation.
8. Density of settled elements in an ambient group does not imply that an individual Frobenius element is settled. The 2026 Ejder--Kocak manuscript is a special-class group theorem, not a general finite-field factorization theorem.
9. Full original 2012 Boston--Jones article and its 2020 erratum could not be downloaded from attempted AMS/author routes. The original OWR report and the 2015 and published 2026 follow-ups were inspected. The finite stability criterion is re-proved with monic/even/odd-characteristic hypotheses, and the monicity warning is independently visible in Goksel's published Remark 1.5.
10. No human review, formal proof-assistant verification, or priority determination was performed. Exact test success verifies the finite computations and catches algebraic errors; it does not validate every sentence of the proof automatically.
