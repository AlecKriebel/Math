## Logical and scope controls

A single example satisfying all target hypotheses and having no chaotic map at any positive time would refute both universal claims. A mixed-time example refutes only the every-time claim. The identity at time zero is irrelevant. Hypercyclicity or mixing alone does not establish dense periodic vectors. The semigroup's periodic set is a union over real periods; each individual map's periodic set uses positive integer powers. No complex-sector, arbitrary operator-semigroup, Li–Yorke or distributional-chaos assertion is substituted for the target.

## Complexification: what can and cannot be inferred

Let S_t act on a real Banach space, and write its complexification as S_t^C(x+iy)=S_t x+iS_t y. For each fixed t, a complex vector has a discrete period n exactly when both real parts have that same period. Two integer periods can be replaced by their least common multiple. Consequently

    Per(S_t^C) = Per(S_t) + i Per(S_t).

Thus periodic-point nondensity of a real time map survives complexification. This is an elementary conditional observation, not a reconstruction of the inaccessible counterexample.

For the whole semigroup, by contrast, a complex periodic vector requires a common positive **real** period for both parts. Separate real periods need not have a common integer multiple. Density of the real semigroup's periodic union cannot simply be replaced with density of the union of paired fixed spaces. Hypercyclicity of the complexification also calls for an argument about the real diagonal action. Neither requirement was verified for the cited example. No claim is made that complexification must fail; the necessary bridge has not been proved here.
