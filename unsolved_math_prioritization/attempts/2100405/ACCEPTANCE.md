## Accepted scoped conclusions

1. Rational tangent-slope uniqueness. For rational θ/π and positive integers k,l, if tan(kθ) and tan(lθ) are finite and nonzero, equality tan(kθ)/k=tan(lθ)/l forces k=l. Equal cyclotomic fields give equal or odd/double-odd root orders; a finite Galois automorphism handles equal orders and the 2-adic norm argument rules out the latter pair. Cyclotomic irreducibility, degrees, conjugacy, elementary norms and valuations are explicit standard algebraic imports.
2. Constant-offset rigidity. Let γ:R/(2πZ)→R² be a C¹ embedding, r>0, α/(2π) rational and 0<α<π. If γ(t+α)−γ(t)=r[γ′(t)+γ′(t+α)] for all t, then γ(t)=c+a cos t+b sin t with a,b independent and r=tan(α/2). The Fourier multiplier and the preceding lemma leave one positive frequency; embedding forces frequency one and nondegeneracy. No analyticity, smallness, first-mode assumption or finite cutoff is needed.
3. Restricted outer application. A nonelliptic strictly convex table cannot have a rational invariant exterior curve given by this constant-offset/rigid-shift ansatz. The n>1 branch of the Bialy–Mironov–Shalom explicit construction has irrational shifts, as the source already states. The n=1 circular case and zero perturbation are excluded from that nonelliptic claim.
4. Inner partial. The support function 1+ε cos(5θ), 0<|ε|<1/24, gives an analytic positive-curvature nonellipse of constant width two and one full period-two invariant phase circle. The differentiable near-circle p/q-family first-order condition removes nonzero Fourier modes divisible by q. The fifth mode passes q=2 and q=3; it does not establish a second caustic.
5. Exact remaining equations. The inner unit-chord reflection system and outer variable-offset midpoint system are valid under their stated regularity, strict-convexity, nonzero-chord, orientation and embedded-exterior hypotheses. Both pairs must be solved for the same boundary with genuinely distinct rational caustics.
6. Versioned source findings. The sine ratio at ρ=1/6,k=3,m=1 equals 2 and refutes the general Lemma 3 in Cyr v1. It does not meet the ordered n−1,n+1 application and does not refute the tangent conclusion. The bounded-strip entire function exp(iz) refutes the complex-valued Lemma 11 in Kaloshin–Koudjinan v1. The permitted first variation cos(5t) gives auxiliary function −exp(−iz)/2 and invalidates the asserted real-valuedness inference. This is not a complete billiard deformation and does not disprove Theorem 9.
