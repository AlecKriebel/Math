# Reconciliation with the finite-generation lemma in the 2025 source

This note records a source-context issue raised during independent review. The direct counterexample and its defining operations remain unchanged. It is important not to use a neighboring source lemma outside its justified scope, or to turn this issue into an unsupported claim that a broader published theorem is false.

## 1. The apparent tension

On printed p.177, immediately after the discussion of the ideal question, Colazzo--Ferrara--Trombetti impose the standing condition that the whole brace B has property (S). Their Lemma 3.12 on p.178, if read in isolation, says that an additive group generated by finitely many property-(s) elements has finite B/Ann(B). Its proof invokes injectivity of a displayed map recording star products and additive commutators with the chosen additive generators.

Our brace is additively generated by

\[
r=(1,0),\qquad x=(0,1),
\]

both of which have property (s), but it does **not** have property (S): (0,2) fails property (s). Thus the surrounding global-(S) context excludes it from a context-restricted application of that lemma. The isolated wording must not be treated as an unconditional theorem that overrides the explicit index calculations.

There is also a concrete reason not to import the displayed injectivity argument without checking it. The following calculation identifies exactly what fails for these additive generators; it does not rely on a general opinion about the paper.

## 2. The annihilator and the displayed map in this example

Equation (6) of the counterexample gives

\[
\ker\lambda=\mathbb Z\times\{0\}.
\]

If (n,0) is in the annihilator, it must also be fixed by every lambda map. The lambda map at (0,2) sends (n,0) to (-n,0), forcing n=0. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(B)=\{(0,0)\}.
\]

For every b=(n,0), direct use of the star formula gives

\[
b*r=b*x=r*b=x*b=(0,0).
\]

All additive commutators vanish. Therefore the map displayed in Lemma 3.12, with additive generators r,x, sends every b=(n,0) to the same zero tuple, despite these being different cosets of the trivial annihilator.

The relevant distinction is that r and x do **not** generate the multiplicative group: their multiplicative subgroup is Z times {0,1}. In particular lambda_r and lambda_x fix all (n,0), but lambda_(0,2) does not. Being fixed by the lambda maps at additive generators does not, by itself, imply being fixed by all lambda maps, since lambda is a homomorphism for the multiplicative group.

This identifies the exact failed inference under an unrestricted reading of the displayed-map argument. We do not assert an intended replacement hypothesis or a correction to every surrounding theorem.

## 3. The cited 2021 theorem has a premise that fails here

The cited source is Jespers--Kubat--Van Antwerpen--Vendramin, *Radical and weight of skew braces and their applications to structure groups of solutions of the Yang--Baxter equation*, Adv. Math. 385 (2021), 107767. I read the complete Theorem 5.4 proof in the author preprint [arXiv:2001.10967v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10967v1), pp.11--12; the final journal-layout version was not compared in this check.

Its converse assumes finiteness of both the whole star-generated additive subgroup B^(2) and the additive commutator subgroup, together with finite additive generation. The counterexample instead has

\[
B^{(2)}=2\mathbb Z\times\{0,2\},
\qquad [B,B]_+=\{0\}.
\]

Indeed every star product belongs to the displayed subgroup, while (-2,0) and (0,2) are themselves star products and generate it additively. Thus B^(2) is infinite. The converse theorem's premise is not met. **This example is not a counterexample to that full theorem.**

The proof's step from fixed values at additive generators to membership in Fix(B) is precisely the step that cannot be transplanted unconditionally into the present setting. Nothing in the original OWR Question 1, however, assumes global property (S), finite B^(2), or two-sidedness. The direct counterexample therefore answers that question while these source distinctions remain explicit.
