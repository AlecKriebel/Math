# Independent cone-deformation audit: exact retained theorem and gap

This is a verification of the geometric literature route used by original PR35, not a new resolution of KP-1.85. The frozen original target is every hyperbolic knot in S³ and the **PSL₂(C) irreducible character component through a fixed oriented complete holonomy**. A genuine embedded nonconstant interval of SO(3)=PSU(2) characters is required. All statements below are about characters, not conjugacy-orbit motion. The original attempt count remains 1/5; this verification adds 0 substantive problem attempts.

## Retained universal conditional theorem

Let K⊂S³ be a hyperbolic knot. Suppose there exists a nondegenerate closed orientable Euclidean cone 3-manifold with underlying pair (S³,K), smooth singular locus exactly K, and cone angle 0<α≤π. Then the fixed oriented complete PSL₂(C) holonomy component contains an embedded nonconstant real analytic arc of PSU(2) characters. The corresponding statement holds for every SL₂(C) canonical component obtained from either oriented complete holonomy and either central lift. This is the content of Dix, Theorem 3.3.3, with the proof dependencies and component bookkeeping supplied below. It is conditional on **existence of the specified metric**; no universal existence theorem is proved here.

The metric must be genuine, not a collapsed or formal limiting object. Smooth singular knot is essential to the q=1 application; the general graph-singularity setting of Porti–Weiss is broader and requires additional boundary bookkeeping. Angle zero is the complete hyperbolic endpoint, not a Euclidean cone metric allowed in this hypothesis.

### 1. The almost-product hypothesis is substantive

Porti–Weiss assume their closed orientable Euclidean cone manifold is not almost product; the bound α≤π alone is not their full hypothesis. Their Lemma 2.1 identifies almost product with rotational holonomy contained in an embedded O(2)⊂SO(3). An element in its component outside SO(2) reverses the invariant axis and is a half turn, hence has rotation angle π. If α<π, a meridian cannot lie in that component. The homomorphism from the knot group to π₀(O(2)) therefore vanishes: a knot meridian normally generates the entire group. Rotational holonomy lies in SO(2). The invariant parallel vector field then gives the Seifert description used in Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.3. The smooth part of an almost-product cone manifold with all cone angles <π is Seifert fibered. A finite-volume hyperbolic knot exterior is not Seifert fibered. Thus the required non-almost-product condition holds for α<π.

It would be invalid to apply this meridian argument unchanged at α=π; a meridian can then be in the other component of O(2). Dix explicitly supplies additional references for that boundary. We checked it independently as follows.

### 2. Independent exclusion of the π boundary

In fact no smooth knot in S³ can support a nondegenerate Euclidean cone metric with angle π. Pull such a metric back to the twofold cover Σ₂(K) branched along K. Near K the transverse angle doubles from π to 2π, so the lifted metric is locally Euclidean across the entire branch locus. Away from K it is locally Euclidean by covering. Compactness makes it complete. The cover is closed, connected and orientable, so it is a closed orientable flat 3-manifold.

For a knot Seifert matrix V, the standard presentation of H₁(Σ₂(K);Z) is coker(V+Vᵀ), explicitly recorded in Borodzik §5, p.503, including the underlying group in Definition 5.1. The skew form V−Vᵀ is the unimodular integral intersection form of the one-boundary-component Seifert surface. Modulo 2, V+Vᵀ equals V−Vᵀ, so det(V+Vᵀ) is odd. In particular it is nonzero, and the cover has finite odd-order first homology. This parity proof applies to all genera, including genus zero with empty determinant 1.

The six closed orientable flat 3-manifold types are listed with actual group presentations in Sell §4, Table 1, p.3816. The torus has positive first Betti number. For each of the four k-twist types (k=2,3,4,6), send the screw generator to 1∈Z, its axial translation to k, and transverse translations to 0; every listed relation vanishes. Thus each has an epimorphism to Z. The remaining type is Hantzsche–Wendt. Its presentation, also Chelnokov–Mednykh (3.1), is

    <x,y,z | xy²x⁻¹y²=1, yx²y⁻¹x²=1, xyz=1>.

Abelianization gives 4y=0, 4x=0, x+y+z=0, hence H₁≅Z/4⊕Z/4, of even order 16. None of the six types has finite odd-order H₁. This contradicts the branched-cover conclusion. The published flat classification is a stated standard input, not a classification newly established by finite tests. The presentations and their algebraic consequences are checked exactly in boundary_controls.py. This excludes α=π rather than using an invalid almost-product argument there. It is not a mechanism extending the cone criterion to arbitrary knots.

### 3. The Euclidean character is regular and has a real unitary arc

Use the now established α<π and non-almost-product hypotheses. Lift the rotational holonomy to SU(2), possible for a knot exterior also directly because H²(K-exterior;Z/2)=0, and denote its character χE. This lift is irreducible: a reducible unitary two-dimensional representation is simultaneously diagonal, so its rotational image lies in SO(2), contrary to the non-almost-product conclusion. This is consistent with the rotational fixed-endomorphism argument of Porti–Weiss Lemma 2.4. Their Proposition 4.3 supplies smoothness of χE in the full SL₂(C) character variety, local complex dimension q, and smoothness of the SU(2) character locus, local real dimension q. Here q=1 because the singular locus is a single closed knot.

The operative proof was read, not merely this proposition's statement: §3 identifies the holonomy cocycle with the periods of the identity tangent-bundle form; the L² parallel-form theorem, Theorem 3.7, and the scalar centralizer give one-dimensional L² cohomology; boundary restriction is injective because the identity class restricts nontrivially. Duality gives dim H¹=q and the H² boundary injection. §4 uses natural obstruction classes, their boundary vanishing, and Artin approximation to integrate cocycles. Dividing the irreducible representation germ by conjugation leaves tangent H¹. For this last step the orbit tangent is **B¹**, as stated correctly immediately before Lemma 4.2; the printed Proposition 4.3 proof accidentally says Z¹. Taking that typo literally would destroy the quotient dimension calculation. We use the standard correct orbit tangent, without altering the source or original package.

Consequently a local embedded one-dimensional manifold of SU(2) **characters** passes through χE. Its nonzero H¹ tangent is not a conjugacy orbit. Neither the facts that the starting holonomy has a rotational part nor that real traces occur would by themselves imply this curve. The complex germ through χE is smooth and has a unique irreducible algebraic component V.

The analytic parallel-form theorem, obstruction integration and local rigidity are explicit imported theorems. This audit checks their hypotheses and the complete operative arguments in the retrieved Porti–Weiss and Kojima primary texts; it does not claim a new proof of every foundational L² estimate or Artin approximation theorem.

### 4. Regeneration reaches the correct local component

In Porti–Weiss §5, Theorem 5.1 requires the appropriate nonzero infinitesimal translational/imaginary tangent, not an arbitrary path of real or complex characters. For q=1, the holonomy cocycle spans the curve's tangent and their §6 Remark 6.1 gives eligible imaginary paths. Lemma 6.2 proves the meridian angle function has a degree-two fold at χE. Constancy would regenerate a nonzero-volume hyperbolic family with constant angles, contradicting Schläfli; degree greater than two would give a hyperbolic branch with increasing angle while volume regenerates from zero, again contradicting Schläfli. Corollary 6.3 therefore gives hyperbolic cone structures with cone angles approaching α from below, with two complex-conjugate branches of opposite orientation.

The derivative of the meridian angle vanishes at χE but the character curve does not vanish: in suitable real analytic coordinates the fold has µ=α+z², the unitary branch has z real, and the hyperbolic branches have z purely imaginary. The exact fold diagnostic is explanatory, not a proof of analytic existence. Choose one of these genuine regenerating branches matching the fixed orientation, and choose a sufficiently small positive parameter τ. Its lifted character χτ lies in the unique component V through χE.

### 5. Global continuation cannot switch components

Kojima's main theorem applies to this compact orientable hyperbolic cone manifold: its singular locus is the original smooth knot and its positive cone angle is <π. Its full operative proof, §§1–7, was read, including the geometric and algebraic convergence distinction. The decreasing-angle deformation is on the marked pair; it reaches the complete hyperbolic structure of the exterior. In §7.5 the positive rational orbifold-angle intermediate construction and the small-angle complete-cusp family connect the terminal part of the path. One must not silently substitute a β=0 limit into a proposition whose hypotheses require a positive lower angle bound. The proof handles that issue separately. This is stronger than merely asserting the exterior admits some hyperbolic metric.

At every positive cone angle along this path, Hodgson–Kerckhoff local rigidity, precisely restated as Kojima Theorem 2.1.1, makes the full local PSL₂(C) conjugacy/character germ smooth of complex dimension one, with a local meridian complex-length coordinate. The image of the path at such a point therefore has a unique irreducible component. An affine character variety has finitely many components. Along a connected interval of smooth points, the unique-component label is locally constant and hence constant. At the complete endpoint, algebraic closedness of that component puts the limit there; no smoothness assumption at the endpoint is needed for this closure argument. Keeping the oriented marking identifies the endpoint with the specified oriented complete holonomy. Alternatively applying complex conjugation exchanges the two orientation choices while preserving compact characters.

A connected path through singular points would not suffice: xy=0 admits a continuous path from one axis to the other through its singular origin. The new exact controls demonstrate this failure mode. They do not assert that all character paths avoid singularities. Here avoidance comes specifically from the correctly applicable positive-angle rigidity theorem.

For identification with the **distinguished** complete component, rather than merely a component containing the endpoint, the usual complete-cusp regularity is also explicit. Heusener–Porti AppendixB, TheoremB.1.2 in the freshly retrieved Astérisque source, states that the meridian-trace map is locally bianalytic at the complete lifted character. Its full operative proof and the supporting end/developing-map construction in §§B.1.1–B.1.3 were read. For one cusp the complete SL character is smooth on a unique complex curve. Its PSL character is Ad-irreducible (the finite-volume complete holonomy is non-elementary and does not preserve a line/geodesic), so Heusener–Porti Remark4.3 makes the finite quotient locally unbranched there. The complete PSL character likewise has a unique irreducible germ. Thus the closed component reached above is exactly the specified canonical component. Positive-angle local rigidity was not improperly applied at angle0; closure alone proves inclusion, and the separate complete-cusp theorem proves unique-component identification.

### 6. Finite SL₂→PSL₂ passage and orientation

Heusener–Porti Proposition 4.2 identifies the liftable PSL character locus with the finite quotient by H¹(π₁;Z/2); its full averaging/invariant-trace proof and the applicable Remark 4.3/Example 4.6 were read. For a hyperbolic knot exterior all PSL representations lift: it is aspherical and H²(exterior;Z/2)=0. Its twisting group is C₂, from the meridian sign. Nonliftable components in the cited paper concern other groups and cannot be suppressed without this hypothesis.

Let p be this finite quotient. Then p(V) is a closed irreducible curve containing p(χτ). At that positive-angle smooth point it equals the unique local/global one-dimensional PSL irreducible component. By the preceding continuation argument this is the canonical component containing the fixed oriented complete holonomy; it also contains p(χE). Equivalently, surjectivity of V→p(V) gives a lift of the complete character in V, so V is an SL canonical component. This uses finite quotient and smooth-point dimension, rather than a general false assumption that every arbitrary map takes a component onto a component.

The local SU(2) arc in V cannot collapse to one PSL character, because p has finite fibers. Choose a smaller interval away from a critical/branch point where a nonconstant analytic projected coordinate has nonzero derivative; its image is an embedded nonconstant real analytic arc. Every character on it is represented in PSU(2). A quotient branch at χE is harmless; we do not need the entire original interval to project injectively. Conversely lifting a PSU(2) representation yields SU(2) after conjugation, since the preimage of PSU(2) in SL₂(C) is SU(2).

Central sign twisting and complex conjugation are separate operations. Both preserve SU(2); twisting permutes the two lifts of a fixed oriented complete holonomy, while complex conjugation exchanges orientations. The four operations need not produce four distinct components. They suffice to transfer the unitary-arc property to each canonical SL component defined by those complete lifts, and to both oriented PSL components. We do not claim a sign twist changes orientation or assume all canonical components coincide.

## Two-bridge corollary, exactly scoped

Porti's primary January 2004 two-bridge manuscript, introduction and §3, supplies a Euclidean transition angle α₀∈[2π/3,π) for each hyperbolic two-bridge knot (and treats related links). The singular knot at that transition is precisely the required cone pair. Applying the retained theorem yields a nonconstant unitary character arc on the canonical component, hence an SO(3) arc after the finite quotient. Dix Corollary 3.3.4 therefore does give arcs, not just a solitary rotational character. Porti Proposition 3.1's interval description is also consistent with the genuine character variation at the fold. We do not extrapolate its later >π discussion or link case to all hyperbolic knots.

We followed the transition citation further rather than treating the 2004 introduction as a new proof. The complete operative Heusener–Porti Appendix A to Boileau–Porti's Astérisque272 was freshly retrieved and read. For the angle-π orbifold of a two-bridge knot, the double branched cover is a spherical lens space, so the orbifold is spherical of cyclic type, while its exterior is hyperbolic. Appendix A proves the limiting hyperbolicity parameter satisfies 0<t∞<1. At t∞=1, the character-curve ideal-point/essential-surface argument would contradict its spherical-cover topology. A finite-character limit factors projectively through the finite spherical orbifold group and is conjugate into SU(2). It is irreducible: otherwise degeneration from irreducibles supplies a nonabelian reducible representation with that same finite-order meridian character; this would also factor through the finite orbifold group, hence be unitary, which is incompatible with nonabelian reducibility. The appendix then computes all orbifold adjoint homology as zero by transfer from S³ and irreducibility, uses tubular-neighborhood Mayer–Vietoris to obtain exterior H¹ dimension1, and shows meridian trace has nonzero differential. The limit is therefore meridian-regular.

For completeness, the local compact-real implication in this last step can be checked algebraically. Near an irreducible SU(2) character whose real meridian trace is a complex local coordinate, complex conjugation fixes that coordinate, so uniqueness of the chart fixes the whole character; all traces are real. An irreducible real-character representation is intertwined with its conjugate. The one-dimensional intertwiner line varies locally continuously (constant rank of its defining linear equations). Normalize its determinant to1 locally; the associated antilinear operator squares to ±I by Schur's lemma. Its sign is locally constant and is −I at the initial SU(2) representation. Choosing a basis where this antilinear operator is standard gives matrices of the quaternionic form [[a,b],[-conjugate(b),conjugate(a)]], with determinant |a|²+|b|²=1, hence SU(2). Thus nearby real-meridian characters would be compact, contradicting the incoming hyperbolic holonomies. This explains why the finite spherical endpoint excludes t∞=1, rather than merely exhibiting an isolated unitary character.

The appendix's Euclidean corollary uses the monograph's Theorem A and Proposition2.3.1, whose exact hypotheses and operative application were checked. The increasing angles now lie in a positive interval strictly belowπ. The geometric-limit alternatives are a hyperbolic continuation, an embedded three-intersection turnover, or a rescaled Euclidean limit with the same pair. Local openness excludes a terminal hyperbolic continuation. For a closed knot in S³ an embedded sphere cannot intersect the knot in exactly three transverse points (mod2 intersection is zero), excluding the turnover alternative. The remaining alternative gives the genuine Euclidean cone metric at α₀=t∞π<π. The general compactness trichotomy, Culler–Shalen essential-surface theorems and branched lens-space construction are stated published inputs; this audit does not claim to independently reproduce all200pages of the monograph or those foundational theorems. The complete Appendix A proof and the exact compactness application are checked, as are all hypothesis transfers needed by the corollary.

## Exact remaining gap

Neither the original attempt nor these sources show every hyperbolic knot admits the Euclidean metric used in the conditional theorem, or reaches a suitable regular compact-real character on its canonical component by another mechanism. In particular a nonhyperbolic branched double cover is only a candidate signal. The Seifert-fibered double cover of a Montesinos knot can carry other geometries, and does not imply the Euclidean cone hypothesis. Dix Chapter 5, Question 5.0.4, retains Montesinos knots as future work and discusses the relevant degeneration issue. Increasing a cone angle until a deformation terminates does not guarantee a Euclidean, noncollapsed, smooth-component endpoint. Global continuation beyond the proved range is an additional unsupported theorem, not a repair of the existing proof.

Thus this route is verified as a credited universal **conditional** result and an established two-bridge subfamily. Its generalization is blocked on a materially missing geometric or canonical-component mechanism. This supplies no counterexample to KP-1.85, no new census, and no universal solution. Original PR35's `unsolved` status is mathematically appropriate.
