# Bounded prior-work and source-status review

Review date: 9 October 2026. Scope: uniqueness of the entire Nash-equilibrium set of an explicitly represented rational bimatrix game with rank(A+B)=1, allowing degeneracy. Pure strategies count as mixed strategies with singleton support. This is a literature-attribution review, not an independent certification of a proposed proof.

## Conclusion

This bounded search did not identify a published theorem establishing the exact rank-one uniqueness hardness statement, nor a published instance of the particular Disser–Skutella flow / exact-penalty / rank-one-game construction under consideration. That negative finding is not a novelty claim or proof that no such work exists.

The principal ingredients have substantial prior provenance. The parametric-flow hardness comes from Disser–Skutella; the zero-sum parameter-family and scalar consistency characterization comes from Adsul and coauthors. A permanent pure default equilibrium in an equilibrium-hardness reduction is also an established pattern in unrestricted bimatrix games. The distinctive mathematical work to assess in the present construction is the rank-preserving combination: encoding the relevant parametric flow optima, excluding false-positive intersections, and retaining exactly one default equilibrium in no-instances. The literature search does not itself establish these properties.

## Closest sources and what they actually establish

### 1. Original question and the later comprehensive rank-one paper

Bernhard von Stengel's abstract, “Algorithms for Rank-1 Bimatrix Games,” appears on p.2332 of *New Directions in Stochastic Optimisation*, Oberwolfach Reports 15(3) (2018), 2303–2384, DOI [10.4171/OWR/2018/38](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2018/38). The report expressly conjectures coNP-hardness of rank-one equilibrium uniqueness. EMS article 16413 is this report; its website records publication on 27 August 2019. The same conjecture appears in the [2018 Waterloo talk abstract](https://uwaterloo.ca/combinatorics-and-optimization/events/tutte-colloquium-bernhard-von-stengel). The relevant report passage was inspected in the [primary PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46760).

Bharat Adsul, Jugal Garg, Ruta Mehta, Milind Sohoni and Bernhard von Stengel, *Fast Algorithms for Rank-1 Bimatrix Games*, Operations Research 69(2) (2021), 613–631, DOI [10.1287/opre.2020.1981](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2020.1981), is the central later reference. The final published article was inspected through the [NSF-hosted PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10317978), alongside [arXiv:1812.04611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04611). The journal article's §3, Lemma 5 and Theorem 1, p.616, gives the rank-reduced family and hyperplane characterization. For rank one this is a parameterized zero-sum family. Its analysis expressly allows degeneracy. Its §10, p.630, still treats second-equilibrium hardness and output-efficient enumeration as open questions. The corresponding discussion is on p.36 of the longer arXiv version. Neither inspected version cites Disser–Skutella.

This establishes the historical open status in that source. It does not establish the status of every subsequent manuscript.

### 2. The flow hardness is an existing theorem

Yann Disser and Martin Skutella, *The Simplex Algorithm Is NP-Mighty*, ACM Transactions on Algorithms 15(1), Article 5 (November 2018), DOI [10.1145/3280847](https://doi.org/10.1145/3280847), was inspected in the [author-hosted published PDF](https://www2.mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de/~disser/pdfs/DisserSkutella18.pdf), particularly §§2–3 and §5.

Corollary 1.7, printed p.5:5 and proved on p.5:17, establishes NP-hardness of deciding whether a parametric minimum-cost flow ever uses a designated arc, and states the corresponding parametric-linear-programming variable-use consequence. Lemma 3.2 supplies the PARTITION-based successive-shortest-path construction. Corollary 1.8 concerns the number of vertices in a two-dimensional polytope projection. These are optimization/path results, not rank-one Nash uniqueness results.

The brief proof of Corollary 1.7 invokes SSP optimality and Lemma 3.2. It does not separately spell out the stronger quantifier required when a reduction must exclude designated-arc use by every optimal flow at every parameter. Therefore the proposed construction must justify its perturbation/all-optima step rather than treating that strengthening as an explicit theorem statement in the source. This is a scope observation, not a claim that the original corollary is incorrect.

### 3. General polytopes and multiplicative objectives are relevant but insufficient

Jugal Garg, Albert Xin Jiang and Ruta Mehta, *Bilinear Games: Polynomial Time Algorithms for Rank Based Subclasses*, WINE 2011, 399–407, DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-25510-6_35](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-25510-6_35), was inspected in [arXiv:1109.6182](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.6182). It extends rank-one equilibrium computation to compact polytopal strategy sets. Its introductory discussion expressly distinguishes this tractability from general rank-one quadratic-programming hardness. It does not establish uniqueness hardness or a polynomial-size, uniqueness-preserving conversion of arbitrary polytopal games into ordinary simplex-strategy games. The latter cannot be obtained merely by listing polytope vertices, which may be exponentially numerous.

Thorsten Theobald, *Enumerating the Nash equilibria of rank 1-games*, CRM Proceedings and Lecture Notes 48 (2009), 115–129, was inspected in [arXiv:0709.1263](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.1263). Its parametric simplex enumeration method and its non-Lemke–Howson-reachable examples concern equilibrium enumeration, not a hardness reduction for uniqueness. Its concluding running-time question does not provide such a theorem.

Tomomi Matsui, *NP-hardness of linear multiplicative programming and related problems*, Journal of Global Optimization 9 (1996), 113–119, DOI [10.1007/BF00121658](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00121658), was inspected in the [December 1995 author/institution technical report](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/1995/METR95-13.pdf). It proves hardness for a product objective over general linear constraints. It neither supplies the bimatrix best-response structure nor the guaranteed default equilibrium needed here. General multiplicative-program hardness is not, by itself, rank-one equilibrium uniqueness hardness.

### 4. The default-equilibrium pattern predates the present construction

Vincent Conitzer and Tuomas Sandholm, *New Complexity Results about Nash Equilibria*, Games and Economic Behavior 63(2) (2008), 621–641, was inspected in the [author PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/nashGEB08.pdf), particularly its construction, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2. Corollary 2 gives NP-completeness of nonuniqueness even for symmetric two-player games, crediting Gilboa–Zemel. The construction has a permanent (f,f) equilibrium and additional equilibria encoding satisfying assignments. From the displayed payoffs, choosing the default payoff ε>0 makes (f,f) strict: unilateral deviations against f pay zero. This last strictness observation is an inference from the construction.

These are unrestricted-rank games. Symmetry means B=A-transpose; it is not a sum-rank-one condition. Thus this source supports attribution of the general default-plus-witness pattern, not the proposed rank-preserving bridge.

## Subsequent literature and tempting false positives

The following primary texts were inspected at the level of their relevant definitions, principal statements, introductory or concluding discussion, and relevant keyword contexts. This was not a full verification of their proofs.

- Nikolas Patris and Ioannis Panageas, [*Learning Nash Equilibria in Rank-1 Games*, ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/27b26dcef9a012953d23107759736e54-Paper-Conference.pdf). Theorem 3.7 concerns convergence to an approximate equilibrium using a modified optimistic-learning algorithm. It cites the 2021 structure theorem; it does not decide uniqueness.
- William Overman, [*Planning Against Learning in Rank-1 Games*, arXiv:2608.18067v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18067), August 2026 preprint. Theorem 5.1 proves additive-value hardness for optimizing cumulative reward against replicator dynamics, using a CLIQUE reduction. It genuinely assumes rank(A+B)=1, but its objective is strategic planning against a learning opponent, not existence of a second Nash equilibrium. No Disser–Skutella reduction was found in it.
- Dana Golden, [*Low-Rank Payoffs and Limit Uniqueness in Global Games*, arXiv:2607.23360v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23360), July 2026 preprint. Definition 2.4 and Remark 2.5 explicitly use individual payoff-matrix rank and distinguish it from sum rank. Its limit-selection result additionally uses supermodularity and a global-games information structure. It is not the present uniqueness decision problem.
- Ruichen Luo, Sebastian U. Stich and Krishnendu Chatterjee, [*Monotone Near-Zero-Sum Games: A Generalization of Convex-Concave Minimax*, arXiv:2512.02690v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02690). The inspected preprint and its related-work appendix distinguish near-zero-sum smoothness conditions from rank-k matrix games. Its unique-equilibrium statements use strong monotonicity. The ICLR 2026 OpenReview PDF encountered browser verification, so the primary arXiv preprint was used instead.
- Constantin Ickstadt, Thorsten Theobald and Elias Tsigaridas, [*Semidefinite games*, arXiv:2202.12035v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12035), published in International Journal of Game Theory 53 (2024), 827–857. Its strategy spaces are positive-semidefinite slices; references to rank-one strategy decompositions do not impose rank(A+B)=1. It concerns equilibrium structure and multiplicity, not this decision problem.
- Constantin Ickstadt, Thorsten Theobald and Bernhard von Stengel, [*A Stable-Set Bound and Maximal Numbers of Nash Equilibria in Bimatrix Games*, arXiv:2411.12385v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12385), September 2025 version. It resolves a maximal-equilibrium-count question for nondegenerate 5-by-5 games. Its reference to rank-one multiplicity credits Adsul and coauthors; it does not establish uniqueness hardness.
- Joseph L. Heyman and Abhishek Gupta, [*Rank Reduction in Bimatrix Games*, arXiv:1904.00457v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00457), later International Game Theory Review 25(1) (2023), Article 2250017, and Heyman's [*A Manifold of Polynomial Time Solvable Bimatrix Games*, arXiv:1904.04251](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04251), concern strategic equivalence and rank reduction under additional conditions. They do not transform arbitrary nonuniqueness-hard games into rank-one games.
- Emanuel Tewolde and Vincent Conitzer, [*Game Transformations That Preserve Nash Equilibria or Best-Response Sets*, arXiv:2111.00076v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00076), studies equivalence-preserving transformations and separate higher-player hardness statements. James P. Bailey's [arXiv:2110.02482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02482) studies time-average learning in generalized network zero-sum games. Both cite rank-one work as background; neither provides the target result.

## Scope distinctions and limitations

1. Finding one equilibrium, finding another when one is supplied, deciding global uniqueness, and enumerating all equilibria have different output requirements. The first is polynomial-time solvable here; that does not decide the others.
2. Uniqueness within a specified support or a completely mixed class does not exclude equilibria on other supports. Uniqueness of payoffs likewise does not imply uniqueness of strategy pairs.
3. Individual low payoff rank, symmetric-game matrix rank, rank-one PSD strategy matrices, and rank(A+B)=1 are different restrictions. The rank-three PPAD-hardness cited by the rank-one papers does not settle rank-one uniqueness. The 2021 article records the rank-two claim as a personal communication; this review does not upgrade it to a verified theorem.
4. The target allows degeneracy. A reduction establishing existence of an additional equilibrium need not make the entire game nondegenerate. Conversely, a strict default equilibrium alone does not make a game globally nondegenerate.
5. Searching included combinations of rank-one/rank-1, bimatrix, uniqueness/nonuniqueness/second equilibrium, hardness/coNP, parametric LP, exact penalty, PARTITION, and Disser–Skutella, plus title-based searches and citation discovery. The OpenAlex record for the 2021 article returned eight citing records, including duplicates; searches found additional citing works, demonstrating that this index was incomplete. The indexed citing records of both the 2018 and 2015 Disser–Skutella publications were also screened by title. Title-only screening of those broader citation lists is not full-text exclusion of every cited work.

No claim of exhaustive coverage, priority, novelty, or independent proof acceptance follows from this report. The defensible attribution is a proposed rank-preserving synthesis of established flow hardness and established rank-one game structure, with the exact connecting lemmas assessed on their own merits.
