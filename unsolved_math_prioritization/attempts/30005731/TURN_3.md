# Substantive turn 3: causal prefixes, free-ray variations, and the suffix test

**Unreviewed partial.** Date: 2026-10-01. This turn attacks the passage from the visible first round to a winding barrier. It proves a causal prefix lemma without convexity, extends the variation to a specified nonradial incoming fire field, and isolates the exact remaining inequality at an unchanged suffix. The original optimizer question remains unresolved.

## 1. Causality from the source's arrival-order hypothesis

Let Z=zeta([0,S]) be a compact rectifiable simple arc, possibly closing only at its last point, disjoint from the open initial burning set. Let u_Z denote the lower-semicontinuous arrival trace defined using closures of reachable sets. Assume that s -> u_Z(zeta(s)) is nondecreasing. Put Z_s=zeta([0,s]) and p=zeta(s).

Then

    u_(Z_s)(p) = u_Z(p).                                      (1)

In particular this statement does not assume a radial graph, local convexity, or a unique minimizing fire ray.

To prove it, removing the future wall gives u_(Z_s)(p)<=u_Z(p). Suppose the inequality is strict and choose a finite T strictly between these two values. There are paths avoiding Z_s, of length at most T, whose endpoints approach p. None can hit Z\Z_s: at its first hit q=zeta(r), r>s, its initial portion avoids the entire old barrier, so u_Z(q)<=T. But monotonicity gives u_Z(q)>=u_Z(p)>T. Thus these paths avoid the whole barrier, which implies u_Z(p)<=T and is a contradiction. The same argument works if u_Z(p)=infinity. First contacts are justified by compactness of Z; an approaching path can be stopped before its first contact, which is exactly the reachable-closure convention used for the trace.

The argument also proves equality of the two reachable sets at times strictly below u_Z(p). It does not assert that modifying Z_s leaves later arrivals unchanged.

This formalizes the useful causal observation in Bianchini–Zizza's Remark 2.5. That remark also explicitly warns that changing already burned walls changes the reached region. The lemma is credited in that context, without a novelty assertion.

## 2. A conditional free-ray chart after an arbitrary fixed prefix

The first-round radial proof can be extended beyond a point or disk center when the incoming arrival field is controlled. Here are explicit sufficient hypotheses, rather than an assertion that every winding barrier satisfies them.

Let P be an unchanged past barrier, and suppose its arrival field has an injective smooth ray chart

    X(v,t)=q(v)+(t-t0)e(v),
    e(v)=(cos(alpha(v)),sin(alpha(v))),
    q'(v)=J0(v)e(v)^perp,
    J(v,t)=J0(v)+(t-t0)alpha'(v)>0.              (2)

Assume u_P(X(v,t))=t throughout the chart. Assume also that the limiting optimal rays (or arbitrarily close realizing paths) for this value reach its entry level t=t0 without meeting the new arc and then follow the displayed rays. More precisely, their earlier portions stay outside the region swept by the proposed arcs. A new terminal arc is the graph

    z(v)=X(v,f(v)),  a<=v<=b,

with f>t0, f'>0, endpoints fixed and its first endpoint attached to P. The whole new arc is inside the chart and no other barrier portion lies there. The same visibility conditions are imposed on sufficiently small graph perturbations. These hypotheses can accommodate a nonradial arrival field generated by earlier walls; they are stronger than bare simplicity and arrival order.

Under these conditions the arrival trace of P union graph(f) on the new arc is exactly f. Adding a barrier cannot lower u_P, and the incoming ray for label v reaches the new arc only at its endpoint, giving the matching upper bound. The same equality holds for the perturbed graph. If the past is already admissible and its trace is at most f(a), the new graph introduces no earlier first-contact obstruction in the past. This follows by the same first-contact argument as above.

Let L0 be the fixed length already constructed. The new prefix length and budget are

    L(v)=L0+integral_a^v F(w,f(w),f'(w)) dw,
    F(v,t,p)=sqrt(p^2+J(v,t)^2),
    G(v)=sigma*f(v)-L(v).                         (3)

For simplicity we require pointwise G>=0 throughout the new arc, which guarantees the ordered construction budget. With nondecreasing trace this is also the source's static prefix-budget condition, including any entire level-set plateau.

## 3. Curvature descent in the nonradial field

Write E=F_t-d/dv(F_p), evaluated along f, and kappa for the signed Euclidean curvature of z, with the orientation induced by increasing v. A direct calculation gives

    E = J*kappa.                                 (4)

For clarity, putting p=f', q2=f'', A=alpha', and J_v equal to the partial derivative at fixed t, the curvature numerator and Euler expression are

    det(z',z'') = A*J^2+2*A*p^2+J_v*p-J*q2,
    E = J*(A*J^2+2*A*p^2+J_v*p-J*q2)/(p^2+J^2)^(3/2).

Thus no convexity assumption on the incoming wavefront, or sign assumption on alpha', is needed; positivity of the chart Jacobian J is needed.

If the arc has negative curvature in an interior interval, choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth bump eta supported there and set f_epsilon=f+epsilon*eta. For small positive epsilon the stated chart/visibility and f'>0 conditions persist. Integration by parts gives

    dG_epsilon(v)/depsilon
      = (sigma-F_p)*eta(v)-integral_a^v E_epsilon(w)*eta(w) dw >= 0.       (5)

Here |F_p|<1<sigma and E_epsilon<0 on the bump support. At b the endpoint term vanishes, so dL_epsilon(b)/depsilon<0. Integrating in epsilon proves simultaneous improvement of every budget on this terminal prefix, even if some budgets were initially active.

Consequently a local length minimizer among these fixed-past, fixed-endpoint, ray-visible C2 terminal arcs has nonnegative curvature. This generalizes Turn 2's polar calculation to nonradial free fire rays. It does not derive the required ray chart or stable incoming-path hypotheses for an arbitrary point of a winding barrier.

## 4. The exact obstruction to splicing an unchanged suffix

Suppose an arc replacement shortens the constructed length by Delta>0, keeps its endpoints and an entire later suffix geometrically fixed, and let u_new and u_old be the actual full-barrier arrival traces. For any fixed suffix point p, the ordered budget changes by the exact amount

    G_new(p)-G_old(p) = Delta + sigma*(u_new(p)-u_old(p)).       (6)

Therefore preservation of every suffix budget is equivalent to

    sigma*(u_old(p)-u_new(p)) <= Delta+G_old(p)

at all such points. At an old saturated point this requires an arrival decrease no larger than Delta/sigma. Even a bound of Delta on that decrease would not suffice when sigma>1. The new curve must separately retain the required simplicity and arrival-order property.

Equations (1) and (5) settle the unchanged past and the modified ray-visible terminal prefix. They do not establish (6) for the later winding suffix. No claim has been made that the shorter arc automatically gives a valid global competitor. Establishing the required signed delay bound, or rebuilding a cheaper continuation under the original objective, is the remaining extension problem.

The source literature confirms that this is a substantive constraint. Bressan–Chiri, arXiv:2012.00799v1, introductory discussion and Figure 1, explains how removing an earlier delaying wall can make a later wall impossible to build in time. Their Lemma 3.1 gives a Hausdorff-perturbation continuity theorem for arrival times off a finite union of suitable components. It does not supply the Delta/sigma bound at later barrier points required here. Their main nowhere-denseness result also does not supply C2 regularity of every optimizer. The full PDF was recovered and these specific passages were read; none of its examples is being claimed as a counterexample to the OWR single-spiral question.

## 5. Objective and angle limits

The variational theorem uses only total barrier length with fixed past and endpoints. It is not a theorem for the weighted burned-area-plus-construction functional J or for the later paper's terminal ray-length objective. Enlarging a burned region can change J even when the wall shortens, and no sign for that term has been proved here.

The geometric hypotheses leave the initial point and tangent unchanged. They do not settle existence of a one-sided initial tangent for arbitrary optimal Lipschitz curves or reconcile the OWR's e2 condition with the later e1 condition. Both source conventions remain separately recorded.
