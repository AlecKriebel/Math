## Exact source and unresolved formulation

The target is Bianchini–Zizza's open-question paragraph in OWR 52/2023, printed p. 2957. It asks whether optimal spiral-like fire-containment strategies automatically have local convexity and A1. The broad definition requires arc-length parametrization, start (1,0), simplicity before the end, and nondecreasing arrival time. The narrower class assumes convexity and A1.

The short OWR passage does not specify the global objective or its complete quantifiers. Its A1 is an angle with vertical e2. The later 2025 preprint uses horizontal e1 and proves a terminal ray-length optimizer within a class already assumed convex. It also discusses a separate weighted burned-area-plus-construction objective and a point-fire normalization. These distinctions are material and have not been silently identified.

## Retained candidate partials

1. **Feasibility does not force local convexity.** A smooth strictly admissible finite radial prefix has changing curvature sign, with explicit construction-speed slack. It is neither confining nor claimed optimal.
2. **Conditional local convexity for a length objective.** In the C2 strictly outward radially visible fixed-endpoint prefix problem, an outward bump on a negative-curvature arc shortens length while increasing every construction-budget margin. No slack assumption is needed. This is not a theorem for the global weighted objective J.
3. **Causal and nonradial reductions.** Arrival order makes the trace at each point independent of the future suffix. A specified transverse incoming-ray chart supports the same conditional curvature descent after a fixed past. For an unchanged later suffix the exact missing bound is that arrival loss must not exceed (length saving + old budget slack)/sigma.
4. **An exact planar failure of that splice bound.** A radial segment, clockwise circular arc and terminal involute form a simple admissible prefix with nondecreasing arrival and a saturated endpoint. Shortening a shared earlier geodesic arc preserves its own prefix admissibility and all geometric/order conditions but advances the fixed suffix by the full length saving Delta. Its endpoint budget becomes −(sigma−1)Delta. The construction satisfies both initial-angle conditions. It is a nonconfining-prefix counterexample to a proof mechanism, not an optimal-strategy counterexample.
5. **Initial tangent regularity is not supplied by feasibility.** A strictly admissible Lipschitz radial prefix has neither a limiting tangent nor a limiting secant direction at its starting point, even though all sufficiently early tangent vectors lie in the first quadrant. It can be shortened in the conditional radial length problem and is not claimed optimal. A global optimality argument is still needed to exclude such behavior for the actual target.

## Remaining mathematical step
