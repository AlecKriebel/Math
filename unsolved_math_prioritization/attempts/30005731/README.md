# 30005731: automatic convexity of optimal fire barriers

**Unreviewed work in progress; 4/5 substantive author turns.** The original optimality question remains unresolved. Turn 1 constructs an explicit admissible nonconvex finite prefix, showing why bare feasibility is insufficient. It is not a confining barrier or an optimal strategy. Turn 2 proves a conditional convexity theorem for length-minimizing C2 strictly outward radially visible prefixes, by an outward perturbation that improves every budget even without slack. Turn 3 proves causal independence from unbuilt future walls, extends the variation to a specified nonradial incoming field, and isolates the exact unchanged-suffix budget condition. The full winding-barrier extension remains unproved.

The exact OWR target asks for automatic local convexity and its stated initial-angle condition for optimal spiral-like fire-containment strategies. The later sharp-threshold preprint assumes convexity in its class definition. The objective and initial-angle conventions need careful reconciliation; see `SOURCE_SCOPE.md`. No queue change or claimed-result PR belongs to this checkpoint.

Turn 4 gives an explicit planar obstruction to retaining an unchanged saturated suffix: a small shortening preserves local prefix admissibility but advances suffix arrivals by the full length saving, causing a budget failure. This is a nonconfining-prefix counterexample to a proof mechanism, not an optimal-strategy counterexample.
