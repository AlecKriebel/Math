# Known-result and source-curation audit

Checked 30 September 2026. The recommendation is **already_solved**, with no discovery credit to this campaign.

## Decisive original-source evidence

The complete [OWR 01/2007 report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46087?nt=1) was downloaded. Vénéreau's contribution is on printed pp. 24–27. On p. 26, Question 1 asks exactly the reduction-map surjectivity question and is immediately followed by an affirmative answer credited to van den Essen and Maubach. A two-variable, square-zero proof follows. Page 26 was rendered and visually inspected, so this is not a search-snippet inference. Page 27 cites the general JPAA result with DOI 10.1016/j.jpaa.2006.09.013.

The complete [2007 Acta Mathematica Vietnamica problem collection](https://math.ac.vn/public/uploads/files/0702303.pdf), edited by Nguyen Van Chau and Stefan Maubach, provides an additional explicit check. Vénéreau's entry on printed p. 317 says the map is surjective for every ring containing Q and all m,n≥1. It then distinguishes questions for other coefficient rings. Those different questions are not substituted for the selected target.

The dataset's `open` assessment therefore contradicts the source it cites. The preserved original record includes that historical assessment; this audit does not rewrite it.

## Published theorem and access limits

The [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022404906001873), the [Basel institutional record](https://edoc.unibas.ch/entities/publication/65022835-950e-4439-9383-7a104c3aa40e), and [Maubach's publication list](https://www.math.ru.nl/~maubach/publications.html) agree on the exact result and citation:

Arno van den Essen, Stefan Maubach and Stéphane Vénéreau, *The special automorphism group of R[t]/(t^m)[x1,…,xn] and coordinates of a subring of R[t][x1,…,xn]*, Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra 210(1), July 2007, 141–146, DOI [10.1016/j.jpaa.2006.09.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2006.09.013).

The institutional abstract explicitly states the full Q-algebra surjectivity, without reducedness. The original OWR introduction temporarily assumes reducedness for a different motivating construction; that must not narrow the published theorem.

The old author-hosted `EMV.pdf` link now redirects to a departmental landing page. An author-profile publication link was found, but its full article endpoint returned an access error. No complete JPAA PDF was recovered. No access barrier was bypassed, no account was created, and no author was contacted.

`KNOWN_THEOREM.md` therefore distinguishes three evidence levels: the original source's explicit answer, the published theorem's verified exact statement, and a self-contained credited proof reconstruction that can be audited without the unavailable article. It does not claim a line-by-line inspection of the full JPAA proof.

## Mathematical and terminology checks

The reconstruction uses only finite polynomial operations: divergence-free coefficients decompose into elementary shear vectors; each shear has an explicit polynomial inverse and exact Jacobian one; finitely many t-adic corrections lift each target jet. It works over nonreduced Q-algebras and includes n=1 and m=1. Its proof uses rational integration and a rational Vandermonde inverse, so the coefficient hypothesis is substantive. The supplied characteristic-p example is outside the selected target.

Here SAut means Jacobian determinant one, as defined in the original report. It must not be silently replaced by the differently named subgroup generated by additive-group actions in other literature. Nor does the result assert tame generation of all special automorphisms over R or solve the Jacobian conjecture.

No fresh open-problem discovery is claimed. One substantive reconstruction/validation family is counted conservatively in `turns.json`; no other proof-search family was pursued once the existing answer was identified.
