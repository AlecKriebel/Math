## Earlier evidence and the exact implication

Denis Auroux's [currently hosted thesis](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~auroux/papers/these.pdf),
bearing defense date January 22, 1999, printed p.7/PDF p.11, explicitly states examples
in $T^4$ with two components of genus $2k^2+1$, representing
$\operatorname{PD}(2k[\omega_0])$ in the normalization
$\omega=4\pi\omega_0$. It sketches a decomposition into two symplectic
classes and smoothing flat tori. The defense date is not a measured date
of public dissemination of the exact currently hosted file. The explicit
affine equations are not printed in that passage. Auroux's public 2020
comment says the intended construction was cut from the thesis; the
retained numerical existence statement and sketch must be distinguished
from that omitted explicit construction.

Emmanuel Giroux, *Remarks on Donaldson's symplectic submanifolds*,
*Pure and Applied Mathematics Quarterly*13(3), 369–388, nominal volume
year 2017, [DOI 10.4310/PAMQ.2017.v13.n3.a1](https://doi.org/10.4310/PAMQ.2017.v13.n3.a1),
Proposition 9 and its full proof, printed pp.376–377 / [arXiv v1 pp.8–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05929v1),
give the explicit disconnected construction and credit Auroux. The actual
publisher title page records receipt January 9, 2018; the manuscript's
December 2017 internal date is not measured public dissemination. The
secure public anchor used here is arXiv v1 submission March 15, 2018,
18:11:56 UTC. Earliest journal public release was not established.

For a disjoint pair $S,S'$ of genus-three components in that construction,
the union is dual to $2[\omega_0]$. The pair sequence and Thom isomorphism
give the diagonal map

\[
H_4(T^4;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\longrightarrow
H_4(T^4,M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2,\qquad1\longmapsto(1,1).
\]

Its cokernel injects into $H_3(M;\mathbb Z)$, obstructing Weinstein
topology. Rescaling the ambient form to $2\omega_0$ gives degree one,
still with two components. This rescaling is a checked deduction from
the earlier theorem, not documentation that an earlier author printed the
degree-one formulation. It establishes the disconnected-surface version;
it does not establish the connected target's historical priority.

The printed assertion that any two unequal translation vectors make the
nodal arrangements disjoint is overbroad. The specific parameters
$a=(0,0,1/4,1/4)$ and $b=(1/2,1/4,0,3/4)$ satisfy all four cross-pair
inequalities. Small smoothing preserves disjointness. This repairs the
existence claim without asserting the general parameter claim.

## Connected extension and limits of priority

The current candidate specializes the prior formulas to those offsets,
uses the free symplectic involution
$\tau(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(x_1+1/2,x_2,-x_3,-x_4)$, and takes the connected
quotient surface. Real transfer checks its class and the connected double
cover obstructs Weinstein topology. This is a short additional corollary
of the explicit construction and standard topology. The bare existential
disconnected-divisor statement does not alone ensure such a symmetry.

No primary passage actually read in the bounded audit explicitly states
this combined connected four-dimensional theorem. That observation is
not a proof that the theorem is new. Logical availability from earlier
formulas and documented historical priority are different claims. The
ambient quotient is a standard type-one bielliptic surface, and homology
above the middle dimension is an established non-Stein obstruction;
neither fact alone is an earlier statement of the combined target.

The [2020 Roux-divisor post and Auroux comment](https://symplectosaurus.wordpress.com/2020/03/06/roux-divisors/)

[Tonkonog–Varolgunes, arXiv:2003.07486v2, Remark 1.27 and footnote 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.07486v2)
cite that example and Giroux's disconnected one. These passages do not
give an ambient-four-dimensional reduction. Smooth toric-complement
obstructions inspected have nonpositive square or fail the required
cohomology condition; partially smoothed positive examples remain nodal.
General non-Weinstein Liouville domains do not automatically admit the
required positive-divisor compactification.
