## Relevant primary sources and what was actually checked

1. **Halldórsson, Kitaev, Pyatkin, Semi-Transitive Orientations and Word-Representable Graphs**. [arXiv:1501.07108v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.07108v1); published in Discrete Applied Mathematics 201 (2016), 164–171, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2015.07.033). Retrieved PDF and inspected the definition, compatible-star construction, Theorem 3/Corollary 1, Theorem 4 and Lemmas 5–7, and concluding question. Corrected general bound: 2N-4 for N>=3; clique refinement 2(N-omega). Apex-crown family has total order 2m+1. The older arXiv:0810.0310 claim R<=N is not used.

2. **Kitaev, A Comprehensive Introduction to the Theory of Word-Representable Graphs**. [arXiv:1705.05924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05924v1) and [author-hosted PDF](https://personal.strath.ac.uk/sergey.kitaev/Papers/wrg-kitaev.pdf). Inspected graph/word conventions, representation number, crown and apex discussion, general bound, and Section 9's question on printed page 35. The author-hosted web copy says May 30, 2017. The downloaded arXiv PDF title page says April 17, 2018 despite the v1 submission marker dated 16 May 2017. These differently dated renderings are not claimed byte-identical. Their mathematical question agrees. The survey's apex-family notation should be resolved using the actual 2m+1 count, not copied uncritically.

3. **Hiraguchi, On the Dimension of Partially Ordered Sets**. Science Reports of Kanazawa University 1(2) (1951), 77–94. [Original scanned article](https://scirep.w3.kanazawa-u.ac.jp/articles/01-02-001.pdf). Retrieved PDF; inspected the final page visually, especially (7.5), giving the maximum dimension floor(N/2) when N>=4. OCR alone was poor and was not relied on for the cutoff.

4. **Kitaev, On Graphs with Representation Number 3**. [arXiv:1403.1616](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1616). Retrieved PDF; inspected the prism definition and Theorems 16–18. The triangular prism's exact value 3 is an established result, also independently checked in this packet at the word level.

5. **Akgün, Gent, Kitaev, Zantema, Solving Computational Problems in the Theory of Word-Representable Graphs**. [arXiv:1808.01215](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01215); Journal of Integer Sequences 22 (2019), Article 19.2.5. Retrieved PDF, whose title page displays March 21, 2022 with an older arXiv submission marker. Inspected the odd-order apex conjecture and comparability theorem, and the connected-graph Table 1 through nine vertices. The table reports maximum finite R of 3 at orders 6,7,8 and 4 at order 9, with non-word-representable graphs accounted for separately. These published computations were not rerun here. They support no counterexample through order 9; our own exhaustive positive witnesses cover only orders through 5 and the prism test at 6.

6. **Hefty, Horn, Muir, Owens, Word-Representable Graphs: Orientations, Posets, and Bounds**. Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 31(4) (2024), P4.2, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.37236/12806), [public primary manuscript](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10601294). Retrieved PDF and inspected the main-results summary, Sections 4.1–4.2 and 5.5. It distinguishes graph representation number from fixed-orientation representation number and develops lower bounds, including bounds for particular bipartite classes. Its order-dimension discussion does not justify replacing an arbitrary graph by its transitive closure.

7. **Mozhui–Krishna, On the Conjecture of the Representation Number of Bipartite Graphs**. [arXiv:2506.01057v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01057v1), 1 June 2025. Retrieved PDF; inspected the standing connected/reduced convention, Corollary 1, Theorem 2, and Theorem 3. The general part-size bound is 1+ceil(m/2), with m the smaller part. The sharper odd-part theorem explicitly assumes 5<=m<=n. The abstract's abbreviated odd-m wording must not be applied to small m. Its balanced-even remaining case is superseded by the next paper.

8. **Colbrook–Drysdale, Crown Graphs Maximise the Representation Number of Bipartite Graphs**. [arXiv:2609.35842v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35842v1), 24 September 2026. Retrieved PDF and HTML; inspected Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2, scope conventions, the proof outline, local ordering construction, and statement of the computational/Lean dependencies. The theorem is R(G)<=ceil(N/4) for every finite simple bipartite graph with N>=9, allowing disconnected graphs, isolates, unequal parts and twins. The even-order maximum equals the crown value, with the small-order exceptions stated in the status file. This is verified attribution to a primary preprint, not an independent reproduction of its finite certificates or Lean build, and not a general-graph resolution.

9. **Hefty, Horn, Muir, Owens, Word-Representation Numbers of Graphs: Bottlenecks and Bounds**. Journal of Combinatorial Theory A 223 (October 2026), 106215, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2026.106215). The publisher's indexed primary abstract and metadata were read. It reports sharp lower bounds for almost every balanced bipartite graph, hypercube lower bounds, limitations of dimension comparisons and results on multi-word representations. Full publisher page retrieval returned HTTP 403; no PDF was obtained, and no numerical theorem from this paper is asserted here. It is also cited as prior work in the September 2026 primary preprint. An abstract check alone is insufficient to rule out every relevant result in the full paper.

10. **Kitaev–Pyatkin, On the Representation Number of Chessboard Graphs**. Acta Informatica 63, article 33 (18 September 2026), [publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00236-026-00547-w). The primary abstract and introductory definitions were inspected. This is a graph-class study, not evidence of a general half-order theorem. No full-paper inspection or PDF hash is claimed.
