# Commuting long virtual knots: the exact normal-form gap

Status: **original problem unresolved in this attempt, 1/5 approach families**. A 2025 prime-decomposition announcement creates an additional current-literature source hold: its full proof and precise uniqueness convention were not located. Separate adversarial review is pending. This is a conditional algebraic reduction and a logical obstruction, not a knot counterexample. No novelty claim.

## 1. Exact source and category

Problem 21, printed p.29 of Fenn–Ilyutko–Kauffman–Manturov, *Unsolved Problems in Virtual Knot Theory and Combinatorial Knot Theory*, arXiv:1409.2823v1, asks the following. If oriented long virtual knots satisfy
\[
K\#K'\simeq K'\#K,
\]
must there exist a long virtual knot \(L\), classical long knots \(Q,Q'\), and integers \(m,n\ge0\) such that
\[
K\simeq L^m\#Q,\qquad K'\simeq L^n\#Q'?
\tag{1}
\]
The exponents can be zero; \(L^0\) is the long unknot. This is one question, not a request to compute a particular diagram invariant.

A long virtual diagram is a generic immersion of the oriented real line in the plane, equal to the standard line outside a compact set, with each double point classical or virtual. Equivalence is generated by the classical Reidemeister moves and virtual detour moves, with the ends kept in their standard position. Concatenation is the well-defined monoid operation obtained by placing the two long diagrams successively. The long unknot is its identity. Chrisman's Definitions 1.1–1.4 state these conventions explicitly.

Classical long knots commute with every long virtual knot, by the local-knot sliding argument recalled in Chrisman's introduction. Consequently, any pair of the form (1) does commute. The only missing direction is the converse.

This is **not** the monoid of closed virtual knots: arbitrary closed connected sum is not a well-defined operation on unbased virtual isotopy classes. It is also not welded equivalence, which allows an additional forbidden move, nor concordance or long-flat equivalence. No cancellation, prime factorization, or invariant from any of those quotients is imported here.

The cases where one of \(K,K'\) is classical are immediate. If \(K\) is classical, take \(m=0,n=1,L=K',Q=K,Q'\) trivial. If both are classical, take both exponents zero. These are credited elementary cases, not new progress on the general nonclassical pair.

## 2. What the existing prime-pair theorem actually supplies

Chrisman's Theorem 1, arXiv:1311.5748v1, states that if \(A,B,C,D\) are nonclassical linearly prime long virtual knots, \(A\#B\) is nonclassical, and
\[
A\#B\simeq C\#D,
\]
then \(A\simeq C\) and \(B\simeq D\). The paper credits the original result to Manturov. A linearly prime knot is nontrivial and admits no factorization into two nontrivial concatenation factors.

In particular, a commuting pair of nonclassical linear primes with nonclassical product is equal, by setting \(C=B,D=A\). For that case (1) holds with \(L=A\), \(m=n=1\), and trivial classical factors. The product hypothesis is preserved in this application.

The survey's Section 8.5.2, printed pp.50–51, calls this a partial result and separately asks for existence and uniqueness of prime decompositions of long virtual knots. It distinguishes other decompositions from linear concatenation. Chrisman's proof in Section 4.3, especially printed pp.26–29, uses linear primeness to trivialize pieces cut off by intersecting decomposition disks. Replacing a prime factor by an arbitrary composite is therefore not an automatic induction step. This note does not independently reprove that full geometric theorem.

## 3. A precise sufficient normal-form theorem

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the source's long-virtual-knot monoid and \(\mathcal C\) its central submonoid of classical knots. The following two assertions would suffice:

- **Existence:** there is a specified set \(\mathcal P\) of nonclassical knot classes such that every \(K\in\mathcal V\) has a presentation \(K=P_1\#\cdots\#P_r\#Q\), with \(P_i\in\mathcal P\), \(Q\in\mathcal C\), and \(r\ge0\).
- **Ordered uniqueness:** whenever two such presentations represent the same long virtual knot, their ordered lists of \(\mathcal P\)-factors are identical, including multiplicities. No assertion about uniqueness of the remaining classical factor is needed for the argument below.

These are explicitly hypotheses, not established facts of this package. In particular, uniqueness of a multiset is not silently substituted for ordered uniqueness.

**Conditional proposition.** Under these two hypotheses, Problem 21 has an affirmative answer.

**Proof.** To a presentation attach its word \(w(K)=P_1\cdots P_r\) in the free monoid on the alphabet \(\mathcal P\). Ordered uniqueness makes it well-defined. Centrality of classical factors lets us present \(KK'\) by concatenating the words and putting the classical factors at the end. Thus
\[
w(KK')=w(K)w(K').
\]
If \(KK'=K'K\), the words \(u=w(K)\), \(v=w(K')\) commute.

The elementary commuting-word lemma says that commuting finite words are powers of one common word. Here is a proof to make the dependency explicit. If one word is empty, the other is a common root with one exponent zero. Otherwise, assume \(|u|\le|v|\). The equality \(uv=vu\) forces \(u\) to be a prefix of \(v\), so \(v=uz\). Cancelling the common prefix in the free monoid gives \(uz=zu\). Induction on \(|u|+|v|\) gives \(u=t^a,z=t^b\), hence \(v=t^{a+b}\). The equal-length case has \(z\) empty and is included. If both words are empty, take the empty common root.

Let \(L\) be the concatenation of the actual knot classes named by the common root word \(t\). The original presentations now read \(K=L^m\#Q\) and \(K'=L^n\#Q'\), exactly as required. No knot cancellation law was used: cancellation occurred only in the free monoid after the unproved normal-form hypotheses had been imposed. QED.

## 4. Why prime-pair rigidity alone cannot justify that normal form

There is a short abstract monoid model which satisfies a prime-pair rigidity statement but violates the common-power conclusion. It is a diagnostic for a proposed proof, **not a realized knot monoid or a knot counterexample**.

Let
\[
M=\langle a,b\mid abba=baab\rangle^+,
\qquad N=\mathbb N\times M,
\]
where the displayed presentation means the free monoid on \(a,b\), modulo replacements of the two length-four words inside arbitrary contexts. In \(N\), multiplication adds the first coordinates and concatenates the second. Designate \(C=\mathbb N\times\{\varepsilon\}\) as the classical submonoid.

Word length is well-defined on \(M\), because every defining replacement preserves length. No replacement can be performed on any word of length less than four. In particular, all length-two words are distinct in \(M\), and the only length-zero word is the identity.

Every element factors into the atoms \((1,\varepsilon),(0,a),(0,b)\). The designated classical submonoid is central. The nonclassical atoms are precisely \((0,a),(0,b)\); every element involving at least two letters, or both a positive first coordinate and a letter, visibly factors. Products of two nonclassical atoms are nonclassical and have unique ordered two-atom decompositions, because no two length-two words are identified. Thus this model has the relevant prime-pair rigidity, as well as finite atomic factorizations and central classical factors.

Nevertheless,
\[
X=(0,ab),\qquad Y=(0,ba)
\]
are distinct and commute: \(XY=(0,abba)=(0,baab)=YX\).

Suppose they had the analogue of (1), with \(L=(c,w)\), classical factors \((q,\varepsilon),(q',\varepsilon)\), and nonnegative exponents \(m,n\). Comparing word lengths gives
\[
2=m|w|=n|w|.
\]
Hence \(|w|>0\), \(m,n>0\), and \(m=n\). Comparing first coordinates gives \(mc+q=nc+q'=0\), so \(c=q=q'=0\). The two purported factorizations are then identical, forcing \(X=Y\), a contradiction.

Thus prime-pair rigidity, centrality of classical factors, and existence of finite atomic factorizations do not by themselves imply the source's conclusion. Additional geometric compatibility or ordered uniqueness is genuinely needed. We do not assert that this abstract model shares every additional property of the actual long-knot monoid.

## 5. Current-literature source hold

The official **AustMS 2025 conference booklet**, printed p.194, records a 10 December 2025 talk by Tilda Wilkinson-Finch, joint with Hans Boden and Zsuzsanna Dancso, entitled *Prime Decomposition Theorems for Long Virtual Knots*. Its abstract announces existence and uniqueness and describes a Diamond-Lemma approach. This is relevant and may close part or all of the needed geometric gap.

However, the abstract does not specify the normal-form equivalence, the full class of allowed decompositions, the ordering convention, or the complete proof. In bounded searches of the exact title and authors, arXiv, and institutional publication pages, no full manuscript or proof was located. It would be inaccurate to turn this abstract into either an independently audited theorem or a claim that the 2014 question is certainly still open in the literature. The conservative status here is **unresolved by this attempt, with a literature source hold**. No author was contacted.

A further current source is Nozomu Sekino's arXiv:2608.27934v1, submitted 28 August 2026. Section 5, Theorem 5.1, proves necessary reciprocal-writhe-polynomial identities for a commuting pair, and Corollary 5.1 constrains a knot commuting with every long virtual knot. The paper's Remark 5.1 explicitly discusses already-known noncommuting pairs that its invariant cannot distinguish under multiplication. These are necessary tests, not sufficient commutativity tests and not a proof of the common-power assertion. We read the full relevant section and its stated dependencies, but did not certify the entire new biquandle construction independently. The current arXiv pages for this paper and Chrisman's show v1 only and no journal reference or withdrawal notice.

## 6. Verification and exact stopping point

The standard-library checker tests the elementary commuting-word lemma exhaustively on binary words of length at most four, verifies the small presentation relation through finite rewrite classes, and checks the abstract common-power obstruction using the grading and all possible short roots. These are exact algebra controls, not an enumeration of virtual knots or isotopies.

The remaining task is geometric: either establish the sufficient ordered normal form (or a weaker centralizer theorem that directly yields (1)), or construct actual commuting long virtual knots that violate (1). The retrieved two-prime theorem and polynomial invariants supply neither. The 2025 announcement must be matched to a full precise proof before using it as a resolution. This is one substantive normal-form/rigidity approach, stopped at that gap rather than promoted to a solution.
