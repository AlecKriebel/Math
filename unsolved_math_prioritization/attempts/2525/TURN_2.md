# Turn 2: finite-index transport and strong-kernel reduction

## Direction and outcome

We test whether passing to an extension can repair failure of CB while preserving an unbounded positive word metric. Two fully proved transport statements restrict this route: MB passes to finite-index overgroups, and adjoining a strongly bounded normal kernel preserves both CB and MB exactly. General infinite extensions with only an MB kernel remain outside the proof. These are structural partials with no novelty claim.

## 1. Directed Schreier rewriting for finite-index overgroups

Let H<=G have finite index m and assume H is MB. Take any monoid-generating set S of G, with positive length l. The directed graph of right cosets Hg, with edges Hg→Hgs for s in S, is strongly connected: every quotient displacement has a positive S word because S monoid-generates G. Choose a shortest positive representative r_C for each coset C, with r_H=1. Removing loops from a shortest path shows l(r_C)<=q<=m-1. Let

    c=max_C l(r_C^{-1}) < infinity.

Its finiteness uses the finite number of cosets and genuine positive generation. It is not bounded by m-1 in general. Put

    T={r_C s r_{Cs}^{-1}: C a right coset, s in S} subset H.

Every T element has positive S length at most q+1+c. Moreover T **monoid-generates** H. To see this, write h=s_1...s_a positively and follow its coset path C_0=H,C_1,...,C_a=H. The product

    (r_C0 s_1 r_C1^{-1})...(r_C(a-1) s_a r_Ca^{-1})

telescopes to h. No inverse T letters occur. Because H is MB, there is k with H=(T union {1})^k. Every g=h r_C then has

    l(g) <= k(q+1+c)+q.

Thus G is MB. This proof does not infer CB of arbitrary finite-index subgroups from CB of G; that inference is false by Khelif's credited announcement. The displayed bound depends on S through c and k, as permitted by the original problem.

## 2. Quotients and a bounded-restriction lemma

Both CB and MB pass to arbitrary quotient groups. If a quotient is generated by X in the relevant sense, choose lifts of X and adjoin every element of the kernel. These elements generate G in the same sense. A finite diameter upstairs projects to a finite diameter in X downstairs; kernel letters disappear.

Call a group N strongly bounded here if it is CB and has no countable increasing exhaustion by proper subgroups. This definition is enough for the next proof; no metric characterization is assumed.

**Bounded-restriction lemma.** If N is a strongly bounded subgroup of G and S monoid-generates G, then its positive word length l is bounded on N, even when S intersect N does not generate N.

Proof: The symmetric sets

    E_n={x in N: l(x)<=n and l(x^{-1})<=n}

increase and exhaust N. Their subgroup closures exhaust N. Uncountable cofinality gives <E_n>=N for some n. CB of N supplies k with N=E_n^k. Hence sup_{x in N} l(x)<=nk. This is the credited Bergman exhaustion argument applied to an ambient length, with its hypotheses explicit. For a symmetric group-generating set S the same proof applies to its ordinary word length.

## 3. Exact preservation under a strongly bounded normal kernel

Let N normal in G be strongly bounded and write Q=G/N. Then

    G is CB iff Q is CB;
    G is MB iff Q is MB.

The forward implications are quotient preservation. For the reverse MB implication, fix an arbitrary monoid-generating S of G. Let l_Q be the positive length for the projected set and let C=sup_N l, finite by the lemma. For every g choose a positive S word w of length l_Q(gN) whose image is gN. Then g=w n for n in N, and

    l_Q(gN) <= l(g) <= l_Q(gN)+C.                 (1)

If Q is MB, l_Q is bounded for this projected set, so l is bounded. Since S was arbitrary, G is MB. The CB proof is identical with symmetric word metrics and Q's CB property. Equation(1) holds for this S even if Q has unbounded positive diameter.

In particular an extension by a finite normal kernel can neither create nor remove a separating example. More generally, a CB/non-MB example with a strongly bounded normal subgroup projects to another CB/non-MB example, and every extension of a separating group by such a kernel is again separating. These are reductions, not existence statements.

## 4. Where the unrestricted extension argument stops

Suppose only that N and Q are MB. A positive short representative r for a quotient coset does not imply a uniform positive bound for r^{-1} as the coset ranges over an infinite Q. The directed Schreier factors r s r'^{-1} therefore need not have bounded ambient positive length. Likewise, MB of N controls a generating set whose letters all have unit cost; it does not automatically bound an arbitrary ambient length on N. The bounded-restriction lemma uses uncountable cofinality essentially at precisely this point.

We have not established that arbitrary MB extensions are MB, nor that their failure would yield the requested CB/non-MB separation. The finite-index result avoids the obstacle because there are only finitely many inverse representatives. The strong-normal-kernel result avoids it by directly bounding all of N in the ambient metric.

## 5. Exact controls

For all nonempty generating subsets in C2 through C8, D6 and D8, the checker enumerates all subgroups, chooses actual shortest directed coset representatives, constructs T and verifies positive generation, telescoping word-cost bounds and the finite-index estimate. For every normal subgroup it computes the exact quotient distances and verifies both inequalities in(1). Every group in these finite controls is already MB; the tests do not certify any infinite extension or settle the target.

The next direction investigates explicit one-sided witnesses and the extra coherence needed when normalizing them across a finite extension. No complete target candidate has emerged after two turns.
