## 3. Independent pole audit, including N=4

The outer normal's bilinear squared norm is proportional to

\[
 D(u)=d_v^2-k^2c_v^2\operatorname{sn}^2u,
\]

not the original-chord-pedal denominator. On the lattice generated by
(2K\) and (2iK'\), `sn²` has degree two. Its two relevant roots are the
distinct points (K+iK'\pm v\). Substitution and differentiation verify the
listed simple-root derivatives. The common Jacobi poles themselves are
removable for the foot: clearing the normal's leading pole leaves a nonzero
leading bilinear norm because (a\neq b\).

Here is a separate way to see the important adjacent-pole cancellation.
Write (N_\pm=n_x\pm i n_y\), (Z=M_x+iM_y\), (W=M_x-iM_y\). The complex
foot coordinates satisfy

\[
 q_+=\frac Z2+\frac{1-N_+W/2}{N_-},\qquad
 q_-=\frac W2+\frac{1-N_-Z/2}{N_+}.
\]

At both roots in one imaginary row, (N_+=0\) and
(N_-=-2/(\alpha k)\neq0\), up to a common real-half-period sign. Thus only
one foot coordinate can have a simple pole. In the other imaginary row the
roles reverse. Any two simultaneously singular adjacent vertices therefore
have their singular parts in the same isotropic coordinate. Their
determinant cannot have a double-pole term.

This argument covers every adjacent determinant separately. At (N=4\) all
four vertices are singular at the same phase, but they still share the same
singular coordinate. In particular the cyclic fourth-to-first edge is
included. No assumption that the two adjacent pairs are disjoint is needed
there. The finite indexing controls independently include all four edges in
that case and the two pairs for larger target periods.

## 4. Reduced torus and uniqueness

The real radial identity implies the required (M\mapsto-M\) and reflected
(M\) invariances of the meromorphic area by analytic continuation. The
normal's Jacobi transformations then give real period (2K\) and imaginary
anti-period (2iK'\). Cyclic reindexing gives the step as an additional real
period. Primitivity supplies the Bezout relation, so the reduced real period
is (h=2K/(N/2)\).

On the compact torus with periods (h,4iK'\), there are at most two simple
area poles, separated by (2iK'\). The shifted trace (S(u+K+v)\) has exactly
these poles and is nonconstant. Its residue does not cancel: at any pole in
an unreduced real cell the pertinent term occurs twice with identical
residue. The distinct translates then descend to the reduced cell. This
does not introduce an erroneous extra multiplicity factor.

Matching one residue cancels the other by anti-periodicity. The difference
is holomorphic on the compact torus, hence constant, and anti-periodicity
forces that constant to vanish. When (N/2\) is even, (K\) is itself a real
period of the trace. This is precisely the needed (N=0\pmod4\) alignment
and yields proportionality to the outer-area trace (S(u+v)\).

The scalar is real on real phases. Combining it with the radial identity
gives the claimed real phase-independent (c_0,c_2\). This argument also
allows the scalar to vanish for a special point (M\); it never divides by
a possibly zero pedal area.

## 5. Positivity and the exact denominator domain

The normal angle increases strictly, with each step lying between zero and
(\pi\) because the parameter step lies between zero and (2K\). Origin feet
are positive multiples of the normals. Every adjacent origin-foot
determinant is positive, giving (c_0>0\), including stars.

For a convex family the positive normal increments over the first half sum
to (\pi\). If all are at most (\pi/2\), the doubled-sine sum is positive
when there are at least three increments. If one is larger, the remaining
positive doubled increments have sum less than (\pi\), and the strict
sine addition inequality makes their sum dominate the one negative term.
This proves (c_2>0\) for convex target periods (N\geq8\).

At (N=4\) the two half-orbit increments are supplementary, so (c_2=0\).
At the axis-endpoint phase the outer rectangle has area (4ab\), and its
origin-foot rhombus area is (2ab\). This proves (c_0=1/2\) and the exact
all-(M\) ratio 2, including the boundary-period case separately.

For the specified strictly noncircular star (k=1/4,N=8,\tau=3\), the exact
amplitude/normal-angle estimates give

\[
 \frac{45\pi}{64}\leq\Delta_i\leq\frac{4\pi}{5}.
\]

This interval is strictly between (\pi/2\) and (\pi\), so every doubled
sine is negative. Hence (c_2<0\). The displayed elliptic expression for
(\rho^2\) is positive and finite; proportionality makes it independent of
phase. Every fixed point on (|M|=\rho\) gives zero pedal area throughout
the family, with finite feet and strictly positive outer area.

The exceptional set is therefore a genuine undefined raw quotient with
nonzero numerator, not a removable (0/0\) value. In all families the zero
set is either empty or exactly the stated circle, since (c_0>0\). These
qualifications do not refute ratio invariance on its natural domain, and
must remain in any public summary.
