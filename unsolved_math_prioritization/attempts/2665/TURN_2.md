# Turn 2: composite genus-one cores and the one-step realization graph

**Unreviewed scoped partial, author turn 2/5. Original KP-1.6 unresolved.**
This turn attacks nonsingular composite-leading cores rather than repeating
stabilization. It obtains further positive cases using the exact published
disjoint-pair criterion, and identifies a small explicit remaining pair.
The prescribed-pair and whole-class questions stay distinct.

## 1. Exact square-discriminant coordinates and credited inputs

Let N=2k+1>=3 be odd. For a relatively prime to N put

    V_(N,a) = [ a  k+1 ] ,   Q_(N,a)=a x²+Nxy.
              [ k    0 ]

The skew part is the standard positive symplectic matrix. The determinant is
−k(k+1), and

    det(tV−V^T)=−k(k+1)t²+(2k(k+1)+1)t−k(k+1).            (1)

The quadratic discriminant is N². Using the same skew convention matters:
a congruence between these displayed Seifert matrices must have determinant
+1, since P^T J P=(det P)J. Hence the SL2(Z) equivalence classes of Q match
these integral bilinear-form congruence classes.

The following are exact inputs from Aka–Feller–Miller–Wieser v5:

- Propositions 6.7–6.8 identify the primitive classes of discriminant N²
  with (Z/NZ)^× by a↦[Q_(N,a)]; composition is multiplication of residues
- Lemma 6.9 identifies the special classes [A x²+xy+C y²] with AC=−k(k+1)
  as residues 2 beta delta, where beta|k and delta|k+1
- Theorem 1.1 says two genus-one forms are simultaneously realizable by
  disjoint interiors at one boundary knot exactly when their ratio is the
  square of such a special class
- Theorem 5.8 (crediting Bandes and Miller) says S-equivalence is the orbit
  relation of the subgroup generated by these special squares

These inputs are not new discoveries. They are used with primitive cores;
no unproved assertion about arbitrary nonprimitive group division is needed.

Define the finite sets

    T_N={4 beta² delta² mod N: beta|k, delta|k+1},
    H_N=the subgroup of (Z/NZ)^× generated by T_N.          (2)

Signs of divisors disappear on squaring, so positive divisors suffice.
Every displayed residue is a unit: N is relatively prime to 2, k and k+1.
The set contains 1 by beta=k, delta=1. It is inverse-closed because replacing
(beta,delta) by (k/beta,(k+1)/delta) changes 2 beta delta to its negative
inverse modulo N. Thus T_N is a subgroup exactly when it is closed under
multiplication, in which case it equals H_N.

## 2. A finite exact criterion that settles additional prescribed pairs

For primitive classes represented by a,b, the source theorems give

    V_(N,a) ~S V_(N,b) iff b a^(−1) mod N lies in H_N,

and

    a disjoint common-boundary realization exists
      iff b a^(−1) mod N lies in T_N.                     (3)

Consequently the following statements are equivalent:

(i) Every S-equivalent pair of primitive genus-one forms of discriminant N²
    has a disjoint common-boundary realization
(ii) T_N=H_N
(iii) T_N is multiplicatively closed.

The nontrivial direction is just as important as sufficiency: if every
primitive pair is realized disjointly, apply it to a=1 and every b in H_N.
Equation (3) forces H_N⊆T_N. No assertion about intersecting pairs is used.

The criterion is finite and decidable by integer divisor enumeration and
multiplication modulo N. When it holds, the prescribed-pair KP-1.6 conclusion
also holds for all larger matrices whose reduced cores are these forms:
first realize the two cores by (3), then reverse each reduction on the same
boundary knot using the geometric enlargement input of turn 1. The resulting
higher-genus surfaces need not remain disjoint, which the main problem does
not require. This is a transfer through a successful core realization, not
an attempted geometric destabilization.

For example, direct exact calculation gives:

    N       T_N = H_N
    5       {1,4}
    7       {1,2,4}
    9       {1,4,7}
    11      {1,3,4,5,9}
    15      {1,4}
    21      {1,4,16}
    33      {1,4,16,25,31}.

Their leading coefficients k(k+1) are composite. Thus these are genuinely
outside the prime/unit criterion of turn 1, although their realization input
is the credited AFMW theorem.

## 3. A proved conditional family, without an infinitude assertion

Suppose k=2^r and k+1 is prime, with r>=0; N=2^(r+1)+1. Then
beta=2^j, 0<=j<=r, and delta is either 1 or k+1. Modulo N,
`k+1=2^(−1)`. Thus the special residues are, up to sign, precisely
`2^j` for 0<=j<=r+1. Squaring gives

    T_N={4^j mod N: 0<=j<=r+1}.                           (4)

Since 2^(r+1)=−1 modulo N, 4^(r+1)=1 modulo N. Formula (4) therefore already
contains the entire cyclic subgroup generated by 4, so T_N=H_N and the
prescribed-pair conclusion follows.

This supplies, for example, N=5,9,33,513,131073 from the known prime values
3,5,17,257,65537 of k+1. No claim of infinitely many such primes is made.
The primality qualification is material to this proof: for r=7, N=257,
exact computation gives |T_N|=24 but |H_N|=128. A blanket extension to all
powers-of-two k would be false as a disjoint-pair claim.

## 4. When these cases also settle a whole S-class

If |H_N|<=2 and T_N=H_N, every primitive S-class has at most two core
congruence types. A single AFMW pair realization realizes those two types
(or take any surface if there is one type). The enlargement argument then
realizes every matrix in that entire S-class at the same chosen knot.

In particular N=5 and N=15 give the stronger whole-class conclusion for
primitive genus-one core classes with leading absolute coefficient 6 and
56 respectively. The source S-equivalence theorem preserves primitivity of
a primitive core: its orbit lies in the primitive class group. All reduced
cores in the S-class have genus one by (1), so no higher-rank core has been
omitted.

For |H_N|>2, complete pairwise realizability does not by itself produce one
knot for the whole class. The boundary knot supplied for different pairs
may differ. We do not turn a finite family of pairwise existence statements
into simultaneous realization, and do not use the K3 remark's “rephrasing”
as a theorem exchanging these quantifiers.

## 5. An explicit remaining composite pair, and why graph connectivity fails

For N=13, k=6, exact computation yields

    T_13={1,3,4,9,10},
    H_13={1,3,4,9,10,12}.

Take

    V_1 = [1  7],    V_12 = [12 7].
          [6  0]             [6  0]

Both are nonsingular Seifert matrices with determinant −42 and Alexander
polynomial `−42 t²+85t−42`. Their classes are distinct because 1 and 12
are different primitive residues modulo 13. They are nevertheless
S-equivalent because 12=3·4 modulo 13 lies in H_13.

The element 4 comes from the square of the special class represented by
`7x²+xy−6y²` (beta=delta=1). The element 3 comes from the square of
`14x²+xy−3y²` (beta=2, delta=1). Hence there is a two-edge realization chain

    [Q_(13,1)] -> [Q_(13,4)] -> [Q_(13,12)].               (5)

Each edge is realized by disjoint genus-one surfaces on a knot. But 12 is
not in T_13, so the two end forms cannot be realized with disjoint interiors.
This does **not** exclude a realization by intersecting surfaces at one knot,
and supplies no negative answer to KP-1.6.

The tempting connectivity argument has a precise gap. AFMW's use of
Scharlemann–Thompson gives, for genus-one surfaces already known to have the
same boundary knot, a chain of same-knot surfaces with consecutive disjoint
interiors. In (5), the existence theorem instead gives potentially different
knots for different edges. Algebraic connectivity of the class graph is
exactly S-equivalence; it does not provide a common knot for that chain.

Connected sum does not cancel the gap. If (A,B) is realized at K_1 and (B,C)
at K_2, then K_1#K_2 realizes the pairs (A⊕B,B⊕C) and (A⊕C,B⊕B) by the
usual boundary-connected-sum construction. Those forms have an additional
whole B block, not merely an Alexander-trivial stabilization. Their Alexander
polynomial has an additional factor Delta_B, which cannot be removed by
S-reduction when Delta_B is nonconstant. Thus this elementary amalgamation
does not realize the required unstabilized end forms.

## 6. Remaining target and next step

This turn settles further composite-leading prescribed pairs and some entire
primitive classes, but it does not settle the N=13 end pair above or arbitrary
composite cores. A successful next route must construct surfaces with one
common boundary despite multiple realization steps, or give an obstruction
that excludes all intersecting realizations. Neither failure of disjointness
nor failure of a chosen geometric reduction has that strength.
