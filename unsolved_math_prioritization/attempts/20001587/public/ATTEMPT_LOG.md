## 1. Local transport and germ realization

Checkpoint: 07:14 UTC. Estimated progress toward the original goal: 10%.

Mechanism: move small clopen pieces into the presumed amenable corner by minimality; use conjugation to transfer amenability. Explicitly construct local extensions with one swap, or two swaps when a germ fixes its basepoint. This checks the isotropy case that a distinct-source/range argument alone misses.

Outcome: Lemmas 1–2; each point has arbitrarily small amenable rigid corners and a finite partition can be chosen with all atom corners amenable. A single partial compression of any nonamenable corner into U is impossible.

Failure of the global route: the groups associated with the pieces are not a directed system containing arbitrary finite subsets of G. An arbitrary group generated by amenable subgroups need not be amenable. No conclusion about G follows from that observation alone.

Prior-work comparison: Scarparo's Lemma 3.3 has a stronger alternating-group local-realization assertion. This local mechanism is credited rather than novel.

## 2. Invariant measures and analytic criteria

Checkpoint: 07:17 UTC. Estimated progress: 15%.

Mechanism: average the H-action on U; extend its probability using a finite family of local transports. Compute the measure on each piece and compare it under an arbitrary g using the two-index refinement A_i intersection g^{-1}A_j.

Outcome: Proposition 3 proves a unique finite invariant extension, normalized-restriction bijection, full support, no atoms, and μ(U)>=1/m. Scarparo's Proposition 3.1 already contains the transfer mechanism; the complete proof was inspected.

Analytic attempt: test the later Alekseev–Finn-Sell criterion and Scarparo's dichotomy for promotion to G-amenability. The former requires all corners, not one; the latter distinguishes the alternating full group from the full group and does not identify the general quotient. These are genuine missing hypotheses, not details to be suppressed.

Gap: an invariant probability on X is not an invariant mean on G. This approach is blocked at that exact distinction.

## 3. Finite partition stabilizers and coamenability

Checkpoint: 07:19 UTC. Estimated progress: 20%.

Mechanism: replace informal finite gluing by an exact kernel and quotient. Fullness gives the product of rigid atom groups as the pointwise partition stabilizer; the setwise stabilizer is a finite extension. Then average an invariant mean on the coset space with a mean on that stabilizer.

Outcome: Proposition 4 proves amenability of both partition stabilizers and the exact equivalence between G-amenability and coamenability of the setwise partition stabilizer. The proof supplies the coset averaging formula and checks representative-independence.

Tested sufficiency: an invariant finite clopen refinement for every finitely generated subgroup would finish the problem. The rooted-tree proof in Juschenko–Nekrashevych–de la Salle has precisely this extra ingredient.

Gap: finite local descriptions of generators do not imply a finite partition invariant under all their products. The missing invariant mean on the partition orbit is not constructed.

## 4. Finite and binary amplification

Checkpoint: 07:23 UTC. Estimated progress: 25%.

Mechanism: preserve multiplicities by mapping each transport atom to its own copy of U, thereby avoiding the unjustified claim that transported ranges are disjoint in U. Conjugation on the resulting clopen subset, followed by identity extension, is an exact group embedding.

Outcome: Proposition 5 and Theorem 6. The universal original assertion is equivalent to amenability preservation under two-layer amplification of full minimal Cantor groups. Iterated doubling gives all finite amplifications; associativity is proved at the level of local maps.

Failed shortcut: the amplified group contains maps whose choice of target layer varies with the base point. It is not automatically H wr Sym(2), and amenability of the underlying product action group does not settle amenability of its full closure.

Gap: binary stability is unproved. This exact equivalent question is a reformulation, not a resolution, and historical novelty is unverified.

## 5. Counterexample construction and countable reduction

Checkpoint: 07:25 UTC. Estimated progress: 25%.

Mechanism: attempt to build a counterexample from free translations in a compact group, and attempt to localize nonamenability on a separate clopen component. At the same time reduce any genuine example to a countable full group containing a finitely generated nonamenable witness.

Outcome: Proposition 7 proves the countable reduction using a countable basis and finite translate covers. Boundary example A gives a free minimal F_2 Cantor action with trivial proper rigid stabilizers, but fails fullness. Boundary example B is full and has an amenable clopen corner, but fails minimality. Their failures are proved explicitly. The residual-finiteness ingredient is constructed from finite completed partial permutations of free-group balls rather than assumed as an unspecified action.

Gap: no construction satisfies minimality, fullness, ambient nonamenability and one amenable nonempty corner simultaneously. A countable reduction does not ensure a Hausdorff germ groupoid, so that additional condition cannot be silently inserted.
