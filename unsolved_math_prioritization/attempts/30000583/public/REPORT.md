# A known counterexample to OWR-1326-004

Problem ID: 30000583. Checked 4 October 2026.

## Result and attribution

The asserted nonexistence is false. The six-dimensional variety of reductions
of `sl_3(C)` is a smooth Fano manifold with second Betti number one, different
from projective space, and contains an embedded projective plane with normal
bundle `O_{P^2}^{⊕4}`. An embedding is, in particular, an immersion.

This is a verification using a known variety, not a claim of a new Fano
construction or a newly discovered counterexample. Iliev and Manivel constructed
and studied the variety in a 2003 preprint, published in 2005. The 2014 paper of
Muñoz, Occhetta and Solá Conde explicitly identifies the associated examples as
counterexamples to the closely related positive-dimensional linear-VMRT
conjecture. The proof below spells out the normal-bundle implication directly.

Recommended mathematical status: **already_solved**, by counterexample.
The research package contains one substantive prior-result verification attempt.
Independent review of this package remains a separate validation step.

## 1. Exact target and source identity

The target is the following assertion over the complex numbers: if `X` is a
smooth projective Fano manifold with `b_2(X)=1`, and `X` is not projective space,
then for every integer `1<k<dim(X)` there is no holomorphic immersion
`P^k -> X` whose holomorphic normal bundle is trivial.

The catalogue code OWR-1326-004 refers to Hwang's Conjecture 3 in *Rigid targets
of surjective holomorphic maps*, within *Komplexe Analysis*, Oberwolfach Report
40/2006, printed page 2420. The catalogue's numerical code does not mean a
2013 workshop. Its statement agrees with this primary source. The source's
nearby cross-references contain numbering slips; our target is the displayed
Conjecture 3, not the neighboring rigid-target conjecture.

The catalogue URL returned HTTP 403 during this check. Its pinned imported
record was therefore cross-checked against the accessible primary report.
The imported August 2026 literature assessment did not include the relevant
counterexample literature and is not a reliable open-status certificate.

## 2. The precise external theorem used

Let `g = sl_3(C)`, let `h` be its two-dimensional diagonal traceless subalgebra,
and define

`X = closure{ Ad(g_0) h : g_0 in PGL_3(C) } ⊂ Gr(2,g)`.

This is the variety called `Y_2` by Iliev and Manivel. The following are
established properties of that variety:

- `X` is smooth and projective, of dimension six.
- Its Picard group is generated by the restricted Plücker bundle `H`.
- `-K_X = 3H`; in particular, `X` is Fano.

Source: Iliev–Manivel, *Severi varieties and their varieties of reductions*,
arXiv:math/0306328v1, Theorem 3.11 (smoothness), Corollary 3.17 (Fano index
and Picard group), and Proposition 4.1 with its following paragraph
(identification with abelian planes in `sl_3`, including the open orbit).
The dimension also follows from `dim PGL_3 - dim N(h) = 8-2=6`.

The subsequent paper Muñoz–Occhetta–Solá Conde, Appendix A, Lemma A.3,
Propositions A.5 and A.8, supplies a geometric corroboration through trisecant
congruences of projected Severi varieties. Its Remark A.9 records the
failure of the linear-VMRT nonexistence conjecture. We do not assume that
Remark A.9 alone proves triviality of the required normal bundle.

Because `X` is Fano, Kodaira vanishing gives `H^1(X,O_X)=H^2(X,O_X)=0`:
apply the theorem to `K_X⊗(-K_X)=O_X`. The exponential exact sequence then
identifies `Pic(X)` with `H^2(X,Z)`. Consequently `b_2(X)=1`.
Also `X` is not `P^6`: their Fano indices are three and seven respectively.

## 3. An explicit embedded projective plane

Put

`A = diag(2,-1,-1)`

and let `W` be the three-dimensional subspace consisting of matrices

`B(x,y,z) = [[0,0,0],[0,x,y],[0,z,-x]]`.

Every element of `W` commutes with `A`, and `C A ∩ W = 0`. Define

`j : P(W) -> Gr(2,g),  [B] -> C A ⊕ C B`.

The Plücker composite is `[B] -> [A∧B]`. Since `B -> A∧B` is injective
on `W`, this is a linear closed embedding. Thus its image `F` is a copy
of `P^2`, and `H|_F = O_{P^2}(1)`.

If `x^2+yz != 0`, the lower two-by-two block of `B` has distinct nonzero
eigenvalues. A change of basis within that block diagonalizes `B` and leaves
`A` fixed. Their span is then exactly a conjugate of the diagonal traceless
subalgebra. These points lie in the defining open orbit of `X`. They form
a dense open subset of `P(W)`, so its entire closed image `F` lies in `X`.
This includes the nonzero nilpotent blocks where `x^2+yz=0`.

## 4. Normal bundle: a pointwise proof valid on all of F

Let `U` be the four-dimensional space spanned by

`E_12, E_13, E_21, E_31`.

Conjugation preserves `X`, so each `D in U` induces a global algebraic
vector field on `X`. Restrict these vector fields to `F` and project to its
normal bundle. This defines a bundle homomorphism

`Phi : U⊗O_F -> N_{F/X}`.

We prove that it is an isomorphism at every point.

At `L=C A⊕C B`, the tangent space of the Grassmannian is
`Hom(L,g/L)`. The vector field associated to `D` is represented there by

`v -> [D,v] mod L`.

Every tangent vector to `F` sends `A` to zero in `g/L`, since every
plane in the defining family contains the fixed vector `A`. Therefore, if
`Phi_L(D)=0`, the vector field is tangent to `F` and necessarily

`[D,A] ∈ L`.

But `[D,A]` lies in the off-block space `U`, whereas `L` consists entirely
of block-diagonal matrices. Hence `U∩L=0` and `[D,A]=0`. For the chosen
basis of `U`, the linear map `D -> [D,A]` is diagonal with entries

`(-3,-3,3,3)`.

It is invertible, so `D=0`. This proves injectivity of `Phi_L` at every
`[B]`, including the nilpotent locus. Finally,

`rank N_{F/X} = dim X-dim F = 6-2 = 4 = dim U`.

A morphism between equal-rank vector bundles that is an isomorphism at every
fiber is a bundle isomorphism. Thus

`N_{F/X} ≅ U⊗O_F ≅ O_{P^2}^{⊕4}`.

All target hypotheses now hold, with `k=2` and `dim X=6`. This disproves
the full universal assertion. No classification of every possible example
is required for that conclusion.

## 5. A geometric cross-check

Here is a second check of the mechanism, without identifying the four normal
sections explicitly. In the projected-Severi construction, the incidence
variety `Y -> X` is a `P^1` bundle and also the blow-up of `P^7` along a
smooth projected `P^2×P^2`. Its exceptional divisor `E` is a `P^2` bundle
over that fourfold `Z`. Each fiber maps isomorphically onto a linear `P^2`
in `X`. The evaluation `E -> X` is dominant and generically finite, of
degree three. These are the properties established in Iliev–Manivel,
Proposition 3.16 and the geometry immediately preceding it, also recovered
in the 2014 appendix.

For a general `z in Z`, differentiating this family gives
`T_z Z⊗O_{P^2} -> N_{P^2/X}`, an isomorphism somewhere by generic
smoothness in characteristic zero. Adjunction gives
`det N = (-K_X)|_{P^2} ⊗ K_{P^2} = O(3)⊗O(-3)=O`.
The determinant of that map is consequently a constant function on `P^2`.
It is nonzero somewhere, so nowhere zero. The map is an isomorphism over
the whole fiber. This confirms the same trivial normal bundle for general
members of that family. The direct proof in Section 4 additionally specifies
the particular plane used in this report.

## 6. Verification scope and nonclaims

- The rank-four argument establishes holomorphic/algebraic triviality, not
  just a vanishing first Chern class or topological triviality.
- Smoothness and the global Fano/Picard properties are cited published
  theorems. They are not certified by the accompanying finite computations.
- The construction is a closed embedding; no ambiguity about self-intersections
  or meanings of immersion can invalidate this example.
- The base field is `C`. Characteristic three is outside the target; the
  commutator calculation deliberately fails there.
- No claim is made that every plane in `X` has trivial normal bundle.
- No conclusion is drawn about Hwang's separate rigid-target conjecture:
  disproving a sufficient hypothesis does not disprove its consequence.
- The broad complete-intersection route considered during triage would not
  address this example. The variety is a special, nontransverse Grassmannian
  linear section, not a complete intersection in projective space.
