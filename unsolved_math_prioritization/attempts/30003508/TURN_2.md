## 1. Statistical theorem and scope

Let D⊂R^d be a known bounded connected C^∞ domain, d>=2, and let Δ>0 be known and fixed. Let S=S^T and μ be C^∞ on its closure, with known class bounds

    ell I<=S<=Lambda I,   0<c<=μ<=C,   integral_D μ=1.

Consider the stationary reflecting diffusion with generator Lu=μ^(-1)div(S∇u) and natural conormal Neumann boundary condition n·S∇u=0. We observe exactly the low-frequency positions

    X_0,X_Δ,...,X_(NΔ).

No trajectory between observations and no additional sensor-noise model is used. Both S and μ are unknown. The known bounds only define a nonparametric class and regularization/clipping ranges; there is no finite-dimensional parameter or identifiability restriction.

**Candidate theorem.** The explicit finite-rank spectral estimator constructed below converges almost surely to S uniformly on every compact interior subset. Its clipped tensor version converges almost surely in L²(D). This is pointwise consistency at every fixed (S,μ) in the stated class; it is not a uniform rate over a class with uncontrolled smoothness norms.

The estimator is the weighted fit from TURN_1.md using all positive eigenvalues of a finite-rank empirical operator, weights κ^4, and ridge parameter tending to zero. Thus the retained number K_N is finite and data dependent, bounded by the empirical kernel rank. The proof does not require a prescribed finite spectral truncation, eigenvalue simplicity or matching individual empirical eigenfunctions.

## 2. Smooth boundary-corrected kernels

There exists a deterministic family of real kernels K_h(x,z), x in a fixed neighborhood of D-bar and z∈D, with the following properties:

1. R_h f(x)=integral_D K_h(x,z)f(z)dz converges to f in C²(D-bar) for every f∈C³(D-bar)
2. For every fixed derivative order r, sup_(x,z)|∂_x^α K_h(x,z)|<=C_r h^(-d-|α|), |α|<=r
3. The product operator R_h⊗R_h converges to the identity on smooth functions on D-bar×D-bar in all mixed derivative norms required below, in particular |α|<=2 in x and |β|<=2 in y
4. Each x↦K_h(x,z) is smooth, with deterministic known kernels depending only on the domain and h

These kernels need not be positive or mass preserving. Those properties are not used.

For completeness, construct them by a finite smooth boundary atlas, a partition of unity, a bounded finite-order extension operator, and ordinary compactly supported smooth mollification. In a flattened boundary chart, take the original function for t>=0 and extend for t<0 by

    E f(x′,t)=Σ_(j=1)^7 α_j f(x′,−jt),
    (α_1,...,α_7)=(28,−112,210,−224,140,−48,7).

The exact identities Σ α_j(−j)^k=1, k=0,...,6, match derivatives through order6 at t=0. Use cutoffs and a sufficiently small exterior collar so the reflected arguments remain in the chart. Interior pieces extend by zero after their cutoff. A finite sum of these pieces is a compactly supported C^6 extension E that equals f on D and is bounded in each C^r norm, r<=6. Applying the same construction separately in each variable handles joint densities.

Define R_h f=(η_h*Ef)|_D for a fixed real smooth compactly supported mollifier η of integral one. Change variables in the finitely many reflected integrals to obtain K_h. Reflection charts have bounded nonzero Jacobians on their cutoff supports, giving the displayed sup derivative bound. Mollification of a C³ extension converges in C²; applying this in both variables proves the mixed version for the smooth joint density. The kernels are globally smooth in x after convolution even though a finite-order extension was used. This gives a concrete boundary correction, rather than assuming an ordinary convolution works at the reflecting boundary.

## 3. Explicit empirical kernels and densities

Write Y_i=X_(iΔ). For N>=2 let j=floor(log_2 N), n_j=2^j and h_j=h_0/(j+1), with a fixed sufficiently small h_0>0. Use only the first n_j+1 states. Define

    μ̃_j(x)=(n_j+1)^(-1) Σ_(i=0)^(n_j) K_(h_j)(x,Y_i),

    q_j(x,y)=(2n_j)^(-1) Σ_(i=0)^(n_j−1)
      [K_(h_j)(x,Y_i)K_(h_j)(y,Y_(i+1))
       +K_(h_j)(y,Y_i)K_(h_j)(x,Y_(i+1))].              (3.1)

The q_j are real, symmetric, smooth in both arguments and finite rank, with rank at most2n_j (indeed their feature span is generated by n_j+1 kernel sections). They need not themselves be nonnegative joint densities or have exact empirical marginal μ̃_j. TURN_1.md does not require either property.

To ensure density positivity for every sample, choose a fixed smooth function ψ:R→[c/2,2C] which equals the identity on [3c/4,3C/2]. Such a function exists because that interval lies strictly inside the desired output interval. Define

    μ_j(x)=ψ(μ̃_j(x))/Z_j,
    Z_j=integral_D ψ(μ̃_j(x))dx.

Then μ_j is smooth and normalized, and a deterministic positive lower bound is

    μ_j >= c/(4C vol(D)).

Its upper bound is also deterministic. Eventual uniform consistency of μ̃_j will make ψ inactive, so this step does not alter the limiting density or its derivatives.

Set T_j(x,y)=q_j(x,y)/sqrt(μ_j(x)μ_j(y)). It is a real symmetric finite-rank operator. Take all its positive eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenspaces for the spectral estimator of TURN_1.md, with ridge λ_j=1/(j+1). The resulting estimator at sample size N is the one indexed by j. Finite initial sample sizes may be assigned any fixed admissible tensor.

## 4. Spectral-gap variance bounds from the true observation model

The generator is self-adjoint in L²(μ dx), with Dirichlet form

    E(f,f)=integral_D (grad f)^T S grad f dx.

Uniform ellipticity, density bounds, connectedness and the Poincaré inequality on a bounded smooth domain imply a strictly positive spectral gap. Thus for the true one-step operator P=P_Δ there is ρ<1 with

    ||P f||_(L²(μ))<=ρ||f||_(L²(μ)) whenever integral f μ=0.

The number ρ depends on the true coefficients; it need not be known or estimated to choose h_j or n_j.

For a bounded real function F(Y_i,Y_(i+1)), let Z_i=F(Y_i,Y_(i+1))−EF. For lag l>=1, conditioning on the endpoints and using the Markov property yields

    Cov(Z_0,Z_l)=<r,P^(l−1)s>_(L²(μ)),

where r(z)=E[Z_0|Y_1=z] and s(z)=E[Z_0|Y_0=z]. Both have mean zero and L² norms at most ||Z_0||_2. Therefore

    |Cov(Z_0,Z_l)|<=ρ^(l−1) Var(F).

Summing covariances gives, for a constant C_ρ<∞,

    Var(n^(-1)Σ_(i=0)^(n−1)F(Y_i,Y_(i+1)))
       <= C_ρ ||F||_∞²/n.                              (4.1)

The analogous bound for functions of Y_i alone follows in the same way. These estimates remain valid for the bandwidth-dependent functions used at each j, because their dependence enters only through the displayed sup norm. No independence of the consecutive pairs is assumed.

## 5. Uniform derivative consistency of the empirical estimates

The true stationary pair density is q(x,y)=μ(x)p_Δ(x,y). Smooth uniformly elliptic coefficients, a smooth conormal boundary and fixed Δ>0 give a smooth positive-time heat kernel on the closed domain. In particular q is smooth in both spatial variables. One way to justify the regularity used here is spectral: the min–max principle compares generator eigenvalues with those of the Neumann Laplacian, giving polynomial eigenvalue counting; iterated smooth elliptic regularity and Sobolev embedding give polynomial-in-eigenvalue bounds on every fixed derivative of u_k. The factor exp(Δν_k) then makes the differentiated heat-kernel series converge absolutely and uniformly at every fixed positive Δ. Multiplication by the smooth densities yields the same conclusion for q. This uses only standard smooth elliptic spectral theory, not statistical eigenpair consistency. Reversibility makes it symmetric, so

    E q_j=(R_(h_j)⊗R_(h_j))q,
    E μ̃_j=R_(h_j)μ.                                   (5.1)

The deterministic biases converge to zero in C² for μ and in the mixed |α|<=2, |β|<=2 norms for q.

Here are full details for stochastic uniform convergence. For each derivative ∂_x^α∂_y^β with |α|,|β|<=2, a summand of the differentiated q_j is bounded by C h_j^(-2d-4)<=C′(j+1)^(2d+4). Equation (4.1) bounds the variance at any fixed (x,y) by

    C(j+1)^(4d+8) 2^(-j).

Take a deterministic ε_j-net of D-bar×D-bar with ε_j=2^(-j/(4d)). It has at most C2^(j/2) points. Chebyshev's inequality, a union bound, and threshold1/(j+1) give

    Pr(max_net |∂_x^α∂_y^β(q_j−E q_j)|>1/(j+1))
       <= C(j+1)^(4d+10)2^(-j/2).

The series of these probabilities converges. Borel–Cantelli therefore proves the net errors vanish almost surely, simultaneously for the finitely many derivative pairs. The differentiated kernels and their expectations are Lipschitz in (x,y) with deterministic bound C(j+1)^(2d+5); one more x or y derivative suffices. The net interpolation error is at most this bound times ε_j, which tends to zero. The kernels were defined in a fixed neighborhood, so this Lipschitz comparison is valid even if D is not convex.

For μ̃_j the identical argument uses d-dimensional nets, summand bounds C(j+1)^(d+2), and the one-state version of (4.1). The resulting summable estimate is even smaller. Thus, on one probability-one event,

    μ̃_j→μ in C²(D-bar),
    q_j→q in all mixed derivative norms |α|,|β|<=2.

Uniform convergence makes ψ inactive eventually; Z_j→integral μ=1. Hence μ_j→μ in C²(D-bar), with the uniform positive lower bound already proved. In particular every deterministic hypothesis of TURN_1.md holds on the same probability-one event. The dyadic reuse of data entails no loss of convergence along all sample sizes N→∞.

## 6. Tensor consistency and output interpretation

Apply TURN_1.md pathwise. The finite-rank spectral weighted fit converges to the true tensor and its divergence uniformly on each compact interior subset. The prescribed clipping to ell I<=S<=Lambda I gives almost-sure L²(D) convergence by bounded dominated convergence. The actual diffusion covariance Σ=2S/μ and drift b=(div S)/μ also follow locally from the estimated fields and density, though the primary target here is S.

All positive empirical eigenpairs are used. Spurious negative eigenvalues are discarded through the continuous functions g(t)=t_+² and h(t)=t_+²log(t_+) in the operator formulas; no unjustified logarithm of a negative eigenvalue is taken. The κ^4 weights make the relevant functions continuous at zero. Multiplicity, empirical splitting and a lack of individual eigenvector labeling cause no gap.

The mathematical estimator uses finite-dimensional spectral calculations and known-domain integrals. Additional numerical quadrature or approximate eigensolver errors are not silently included in the statistical model; they would require a further numerical-error control. This is a convergence theorem for the precisely defined estimator, not a claim of computational efficiency. The deliberately slow bandwidth and dyadic sample schedule establish consistency and make no optimal-rate assertion.
