## Retained results

1. **Fixed arcsine obstruction.** A nontrivial projection of trace p!=1/2 paired with a free symmetric Bernoulli yields a squared commutator that cannot have the source's fixed arcsine-on-[0,4] positive multiplicative factor. The forced shifted moment determinant is t^3(t-1/4)<0, t=p(1-p). Arbitrary putative positive factors are covered by a boundedness argument
2. **Scalar imaginary-axis rigidity.** Keeping the symmetric imaginary-axis-preserving scalar functions and the equation with the original input Cauchy transform forces the input law to be symmetric, without moment assumptions. Centered nonsymmetric two-point laws violate the equation pointwise
3. **Universal-factor classification.** The only fixed positive laws that can divide every sufficiently small-trace projection commutator square under free multiplication are nonzero Dirac laws. A compact-product/compact-factor lemma removes hidden moment assumptions
4. **Bounded matrix workaround.** An explicit three-by-three self-adjoint pencil gives the commutator resolvent. Its regularization error is bounded by [2 epsilon^2 A B+epsilon(A^2+B^2)]/[(1+epsilon^2) Im(z)^2]. Existing Belinschi–Mai–Speicher subordination is credited, not claimed new
5. **Arbitrary-law tail control.** Clipping the two input laws at T changes the Cauchy transform by at most 2 min(1,2q_a(T)+2q_b(T))/Im(z). Combined with the bounded construction and epsilon=T^(-3), this gives a controlled matrix-valued limit for arbitrary Borel laws. It does not give a finite fixed-point stopping bound or the source's stronger scalar format

## Exact remaining gap

The unchanged factor and scalar ansatzes tested here are impossible in the stated generality. A genuinely revised nonsymmetric scalar representation with its full analytic mapping, normalization and uniqueness requirements is still missing. The credited larger matrix construction does not establish that representation and cannot be promoted to a new full solution merely because it determines the commutator law.

The numerical-looking controls are exact rational/symbolic checks. They supplement proofs; finite matrices are never assumed free, finite parameter samples do not replace uniform proofs, and no real-axis density convergence is inferred from upper-half-plane resolvent bounds.
