# Turn 1: a positive-factor obstruction for nonsymmetric projection inputs

**Disposition: partial obstruction; the full source representation problem remains unresolved.** This is a genuine first proof turn after the source/prior-attempt gate. No historical novelty is claimed. Nica–Speicher, Remark 1.12, already establishes related failures of positivity for formal free square roots and even-cumulant truncation; the calculation below tests the particular fixed positive factor appearing in the OWR representation.

## 1. The precise ansatz tested

Let rho be the arcsine probability measure on [0,4], with density

    1 / (pi sqrt(x(4-x))).

The OWR positive-factor representation for the square of the commutator uses rho as a fixed free-multiplicative factor. We test whether that same factor can be retained for all nonsymmetric inputs, even allowing its remaining factors to depend jointly on both inputs. Any representation rho boxtimes nu_a boxtimes nu_b with positive probability factors would imply a representation rho boxtimes nu with a single positive probability measure nu, by associativity and positivity of free multiplicative convolution.

**Proposition.** Let P be a projection with trace p in (0,1), and let U be a self-adjoint unitary with trace zero, free from P. Put C=i(PU-UP) and t=p(1-p). If p != 1/2, there is no probability measure nu on [0,infinity) such that

    law(C^2) = rho boxtimes nu.

For p=1/2, such a representation exists with nu=delta_(1/4). For p=0 or 1, C=0 and nu=delta_0 works. Thus the failure occurs for every nontrivial nonsymmetric projection law, not only for a sampled parameter range.

## 2. Moment finiteness is not an assumption on a putative factor

We first justify using three moments even if nu was allowed unbounded support. The following elementary free-product observation suffices. Let A>=0 be bounded with nonzero mean alpha, let B>=0 be free from A, possibly affiliated and unbounded, and suppose R=B^(1/2) A B^(1/2) has compactly supported distribution in a faithful tracial realization. Then R is bounded. For the spectral cutoff e_m=1_[0,m](B), freeness gives

    E_B(e_m R e_m) = alpha B e_m.

Indeed, B^(1/2)e_m is bounded, conditional expectation is bimodular, and E_B(A)=alpha 1. Positivity and contractivity imply alpha B e_m <= ||R|| 1. Increasing m shows B<=||R||/alpha. Thus nu is compactly supported and all moments used below exist. The same argument is valid using bounded spectral compressions of the positive quadratic form defining R.

Here A has law rho and mean 2. Since ||P||=||U||=1, C is bounded; in fact the computation below gives C^2=(P-UPU)^2<=1. Therefore any putative nu is supported in [0,1/2]. We will only need nonnegativity and finite third moment, so this bound is stronger than required.

## 3. The first three moments of C squared

Set P'=UPU. The two projections P and P' are free and have the same law. To verify freeness directly, expand an alternating product of centered elements from the algebras generated by P and P': replace every centered P'-algebra element by U times the corresponding centered P-algebra element times U. Since U is centered, the resulting word alternates centered elements of the original free algebras. Its trace is zero. End factors U cause no exception, and the same argument applies in either starting algebra.

Because U^2=1,

    C^2=(P-UPU)^2=(P-P')^2.

This identity also proves the norm bound in the previous section. Write r_j for the free cumulants of P. The projection moment sequence is m_j(P)=p for every j>=1. The ordinary noncrossing moment-cumulant recursion gives

    r_2=t,
    r_4=t(1-5t),
    r_6=t(1-14t+42t^2).

For D=P-P', all odd cumulants vanish and its even cumulants are 2r_(2j). Consequently, by the even moment-cumulant identities,

    m_2(D)=k_2,
    m_4(D)=k_4+2k_2^2,
    m_6(D)=k_6+6k_4 k_2+5k_2^3.

It follows that Y=C^2 has

    m_1(Y)=2t,
    m_2(Y)=2t-2t^2,
    m_3(Y)=2t-4t^2+4t^3.                       (1)

These are polynomial identities in p, not numerical estimates. The accompanying checker obtains the cumulants from the projection moments and separately computes the commutator's moments by expanding noncommutative words and applying freeness directly.

## 4. Forced factor moments and contradiction

The arcsine factor has moments m_1(rho)=2, m_2(rho)=6, m_3(rho)=20. If A and B are free positive variables with laws rho and nu, cyclicity gives the moments of B^(1/2)AB^(1/2) as the alternating word moments of AB. The noncrossing expansion gives, writing b_j=m_j(nu),

    m_1(rho boxtimes nu)=2b_1,
    m_2(rho boxtimes nu)=4b_2+2b_1^2,
    m_3(rho boxtimes nu)=8b_3+12b_1 b_2.       (2)

The last coefficient of b_1^3 vanishes because 20-3*2*6+2*2^3=0. The checker reconstructs all three formulas by the independent colored noncrossing recursion, rather than assuming an S-transform normalization.

Comparing (1) and (2) forces

    b_1=t,
    b_2=t/2-t^2,
    b_3=t/4-5t^2/4+2t^3.

Hence the shifted moment determinant is

    b_1 b_3-b_2^2 = t^3(t-1/4).              (3)

For 0<p<1, p!=1/2, one has 0<t<1/4, making (3) strictly negative. Every measure on [0,infinity) satisfies b_1 b_3>=b_2^2 by Cauchy-Schwarz applied to x^(1/2) and x^(3/2). Equivalently the explicitly nonnegative polynomial x(x-(1/2-t))^2 would have integral

    b_3-b_2^2/b_1=t^2(t-1/4)<0.

This contradiction proves the proposition without any root selection, numerical density reconstruction or moment-indeterminacy issue.

## 5. Boundary cases and interpretation

For p=1/2, the centered projection P-1/2 is a symmetric two-point variable with values +/-1/2. The difference of two free copies has the arcsine law on [-1,1], so its square is rho dilated by 1/4. Thus nu=delta_(1/4) works; its forced variance is zero. This standard symmetric endpoint also agrees with the OWR formula. For p=0 or 1, faithfulness makes P deterministic and C=0. Reflection p<->1-p leaves t and the conclusion invariant. Scaling P or U by nonzero real constants rescales C squared and does not remove the obstruction, although that extension is not needed for the counterexample family.

The result disproves the literal universal continuation of the OWR's **unchanged fixed-arcsine positive-factor ansatz**. It does not disprove the source's broader request for some appropriate nonsymmetric representation. In particular the general identity with rho on the opposite side, already in the source, is unaffected; other analytic systems or different positive factors remain possible. A formal deconvolution series is not a probability measure, as the negative determinant demonstrates.
