# Turn 3: no universal nondegenerate positive multiplicative factor

**Disposition: stronger partial obstruction, not a solution of the general representation problem.** Replacing the source's fixed arcsine factor by some other fixed nondegenerate positive law cannot repair the factorization for all inputs. The proof below allows arbitrary positive probability laws at the outset and does not assume moments of either factor.

## 1. Statement

For 0<p<1 let P_p be a projection of trace p and U a free centered self-adjoint unitary. Set

    Y_p = (i(P_p U-U P_p))^2,   t=p(1-p).

**Theorem.** Suppose rho is a probability measure on [0,infinity) such that, for every sufficiently small p>0, there exists a probability measure nu_p on [0,infinity) with

    law(Y_p) = rho boxtimes nu_p.                  (1)

Then rho=delta_a for some a>0. Conversely each delta_a with a>0 satisfies this property, simply by rescaling law(Y_p).

Thus the only universal fixed positive factors even for this small family of bounded commutators are scalar dilations. In particular no universal nondegenerate fixed factor can work for every nonsymmetric input pair. The theorem concerns a fixed factor independent of p; it does not exclude useful input-dependent factors, matrix-valued representations or other scalar equations.

## 2. Compact product forces compact positive factors

We use the standard realization of free multiplicative convolution by free positive affiliated operators in a faithful finite tracial von Neumann algebra. On positive probability measures the operation is commutative; this background is treated in the source's cited arithmetic reference. The following boundedness argument is included rather than imposing hidden moment assumptions.

**Lemma.** If lambda=rho boxtimes nu is compactly supported and neither factor is delta_0, then both factors are compactly supported.

Let A,B be positive free affiliated realizations and R=B^(1/2) A B^(1/2) the associated positive product. Its compact distribution and faithfulness imply 0<=R<=M for some finite M. Choose a bounded spectral truncation A_n=min(A,n) with alpha_n=trace(A_n)>0, which is possible because rho!=delta_0. As quadratic forms,

    0 <= B^(1/2) A_n B^(1/2) <= R <= M.

For e_m=1_[0,m](B), compress and condition onto the algebra generated by B. All compressed factors are bounded, so bimodularity and freeness give

    alpha_n B e_m
      = E_B(e_m B^(1/2) A_n B^(1/2) e_m) <= M.

Letting m increase yields B<=M/alpha_n. Thus nu is compactly supported. It has positive finite mean beta because nu!=delta_0. By commutativity, lambda is also the law of A^(1/2) B A^(1/2); equivalently the two positive products have the same nonzero spectral distribution. Repeating the preceding spectral-compression argument with the now-bounded B gives A<=M/beta. Hence rho is compactly supported. This proves the lemma. The argument uses positive quadratic forms only until their bounded compressions are taken; no conditional expectation of an undefined unbounded product is used.

In (1), Y_p is supported in [0,1] and has nonzero first moment for 0<p<1. Neither factor can therefore be delta_0, and the lemma applies as soon as (1) holds for even one p. All following moments are consequently finite.

## 3. Normalize the putative universal factor

Let alpha=m_1(rho)>0 and replace A by A/alpha while replacing B by alpha B. This leaves the product law unchanged. We may therefore assume m_1(rho)=1. Write

    r=m_2(rho),   s=m_3(rho).

Positivity gives r>=1, with equality if and only if rho=delta_1. We show that r>1 is incompatible with (1) for arbitrarily small positive p.

Turn 1 proved exactly that

    y_1=2t,   y_2=2t-2t^2,   y_3=2t-4t^2+4t^3.

For a positive second factor with moments b_j, the colored noncrossing expansion for a mean-one first factor gives

    y_1=b_1,
    y_2=b_2+(r-1)b_1^2,
    y_3=b_3+3(r-1)b_1 b_2+(s-3r+2)b_1^3.        (2)

Solving these triangular equations yields

    b_1=2t,
    b_2=2t+(2-4r)t^2,
    b_3=2t+(8-12r)t^2+(24r^2-12r-8s)t^3.

Thus the shifted Hankel determinant is exactly

    b_1 b_3-b_2^2
       = t^3[-8(r-1)+(32r^2-8r-16s-4)t].       (3)

This calculation is checked both symbolically and by a separate colored noncrossing word expansion in `verify_turn3.py`.

## 4. Small-trace contradiction and all endpoints

If r>1, the constant term in the brackets in (3) is strictly negative. Put K=32r^2-8r-16s-4. For K<=0 the determinant is negative for every t>0. For K>0 it is negative whenever

    0<t<8(r-1)/K.

Because p(1-p) tends to zero as p decreases to zero, some sufficiently small p always lies in this strict interval. But every positive probability measure with finite third moment satisfies b_1 b_3-b_2^2>=0. This contradicts (1). Hence r=1 and normalized rho=delta_1; undoing the normalization gives rho=delta_alpha.

Conversely, for rho=delta_alpha, alpha>0, choose nu_p as the pushforward of law(Y_p) under x->x/alpha. Its free multiplicative product with the scalar alpha has exactly law(Y_p). The zero scalar does not qualify, because every Y_p in the stated range has mean 2p(1-p)>0. The limit p=0 is not itself used as a counterexample; a strictly positive p is always selected. Reflection p<->1-p gives the same small-variance obstruction near p=1.

For the normalized original arcsine factor, r=3/2 and s=5/2, (3) reduces to 16t^3(t-1/4), agreeing with Turn 1 after its factor-of-two scaling. This independently checks the normalization.

## 5. Consequence for the research route

The issue is not a poor choice of one fixed universal positive factor: every nondegenerate choice fails. The obstruction comes from the rare-projection regime, which makes the forced second-factor shifted moment matrix nonpositive. The result also rules out finitely many fixed positive factors whose free product is nondegenerate, since they can be combined into rho.

The scalar-factor case is tautological and supplies no effective representation from the two separate input laws. A successful replacement must introduce genuine input dependence, change the type of convolution/auxiliary object, or use a broader analytic system. None of those constructive routes is completed here. The original OWR general representation request therefore remains unresolved.
