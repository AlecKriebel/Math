# Source and status audit — 2026-10-08 UTC

## Exact source question and conventions

The authenticated MFO PDF of Oberwolfach Report 50/2017 dates the meeting to 22–28 October 2017. Shelly Harvey's report, joint with JungHwan Park and Arunima Ray, is on PDF pages 34–35. Question 3 is the target: ordinary (P_m^{(n+2)}\subset F_n^m) for integer (n\ge1). The preceding sentences define (G^{(0)}=G), (G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]), credit (P_m^{(2)}\subset F_0^m) to Martin, and use all strand numbers. The target is the classical disk pure braid group inside smooth string-link concordance; closure means the ordinary link closure. The title's 2018 publication label in the imported catalog is not the workshop year.

The catalog's phrase “squared pure braid group” is not an accurate name for the second derived subgroup. OWR also announces (P_m^{(2)}\subset W_2^m) (Theorem 4). That is a symmetric Whitney-tower height statement. It is not the same as the separate Question 5 about symmetric gropes. No tower-height/order or grope-height/class identification is used in the candidate proof.

Otto Definition 2.3, pp. 2631–2632, supplies the exact smooth, integral, zero-surgery definition and states that a string link is solvable when its closure is. The filtration decreases with the index, including the half-integer levels. The typography/extraction of (F_{-0.5}) in some Otto search snippets loses the minus sign; no argument here uses that damaged snippet. Martin's final arXiv v2 (27 May 2022), Corollaries 4.2 and 5.1, confirms the zero-level classification and abelian quotient underlying the credited base containment.

## Essential external inputs

1. Erle's 1999 version of record: §2, pp. 162–165, gives the checkerboard/Goeritz computation and correction. The relevant diagram, sign convention and block picture were visually inspected. Its off-diagonal indexing line in Proposition 2.3 disagrees with its immediately following block picture. The packet explicitly counts signed cycle-edge contributions using the picture and crossing rule, and independently cross-checks with Meyer. All target blocks have even absolute exponent at least two, so small-cycle coincidences and single-generator exceptions do not arise.
2. Toffoli's published 2022 article: equation (1.1), Definitions 4.12/4.19, Remark 4.25 equation (4.21), Lemma 4.27 and Corollary 4.28. The proofs of Proposition 4.18 and Theorem 4.24 and their relevant gluing/correction steps were inspected. For one coloring and a zero linking/framing matrix, the surgery formula is ρ=-σ with no correction; ω=-1 is allowed. The packet proves zero twisted signature of a 1-solution directly by reduction modulo 2 and its lifted hyperbolic basis. It does not substitute relative exterior cobordism for zero-surgery solvability.
3. Gambaudo–Ghys (2005), Theorem A, computational Meyer definition, and §4.2: a secondary independent signature method, with explicit matrices and normalization controls. This is not needed by the main Goeritz proof.
4. Cha (2010), Theorem 8.2 and its proof, provides a published corroboration of the finite-character obstruction at (n=1,p=d=2). The packet's elementary proof does not require accepting the general p-tower machinery.

## Exploratory sources deliberately not used as proof dependencies

Conway–Nagel–Toffoli's 0.5-solvable-cobordism signature theorem concerns a relative exterior notion. No implication from the target's absolute definition to that relative notion was assumed.

Seger's 2019 thesis was examined as a possible source for a lower-order Seifert-form obstruction, but it is not relied on. The displayed bilinearity calculation in its Proposition 3.4 drops cross terms when arbitrary representatives are changed by boundary classes. Without additional hypotheses that presentation cannot be used verbatim to define the stated closed-surface Seifert pairing. This narrowly identified concern is not a claim that all results of that thesis are false. The candidate instead uses the independent direct 1-solution argument and the published surgery formula.
