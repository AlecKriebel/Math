# Koszulness from the Kähler package

## Disposition and exact target

The universal problem remains **unsolved in this investigation**. Five approach families are exhausted. This packet proves scoped results and supplies exact controls; it does not offer a counterexample satisfying all hypotheses or a proof of the universal assertion. No novelty, first-priority, formal-verification, or human-peer-review claim is made. AI assistance was used extensively.

The selected record is 30005418 / OWR-12697689-014, rank 797. The primary question is Question 2 on printed page 379 of *Resolutions in Local Algebra and Singularity Theory*, Oberwolfach Reports 20 (2023), 327–395, DOI [10.4171/OWR/2023/6](https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/12697689). It asks whether every Artinian quadratic Gorenstein algebra with the Kähler package is Koszul. The same target occurs as Question 6.8 in [Mastroeni–McCullough, *Chow rings of matroids are Koszul*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00393). The OWR contribution explicitly distinguishes hard Lefschetz alone from the full package.

We work with ordinary commutative, connected, standard graded algebras

A = ⊕_{i=0}^d A^i,  A^0 = R,

finite-dimensional and generated by A^1. Thus “quadratic” means that the kernel of Sym_R(A^1) → A is generated in degree two. This is not the supercommutative convention in which degree-one elements anticommute. In cohomological examples these degrees are half of even cohomological degrees. A rational algebra is interpreted after tensoring with R for positivity. Koszulness before and after that field extension is equivalent, since the normalized bar complex commutes with field extension and R is faithfully flat over Q. No positive-characteristic version of an ordered-real positivity condition is asserted.

Fix an orientation deg: A^d → R. Poincaré duality means each multiplication pairing A^i × A^{d-i} → R is perfect. For 0 ≤ i ≤ floor(d/2), the full package at a common element ℓ ∈ A^1 requires:

- L_i: a ↦ ℓ^{d-2i}a is an isomorphism A^i → A^{d-i};
- Q_i(a,b) = (−1)^i deg(abℓ^{d-2i}) is positive definite on P_i = ker(ℓ^{d-2i+1}: A^i → A^{d-i+1}).

In particular, the i=0 condition says deg(ℓ^d)>0. These are the signs and primitive spaces in McCullough's 2023 talk, slide 57 (PDF page 84), and in Definition 2.3 of [Schweitzer–Vecchi v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00782v2). The displayed general-field notation in the talk does not give a meaning to positive definiteness over every field.

A hard-Lefschetz element alone is insufficient. A full-package element is stronger. An externally prescribed geometric cone and all mixed relations on that entire cone require separate justification. The next proposition explains the limited local relationship between the point and cone formulations.

## 1 A full-package point gives a small cone

**Proposition 1.** Suppose d≥1 and A has the full package at ℓ with its fixed orientation. There is an open convex cone C containing ℓ on which all the following mixed conditions hold. For each i and m=d−2i, multiplication by ℓ_1⋯ℓ_m maps A^i isomorphically to A^{d-i}, and

(−1)^i deg(abℓ_1⋯ℓ_m)

is positive definite on ker(ℓ_0ℓ_1⋯ℓ_m: A^i → A^{d-i+1}), for every ℓ_0,…,ℓ_m ∈ C. Empty products mean the identity. This does not identify C with any previously specified ample cone.

**Proof.** Choose a real linear functional φ on A^1 with φ(ℓ)=1. Work first on the affine slice φ=1. At the diagonal tuple (ℓ,…,ℓ), each mixed Lefschetz determinant is nonzero. For i≥1, the primitive defining map has full row rank: on ℓA^{i−1}, it includes the isomorphism ℓ^{d−2i+2}: A^{i−1} → A^{d-i+1}. For i=0 the target is zero. Hence these ranks remain constant near the diagonal, and the kernels form locally trivial vector bundles. In a continuous local kernel basis the restricted form remains positive definite, by openness of that condition on real symmetric matrices. There are only finitely many i. Take a sufficiently small convex open ball U around ℓ in the slice such that every required tuple in U^{m+1} satisfies these conditions, and set C={tu:t>0,u∈U}. This is an open convex cone. Independent positive rescaling of each ℓ_j multiplies each form by a positive scalar and each primitive map by a nonzero scalar, preserving every condition. ∎

This is a continuity statement in a fixed finite-dimensional algebra. It says nothing about Koszulness under deformation of the algebra. In a proposed deformation argument one still has to prove that quadratic generation and a nonzero off-diagonal Tor class survive while entering the HR locus; none of those steps follows merely from positivity being open.

## 2 The square-zero obstruction and a genuine Lefschetz control

**Proposition 2.** Let d≥2. If A has the full package at ℓ, then B_ℓ(a,b)=deg(abℓ^{d−2}) on A^1 has signature (1,h_1−1), where h_1=dim A^1. Every subspace W⊂A^1 satisfying WW=0 has dimension at most one.

**Proof.** Hard Lefschetz and duality make B_ℓ nondegenerate. The line Rℓ is positive because B_ℓ(ℓ,ℓ)=deg(ℓ^d)>0. Its orthogonal complement is precisely P_1, on which B_ℓ is negative definite by HR. This proves the signature. If dim W≥2, there is a nonzero w∈W orthogonal to ℓ. But B_ℓ(w,w)=0 because w²=0, contradicting negative definiteness on P_1. ∎

**Corollary.** A graded trivial extension R⋉M with dim M^1≥2 cannot have the full package, even if it is quadratic, Gorenstein, and hard Lefschetz. Indeed, the ideal M has square zero. In the canonical-module idealizations of level Artinian algebras, dim M^1 is the type of the base algebra. The type-at-least-two idealization route is therefore excluded. This does not exclude all possible quadratic Gorenstein algebras.

The cited non-Koszul examples in [McCullough–Seceleanu, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10237v2) use idealization. We reconstruct one explicitly, rather than taking its Hilbert vector as evidence.

Let R=Q[u,x,y,z]/I, where

I=(x²+yz+u², xu, x²+xy, xz+yu, zu+u², y²+z²).

The degree-two basis is (xy,xz,yz,z²). Its exact multiplication table is regenerated by the checker from the six displayed quadrics. All twenty degree-three monomials lie in I, so h_R=(1,4,4). Define

A^i = R_i ⊕ (R_{3-i})*,

with multiplication (r,α)(s,β)=(rs,rβ+sα), and deg on A^3=(R_0)* given by evaluation at 1. The action on the dual is (rβ)(t)=β(rt). This definition avoids ambiguities in canonical-module shift notation. It gives a unital commutative algebra by the ordinary module law. The summands pair perfectly, so A is Gorenstein with h_A=(1,8,8,1).

The checker forms the kernel of Sym²(A^1)→A^2, obtaining 28 independent quadratic relations. Their exact Macaulay ranks in degrees 2,3,4 are respectively 28,119,330, against polynomial dimensions 36,120,330. A^1 generates A^2: the multiplication-by-z map R_1→R_2 is invertible, so the products R_1·(R_2)* span (R_1)*, and R_1 generates R_2. It also generates A^3. Thus the quotient by these quadrics maps onto A; the degree counts and vanishing from degree four prove it is exactly A. Quadraticity is certified, not assumed.

For clarity, the matrix of multiplication by z, with R_1 basis (u,x,y,z) and the degree-two basis above, is

    −1  0  0  0
     0  1  0  0
     1  0  1  0
     0  0  0  1

and has determinant −1. Let η be the functional dual to z² in R_2, and take L=z+η∈A^1. Then deg(L³)=3. The matrix B_L has block form

    C   Tᵀ
    T    0

with T invertible. It is congruent to a hyperbolic form, hence has inertia (4,4,0). In particular, it is nonsingular, which proves A^1→A^2 by L is an isomorphism, and L³≠0 proves the degree-zero Lefschetz condition. This A really is hard Lefschetz. It cannot satisfy HR at any element: (R_2)* is a four-dimensional square-zero subspace. At L specifically, the primitive Q_1 has four positive and three negative directions.

There is also a direct non-Koszul certificate. In the reduced bar complex of R in internal degree 4,

C_2 has dimension 16, C_3 dimension 192, C_4 dimension 256.

The exact differentials have ranks 16 and 175, and their composition vanishes. Consequently dim Tor^R_{3,4}(Q,Q)=192−16−175=1. More explicitly,

c = [u|u|z²] + [u|z|z²]

is a cycle: u²+uz=0 and all degree-three products vanish. MATH_RESULTS.json gives a rational functional λ on the 192 bar words, zero on every boundary, with λ(c)=1. The checker reconstructs and verifies all its coefficients. This is a nonzero off-diagonal homology class. The inclusion R→A and projection A→R are augmentation-preserving algebra maps with composite identity, so they induce a retract of normalized bar complexes. The class injects into Tor^A_{3,4}; therefore A is not Koszul, independently of Hilbert-series guesses.

This reconstructs a credited known family and provides the precise failure of the target hypothesis. It is not a counterexample to the OWR question.

## 3 The full target in socle degrees at most two

**Proposition 3.** Every real algebra in the target class with socle degree d≤2 is Koszul.

**Proof.** For d=0 the algebra is R. For d=1, duality and standard grading give R[t]/(t²), which has a quadratic monomial presentation. Let d=2. Its multiplication pairing B on A^1 is Lorentzian by Proposition 2. Put n=dim A^1. If n=1, the only such standard graded Gorenstein algebra is R[t]/(t³), which is not quadratic and hence is excluded.

For n≥2 choose a hyperbolic basis u,v,z_1,…,z_{n−2} of B with B(u,v)=1, B(z_i,z_j)=−δ_ij and all other pairings zero. Let w=uv. The algebra has the presentation

B_n=R[u,v,z_1,…,z_{n−2}]/(u²,v²,uz_i,vz_i,z_i z_j (i<j),z_i²+uv).

Use graded reverse lexicographic order z_1>⋯>z_{n−2}>u>v. The leading monomials are exactly all quadratic monomials except uv. The candidate initial quotient has standard monomials 1; u,v,z_i; uv, and none in degree ≥3. On the other hand the displayed quotient maps onto A, whose Hilbert vector is (1,n,1). The two dimension bounds agree, so these quadrics form a Gröbner basis and the displayed map is an isomorphism. The standard quadratic-Gröbner-basis criterion implies Koszulness. This criterion is a known theorem, recalled in the background of McCullough–Seceleanu; it is not claimed here as new. ∎

The checker verifies every Buchberger S-pair for B_n for n=2,…,10. Those tests are controls for the proof, rather than a replacement for its arbitrary-n argument.

## 4 An explicit cubic family with an entire mixed positive cone

Let A_n=B_n⊗R[t]/(t²), n≥2, and orient it by deg(wt)=1. Tensoring the displayed Gröbner presentation with the independent quadratic relation t² gives a quadratic Gröbner basis. Thus A_n is Koszul, quadratic and Gorenstein, with h=(1,n+1,n+1,1).

Write V=B_n^1 and retain its Lorentz pairing B. Let T be one component of {a∈V:B(a,a)>0}, chosen to contain u+v. Define

C={a+st : a∈T, s>0}.

The Lorentz time cone T is open and convex: in a diagonalized basis it is the set a_0>||a'||. Hence C is open and convex. For L=a+st,

B_L((x,r),(y,q)) = sB(x,y)+qB(x,a)+rB(a,y),

deg(L³)=3sB(a,a)>0.

The Schur complement with respect to sB is −B(a,a)/s<0. Since sB has inertia (1,n−1), B_L has inertia (1,n) and is nonsingular. The positive line RL has negative-definite orthogonal complement, proving HR at every L∈C and all required Lefschetz isomorphisms.

For mixed HR take L_0=a_0+s_0t and L_1=a_1+s_1t in C. The elementary Lorentz inequality for vectors in the same time cone gives B(a_0,a_1)>0. Then

B_{L_1}(L_0,L_0)=s_1B(a_0,a_0)+2s_0B(a_0,a_1)>0.

Thus B_{L_1} restricts negatively to L_0's B_{L_1}-orthogonal complement, exactly the mixed primitive space ker(L_0L_1:A^1→A^3). Its nonsingularity gives mixed degree-one Lefschetz. The mixed degree-zero products are

deg(L_0L_1L_2)=s_0B(a_1,a_2)+s_1B(a_0,a_2)+s_2B(a_0,a_1)>0.

These exhaust all mixed conditions in socle degree three. Therefore the entire explicitly specified cone is certified, without inferring a global cone from a sample point.

At L=u+v+t, a primitive basis is u−v,z_1,…,z_{n−2},u+v−2t. The Q_1 matrix in that basis is diag(2,1,…,1,6), and deg(L³)=6. For n=7 this Koszul full-package algebra has the same Hilbert vector (1,8,8,1) as the non-Koszul hard-Lefschetz algebra above. The Hilbert vector therefore does not distinguish these examples. Their HR behavior does.

The checker verifies nine quadratic families, 36 rational cone points, 144 mixed degree-one pairs and 576 degree-zero triples, as well as 7,512 Buchberger pairs across B_n and A_n. Finite sampling is not used to claim the universal cone: the preceding inequality argument supplies that proof.

## 5 Inverse systems and the remaining gap

For a real homogeneous cubic F with no linear annihilators, its apolar algebra A_F=R[∂_1,…,∂_e]/Ann(F) has Hilbert vector (1,e,e,1). Choose deg(D)=D(F)/6 in degree three. For the directional differential operator L associated to a vector l,

deg(L³)=F(l),  B_L=(1/6) Hess_F(l).

Thus F(l)>0 and Hess_F(l) of inertia (1,e−1) are exactly the degree-zero and degree-one HR/Lefschetz conditions in this socle degree. Quadratic generation of Ann(F) is an additional requirement, independent of merely finding that signature. Non-Koszulness still needs a nonzero Tor_{i,j}, j≠i, or an equivalent rigorous certificate.

For example F=X³−Y³ gives

A_F=R[x,y]/(xy,x³+y³).

At L=2x+y, the B_L matrix is diag(2,−1), deg(L³)=7, and the primitive generator x+4y has Q_1 value 14. This algebra has the full package and consequently has a small mixed cone by Proposition 1. It is nevertheless outside the target: its only quadratic relation is xy, and the independent cubic x³+y³ cannot follow from it. Since Koszul algebras are quadratic, this is a non-Koszul example only after dropping quadraticity. The checker explicitly labels it nonquadratic.

The inverse-system route has therefore supplied rigorous exclusion and validation tests, but no polynomial with all three of: a quadratic annihilator, a certified HR element/cone, and a nonzero off-diagonal Tor class. The idealization route gives quadratic non-Koszul examples with actual Lefschetz isomorphisms but a global square-zero obstruction to HR. The positive family gives full mixed HR and quadraticity but is Koszul. Small socle-degree arguments settle only d≤2. Nothing here proves that all remaining quadratic real Gorenstein HR algebras are Koszul.

A bounded primary-source search through 5 October 2026 did not locate a resolution of the universal question. The 2024 Bott–Samelson work proves a structured quadratic-complete-intersection class; the 2026 Chow-polynomial work constructs full-package Gorenstein algebras with certain Hilbert series, without asserting quadratic presentations for all of them. Neither is used to settle this question. Search silence is not a proof of openness or novelty.
