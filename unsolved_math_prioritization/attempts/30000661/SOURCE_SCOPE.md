# Exact source and scope

Numeric ID 30000661, OWR-1452-025, rank 249. The primary source is L. Makar-Limanov's problem-session paragraph in *Affine Algebraic Geometry*, OWR 01/2007, printed p. 73 (PDF p. 69), DOI 10.4171/owr/2007/01. The full PDF was recovered and this page was visually checked. The imported map agrees exactly:

    Delta=x^2*y+z^2,
    gamma(x)=x,
    gamma(z)=z+x*Delta,
    gamma(y)=y-2*x*y*z-Delta^2
             +2*(3*z^2*Delta+3*x*z*Delta^2+x^2*Delta^3).

The ground field is C and the ambient polynomial algebra has three variables. The source defines generalized tame automorphisms to be the subgroup generated by tame automorphisms and exp(D) for locally nilpotent derivations D of C[x,y,z]. It credits a broader generation conjecture to A. Joseph and presents this gamma as a potential counterexample, not as a proved one. The question is whether gamma belongs to that subgroup.

Tame means generated by affine and elementary triangular polynomial automorphisms. All exponential factors in a valid answer must be automorphisms of the polynomial ring, and the requested factorization stays in three variables. Although gamma fixes x, the source does not require every allowable factor to fix x. Proving nonmembership in an x-preserving subgroup would therefore not settle the full question without an additional reduction.

The source explicitly notes that gamma factors into two exponential automorphisms over the rational function setting. Such rational/localized factors need not preserve the polynomial ring and are not automatically allowed generators. The phrase about exponentials of rational automorphisms should be interpreted through rational flows or a suitable localized polynomial ring; a nonzero derivation of a characteristic-zero field is not locally nilpotent on every field element. No field-level factorization is being counted as a polynomial generalized-tame decomposition.

## Known stable result, with a different scope

Berson–van den Essen–Wright, *Stable Tameness of Two-Dimensional Polynomial Automorphisms Over a Regular Ring*, Advances in Mathematics 230 (2012), 2176–2197, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2012.04.017, proves stable tameness over regular coefficient rings. The full final preprint arXiv:0707.3151v10 (19 April 2012) was recovered. Theorem 4.10 is the main regular-ring statement. Remark 4.8 and Corollary 4.9 imply that a three-variable automorphism over a characteristic-zero field fixing a coordinate becomes tame after adjoining three variables, as an element of TA6.

This applies to gamma because it fixes x and is an automorphism of C[x][y,z]. It is credited existing stable-tameness information, not a resolution of membership in the original three-variable generalized-tame group. No destabilization implication is supplied by that theorem. The older v9 edition uses different numbering; the cited numbers here are v10.

The source itself points out that Nagata belongs to the generalized group. Thus failure of ordinary tameness would not by itself establish failure of generalized tameness. Density or approximation by tame maps likewise does not produce a finite exact generator factorization.
