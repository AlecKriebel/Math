# Substantive turn 2: polynomial flow words off a limit, and exact finite jets

**Unreviewed partial; the original finite-factorization question remains unresolved.** Date: 2026-10-01. This route genuinely allows factors moving x. It constructs at most 16 allowed exponential factors for each nonzero member of a relative family, and puts gamma in the coefficient closure of a subgroup generated by eight fixed polynomial additive-group flows. It also proves exact membership at every finite jet. Neither assertion establishes finite exact membership for gamma itself.

Use the ring-composition convention of Turn 1 and let U be the subgroup generated by all polynomial LND exponentials on R=C[x,y,z].

## 1. A polynomial family through the identity

For t in C define

    gamma_t(x)=x,
    gamma_t(z)=z+t*x*Delta,
    gamma_t(y)=y-2*t*x*y*z-t^2*Delta^2
                +6*t^2*z^2*Delta+6*t^3*x*z*Delta^2
                +2*t^4*x^2*Delta^3,                    (1)

where Delta=x^2*y+z^2. Put Theta_t=Delta-2*t*x*z^3. The inverse is polynomial in t and the coordinates:

    gamma_t^(-1)(x)=x,
    gamma_t^(-1)(z)=z-t*x*Theta_t,
    gamma_t^(-1)(y)=y+2*t*x*y*z-4*t^2*z^4-t^2*Theta_t^2. (2)

Thus this is an automorphism family over C[t], gamma_1=gamma, and gamma_0 is the identity. Its Jacobian is one: the Jacobian is a unit of C[t] for an automorphism over that ring, and at t=0 it is one. This family alone is not an additive-group action.

For lambda nonzero, define the determinant-one diagonal map

    d_lambda(x)=lambda^3*x,
    d_lambda(y)=lambda^(-4)*y,
    d_lambda(z)=lambda*z.

Direct weighted substitution gives

    d_lambda gamma d_lambda^(-1)=gamma_(lambda^4).     (3)

The weights of (x,y,z) are (3,-4,1), Delta has weight two, and the successive coefficients in (1) have parameter weights 4,8,12,16. The diagonal map itself does not extend as an automorphism at lambda=0; only the conjugated polynomial family does.

## 2. A finite word in allowed flows for every nonzero relative parameter

Each d_lambda is a product of eight elementary transvections. For an explicit convention, write

    T_ij(c)=exp(c*x_j*partial_(x_i)).

For distinct coordinate indices i,j, the ring word

    T_ji(-a) T_ij(a^(-1)-1) T_ji(1) T_ij(a-1)         (4)

scales x_i by a and x_j by a^(-1), fixing the other coordinate. This follows by multiplying the two-by-two coordinate matrices in reverse order, as required for composition of ring maps. Use (4) with a=lambda^3 on (x,y) and then with a=lambda^(-1) on (y,z).

Let the resulting eight factors be E_1,...,E_8, each a polynomial LND exponential. Then

    eta_lambda := gamma_(lambda^4) gamma^(-1)
                 =d_lambda (gamma d_lambda^(-1) gamma^(-1))              (5)

is a product of those eight factors and eight conjugated inverse factors. For every polynomial LND D, gamma D gamma^(-1) is again a polynomial LND, irrespective of whether gamma itself belongs to U. Consequently (5) is a word of at most sixteen allowed polynomial LND exponentials.

There is no assumption of gamma's desired membership in this argument. Gamma and its inverse are already explicit polynomial maps. They define the new derivations as actual polynomial operators, and nilpotence follows by conjugacy on R, not by assuming gamma lies in U.

These factors genuinely use the larger group, including motion of x. For example, with B=y*partial_x,

    (gamma B gamma^(-1))(x)=gamma(y) != 0.

Its nilpotence on the coordinates follows from B^2(gamma^(-1)(x))=0, B^5(gamma^(-1)(y))=0 and B^4(gamma^(-1)(z))=0. Conjugating these identities gives a direct finite certificate; nilpotence on arbitrary polynomials then follows by the Leibniz rule.

Only eight fixed derivation directions are needed, with varying complex time parameters:

    y*partial_x, x*partial_y, z*partial_y, y*partial_z,
    and their four conjugates by gamma.                (6)

Let V be the subgroup generated by these eight additive-group flows. Then V is contained in U, all its generators fix the origin, and eta_lambda belongs to V for every lambda!=0.

## 3. A coefficient degeneration to the actual target

The inverse family

    phi_lambda := eta_lambda^(-1)
                 = gamma gamma_(lambda^4)^(-1)          (7)

has polynomial coordinate coefficients in lambda by (2). Both it and its inverse have uniformly bounded degrees in the coordinates. For every nonzero lambda it belongs to V, while

    phi_0=gamma.

Therefore gamma lies in the Zariski closure, and also in the ordinary coefficient-topology closure, of V inside a bounded-degree space of polynomial automorphisms. This proves a genuine approximation statement using allowed polynomial flows which need not fix x.

It does not prove gamma belongs to V or U. The times appearing in (4) contain negative powers of lambda. Taking their limit separately is invalid. The polynomial word map from its finite-dimensional parameter space need not be proper or have closed image. No closedness assertion for V or U in the infinite-dimensional automorphism group has been established here.

In particular, any group homomorphism killing U which is continuous along the coefficient family (7), with Hausdorff target, must also kill gamma. The same conclusion holds for a regular algebraic quotient with closed identity point. Such invariants cannot separate gamma from U. This statement does not rule out abstract, discontinuous group-theoretic obstructions.

## 4. Exact finite-jet membership

There is a stronger finite-dimensional consequence. For every m>=1, the m-jet of gamma at the origin is the m-jet of a finite word in the eight flows (6).

All these maps fix the origin. Hence truncation modulo (x,y,z)^(m+1) defines a group homomorphism to the finite-dimensional algebraic group G_m of automorphisms of that truncated local algebra. Each of the eight flows has a connected algebraic image in G_m. It is the exponential of a nilpotent linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space, so its image is either an additive one-parameter subgroup or the identity.

We use the following elementary algebraic-group fact. A subgroup H of a finite-dimensional algebraic group generated by connected algebraic subgroups H_i is closed and connected. Here is a proof adequate for this application. Starting from {e}, enlarge a product P of the H_i whenever left multiplication by one of the H_i increases the dimension of its Zariski closure. After finitely many increases this stops, because the ambient dimension is finite. The irreducible closure K of the resulting product is stable under left multiplication by every H_i: the closure of H_i K is irreducible, contains K and has the same dimension. Therefore H is contained in K and is dense there, so K is a subgroup. By Chevalley's theorem, P is constructible and contains a nonempty open subset O of K. For any k in K, the open sets O and kO intersect, since K is irreducible. Thus k=a*b^(-1) for a,b in O, showing K=P*P^(-1) is contained in H. Hence H=K.

The constructibility input is [Chevalley's theorem, Stacks Project tag 054K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/054K); no infinite-dimensional closedness is inferred from it. The argument even gives a nonuniform bound of twice the ambient group dimension on the number of chosen subgroup factors. The dimension can grow with m.

Apply this fact to the eight jet-flow images. Their generated subgroup is closed in G_m and contains the jets of phi_lambda for all nonzero lambda. The jet family is regular at zero, so it also contains the jet of phi_0=gamma. This proves the claim.

For clarity, a crude dimension bound is

    dim G_m <= 3*(binomial(m+3,3)-1),

because the coordinate images have no constant term and degree at most m. The preceding existence argument consequently permits a jet word with at most six times that monomial count of factors. It supplies no uniform bound independent of m, no specified time parameters, and no bound forcing the corresponding full polynomial word to have the degree of gamma.

Thus agreement at every finite jet is exact, but the words can change with the truncation order. Passing from these words to a single finite polynomial identity would require a further argument. Finite-jet tests alone cannot be a nonmembership certificate for this map and these allowed generators.

## 5. Why the route does not yet solve the source question

The construction explicitly addresses generators moving x and improves the merely localized factorization from Turn 1: every nonzero relative-family member has a finite word of genuine polynomial LND exponentials. The limiting map is still the requested gamma. No valid step currently turns the limiting word, whose individual times can diverge, into a finite word for gamma itself.

A possible full solution would need a polynomial factorization of that endpoint, a uniform finite-word/degree mechanism which makes a sufficiently high jet decisive, or a genuinely abstract invariant surviving all polynomial LND generators. The present result rules out regular or coefficient-continuous quotient invariants of the indicated kind but does not supply such an abstract obstruction.

The broader Exponential Generators Conjecture must not be assumed. A current 2026 primary preprint, Adamus–Hajto arXiv:2607.09340, proves a plane result via Jung–van der Kulk; its abstract does not state a three-variable solution. Likewise, the already credited stable-tameness theorem uses extra variables. Neither result closes this finite-factorization gap.

`turn_2_check.py` passes 27 exact identities: the polynomial inverse family, determinant-one weighted conjugacy, four-transvection block, a conjugated LND moving x with explicit nilpotence bounds, and the relative-family limits. The general finite-jet conclusion is proved by the algebraic-group argument above, not by sampled jets.
