# Known resolution: chi_3(S^3) is infinite

Proposed status: **already_solved, 0/5 new author turns**, subject to separate
source and theorem review. The result is due to Seunghun Lee and Eran Nevo.

## Exact original target

OWR45/2015, Problem SEVEN, Frank Lutz attributing the question to Jesper M.
Møller, printed pp.2693–2694, asks for chi_3(S^3). Vertices of a finite
simplicial triangulation of the topological 3-sphere are colored. Triangles may
be monochromatic, but tetrahedra may not. For a triangulation T, chi_3(T) is
the minimum number of colors meeting that rule, and chi_3(S^3) is the supremum
over all such triangulations, as defined in Lutz–Møller, Definition1.

There is no metric, unit-distance, radius, covering or measurable-coloring
condition. There is no restriction to flag complexes, a fixed triangulation,
or boundaries of convex 4-polytopes. Each individual finite triangulation
has finite coloring number; infinitude means there is no bound uniform over
all triangulations.

The imported statement gives the correct coloring question, but its dated
literature assessment discusses knot-complement treewidth. That paragraph
belongs to the adjacent Problem EIGHT on p.2694 and is not evidence about
this target's status. Both original pages were checked visually.

## Exact published theorem

Lee–Nevo, *On Colorings of Hypergraphs Embeddable in R^d*, Discrete &
Computational Geometry **76 (2026), 1942–1959**, DOI
10.1007/s00454-026-00856-4, published 3 July 2026. The complete final
open-access PDF was obtained and inspected. Main Theorem D, printed p.1943,
says that for every fixed triangulable d-manifold M with d>=3, its s-face
weak chromatic number is infinite for 1<=s<=d. The theorem defines the same
supremum over the hypergraphs of (s+1)-vertex faces of triangulations.

Apply it with d=s=3 and M=S^3. This yields exactly

                         chi_3(S^3) = infinity.

No new proof is asserted. The following map of its proof records why the
geometric and coloring hypotheses agree, especially the preservation of
original tetrahedra when an embedded complex is extended to a sphere.

## Proof correspondence and the essential extension step

1. Fix an arbitrary finite number c of colors. The classical Hales–Jewett
   theorem supplies an N such that every c-coloring of {1,2,3,4}^N contains
   a monochromatic combinatorial line. Each line has four words; some
   nonempty set of coordinates varies together through 1,2,3,4, and the
   other coordinates have fixed values.
2. The resulting four-uniform hypergraph is linear: if two lines contain
   distinct common words u,v, the active set is precisely the coordinates
   in which u and v differ. The other fixed values are also determined.
   Thus the two lines coincide. Every pair of distinct edges meets in at
   most one vertex.
3. Lee–Nevo Lemma3.1, pp.1948–1950, proves that every linear (d+1)-uniform
   hypergraph is PL embeddable in R^d for d>=3. For d=3 this applies to
   the abstract simplicial complex generated by our four-vertex edges.
   Their proof embeds its bipartite incidence graph, replaces each edge-node
   by a small polyhedral ball with thin arms to its four vertex-nodes, and
   gives compatible simplicial subdivisions identifying each ball with a
   subdivided tetrahedron. Linearity controls all overlaps. This is PL
   embeddability; it is not asserted to be a straight-tetrahedron embedding
   on the original vertex set in Euclidean R^3.
4. Their Theorem1.1 and Main Theorem D use an extension theorem that keeps
   the original abstract complex, not just a subdivision, as a subcomplex
   of a PL triangulation. One can also apply Adiprasito–Patáková's Theorem2
   directly after placing the PL-embedded complex in a coordinate ball of
   the standard PL S^3. It yields a triangulation of S^3 containing the
   original complex as an abstract subcomplex. The embedding map may
   change and additional vertices are allowed; the hyperedge vertices and
   their four-vertex tetrahedra are not lost.
5. Any c-coloring of that sphere avoiding monochromatic tetrahedra restricts
   to a c-coloring of the embedded Hales–Jewett hypergraph with no
   monochromatic line. This contradicts step1. Since c was arbitrary,
   the required supremum is infinite.

All these inputs and the application are already in the cited literature.
A mere triangulation containing a subdivision of the original hypergraph
would not suffice: subdivision can remove the original four-vertex coloring
constraints. The full unsubdivided-subcomplex theorem is the reason the
argument proves the actual target.

## Extension source and access

Adiprasito–Patáková, *A higher dimensional version of Fáry's theorem*,
Bull. London Math. Soc.57(5) (2025),1409–1414, DOI10.1112/blms.70036,
Theorem2: a finite complex PL embedded in a PL manifold is contained as a
subcomplex of a triangulation of that manifold. The final publisher HTML
and its full proof were read, together with the complete April2024 author
PDF. The proof uses induced/strongly induced subdivisions and compatible
edge contractions to restore the original complex after an initial
subdivision. It expressly permits changing the embedding map.

For this question, S^3 with its usual PL structure satisfies the theorem's
manifold hypothesis. The constructed PL triangulations are among the
triangulations allowed by the original source, regardless of whether one
allows additional broader categories.

## Other literature and scope limits

- Lutz–Møller's arXiv:1503.08251v3 (2019) supplies the exact hierarchy and
  manifold supremum definition. Its earlier unboundedness for s<=ceil(d/2)
  does not alone answer s=d=3.
- Briggs–Dobbins–Lee (arXiv:2111.06560v3) and Novik–Zheng's July2024
  manuscript concern lower bounds and transversal ratios. Those are related
  quantities and are not substituted for the direct Lee–Nevo theorem.
- Dobbins–Lee, arXiv:2603.16298v1 (2026), gives further polytopal results in
  higher dimensions and explicitly cites the unbounded 3-sphere chromatic
  number. Its remaining 4-polytope/straight-line questions are distinct from
  the unrestricted triangulation target here. We do not resolve them.

The theorem gives no small explicit witness for a prescribed c, optimal
vertex-growth bound, or efficient coloring algorithm in this assessment.
No historical novelty or independent discovery is claimed. Finite checks
of the line incidence and the restriction logic do not prove the Hales–Jewett
or PL extension theorems.
