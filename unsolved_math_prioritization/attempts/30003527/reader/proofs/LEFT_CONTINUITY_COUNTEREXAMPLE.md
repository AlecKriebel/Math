# Left-Hausdorff continuity does not imply a movable vertical invariant core

Problem 30003527 / queue row 1767. Authored 13 October 2026 as complementary work within the fifth and final shared substantive pass. This is a counterexample to the last proposed sufficient condition, not to the full invariant-enlargement problem or the existential-good-radius assertion.

## Result and scope

There is a compact movable stationary set C in R³ such that, for the full flow generated by

F_C(x,y,z) = min(1, dist((x,y,z),C)) e_3

and its metric invariant cores M_r(C) = Inv({dist(.,C) <= r}),

1. M_1(C) is nonmovable.
2. The increasing family r |-> M_r(C) is Hausdorff-continuous at r=1, from both sides.
3. Every M_r(C) with 0<r<1 is movable.
4. The ambient distance function has no Clarke critical value in [3/5,infinity); in particular the bad continuous radius is also an ambient regular radius.

Thus even the conjunction of left-Hausdorff continuity, ambient regularity, and movability of every strictly smaller metric core does not imply movability at the limiting radius. The stationary set C is itself movable, so B=C satisfies every instance of the original enlargement request for this particular flow and stationary set. No all-enlargements obstruction, bad interval of radii, or failure of arbitrarily small good radii follows.

The central idea is explicit: auxiliary isolated columns, each admitted at an earlier radius, approach the binding columns of a nonmovable stretched book. This removes the macroscopic left jump without removing the nonmovable retract.

## 1. Accepted input and its elementary empty-interior property

Use the accepted stretched-book compactum from pass 2, in the normalization of the independently accepted regular-wall example from pass 4:

D = (Q_0 x {-2}) union (Q_1 x {2}) union (T x [-2,2]),

where D is nonmovable and, in planar coordinates (x,y),

Q_0 subset [0,1/10] x [9/10,11/10],
Q_1 subset [0,1/10] x [1,11/10],
T subset [0,1/10] x {1},
Q_0 intersection {y=1} = Q_1 intersection {y=1} = T.

All three sets are nonempty compacta. In particular Q_0 intersection Q_1 lies in y>=1. These sets are obtained by embedding a dyadic solenoid K in a three-page book, separating one page into the upper horizontal sheet, and stretching the binding points into vertical intervals. The accepted direct near-homeomorphism proof establishes nonmovability of D.

We also need that Q_0 has empty planar interior below y=1. This follows directly from the solenoid input. A planar open disk contained in Q_0 at y<1 would correspond to an open disk in a page away from the book's binding. A smaller such disk would be an open connected subset of K. A dyadic solenoid has no nonempty open subset homeomorphic to a planar open disk. Its sufficiently small local charts are products J x Z_2 of an open interval and the Cantor space of 2-adic integers, and any connected subset of one chart is contained in one interval fiber, which has empty interior. If an open disk in K were given, choose a smaller connected open disk within one of these charts; projection of that smaller disk to Z_2 is a singleton, contradicting its openness in the product. Hence the asserted planar disk cannot exist.

Only this empty-interior fact, not any extra regularity of the embedding or binding T, is used below. T need not be assumed zero dimensional.

## 2. Choose the auxiliary bases carefully

Let b_n=2^(-n-5), n>=1. For each n choose a finite set R_n contained in T which is a b_n-net of T; thus each t in T lies within b_n of some point of R_n.

For every t in R_n choose one planar point q=q(n,t) satisfying

- 0 < q_x < 1/10;
- 1-b_n < q_y < 1;
- |q-t| < b_n;
- q is outside Q_0 union Q_1;
- q differs from every point chosen previously.

Such a point exists. The intersection of the open disk B(t,b_n) with 0<x<1/10 and 1-b_n<y<1 is nonempty and open, even if t lies at an endpoint of the allowed x interval. Q_0 has empty interior there; Q_1 is absent because y<1. Removing the finitely many previously chosen points still leaves a possible choice. For each finite R_n make the choices successively.

Write Q for the countable union of these chosen points, and retain the assigned point t(q) in R_n for each q chosen at level n. Then:

- Q is disjoint from Q_0 union Q_1 and lies in [0,1/10] x (9/10,1).
- All accumulation points of Q lie in T, because b_n tends to zero and each level is finite.
- Every point of T is an accumulation point of Q, by the net property.
- Every q in Q is isolated in Q union Q_0 union Q_1. Indeed all accumulation points of Q lie in T, while q is outside the compact Q_0 union Q_1 containing T.
- For any a<1, only finitely many q in Q satisfy q_y<=a. For all sufficiently large n, 1-b_n>a.
- If q_j is any sequence of distinct points in Q converging to t in T, then |q_j-t(q_j)| tends to zero and t(q_j) tends to t.

Consequently Q_i union Q is compact for i=0,1. Although Q itself is not closed, all of its missing limit points already belong to each Q_i.

## 3. The movable stationary set and the exact metric-core formula

Put

P_0 = Q_0 union Q,
P_1 = Q_1 union Q,
W = [-1,1] x {0} x [-2,2],
C = (P_0 x {-2}) union (P_1 x {2}) union W.

The three pieces of C are pairwise disjoint compact planar sets; their separation is positive. Each is movable by planar movability, and their finite disjoint union is movable. This is the legitimate disjoint-union argument, not an unrestricted finite-gluing principle.

The field F_C is bounded and globally Lipschitz, so it generates a unique full continuous flow. It fixes C pointwise. Every other orbit is an entire open complementary interval of one vertical fiber. A compact invariant superset of C can add only whole bounded gaps, and M_r adds exactly those bounded gaps whose closed segments have distance at most r from C everywhere.

Above the wall projection, fibers are already intervals. Every other bounded fiber gap has endpoints -2 and 2 and base in

P_0 intersection P_1 = (Q_0 intersection Q_1) union Q.

The equality uses the deliberate disjointness of Q from both original bases. Thus there are no additional unintended fiber configurations.

Fix any base p=(x,y) in this intersection. Its x coordinate lies in [0,1/10], and 9/10<y<=11/10. At every height -2<=z<=2, the same-height wall point (x,0,z) is present, so

dist((p,z),C) <= y.

At the midpoint (p,0), the distance to the wall is exactly y, while every point in either horizontal sheet is at vertical distance 2 or more. Since y<2,

dist((p,0),C) = y.

Therefore the maximum of the distance over the entire closed fiber gap is exactly y. In particular, for every r>0 the exact formula is

M_r(C) = C union (((Q_0 intersection Q_1) union Q) intersection {y<=r}) x [-2,2].       (1)

Here the Cartesian product in the last term applies to the entire displayed planar set. Unbounded orbits add no points. Formula (1) proves in particular that other auxiliary endpoints do not contaminate the decisive distance test: all such endpoints remain on z=+/-2.

At r=1, the original common bases selected are exactly T and every auxiliary base is selected. Hence

M_1(C) = W union X,
X = D union (Q x [-2,2]).                                                         (2)

Both W and X are compact and separated by positive distance.

## 4. Nonmovability survives as an actual retract

Define R:X->D by

R(d)=d for d in D,
R(q,z)=(t(q),z) for q in Q and -2<=z<=2.

The formulas are unambiguous because Q is disjoint from the original planar bases and from T. Each auxiliary column is isolated in X, so R is continuous there. At an accumulation point of distinct auxiliary columns, their bases converge to a point t in T, their assigned t(q) converge to the same point t, and their heights converge to the corresponding height. Thus their images converge to that same point of T x [-2,2]. This proves continuity at all possible remaining points. R fixes D, so D is a retract of X.

For completeness, movability passes to retracts by the full neighborhood-homotopy definition. Let A be a retract of a movable compactum X in Euclidean space, with retraction R:X->A. Extend the coordinates of R from the closed set X to a continuous map f on the ambient Euclidean space, using Tietze extension. Fix an outer neighborhood O of A. The set O_X=f^(-1)(O) is an open neighborhood of X. Apply movability of X to obtain a neighborhood V_X within O_X. Since f fixes A, choose an open neighborhood V of A contained in V_X intersection O such that every straight segment from v to f(v), for v in V, stays in O. This is possible by continuity and compactness of A, or by first choosing a sufficiently small uniform metric neighborhood of A.

For any target neighborhood U of A, f^(-1)(U) is a neighborhood of X. Movability of X gives a homotopy of V_X within O_X from inclusion to a map into f^(-1)(U). Compose with f and restrict to V. It starts at f|V, stays in O, and ends in U. Prepend the straight-line homotopy from inclusion to f|V. The neighborhood V is independent of U, which proves movability of A.

Thus X cannot be movable, because its retract D is nonmovable. Equivalently, extend R to all of M_1 by mapping the separated component W constantly to any point of D; this makes D a retract of M_1 itself. It follows directly that M_1(C) is nonmovable.

## 5. Every smaller core is movable, yet the cores converge to the bad core

If 0<r<1, no original base in Q_0 intersection Q_1 is selected. The selected auxiliary set

Q(r) = {q in Q : q_y<=r}

is finite. Equation (1) becomes

M_r(C) = C union (Q(r) x [-2,2]).                                                 (3)

Each selected base q is isolated in P_0 union P_1. Remove its two endpoints from C and regard its whole column as a separate component. After removing all finitely many selected endpoint pairs, the remaining parts of the two horizontal sheets are compact planar sets. They, the wall, and the finitely many selected interval columns form a finite family of pairwise disjoint compact movable sets. Positive separation holds because each selected base is isolated and the family is finite. Their union is exactly (3), and so M_r(C) is movable.

Nevertheless,

closure(union_{0<r<1} M_r(C)) = M_1(C).                                           (4)

Every auxiliary column occurs in some M_r with r<1. Their bases approach every point of T, so their closure contains all binding columns T x [-2,2]. All other points of M_1 already lie in C. Conversely every M_r for r<1 is contained in the closed M_1. This proves (4).

An increasing family of nonempty compact subsets of one compact metric space converges in Hausdorff distance to the closure of its union as the parameter approaches its endpoint from below. To check the uniform statement directly, cover the compact limit by epsilon-balls centered at points of the union, take a finite subcover, and choose one parameter containing all finitely many centers. Formula (4) therefore gives

lim_{r up to 1} d_H(M_r(C), M_1(C)) = 0.                                         (5)

The family is also right-continuous. More generally, for any full flow and compact nested metric neighborhoods N_r of a fixed compact C,

intersection_{s>r} Inv(N_s) = Inv(intersection_{s>r} N_s) = Inv(N_r).

All these invariant sets lie in a fixed compact N_{r+1} when r<s<=r+1. Decreasing compact sets converge in Hausdorff distance to their intersection, giving right continuity. One can also read this directly from (1). Together with (5), this proves full Hausdorff continuity at the nonmovable radius 1.

## 6. Ambient regularity is preserved

The regular-wall distance proof applies without alteration of its constants, because both new sheet bases P_i still lie in [0,1/10] x [9/10,11/10]. For clarity, the full elementary convex-hull calculation is repeated here.

Let a=(x,y,z), rho=dist(a,C)>=3/5, and suppose a lies in the convex hull of its nearest points in C. Convex-hull containment forces

-1<=x<=1, 0<=y<=11/10, -2<=z<=2.

The nearest wall point is (x,0,z), so rho<=y<=11/10. Both sheets cannot contain nearest points, because their separation 4 is greater than 2rho. At least one sheet must occur in a convex representation, because using only wall points would give y=0. Let its height be sigma in {-2,2}, and let lambda be the coefficient of the wall point (zero if absent). Since y>0, lambda<1. The height equation

z=lambda z+(1-lambda)sigma

forces z=sigma. The x-coordinate equation similarly forces x in [0,1/10]. Now any point c of the participating sheet satisfies

|x-c_x|<=1/10, |y-c_y|<=1/2, z=c_z.

Thus rho<=|a-c|<=sqrt(26)/10<3/5, a contradiction.

Accordingly no such a belongs to the convex hull of its nearest points. Strict separation of the compact nearest-point set supplies a direction with uniformly negative local directional derivative of distance, and therefore excludes zero from the Clarke subdifferential, exactly as proved in the accepted pass-4 regular-radius report. Hence there is no ambient Clarke critical value at distance at least 3/5.

The standard regular-level consequence gives a compact Lipschitz unit distance sphere. Near each binding midpoint (t,0), t in T, the nearest stationary point is uniquely its wall projection, and the distance function is locally the planar y-coordinate. These smooth boundary patches still contain the binding orbits. Hausdorff continuity of the invariant-core family does not turn this non-isolating metric neighborhood into an isolating block.

## 7. Why the countability observation cannot supply the missing theorem

For any increasing compact-valued family within a fixed compact metric space, put L_r=closure(union_{s<r} M_s). There are at most countably many radii with L_r properly contained in M_r. Indeed choose a countable base of open balls. At any such radius there is a base ball meeting M_r and disjoint from L_r. The same ball cannot witness a jump at a later radius, because its point in M_r then belongs to that later left-limit union. Assigning one witness ball to each jump gives an injection into a countable set.

For the metric-core family the right-continuity proof above is valid as well. Neither fact has been disputed. What fails is the further implication from a nonjump radius to movability. The present construction has L_1=M_1 while M_1 is nonmovable. Thus the proposed route to co-countably many good radii has no justification from these continuity facts alone.
