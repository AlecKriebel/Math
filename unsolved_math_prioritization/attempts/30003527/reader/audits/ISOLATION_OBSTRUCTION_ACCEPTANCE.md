# Independent acceptance: finite ambient winding for isolated circle components

Date: 13 October 2026 UTC. Target context: row 1767 / 30003527 / OWR-15575-006. This is adversarial verification within the existing substantive pass 2, not a new proof-search pass.

Audited mathematical draft: `SATELLITE_ISOLATION_OBSTRUCTION.md`, SHA-256 `892977eb6aa28395481ae0b6758d3e04cef0c2691603e7655f0cf84465416766`.

## Verdict

**Accepted with the explicitly imported topological-flow results below. No mathematical qualification or proof repair is required.**

For an isolated invariant compactum K of a continuous full flow on R³, an open O containing K, and one fixed integral class u in H¹(O), the absolute coefficients of u on the clopen circle components of K take only finitely many distinct values. The accepted pass-1 satellite compactum has coefficients 3·2ⁿ, so it cannot be isolated for any such flow.

This verifies an obstruction to the isolated realization of that specific compactum. It establishes neither a counterexample nor a positive answer to the unrestricted invariant-enlargement question. In particular, additional invariant structure may invalidate the clopen-component hypotheses when the ambient maximal invariant set is enlarged.

## 2. A single block and a genuine compact entry surface

Choose the block N after O is fixed, with K⊂int N and N⊂O. This order is essential: all subsequent inclusions and flow homotopies take place in O. N may be disconnected. A compact manifold has finitely many components, and its boundary is orientable because N is a codimension-zero manifold in R³. Thus E=Nⁱ is a compact orientable surface with finitely many components; some may have empty boundary, and E itself may be empty.

Set D=N⁺ and L=D∩E. D is closed in compact N, since remaining in N at each forward time is a closed condition. E is closed as well, so L is compact. A point on ∂E is an exterior tangency and immediately leaves N in forward time. It cannot lie in D. Similarly, no transverse exit point belongs to D. Consequently D∩∂N=D∩E=L⊂Int E. This verifies precisely the interior hypothesis needed for the surface theorem; no unsupported regularity of L is inferred.

The groups R_q=Čech H^q(D,L;Z) are finitely generated by the imported identification with H^q(N,E;Z). Finiteness of the latter also follows from the pair sequence and finite generation for the compact manifolds N and E. Simultaneous triangulation of the pair is not an additional needed assumption. This step concerns (N,E), not (N,K), whose cohomology need not be finitely generated.

## 3. Stable pieces, limits, and the component assignment

Every component of K is invariant: the orbit of a point is connected and remains in K, and therefore cannot leave its component. Thus C_n and the compact clopen complement K\C_n are both invariant, even when the circle contains equilibria or has nonperiodic dynamics.

The sets D·t are compact, decrease with t≥0, and have intersection K. Indeed, belonging to every D·k means that the full negative orbit as well as the forward orbit stays in N. Conversely every point of K belongs to every D·t. For a relative neighborhood V of K in D, if no D·k were contained in V, a nested-compact-set argument applied outside an open neighborhood contained in V would produce a point of K outside V. Hence D·[T,∞)⊂V for a sufficiently large T.

For each fixed n choose disjoint closed relative neighborhoods U_n and U'_n of C_n and K\C_n. After a sufficiently large T_n every forward tail lies in their union. The inverse images under p↦p·T_n give a two-piece compact clopen partition of D. The tail of each orbit is connected, so it cannot change from one side to the other.

For p∈D the omega-limit set is nonempty because its forward orbit stays in compact D. It is contained in K, and is connected by the nested-closure description of orbit tails. Thus membership in the piece D_n is equivalent to ω(p)⊂C_n: one implication follows from confinement to U_n; the other follows because confinement to U'_n would give a nonempty limit set in K\C_n. In particular:

- D_n is intrinsically defined, independently of the auxiliary neighborhoods and T_n.
- D_n is positively invariant, because a time shift does not change ω(p).
- D_n∩K=C_n.
- Distinct D_n are disjoint, because a nonempty omega-limit set cannot be contained in two disjoint circles.

The argument requires a separate T_n for each n, and never asserts a uniform time over infinitely many clopen components. It does not assume every point of D is assigned to one of the listed circles: the unassigned remainder is retained in every finite decomposition. There is no decomposition off of a limiting component which is not clopen.

## 4. Inclusion-induced equivalence with the circle

For each n put Y_k=D_n·k. Positive invariance gives nested compact Y_k. Their intersection is C_n: it is contained in K∩D_n, and invariance of C_n gives the opposite inclusion.

The time-one map f:Y_k→Y_{k+1} is a homotopy inverse of the inclusion i:Y_{k+1}→Y_k. The composition i∘f is the time-one map on Y_k and is homotopic to its identity by forward flow for times in [0,1]. The other composition f∘i is similarly homotopic to the identity on Y_{k+1}; positive invariance keeps this second homotopy in Y_{k+1}. This checks both domains rather than merely noting that time-one is an ambient homeomorphism.

Čech continuity therefore makes the actual restriction map from D_n to C_n an isomorphism in every degree. In particular H⁰(D_n;Z)=H¹(D_n;Z)=Z. Although connectedness was not assumed in constructing D_n, it follows here: a disconnected compact Hausdorff space has a nontrivial clopen separation, giving at least two independent locally constant integral functions. No path-connectedness, local connectedness, deformation retraction, or pointwise limiting retraction is required.

## 5. Finite relative exceptions, including torsion

For every finite set of indices J, the sets D_n for n∈J and their complement form a finite clopen partition of D. Intersecting each with L gives a finite disjoint decomposition of compact pairs. Finite additivity of relative Čech cohomology therefore splits each H^q(D_n,L_n;Z), where L_n=L∩D_n, as one of simultaneously independent direct summands of R_q.

Write R_q≅Z^{r_q}⊕T_q with T_q finite. In a finite direct-sum decomposition of R_q, at most r_q nonzero factors can have positive rank. Every rank-zero factor is finitely generated, as a direct summand of R_q, and hence finite. For its nonzero factors, the product of torsion orders divides |T_q|. There are at most floor(log₂|T_q|) such rank-zero factors. Thus the number of nonzero factors is bounded independently of J. Choosing a finite J larger than this bound would contradict any assertion that infinitely many indices have nonzero H^q(D_n,L_n).

Apply this separately in q=0,1,2 and take the union F of the three resulting finite exceptional sets. This gives one finite F valid simultaneously in all degrees needed below. There is no illicit inference from finite rank alone: the finite torsion is explicitly controlled. Nor does the proof form or assume an infinite direct-sum decomposition of (D,L).

For n∉F the exact pair sequence reads, in the relevant portion,

0 → H⁰(D_n) → H⁰(L_n) → 0 → H¹(D_n) → H¹(L_n) → 0.

Both displayed restriction maps are therefore isomorphisms over Z. In particular L_n is nonempty and connected, and H¹(L_n;Z)=Z. The degree-zero assertion is important: first cohomology alone would not exclude extra entry-trace components.

## 6. Annuli at arbitrary scale and exact integer transport

Each L_n is compact in Int E. A compact subset of an open surface has arbitrarily small compact surface neighborhoods with the subset in their interiors; this follows, for example, by taking a finite regular neighborhood in a sufficiently fine surface triangulation. Given finitely many pairwise disjoint L_n, first choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods and then such compact surface neighborhoods inside them.

Apply Theorem 13 to each of these initial surface neighborhoods individually. Its resulting P_n remains inside the chosen neighborhood, contains L_n in its interior, and the inclusion L_n→P_n induces the asserted cohomology isomorphisms. P_n is orientable as a subsurface of E and is connected because H⁰(P_n)=Z. A closed connected orientable surface has even first Betti number, so P_n is not closed. The formula rank H¹(P_n)=2g+b−1=1 then forces g=0 and b=2. Thus P_n is an annulus.

Only a finite selection is made at any one time. There is no claim that an infinite family of entry traces admits simultaneously disjoint annular neighborhoods. Accumulation of the L_n in E does not obstruct the finite selection needed for the contradiction.

Let τ_n be an annular core. Every arrow in

C_n → D_n ← L_n → P_n ← τ_n

is an actual inclusion of subspaces of O and induces an integral H¹ isomorphism. Regard the ordinary class u on the manifold O as a Čech class using the natural identification on manifolds. Its restrictions commute with every arrow in the diagram. Between infinite cyclic groups an isomorphism multiplies a generator only by +1 or −1. Consequently the coefficient of u on τ_n is exactly ±w_n, not an arbitrary multiple and not merely an equality of rational ranks.

No simple-curve representative inside the wild trace L_n is assumed. No singular homology statement about L_n replaces its Čech cohomology. No retraction from L_n to a circle is used. Possible wildness of C_n itself does not affect this cohomological transport.

## 7. Surface bound and all low-complexity cases

The compact surface E has finitely many connected components. For each component, a disjoint collection of pairwise nonisotopic simple closed curves that are neither contractible nor boundary-parallel is bounded by the usual pants-decomposition count; boundary-parallel classes add at most one per boundary component. Spheres and disks contribute no noncontractible classes. An annulus contributes at most one; a closed torus also contributes at most one, because disjoint essential curves on a torus are parallel. The draft's loose bound

b(E)=Σ_i max(1,3g_i+2b_i)

dominates these counts. Here b_i is the number of boundary components of E_i. If E is empty the sum is zero. Adding across disconnected components is legitimate.

If w_n≠0 then τ_n cannot be nullhomotopic in E, because its image in O would then be nullhomotopic and the restricted class u would vanish. If two τ_n are isotopic in E, their parameterized loops agree up to free homotopy and possibly orientation reversal, so their u-coefficients have equal absolute value. This implication does not require the reverse assertion that equal coefficients force isotopy.

Infinitely many distinct nonzero |w_n| outside F would permit selection of b(E)+1 such indices. Section 6 supplies a finite family of disjoint core curves with those coefficients. They are essential and pairwise nonisotopic, contradicting b(E). The finite F adds finitely many values and zero adds at most one. Thus the entire winding spectrum is finite. More explicitly, the proof bounds the number of distinct values by |F|+b(E)+1; no bound on the numerical sizes of the exceptional values is asserted.

An empty entry surface causes no defect: L_n is then empty for every n, so H⁰(D_n,L_n)=Z. Finite generation of R_0 forces only finitely many listed circle components, and all can be exceptional. If K is empty there are no circle components and the conclusion is immediate.

## 8. Application to the accepted satellites and exact remaining scope

In the accepted geometry, C_n lies in int T_n\T_{n+1}, has longitudinal degree 3 in T_n, and T_n has degree 2ⁿ in T₀. Thus a generator on O=int T₀ restricts with absolute coefficient 3·2ⁿ. For a fixed n, the tail consisting of the solenoid and later circles is contained in T_{n+1}, which is compact and disjoint from C_n. The finitely many earlier circles are also compact and disjoint. Therefore the compact remainder X\C_n has positive distance from C_n. Each C_n is a clopen component of X, and X⊂O.

All hypotheses of the new theorem are satisfied under the supposition that X is isolated. Its unbounded, pairwise distinct coefficients contradict the finite-spectrum conclusion. Hence the impossibility of an isolated realization holds for every continuous full flow on R³, regardless of its dynamics on the circles or solenoid.

This conclusion does not establish that the circles and solenoid cannot be simultaneously invariant. It does not exclude isolated invariant supersets in which they are no longer components. It does not establish movability of such supersets, movability of all isolated compacta, or the existence or nonexistence of a movable invariant B in every prescribed neighborhood. No new claim of resolution or priority is certified.
