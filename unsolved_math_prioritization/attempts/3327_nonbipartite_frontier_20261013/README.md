# Pentagon scarcity, the three-square skeleton, and the first possible order

Status: unsolved. Problem 3327 / OPG-172. Third newly documented substantive investigation, 13 October 2026; lifetime investigation count unknown. No history reset or new investigation budget is asserted.

The target is a finite ordinary simple graph G and h≥1 that does not embed in N_(h+1), while every proper minor embeds in N_h. Both edge deletion and contraction are required. E denotes Euler genus and ν minimum positive nonorientable genus.

## New partial results

For h=2 targets and ordinary Euler hoppers of Euler genus four:

- Every pentagon contains at most two three-square edges.
- Vertices incident with four three-square edges are independent, have only two-three-square-edge neighbors, and possess a forced pair of all-three-square-edge squares.
- Every component of the three-square-edge subgraph containing a degree-four vertex has a unique square decomposition. The remaining components containing edges are cycles. At least one such degree-two-only cycle has length at least six; their total length outside the four-cycles is a positive multiple of four, hence at least eight.
- Complete rooted labeled enumeration excludes orders at most 15. Every surviving target in these two classes has at least 16 vertices. All 37 square-rich graph certificates are retained: 31 square-disk embeddings and six bipartite exclusions.

The full proofs state their precise hypotheses. No target graph, universal nonexistence theorem, upper order bound, novelty claim, or claim about the complete current literature is supplied. Higher orders, higher genera, and the orientably simple Euler-critical route remain unresolved.
