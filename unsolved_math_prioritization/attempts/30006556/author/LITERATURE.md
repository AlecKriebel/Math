# Sources, attribution and bounded status check

Checked 2026-10-05 UTC. The original question remains unresolved in this packet.
No exhaustive or authoritative claim about all current literature is made.

1. János Pach, Erdős–Pach Question 1, Problem Session, in *Combinatorics*,
   Oberwolfach Report 1/2026, printed p.69 (PDF page 65).
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2026/1
   Official repository: https://publications.mfo.de/handle/mfo/4435
   The downloaded official page was read as text and visually inspected.
   It confirms n>4, unordered distance multiplicities, and the intended
   second distance besides the diameter. This is the governing primary source.

2. Felix Christian Clemen, Adrian Dumitrescu and Dingyuan Liu,
   *On multiplicities of interpoint distances*, Acta Mathematica Hungarica
   177 (2025), 231–245, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-025-01562-y .
   Latest observed preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04283v5 , revised
   3 February 2026; 11 pages. The version-of-record PDF was also retrieved
   from https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000188330/170672891 .
   Relevant locations: Conjecture 1.1, Theorems 1.2–1.3, Corollary 1.4,
   Proposition 1.5, and their Section 2 arguments. These establish the
   convex and specified hull-layer partial cases and an obstruction to
   using just the two extreme candidate distances. The 61-point example
   here is a concrete specialization of the credited construction idea,
   with independently supplied exact formulas and finite certification.
   The paper is not being claimed as a full solution of Conjecture 1.1.

3. Hopf and Pannwitz, Aufgabe 167, Jahresbericht der Deutschen
   Mathematiker-Vereinigung 43 (1934), 114. The diameter bound is used
   as a classical theorem, explicitly recorded in both primary sources
   above; the 1934 original was not separately retrieved or audited.

4. Rafik Zeraoulia, *Erdős–Pach rich-but-not-too-rich distances (Erdős
   problem #132): Verification for n=7 and a reduction for n=8*, working
   draft dated 28 January 2026, https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.26754.95684 .
   The author-uploaded manuscript listing and public forum account were
   inspected in indexed text. The note claims an n=7 result using a
   three-distance-set classification and gives the n=8 profile (1,9,9,9).
   It is not a published general resolution; its displayed n≥2 formulation
   also needs the original n≥5 restriction. Its classification input was
   not independently checked here and is not used in our circle theorem.
   Forum source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/132 .
   Direct live tracker/forum retrieval returned HTTP 403 on some attempts;
   indexed text is distinguished from a successful live-page inspection.

name and later revisions, Erdős 132, Erdős–Pach distance multiplicities,
and the all-but-one-cocircular special case. They found no full-resolution
