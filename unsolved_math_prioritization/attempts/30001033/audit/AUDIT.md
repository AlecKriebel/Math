## 2. Algebraic partials

**Abelianization.** An independent integer Heisenberg-matrix evaluation gives the class-two normal-form coordinates (0,0,6), (-4,4,0), and (4,8,26). The abelian relation lattice has determinantal divisors 4 and 48, yielding C4 x C12. No additional relator can be omitted when abelianizing: these are precisely the three presentation relators.

**First commutator factor.** In the class-two quotient, r1 gives c^6=1 and the commutator of r2 with x gives c^4=1, so c^2=1. A two-generator class-two derived subgroup is generated by c. Independently, sending x and y to the two reflections i -> -i and i -> 1-i of the four-cycle makes every relator trivial and their commutator nontrivial of order two. This confirms gamma_1/gamma_2 = C2 without relying on the polynomial representation.

**Exponent propagation.** The graded commutator pairing is bilinear and its images under bracketing with x,y generate the next lower-central factor. A finite elementary abelian degree-n factor therefore gives a finite elementary abelian degree-(n+1) factor. Starting with the first commutator factor proves exactly the positive-index assertion. The argument does not incorrectly include the abelianization.

**Indices 2 and 3.** Two weight-three commutators span at i=2. The graded Jacobi identity identifies the two mixed weight-four commutators because the remaining bracket is a multiple of [c,c]=0. Consequently the respective upper bounds are 2 and 3. The separately verified matrix lower bounds match, so the exact indices are 4 and 8. This does not provide an upper bound of 3 at all subsequent indices.

## 3. Polynomial representation and all-index lower bounds

The independent checker uses ordinary dense coefficient matrices over F2, Gaussian elimination for the constant inverses, and exact polynomial convolution. It does not import the package's controls module. Both left and right polynomial inverse identities hold for X and Y. Every presentation relator evaluates to the identity. Appending x to the first relator is correctly rejected.

This proves a homomorphism from the presented group to the polynomial matrix group; injectivity is neither supplied nor needed. The block-Toeplitz conversion respects multiplication. The constant blocks are upper unitriangular with identity 3-by-3 diagonal blocks, so the stated filtration containment is valid with the given indexing.

The first possible term of [U,V] is AB-BA. On periodic 3-by-3 diagonals this is precisely

u_r v_(r+i+1) + v_r u_(r+1)

when u lies in position i+1. Products add this leading coefficient, and conjugation leaves it unchanged. Since gamma_(i+1) is generated by all brackets of gamma_i with G, the recurrence computes the **exact leading-image subspace**, not merely a collection of witness vectors. An input with vanishing leading term contributes zero at this next degree and cannot create a missing visible vector.

I checked the recurrence independently with explicit sets of all vectors in each span, avoiding the package's row-reduction implementation. The spaces have ranks 1,2,3,3,2 at indices 1 through 5; the entire spaces at indices 2 and 5 agree, and their phases modulo 3 agree. The explicit transition table in PROOF.md agrees entry by entry. Determinism then supplies the induction for every index. This is a genuine finite-state certificate, not an extrapolation from repeated ranks.

The leading map annihilates gamma_(i+1), giving a surjection from the full lower-central factor onto the certified visible space. Thus the direction of the resulting bound is correctly **lower**, not upper.

## 4. Finite-image computations

The original controls output reproduces byte for byte, and all eight packaged tests pass. I also wrote a separate implementation using band-truncated dense unitriangular matrices. It independently enumerates the five generated finite groups and their lower-central sequences. It verifies normality of every computed term under both generators and both inverse generators.

The independently reproduced orders and consecutive indices are:

- cutoff 1: order 4; indices 4
- cutoff 2: order 32; indices 16,2
- cutoff 3: order 128; indices 16,2,4
- cutoff 4: order 1024; indices 16,2,4,8
- cutoff 5: order 8192; indices 16,2,4,8,8

The generation rule using all [k,x] and [k,y], k in the current normal subgroup K, is sound. If L is the subgroup these generate, then l^s=l[l,s] is in L for l in L and s=x,y. Normality under inverses follows by applying the same identity to l^(s^-1), which lies in K. Hence L is normal and K/L centralizes the generators, proving L=[K,G]. The independent explicit normality checks provide an additional implementation control.

These calculations concern images. They do not determine universal nilpotent quotients. In particular, cutoff 5 does not prove the full i=4 factor has rank 3. The package keeps this distinction clear.

## 5. Other routes and exact obstruction

The independent degree-four Magnus expansion agrees with all three printed initial forms. Their independence is immediate from the distinguishing monomials described in the proof. The direct Golod–Shafarevich polynomial has its minimum at 6^(-1/3), and that minimum is positive. The stated failure of the sufficient test, without stronger conclusions, is correct.

The pro-2 reduction is also valid. For positive i, G/gamma_(i+1) is finite nilpotent, its 3-part has order exactly 3, and it splits as a 2-group times C3. Its commutator subgroup lies in the 2-group factor, so projection detects every nonzero class in gamma_i/gamma_(i+1). This proves preservation by the full pro-2 completion. It supplies no identification with the special matrix congruence completion.

The exact sequence with K_i is valid: it is precisely the kernel of the leading-coefficient map after quotienting by gamma_(i+1). Since the factors are finite F2 spaces, the equality

rank(gamma_i/gamma_(i+1)) = d_i + dim(K_i)

is justified. K_2=K_3=0 follows from the low-weight upper bounds. The missing assertion is K_i=0 for every i>=4. Faithfulness of the group representation alone would not prove it, and none of the finite controls or recurrence does so. The package does not claim otherwise.
