# A partial analysis of the 14-triangle-complex finite-width conjecture

**Target:** 30001033 / OWR-2053-013, queue rank 610.  
**Disposition:** unsolved; five approaches, no complete candidate.  
**Date:** 2026-10-04 UTC.

## 1. Exact target, conventions, and what is proved

Let

\[
G=\langle x,y\mid r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle,
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=yxyxyxy^{-3}x^{-3},\\
r_2&=yx^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-3}y^2x^{-1}yxy,\\
r_3&=y^3x^{-1}yxyx^2y^2xyx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Use the zero-based **ordinary lower central series**
\[
\gamma_0=G,\qquad \gamma_{i+1}=[\gamma_i,G],
\qquad [u,v]=u^{-1}v^{-1}uv.
\]
The target is the exact formula
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}(\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1})=d_i,
\quad d_i=\begin{cases}2&i\equiv2\pmod3,\\3&i\equiv0,1\pmod3,\end{cases}
\qquad i\ge2. \tag{T}
\]
We establish that these quotients are finite elementary abelian 2-groups, so this dimension notation is justified. We prove the following partial conclusions:

1. \(G_{\rm ab}\cong C_4\times C_{12}\).
2. \(\gamma_1/\gamma_2\cong C_2\).
3. Every \(\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\), \(i\ge1\), is elementary abelian of finite rank.
4. Its rank is **at least** \(d_i\) for every \(i\ge2\).
5. Equality holds in (T) at \(i=2,3\).
6. A precisely defined extra kernel, not controlled here, measures the gap for all later indices.

These conclusions do not prove finite width. The periodic matrix mechanism is already present in Peyerimhoff–Vdovina; this is an independently checkable reconstruction and low-degree deduction, with no novelty claim.

### Source normalization

The Oberwolfach report, printed p. 2391, Conjecture 3, calls this the lower central series but prints the recurrence \([\gamma_i,\gamma_i]\). That recurrence defines the derived series, and is inconsistent with the named series and the cited commutator scheme. We explicitly normalize it to \([\gamma_i,G]\), retaining the report's \(\gamma_0=G\) indexing. The 2008 preprint also prints the analogous self-commutator typo for the lower exponent-2 series; the later 2013 paper, Remark 3.2, prints its usual recurrence correctly. We do not exploit either typo as a disproof of the intended problem.

The catalogue's “equivalently, finite width 3” is not used as a logical equivalence. A uniform rank bound of 3 does not, by itself, force the rank to equal 2 at the designated congruence classes. The exact pattern (T) is the target.

## 2. Integral collection

In the free class-two group on \(x,y\), put \(c=[y,x]\). Every element has the normal form \(x^a y^b c^e\), and multiplication is
\[
(a,b,e)(a',b',e')=(a+a',b+b',e+e'+ba'). \tag{1}
\]
Collecting the relators gives
\[
r_1=c^6,\qquad r_2=x^{-4}y^4,\qquad r_3=x^4y^8c^{26}. \tag{2}
\]
For example, each time an \(x^n\) is appended to a partial word with current \(y\)-exponent \(b\), add \(bn\) to the central exponent. This yields central exponents \(6,0,26\); `collect` in the control script implements exactly (1).

The exponent-sum lattice of the presentation is generated by \((-4,4)\) and \((4,8)\). Its matrix has determinant of absolute value 48 and greatest common divisor of entries 4. Its Smith factors are therefore 4 and 12. This proves conclusion 1.

Work now in \(G/\gamma_2\), which has class at most two. Equation (2) gives \(c^6=1\). Since \(r_2=1\), also \([r_2,x]=1\). Centrality of \(c\) and (2) give \([r_2,x]=c^4\). Thus \(c^2=1\). The commutator subgroup of a two-generator class-two group is generated by \(c\), so
\[
|\gamma_1/\gamma_2|\le2. \tag{3}
\]
The nonzero leading coefficient of the matrix commutator in §4 proves that \(c\notin\gamma_2\). Hence equality holds in (3).

One can also prove nontriviality without matrices by sending \(x,y\) to two generating reflections of the dihedral group of order eight. They are involutions and their product has order four. Direct substitution makes each relator the identity, while their commutator has order two.

## 3. Exponent propagation and the first two target cases

For this paragraph use conventional one-based notation \(L_n=\gamma_{n-1}\). The associated graded abelian groups \(L_n/L_{n+1}\) form a Lie ring under commutators. In particular the commutator induces a bilinear surjection from a sum of copies of
\[
(L_n/L_{n+1})\otimes (G/G')
\]
onto \(L_{n+1}/L_{n+2}\). Concretely, the latter is generated by bracketing generators of the former with \(x,y\). The elementary commutator identities modulo the next lower-central term prove this bilinearity; conjugation is trivial there.

We proved that \(L_2/L_3\) is cyclic of order two. Induction therefore proves that every \(L_n/L_{n+1}\), \(n\ge2\), is generated by finitely many elements of order dividing two. In zero-based notation, every \(\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\), \(i\ge1\), is a finite elementary abelian 2-group. The crude recursive rank bound is
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\le2^{i-1}. \tag{4}
\]

In weight three the generators are \([c,x]\), \([c,y]\). Thus the rank at \(i=2\) is at most two. In weight four the four apparent generators are
\[
[c,x,x],\ [c,x,y],\ [c,y,x],\ [c,y,y].
\]
The graded Jacobi identity identifies the two middle elements: their difference is a bracket of \(c\) with \([x,y]\), which is zero since these two degree-two elements differ only by sign. Consequently the rank at \(i=3\) is at most three. Section 4 gives matching lower bounds, proving
\[
[\gamma_2:\gamma_3]=4,\qquad [\gamma_3:\gamma_4]=8. \tag{5}
\]
No claim about the rank at \(i=4\) follows from this argument alone.

## 4. A finite certificate for all-index lower bounds

### 4.1 The representation

`matrices.json` specifies four explicit 9×9 matrices \(A_0,A_1,B_0,B_1\) over \(\mathbf F_2\), and `controls.py` contains the same data in binary row format. Put
\[
X=A_0+tA_1,\qquad Y=B_0+tB_1.
\]
Exact polynomial multiplication verifies that both are invertible over \(\mathbf F_2[t]\), with polynomial inverses of degree at most one, and that all three relators evaluate to the identity. This is a finite, exact certificate for a homomorphism
\[
\rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_9(\mathbf F_2[t]),
\quad x\mapsto X,\ y\mapsto Y. \tag{6}
\]
Faithfulness is **not assumed or needed** for any lower bound below. These are the source paper's matrices with \(t=1/y_{\rm source}\); the source's variable is unrelated to our generator \(y\).

Convert a polynomial matrix \(\sum C_jt^j\) into the infinite block Toeplitz matrix with \(C_j\) in block position \((r,r+j)\), then divide each 9×9 block into 3×3 blocks. The result is upper unitriangular and each block diagonal repeats with period three. Its \(d\)-th diagonal is a triple of 3×3 matrices. Let \(H_i\) mean that the first \(i\) such diagonals vanish. Multiplication of upper triangular matrices gives
\[
[H_i,H_j]\subseteq H_{i+j+1},\qquad
\rho(\gamma_i)\subseteq H_i. \tag{7}
\]
Here \(H_0\) is the whole upper-unitriangular group.

A 3×3 matrix \(M\) is encoded by
\[
\operatorname{enc}(M)=\sum_{r,c=0}^2 M_{rc}2^{3r+c}.
\]
The first diagonal of \(X\) is \(a=(416,416,416)\), and that of \(Y\) is \(b=(464,14,162)\) in this encoding.

### 4.2 Leading commutator formula

Suppose \(U\in H_i\) has first possible nonzero diagonal \(u=(u_0,u_1,u_2)\) in position \(i+1\). If \(V\) has first diagonal \(v\), multiplication and inversion, retaining only degree \(i+2\), give
\[
\operatorname{lead}_{i+2}[U,V]_r
=u_rv_{r+i+1}+v_ru_{r+1},\qquad r\pmod3. \tag{8}
\]
All operations on the right are 3×3 matrix operations over \(\mathbf F_2\). Terms of greater degree cannot affect this formula. Products in \(H_i\) add their leading diagonals, and conjugation by an element of \(H_0\) leaves that diagonal unchanged.

Let \(V_i\) be the leading-diagonal image of \(\gamma_i\) in position \(i+1\). The normal closure of \(c=[y,x]\) is \(\gamma_1\). Formula (8), product additivity, and conjugation invariance show that \(V_1\) is spanned by \((224,402,76)\). Since \(\gamma_{i+1}=[\gamma_i,G]\) and the first diagonal of each element of \(G\) lies in \(\langle a,b\rangle\), the exact recurrence is
\[
V_{i+1}=\operatorname{span}\{D_{i,a}(u),D_{i,b}(u):u\in V_i\}, \tag{9}
\]
where \(D\) is (8). This is equality: all brackets produce vectors in the indicated span, and every indicated vector comes from an actual bracket. Brackets with elements of larger filtration depth have zero leading coefficient at this degree and do not invalidate the recurrence.

The span of each row block is the next displayed space; the span after index 4 is precisely the index-2 space. The recurrence depends on \(i\) only modulo three. Thus equality of the entire subspace and phase at indices 2 and 5 proves by induction
\[
\dim V_i=d_i\qquad(i\ge2). \tag{10}
\]
This is not an inference from a long finite string of equal ranks: a complete state of the exact recurrence repeats. Row reduction over \(\mathbf F_2\) in `leading_orbit` checks the spaces as well as their dimensions.

By (7), \(\gamma_{i+1}\) has zero diagonal in position \(i+1\). Hence the leading map factors as a surjective homomorphism
\[
\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\twoheadrightarrow V_i. \tag{11}
\]
This proves all-index lower bounds and supplies the nontriviality needed in §2. It also completes the proof of (5).

### 4.4 The exact remaining gap

Put
\[
F_{i+1}=\{g\in G:\rho(g)\in H_{i+1}\},\qquad
K_i=(\gamma_i\cap F_{i+1})/\gamma_{i+1}.
\]
By (7), this is well-defined. Equation (11) yields the short exact sequence of finite vector spaces
\[
0\longrightarrow K_i\longrightarrow\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}
\longrightarrow V_i\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Therefore, for \(i\ge2\),
\[
\log_2[\gamma_i:\gamma_{i+1}]=d_i+\dim K_i. \tag{13}
\]
We proved \(K_2=K_3=0\). The target is equivalent to \(K_i=0\) for all remaining indices \(i\ge4\). Neither the leading-diagonal recurrence nor faithfulness of the entire matrix representation proves this subgroup equality.

## 5. Exhaustive finite-image controls

The control script enumerates the finite image \(Q_m\) obtained by retaining block diagonals 1 through \(m\), for \(1\le m\le5\). The multiplication law is
\[
(PQ)_j(r)=P_j(r)+Q_j(r)+\sum_{s=1}^{j-1}P_s(r)Q_{j-s}(r+s),
\]
with phase indices modulo three. Inversion follows by solving these equations successively. The routine enumerates the generated subgroup exactly and iteratively computes its lower central series using all commutators with \(x,y\). For a normal subgroup \(K\), these commutators generate \([K,G]\): the identity for \([k,uv]\) reduces words in generators, and conjugates are again products of such commutators.

The complete finite-image results are:

| m | order of Q_m | consecutive lower-central indices, starting at Q_m |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 4 | 4 |
| 2 | 32 | 16, 2 |
| 3 | 128 | 16, 2, 4 |
| 4 | 1024 | 16, 2, 4, 8 |
| 5 | 8192 | 16, 2, 4, 8, 8 |

All three relators are checked independently in every truncation. There is a hard maximum cutoff of five and an element cap of 65,536. There is no sampling in these enumerations. They are calculations in **images**, not calculations of the universal quotients \(G/\gamma_i\). Their finite range does not settle (T), including the kernel at \(i=4\). The abelianization of a sufficiently deep 2-group image has order 16, while the full abelianization has order 48; this alone illustrates the need to track the map's kernel.

## 6. Magnus and Golod–Shafarevich attempt

In the noncommutative power-series ring \(\mathbf F_2\langle\!\langle U,V\rangle\!\rangle\), substitute \(x=1+U,y=1+V\), expanding inverses geometrically. Every relator has initial degree four. The initial forms, using words in U,V, are the following sets of monomials with coefficient one:

- r1: UUUV, UUVU, UUVV, UVUU, UVUV, UVVU, UVVV, VUUU, VUUV, VUVU, VUVV, VVUU, VVUV, VVVU
- r2: UUUU, UVVV, VUVV, VVUV, VVVU, VVVV
- r3: UUUU, UUVV, UVUV, UVVU, VUUV, VUVU, VVUU

`magnus` computes exactly through degree eight; higher terms cannot alter the certified first nonzero homogeneous part. These three initial forms are linearly independent: r1 has a UUUV term absent from the others; r2 has VVVV absent from the others; r3 is nonzero.

The direct Golod–Shafarevich test polynomial from this presentation is
\[
f(t)=1-2t+3t^4.
\]
It is positive on \([0,1]\). Indeed its sole interior critical point is \(t_0=6^{-1/3}\), where \(f(t_0)=1-3t_0/2>0\), since \(t_0<2/3\); the endpoints are also positive. Thus the usual negative-polynomial criterion supplies no certificate here. Failure of this sufficient test does not show finiteness, finite width, or failure of any stronger weighted test.

Nor does the list of three initial forms describe all relations in the associated graded algebra: consequences and overlaps may produce additional initial forms. The dimension filtration underlying Magnus expansions also differs from the ordinary lower central series. A complete overlap calculation and a justified filtration comparison would be needed to turn this route into an upper bound. No such claim is made.

## 7. Pro-2 completion: a useful reduction, with a remaining gap

There is no loss of the lower-central ranks \(\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\), \(i\ge1\), on passing to **all** finite 2-group quotients. To see this, \(N=G/\gamma_{i+1}\) is a finite nilpotent group: it has a finite abelianization of order 48 and finitely many elementary-abelian 2-group lower-central factors by §3. Its order has 3-part exactly three. Finite nilpotent groups are direct products of their Sylow subgroups, so
\[
N=S\times C_3
\]
with \(S\) a finite 2-group. Its commutator subgroup is contained in \(S\). Projection to \(S\) therefore embeds the particular subgroup \(\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\), while killing \(\gamma_{i+1}\).

Equivalently, if \(P\) is the pro-2 completion of G and \(C_i\) is the closure of \(\gamma_i\) in P, then the natural map
\[
\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}\longrightarrow C_i/C_{i+1}
\]
is an isomorphism for \(i\ge1\). Its image is dense and finite, hence closed and surjective; the finite 2-group projection just constructed proves injectivity. This is a rigorous reduction to the full pro-2 completion.

The matrix congruence completion, however, uses only the particular quotients \(Q_m\). No proof is supplied that this family detects all the required lower-central cosets, or that the lower-central subgroups inside that completion have the claimed congruence depth. Identifying the full pro-2 completion with a matrix closure would itself require justification; even that identification would still need the filtration comparison in (12).

Residual separation of elements is weaker than separation of cosets of a subgroup. As a simple warning, \(3\mathbf Z\subsetneq\mathbf Z\) maps onto every \(\mathbf Z/2^m\mathbf Z\), although the intersection of all \(2^m\mathbf Z\) is zero. This example does not assert that G has such a defect; it shows why an abstract density argument cannot replace the missing target-specific comparison.

## 8. Conclusion

The finite periodic **visible image** is proved, and the first two requested indices are proved exactly. Every factor under study is a finite elementary abelian 2-group. The missing statement is the uniform vanishing of the extra kernels \(K_i\) in (12) for all \(i\ge4\). No proof or nonzero witness was obtained. The correct outcome remains **unsolved, 5/5**.
