## 2. Turn 1: strong countable tail fusion

The preprocessing Q=⋃_i s_i⁻¹(P_i) is computable on positive open names. For a named cylinder of P_i, enumerating all legal i-entry prefixes gives exactly its inverse image under the shift. Degenerate cylinders are handled correctly; an empty cylinder cannot occur on a valid promised input if it would make that input the entire space.

The domain proof is valid. Starting below an arbitrary infinite X, apply open Ramsey separately to each P_i and repeatedly thin the preceding reservoir above the chosen point. The promise excludes landing solutions at every stage. Nested reservoirs ensure that the i-th tail of every subsequence of the diagonal h avoids P_i. Hence h avoids Q, and because this construction exists below every infinite X, no X can land homogeneously in Q. This is a classical existence argument for validity, not a computable step of the reduction.

For an arbitrary allowed output h, prepend h's first i entries to any subsequence of its i-th tail. The result is still a subsequence of h, proving that every decoded tail solves its original instance. The decoder uses h only, so the asserted reduction is genuinely strong. The reverse constant-sequence reduction is strong as well.

The unshifted countable union counterexample is correct. Countable parallelization has not been equated with arbitrary sequential composition.
