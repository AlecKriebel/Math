## Scoped results retained

1. **Countable tail fusion.** Promised avoidance A is strongly equivalent to its countable parallelization. The i-th open constraint is pulled back under the i-fold shift, and any answer yields the required sequence of tails. The ordinary countable union need not preserve the promise.
2. **Indexed adaptive-call absorption.** An adaptive protocol whose instances come from a uniformly listed, all-valid countable menu reduces to one A-call, even if it makes unboundedly many discrete requests while producing its output. Arbitrary continuous formation of an entire new open name need not have countable range; an explicit easy clopen family demonstrates that logical distinction without being a hard-instance counterexample.
3. **Finite-global-arity obstruction.** Both full Ramsey and promised avoidance restricted to finite global arity fail to compute Baire choice, even with no supplied arity bound or negative name. A computable instance has an arithmetical homogeneous solution.
4. **First-value lookahead obstruction.** The same conclusion holds for a computable, possibly unbounded lookahead depending on the first value, with its program index withheld. If an index is supplied, a homogeneous solution is uniformly computable from the omega jump plus two jumps. This is a hyperarithmetical basis argument, not a computable selector.
5. **Semantic-clopen classification.** With only a positive open name supplied, the full-Ramsey and promised-avoidance restrictions to topologically clopen sets are both ordinary Weihrauch equivalent to the known UC_{N^N} degree. Recovering the missing complement name has exactly the degree of one Turing jump; the credited unique-choice absorption theorem then supplies the upper bound. Consequently a successful full Baire-choice reduction must use genuinely nonclopen open instances on a hard computable input.

The fifth result subsumes the qualitative obstructions in turns 3–4. Their explicit finite and omega-jump bounds under the stated extra hypotheses remain finer information. All historical files are retained unchanged.

## Sharp gap and interpretation

No proof constructs either unrestricted source reduction or separates C_{N^N} from either unrestricted target. The remaining cases include genuinely nonclopen open sets and arbitrary dependence of a later instance on an entire earlier infinite answer. Countable parallelization is not sequential-composition closure. A failed coding strategy, uncountable instance family, or strong nonreduction is not an ordinary-reduction counterexample.
