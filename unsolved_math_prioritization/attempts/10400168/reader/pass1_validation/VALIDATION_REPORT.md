They do not provide a computational proof of the general defect-TQFT theorem, decide historical source coverage, or introduce an equivalence/injectivity requirement absent from the adopted target.

The examples use the specified full inclusion `Vec_C3 → Vec_S3` with trivial associators and standard spherical structures. The cyclic subgroup is generated by the permutation `(0 1 2)`; the ambient symmetric group is generated by that cycle and `(0 1)`. The script constructs these groups from generators, checks their group laws, and does not import multiplication tables, datasets, another lane's implementation, or external Python packages.

## 1. Closed-manifold normalization: a direct derivation

For untwisted `Vec_G` with standard spherical structure, every simple has dimension 1, `Dim(Vec_G)=|G|`, and the tetrahedral weight of every admissible coloring is 1. A face is admissible precisely when its oriented edge labels satisfy `g_ij g_jk = g_ik`.

For a connected triangulation with `v` vertices, choose a root and a spanning tree. Gauge transformations equal to the identity at the root act freely on flat edge assignments, and uniquely trivialize the tree edges. The remaining assignments are based homomorphisms `π₁(M) → G`. There are therefore

`|G|^(v−1) · |Hom(π₁(M),G)|`

flat assignments. Multiplying by the theory's own vertex factor gives

`TV_Vec_G(M) = |Hom(π₁(M),G)| / |G|`.

This is the cardinality of the representation action groupoid:

`Σ_[ρ] 1 / |C_G(im ρ)|`.

It is not generally the number of conjugacy classes. Indeed each orbit contributes its size divided by `|G|`, which equals the reciprocal stabilizer order. The script enumerates the orbit sets and stabilizers separately and checks this equality exactly.

The historical displayed state sum uses its own `w^(-v)` with `w=Σ μ_i²`. Its next-page description of the untwisted group case as an unweighted conjugacy-class count disagrees with this normalization, already on `S³`. We retain the displayed normalized construction, recording the discrepancy rather than replacing one count with the other. [Ohtsuki, printed pp. 509–510, equation (48) and the following group example](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf#page=137).

The resulting sphere and `S²×S¹` values agree with the explicit computations in [Turaev–Virelizier, §6.3, printed p. 16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.3501v5#page=16).

## 2. A triangulation-level check of the vertex factor

The script generates the boundary of the 4-simplex as the five four-element subsets of `{0,1,2,3,4}`. This is a triangulated `S³` with five vertices. It then subdivides one tetrahedron by a new interior vertex, performing a single 1–4 move and producing a six-vertex triangulation of the same manifold.

For **each** triangulation, two algorithms count the flat assignments:

1. Assign edges by exhaustive backtracking, pruning only when a completed triangular face violates its flatness equation.
2. Generate all vertex potentials with the root potential fixed to the identity, form `g_ij = h_i^(-1)h_j`, check every face equation, and check that all resulting edge assignments are distinct.

The counts agree exactly:

| Group | Five-vertex flat assignments | Six-vertex flat assignments | Correct value on both |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trivial | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| C2 | 16 | 32 | 1/2 |
| C3 | 81 | 243 | 1/3 |
| S3 | 1296 | 7776 | 1/6 |

A decisive negative test retains only the C3 labels but incorrectly uses the ambient S3 vertex factor. It produces

`81/6^5 = 1/96`, but `243/6^6 = 1/192`.

It is not invariant under this one move. In general this wrong partial sum differs from the correctly normalized subcategory invariant by `(|H|/|G|)^v`. Each bulk must use its own global dimension. This check concerns closed-bulk normalization; it does not replace the separate boundary-conjugation analysis in the interface/theorem lanes.

## 3. Torus state spaces: projector images, not groupoid masses

For the untwisted pointed theory, the torus flat-label space in a one-vertex presentation has a basis indexed by commuting pairs `(a,b)`. A flat cylinder has a temporal holonomy `h`; changing it acts by simultaneous conjugation. The normalized cylinder is therefore the gauge-average projector

`P = (1/|G|) Σ_h U_h`,

where `U_h(a,b)=(hah^(-1),hbh^(-1))`. Its image consists of functions, or orbit-sum vectors, constant on each simultaneous-conjugacy orbit. The script constructs the whole rational matrix, checks `P²=P` entry by entry, and computes its rank by exact Gaussian elimination.

| Group | Flat-pair pre-space dimension | Cylinder image rank | Pair-groupoid cardinality |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| C3 | 9 | 9 | 3 |
| S3 | 18 | 8 | 3 |

The surface groupoid cardinality is **not** the dimension of the surface state space. In particular its value `3` in both rows cannot be substituted for `9` or `8`.

Three checks independently agree with the projector rank:

- Explicit simultaneous-conjugacy orbit enumeration gives 9 and 8.
- Burnside's sum of fixed commuting pairs divided by `|G|` gives 9 and 8.
- Pairwise commuting triples number 27 and 48. Thus `Z(T²×S¹)=27/3=9` or `48/6=8`, as required by closing the identity cylinder and taking its linear trace.

The centralizer count also matches. In C3 there are three conjugacy classes, each with three centralizer conjugacy classes, giving `3+3+3=9`. In S3 the identity, transposition, and three-cycle classes have centralizers of orders 6, 2, and 3, with 3, 2, and 3 conjugacy classes, giving `3+2+3=8`. Over the complex numbers these equal their numbers of irreducible representations. The center interpretation is a cross-check; neither it nor separate bulk-center equivalences is being used to invent an inclusion map between centers.

### Precisely justified counterchecks

1. The two ordinary normalized TQFTs are not isomorphic: their torus dimensions and their sphere partition functions differ.
2. Every linear map from the C3 torus space to the S3 torus space has a nonzero kernel because `9>8`. This includes any wall-cylinder map in that direction. No particular matrix or exact rank of the CM wall map is asserted here.
3. A monoidal natural transformation between the two ordinary normalized theories, in either direction, would have an invertible component at the empty surface. Naturality for the closed bordism `S³: ∅→∅` would force `1/3=1/6`. Thus no such transformation exists for this pair.
4. None of these counterchecks obstructs an inclusion-induced topological interface in a common decorated-bordism theory. In particular, wall sliding through a general topology-changing ordinary bordism has not been assumed.

## 4. Algebraic verification of the inherited trace

Let `i:D→C` be the declared full spherical inclusion, and let `M=C` carry the left action through `i` and the regular right action. For an endomorphism of `m≅⊕_s s⊗V_s`, define

`t_m(f)=Σ_s d_s Tr(f_s)`.

Every simple spherical dimension `d_s` is nonzero: the unit occurs once in `s*⊗s`, and the nonzero evaluation and opposite coevaluation respectively project from and include into that single simple summand. Their composite is nonzero. Consequently `t` gives a nondegenerate pairing on all Hom spaces, including rectangular blocks: pair a nonzero matrix entry with its opposite matrix unit. Rectangular matrix-trace cyclicity gives `t_m(gf)=t_n(fg)`.

For `F∈End(i(d)⊗m)`, expand the left categorical trace using the tensor-product duality. Closing the `i(d)` strand first gives precisely

`t_(i(d)⊗m)(F) = t_m(ptr^L_d(F))`.

This is the partial trace from D because every evaluation, opposite coevaluation, pivotal map, and coherence map for `d` is inherited. For `F∈End(m⊗c)`, the analogous expansion of the right trace gives

`t_(m⊗c)(F) = t_m(ptr^R_c(F))`.

Sphericity identifies these left and right total traces with the **same** `t`. The two outer closures in `i(d)⊗m⊗c` commute by interchange and monoidal coherence, giving the simultaneous two-sided identity. All statements concern arbitrary endomorphisms, not only dimensions of identity maps. The normalization is fixed by `t_1(id)=1`; the right-regular trace identity with `m=1` forces any other normalized right-compatible trace to equal this one.

The trace conventions used here agree with [Turaev–Virelizier, §§1.1–1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.3501v5#page=3). The argument itself is an explicit algebraic restriction check, rather than a claim that arbitrary spherical module categories admit compatible traces.

### Exact matrix-unit tests

In `Vec_G`, a pivotal character `χ:G→{+1,−1}` makes the simple traces equal to `χ(g)`. For a graded object with homogeneous basis, the trace is the sum of the diagonal coefficients weighted by these signs. Left and right partial traces of matrix units contract matching outer basis indices and weight them by the corresponding sign.

The script verifies the character identities, then tests every grade-preserving matrix-unit endomorphism of the stated test objects. By linearity, these tests cover all endomorphisms of those particular objects; the preceding argument handles arbitrary objects and spherical categories.

| C3⊂S3, standard | 134 | 134 | 2094 | 1982 | 121 |

The signed example has a **non-positive chosen spherical structure**. Its underlying fusion category is still pseudo-unitary; this example must not be described as a non-pseudo-unitary fusion category. It demonstrates that positivity of the selected simple traces is unnecessary for these axioms. The finite-group partition-function calculations above use the standard spherical structure, not this signed structure.

For `1⊕g` in sign-pivotal `Vec_C2`, the dimension is zero, but the trace pairing on its endomorphism algebra has matrix `diag(1,−1)` and exact rank 2. A zero dimension for a nonsimple object therefore does not imply a degenerate Calabi–Yau trace.

## 5. Incompatible pivotal structures: a genuine excluded case

Give D and C the same tensor category `Vec_C2`, use the identity tensor inclusion, and put the sign spherical structure on D and the standard one on C. Let `t_1,t_g` be the values of any candidate trace on the two simple identities. The two module axioms force

`t_g=−t_1` from the left action, and `t_g=t_1` from the right action.

The constraint matrix on `(t_1,t_g)` is `[[1,1],[-1,1]]`; exact rational elimination gives rank 2. Only the zero trace is possible, so no nondegenerate shared trace exists. As a control, giving both sides the sign structure yields rank 1 and the normalized solution `(1,−1)`.

This is outside the declared inherited-spherical hypothesis. It validates the necessity of pivotal compatibility and does not contradict the accepted interface construction.
