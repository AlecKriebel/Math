# Scholarly and version notes

This reader distinguishes the accepted mathematical claim, historical construction credit, and bounded bibliographic inspection. Its date is 14 October 2026 UTC.

## Source question

Michael Frank, “Approximation of Frames by Normalized Tight Ones,” in *Mini-Workshop: Wavelets and Frames*, Oberwolfach Reports 1 (2004), report 10, question at printed p.531. The inverse-frame convention S=(θ*θ)^−1 makes the questioned S^(1/2) family the usual F^(−1/2) canonical tightening. [Published report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45879), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2004/10).

The semi-orthogonal positive case is credited to Deguang Han, *Approximations for Gabor and wavelet frames*, Trans. AMS 355 (2003), 3329–3342, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-03-03047-2). Han's full text was not recovered during the source inspection. The present proof does not need an uninspected theorem from that paper.

## Classical family and mechanism

- Ingrid Daubechies and Bin Han, *The canonical dual frame of a wavelet frame*, ACHA 12 (2002), 269–285, p.271 equation (1.6): the identical perturbation family η+ε√2η(2·), with 0<ε<1 and η an orthonormal wavelet. [Published author-hosted PDF](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~ingrid/publications/ACHA_12_2002.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/acha.2002.0381).
- Ingrid Daubechies, *The wavelet transform, time-frequency localization and signal analysis*, IEEE Trans. Information Theory 36 (1990), 961–1005, pp.988–989: construction attributed to P.-G. Lemarié, communicated by Y. Meyer; partial-isometry/even-odd-adjoint mechanism in canonical-dual analysis. [Published author-hosted PDF](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~ingrid/publications/ieee36-1990.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.57199).
- Charles K. Chui and Xianliang Shi, *Inequalities of Littlewood–Paley Type for Frames and Wavelets*, SIAM J. Math. Anal. 24 (1993), 263–277. Credited through the explicit 2002 attribution; its full text was not inspected. [Publisher/DOI](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0524017).

These are prior construction and canonical-dual sources. The direct canonical-tight calculation in this reader is independently proved and audited. No priority claim is made.

## Precise scope and related published results

- Marcin Bownik, John Jasper and Darrin Speegle, *Orthonormal dilations of non-tight frames*, Proc. AMS 139 (2011), 3247–3256. Theorem 4.1, equation (4.3), and Remark 4.1 on p.3255 formulate canonical-Parseval preservation through the indexed local commutant. [Published PDF](https://pages.uoregon.edu/mbownik/papers/44.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-2011-10887-6), [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3552v1). The inspected arXiv listing has v1 dated 20 April 2010 and the 2011 journal reference. Its abstract dilation convention must be aligned with the inverse physical dilation.
- Marcin Bownik and Jakob Lemvig, *The canonical and alternate duals of a wavelet frame*, ACHA 23 (2007), 263–272. [Published PDF](https://pages.uoregon.edu/mbownik/papers/29.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2007.04.004). The paper treats F^−1 and corrects a later canonical-dual argument in the 2002 predecessor. Its corrected theorem underlies only the optional bridge application, not the explicit Haar proof. The correction is not a reason to discard the directly observed 2002 construction formula.
- Marcin Bownik and Jakob Lemvig, *Affine and quasi-affine frames for rational dilations*, Trans. AMS 363 (2011), 1887–1924. [Published PDF](https://pages.uoregon.edu/mbownik/papers/42.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2010-05200-6). The affine/quasi-affine and dual distinctions do not identify the corresponding canonical tightenings.

A peripheral source-recovery item, *Wavelet frames: Spectral techniques and extension principles*, was inspected as [arXiv:1901.08024v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08024v1), submitted 23 January 2019. Its journal status was not verified. It is not a dependency of the accepted counterexample.
