## Hypotheses that do real work

The theorem assumes all of the following, rather than deriving them from a potential or the AIM question:

1. A common invariant probability that is ergodic for the distinguished expanding map f.
2. Uniform contraction of intrinsic leaf distance under every backward iterate of f.
3. A nonzero whole-leaf Radon family, measured in intrinsic leaf topology.
4. Exact whole-leaf Gibbs rebasing on the given domain D.
5. Measurable section-indexed finite plaque kernels and their image kernels, with the required conditional measure classes.
6. The exact whole-leaf f transport law and whole-leaf g absolute continuity.
7. The scalar square and regularity giving the normalized g rebasing identity.

These hypotheses suffice for the argument. They are substantially stronger than bare projective leafwise measures. In particular, neither the measurable-kernel assumption nor the existence of the normalized family can be omitted by appeal to ergodicity. Ordinary second-countable foliation leaves are also being used when counting overlap components and exhausting a whole leaf. The proof should not be read as a theorem about arbitrary measurable equivalence relations lacking that structure.

## Normalization and density construction

### Algebra and signs

The telescoping identity has the correct sign and endpoint. Commutation moves g past each backward iterate; the scalar square then changes each summand into a difference of successive b-values. The remaining endpoint difference tends to zero because ambient distance is bounded by intrinsic distance, backward intrinsic distance contracts, and b is uniformly continuous on compact M.

Consequently c(gx,gy)/c(x,y) is exp(b(x)-b(y)). With sigma_x=exp(-b(x))g_*nu_x and tau_x=nu_(gx), both measures rebase by the same factor c(gx,gy). The common f covariance coefficient is exp(a(gx)), not exp(a(x)) alone. These calculations were rederived independently and also checked in finite rational models.

### Joint measurability

For a fixed plaque coordinate space, take a countable generating algebra and the finite partitions generated by its first n members. The atom ratio Sigma_t(A)/T_t(A), with a fixed value on zero-denominator atoms, is jointly measurable in the plaque parameter and coordinate. For each good parameter it is a conditional expectation of the ordinary Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to the finite partition. Martingale convergence therefore identifies its limit with that derivative almost everywhere for the denominator measure. The simultaneous scaling needed to make both kernels uniformly bounded must use the same divisor, for example 1+Sigma_t(total)+T_t(total).

This agrees with the finite-kernel statement in the primary [Mathlib kernel Radon–Nikodym documentation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/RadonNikodym.html). The source requires a countably generated target or a countable parameter space; ordinary plaque coordinates give the former. No Lean formalization is claimed.

The parameter may be completed-measurable: a parameter-null set lifts to a null set under the disintegrated measure. Conditional-class equivalence transfers the denominator almost-everywhere statement to the ambient probability in the target box. Positivity and finiteness hold in that class, and representatives can be fixed off the remaining null set.

### Why this avoids the diagonal fallacy

Joint measurability alone would not justify taking a derivative indexed by x and evaluating it at x. For example, on a nonatomic interval, h_x(z)=1+1_{z=x} is jointly Borel and is a valid derivative of Lebesgue measure with respect to itself for every fixed x. Nevertheless h_x(x)=2 everywhere, while the correct density is 1 almost everywhere.

The reconstructed proof does not use that operation. It first chooses one measure pair per plaque and constructs its derivative as a function of the actual plaque coordinate. The common rebasing factor makes different choices of the basepoint yield the same derivative class.

### Gluing and transport

On an overlap, the two selected basepoints lie on the same whole leaf, so the two numerator/denominator pairs are related by one common positive scalar. Radon–Nikodym uniqueness gives agreement on each common plaque piece. Within a second-countable leaf, only countably many local overlap pieces are needed. Bad plaques from the other chart form an ambient null set; disintegrating in the first chart removes them on almost every first plaque. Countable chart and refinement coverage then gives an ambient almost-everywhere equality.

The same argument applies to f-image charts. Exact covariance of both measures identifies the derivative on an image plaque with the original derivative composed with f inverse. Invariance of mu and the stipulated conditional classes make q(fz)=q(z) an ambient almost-everywhere identity. Ergodicity therefore makes q a positive finite constant.

This step yields equality of the two measures on almost every good local plaque. It does not yet yield equality on every whole leaf, and it does not presume a leaf-saturated conull set.

## Local equality to whole-leaf equality

The backward recurrence argument is valid. Its essential steps are:

1. Local plaque equality supplies a positive intrinsic radius for almost every basepoint.
2. A countable radius decomposition supplies one positive-measure set with a common radius delta.
3. Birkhoff's theorem for f inverse supplies infinitely many backward visits to that set for almost every basepoint.
4. Exact covariance transports the local zero defect forward from each visit.
5. For any fixed intrinsic compact Q, the bound C theta^n max_{z in Q}d_W(z,gx)<delta holds at all sufficiently late visits. Thus the transported zero regions eventually contain Q.
6. A countable intrinsic compact exhaustion or relatively compact open cover yields equality of the two positive measures on the whole leaf.

No nesting of the zero regions is needed. The proof only uses local differences of Radon measures, so it does not subtract two infinite total masses. Commutation ensures that the backward center is g f^(-n)x. The resulting full-measure recurrence set can be taken Borel; intersecting all integer f,g translates gives an invariant Borel conull subset of D.

### Clarification recommended for the radius sets

At line 115, a whole intrinsic ball need not itself be one chart plaque. Read the countable-generator test as a chart-local restriction/exhaustion test, using the measurability assumptions in Section 2. For ordinary open foliation boxes there is an especially simple equivalent route: choose a good target box V, restrict to points whose ambient distance from M minus V exceeds a fixed positive delta, and use only its good plaques. A leafwise path of length less than delta remains in V and the same plaque, so the intrinsic delta-ball lies in the local zero-defect region. The countable box and radius cover gives a positive-measure such set. This proves exactly the recurrence input without testing one potentially large ball as though it were a single plaque.

This is a useful proof expansion, not an additional dynamical assumption. It also makes clear that an arbitrary ambient null set was never declared null on every leaf. Only disintegration on good plaques and later recurrence were used.

## Exceptional toral leaves

The counterexample is valid, with the standard product flat metric on T2 times T2 understood.

- The matrices are integral, invertible over the integers, commuting, hyperbolic, and Haar-preserving.
- No positive power of diag(B,B) has eigenvalue one. Thus every nonzero integer Fourier frequency has an infinite orbit. The invariant Fourier coefficients of an L2 function vanish on those orbits by square summability, proving Haar ergodicity of f.
- The first expanding line is one coarse Lyapunov leaf direction: the four action weights are (m+n), -(m+n), (m-n), and -(m-n), times log(lambda), and only the first lies on its positive ray.
- For a nonzero integer k, B^k-I is an invertible integer matrix. Equality of the second coordinates of two orbit leaves would therefore make sqrt(3) times a nonzero integer vector integral, which is impossible.
- After the second-coordinate labels agree, equality of first-factor leaves would give sqrt(2) ell(w)=ell(z), where w is a nonzero integer vector and ell has coefficients in Q(sqrt(5)) and annihilates the expanding line. Irrational slope implies ell(w) is nonzero; hence sqrt(2) would belong to Q(sqrt(5)), a contradiction. This proves distinctness for all integer labels, not just those sampled by the finite controls.
- Each leaf is the injective continuous image of R and hence Borel by Lusin–Souslin. Its countable smooth-arc exhaustion has four-dimensional Haar measure zero. Their countable union is an invariant Borel Haar-null set.
- The label r is Borel and constant along each entire leaf, with r composed with f equal to r and r(gx)-r(x)=1 on the exceptional union. Thus exp(r) times intrinsic arclength is measurable, locally finite on intrinsic leaves, and in the Haar conditional class locally.
- Pushforward of arclength by a length expansion lambda has density lambda inverse. The normalized g factor is exp(-1) on the exceptional union and 1 elsewhere; hence a single constant fails on D=M and works on its invariant conull complement.

The algebraic point involving sqrt(2) and sqrt(3) is proved to have distinct orbit leaves. It is not proved to be Haar-generic, and no such genericity is needed: the example explicitly uses D=M. This review does not infer equidistribution or genericity for that point.

The normal release verifier passes all nine payload file hashes and byte counts and replays all 4,177 original finite assertions. The archive has exactly the eleven intended members, and every member equals the frozen release file byte-for-byte.
