# Conull compatibility of an existing Gibbs leaf measure family

Reconstructed research note, 5 October 2026. Problem ID 20001730, rank 673, AIM-GEOMETRY-0068. Status: conditional partial result; independent review pending. The original construction problem remains unsolved. No novelty or historical-priority claim is made.

## 1. Scope and provenance

Kurt Vinhage's Problem 5.1 in the AIM list *Equilibrium states for dynamical systems arising from geometry* asks for a construction of conditional measures along coarse Lyapunov leaves, with suitable cocycle laws, for commuting hyperbolic diffeomorphisms and a given potential. Source: http://aimpl.org/equibdynsysgeom/5/ . Its displayed status field was empty when retrieved on 5 October 2026.

This note assumes that a common invariant probability and an appropriate measurable whole-leaf family already exist. It therefore does not solve that construction problem. Its purpose is to correct a measure-theoretic quantifier: ergodicity gives compatibility after restriction to a possibly smaller invariant conull set of basepoints. It does not give the same compatibility at every basepoint in an arbitrarily prescribed original conull set.

The former release and audit bytes were unavailable in the current workspace and were not recovered from the targeted repository searches. This is a new reconstruction with explicit hypotheses and a new hash, not a byte-identical recovery or an exact diff against the missing release. Section 5 proves a counterexample to retaining the original domain. Section 6 lists what is not established.

## 2. Explicit hypotheses

Let M be a compact smooth manifold. Let f and g be commuting C1 diffeomorphisms preserving a foliation W with connected leaves and the leafwise Riemannian metric induced by a fixed ambient Riemannian metric. Assume the foliation admits a countable collection of measurable foliation boxes with relatively compact plaques; their smaller interiors cover M. Leaf distances and plaque integrals used below are measurable. All measures on a whole leaf are Radon for the intrinsic leaf topology, not necessarily Radon after pushforward to M.

Assume that f inverse contracts intrinsic distances uniformly: constants C >= 1 and 0 < theta < 1 satisfy

    d_W(f^(-n)y, f^(-n)z) <= C theta^n d_W(y,z)

for every n >= 0 and every y,z on the same leaf. This follows, for example, from uniform contraction of the leafwise derivative of f inverse by applying the derivative bound to leafwise paths.

Let mu be a Borel probability, invariant under both f and g, and ergodic under f. Let D be an f- and g-invariant Borel set with mu(D)=1.

Let a,b:M -> R be Holder continuous functions satisfying, everywhere,

    a(gx)-a(x) = b(fx)-b(x).                                      (2.1)

The stronger global regularity and explicit rebasing below are hypotheses of this reconstructed result. They are not asserted to be present verbatim in the unavailable former release. Define, for points x,y on the same leaf,

    c(x,y) = exp(sum over k>=1 of [a(f^(-k)x)-a(f^(-k)y)]).         (2.2)

The sum converges absolutely: Holder continuity and backward contraction give a geometric majorant for each pair. It is continuous in leafwise local coordinates and measurable on the leaf relation. We assume the usual measurable foliation structure needed for that last statement; a uniformly expanding foliation has its global leaf relation generated by countably many backward images of local plaque relations.

For every x in D let nu_x be a nonzero positive whole-leaf Radon measure on W(x). Assume:

1. **Measurable kernels.** In foliation coordinates, evaluations of nu_x on relatively compact plaque sets are measurable in the parameters. A completed-measurable section through D may be chosen on almost every plaque of each disintegration. The resulting section-indexed restrictions and their g-images are measurable measure kernels. Ordinary Borel foliation charts, Borel kernels, and measurable selection on the completed quotient probability space suffice. This measurability requirement is explicit, rather than an unstated diagonal choice of derivatives.
2. **Whole-leaf Gibbs rebasing.** If x,y belong to D and lie on the same whole leaf, then

       nu_y = c(x,y) nu_x.                                       (2.3)

3. **Conditional classes.** In each box, for almost every plaque in the disintegration of mu restricted to the box, and for almost every basepoint x of that plaque, the plaque restriction of nu_x is equivalent to the conditional probability. By (2.3), equivalence then holds for every chosen basepoint in that plaque intersected with D. In particular the selected measure on such a plaque is finite, nonzero, and has the same null sets as the conditional probability. The same requirements apply to the countable refinements and f- or g-images of boxes used in the proof; alternatively they follow from local uniqueness of disintegration and mu-invariance.
4. **Exact f law on D.** As whole-leaf measures,

       f_*nu_x = exp(a(x)) nu_(fx).                               (2.4)

5. **g equivalence on D.** The whole-leaf measures g_*nu_x and nu_(gx) are mutually absolutely continuous.

One may replace (2.2), Holder regularity, and (2.1) in the theorem by an already specified measurable positive rebasing function c that satisfies (2.3), the f law (2.4), the scalar square (2.1), and the identity (3.1) below. Mere projective rebasing without (3.1) is insufficient for the plaque-independent density construction.

## 3. Corrected theorem and proof

**Theorem.** Under Section 2 there are a constant K_g in (0,infinity) and an f- and g-invariant Borel conull set D' contained in D such that, for every x in D',

    g_*nu_x = K_g exp(b(x)) nu_(gx)                              (3.0)

as measures on the entire leaf W(gx). No conclusion is asserted for basepoints in D minus D'.

### 3.1 The normalized rebasing identity

Telescoping (2.1) gives

    log c(gx,gy)-log c(x,y)
       = b(x)-b(y) - lim as N->infinity
                           [b(f^(-N)x)-b(f^(-N)y)]
       = b(x)-b(y).

The last limit is zero by continuity on compact M and backward contraction. Thus

    c(gx,gy) = exp(b(x)-b(y)) c(x,y).                            (3.1)

Write sigma_x = exp(-b(x)) g_*nu_x and tau_x = nu_(gx), both on W(gx). For x,y in D on one leaf, (2.3) and (3.1) imply

    sigma_y = c(gx,gy) sigma_x,
    tau_y   = c(gx,gy) tau_x.                                   (3.2)

Consequently their Radon–Nikodym derivative, when represented on an actual plaque, does not depend on the choice of basepoint of that leaf, up to the relevant leaf-measure null set.

### 3.2 Construction of a measurable density on M

Choose a countable covering by sufficiently small source boxes; their g-images are also boxes for W. Disintegrate mu in each source box. On almost every source plaque P_t choose a completed-measurable section s(t) in D. Its image gP_t is a target plaque. Restrict sigma_(s(t)) and tau_(s(t)) to gP_t, and call these two finite equivalent kernels Sigma_t and T_t.

There is a jointly measurable Radon–Nikodym derivative h(t,z) = dSigma_t/dT_t(z). For the finite-kernel theorem and its applicability, see `SOURCE_AUDIT.md`; if a uniformly bounded kernel convention is used, divide both kernels by 1 plus their total masses first, without changing the derivative. One concrete construction uses the target plaque coordinates: take a sequence of refining finite Borel partitions generating their Borel sigma-algebra, form the measurable ratios Sigma_t(A)/T_t(A) on each atom A, assign an arbitrary value on zero-denominator atoms, and take the limit where it exists. The Radon–Nikodym martingale convergence theorem gives the derivative for T_t-almost every z on almost every plaque. Exhaust by relatively compact subplaques if needed. This describes a derivative as a function on the plaque coordinates; it does not evaluate an arbitrarily chosen family dSigma_x/dT_x at a diagonal point.

Since g preserves mu, the conditional class on gP_t is that of g_*nu_(s(t)); by assumed equivalence this is also the class of T_t. Thus h defines a measurable, positive, finite function q on this target box, modulo mu-null sets.

These definitions agree on overlaps, modulo mu-null sets. Indeed, any two sections whose target plaques meet provide, on their common leaf, two numerator/denominator pairs related by the same scalar (3.2). Uniqueness of the Radon–Nikodym derivative gives equality on the overlap in the leaf measure class. Disintegrating and using the conditional-class hypothesis makes the overlap exceptional set mu-null. Countability of the charts and refinements allows the exceptions to be discarded simultaneously. Fix a global completed-measurable representative q:M -> (0,infinity), modified arbitrarily on the remaining null set.

### 3.3 Invariance and ergodicity

Using commutation, (2.4), and (2.1),

    f_*sigma_x = exp(a(x)-b(x)+b(fx)) sigma_(fx)
                = exp(a(gx)) sigma_(fx),
    f_*tau_x   = exp(a(gx)) tau_(fx).                            (3.3)

The two measures are multiplied by the same scalar. Radon–Nikodym uniqueness therefore identifies the derivative on an f-image plaque with the derivative on the initial plaque composed with f inverse. Compare these derivatives with the global q on the countably many overlap refinements. Conditional-class equivalence and mu-invariance yield

    q(fz) = q(z) for mu-almost every z.

Ergodicity implies q=K_g mu-almost everywhere, for some K_g in (0,infinity). For completeness, each sublevel set of an almost invariant real measurable function is invariant modulo null sets and so has measure zero or one; its distribution is therefore concentrated at a point. Positivity and finiteness of q rule out zero and infinity.

Disintegrating q=K_g in the same countably many target charts now gives Sigma_t=K_g T_t on almost every target plaque. Equation (3.2) extends this local measure identity to every allowed basepoint on each good source plaque. Since the smaller box interiors cover M, for mu-almost every x in D there is a positive intrinsic radius rho(x) such that

    H_x := sigma_x - K_g tau_x

vanishes on B_W(gx,rho(x)). Here H_x is the local signed Radon difference on W(gx); the assertion H_x=0 means equality of the two positive measures, and does not require subtracting two infinite total masses. This is only a local identity so far; no whole-leaf conclusion has been obtained by diagonal evaluation or by silently assuming a leaf-saturated good set.

### 3.4 Whole-leaf globalization by backward recurrence

For integer j>=1 let

    A_j = {x in D : H_x restricted to B_W(gx,1/j) is zero}.

The sets may first be taken completed-measurable: equality of locally finite kernels on a variable-radius plaque is tested by a countable generating family in chart coordinates. Replace them by Borel subsets with the same mu-measure. Their union has full mu-measure. Hence at least one A_j has positive measure. Fix such a j and put delta=1/j.

By ergodicity of f inverse, for mu-almost every x there are integers n_l tending to infinity with f^(-n_l)x in A_j. This follows directly from Birkhoff's theorem applied to 1_(A_j), whose backward averages converge to mu(A_j)>0.

Equation (3.3) gives the exact whole-leaf covariance

    f_*H_x = exp(a(gx)) H_(fx).                                 (3.4)

For a recurrent x in D and y_l=f^(-n_l)x, iteration of (3.4) therefore makes H_x vanish on

    f^(n_l) B_W(g y_l,delta).

Let Q be any compact subset of W(gx), and let R=max_{z in Q}d_W(z,gx), which is finite. Commutation and uniform contraction give

    d_W(f^(-n_l)z, g y_l) <= C theta^(n_l) R < delta

for all z in Q and all sufficiently large l. Thus Q is contained in the displayed zero-defect region. This argument proves that these regions eventually contain every compact subset; it does not assert they are nested. Since an intrinsic leaf is sigma-compact, equality on its compact exhaustion, or on a countable relatively compact open cover, gives H_x=0 on the whole leaf.

This supplies a mu-conull measurable set E contained in D on which (3.0) holds everywhere as an identity of whole-leaf measures. Choose a Borel conull subset E_0 of E and define

    D' = intersection over m,n in Z of f^m g^n E_0.

The intersection is countable, Borel, conull, contained in D, and invariant under f and g. Every point of D' lies in E_0, so (3.0) holds there. This completes the proof.

## 4. Finite commuting families

If finitely many diffeomorphisms g_i separately satisfy Section 2 with functions b_i and the same f,mu,W,D, apply the theorem to each. Intersect the resulting good sets and all translates under the countable group generated by f and the g_i. Provided all these generators preserve mu, this remains conull and gives all individual whole-leaf laws simultaneously, with constants K_i.

This observation is still conditional on the existence, measurability, rebasing, and conditional classes of the given family. It supplies no construction of such a family, no general potential-to-measure theorem, and no unproved assertion that constants alone form a group character without appropriate scalar cocycle and relation hypotheses.

## 5. Why the original domain cannot in general be retained

We give a counterexample even with continuous constant a,b, exact Gibbs rebasing, a smooth linear coarse Lyapunov foliation, and a common invariant ergodic Haar probability.

Let B be the integer matrix with rows (2,1),(1,1), let lambda=(3+sqrt(5))/2, and put M=T2 x T2. Define

    f(u,v)=(Bu,Bv),       g(u,v)=(Bu,B^(-1)v).

They commute, are Anosov, and preserve Haar probability mu. The eigenvalues of B are lambda and lambda inverse. The action of f is ergodic: every nonzero integer Fourier frequency has an infinite orbit under f transpose because no positive power of f has eigenvalue one. An invariant L2 function has constant Fourier coefficients along each such orbit; square summability forces all those coefficients to vanish. Thus invariant functions are constant.

Let E^u be the expanding line of B, with irrational slope lambda-2. Let W be the first-factor expanding-line foliation, keeping the second coordinate fixed. Under the Z2 action generated by f,g, its exponent is (m,n) -> (m+n) log(lambda), and the other joint eigendirections have the three exponents -(m+n) log(lambda), (m-n) log(lambda), and -(m-n) log(lambda). None is a positive multiple of the first exponent, so W is a coarse Lyapunov foliation. Both f and g multiply its intrinsic length by lambda, so f inverse uniformly contracts it.

Choose the explicit point

    x_0 = ((sqrt(2),0) mod Z2, (sqrt(3),0) mod Z2)

and let L_(m,n)=W(f^m g^n x_0), for m,n in Z. These leaves are pairwise distinct. To verify this, write p=m+n and q=m-n. Equality of second coordinates forces q=q': if q-q'=k is nonzero, then sqrt(3)(B^k-I)e_1 would be an integer vector, impossible since B^k-I is an invertible integer matrix and sqrt(3) is irrational. With q=q', equality of first-factor leaves would require

    sqrt(2)(B^(p-p')-I)e_1 - z in E^u

for an integer vector z (after applying B^(-p'), which preserves E^u and the integer lattice). For nonzero p-p', put w=(B^(p-p')-I)e_1, a nonzero integer vector. A nonzero linear functional ell with coefficients in Q(sqrt(5)) annihilating E^u has ell(w) nonzero, because E^u contains no nonzero integer vector. The displayed condition gives sqrt(2)=ell(z)/ell(w) in Q(sqrt(5)), a contradiction. Thus p=p', and (p,q) determines (m,n).

Each leaf is Borel: its expanding-line parametrization is a continuous injective map from R into M, and the Lusin–Souslin theorem applies. Each leaf is Haar-null; indeed it is a countable union of one-dimensional smooth images in a four-dimensional torus. Consequently

    S = union over (m,n) in Z2 of L_(m,n)

is Borel, Haar-null, and invariant under both f and g. Set r(x)=n on L_(m,n), and r(x)=0 outside S. This is a Borel leaf-constant function satisfying

    r(fx)=r(x) everywhere,
    r(gx)-r(x)=1 on S and 0 off S.

Let ell_x be intrinsic arclength on the entire leaf W(x), and define

    nu_x = exp(r(x)) ell_x,         D=M,
    a(x)=b(x)=-log(lambda).

This is a measurable nonzero leafwise Radon family. It has Haar conditional classes in foliation boxes. Since r is leaf-constant and a is constant, c(x,y)=1 and the exact Gibbs rebasing law holds. Pushforward of arclength by expansion with factor lambda is lambda inverse times target arclength. It follows that

    f_*nu_x = exp(a(x)) nu_(fx) everywhere,
    g_*nu_x = exp(b(x)) exp(r(x)-r(gx)) nu_(gx) everywhere.

The scalar square holds identically, and g equivalence holds everywhere. Nevertheless the normalized g factor is exp(-1) on S and 1 off S. No single K_g works for every basepoint in the original D=M. The corrected theorem works on the invariant conull set D'=M minus S, with K_g=1.

This refutes only retention of an arbitrary original domain. It does not refute existence of compatible representatives after removal of exceptional leaves, and it is not a counterexample to the AIM construction question.
