## Argument-level review of the final geometry

### The complete two-root model

The base block F has whole edges 12, 23, 34, 14, 135, and 245. Its root objects are the half-planes x<=0 and y<=0. With P=(-1,2), Q=(2,-1), R=(2,2), S=(-1,4), and T=(4,-1), the remaining initial objects are the triangles conv(P,R,T), conv(Q,R,S), and the segment [P,Q].

Substitution on x+y=1 makes the first triangle meet the segment only at P and the second only at Q. The two triangles meet at R, but their common part lies strictly in the positive quadrant: the bounds y<=2 and 5x+3y>=7 rule out x<=0, with the symmetric argument ruling out y<=0. Each bounded object avoids the intersection of the roots. These observations exclude exactly the eight forbidden triples; P and Q witness the two allowed triples. The other four maximal pair-edges have the displayed witnesses. All ten pairs occur, and every larger forbidden family contains a forbidden triple. This is a complete nerve proof.

Centroid dilation and the displayed three-point segment replacement make all objects nondegenerate and all required edge witnesses strict. Empty subfamilies involving unbounded roots still have a positive common-distance gap because each relevant nonface contains a bounded nonroot object. The maximum of the distances is coercive and attains a strictly positive minimum. Thus the argument does not incorrectly invoke compactness of the root half-planes. The exact eta=1/1000 coordinates and their witnesses are retained; the smallest unnormalized witness slack is 9/500000. The earlier accepted exact dual-certificate analysis remains intact in the complete review, including its normalized positive infeasibility margins.

### Normalization before fixed old coordinates

The stable generic normalization is essential. A sufficiently small increase in supporting right sides gives strict witnesses while finite nonface gaps preserve the nerve. The resulting open neighborhood of vertex coordinates contains rational choices avoiding the finitely many cross-triangle parallel-side and vertex-on-side equalities. For an initially real representation, the preliminary enlargement can be real, followed by rational choice in that open neighborhood. There is no premise that an arbitrary input was already rational or generic.

At a maximal pair-edge {u,v}, degree at least two at each root prevents either root triangle from containing the other. Otherwise another edge through the contained root supplies a third triangle and a common point with both roots, contradicting maximality of the pair-edge. Linearity ensures that this other edge does not already contain the other root. This is a full-nerve argument.

After normalization the roots have interior overlap, neither contains the other, and their boundaries meet. General position makes a boundary intersection a rational crossing of two nonparallel side interiors. Pair-edge maximality excludes every other old triangle from that point. Finiteness and closedness give a private neighborhood, and shrinking it makes each root exactly its active half-plane throughout the neighborhood. The two affine supporting functions supply a rational invertible chart.

Consequently the insertion theorem fixes the normalized old family. It does not guarantee that arbitrary original coordinates survive the preliminary normalization. If suitable rational old coordinates already supply the needed crossing, insertion itself leaves every old triangle unchanged. Later normalization at a different pair may again change earlier coordinates. Those qualifications appear in the proof, integration text, and independent review.

### Exhaustive insertion argument

For a suitable two-half-plane model, rationally scale its finitely many bounded nonroot triangles into the private chart. Old-only subfamilies are unchanged. Subfamilies involving new triangles and only the roots have exactly the model intersections, since the old roots agree with the model half-planes wherever a new triangle lies. A subfamily containing a new triangle and any old nonroot is empty already on that pair. These cases exhaust all subfamilies, including higher intersections.

An old and a new nonshared edge can meet only at a root, and neither contains both roots. Hence linearity and the whole maximal-edge inventory are preserved, with the shared pair counted once. Each fresh F vertex has degree at least two, making subsequent eligible pair insertions possible. Reusing one private crossing permits smaller scales while fixing all earlier triangles. ## Argument-level review of incidence dimension

For a covered clutter, the two stated vertex-order conditions suffice: every vertex pair occurs in both orientations, and every excluded vertex is placed after all members of its excluded edge in some order. Expanding each order by placing an edge immediately after its last member gives all incidences. The first condition handles vertex pairs. For a missing incidence, the second gives edge-before-vertex, while reversing the excluded vertex and any edge member gives the other orientation. For two different edges, the clutter condition supplies a member exclusive to each edge; the two corresponding missing-incidence witnesses reverse the edge pair through those members. Thus all four vertex/edge pair types are covered, including arbitrary edge ties at a last member. Restriction of a full realizer proves the converse criterion.

The local F permutations 1 2 3 5 4, 1 4 3 5 2, and 2 4 5 3 1 satisfy both conditions. The full sixteen missing-incidence witnesses are listed and agree with these orders. The ear permutations 1 2 z, 1 z 2, and 2 z 1 have the same required rooted forms.

The compatible-piece theorem assumes forms r s A_i, r B_i s, and s C_i r. It concatenates pieces increasingly in the first order and decreasingly in the other two. Local witnesses survive. Opposite piece direction reverses cross-piece vertex pairs. For a cross-piece missing incidence, the first order handles the increasing direction; the second handles the reverse direction for edges containing r, the third handles those containing s, and either handles edges containing neither. Linearity excludes a noncommon edge containing both roots. The common pair precedes every nonroot in the first order. This is a complete uniform proof under the displayed hypotheses.

An induced S3 supplies the lower bound: use an F block if k>=1, or the first ear when k=0. Each of its three diagonal incomparable pairs needs a reversal, and no linear extension can reverse two without contradicting the cross-incidences. Thus dimension is exactly three whenever k+t>=1. The excluded empty-piece case k=t=0 has dimension two. The proof does not assume the compatible local orders exist for an arbitrary dimension-three hypergraph.
