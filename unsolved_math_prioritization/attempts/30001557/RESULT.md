# Result: first-turn candidate, independent review pending

For every noninteger real alpha>1, no finite or infinite family of unrestricted nonerasing word patterns characterizes the occurrence of powers with exponent at least alpha over arbitrary finite alphabets. The proof gives an explicit sufficiently large fixed alphabet on which the characterization already fails, by a nonerasing endomorphism of that same alphabet.

The mechanism is exact: a truncated repetition of a block of distinct letters has maximal factor exponent equal to its whole-word exponent. Expanding one letter lowers this maximum across alpha, whereas any language defined by pattern occurrence is morphism-closed.

See [PROOF.md](PROOF.md). There is one substantive author turn, with 13,071 supplementary exact finite controls. The proof covers irrational alpha and infinite pattern sets without a computation or compactness assumption.

The source is Shallit's Problem38, printed2231 in OWR37/2010. The source's partial negative ideas by Currie and Cassaigne and the existing desk-review closure suggestion are credited. No historical novelty is asserted.

## Required scope limitation

This is the alphabet-unrestricted question. It is **not** an impossibility theorem on every fixed small alphabet: unary alphabets and binary alphabets with 1<alpha<=3/2 admit explicit characterizations, proved in the packet. A classification of all fixed-alphabet exceptions is not claimed. The independent reviewer must decide whether this answers the source's intended quantifiers.
