# Exact source and interpretation

Primary source: Jeffrey Shallit, “On Patterns and Pattern Avoidance,” OWR37/2010, printed2230–2236. The original workshop occurred August22–28,2010; EMS published the report March2,2011. Problem38 is printed2231, PDFpage37; the alphabet and matching definitions immediately precede it on2230, PDFpage36. The complete target page was visually inspected.

The question permits real noninteger alpha>1, arbitrary finite or infinite pattern sets, finite words and factors, variable-only patterns, and nonempty substitution images. Having an alpha-power means having some factor whose length/period is at least alpha. It is not exact equality with an irrational number, nor strict alpha-plus avoidance. There is no abelian matching, erasure, terminal constants, constrained image lengths, or injectivity condition.

The alphabet is introduced as an arbitrary nonempty finite set; Problem38 does not specialize its cardinality. We interpret its characterization as valid for words over arbitrary finite alphabets. Our proof actually fails on a single D-letter alphabet determined by alpha, even if the proposed pattern set depends on that alphabet. We do not silently claim failure for every prescribed smaller alphabet. The unary/binary examples in the proof make the distinction explicit. Review must independently assess this source reading.

## What a full result means here

A proof covering every noninteger alpha in the alphabet-unrestricted interpretation answers the assigned target. The explicit fixed-alphabet obstruction is part of that proof, not a classification for all alphabet sizes. Integer thresholds are excluded by the source and have the one-pattern characterization X^k. Infinite-word avoidance or the Dejean repetition threshold is not the assigned question.
