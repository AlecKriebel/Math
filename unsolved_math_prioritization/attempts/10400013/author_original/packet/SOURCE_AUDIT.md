# Source audit and status limits

Inspected 2026-10-08 UTC. Bibliographic labels below correspond to SOURCE_IDENTITIES.json, which binds the exact retained PDF bytes. Page counts include repository-generated covers where present. Sources and extracts remain outside the author distribution.

1. **T. Ohtsuki (editor), Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds**, Geometry & Topology Monographs 4 (2002), 373–572. Publisher PDF: https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf . Problem 1.13 is on printed p.391, one-based PDF p.19. The relevant page was extracted and visually inspected. The actual bold heading has no descriptive subtitle; attribution is to A. Stoimenow. The catalog truncation is a title-extraction fault. The printed item leaves its ambient link/knot domain implicit; we retain both readings. We do not alter the queue title under a permission scoped only to status/turns/findings.

2. **P. Traczyk, 3-braids with proportional Jones polynomials**, Kobe Journal of Mathematics 15(2) (1998), 187–190. Official version-of-record repository: https://doi.org/10.24546/E0003672 ; PDF https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/E0003672/E0003672.pdf . Full four-page article inspected in text; the construction/formula on printed p.187 (PDF p.2 after its cover) also inspected visually. The claimed family is two-component, not a knot family. Its polynomials differ by monomial factors and have span 2. The repository adds a retrieval-date cover, so its whole-PDF hash may change on a future download even when article content does not.

3. **A. Stoimenow, Properties of closed 3-braids**, author version dated January 27, 2006, first version September 22, 2004. https://stoimenov.net/stoimeno/homepage/papers/mwf.pdf . Inspected printed pp.23–26, including the Traczyk discussion, the distinction between fixed polynomials and units, representation conventions, and the three-braid Jones/Burau identity. The packet credits established formulas rather than certifying all global theorems of this manuscript. Corollary 3.8 concerns fixed Jones polynomials of closed three-braids; it is not fixed-span finiteness for arbitrary links.

4. **A. Stoimenow, The second coefficient of the Jones polynomial**, author research-expository conference manuscript associated with Intelligence of Low Dimensional Topology 2004. https://stoimenov.net/stoimeno/homepage/papers/talk.pdf . Inspected the introduction and span discussion on printed p.5. It explicitly separates the negative link phenomenon from the knot problem. Its historical unknown-status statement is not treated as current proof that the question remains open.

5. **A. Stoimenow, The Jones polynomial, genus and weak genus of a knot**, Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse 8(4) (1999), 677–693. https://doi.org/10.5802/afst.949 ; PDF https://afst.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/afst.949.pdf . Inspected introduction/notation, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.4 (printed pp.685–686). The bounded genus in the fixed-span statement is canonical/weak genus, not arbitrary minimal Seifert genus. The coefficient-list-to-shift argument is elementary; the finite diagram-generator input is a credited source theorem, not independently reconstructed here.

6. **V. F. R. Jones, The Jones Polynomial**, author survey dated August 18, 2005. https://math.berkeley.edu/~vfr/jones.pdf . Inspected Section 4, especially printed pp.11–12, for the Markov-trace normalization, unitary root-of-unity representations and evaluation bound. The braid strand count and the root order are different parameters despite the source's reused notation. The interpolation corollary in our proof requires a fixed strand bound; it does not reverse the Morton–Franks–Williams inequality.

7. **T. Kanenobu, K. Kishimoto, T. Sumi, Jones polynomial of a knot with small span**, Kobe Journal of Mathematics 42 (2025), 25–31. Official version of record https://doi.org/10.24546/0100498237 ; PDF https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/0100498237/0100498237.pdf . Full mathematical text inspected, including Theorem 1.1, Section 2 restrictions, Section 3 calculation, Theorem 4.1 and Question 4.3. The finite candidate list covers span at most six. Some listed candidates have no known realization in that paper. Infinite formal candidates at span >=7 are not infinite actual knot values. We do not reproduce its table or certify its omitted span-six enumeration. Its published theorem is credited with those limits.

8. **T. Ishikawa, T. Kanenobu, K. Kishimoto, T. Sumi, Q polynomial of a knot with small degree**, Kobe Journal of Mathematics 43 (2026), 1–6. Official version of record https://doi.org/10.24546/0100501377 ; PDF https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/0100501377/0100501377.pdf . Full mathematical text inspected, including Theorems 1.1 and 3.1 and Question 3.3. Actual values are classified through degree four; above this it gives formal candidates and poses their knot realization. The odd constant-coefficient property makes knot Q degree equal span. This is not a general solution of the Q clause. We do not reproduce the classification table.

9. **K. Ichihara, Purely cosmetic surgeries on knots with low-span Jones polynomials**, arXiv:2609.32507v1, submitted September 26, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32507v1 ; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32507v1 . The primary landing page and HTML Sections 1–2 were inspected; the complete PDF was retained and hashed, but its full proof and scripts were not audited. Its theorem is a publicly stated manuscript claim about cosmetic surgery, not general fixed-span finiteness. It is only a recent-status cross-check and is not a premise of our proofs.

The source search was bounded, included exact problem wording, Stoimenow/fixed-span terminology, Traczyk, and 2025–2026 small-span literature. It supports the credited scoped results; it does not establish global novelty or exhaustive current-literature coverage.
