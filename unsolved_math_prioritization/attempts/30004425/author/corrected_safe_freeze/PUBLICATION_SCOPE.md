## Explicit source choices

Escobar–Harada, arXiv:1912.04809v2, Section 2, equation (2.4), the choices preceding Theorem 2.7, and Section 3 supply the following conventions.

Choose linearly independent integral common rows u_1,...,u_d in the shared face C, spanning it, with u_1=(1,...,1). Choose w_i in C_i so that w_i + sum_j u_j belongs to the relative interior of C_i. The matrix M_i has these d common rows followed by w_i, so its shape is (d+1) by n. These choices specify the valuation construction; merely choosing an arbitrary vector on either side of a wall is insufficient.

The order first compares the degree coordinate in reverse order, then compares the remaining coordinates lexicographically. Explicitly, (a,v) precedes (b,w) when a>b, or when a=b and v precedes w in the standard lexicographic order. Initial forms use the minimum in this order. Because the defining ideal is homogeneous, the reversed degree convention is consistent with the homogeneous computations in the example.

Let G(S_i) be the lattice generated by the value semigroup. The vertical subgroup is G(S_i) intersected with the line on which the first d coordinates vanish. Normalize vertical length using the positive generator of that rank-one subgroup. The normalized fiber lengths agree; the ordinary-coordinate lengths satisfy L_2=kappa L_1 for a positive constant determined by the lattice choices. The formulas in REPORT.md use exactly this latter definition of kappa. Rescaling the last row changes ordinary lengths and does not justify setting kappa to one. In the retained example, both column groups are Z^3 and kappa=1, as proved in EXACT_CONTROLS.md.

These are clarifications of the cited source conventions. No new mathematical claim is added. The example proofs, verifier, and saved arithmetic results are byte-identical to the original author freeze.
