# Exact mathematical controls

These calculations explain and independently replay Escobar–Harada Example 4.5, [arXiv:1912.04809v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04809v2). They claim no novelty. The general wall-crossing existence theorem remains an explicitly imported published result.

## The hypersurface and the three cones

Put a=(0,11,0,0), b=(6,0,4,1), c=(7,0,1,3), and f=x^a-x^b-x^c. This homogeneous degree-11 polynomial is irreducible. Indeed, viewed as a monic polynomial in x_2 over the UFD C[x_1,x_3,x_4], it is Eisenstein at the prime x_4: its constant coefficient is divisible by x_4 but not x_4^2. Gauss's lemma therefore applies. Hence A=C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]/(f) is a graded domain of dimension 3, and Proj(A) is a surface.

Let u=(1,1,1,1), v=(0,1,2,3), w_1=(0,0,-1,4), w_2=(0,0,3,-1), and L=span_R(u,v). The common equalities w.a=w.b=w.c define exactly L because the two exponent differences are independent. The three-term tropical hypersurface has three maximal cones, given by equality of two minimal weights and the remaining inequality. In particular

    C_1 = L + R_nonnegative w_1,
    C_2 = L + R_nonnegative w_2.

The three monomial weights are (0,0,11) for w_1 and (0,11,0) for w_2. Therefore C_1,C_2 are distinct dimension-3 cones, meet exactly in L, and share a dimension-2 facet. Initial ideals of a principal ideal are generated by the initial polynomial: multiplicativity of initial forms proves this directly.

Their initial polynomials are g_1=x^a-x^b and g_2=x^a-x^c. The third ray may be chosen as w_3=(0,0,-2,-3); its weights are (0,-11,-11). Its initial polynomial is

    -x_1^6 x_3 x_4 (x_3^3 + x_1 x_4^2).

The resulting principal ideal is not prime, and contains no monomial: a polynomial with the nonmonomial factor x_3^3+x_1 x_4^2 cannot divide any monomial. This verifies directly that maximal tropical does not imply prime.

## Primeness and the correct value lattices

Use matrices M_i with rows u,v,w_i. Their ranks are 3. Their integer kernels are generated respectively by

    r_1=(-6,11,-4,-1),
    r_2=(-7,11,-1,-3).

For M_1 the first three column minor is -1; for M_2 columns 1,2,4 have minor -1. Thus each column group is the whole Z^3. The displayed kernel vector in each case is primitive, spans the rational kernel, and has a coordinate of absolute value one; consequently it generates the integer kernel.

Map x_j to the Laurent monomial with exponent the j-th column of M_i. Its image is a subring of a Laurent polynomial ring, so its kernel is prime. That kernel is exactly (g_i). To see this without assuming irreducibility, group terms in a polynomial by their equal image exponents. Its kernel is spanned by binomial differences with exponent difference in Z r_i. After removing a common monomial factor, each such binomial is a difference of equal positive powers of x^a and x^b (or x^c). The identity U^k-V^k=(U-V)(U^(k-1)+...+V^(k-1)) shows divisibility by g_i. This proves equality of ideals and hence primeness. The two cones satisfy all the prime-cone hypotheses.

## Standard representatives and the algebraic map

Choose a monomial order with leading monomial x_2^11, for example lexicographic order with x_2 first. The common standard monomials have exponent alpha with 0<=alpha_2<11. Since x_2^11 is also the leading monomial of each g_i, the cones C_i lie in the closure of the Gröbner chamber for that leading term. More explicitly, that chamber is given, in the minimum-weight convention, by w.a<=w.b,w.c; the equalities defining C_i are its faces.

Let S_i=M_i N^4. Every element of S_1 has exactly one standard representative. Existence follows by replacing each block of 11 in alpha_2 by the nonnegative exponent vector b; the image under M_1 does not change. Uniqueness follows because two representatives differ by an integer multiple of r_1, while their second entries are both in [0,10]. The same proof uses c for S_2.

Therefore assigning M_1 alpha to M_2 alpha for standard alpha is a bijection S_1→S_2. The first two coordinates are preserved. This is precisely the published algebraic wall-crossing construction in this example.

Let q=(1,1,0), the second column of either matrix. Since x_2 is standard, Theta(q)=q. But 11q=M_1 b and b is standard, so

    Theta(11q) = M_2 b = (11,11,11) != (11,11,0) = 11 Theta(q).

It follows that Theta is not a semigroup homomorphism. If one drops the standard-representative requirement, the proposed formula is not even well-defined: a and b have the same M_1 image but different M_2 images.

For degree n, standard exponents number

    h(n) = binomial(n+3,3) - binomial(n-8,3) if n>=11,
    h(n) = binomial(n+3,3) if 0<=n<11.

This follows by removing monomials divisible by x_2^11. The all-degree proof is above; the program also enumerates the exponents through degree 22 and checks injectivity, image equality, inverse consistency, and the Hilbert count.

## Polygons and their geometric maps

Delete the degree coordinate. The first polygon has vertices (0,0),(2,-1),(3,4); its remaining generating point (1,0) is interior. The second has vertices (0,0),(2,3),(3,-1), again with interior generating point (1,0). Direct equations for the edge lines give, for 0<=x<=3,

    a_1(x)=max(-x/2,5x-11),  b_1(x)=4x/3,
    a_2(x)=-x/3,             b_2(x)=min(3x/2,11-4x).

On [0,2], both fiber lengths equal 11x/6. On [2,3], both equal 11-11x/3. Thus kappa=1 for these matrices. The shift is z↦z-a_1(x)+a_2(x); the flip is z↦-z+a_1(x)+b_2(x). The latter simplifies to z↦x-z on both intervals. At q we obtain S(q)=(1,1,1/6) and F(q)=(1,1,1). Neither equals Theta(q).

More generally, given rational polytopes with common projection and interval endpoints satisfying b_2-a_2=kappa(b_1-a_1), the formulas in REPORT.md are bijective. On each fiber the shift is increasing, takes endpoints to corresponding endpoints, and has inverse z↦a_1+(z-a_2)/kappa. The flip is decreasing, exchanges endpoints, and has inverse z↦a_1+(b_2-z)/kappa. Endpoint functions are piecewise-affine because the bodies are polytopes; hence so are the maps. This argument includes zero-length fibers. Homogeneous extension from the degree-one slice fixes the origin and is piecewise-linear on the entire cone.

This elementary verification does not prove the imported constant-ratio theorem for arbitrary tropical pairs.

## Saturation and normality controls

The integer vector h=(1,1,1) lies in both real cones and in both value groups Z^3. In degree one, S_i consists exactly of the four columns, so h lies in neither S_i. However

    11h = M_1(7,0,1,3) = M_2(6,0,4,1).

Thus each S_i has a saturation hole. Algebraically, the Laurent monomial with exponent h belongs to the fraction field of C[S_i], is integral because its 11th power belongs to C[S_i], and is absent from C[S_i] by linear independence of Laurent monomials. Therefore both affine semigroup rings are nonnormal. In this example the projective hypersurfaces are nonnormal too: on the codimension-one line x_1=x_2=0 all first derivatives of g_i vanish, so the hypersurface is not regular in codimension one. Over C the hypersurface Jacobian criterion and the necessary R1 condition for normality establish this claim.

In particular, F maps q in S_1 to a hole of S_2, while S maps q outside the lattice. Passing to saturated semigroups would change the problem. In fact F(d,x,z)=(d,x,x-z) is a unimodular linear map taking the two full cones to one another; that stronger convex/lattice fact still does not preserve the original semigroups.

## A genuine flat family in the example

Over R=C[s,t], form the graded algebra

    B = R[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4] /
        (x_2^11 - s x_1^6 x_3^4 x_4 - t x_1^7 x_3 x_4^3).

Division by the monic polynomial in x_2 gives an R-basis of monomials with x_2 exponent below 11. Hence every graded part B_n is a finite free R-module of rank h(n), and B is R-flat. The projective family Proj_R(B)→Spec(R) is flat: its standard affine charts are degree-zero summands of graded localizations of B, which are R-flat. Its fibers at (1,1), (1,0), and (0,1) are X, X_1, and X_2 respectively.

The paths (s,t)=(1,lambda^11) and (lambda^11,1) are the two weight degenerations. For the first path the substitution x_j↦lambda^(w_1)_j x_j sends f to x^a-x^b-lambda^11 x^c; for the second the analogous w_2 substitution gives x^a-lambda^11 x^b-x^c. For lambda nonzero these substitutions are invertible diagonal changes of projective coordinates. Thus these are actual flat one-parameter toric degenerations with the required generic fibers, not just equal Hilbert polynomials. No conclusion about a morphism X_1→X_2 follows from these families.

## Scope of the checks

All displayed example-specific conclusions have the algebraic proofs above. The Python program checks their finite integer/rational certificates and a small all-monomial replay range. It uses no floating point, optional package, network, random sampling, or external dataset. It is not a formal proof assistant and does not verify the cited general existence theorems.
