## The question and its actual setting

The live public record asks for the relation between toric degenerations from two adjacent maximal-dimensional prime cones, including maps and value semigroups. The numeric landing page could not be read; the corresponding complete dataset-server row was retrieved and its identity checked. The record is a broad question, not a specified equivalence theorem.

The source is Megumi Harada's contribution, joint with Laura Escobar, in [Oberwolfach Report 10/2020](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46846), printed pages 580–582. There is no fixed named variety in the general statement. The exact setting is:

- A standard graded complex domain A = C[x_1,...,x_n]/I, generated in degree one, with Krull dimension d+1; X = Proj(A) is an irreducible projective variety of dimension d.
- The tropical fan consists of weights whose initial ideals contain no monomial, using a fixed initial-term convention. A prime cone has a prime initial ideal. Both cones C_1,C_2 have dimension d+1 and their common face C has dimension d.
- Choose independent integral rows u_1,...,u_d spanning C, with u_1=(1,...,1), and complementary rows w_i on the corresponding sides. Form M from the common rows and M_i by appending w_i. The matrices have d+1 rows and n columns, not the transposed shape.
- The Kaveh–Manon weight valuations nu_i on A have rank d+1, one-dimensional leaves, and finitely generated semigroups S_i generated by the columns of M_i. Their associated graded rings are the corresponding prime binomial initial quotients, equivalently C[S_i]. The common rank-d valuation is the projection of either nu_i.
- The toric special fibers may be nonnormal. No saturation of S_i, smoothness, Fano condition, reflexivity, cluster structure, or normality of X is a hypothesis of the basic wall-crossing result.

These controls matter: a maximal tropical cone need not be prime; a prime binomial quotient need not be normal; an equality of convex bodies loses semigroup holes.

## What is already proved

Write Delta_i = conv(columns(M_i)) and let p forget the final coordinate. Both Delta_i project to the same body Delta. Over u in Delta, write their vertical fibers as [a_i(u),b_i(u)]. Their lengths satisfy

    b_2(u)-a_2(u) = kappa (b_1(u)-a_1(u))

for a positive constant kappa independent of u. Define kappa by this equation, avoiding any normalization ambiguity. It depends on the coordinate/lattice choices. The corresponding shift and flip are

    S(u,z) = (u, a_2(u) + kappa (z-a_1(u))),
    F(u,z) = (u, b_2(u) - kappa (z-a_1(u))).

They are piecewise-affine bijections on the degree-one slices and extend homogeneously to piecewise-linear maps of the cones. They cover the identity on Delta. They scale ordinary vertical Euclidean length by kappa; after the appropriate lattice normalization the scale is one. Unqualified equality for arbitrary row rescalings is false. The brief OWR formulation should be read with the full paper's global-constant qualification.

The published proof first identifies the common projection. It then uses the wall initial variety Y = Proj(C[x]/in_C(I)): its two further initial degenerations are the toric fibers X_i. Common torus-equivariant flat degenerations preserve the degrees of the corresponding one-dimensional GIT quotients. These degrees compute normalized fiber lengths. This is the substantive imported theorem, not an inference from adjacency alone. Section 4 then gives the displayed elementary maps. Ilten's Appendix supplies the complementary complexity-one T-variety explanation.

For semigroups, assume additionally that both C_i are faces of one maximal cone of the Gröbner fan. One standard-monomial basis is then adapted to both valuations. If x^alpha is a standard monomial, the construction is

    Theta(M_1 alpha) = M_2 alpha.

It is a bijection covering the identity on the common value semigroup. The restriction to standard representatives is essential: the formula need not be well-defined for arbitrary representatives. In general Theta is neither additive nor the restriction of either F or S. For the standard Plücker embedding of Gr(2,m), Escobar–Harada establish compatibility with the flip map in the setup of their Theorem 5.15.

A semigroup bijection is not a semigroup isomorphism. A piecewise-linear map of bodies is not automatically an algebraic morphism of special fibers. Even a unimodular map between their saturated cones need not preserve the original, nonsaturated semigroups.

## Exact replay of the negative boundary

The companion proof and program reconstruct the published Example 4.5 with exact integer and rational arithmetic. The example is the surface

    x_2^11 - x_1^6 x_3^4 x_4 - x_1^7 x_3 x_4^3 = 0 in P^3.

The two prime initial matrices have common rows (1,1,1,1), (0,1,2,3) and last rows (0,0,-1,4), (0,0,3,-1). The proof verifies primeness, dimension, adjacency, common Gröbner cone, and all-degree uniqueness of the standard representatives. For v=(1,1,0),

    Theta(v) = v,
    Theta(11v) = (11,11,11),
    S(v) = (1,1,1/6),
    F(v) = (1,1,1).

Thus additivity and both proposed geometric restrictions fail, already under the common-Gröbner-cone hypothesis. Moreover F(v) is a saturation hole in the second semigroup. A third maximal tropical cone in this same example is explicitly nonprime. These are established counterexamples, not discoveries of this investigation.

The replay additionally checks degree 0 through 22 against the exact Hilbert function. The proof, rather than that bounded enumeration, establishes the representative formula in every degree. An elementary monic two-parameter family is also given in the companion proof. This illustrates a genuine flat family without confusing it with the PL maps.

## Later primary literature and remaining scope

Cas Proost's [2024 master's thesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14515v2), especially Sections 3–4, applies prime-cone wall crossing after changing the embedding. Its general procedure requires suitable adjacent prime lifts; it does not prove that this always happens. Its Gr(3,6) computations are a case study. Their large tropical computation was inspected as a stated result, not independently rerun here.

Escobar, Harada and Manon's [Geometric families of degenerations from mutations of polytopes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01785v3), revised 28 September 2026, develops polyptych lattices. Theorems 7.11, 7.21 and 7.22 require a finite polyptych lattice, strict dual data, a suitable detropicalization with convex adapted basis, and stated polytope hypotheses; integrality enters the toric-degeneration theorem. These are powerful conditional geometric constructions. Their existence for every arbitrary pair of prime tropical cones is not supplied simply by citing the theorem. The inspected arXiv record labels this a preprint; no publication upgrade is asserted.

Ilten's combinatorial-mutation discussion also has extra hypotheses and involves a dual polyhedral construction. It is not a blanket assertion that every pair here is related by a single ordinary lattice mutation in every choice of coordinates.

The precise remaining boundaries are therefore:

1. The known general theorem concerns convex bodies and compatible valuations under the prime adjacent-cone hypotheses.
2. The particular standard-monomial algebraic map uses a common Gröbner chamber. The published counterexample rules out its universal additivity or universal equality to the geometric maps.
3. A characterization of when that map equals a geometric restriction is a stronger question; this report supplies no general classification.
4. Re-embedding arbitrary nonprime cones while preserving the needed adjacency, or lifting arbitrary PL data to algebraic families with prescribed original semigroups, needs further hypotheses and work.
5. No categorical equivalence, isomorphism of the special fibers, canonical birational transformation, or universal direct morphism between them was demanded precisely by the source or proved here.

The broad catalogue item should not continue as if the 2019–2022 results did not exist. If a narrower research problem is retained, it needs a precise additional target rather than the original generic wording.
