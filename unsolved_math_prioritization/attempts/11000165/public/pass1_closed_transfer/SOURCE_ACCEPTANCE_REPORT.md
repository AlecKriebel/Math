# Closed transfer, integral quotient, and generator acceptance

Audit date: 14 October 2026. Scope: the single shared first mathematical pass for Birman Problem 2.8, row 1788 / ID 11000165. This is an independently authored source-and-acceptance audit, not a claim of new prior-art discovery and not a publication action.

## Verdict

1. **Closed Omori transfer passes, for every g≥3.** The disk-fixed theorem transfers to ordinary generation of the exact closed group HI_g by ambient handlebody conjugates of one standard genus-one homotopical bounding-pair map. The ambient conjugating group is H_g, not HI_g. This is an application of existing work.
2. **The exact integral quotient passes:** HI_g/HK_g is the lattice W/κ(H), with κ(x)=x∧ω and W the span of all exterior monomials containing a meridian factor. There is no rationalization, saturation assumption, or replacement of HK_g by a Luft subgroup. This is the classical Morita image calculation combined with Johnson's kernel theorem and capping.
3. **The proposed unrestricted Schreier family is non-tautological.** Its parameters are arbitrary integer vectors and words in a fixed finite alphabet of explicit ambient maps. Each displayed output is automatically in HK_g; parameters are never selected by testing membership in HK_g. Telescoping proves ordinary generation. The detailed lift-word audit below passes for the explicitly inspected constructive note.
4. **Acceptance is at the explicit algebraic-construction level stated in this report.** This does not assert finite generation, finite normal generation, a short geometric generating list, a presentation, efficiency, or a new theorem of established novelty. Genus two is outside the Omori argument and is addressed separately by the genus-two lane.
5. Holden's separating-meridian proposal is a **dated conjecture**, not an unconditional generation theorem. The conditional rational-abelianization theorem is a different statement. The independently checked outer-action obstruction does not by itself answer Birman's request, while the constructive family can answer the requested higher-genus Johnson branch.

## 1. Exact groups and notation

Fix an oriented genus-g handlebody V with closed oriented boundary S. Let H_g⊂Mod(S) be its handlebody group. Let I_g be the kernel of the action on H=H₁(S;Z), and K_g the subgroup generated by separating surface twists. Set HI_g=H_g∩I_g and HK_g=H_g∩K_g.

For this report, superscript D means that an embedded boundary disk is fixed pointwise. It does not mean only a point is fixed. Omori writes a subscript g,1 for these disk-fixed groups. Hensel and Holden use other placements of the same decorations, so none of the comparisons below is inferred from typographical agreement.

Choose a symplectic basis a₁,…,a_g,b₁,…,b_g of H with the a_i spanning the meridian lattice A=ker(H→H₁(V;Z)). Put B=span{b_i} and ω=Σ_i a_i∧b_i. The constructive lane uses this convention. Holden's papers instead make the b_i meridians; their descriptions must be translated accordingly.

## 2. Closed transfer of Omori's normal-generation theorem

### 2.1 The forgetful map on the correct intersection

Let c:Mod(S with D fixed)→Mod(S) forget D. Capping the complementary once-bordered surface induces the natural isomorphism on integral first homology. Therefore

    c⁻¹(I_g)=I_g^D.

Hensel's primer, §3, printed/PDF pp.10–11, proves that the pointed handlebody group is the full preimage of the unmarked handlebody group and that the one-spotted handlebody group is the full preimage of the pointed one. Both forgetful maps are surjective. Composing yields

    c⁻¹(H_g)=H_g^D,       c(H_g^D)=H_g.

Consequently the restriction c:HI_g^D→HI_g is surjective. This is stronger than merely knowing that a disk-fixed Torelli element remains Torelli after capping. Given f∈HI_g, take any lift under the surjective surface forgetful map; the two full-preimage identities put it in both H_g^D and I_g^D.

For a direct geometric justification of the extension assertion, an isotopy on S between a chosen boundary representative and an extendible representative extends over a collar S×[0,1] of the boundary and by the identity farther inside V. Thus extension over V is invariant under changing representatives by the relevant boundary isotopy. No splitting of the forgetful map is asserted or required.

### 2.2 Images of normal closures

Omori, Theorem 1.2, pp.1–3, proves for g≥3 that HI_g^D is the normal closure in H_g^D of β^D=T_C₁T_C₂⁻¹. The displayed curves are homologous, nonseparating, bound a genus-one subsurface of the boundary, and cobound an embedded annulus in V; neither is a meridian. Their annulus twist extends over V and the bounding-pair condition gives the Torelli property.

Let β=c(β^D). Applying c to products of conjugates gives

    HI_g = ⟨ h β^ε h⁻¹ : h∈H_g, ε∈{1,−1} ⟩,      g≥3.

Surjectivity of c on H_g^D proves that every displayed ambient conjugate is obtained, and surjectivity on HI_g^D proves the reverse generation inclusion. This elementary image-of-normal-closure argument is exact; it does not confuse normal generation with cyclic generation or finite ordinary generation.

The standard curves remain a nontrivial bounding pair after capping for g≥3. Both sides of the pair have positive genus after capping (one side has genus one, the other g−2), so they do not become isotopic through an annulus. In genus two that argument fails and the theorem itself does not apply. No extrapolation to g=2 is permitted.

Holden, arXiv:2607.07587v1, §6.2 and footnote 5, p.10, states the corresponding closed generation result. It corroborates the comparison above, but is not being used in place of its proof.

## 3. Exact Johnson homomorphism and quotient

### 3.1 The surface kernel is integral and closed

The closed Johnson homomorphism is

    τ_g:I_g→Λ³H/κ(H),       κ(x)=x∧ω.

Johnson's kernel theorem identifies ker τ_g with K_g as defined by separating twists. Putman's independent proof, Theorem A specialized to the whole closed surface, verifies this identification; §2.4 and Lemma 2.8 give the integral target and capping diagram. Restricting to HI_g gives

    ker(τ_g|HI_g)=H_g∩K_g=HK_g.

This is an equality of groups, not a statement about the rational abelianization of HI_g. In particular no assertion that τ_g computes all of H₁(HI_g;Q) is necessary.

### 3.2 Image and capping

Define

    W=ker(Λ³H→Λ³(H/A))
     =Λ³A ⊕ (Λ²A⊗B) ⊕ (A⊗Λ²B).

Morita's 1989 Lemma 2.5 is an integral calculation for the disk-fixed handlebody intersection. It gives τ^D(HI_g^D)=W, in the paper's own choice of which basis vectors are meridians. The original scan, printed pp.310–312, was inspected, including the five generating monomial patterns and the integral geometric realizations. The image statement is also recorded in Holden 2509.03742v1, Proposition 9.1, pp.26–27.

The closed map satisfies τ_g∘c=π∘τ^D, where π is quotient by κ(H). Every monomial in κ(x) contains a meridian factor, so κ(H)⊂W. Because c(HI_g^D)=HI_g,

    τ_g(HI_g)=π(W)=W/κ(H).

The first isomorphism theorem now gives the claimed exact integral quotient

    HI_g/HK_g ≅ W/κ(H).

Holden 2607.07587v1, Proposition 7.3, p.12, explicitly records the same closed quotient and credits Morita. It is prior work, not a new assertion introduced by the construction.

### 3.3 Integral coordinates need no division

For each basis vector x∈{a_i,b_i}, κ(x) consists of g−1 monomials with coefficients ±1. The supports of these 2g vectors are pairwise disjoint: every monomial in such a support has one complete symplectic pair a_j,b_j and a unique extra basis vector x, and cannot have two complete pairs because it has degree three. Choose one monomial in the support of each κ(x). These are 2g distinct unit pivots.

For any v∈W, subtract the unique integer multiples of κ(x) which kill all selected pivot coefficients. The surviving nonpivot monomials form an integral basis of W/κ(H). Independence follows because a combination of the κ(x) with no pivot coefficient must have every coefficient zero. In particular the quotient is free abelian, with rank

    r = binomial(2g,3) − binomial(g,3) − 2g.

For g=3, r=13. The proof establishes saturation over Z; a rational rank computation would not suffice.

### 3.4 Source-reading cautions

Two convenient-looking source formulas must not be imported uncritically:

- Holden 2509.03742v1, p.27, displays a contraction projection involving 1/(g−1). In general this projection is rational, not an integral lattice section. For example C₃(a₁∧b₁∧b₂)=±b₂, which is not divisible by g−1 when g≥3. The unit-pivot construction above avoids this issue.
- The actual upper-triangular symplectic stabilizer in the a-meridian convention is the set of matrices [P R;0 P⁻ᵀ] with P⁻¹R symmetric. Omori p.9 gives exactly this condition. A formula permitting an arbitrary symmetric off-diagonal block for every P would generally be false. The elementary block-diagonal changes and disk shears used in the construction are symplectic and satisfy the correct condition.

These cautions concern how to use selected formulas, not rejection of the cited generation or image theorems.

## 4. Acceptance of the higher-genus algebraic family

### 4.1 Concrete input and unrestricted parameters

Wajnryb, *Mapping class group of a handlebody* (1998), Definitions 2–3 and Theorem 19, gives a finite geometric alphabet X={a₁,s₁,r₁,₂,t₁,u} generating the closed handlebody group for g>1. The original paper defines its group intrinsically on the handlebody and embeds it in the boundary mapping class group; it is not secretly a disk-fixed group. Here the a₁ in the alphabet is a mapping class, while a₁ in H is a homology vector; a final exposition should distinguish these typographically.

Let β be the closed Omori map. Set s(w,ε)=wβ^εw⁻¹ for any finite word w in X and inverses and either sign ε. Omori's transferred theorem says the s(w,ε) generate HI_g. Fix explicitly constructed elements p₁,…,p_r∈HI_g whose Johnson values are the chosen integral basis vectors λ₁,…,λ_r. For n∈Z^r set

    t_n=p₁^n₁⋯p_r^n_r,       t_0=1.

No commutativity of the p_i is assumed. Only the additive Johnson value τ_g(t_n)=n is needed. Let c(w,ε) be the integral coordinate vector of τ_g(s(w,ε)); equivariance gives it by applying Λ³ of the homology matrix of w to ετ^D(β^D) and performing the unit-pivot reduction.

The proposed generators are

    G(n,w,ε)=t_n s(w,ε) t_(n+c(w,ε))⁻¹,

with **all** n∈Z^r, **all** finite ambient words w, and both signs allowed.

### 4.2 Membership and ordinary generation

All factors are in HI_g and hence extend over the fixed V. Additivity gives

    τ_g(G(n,w,ε))=n+c(w,ε)−(n+c(w,ε))=0.

Therefore every output is in HK_g, by the exact kernel theorem. Conversely let f∈HK_g. By Omori, write f=s₁⋯s_m with each s_j=s(w_j,ε_j). Put n₀=0 and n_j=Σ_(k≤j)c(w_k,ε_k). Since τ_g(f)=0, n_m=0. Then

    ∏_(j=1)^m G(n_(j−1),w_j,ε_j)
      =t_0 s₁ t_n₁⁻¹ t_n₁ s₂ ⋯ s_m t_n_m⁻¹
      =f.

This proves equality with the subgroup ordinarily generated by the displayed family, rather than only normal generation or generation of a quotient.

### 4.3 Why this is not target-member enumeration

The construction never says “take all words representing HK_g” or “retain the words whose Johnson value vanishes.” Every allowed parameter tuple has an output. Its ingredients are a finite ambient geometric alphabet, finitely many fixed lift words for each fixed g, integer vectors, and computable exterior-algebra matrices. The corrective terms impose zero Johnson value by formula. A finite search for a word with a **specified integral homology matrix**, if used to fix auxiliary lifts, is an ambient linear computation with termination guaranteed by the known image theorem; it is not an oracle for the unknown target subgroup.

This is a standard Schreier mechanism applied to explicit known generators and an explicitly split-as-a-set abelian quotient. Its usefulness is algebraic: it gives actual words with certified membership and exact generation. It does not purport to be a short list of curve types or resolve finer geometric or finiteness questions. Under the stated acceptance standard, which permits infinite algebraically specified families, this is an answer of that kind. Calling it a new solution to a long-standing open problem would require a separate novelty and significance judgment.

All three levels of monomial lifts pass:

- A permutation of the seed gives a_i∧b_i∧b_j. If i,j,k are distinct, the block-diagonal elementary map a_j↦a_j+a_i, b_i↦b_i−b_j fixes b_j,b_k; its difference on a_j∧b_j∧b_k gives exactly a_i∧b_j∧b_k. The repeated i=j or i=k cases use the seed or its correctly signed inverse.
- A disk shear b_j↦b_j+a_j gives a_i∧a_j∧b_k when k≠j. For k=j, using the i-shear on a_j∧b_i∧b_j and then inverting supplies the needed sign without a repeated wedge factor.

These identities take place in the integral exterior algebra; equivariance and additive Johnson values turn each difference into the displayed map Δ_f(z)=fzf⁻¹z⁻¹. No disk-fixed compatibility of the chosen closed ambient lifts is needed. The finite matrix-lift search, the selected cyclic successor pivots, red(v), basis lift assignment, t_n, c(w,ε), and cancellation order are all correct. The note's warning that t_n need not be a homomorphic or prefix-closed section is accurate; its direct telescoping proof needs neither property.

## 5. Holden's dated conjecture, theorem, and other nearby results

The arXiv landing page inspected on 14 October 2026 lists only v1, submitted 8 July 2026, for 2607.07587. In that version Conjecture 7.1 proposes separating meridian twists for full HK_g=H_g∩K_g. The same paper's §2.3 identifies the twist group with ker(H_g→Out(F_g)). Therefore a product of separating meridian twists is necessarily in that outer-action kernel.

The obstruction lane constructs a commutator of two HI_g lifts of noncommuting free-group partial conjugations. The commutator lies in HK_g because τ_g is abelian, but has nontrivial, indeed infinite-order, outer action for g≥3. This obstructs the conjectured family for the source's full HK_g. It must not be described as finding an incorrect published theorem. Nor can one silently replace the conjecture's group by HK_g∩ker(H_g→Out(F_g)).

Theorem D in the same version assumes g≥4 and the finite-index condition [Γ∩HK_g:K(Γ)]<∞. Its conclusion is rational first-homology vanishing. Corollary 7.2 explicitly assumes Conjecture 7.1. Neither supplies unconditional HK_g generation. The construction here does not use either assertion.

Other inspected sources do not provide a competing claimed complete geometric generating theorem: Holden 2509.03742v1 studies Johnson-detected rational cohomology, and Holden 2607.25191v1 studies BCJ images and cup products. Those homomorphic images are not substitutes for the groups. Hensel's primer records prospective work on Johnson-kernel generators, but that historical statement does not establish current open status.

## 6. Novelty, scope, access, and finality limits

A bounded public search used the phrases “handlebody Johnson kernel generators,” “handlebody Johnson kernel generating,” and “handlebody Schreier Johnson,” plus Omori's normal-generation and Putman's kernel-paper titles. Primary-paper results and the already retained packets were inspected. No claim of exhaustive novelty checking is supported by those searches. The generation theorem of Omori, the image theorem of Morita, the kernel theorem of Johnson, the ambient alphabet of Wajnryb, and the Schreier argument all predate this work. The present explicit combination and any chosen lift formulas must be described as an application/reformulation unless a separate novelty study establishes more.

The cited source statements and public inspection metadata support this report. No copied source documents are included in this public edition.
