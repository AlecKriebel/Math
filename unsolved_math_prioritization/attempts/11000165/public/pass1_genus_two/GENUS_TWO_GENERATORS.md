# Explicit generators for the closed genus-two intersection

## Result and scope

Let V be an oriented genus-two handlebody and S=∂V its closed, connected, oriented boundary. There is no marked point, fixed boundary disk, or retained boundary component. Let H₂ be its handlebody subgroup in Mod(S), and let I₂ be the kernel of the action on H₁(S;ℤ). Write K₂ for the subgroup generated by separating surface twists. The standard closed-surface equality I₂=K₂ identifies the two targets in this genus.

The construction below proves an ordinary, explicitly indexed, generally infinite generating family for H₂∩I₂=H₂∩K₂. It is a direct application of Luft's twist-kernel theorem and Nielsen's rank-two theorem, followed by an elementary splitting argument. No novelty claim is made. It does not assert that separating meridian twists alone generate the intersection, and it does not invoke Omori's or Pitsch's genus-at-least-three theorems.

### The family

Choose three pairwise disjoint, pairwise nonisotopic nonseparating meridian disks D₀,D₁,D₂ forming a theta-graph disk system. Orient their boundaries so that

    a=[∂D₀],  b=[∂D₁],  [∂D₂]=a+b.

Here a,b form an integral basis of L=ker(H₁(S;ℤ)→H₁(V;ℤ)). Put tᵢ=T_{∂Dᵢ}, with all twists right-handed.

For every isotopy class of properly embedded essential disk D⊂V, choose either orientation of ∂D and calculate the unique integers p,q with

    [∂D]=pa+qb.

Define

    B_D = T_{∂D} t₀^{pq−p²} t₁^{pq−q²} t₂^{−pq}.                 (1)

Then

    H₂∩I₂ = H₂∩K₂ = ⟨ B_D : D⊂V an essential properly embedded disk ⟩.  (2)

This is ordinary subgroup generation: every target element is a finite word in the displayed B_D and their inverses. It is not merely normal generation in a larger group. The indexing condition involves disks in the fixed handlebody; it does not require deciding membership in either target group. Each word has one arbitrary meridian twist followed by powers of three fixed meridian twists. The two orientation choices replace (p,q) by (−p,−q), leaving (1) unchanged.

When D is separating, p=q=0 and B_D=T_{∂D}. When D is one of the three chosen reference disks, B_D=1; these three redundant entries may be omitted.

## Proof

### 1. Construction of the reference system

Present V as a regular neighborhood of a theta graph: two vertices joined by three edges. Take a transverse co-core disk in each edge tube. These three disks are pairwise disjoint and essential. Removing any one edge from the theta graph leaves a connected graph, so every disk is nonseparating. Cutting along all three disks yields two balls. Correspondingly, cutting S along their boundaries yields two pairs of pants.

Deleting two of the theta graph's edges leaves a single edge joining the vertices, hence a tree. Cutting V along D₀ and D₁ therefore gives a ball: they are a complete disk system. Their oriented boundary classes form an integral basis a,b of L. The oriented boundary relation in either complementary pair of pants says that the three boundary classes sum to zero. Orient ∂D₂ oppositely to the boundary orientation of that pair of pants and choose the orientations of ∂D₀,∂D₁ consistently; this gives [∂D₂]=a+b. In particular no factor of two or finite-index sublattice occurs.

All tᵢ commute because their twist curves are disjoint. A meridian twist extends across its meridian disk, so all tᵢ and every T_{∂D} belong to H₂.

### 2. Why the full genus-two Torelli intersection lies in the twist group

Let

    Θ:H₂→Out(π₁V)=Out(F₂)

be the natural outer-action homomorphism, and put T₂=ker Θ. Luft's theorem says that T₂ is generated by all meridian twists T_{∂D}. This is the closed, unmarked Out version of the theorem.

If f∈H₂∩I₂, its action on H₁(S;ℤ) is the identity. Since inclusion S→V induces a surjection on first homology, its induced action on H₁(V;ℤ) is also the identity. The natural map

    Out(F₂)→GL(H₁(F₂;ℤ))=GL₂(ℤ)

is injective, indeed an isomorphism, by Nielsen's rank-two theorem. Thus Θ(f)=1 and f∈T₂. Consequently

    H₂∩I₂ = T₂∩I₂.                                            (3)

This is precisely where genus two is used. For rank at least three the analogous outer-action map has a nontrivial kernel, so this argument cannot identify the full handlebody Torelli intersection with a Luft intersection.

### 3. The exact integral homology image

Extend a,b to an integral symplectic basis a,b,x,y of H₁(S;ℤ). Adopt the intersection-pairing convention in which a right twist with oriented homology class v acts by

    z ↦ z + ⟨v,z⟩v,

with ⟨a,x⟩=⟨b,y⟩=1. Choosing the opposite common convention changes every transvection sign and does not change any exponent in (1).

Every disk boundary belongs to the Lagrangian L. If [∂D]=pa+qb, the homology action of its twist has block matrix

    U(Q_D) = [ I₂  Q_D ],    Q_D = [ p²  pq ].
             [  0   I₂ ]            [ pq  q² ]

Here the basis is ordered a,b,x,y. Because all meridian homology classes lie in the same isotropic lattice L,

    U(Q)U(R)=U(Q+R).

Luft's generation theorem therefore gives a homomorphism

    χ:T₂→Sym₂(ℤ)

which records exactly the upper-right block of the boundary homology action. Its kernel is T₂∩I₂. For the three reference twists,

    χ(t₀)=[1 0;0 0],  χ(t₁)=[0 0;0 1],  χ(t₂)=[1 1;1 1].

These are an integral basis of Sym₂(ℤ). Explicitly,

    [r s;s t] = (r−s)χ(t₀)+(t−s)χ(t₁)+sχ(t₂).                 (4)

In coordinates (upper-left,off-diagonal,lower-right), the three columns form the matrix

    [1 0 1]
    [0 0 1]
    [0 1 1],

whose determinant is −1. Thus χ is surjective, not just rationally surjective.

Let A=⟨t₀,t₁,t₂⟩. Since the tᵢ commute, the formula

    σ([r s;s t])=t₀^{r−s}t₁^{t−s}t₂^s

defines a homomorphism Sym₂(ℤ)→A, and (4) proves χσ=id. Moreover χ|_A is injective: if t₀^u t₁^v t₂^w has zero χ-image, the off-diagonal entry gives w=0 and then the diagonal entries give u=v=0. Consequently A≅ℤ³ and χ|_A is an isomorphism onto Sym₂(ℤ).

For Q_D above,

    σ(Q_D)=t₀^{p²−pq}t₁^{q²−pq}t₂^{pq},

so B_D=T_{∂D}σ(Q_D)⁻¹. It follows directly that χ(B_D)=0. Together with extension over V, this proves every displayed generator lies in H₂∩I₂, hence in H₂∩K₂.

### 4. Ordinary generation, with no omitted conjugates

Put W=⟨B_D : D an essential disk⟩. We have proved W⊂ker χ.

Every s∈A preserves the disk set because s extends over V. Its homology action fixes L pointwise. Thus, if [∂D]=pa+qb, then [∂s(D)]=pa+qb as well. The conjugacy formula for Dehn twists and the fact that A is abelian give

    s B_D s⁻¹
      = (s T_{∂D} s⁻¹)(s σ(Q_D)⁻¹ s⁻¹)
      = T_{∂s(D)} σ(Q_D)⁻¹
      = B_{s(D)}.

Hence A normalizes W, and WA is a subgroup of T₂.

For each disk D,

    T_{∂D}=B_D σ(Q_D)∈WA.

The meridian twists generate T₂ by Luft's theorem, so T₂=WA. If f∈ker χ, write f=ws with w∈W and s∈A. Then 0=χ(f)=χ(s), because χ(w)=0. Injectivity of χ|_A implies s=1. Therefore f=w∈W and ker χ=W.

Combining this with (3) proves (2). In fact the argument establishes the split extension

    T₂ = (H₂∩I₂) ⋊ A,    A≅ℤ³.

The conjugation computation explains why no additional ambient conjugates need be appended to family (1). The normalization required for the proof is by A, not an unstated claim that the individual family is invariant under all of H₂.

## What this establishes, and what it leaves open

- It supplies a useful four-factor type of ordinary generating family in the exact closed genus-two model, with integral homology and both target branches coincident.
- It is an application/reformulation of classical input, not a claim of a newly discovered theorem or an exhaustive novelty search.
- It makes no finite-generation, finite-normal-generation, minimality, free-basis, presentation, or effective disk-word-decomposition claim.
- It includes all separating meridian twists, but does not prove they suffice. The outstanding stronger claim would be to express every nonseparating balanced word B_D as a product of separating meridian twists. That additional assertion is neither assumed nor needed here.
- It does not establish any Johnson-kernel generator theorem in genus at least three.
- Pitsch's retained arXiv:math/0605725v1 sets g≥3 in its General conventions. His Luft–Torelli CBP theorem is therefore not used in genus two. Omori's arXiv:1607.06553v1 Theorem 1.2 also explicitly has g≥3 and is not extrapolated.
