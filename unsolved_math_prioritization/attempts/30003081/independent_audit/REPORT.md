## 2. Common setup and kernel

Let X be smooth over C, H a smooth effective Cartier divisor, and A reduced with no component shared with H. Let R be the reduced divisor induced on H. Write F = Der_X(-log A), G = Der_X(-log(A+H)), E = Der_H(-log R), and i:H -> X. These are actual logarithmic tangent sheaves. They must not be silently replaced by a differently twisted reduced homogeneous derivation module. The normalization agrees with equation (1) of the Schenck–Terao–Yoshinaga preprint: the actual tangent sheaf is the sheaf associated to D_0, twisted by O(1); its Theorem 1.5 has the displayed curve quotient.

Locally let H=(z), A=(f), with z not dividing f. A vector field logarithmic along fz preserves both (f) and (z): factor divisibility in a characteristic-zero regular local ring proves this. A tangent field restricts to H, and preserving f mod z implies preservation of its reduced factors. This constructs the map G -> i_*E, rather than assuming that its desired exactness makes it exist.

If such a field restricts to zero, all coefficients, including its normal coefficient, are divisible by z. Write it as z nu. Since f divides z nu(f), coprimality gives f divides nu(f). Conversely, z nu is logarithmic along fz whenever nu is logarithmic along f. Therefore the kernel is exactly F(-H). This local statement glues through the Cartier ideal sheaf; it does not require a global equation z.

## 3. Route 1: product and SNC models

**Accepted under the stated product hypothesis.** If f=g(x) is independent of z, then the tangential logarithmic module is extended from the x-ring, while the normal summand is free. After adding H, the normal summand becomes generated by z partial_z. Restriction is surjective on the tangential part and kills the normal part, and the kernel is exactly zD(g). Flat extension, or coefficientwise power-series expansion, supplies the needed module identification.

For an SNC divisor, adapted coordinates give g=x_1...x_r, so the displayed generators verify all assertions directly. E has rank dim(X)-1. On a curve H, preserving a reduced divisor R means T_H(-R), hence O_H(-K_H-R). In higher dimension that line bundle has the determinant role in the locally free case and cannot be the whole generic-rank quotient. No extension from SNC/product geometry to arbitrary quasihomogeneous pairs has been made.

## 4. Route 2: credited seven-plane obstruction

**Accepted, including its projective and higher-dimensional scope.** The seven linear factors are exactly those of [Abe–Kawanoue, Example 4.3(3), arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00305). Their finite Euler-cokernel result is credited; the author's explicit quadratic reconstruction is independently verifiable.

In the affine chart w=1 of P^3 take H=(z) and the six-factor deletion

f = xy(x-z)(y-z)(x-y+z)(x-y-z).

The restriction has reduced equation xy(x-y), and eta=x(x-y) partial_x is tangent to each reduced component. The vertex [0:0:0:1] is an actual projective point in this chart. A hypothetical analytic or algebraic germ lift yields a formal lift; its degree-two coefficient part still preserves every linear ideal because those ideals are homogeneous. Thus a homogeneous quadratic contradiction proves germ nonliftability, without assuming that every germ lift was polynomial.

Tangency to x,y,z and the prescribed restriction force

u=x(x-y+az), v=byz, t=z(cx+dy+ez).

The x-z condition gives d=-1 and c+e=1+a. The y-z condition gives c=0 and b=d+e, so e=1+a and b=a. On x-y+z=0, the tangency expression u-v+t is -2z(y-z), independently of a. It is a nonzero polynomial over C. Therefore no lift exists. The unused seventh tangency is harmless: retaining its factor preserves the exact credited example.

The hyperplanes are smooth, and a local linear arrangement becomes a central arrangement after discarding unit factors and translating to the point. Thus the requisite local quasihomogeneity holds. For n>3, specializing additional smooth coordinates preserves the equations and the prescribed field, so extra directions cannot supply a lift. Conversely, projectivizing only the original three-variable cone to P^2 removes its affine vertex. The P^3 argument does not contradict the plane theorem.

An independent rational calculation introduced unknown divisibility multipliers for each linear form, rather than using the author's remainder substitution. It recovered target dimensions 1,3,5,7,9 and image ranks 1,2,4,7,9 in degrees 1 through 5. The augmented quadratic system for this exact eta has rank one greater than its coefficient system. These checks corroborate the proof. They do not by themselves show that no later-degree defect exists; the global total-length-two claim remains correctly attributed to the paper.

The conclusion is failure of surjectivity onto the specified natural sheaf E. It is not a disproof of every possible higher-dimensional theorem and not a resolution of the original open-ended request.

## 5. Route 3: Tjurina-torsion obstruction

**Accepted with precisely the stated local target and image.** In the convergent or formal regular local ring O, f_0=f mod z is nonzero. For a nonzero product of powers of irreducible factors, preserving its principal ideal is equivalent in characteristic zero to preserving every reduced factor. Localizing at a height-one factor and dividing off its repeated power proves the nontrivial direction; factoriality brings divisibility back to O/(z). Units do not change this conclusion.

Given eta in D((f_0)_red), the identity eta(f_0)=b_0 f_0 determines b_0 uniquely. Lift eta to a z-tangent delta and lift b_0 to b. Then delta(f)-bf=zc. In M_f=O/(f,partial f), the class [c] is killed by z. A change of b adds a multiple of f to c. A change of delta preserving the same restriction is z nu and adds nu(f), hence zero in M_f. The same calculation establishes linearity over O/(z).

If [c]=0, write c=af+nu(f). Replacing delta by delta-z nu gives derivative (b+za)f and keeps the normal coefficient divisible by z, so it is a logarithmic lift. Conversely, compare delta with any logarithmic lift: their difference is z nu, and their logarithmic scalar coefficients agree modulo z because f_0 is nonzero. This places c in (f,partial f). Thus the kernel of the obstruction is exactly the restriction image, and the restriction cokernel is isomorphic to im(ob).

The map need not surject onto all z-torsion. An independent small control makes the distinction concrete: take f=x(x-z). Then M_f=C{x,z}/(x,z) is one-dimensional and entirely killed by z. Nevertheless D_H(x) is generated by x partial_x, which lifts to x partial_x+z partial_z. With delta=x partial_x and b_0=2, the obstruction numerator is xz, so c=x is zero in M_f. Thus torsion is nonzero while restriction is onto. The non-zero-divisor condition is sufficient and genuinely unnecessary.

This is a local obstruction presentation; the packet correctly declines to globalize M_f without the transition data and twists. Its later global object is the actual cokernel Q.

## 6. Route 4: complete characteristic-class defect

**Accepted on the stated projective variety P^n.** From the constructed restriction map and proven kernel, the four-term exact sequence gives

[F(-H)] - [G] + [i_*E] = [i_*Q].

The sign is correct. GRR for a smooth Cartier embedding gives ch(i_*E)=i_*(ch(E) td(N_H/X)^(-1)), and for the normal line bundle td(N)^(-1)=(1-exp(-h_H))/h_H. This is a power series in the Chow ring, not an inversion of a potentially noninvertible class. Coherent logarithmic sheaves admit finite locally free resolutions on smooth projective space, so their Chern characters are defined without freeness assumptions.

Consequently the displayed Delta is exactly ch(i_*Q). If it vanishes, HRR makes the entire twisted Euler-characteristic polynomial vanish. For t sufficiently large, a nonzero coherent sheaf Q(t) is globally generated and has no higher cohomology; global generation of a nonzero sheaf requires a nonzero section. This contradicts the zero polynomial. Hence Q=0. The reverse implication is immediate. The same positivity argument proves the complete-Hilbert-polynomial formulation.

The proof uses the already specified map and kernel. An arbitrary numerical identity among unrelated sheaves would not establish an exact sequence. It also requires the full defect: the Koszul resolution of a point in P^3 gives ch(O_p)=(0,0,0,[p]), so rank and lower terms cannot detect it. Independent binomial-moment computation reproduced this character.

The warning about comparing logarithmic and CSM classes is appropriate. [Liao's paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3843) assumes local quasihomogeneity and freeness in the relevant higher-dimensional comparison; those conditions cannot be deleted merely because CSM classes are additive. No unconditional vanishing of Delta is proved. The standard GRR, Serre and Riemann–Roch theorems are imported, not formally verified in this audit.

## 7. Route 5: depth and reflexive extension

**Accepted under both explicit hypotheses.** Suppose F and G are locally free, and restriction is onto at every point of H of codimension at most one. The quotient C=G/F(-H) is killed by z because G is contained in F. It is therefore the pushforward of C_H, and the kernel theorem embeds C_H into E.

At a point p, let O=O_X,p have dimension d. The two free modules in the presentation of C_H have rank dim(X), not necessarily d; the author correctly keeps these two dimensions separate. The depth lemma gives depth_O(C_H)>=d-1. For a module killed by z, its depth over O equals its depth over the regular local ring O/(z). If nonzero, this is maximal depth on H; regularity supplies finite projective dimension, and Auslander–Buchsbaum gives projective dimension zero. The zero module causes no exception. Hence C_H is locally free.

E is reflexive: inside the rational tangent space, membership is equivalent to regularity and logarithmic tangency at every height-one prime. A finite torsion-free module over a normal domain equals the intersection of these localizations precisely when reflexive. Since C_H and E agree away from a codimension-at-least-two closed subset of H, reflexive extension makes their given inclusion an isomorphism everywhere. [Auslander–Buchsbaum](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/090U), [reflexive modules](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVT).

The source of codimension-one surjectivity is explicitly conditional: a simultaneous local product with a plane pair satisfying the imported plane theorem. Derivations in each extra smooth direction are free summands, and restriction/kernel are checked coefficientwise. The proof never assumes that local quasihomogeneity forces this product structure or ambient local freeness.

For hyperplane multiarrangements, [Abe–Denham v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04816) has a stronger free-surjection theorem using only freeness of the deletion and the appropriate Euler multiplicity. The packet attributes it correctly. Its [published record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2026.103114) is corroborated by the publisher's indexed record. The inspected v3 Theorems 1.2 and 4.8 support the stated free-surjection and Ext-continuation descriptions; the full paper is not independently proved here.

## 8. Separate unexpected-hypersurface discussion

**Accepted only in its stated codimension-two scope.** [Trok's v1 Corollary 4.15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02397) uses a general codimension-two linear space Q and splitting degrees of the reduced homogeneous logarithmic derivation sheaf. The character formula is sum_i max(0,d-a_i), with these exact degrees. Twisting to the actual tangent sheaf would shift splitting degrees and cannot be done silently.

Choose two normal coordinates to Q. Degree-d forms in I_Q^(d-1) consist of d+1 normal-only monomials and d(n-1) monomials with one tangent-coordinate factor. The dimension is therefore nd+1 for d>=1. Subtracting the corresponding number of conditions from h^0(I_Z(d)), with a zero floor, gives exactly the packet's expected dimension. Combining it with the imported duality yields the stated ordinary unexpectedness inequality for this Q; it does not establish Trok's duality anew.

In P^3 at d=3, a double line has ten conditions and a double point has four. Thus the codimension-two result is not a general-fat-point characterization when n>2. The independent calculation checked 96 normal-degree count cases. The inspected v1 Theorem 5.27 does display the opposite inequality from its following dimension-count argument. The packet appropriately excludes that theorem from its inference; this audit does not certify all of Section 5.

The [Janasz–Malara–Tutaj-Gasińska preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10772v1) likewise explicitly concerns prescribed multiplicity along a general codimension-two space and presents a sufficient criterion. The record identifies v1 with submission on 13 November 2025; the HTML itself displays an August 2026 manuscript date. Neither timestamp changes the mathematical scope. Only the relevant introduction and statements were cross-checked here, not all proofs. Neither paper is evidence that Schenck's general deletion question has been fully answered.
