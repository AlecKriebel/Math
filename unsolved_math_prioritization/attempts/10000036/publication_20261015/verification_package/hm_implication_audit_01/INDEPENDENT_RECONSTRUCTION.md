# Independent pre-source reconstruction

2026-10-15 06:34 UTC. This note records independent reasoning from the lead before reading the published construction or any classical-audit verdict.

1. Let T be a one-ended spanning tree of the square lattice and T* its complementary dual tree, also one-ended. Deleting an edge t of T has a finite component A. Both A and its complement are connected in the primal lattice because they are connected in T. Thus its dual edge boundary should be a finite simple dual circuit: its only absent T* edge is t*, and all its other edges form the unique simple T* path between the endpoints of t*. This needs a careful planar proof, including exclusion of degree-four boundary vertices.
2. Along a finite simple path in a one-ended tree, orient all edges toward the unique end. The path is the union of two chains oriented toward a common highest vertex. Along either chain the finite descendant set sizes strictly increase. Hence each possible finite-side size appears at most twice. This applies to the subdivided dual tree if HM's site flip rate is exactly a decreasing summable function of those sizes.
3. A dual circuit with one initially open midpoint and otherwise initially closed dual-tree sites would then have conditionally expected final open-site count at most 1 plus twice the sum of flip rates. Fatou would make the liminf count finite almost surely for any tree-measurable exhausting sequence of such circuits.
4. The finite components along the root's ray increase and exhaust the lattice. Their boundaries escape every fixed finite set, so a subsequence of bounded-open-count circuits can be chosen disjoint as site sets. Conditioned on the final configuration, their open portions are disjoint bounded-cardinality separators. Independent p-thinning fails to cross infinitely many such separators almost surely for every p<1. Empty separators only strengthen extinction.
5. Countability must be handled: prove extinction for all lattice roots and rational p<1 simultaneously, then use monotonicity for arbitrary p. No independence is required among the original flips across different cycles for the Fatou step; independent thinning on disjoint separator sites suffices.
6. Full invariance and mixing require care: HM's parity-based site construction uses a random phase and can carry a periodic phase detectable from the entire configuration, so HM's site law itself may be translation invariant but nonmixing. A direct bond law defined intrinsically from T and T* avoids the random parity phase, provided the UST law is mixing under full lattice automorphisms. Whether this is a genuinely immediate rephrasing of HM must be separated from the theorem actually printed.

Potential failure points to falsify: exact definition of b and flip probabilities; whether the dual path sits entirely in closed sites; simple/nested boundaries in the original lattice; transfer from site p_c=1 to bond p_c=1 via HM Lemma 3.1; whether any published statement explicitly claims the post-flip p_c=1 conclusion. The same d=2 mechanism does not supply d>=3.
