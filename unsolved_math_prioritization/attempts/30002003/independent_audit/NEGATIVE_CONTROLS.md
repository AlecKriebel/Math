# Adversarial controls and rejected inferences

All controls are scoped to the audited object and the author's claimed routes. Passing them does not certify the qualified conjecture.

## Independently implemented algebraic and numerical controls

- **Four-variable substitution:** Removing x5^2 leaves x1 irreducible but not prime, since x3 and x4 are nonzero modulo x1 and their product vanishes. The factorial proof would fail, and the resulting ring is not a UFD.
- **Loss of strictness:** Multiplication by C* turns the cone's positive Euler deficit 1/3 into zero. A singular factor does not force positive product deficit without positive Euler hypotheses.
- **Wrong invariant:** E_st(X)-E(X)=(z-1)z^3/(1+z+z^2) is nonzero although its Euler specialization is zero. The Euler counterexample does not disprove an E-function equality criterion.
- **Wrong discrepancy weight:** Discrepancy 2 means denominator 3, not 2.
- **Wrong proposed counterexample:** The cone itself has e_st=4/3 and e=1. It satisfies the conjectured strict inequality.
- **Three degeneration shortcuts:** The proper flat quadric family changes ordinary Euler (6 to 5), stringy Euler (6 to 16/3), and deficit (0 to 1/3).
- **Insufficient positivity:** A positive weight multiplying a nonnegative integer need not be at least one; 1/2 times 1 is 1/2.
- **Positive factor comparison:** Multiplication by P^1 preserves the nonzero deficit, doubling 1/3 rather than killing it.
