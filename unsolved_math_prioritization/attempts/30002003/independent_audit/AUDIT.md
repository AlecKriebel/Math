## 2. Independent counterexample proof

### 2.1 Dimension, irreducibility, singular locus, normality

Put q=x1*x2+x3*x4+x5^2 and R=C[x1,...,x5]/(q). Its symmetric quadratic matrix has rank five. A reducible homogeneous quadratic over C is a product of linear forms, whose symmetric matrix has rank at most two. Thus q is irreducible, R is a domain, and C=Spec R has dimension four.

The gradient is (x2,x1,x4,x3,2*x5); it vanishes precisely at the origin. The tangent dimension there is five, so the origin is singular, while every other point is smooth. As a hypersurface in a regular ring, R is Cohen-Macaulay and satisfies S2. Its isolated singular point has codimension four, so R satisfies R1. Serre's criterion proves normality.

Adding the invertible variable t raises dimension to five. The same Jacobian criterion gives Sing(X)={0} x G_m; the tangent dimension at such a point is six. X is therefore genuinely singular, and no completeness assumption is being smuggled into its definition.

### 2.2 Factoriality and class group

R[x1^-1] is isomorphic to C[x1,x1^-1,x3,x4,x5], by eliminating x2. It is a UFD. Moreover

    R/(x1) = C[x2,x3,x4,x5]/(x3*x4+x5^2)

is a domain: the residual quadratic has rank three, so the same linear-factor rank argument proves its irreducibility. Consequently x1 is a prime element, not merely an irreducible element.

Nagata's factoriality criterion applies because R is a noetherian domain and the localization is obtained by inverting a prime element. Thus R is a UFD. Equivalently, the normal-domain localization sequence makes Cl(R) generated by the sole prime divisor above x1, and that divisor is principal. Its coefficient is one because the prime ideal is generated by x1; localizing at its generic point gives a DVR with uniformizer x1. Hence Cl(R)=0.

The polynomial ring R[t] is factorial by Gauss's lemma; localizing at t preserves factoriality. Thus R[t,t^-1] is a UFD, its divisor class group vanishes, and all its local rings are factorial. This verifies the needed property without importing an unproved assertion that this cone is factorial. [Nagata](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AFU)

A useful adversarial control is the four-variable cone x1*x2+x3*x4=0. There x1 remains irreducible by grading, but its quotient has the nonzero zero divisors x3 and x4. It is not prime; the corresponding ring is not a UFD. The fifth variable and residual rank-three argument are substantive.

### 2.3 Explicit reductive group and Borel open orbit

Over C a linear change of coordinates puts q in the form 2xy+w1^2+w2^2+w3^2. Explicitly take x=x1/2, y=x2, w1=(x3+x4)/2, w2=(x3-x4)/(2i), w3=x5. Let G0=SO(q), the connected reductive complex special orthogonal group in dimension five.

For v in C^3 define

    y' = y,
    w' = w+yv,
    x' = x-w.v-y(v.v)/2.

These transformations preserve q, have determinant one, compose by addition of v, and are unipotent. They form the unipotent radical of the parabolic stabilizing the isotropic x-axis. They belong to a Borel contained in that parabolic. The torus diag(lambda,lambda^-1,1,1,1) belongs to the same Borel.

On y!=0 choose v=-w/y; then w'=0 and x'=q/(2y). Choose lambda=y to make y'=1. On q=0 the resulting point is (0,1,0,0,0). Thus this Borel acts transitively on the nonempty open subset {q=0,y!=0}, proving sphericity directly. This also verifies the analogous open orbit on q=t for the degeneration in Approach 5.

SO(q) is transitive on all nonzero isotropic vectors. One proof extends an isometry between their hyperbolic planes; any determinant sign can be corrected on the nondegenerate three-dimensional orthogonal complement. Therefore C has exactly two G0-orbits: its vertex and its complement. The stabilizer of the x-axis vector contains a maximal unipotent subgroup (the parabolic unipotent radical and the maximal unipotent of its SO(3) Levi factor). This also confirms the stronger horospherical assertion.

For X use G=G0 x G_m, with the second factor multiplying t. The group is connected reductive, and the product of the preceding Borel open orbit with G_m is open and dense in X. Its two G-orbits are (C minus {0}) x G_m and {0} x G_m. The latter is the unique closed orbit.

### 2.4 Gorenstein property, resolution, and discrepancy

Both C and X are hypersurfaces in smooth varieties, so they are Gorenstein. Blow up the vertex of C. The strict transform is the total space of O_Q(-1) over the smooth projective quadric Q=Q^3. This is smooth; its exceptional divisor is the zero section Q. The map is proper and an isomorphism off the vertex, so it is a log resolution with one smooth exceptional divisor.

All five blowup charts were independently checked: after dividing by the square of the exceptional parameter, the strict-transform equation is q with one projective coordinate set equal to one. Its equation and partial derivatives generate the unit ideal. In the x1 chart, write x1=lambda, x3=lambda*a, x4=lambda*b, x5=lambda*c, and x2=-lambda*(a*b+c^2). The hypersurface canonical residue form

    dx1 wedge dx3 wedge dx4 wedge dx5 / x1

pulls back to lambda^2 d(lambda) wedge da wedge db wedge dc. Hence the discrepancy is exactly 2. This independently agrees with ambient adjunction: the blowup of A^5 has discrepancy 4 and the defining hypersurface has multiplicity 2.

For X take the product resolution with G_m. Its single exceptional divisor is Q^3 x G_m, its discrepancy remains 2, and its exceptional divisor is smooth. The inequality 2>-1 verifies log terminality on this log resolution. In particular the stringy invariant used below is defined; this conclusion does not rely merely on an unsupported slogan about spherical varieties.

### 2.5 Euler conventions and stringy computation

Use compactly supported Euler characteristic in the resolution formula. For a log resolution with exceptional strata E_J^o and discrepancies a_j, the formula is the sum of e_c(E_J^o) times the product of 1/(a_j+1). This is the limit of the stringy E-function, not substitution into uncancelled 0/0 factors. The compact-support convention and the discrepancy definition were inspected in Batyrev-Moreau, Definitions 1.5 and 5.2. [BM primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1203.0671v2)

The punctured cone is a Zariski locally trivial G_m-bundle over Q^3. The standard affine-cell decomposition of Q^3 has one cell in each dimension 0,1,2,3, giving e(Q^3)=4. Since e_c(G_m)=e_c(A^1)-e_c({0})=0, the punctured cone has Euler number zero. Therefore e_c(C)=1, and the single-divisor resolution gives e_st(C)=4/(2+1)=4/3.

On the product resolution each exceptional stratum is its old counterpart times G_m. Discrepancies are unchanged and Euler characteristics multiply, so every term is zero. Thus e_st(X)=0=e_c(X).

This is also the answer for ordinary topological Euler characteristic. More directly than appealing to its general agreement with e_c for complex algebraic varieties, C contracts continuously to its vertex by scalar multiplication. Thus X has the homotopy type of C*, namely a circle, and its usual Euler characteristic is zero. There is no convention-dependent escape from the counterexample.

As a stronger arithmetic control, write z=uv and E_Q=1+z+z^2+z^3. Then E(C)=z^4 and

    E_st(C)-E(C)=z^3/(1+z+z^2),
    E_st(X)-E(X)=(z-1)*z^3/(1+z+z^2).

The second expression is nonzero as a rational function but vanishes at z=1. The example concerns Euler equality only; it does not assert equality of E-functions.

### 2.6 The exact failed hypothesis

The closed orbit {0} x G_m is not projective. For example, its regular function t is nonconstant, whereas all global regular functions on a connected projective complex variety are constant. Equivalently, it is a positive-dimensional affine torus and is not complete.

The cone C alone has a projective closed orbit, a point, and strict inequality 4/3>1. Multiplying by the smooth zero-Euler torus destroys the strictness exactly while losing the relevant closed-orbit property. Therefore this example cannot be promoted to a counterexample to BM Conjecture 6.7.

## 3. Review of the five retained routes

1. **Toric route: valid special case.** Cartier data for each invariant divisor yield integral dual vectors. They split the ray lattice as a direct summand, rather than merely establish linear independence or simpliciality. The resulting affine charts are smooth. Colored spherical geometry is not eliminated by this argument, as the explicitly factorial singular horospherical cone demonstrates.

2. **Cone route: valid with its stated hypotheses.** The degree-one complete section ring and very ample embedding identify the vertex blowup with the line bundle resolution. Q-Gorensteinness is assumed, not inferred from normality. Adjunction gives a=r-1; Euler calculation gives e_st=e(V)/r. Under odd-cohomology vanishing, ample powers give e(V)>=d+1. Kodaira vanishing supplies Hilbert-polynomial roots -1,...,-(r-1), hence r<=d+1. In the equality case its normalized polynomial gives h^0(L)=d+1, so the very ample embedding is P^d with O(1). The projective-cone complement is a line bundle, and the corollary follows. No reduction of general spherical slices to such cones was proved. The known cone calculation is consistent with De Deyn, Proposition 3.1.

3. **Orbitwise route: valid conditional reduction and upper bound.** Connectedness preserves each exceptional component; equivariance makes fiber Euler values constant on G-orbits; finiteness and constructible Euler pushforward justify the displayed sum. The homogeneous-space fixed-point argument is valid, including possibly disconnected H: tori lie in H^0 and are conjugate there. With T contained in H the fixed points identify with N_G(T)/N_H(T), a finite set. Projective orbits have positive Euler number. The missing local lower/strict weighted bounds are genuinely unproved. The derived upper bound e_st(X)<=e(Y) uses nonnegative stratum Euler numbers and discrepancies >=0, and does not imply the desired lower bound.

4. **Product route: valid, with the split result above.** Product resolutions establish multiplicativity and the deficit identity. Positive Euler factors preserve a positive deficit under the stated assumptions. Zero Euler factors do not. The group, factoriality, and closed-orbit checks are essential and have been supplied explicitly.

5. **Degeneration route: valid negative control.** The projective family q(x)=t*z^2 is proper. Its homogeneous algebra is free over C[t,x1,x2,x3,x4,z] with basis 1,x5, hence flat over C[t]; localization and the degree-zero direct summand preserve flatness on projective charts. Smooth fibers are Q^4 with Euler 6. The special projective cone has ordinary Euler 5 and stringy Euler 16/3. Its vertex chart is the audited factorial cone; its remaining charts are smooth. The explicit Borel normalization proves sphericity of both fiber types. These invariants and their deficit change despite proper flatness, so naive transport fails. No general specialization inequality is obtained.

Standard foundations are still used: Serre's criterion, hypersurface Cohen-Macaulay/Gorenstein properties, Nagata's criterion, Gauss's lemma, canonical adjunction, the log-resolution criterion for klt, Borel/parabolic structure of SO(5), constructible Euler integration, and Kodaira vanishing. They are not replaced by finite computations. The counterexample has independent explicit proofs of the specific places where these inputs apply.
