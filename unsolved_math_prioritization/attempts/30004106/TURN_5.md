# Turn 5: a symmetric induced-endotrivial algebra for elementary abelian groups

**Original universal symmetry question unresolved, 5/5 substantive author turns.** Let E be a nontrivial elementary abelian p-group, and let k be any field of characteristic p. This turn identifies a closed subalgebra of the actual completed Green ring of E, proves a bounded decomposition of its completion, and proves its symmetry. An explicit module shows that the subalgebra need not be the whole Green algebra.

The prerequisites are the split Green algebra, its dimension-weighted norm and stable quotient from SOURCE_SCOPE.md, and the endotrivial growth and p-group splitting results proved with credited inputs in TURN_2.md. In particular, Benson's Lemma 4.4.7 and Theorem 4.4.8 supply the subexponential dimension growth for powers of an endotrivial class. Mackey decomposition and tensor-induction identities are classical; they also appear in Benson's induction/restriction discussion, including Section 4.10. The subgroup-lattice calculation below is proved explicitly. No novelty claim is made.

## 1. The actual modules and their independence

For each nontrivial subgroup H of E, write T(H) for the abelian group of stable endotrivial classes over k. Let M_t^H be the unique indecomposable nonprojective core representing t in T(H), and set

    a_H=[E:H],    I(H,t)=Ind_H^E M_t^H,
    b_(H,t)=[I(H,t)]/a_H in A_st(E).                    (1)

The stable Green algebra is the quotient by the closed span of projective indecomposables. In a p-group there is only one indecomposable projective, the regular module. Its quotient norm is the coordinate dimension-weighted l1 norm on the other indecomposables.

We first justify that all the symbols in (1) are distinct actual indecomposable nonprojective modules. This is necessary: an abstract intersection algebra alone would not establish a theorem about a Green subalgebra.

**Indecomposability.** Choose a complement K so that E=H times K. As an E-module,

    I(H,t)=M_t^H tensor_k kK.

The endomorphism algebra is

    End_(kE)(I(H,t)) = End_(kH)(M_t^H) tensor_k (kK)^op. (2)

The finite-dimensional endomorphism algebra D_0=End_(kH)(M_t^H) is local by indecomposability and the Fitting lemma. The augmentation ideal J_K of kK is nilpotent, and kK/J_K=k. The two ideals rad(D_0) tensor kK and D_0 tensor J_K in (2) commute as ideals and are nilpotent. Their sum is nilpotent: every sufficiently long product has enough factors from one of the two ideals to vanish. The quotient by this sum is the division algebra D_0/rad(D_0). Thus (2) is local. In particular it has no nontrivial idempotent, so I(H,t) is indecomposable. This proof works over an arbitrary k, without absolute indecomposability assumptions.

**Recovering H.** Let C be a subgroup of E of order p. If C is contained in H, abelian Mackey decomposition gives

    Res_C^E I(H,t) = (Res_C^H M_t^H)^(direct sum a_H).

Restriction of an endotrivial module is endotrivial up to projectives. Its restriction to nontrivial C is not projective: an endotrivial module N has (dim N)^2 congruent to 1 modulo p, whereas a projective kC-module has dimension divisible by p. Hence this restriction is nonprojective. If C is not contained in H, C intersect H=1, and Mackey decomposition expresses Res_C^E I(H,t) as a direct sum of modules induced from the trivial subgroup. It is therefore free over kC. Consequently

    {C of order p : Res_C I(H,t) is nonprojective}
       = {C of order p : C is contained in H}.           (3)

This set is nonempty and determines H, since H is generated by its order-p subgroups. It also proves that I(H,t) itself is nonprojective. Thus modules arising from distinct H cannot be isomorphic.

For a fixed H, Res_H I(H,t) is the direct sum of a_H copies of M_t^H. Krull-Schmidt uniqueness then shows that I(H,t) and I(H,s) are isomorphic only if M_t^H and M_s^H are isomorphic, equivalently t=s. This completes the independence proof.

Define w_H(t)=dim_k M_t^H. Because of that independence, the closed coordinate span B of the b_(H,t), for all nontrivial H and all t, is a well-defined Banach subspace of A_st(E), and its norm is exactly

    || sum_(H,t) c_(H,t) b_(H,t) ||
        = sum_(H,t) |c_(H,t)| w_H(t).                   (4)

There are finitely many H, but the sets T(H) may be infinite.

## 2. Normalized multiplication and the intersection semilattice

For nontrivial H,K put L=H intersect K. The tensor-induction formula and abelian Mackey decomposition give

    I(H,t) tensor I(K,s)
       = (Ind_L^E (Res_L^H M_t^H tensor Res_L^K M_s^K))
           ^(direct sum [E:HK]).                        (5)

If L is nontrivial, the module in parentheses inside Ind has endotrivial core indexed by

    res_L^H(t) res_L^K(s) in T(L).

All its remaining summands are L-projective, and induction takes them to E-projectives. If L=1, all of (5) is E-projective. Using

    [E:HK][E:L]=[E:H][E:K],

equation (5) therefore gives the exact stable multiplication

    b_(H,t) b_(K,s) = b_(L, res_L^H(t) res_L^K(s)), if L!=1,
                   = 0,                              if L=1. (6)

Duality sends b_(H,t) to b_(H,t^(-1)). In particular B is a closed unital Banach *-subalgebra, with identity b_(E,1)=[k]. Its multiplication is continuous for (4), also directly because restriction cannot increase the dimension of the nonprojective core and tensor core dimensions are submultiplicative.

Put e_H=b_(H,1) for H!=1 and e_1=0. Here 1 in T(H) is represented by the trivial kH-module. These elements are self-adjoint and

    e_H e_K=e_(H intersect K),     e_E=1.              (7)

Let mu(L,H) be the incidence Möbius function of the finite subgroup lattice of E. For each nontrivial H define

    p_H=sum_(L<=H) mu(L,H)e_L.                          (8)

The term L=1 vanishes because e_1=0. The p_H are nonzero pairwise orthogonal self-adjoint idempotents and sum to 1. Here is a finite algebra proof rather than a reliance on an abstract completion. The e_H for H!=1 are linearly independent by Section 1. On their span define, for each nontrivial J, the multiplicative functional e_K -> 1_(J<=K). The resulting zeta matrix is triangular with diagonal 1 after a linear extension of the subgroup order. It is invertible. Möbius inversion says that the image of p_H at this functional is delta_(J,H). All the assertions about (8) follow from this injective finite-dimensional transform. In addition,

    p_H e_K = p_H if H<=K, and 0 otherwise.             (9)

The same relations and (6) show, for any generator b_(K,s),

    p_H b_(K,s) = 0,                         if H is not contained in K,
                = p_H b_(H,res_H^K(s)),       if H<=K. (10)

For the second identity use p_H e_H=p_H and e_H b_(K,s)=b_(H,res_H^K(s)). For the first use b_(K,s)=e_K b_(K,s).

## 3. The completed blocks: bounded injection and bounded inverse

For each H!=1 let C_H be the finite constant

    C_H=sum_(1!=L<=H) |mu(L,H)| >=1.

Let l1(T(H),w_H) denote the dimension-weighted group algebra of TURN_2.md. Define initially on finitely supported sums

    Phi_H(delta_t)=p_H b_(H,t).                         (11)

By (6) and the idempotent property this is a unital *-homomorphism into the block p_H B, whose identity is p_H. Its expansion is

    Phi_H(delta_t)=sum_(1!=L<=H) mu(L,H)b_(L,res_L^H(t)). (12)

The coefficient in the H-level is exactly delta_t, because mu(H,H)=1. Distinct t at that level are distinct actual module coordinates by Section 1. Consequently, for every finitely supported complex f=sum_t c_t delta_t,

    ||f||_(l1(w_H)) <= ||Phi_H(f)||_B
                      <= C_H ||f||_(l1(w_H)).           (13)

The lower bound uses only the H-level coordinates in (4). The upper bound is the triangle inequality applied to (12), together with

    w_L(res_L^H(t)) <= w_H(t),

since the restricted module has unchanged vector-space dimension and its nonprojective endotrivial core is a direct summand. Collisions of restricted classes at lower levels can only reduce that upper bound and do not affect the leading-coordinate lower bound.

Thus Phi_H extends uniquely to a bounded map on the completed weighted l1 algebra. It is injective, bounded below by 1, and has closed range; its inverse on that range has norm at most 1. Formula (10) says that its range contains every projected generator p_H b_(K,s). Their linear span is dense in p_H B, since multiplication by the finite element p_H is a bounded projection. The range is closed, so Phi_H is onto p_H B.

For completeness, the entire finite-product decomposition is a bounded Banach *-isomorphism, not just an algebraic decomposition. Give the finite product of the l1 factors the sum norm, put C=max_H C_H and N=#{H!=1}, and define

    Phi((f_H)_H)=sum_H Phi_H(f_H).

Then ||Phi||<=C. Conversely, for b in B its H-coordinate in the inverse is Phi_H^(-1)(p_H b), and

    sum_H ||Phi_H^(-1)(p_H b)||
       <= sum_H ||p_H b||
       <= (sum_H ||p_H||) ||b||
       <= (sum_H C_H) ||b||.                            (14)

There are only finitely many H. Orthogonality and sum p_H=1 prove that these formulas are mutual inverses. Therefore

    B ≅ product_(1!=H<=E) l1(T(H),w_H)                 (15)

as unital Banach *-algebras, with the explicit bounds (13)-(14).

## 4. Symmetry and the characters

TURN_2.md proves, using the credited endotrivial gamma theorem, that

    lim_(n->infinity) w_H(t^n)^(1/n)=1

for every t in T(H). A bounded character of l1(T(H),w_H) must have unit-modulus value at delta_t: apply the bound to its positive powers and to powers of t^(-1). Conversely every unitary group character extends continuously, because w_H>=1. All characters respect inversion and coefficient conjugation. Thus every factor in (15) is symmetric, and the finite product is symmetric. Equivalently, all bounded characters of B have the following explicit form, indexed by a nontrivial H and a unitary group character chi:T(H)->S1:

    s_(H,chi)(b_(K,t)) = chi(res_H^K(t)), if H<=K,
                      = 0,              otherwise.      (16)

These formulas respect duality. On the actual unnormalized module I(K,t), the value in (16) is multiplied by a_K=[E:K].

Return to the full Green algebra A(E), and let B_full be the closed coordinate span of the regular projective module kE and every I(H,t). The p-group splitting of TURN_2.md, with e=[kE]/|E| and J(x)=x-D(x)e for a canonical nonprojective representative, identifies

    B_full ≅ C e times J(B).                            (17)

The inverse of the stable projection on J(B) is bounded with 1<=||Jx||/||x||<=2. Including the scalar factor yields another bounded Banach *-isomorphism. It follows that B_full is symmetric as well.

This includes the permutation modules k[E/H], the endotrivial cores for E itself, and products and completed linear combinations of these induced-endotrivial modules. The conclusion concerns intrinsic symmetry of this closed algebra. In particular, restrictions of ambient characters to this unital subalgebra must be Hermitian. It does not say that arbitrary subalgebra spectra equal ambient spectra, or that the entire Green completion is symmetric.

## 5. The family can be proper: a two-dimensional module outside it

Let E=C2^3, and let k=F8=F2[z]/(z^3+z+1). Let N be the two-dimensional module on which the three distinguished generators act by

    1+J,    1+z J,    1+z^2 J,
    where J=[[0,1],[0,0]].                              (18)

The matrices commute and square to 1. Each nonidentity element of E acts by 1+lambda J for a nonzero lambda in F8, because 1,z,z^2 are F2-independent. Thus on every order-two subgroup C the restriction of N is the unique nontrivial two-dimensional Jordan module, which is free over kC.

N is indecomposable: its endomorphism ring is the centralizer of J, namely {a·1+bJ:a,b in F8}, a local algebra. It is nonprojective over kE because a projective module over the local ring kE is free and hence has dimension divisible by 8, whereas dim N=2.

Every I(H,t) in our family has a nonprojective restriction to at least one order-two subgroup, by (3). Therefore N is not any I(H,t), and it is not projective. Since the Green norm is a coordinate l1 norm, a missing indecomposable basis vector is not in the closed span of the others. Hence N lies outside B_full, proving the family proper in this actual group and field.

This example is not asserted to have a non-Hermitian species. Being free on all order-two subgroups does not imply projectivity over the higher-rank elementary abelian group E. The original universal symmetry question remains unresolved even for the directions omitted by this positive theorem.

## 6. Finite controls and final gap

verify_turn5.py checks exact subgroup-lattice Möbius identities and normalized index identities for several elementary abelian groups, finite coherent group-functor models of the block maps, and the F8 module in (18). The finite auxiliary groups used for block controls are not claimed to equal the actual endotrivial groups. These controls support formulas; the actual-module and completed-norm arguments are the written proofs above.

The five-turn endpoint is **unsolved** for the original question: no actual finite group with a nonsymmetric completed Green algebra is established, and no universal symmetry proof is obtained. Turn4's finite abstract obstruction is explicitly nonrealizable as a based finite-group Green ring; turn5's actual positive family is explicitly proper. The sharp remaining requirement is control of bounded species on the other indecomposable directions, beyond endotrivial and induced-endotrivial spans.
