# Sections of positive Lefschetz fibrations: exact abelian diagnostics

Original KP2.22 remains unsolved, 2/5. The results below are homological diagnostics and an abelianization of a recent nonabelian criterion. They do not construct a positive Lefschetz fibration without a section. Independent review pending; no novelty claim.

## 1. Exact source and current literature scope

*K3: A New Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topology*, Problem2.22, printed pp.103–104, asks whether every Lefschetz fibration over S^2 admits a section, expressly assuming critical points. Its monodromy formulation uses positive Dehn twists. It excludes the easy nonsingular torus-bundle obstruction and distinguishes achiral fibrations, which allow both signs. The remarks also ask about multisections and two-sections. The correct full source is https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf ; the AIM workshop-summary URL in the imported record is not this manuscript.

We work with closed oriented smooth four-manifolds, closed connected oriented regular fibers, and the source's positive Lefschetz local model. A smooth section s satisfies f composed with s=id and consequently avoids critical points. The homological necessary conditions below also apply to continuous sections, but no general smoothing assertion is made.

Gompf's *On sections of maps from 4-manifolds to the 2-sphere*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18066v1 , constructs achiral and generic-map counterexamples. Its Section2 fiber-divisibility obstruction is relevant as a diagnostic, but those examples do not settle the positive case. Hillman–Pedrotti, *On sections of Lefschetz fibrations and bundles over2-complexes*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10943v1 , explicitly retains the sphere question as open in its introduction. Theorem3.19 gives a nonabelian extension criterion for a specified boundary section over a disk, with an additional local multiplicity restriction for smoothability. Its bundle theorem cannot be substituted for a singular-fibration theorem. We use the stated algebraic expression below; its geometric clasper/section construction is imported, not independently re-proved.

## 2. Degree and fiber divisibility

Let f:X->S^2 be a smooth map with an oriented regular fiber F, and let eta generate H^2(S^2;Z). Then the fiber class is Poincaré dual to f^*eta. Define

D_f={evaluation of f^*eta on A : A in H_2(X;Z)}=d_f Z,

where d_f>=0 is the nonnegative generator, with d_f=0 when all evaluations vanish. Torsion classes contribute zero.

If a section s exists, naturality gives evaluation of f^*eta on s_*[S^2]=1. Thus d_f=1. More generally, if an oriented embedded surface S is a positive degree-d multisection, f|S is a degree-d branched covering and evaluation on[S] equals d. Consequently d_f divides d; when d_f=0 no positive degree is possible. This condition alone makes no assertion of sufficiency.

For a connected simple branched d-section of genus h with b positive simple branch points, Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2-2h=2d-b, or b=2h+2d-2. In particular an unbranched degree-one section is a sphere. These are constraints on an existing multisection, not existence proofs for a branched cover embedded in X.

For a closed oriented X, the intersection form on H_2(X;Z)/torsion is unimodular. If Q is its matrix and v is the free-coordinate vector of F, the degree image is generated by the coordinates of Qv, so d_f=gcd(Qv). Since Q is integral unimodular, this equals gcd(v). When d_f=1 an integral homology class pairing to one exists, but it need not have an embedded spherical representative or arise from a section. Passing from the lattice equation to a section would be precisely an unjustified geometric step.

## 3. Disk boundary classes and vanishing cycles

Let E->D^2 be a Lefschetz fibration with regular fiber Sigma_g and vanishing cycles V_1,...,V_k for chosen ordered paths. Its standard handle description starts with Sigma_g times D^2 and adds one two-handle along each vanishing cycle. Hence

H_1(E;Z)=H_1(Sigma_g;Z)/L, where L=span_Z{v_1,...,v_k} and v_i=[V_i].

This uses the integral span, not its rational span or saturation. The framing sign of a two-handle does not alter this H_1 quotient, although positivity remains essential to the original problem.

Choose the usual reference disk section disjoint from the vanishing-cycle handles; equivalently choose a base point off their supports in the monodromy description. Its boundary loop is null-homologous in E. A boundary section differs from it by a fiber loop alpha under the chosen mapping-torus identification. If that boundary section extends over the disk, its loop bounds the image of the disk, so its class in H_1(E;Z) vanishes. Therefore

[alpha] belongs to L

is a necessary test. It is not asserted sufficient for extension. It can be decided from an integer presentation, for example by Smith normal form. Replacing it with a real-linear span test would lose finite-index obstructions.

## 4. The abelianized smooth criterion loses all local multiplicity information

We now compute the exact abelian shadow of the algebraic expression in Hillman–Pedrotti Theorem3.19. Let H=H_1(Sigma_g;Z) with its integral unimodular intersection pairing omega. Choose the sign convention in which a positive twist acts by

T_i(x)=x+omega(x,v_i)v_i.

Reversing that convention changes signs below but not the resulting subgroup. A separating vanishing cycle has v_i=0; a nonseparating one has primitive v_i, and omega(-,v_i):H->Z is surjective.

The cited theorem factors a based boundary loop as a product of transported pieces. Each piece has the form zeta_i V_i^(m_i) tau_i^(-1)(zeta_i)^(-1), where m_i is integral for its continuous criterion and takes values0 or1 for its smoothability criterion. Abelianization gives

a_i=m_i v_i+(I-T_i^(-1))z_i=(m_i+omega(z_i,v_i))v_i.

Let P_(i-1)=T_(i-1)...T_1, P_0=I, and w_i=P_(i-1)^(-1)v_i. The full abelianized expression is

[alpha]=sum_i (m_i+omega(z_i,v_i)) w_i.

**Proposition.** The set of all classes furnished by this abelianized expression, already with every m_i=0, is exactly L. The same set is obtained when m_i ranges over{0,1} or over all integers.

**Proof.** When v_i is primitive, omega(z_i,v_i) ranges over every integer, so its coefficient is arbitrary even when m_i=0. When v_i=0 the whole term is zero regardless of its coefficient. Thus the set is span_Z{w_i}. Each preceding transvection and its inverse changes a vector only by an integral multiple of a preceding v_j. It follows that w_i-v_i lies in span_Z{v_1,...,v_(i-1)}. Induction on i shows that the two integral spans agree, not merely their rationalizations. Hence span{w_i}=L. QED.

This is an exact classification of the abelianized expressions, not of the original group words. Solving the integer equation does not produce the nonabelian twisted conjugators satisfying the original based-loop identity. In particular homology cannot detect the local continuous-versus-smooth multiplicity restriction from this criterion. The global sphere problem further fixes a boundary loop through the gluing to the regular disk; arbitrary choices of disk sections cannot silently change that datum.

## 5. Concrete finite-index diagnostic and the remaining gap

Even primitive vanishing-cycle homology vectors can span a nonprimitive sublattice. In H=Z^2 with its standard intersection form, v_1=(1,0), v_2=(1,2) are primitive but span exactly the vectors with even second coordinate. The quotient is Z/2. A boundary difference (0,1) therefore fails the homological disk-extension test for a disk fibration with these cycles. Both primitive slopes are realized by simple curves on a torus, so this is a valid disk-level input. It is not a positive factorization of the identity over a sphere, and no closed counterexample is inferred.

The original questions remain open in this package: no general section, multisection or two-section is constructed, and no positive closed Lefschetz counterexample is given. The fiber-degree condition and abelianized boundary criterion are only necessary filters. Existing achiral examples remain explicitly outside the source's positive question; the recent disk criterion is not a general solution of the sphere gluing problem.

The executable controls test exact symplectic transvections and their inverses, equality of transported/original integral spans through explicit integer combinations, finite-index examples, and the unimodular lattice gcd calculation. They do not realize arbitrary formal matrices as a positive identity factorization or perform nonabelian mapping-class computations. Those limitations are part of the result.
