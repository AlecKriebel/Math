## 1. Original geometric scope and current source use

The correct complete [K3 manuscript](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf), Problem 2.22, printed pp. 103–104, asks whether every Lefschetz fibration over the sphere has a section and expressly assumes critical points. The remarks formulate the positive Dehn-twist lifting problem, ask about multisections and two-sections, and distinguish known achiral counterexamples. Thus a nonsingular torus bundle or an achiral fibration would not answer the target. The submitted artifact retains all of these distinctions.

[Gompf's 2025 note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18066), especially Theorem 1.1 and §2, constructs achiral examples by doubling a pencil complement and uses fiber divisibility to obstruct sections. The candidate correctly credits that obstruction and does not transfer the examples to the positive category.

[Hillman–Pedrotti's 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10943) explicitly retains the closed sphere question in its introduction. Its Theorem 3.19 treats extension of a **specified boundary section over a disk** using a nonabelian based-loop identity; it does not dispose of the global sphere gluing problem. The candidate studies only the abelianization of that expression. Its proof does not rely on independently recertifying the geometric construction in the cited preprint.

## 2. Degree image and multisections

For a smooth map f:X→S² and an oriented regular fiber F, naturality of the Thom class gives PD[F]=f*η for the positive generator η of H²(S²;Z). Consequently the image of evaluation on integral H₂(X) is a subgroup d_f Z of Z. Torsion homology classes have zero evaluation.

If s is a continuous section, functoriality gives

    <f*η,s*[S²]>=<η,(f∘s)*[S²]>=1.

Thus d_f=1 is necessary even for a continuous section. If s is smooth, the derivative of f∘s=id shows that it is an immersion and that its image avoids every critical point; it is an embedding because it is a section. No smoothing of a continuous section follows from this observation.

For an existing oriented degree-d multisection, pushforward of its fundamental class is d[S²], giving the required divisibility d_f|d. If d_f=0, no positive degree is possible. Riemann–Hurwitz for a connected simple branched covering gives b=2h+2d−2. This is only a necessary numerical relation; it does not realize a prescribed branch count or an embedded multisection.

For closed oriented X, the integral intersection pairing on H₂(X)/torsion is unimodular. With matrix Q and fiber vector v, its degree image is generated by Qv. Every entry of Qv is an integral combination of v, and every entry of v is an integral combination of Qv using Q⁻¹ over Z. Therefore gcd(Qv)=gcd(v), including the zero-vector convention. Primitive free homology of the fiber produces a class pairing to one, but not an embedded sphere or a section. The text correctly stops at this distinction.

## 3. The disk test really is integral

The standard handle construction of a Lefschetz fibration over D² begins with Σ×D² and attaches a two-handle for each vanishing cycle. On H₁, these attachments impose exactly the integral classes v_i. Their framings affect geometry and intersection data but not this H₁ quotient. Hence

    H₁(E;Z)=H₁(Σ;Z)/L,       L=sum_i Z v_i.

A reference section can be chosen through a point outside the finitely many vanishing curves and the supports of sufficiently small twist annuli. In the handle description the product disk at that point avoids the attaching regions and persists as a regular disk section. Its boundary is null-homologous in E.

Under the resulting mapping-torus marking, a boundary section differs by a fiber loop α. Its class in E is exactly the image of [α] in H/L: the base-circle contribution dies by the reference disk. An extension over D² makes that boundary loop null-homologous, so [α]∈L is necessary. This is not a sufficiency assertion.

The criterion is unaffected by the usual ambiguity of a mapping-torus representative. The image of I−T for the total monodromy lies in L, since each individual transvection is the identity modulo L. Thus an integral torsion or finite-index obstruction cannot be removed merely by choosing another representative. Replacing L by its rational span would, however, discard such obstructions.

## 4. The inverse loop and transported generators

I visually inspected Theorem 3.19 on the rendered original p. 17. Its final loop is **τ_i⁻¹ of the reversed ζ_i**. The overbar is easy to lose in text extraction. The candidate's expression τ_i⁻¹(ζ_i)⁻¹ is equivalent and therefore has the correct sign on abelianization.

Choose the candidate's convention T_i(x)=x+ω(x,v_i)v_i. Alternation gives ω(v_i,v_i)=0, so

    T_i⁻¹(x)=x−ω(x,v_i)v_i.

Therefore the abelianization of the displayed twisted-conjugacy word is

    z_i+m_i v_i−T_i⁻¹z_i
      =(m_i+ω(z_i,v_i))v_i.

Transporting the ith term by (T_(i−1)…T₁)⁻¹ gives exactly the vector w_i in the candidate, with the indicated order. Neither reversing the surface intersection convention nor reversing a vanishing-cycle orientation changes the generated subgroup.

For a nonseparating simple vanishing cycle, v_i is primitive: a simple curve meeting it once gives a homology class with pairing one. Equivalently, unimodularity identifies primitive vectors with primitive integer linear functionals. Thus ω(z_i,v_i) takes every integer value. Any such z_i is represented by a based loop because π₁(Σ) surjects onto H₁(Σ). For a separating cycle, v_i=0 and the term vanishes. These facts include a homologically trivial but nontrivial separating vanishing curve; its nonabelian information is not silently retained in H₁.

It follows that, already for m_i=0 at every index, the set of possible abelianized expressions is sum_i Z w_i. Allowing m_i∈{0,1} or m_i∈Z leaves that set unchanged. This assertion concerns the images of all such words in homology. It does not say that a given original word α equals one of them.

## 5. Integral span equality, including multiplicities zero

Applying any preceding inverse transvection changes a vector by an **integer** multiple of its preceding vanishing-cycle vector. Hence

    w_i=v_i+sum_(j<i) c_(ji) v_j,       c_(ji)∈Z.

For a sequence of k cycles, form the k×k matrix C with these coefficients as columns. It is unit upper triangular. Its inverse is integral, either by back-substitution or by the finite geometric series for its strictly upper triangular part. If V and W are the matrices of original and transported columns, then

    W=VC,        V=WC⁻¹.

This proves equality of the integral spans, without independence of the columns, primitivity of their combined span, or a saturation operation. Zero columns and repeated vanishing cycles cause no difficulty. Combining this with Section 4 yields exactly L in all three allowed multiplicity ranges.

The local multiplicity distinction between continuous and smooth sections has therefore disappeared from this particular homological test. This does not prove that the two kinds of section coincide, nor that the nonabelian criterion or global positive identity-factorization problem has been solved.

## 6. The finite-index example and the realization boundary

The vectors (1,0) and (1,2) are primitive torus slopes. Their integral combinations are exactly (a+b,2b), so the quotient is Z/2 and (0,1) does not lie in their span. Attaching the corresponding positive Lefschetz handles gives a valid disk fibration. It does not provide a positive identity factorization for a closed sphere fibration. The artifact correctly declines to infer a closed counterexample from this disk-level obstruction.

This example also explains why the abelian diagnostic is more than a real-linear test: these two vectors span Q², so rationalization would erase the obstruction completely.
