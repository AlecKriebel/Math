## Literature distinctions

1. The original report and Payne's 2006 paper establish the special roles of divisors and curves. They do not settle arbitrary-dimensional cycles.
2. Fulger and Lehmann's Zariski-decomposition paper supplies family definitions, compatibility with intersections and pushforwards, a birational basepoint-free description, and explicit projective-bundle cones. The scroll calculation in PARTIAL.md is therefore prior mathematics with a direct worked proof.
3. Brambilla, Dumitrescu, Postinghel and Santana Sánchez (2025), Introduction and Theorem 1.1, prove polyhedrality of cones of divisors whose base loci satisfy codimension bounds. Their moving objects in the dual discussion are still one-dimensional curves. The index k there does not turn those into the d-dimensional subvarieties in the OWR question.
4. Huang, Huh, Michałek, Wang and Wang (2025), Definition 1.5 and Theorems 1.6/1.8, concern representability by one irreducible surface, up to multiplication. Nonlinear conditions for that set do not give nonpolyhedrality of its convex movable cone. The explicit Hodge-index control in PARTIAL.md makes this distinction checkable.
5. Fulger and Lehmann's positive-cones paper, Example 5.12, separates BPF from Nef on a toric fourfold. Its conclusion must not be promoted to a Mov statement. Its use here is only to stop that invalid replacement.
6. Katz and Payne's realization-spaces paper, Theorem 1.4, was also checked during source triage. Tropical realization constraints are additional to balance equations. No inference about the closed convex Mov cone is made from those realization constraints.

The current author research page for Brian Lehmann was checked. It lists an erratum about the symmetrized M_0,7 calculations in the Zariski-decomposition paper; those calculations are not used here. No blanket assertion about the absence of other errors is made.

Searches included the exact title/code, toric movable cycles, toric higher-dimensional movable cones, strongly movable toric classes, and primary-source links in the supplied record. They identified no verified full resolution of the selected question. That is a bounded search result, not proof that no resolution exists.

## Success criterion

An affirmative result must provide finitely many rational generators or equivalent finite rational inequalities for the entire cone in every case in the intended source category. A counterexample must specify a qualifying X and prove that its Mov_d(X) is not rational polyhedral. A strict containment between familiar cones, a nonconvex set of irreducible classes, or a computation for finitely many examples does not pass this criterion.
