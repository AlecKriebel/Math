The coefficient-A differential is I-rho, where rho moves the final letter to the beginning. The zero-degree quotient is coinvariants E_n/(I-rho)E_n. This is a quotient by the commutator subspace, not the two-sided commutator ideal. On cycles, the left e_A cap of a tensor v is va; the equation sum(av-va)=0 makes its sum equal sum(av). In any case [va]=[av] in coinvariants. Thus the higher differential is the canonical invariant-to-coinvariant map, without an extraneous internal-weight scalar.

For an orbit O of size d, invariants are the line spanned by the sum s_O of its d distinct words. Coinvariants are the line spanned by one word: the coefficient-sum functional descends and takes that word to 1 in every characteristic. The canonical map sends s_O to d times the common word class. It is zero exactly if char(k)>0 divides d. Its kernel and cokernel then both have dimension one; otherwise both vanish. This proves the complete positive-weight orbit formula without division by d. All higher degrees vanish because their ordinary homology groups are zero. Weight zero contributes k only in degree zero.

Their sum is a nonzero ordinary cycle, as its boundary telescopes even over the integers. Its higher image is p times one nonzero coinvariant class, hence zero over k. No degree-two chains mean no ordinary boundaries; no degree-two ordinary homology means no incoming higher boundaries. This works over every extension of F_p, and includes p=2 without the unrelated arbitrary-derivation restriction.

Koszulness can be verified without importing a characteristic-sensitive comparison: the graded free left resolution 0 -> A tensor V -> A -> k -> 0, whose first map is multiplication av, is exact because every nonempty word has one final letter. The first module is generated in degree one, hence the resolution is linear. In the original BLS bimodule definition, for any fixed word of length n the differential from A tensor V tensor A to A tensor A is the incidence map between n edges and n+1 cuts on a path. It is injective in every field, with image the kernel of the map identifying all cuts. Thus the bimodule definition agrees directly. Neither multiplication map is the coefficient-A commutator differential above.
