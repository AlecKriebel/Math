## Leininger–Reid (2002)

Read the original author-hosted PDF at https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/areid/corank.pdf, printed pp.38–40. Section2.1 defines Sigma_g to be a closed oriented surface. Lemma2.1 establishes corank(pi1(Sigma_g))=g. Lemma2.2 realizes an arbitrary epimorphism from that surface group onto the free group of rankg by an identification of Sigma_g with the boundary of a genus-g handlebody, followed by inclusion. The group identification realizes the given homomorphism, not merely a homology quotient. This does not by itself prove that an arbitrary 3-manifold null-homotopy can be replaced by a handlebody filling; that is a separate geometric lemma to review.

## Razborov (1985)

Read the complete publisher PDF text via https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=im&paperid=1492&what=fullteng. Section9 begins on printed p.151. It defines a solution's rank as the rank of the subgroup generated by the solution tuple, and the system's rank as the maximum over solutions; it explicitly identifies this with maximal rank of a free homomorphic image of its associated group. Theorem3 on printed p.156 gives an algorithm computing rank, with proof through p.158.

Its Section1 associated group G(phi) is a radical quotient, not naively the finite-presentation group. However Lemma1.2 and its remark on p.117 state that the same solution/homomorphism correspondence holds for the ordinary finite presentation. Consequently the two groups have the same collection of free-image ranks, which is the only invariant needed here. The equations must be coefficient-free; the surface relator and one input-curve word have that property.
