## 2. Character construction and normalization

**Accepted.** The multiplication on U × W is associative because β is bilinear. Its commutator is β, its odd-prime exponent condition is correct, and collecting the defining presentation gives the asserted order p^(n+m). Edge variables are independent, with no imposed equalities between different edges.

The displayed operators really form a representation. In the product, the second translation introduces x·y′. The half-commutator and the cross terms in (x+x′)·(y+y′)/2 produce exactly the same phase. In particular, the translation sign and the factor 1/2 are consistent; the construction is not an antirepresentation.

The trace vanishes off the radical and equals q^i times the indicated additive character on it. The radical has q^(n−2i) elements, so the squared character norm is one. Distinct central labels are orthogonal by summing over W; distinct radical labels with the same central label are orthogonal by summing over the radical. The sum of squared degrees is the group order, establishing completeness by elementary finite-group character theory.

For a fixed nontrivial additive character of F_q, composition with F_q-linear functionals parametrizes all additive characters of a finite-dimensional F_q-space. Injectivity does not require that the chosen character be faithful: a nonzero linear functional is surjective, so its composition cannot be identically trivial. Cardinalities then give surjectivity of the parametrization. This addresses the nonprime-field case.

Consequently the factor is precisely q^(n−2i), and N_0=1 gives q^n linear characters. Isolated vertices remain in U, so no central factor disappears. The same coordinate model satisfies the scalar-generator relations for Rossmann’s graphical group over F_q: take x_j(a)=(a e_j,0) and z_jk(b)=(0,b e_jk), and use the same collection argument. Thus the prime-power notation is consistent as well.

## 3. Universal degree-q formula

**Accepted.** A rank-two alternating form factors through its two-dimensional symplectic quotient. Equal matrices XᵀJX have the same radical; the resulting isomorphism of quotient maps is a unique element of Sp_2(F_q)=SL_2(F_q). The fibre therefore has exactly q(q²−1) elements. There is no missing scalar quotient.

On the nonzero-column support S, a forbidden edge means a zero determinant, hence equality of projective directions. The components controlling the count are the components of the **complement of the induced graph**, as stated. An allowed graph edge may still receive the value zero; distinct complement components are not required to receive distinct directions.

It follows that the direction count is (q+1)^c−(q+1), even when c>q+1. Removing only the constant assignments is correct: any two distinct directions already span the two-dimensional space. Each nonzero column independently has q−1 representatives. Dividing by the fibre order and expanding the numerator gives the stated polynomial over Z, rather than merely a rational or integer-valued polynomial.

The excluded empty support, the single-component zero contribution, edgeless graphs, n<2, and q=3 are handled correctly. The coefficient-positivity assertion after substituting q=t+1 follows directly from the explicit sum. Orthogonality in higher symplectic dimension is not equality of projective directions, so the stated obstruction to extending this argument is genuine.

## 4. Forests and the matching rank bound

**Accepted.** On a forest support, a leaf pivot has a nonzero 2×2 alternating block and zero first row into the residual vertices. Eliminating the other incident entries leaves the residual principal block unchanged. Thus nonzero edge weights cannot cancel in this induction.

A maximum matching can always be chosen to contain the leaf edge, by the stated exchange argument. Rank equals twice the support’s matching number, and summing (q−1)^|A| over supports gives every rank count. Zero weights are correctly handled by passing to the support forest before choosing a leaf.

For arbitrary graphs, the proof of a nonsingular principal submatrix of size equal to the rank is valid: a nonzero alternating pivot splits off a rank-two block, and the Schur complement and block determinant formula give the induction. The determinant cancellation proof is also valid. Reversing the odd cycle selected by its smallest vertex is a fixed-point-free involution on the relevant terms, preserves the permutation sign, and reverses the alternating-entry product sign. The remaining even cycles supply a perfect matching. Hence rank ≤ 2ν(Γ), with equality attainable by weighting a maximum matching and setting other edges to zero.

The C4 calculation has the correct sign for the stated edge order: its determinant is (ad+bc)². The two displayed F_3 weightings have ranks four and two on the same nonzero support. This refutes only the proposed support-only extension, as the packet explicitly says.

## 5. Class-count moment and matching number at most three

**Accepted.** For a vector with support S, the equations β(v,u)=0 have the stated solution dimension n−|N[S]|+c(S). The constraints inside S, on its external neighbourhood, and outside its closed neighbourhood are exhaustive. The resulting conjugacy-class count is exactly K_Γ(q). Its displayed exponents are nonnegative by the spanning-forest and external-neighbour edge count.

The two moment identities follow from total matrix mass and the complete character construction. After N_0 and N_1, graphs with ν≤3 have at most two remaining unknowns. Solving gives formula (8), with the powers q^(n−6) and q²−1 in the correct positions. Every graph with at most seven vertices satisfies the required matching cap.

The divisibility lemma is valid, including its stronger conclusion over Z. Division by a monic polynomial leaves an integer-coefficient quotient and a proper remainder. At unbounded prime arguments, the remainder divided by the denominator tends to zero; if these values are integers, they eventually vanish. The remainder must then be the zero polynomial. The two recovery denominators are monic after clearing q^(6−n), namely Q^(n−6)(Q²−1). They never vanish at an odd prime power. Thus the argument proves one polynomial valid at **every** odd prime power, with integer coefficients and no omitted finite set of exceptional odd primes.

For ν=4 the claimed vector (t,−(q²+1)t,q²t) is indeed in the nullspace of both moments. The packet carefully does not claim that arbitrary such perturbations are realizable by graphs. Its conclusion is limited to the insufficiency of those two equations.

## 6. Higher-rank reductions and remaining gap
