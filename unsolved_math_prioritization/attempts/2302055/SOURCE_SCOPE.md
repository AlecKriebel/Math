# Source and prior-attempt gate: 2302055 / AMR-022-2055

## Exact target

Let f_1, f_2, f_3 be nonconstant entire functions of one complex variable. Put

    V = {(z_1,z_2,z_3) in C^3 : f_1(z_1)+f_2(z_2)+f_3(z_3)=0}.

The question is whether every entire function F on C^3 whose restriction to V is bounded must have one constant value on all of V. No finite-order, polynomial, exponential, smoothness or connectedness assumption is added. The conclusion concerns F restricted to V, not F on the ambient space. Counting branches, assuming connectedness without justification, or proving only a polynomial/exponential special case does not resolve the arbitrary-entire target.

This is Problem 2.55, attributed to J. M. Anderson and L. A. Rubel, in Hayman and Lingham, *Research Problems in Function Theory (New Edition)*, arXiv:1809.07200v2 (21 September 2018), printed p43 / PDF44. The complete problem and Update 2.55 were read and visually verified. Update 2.55 credits Demailly with progress in a special case. The source's bibliographic entry [189], printed p214 / PDF215, identifies Demailly's 1979 paper.

## Published special cases and dependencies

Demailly, *Fonctions holomorphes à croissance polynomiale sur la surface d'équation e^x+e^y=1*, Bull. Sci. Math. 103 (1979), 179–191, is available on the author's university site:
https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/ex%2Bey1.pdf
The downloaded file contains the 13-page published article plus a five-page thesis addendum. The first two pages, exact theorem statements and final published page were visually checked. The paper establishes the Liouville property of the exponential curve, in fact a polynomial-growth extension theorem, and explicitly states on p180 that the separated-sum question is already affirmative when one input is rational. In the present entire setting this includes every case with one polynomial input. These are credited prior results, not new author progress.

The companion C. R. note, 288 (8 January 1979), A39–A40, was read in full and visually verified:
https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/cras_ex%2Bey1.pdf
Its Corollary 2 says that for nonzero meromorphic f on C^n, the hypersurface e^x+e^y=f(w) has no nonconstant bounded holomorphic functions. It credits the bounded exponential-curve result independently to I. Wakabayashi. This is already stronger than just the all-exponential three-coordinate example. It remains a special case of the original arbitrary f_1,f_2,f_3 question.

Demailly explicitly cites the irreducibility theorem of Rubel, Squires and Taylor for sums of at least three nonconstant one-variable meromorphic functions. The Annals publisher confirms the final paper: *Irreducibility of certain entire functions with applications to harmonic analysis*, Ann. Math. 108 (1978), 553–567, DOI https://doi.org/10.2307/1971188, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1978/108-3/p07. Its full proof has not yet been retrieved. Any use must identify this classical input and distinguish a checked theorem statement from an independently reconstructed proof.

A relevant later framework is Lin and Zaidenberg, *Liouville and Carathéodory coverings in Riemannian and complex geometry*, arXiv:alg-geom/9611020v2 (1997): https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9611020v2. Sections 1.3, 1.6, 1.13–1.19 and the abelian-cover mechanism were read. Abelian covers of an ultra-Liouville base are Liouville; smooth quasiprojective varieties are ultra-Liouville by bounded plurisubharmonic extension to a compactification. The paper warns that an abelian cover of a merely Liouville noncompact base can fail to be Liouville. No application to arbitrary separated entire functions is automatic.
