## Results and exact boundaries

- For any even positive-order full-rank pressable symmetric binary matrix M, an explicit four-vertex wrapper has exactly two disjoint copies of its entire successful language. Its word count doubles, all central-to-outside and opposite-side comparisons have probability 1/2, and its variable-order graph is connected. The complete proof rules out both parity types of premature outside press, including legal prefixes with no completion.
- Forced-prefix padding followed by this wrapper gives arbitrarily large variable-order-connected matrices with exactly four successful words, and separately with exactly six. These examples are balanced. They disprove only a variable-component-size bound required uniformly for all matrices with the specified word count. A dimension bound restricted to hypothetical unbalanced counterexamples remains possible. No five-word construction or contraction theorem is claimed.
- A hypothetical unbalanced family with four to six words has a transitive majority order, pairwise disjoint inversion sets, at most one nonsingleton instructional class of size two or three, and no reachable/completable prefix set with both at least two prefixes and at least two successful suffixes. Nonminimum first-letter and nonmaximum last-letter branches have exactly one word when nonempty. These are necessary restrictions, not a proof of impossibility.
- Two common-context words PabcR and PcbaR force all six internal permutations. Each internal pair has at least three full words in each direction, giving balance when Z<=9, including equality. The abstract four-word language {012435,012534,013425,230415} is thereby excluded as an exact pressing language.
- Three common-context support words PabcdR, PabdcR and PcdabR force a fixed pair with at least two words in each direction, so Z<=6 suffices. No failure/absence hypothesis is required. The full audit retains both explicit residual matrices and replaces an informal disjoint-swap root phrase with its exact setwise-residual argument.
- Moving one label across a fixed k-letter block between two successful words forces all k+1 insertion positions. For k>=1, the middle comparison has at least floor((k+1)/2) words in each direction; Z<=3 floor((k+1)/2) suffices. The thresholds are sufficient and inclusive, not asserted optimal among all pressing languages.
- For one fixed local label set, each successful local order in a qualifying preceding-set context has exactly F(S)G(S union Q) full realizations. Contexts with different preceding sets are disjoint. With W their summed multiplicity, the reversal and relocation thresholds become Z<=9W and Z<=3 floor((k+1)/2)W. The four-letter threshold Z<=6W requires fixing one certified pair before summing; case-dependent pairs cannot be combined indiscriminately. These are counts of guaranteed bundles, not universal coverage bounds.
- Exceptional first and last branches give unique pressability of, respectively, the first-press Schur residual and the original last-deleted principal restriction. When expressed through triangular root blocks, the statements concern their symmetric Gram matrices. No external unique-root or unique-pressability classification is used. A 20-word example refutes selected-triple majority closure only; it is not a small-count unbalanced pressing example and says nothing adverse about majority closure for the whole language.
