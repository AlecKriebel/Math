Question 10.6, printed/physical page 25: Is there a uniform bound on Godbillon-Vey invariants of taut foliations of a hyperbolic manifold in terms of its volume? The question is located in section 10.3, interactions with hyperbolic structures (heading begins p24). The following full remark contrasts some fixed-manifold triangulation estimate with lack of a priori geometric/combinatorial control of that triangulation. It does not state a linear bound or give a theorem bounding this invariant.

A precise sufficient negative answer: exhibit one closed oriented hyperbolic 3-manifold M with smooth (C2 sufficient) codimension-one taut foliations F_n for which |<GV(F_n),[M]>| tends to infinity. Then no finite bound B(Vol(M)) exists, regardless of linearity. A positive claim must control every sufficiently regular taut foliation and every relevant hyperbolic M by volume alone. A weaker disproof of a particular C*volume bound would not decide the literal general bound. The printed question does not explicitly say closed, oriented, co-oriented, or finite volume; choosing a closed oriented co-oriented example makes the numerical pairing unambiguous and is sufficient for a negative answer to the broader natural question. No additional hidden smoothness/closedness assertion is attributed to the literal wording.

Definition 1.1 (p1) uses a single transverse circle intersecting every leaf to define tautness. Essentiality or Reeblessness alone must not substitute silently for that definition. Section 13.1 (p29) states C2 is sufficient for GV, a foliated cobordism invariant; p30 notes a transverse Lipschitz plus quadratic-variation threshold and distinguishes GV from topological foliation invariants. Thus a topological deformation/conjugacy does not alone transport a numerical GV argument. A global construction, regularity at all gluings/branching/fillings, tautness, and an actual numerical GV computation are essential to the counterexample. Definitions 1.3-1.10 (pp2-3) do not equate leafwise hyperbolic metrics with a hyperbolic ambient 3-manifold.
