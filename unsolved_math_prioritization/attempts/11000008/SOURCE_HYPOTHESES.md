The cited inputs support the accepted partial theorem in their stated ranges. The proof uses integral duality with arbitrary coefficient modules, ordered sphere markings, and the unmarked closed genus-two covering surface. None of those conventions can be silently changed. ## Source by source

1. **Benson Farb, Some problems on mapping class groups and moduli space.** The author-hosted complete book has 402 PDF pages. Chapter 2 conventions occur on PDF17 / printed10, and Question 2.1 with its definition occurs on PDF22 / printed15. The subject is the orientation-preserving mapping class group of a closed oriented surface and finitely generated H. The definition is the end count of a quotient of a proper connected cocompact geometric model. The observation about untruncated moduli-space covers is expressly insufficient to answer the question. The submitted proof uses a Cayley graph model and retains finite-index H. The question's text supplies no four-value upper bound. [Source](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf).

2. **John L. Harer, The cohomology of the moduli space of curves.** This is Harer's 1988 exposition of his results, rather than an independently retrieved copy of the original 1986 Inventiones article. PDF4 / printed141 defines the marked group by fixing each ordered point, which is the pure convention used here. PDF5-6 / printed142-143 gives Teichmuller coordinates and proper discontinuity; the former makes the relevant marked sphere spaces contractible. PDF7 / printed144 gives virtual torsion-freeness and the point-forgetting sequence. PDF39-40 / printed176-177 gives Corollary 4.2 with integral coefficients, arbitrary module M, and dimension 4g-5 for s=0 and g>1. Its proof continues through PDF42 / printed179. The candidate applies these formulas within exactly that closed-surface range. Its sphere forgetting steps have four or five retained points, so none is a low-puncture exception. The original 1986 article remains a historical citation, not a separately inspected source. [Complete chapter](https://imag.umontpellier.fr/~calaque/GdT-CGEMC-Harer.pdf).

3. **Nikolai V. Ivanov and Lizhen Ji, Infinite topology of curve complexes and non-Poincare duality of Teichmuller modular groups.** The inspected file is arXiv:0707.4322v1, with 11 PDF pages. Theorem 1.1 on PDF2 states the duality result for every torsion-free finite-index subgroup of the closed genus-g group, g>=2. Equation (3) on PDF5 specifies a right dualizing module and every left coefficient module. These match the candidate's module conventions. The theorem distinguishing duality from Poincare duality is not needed as an extra vanishing hypothesis; it warns against discarding the dualizing module. The local PDF and public arXiv record agree on version. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4322).

4. **Nic Koban, Jon McCammond and John Meier, The BNS-invariant for the pure braid groups.** The inspected 16-page author manuscript is dated January 22, 2014. Definition 2.3 puts the labeled points in convex cyclic position. Definition 2.4 on PDF6 supplies pair swing generators; Lemma 2.5 on PDF7 gives the free abelianization with those generators as basis. Remark 2.6 gives commutation for disjoint convex hulls and identifies the full swing as a central generator. Pairwise winding verifies the full twist's coordinates all equal one in that basis. The pentagon's clockwise versus counterclockwise global convention does not affect the zero character value or central subgroup killed. Neither the paper's full BNS theorem nor its exceptional-character classification is required. [Author manuscript](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mccammon/papers/sigma-pure-braids.pdf).

5. **Dan Margalit and Rebecca R. Winarski, The Birman-Hilden theory.** This is a survey, inspected as arXiv:1703.03448v1, with 20 PDF pages; it is not mislabeled as the original proof. PDF1-2 states the genus-two exact sequence. PDF6, section 4, separately checks that all sphere mapping classes lift and that all genus-two mapping classes are symmetric, using half-twist lifts and the invariant Humphries curves. Those extra identifications are essential to obtaining the full genus-two group rather than just a symmetric subgroup. The audit does not rely on the displayed group presentation or on its unrelated relations. The original research input is item 7 below. [Survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03448).

6. **Edward Fadell and Lee Neuwirth, Configuration spaces.** The complete published eight-page scan has a blank text layer, so PDF1-2 / printed111-112 were inspected as images. Theorem 1 assumes a connected Hausdorff manifold of dimension at least two and n>1. Projection to one moving point is a locally trivial bundle with the prescribed remaining configuration fiber; the asserted section when at least one fixed point is removed is also compatible with the uses here. Applying the theorem to C with two or three fixed punctures yields the exact fibers used in the independent proof audit. Fundamental-group injectivity additionally uses pi_2 of the punctured-plane base equal to zero; it is not inferred from local triviality alone. The direct PDF URL returned an internal web-tool error during this audit, but the DOI landing page succeeded and confirmed the authors, journal, pages, year, and PDF link. The authenticated local PDF remained available and was inspected. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-10517).

7. **Rebecca R. Winarski, Symmetry, isotopy, and irregular covers.** The inspected original research manuscript is arXiv:1309.3650v1, with 21 PDF pages. Theorem 1.1 on PDF2 assumes a finite surface cover with negative Euler characteristic upstairs and property NU, meaning no unramified preimage of any branch point. The genus-two hyperelliptic double cover satisfies each condition. The downstairs six branch points are marked and their upstairs preimages are not extra marked points. The forward direction of Proposition 4.1, Proposition 4.2, and the bigon and annulus arguments in Lemmas 4.4-4.5 lead to the completed proof on PDF16. The candidate uses this actual sufficient theorem, not an assertion that every arbitrary branched cover has the property. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3650).
