## 3. Finite affine action, centering, and metric

The action `(g star R)(z) = z^(t_g) R(z^(A_g))` is correctly a **linear** action on the rational-function vector space. Composition gives the affine product `(A_g A_h, A_g t_h + t_g)`. It need not preserve multiplication, and the candidate neither assumes nor uses multiplicativity.

The average of the finite orbit of zero is a rational common fixed point. Affine maps preserve averages whose coefficients sum to one, so averaging is valid even when translations are nonzero. The fixed-point identity is `A_g c + t_g = c`.

The form `Q = sum_g A_g^T A_g` is rational, positive definite, and invariant. Its positivity follows from the identity summand; its invariance follows by right multiplication reindexing the finite group. The form acts on **exponent vectors**, matching GPZ's space of linear forms. No inverse-metric confusion is present.

For a centered exponential germ, the residual affine exponential multiplier is `exp((t_g - c + A_g c)^T x)`, which is exactly one. The remaining action is pullback by `A_g^T`. This maps `L_v` to `L_(A_g v)`. It preserves independence, multiplicity, holomorphic germs, and GPZ orthogonality when `A_g` is a Q-isometry. Both direct summands are therefore stable. Uniqueness yields equivariance of the projection, and evaluating at the fixed origin yields equal scalars on the entire affine orbit.

The centered evaluator is not claimed to be multiplicative or translation-invariant. Those stronger properties would be false in general and are unnecessary.

Auxiliary data are computable. With a finite group explicitly listed, the two averages suffice. With generators and the promise of finiteness, exact finite closure enumeration terminates. Alternatively, rational linear systems determine a common fixed point and the vector space of invariant symmetric forms; an effective enumeration of rational points in that space eventually reaches a positive-definite form whose leading principal minors pass exact positivity tests. Existence of the averaged form proves termination. This is an existence-backed finite search, not a quantitative complexity claim.

## 4. Jet sufficiency, including multiplicities and dependent poles

For a seed with **m total binomial factors**, counted with multiplicity, the transformed denominator has homogeneous degree m. The holomorphic numerator is the centered exponential times m Todd factors and a sign `(-1)^m`. The Bernoulli convention `B_1 = -1/2` and that overall sign are correct.

Partial fractions preserve total denominator degree. Subsequent numerator cancellations lower numerator degree and denominator degree together. Thus homogeneous degree q can contribute to the value at zero only if q = m. Lower degrees leave polar pieces; higher degrees leave either polar pieces or positive-degree holomorphic pieces vanishing at zero. The relevant quantity is total pole degree, not rank or the number of distinct directions.

The infinite Taylor tail is also handled rigorously. First make the denominator support independent, preserving degree m. In rational linear coordinates, a holomorphic numerator is split into its restriction to the orthogonal complement and finitely many coordinate-divisible terms. Divided differences reduce vanishing order by at most one. A tail of order at least m+1 can undergo at most m pole cancellations, and every resulting holomorphic term still vanishes at zero. Hence the proof does not silently commute an infinite Taylor sum with an unspecified continuous projection.

All required coefficients through degree m are exact rational numbers and effectively computable. The m = 0 case gives one for a monomial and is handled explicitly in the code.

## 5. Partial fractions and orthogonal reduction

### Dependent poles

Combining proportional forms requires preserving the reciprocal scalar from each occurrence, including negative scalars. The implementation does so. The identity in equation (11) follows by multiplying through by a common denominator and using the chosen linear relation. Its sign and multiplicity updates are correct.

For a fixed support, the chosen relation and pivot remain fixed while multiplicities change. Along a branch, the sum of nonpivot exponents in that relation decreases strictly until a factor disappears. Once support shrinks, a new relation can be selected. Support never increases, and the total pole degree stays fixed. This is a valid finite termination argument even for several simultaneous dependencies.

The implementation chooses the first nullspace relation of a matrix depending only on the sorted support, so it does preserve the relation between support changes. It does not inadvertently choose a multiplicity-dependent pivot that could defeat the argument.

### Independent supports

With independent denominator vectors, their span and its Q-orthogonal complement are a direct sum because Q is positive definite. Rational linear algebra produces a rational complementary basis and an invertible rational coordinate matrix.

Splitting the polynomial into its complement-only part and coordinate-divisible parts is an exact polynomial identity. The complement-only quotient is GPZ-polar. Every retained branch cancels a real denominator factor and decreases its total degree. When a factor vanishes from the support, recomputing the orthogonal complement is essential and is performed by the code. When no denominator remains, evaluation at zero is valid.

Consequently every discarded object has zero GPZ holomorphic projection, and the surviving terms supply precisely the correct constant. The output is independent of the allowed splitting choices by the published uniqueness theorem.

The algorithm does not mistake denominator coordinates for an orthogonal coordinate system. The example `E_Q(x_1/x_2)=1/2` for the stated nondiagonal Q is correct. The reference controls deliberately exercise the more general phenomenon that terms with surviving numerator-to-pole ratios can contribute to the scalar.

## 6. Orbit sizes, stabilizers, cancellations, and the original value

If H fixes a rational seed as a function, then the affine action makes each coset contribution well defined. Left cosets `gH` are the correct quotient convention for the left action. The sum has `[G:H]` equal regularized scalars.

If H is the full stabilizer, this is the number of distinct rational-function orbit members. If H is a proper subgroup of that stabilizer, repeated orbit members are legitimate only when those repetitions are part of the stated input. The certificate says this explicitly. Multiplying by `|G|` would overcount a distinct-orbit presentation with a nontrivial stabilizer; the independent test detects exactly that error.

Computing a stabilizer can use rational-function equality, which is decidable by clearing denominators. Equality of raw exponent tuples is neither assumed in the proof nor sufficient as a general substitute. Denominator permutations alone already produce a nontrivial stabilizer in the triangle control.

Linearity gives the weighted sum of representative scalars. The centered germ of the **total** is holomorphic by the input promise, so its holomorphic projection is itself. Its exponential centering factor equals one at zero, and the coordinatewise exponential maps zero to the all-ones point. The resulting scalar is therefore the ordinary original value, including every cancellation among different orbits or terms.

There is no assumption that individual orbit sums are already holomorphic, that each regularized seed is a nonnegative count, or that individual contributions are metric-independent. Only the final holomorphic total is independent of the auxiliary choices. This distinction is necessary and correctly stated.

## 7. Genuine use of orbit representatives

The core computational work for a representative depends on its exponent data, the rational center and metric, and its pole count. It computes one finite jet and one rational reduction. The scalar is then multiplied by the orbit size. No generic evaluation direction and no evaluation of other orbit members is used in the proof or algorithm.

An orbit may need to be enumerated to obtain missing multiplicities, and group enumeration may be used for preprocessing. This does not turn the evaluation procedure into expansion followed by termwise evaluation; given the allowed multiplicities and verified auxiliary data, the evaluator need not generate any orbit member. The alternative generator-based construction of the metric also demonstrates that enumeration is not logically built into the scalar evaluation stage.

Thus the certificate satisfies the finite orbitwise requirement more concretely than formal averaging of an unspecified evaluator. It does not establish that preprocessing or scalar evaluation is cheaper than competing methods in any particular parameter regime.
