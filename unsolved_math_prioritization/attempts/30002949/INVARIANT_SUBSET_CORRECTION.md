# The invariant subset used in the Coxeter stratification proof

AI-assisted correction and proof exposition.

## Conclusion and version boundary

The reconstruction uses the correct subset

    Q = I(K,w,σ) = S \ (P ∪ Σ),

where P is the θ-support, θ=Ad(τ)σ, w=xτ, and Σ is the external graph boundary of P, with the stipulated replacement Σ=orbit_θ(v) when P is empty. This formula is valid in every rank of all thirteen classified rows. The proof below establishes it directly from the definition of I. Finite computational checks are unnecessary for this conclusion.

There is a separate wording problem in Lemma 4.6.1 of the inspected 28-page Görtz–He author copy, printed p. 13. Its unqualified commutant description omits restrictions that its proof actually needs. This note does not attribute that wording to an unseen corrected publication or erratum. It is an auxiliary-source correction, not a counterexample to the target stratification theorem.

## Two explicit counterexamples to the literal wording

The definition in GH §3.1 is

    I(K,x,θ) = max { R ⊆ K : Ad(x)θ(R)=R }.

The maximum is a subset of simple reflections; the action must send each of its members to another simple reflection in that same subset.

1. Empty support, affine A2. Let S={0,1,2}, K={1,2}, θ=(012), and x=1. This is the empty-support element in the split minuscule A row. Every member of K commutes with every member of the empty support. Nevertheless I(K,1,θ)=∅, because a nonempty θ-stable subset contains the complete three-cycle and cannot be contained in K. Thus the literal commutant criterion is insufficient even if self-commutation is avoided.

2. Nonempty support, affine A3. Let S={0,1,2,3}, K={1,2,3}, θ=(02)(13), and x=s_0. Then x is a left K-minimal θ-Coxeter element with P={0,2}. The simple reflection s_2 commutes with both s_0 and s_2, so the literal criterion includes node 2. But Ad(s_0)θ(s_2)=s_0, which is outside K. Nodes 1 and 3 are sent to reflections that are not simple. Consequently I(K,x,θ)=∅. This is the nonempty support in the classified (affine A3, ω2, split) row; no enlargement of the source hypotheses is needed.

The first example isolates the missing θ-stability. The second also makes clear why nodes of P cannot simply be retained on the ground that a reflection commutes with itself.

## Correct statement

Let (W,S) be a Coxeter system, with the standard real geometric representation and simple roots α_s. Let θ permute S. Suppose P⊂S is finite, spherical, and θ-stable. Let

    x = s_i1 ··· s_ir

use exactly one representative of each θ-orbit in P, each once, and no other simple reflections. Assume x is minimal in its left W_K coset, equivalently x∈{}^K W. Then

    I(K,x,θ)
      = the largest θ-stable subset of
        { j∈K\P : s_j commutes with every s_i for i∈P }.

In particular, writing C for the set of nodes outside P with no edge to P,

    I(K,x,θ) = C ∩ ⋂_{m∈Z} θ^m(K).

Here C is θ-stable. No assertion that θ preserves K is required.

## Elementary fixed-vector lemma

On the positive-definite geometric root space V_P, the operator xθ has no nonzero fixed vector.

To see this, suppose xθ(u)=u. Apply the reflections making up x one at a time, in their action order, to θ(u). Each step changes the vector by a scalar multiple of its own simple root. Consequently

    u − θ(u) = Σ_{a=1}^r c_a α_ia.

Average this identity over the finite permutation group generated by θ on V_P. The left side averages to zero. On the right, the average of α_ia is a nonzero multiple of the sum of the roots in its θ-orbit. These orbit sums have disjoint supports in the simple-root basis and are linearly independent. Thus every c_a is zero.

It follows that u=θ(u), and, in the sequential reflection calculation, every reflection in x fixed the unchanged vector u. Hence u is orthogonal to α_ia for every chosen representative. The θ-invariance of u and of the inner product makes u orthogonal to every simple root in P. Positive definiteness on V_P gives u=0.

This argument also proves the special case used below: if a product of distinct simple reflections fixes a vector, then each of those reflections fixes that vector. Indeed the change at each step is a multiple of a different independent simple root, so the vanishing total change forces every step's change to vanish.

## Proof of the corrected statement

Put I=I(K,x,θ). By definition, Ad(x)θ permutes the simple reflections in I. Since x is left K-minimal, x^{-1}(α_k) is positive for every k∈K. Therefore, whenever j∈I and Ad(x)θ(s_j)=s_k with k∈I, one has

    xθ(α_j)=α_k,

with positive sign. A negative sign would give x^{-1}(α_k)=−θ(α_j), contrary to left minimality.

First, I∩P must be empty. The space V_P is preserved by xθ, so the preceding positive simple-root permutation preserves the finite set I∩P. If it were nonempty, the nonzero vector Σ_{j∈I∩P} α_j would be fixed by xθ. This contradicts the fixed-vector lemma.

Next consider j∈I. Both j and its image k lie outside P. The action of x∈W_P is trivial on the quotient of the full simple-root space by V_P. Reducing xθ(α_j)=α_k modulo V_P thus gives α_{θ(j)}=α_k modulo V_P. Independence of the simple roots outside P implies k=θ(j). Consequently I is θ-stable and x fixes α_j for every j∈I.

Because the simple reflections in the displayed word for x are distinct, the final assertion of the fixed-vector lemma implies that every selected s_ia fixes each α_j, j∈I. Thus s_j commutes with every selected reflection. Applying θ and using θ(I)=I extends this commutation to every simple reflection in P. This proves that I is contained in the stated θ-stable commutant outside P.

Conversely, if R⊆K\P is θ-stable and its generators commute with all generators in P, then x commutes with each s_j, j∈R. Hence Ad(x)θ(s_j)=s_{θ(j)}, and R is Ad(x)θ-stable. Maximality of I gives R⊆I. The two inclusions establish the formula.

## Application to the thirteen rows in every rank

Here K=S\{v}. For every nonempty support in the reconstruction's table, v∈P. Since P is θ-stable, the entire θ-orbit of v lies in P. The set

    C = S \ (P ∪ external_boundary(P))

is θ-stable and contained in K. The formula proved above therefore reduces to

    I(K,x,θ)=C=S\(P∪Σ).

For P=∅ and x=1, the definition itself gives

    I(K,1,θ)=⋂_m θ^m(K)=S\orbit_θ(v).

This is again S\(P∪Σ), because the reconstruction defines Σ to be the orbit of v in the empty-support case.

This covers all ranks, all four fork rows, and the superbasic row. It also proves the exact properties needed by the reductive stabilizer argument: Q⊆K, θ(Q)=Q, P and Q are disjoint and commute, and P∪Q=S\Σ is spherical. The last assertion uses that S is a connected affine diagram and Σ is nonempty; every proper standard parabolic is finite.

## Effect on acceptance

The actual Q in the pinned reconstruction is unchanged. The proof of the fine-to-coarse decomposition and its exact coset stabilizer can use this corrected calculation in place of the overbroad sentence in GH Lemma 4.6.1.
