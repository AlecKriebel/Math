## Full proof audit

### Exact target and degree reduction

The source defines K_tr as the maximal totally real subfield and K^(d) as a compositum of bounded-degree generating extensions. Thus the report's K_d is the intended full field. The EMS report's definitions are at PDF page 20, printed page 2812; Question 4 is at PDF page 23, printed page 2815. The notation does not impose degree <= d on individual elements. [1]

Both relevant infinite extensions are Galois. Every finite intermediate field of the multiquadratic extension is Galois, but that fact is not assumed for arbitrary finite subfields of K_d. For a monic degree-n integer polynomial with roots of modulus at most C, the jth coefficient is bounded by binomial(n,j) C^j. This gives a finite list when n is bounded. Conversely a finite set has bounded degrees, with any positive bound chosen if it is empty. The reduction is sound and is not a solution of the missing degree estimate.

The height inequality is used only for nonzero integers; zero is an exceptional singleton. Squaring maps nonzero totally real integers to totally positive integers with squared house and fibers of size at most two. The cited NPI language is therefore compatible without strengthening the source theorem. [2]

### The d <= 2 proof

Write R for the compositum of the positive quadratic fields. Every imaginary quadratic field lies in R(i), so Q^(2)=R(i); its totally real subfield is R. If a in R generates F, then Gal(F/Q) is a finite elementary abelian 2-group. At an odd ramified prime the inertia group is tame, cyclic, nontrivial, and therefore exactly C2. Abelianity identifies this same inertia subgroup at every prime above p.

For its nonidentity element t, the difference b=a-t(a) is nonzero because a generates F. Inertia fixes the residue class of every algebraic integer, so b belongs to each prime above p. With n=2fg the norm has p-adic valuation at least fg=n/2. Every complex embedding gives modulus of b strictly less than 2X. Consequently p^(n/2) <= |N(b)| < (2X)^n and p<4X^2. The strict inequality and the rational-element case are handled correctly.

Every positive squarefree radicand of a quadratic subfield has each odd prime divisor ramified there, hence in F. Adding 2 to the finite permitted prime set covers the sole wild prime without needing an exponent estimate. An elementary abelian extension is generated by its quadratic subfields, so all such F lie in one finite field generated by square roots of permitted primes. The coefficient lemma completes finiteness. There is no illicit replacement of bounded local degree by bounded global degree.

### Fixed d, house <= 2

Let E=lcm(1,...,d). Restriction embeds Gal(Q^(d)/Q) into the product of normal-closure groups of all the generating fields. Each is a subgroup of S_n with n<=d, so every element has order dividing E. Quotients corresponding to finite Galois subfields inherit that exponent bound.

If a is integral and all conjugates lie in [-2,2], either root z of T^2-aT+1 is integral, nonzero, and has every conjugate on the unit circle. All positive powers of z have degree at most [Q(z):Q] and house 1. Coefficient finiteness forces two powers to coincide, proving that z is a root of unity without any unproved degree estimate for a.

For z of order n>2, Q(a)=Q(z+z^(-1)) is exactly the maximal real cyclotomic field: adjoining the nonreal z has degree exactly 2. Its Galois group is (Z/nZ)^*/{+1,-1}, so u^(2E)=1 modulo n for every unit u. Projection onto each prime-power factor is surjective. At an odd prime power p^a|n, cyclicity gives p^(a-1)(p-1)|2E. At 2^a|n, the exponent is 2^(a-2) for a>=3, with a=1,2 separately allowed. Hence n divides

M = 2^(v_2(2E)+2) product_{p odd, p-1|2E} p^(v_p(2E)+1).

The product is finite because p<=2E+1. This is a necessary bound, not an exact field-membership test; for instance M=24 at d=1 is intentionally loose. Pairing the Mth roots under inversion has two fixed points and (M-2)/2 pairs, yielding M/2+1 distinct sums. This validates the endpoint as well as all strict bounds <=2. Unrestricted real cyclotomic integers do not contradict the fixed-d result.

### Exact S3 ramification and valuation normalization

For f=T^3-4T+1, the rational-root test proves irreducibility and the polynomial discriminant is the prime 229. The index square divides that discriminant, so Z[a] is the full cubic ring of integers. Three disjoint sign-change intervals show that all roots are real. The irreducible cubic's nonsquare discriminant gives Galois group S3, and its splitting field L lies inside K_3.

Modulo 229 the factorization is (T-29)^2(T-171). Index one permits Dedekind factorization: the cubic field U has primes q and r with (e,f)=(2,1) and (1,1), respectively. Local factorization gives a ramified quadratic factor and a linear factor, so inertia in L/Q is a transposition. Decomposition normalizes inertia. The normalizer of a transposition subgroup in S3 is that subgroup itself, whence D_w=I_w=C2 at every prime above 229. Therefore L/Q has residue degree 1 and exactly three such primes.

Choose w_0 over r. The tower formula gives e(w_0/r)=2, forcing I_(w_0)=Gal(L/U), so U=L^I. At a prime w over q, e(w/q)=1 instead. Multiplication by b=3a^2-4 has determinant -229 in the integral basis 1,a,a^2. It is a unit at r and vanishes at q; since f(q/229)=1, the norm factorization forces v_q(b)=1. For discrete valuations with value group Z, w|_U=e(w/q)v_q, so w(b)=1 and w(229)=2. Thus |I|=2 does not divide w(b). The assertion is false at other primes despite being valid at the selected prime w_0. Using a valuation normalized by w(229)=1 would change the displayed value to 1/2 and would not be the normalization in the divisibility assertion.

This is an obstruction to a proof step, not a counterexample to bounded-house finiteness. The derivative spells out decomposition groups and this normalization; the original conclusion was correct.

### 2026 sources and ring scope

Castillo's version-2 arXiv record is withdrawn, dated 2 June 2026, with a notice identifying the failed divisibility step on page 4, line 5. The historical version's proof passes from a fixed inertia subgroup to an arbitrary place. The example above audits this type of universal implication; no claim is made that it reproduces every coefficient choice in that withdrawn argument. Its full theorem is not accepted. [3,4]

The August preprint by Man, Technau, Widmer and Yatsyna gives Northcott finiteness for the ring generated by the specified component integer rings. Corollary 1 requires the stated disjointness, bounded degrees, and no proper intermediate fields; its cubic specialization covers a minimal cubic tower over Q^(2). It does not supply the full integral-closure transfer needed here. Proposition 1 provides a Northcott subring with the same fraction field. [5]

Applying that proposition to S=O_(Q_tr) yields T containing Z and integral over Z, with fraction field Q_tr. Every element of O_(Q_tr) is integral over T, and every element integral over T is integral over Z by transitivity. Thus the integral closure is O_(Q_tr), whose real cyclotomic integers have bounded house. This correctly disproves a general integral-closure transfer principle without asserting that the particular cubic composite ring is proper or that its full integer ring lacks Northcott finiteness.

## Execution finding and correction

The original checker has 16 assertion sites, including the checks inside the residue and conductor loops. It performs no imports, filesystem access, or network access. Under normal isolated execution it passes. Under optimized isolated execution it also prints success even after changing the discriminant equality to the false value 230. This is a reproduced false-positive defect in supplementary verification, not a mathematical counterexample.

The checker remains import-free. The canonical result is unchanged. A separately pinned bootstrap validates the outer ZIP, external manifest, exact eight-member set, sizes, and every member hash before executing only the pinned checker in a fresh directory.
