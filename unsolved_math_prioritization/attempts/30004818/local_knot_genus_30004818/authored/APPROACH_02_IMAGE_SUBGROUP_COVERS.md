# Author approach 2: preserve genus in the image-subgroup cover

## Target and attempt

The full target remains a smooth local knot K in an orientable three-manifold M with g_{M×I}(K)<g_4(K). The finite-image argument in approach 1 does not handle infinite image. This second attempt changes the cover: use the subgroup H=image[π_1(F)→π_1(M)], so that the original surface F lifts without changing its topology. The new burden is to embed the corresponding covering three-manifold into S³.

The attempt produces a restricted theorem for hyperbolic manifolds, including infinite cyclic and rank-two parabolic image. It does not settle arbitrary nonabelian image or arbitrary orientable M. Approach 1 is left unchanged and remains subject to fresh review. No novelty is claimed for these deductions.

## General image-cover criterion

Let M be an oriented smooth three-manifold and let F⊂M×I be a compact connected oriented smooth proper surface of genus g, with its sole boundary a local knot K⊂B⊂M×{0}. Let H be the image of the induced map π_1(F)→π_1(M). Suppose the cover p:M_H→M corresponding to H admits an orientation-preserving smooth embedding into S³. Then g_4(K)≤g.

Proof. The covering-space lifting criterion lifts the map F→M×I to M_H×I because its fundamental-group image is contained in p_*(π_1(M_H))=H. This lift is a smooth embedding: any coincidence of two lifted points would project to a coincidence in the original embedded F, and the covering map is a local diffeomorphism. The lifted surface is compact and homeomorphic to F, not a nontrivial cover of F. Its boundary lies in one lift of the local ball B, so it is the same oriented classical knot. Composing with M_H×I→S³×I gives a genus-g classical bounding surface. Properness follows from compactness and the original boundary condition. ∎

## Restricted theorem: abelian image in a hyperbolic manifold

Let M be any connected orientable complete hyperbolic three-manifold, and let F be as above. If H=image[π_1(F)→π_1(M)] is abelian, then g_4(K)≤g(F).

This handles infinite H as well as trivial H. Neither compactness nor finite volume of M is needed. It applies in particular to every closed orientable hyperbolic M.

### Classification of the relevant subgroup covers

Write M=H³/Γ for a torsion-free discrete subgroup Γ of the orientation-preserving hyperbolic isometry group. An abelian subgroup H is one of the following:

1. The trivial group.
2. Infinite cyclic generated by a loxodromic isometry.
3. Infinite cyclic generated by a parabolic isometry.
4. A rank-two free abelian parabolic group.

The standard classification is recorded, for example, in Culler–Shalen, §1.5. Here is the argument needed for this application.

If H contains a loxodromic element A, every element commuting with A preserves its two fixed points at infinity individually. Exchanging them would conjugate A to A^{-1}, which cannot commute with A because A has infinite order. In axis coordinates the orientation-preserving centralizer is R×SO(2), recording signed translation along the axis and rotation about it. A discrete torsion-free subgroup of this centralizer has trivial intersection with SO(2), since a discrete subgroup of a compact group is finite. Its translation image is discrete: otherwise elements with bounded translations would have rotational parts in the compact circle, and two arbitrarily close such elements would have a nontrivial quotient approaching the identity, contradicting discreteness. The translation subgroup is therefore infinite cyclic, and H is infinite cyclic.

If H contains a parabolic P and no loxodromic element, commuting elements preserve P's unique fixed point. Conjugate that point to infinity in the upper-half-space model. The centralizer of a nontrivial parabolic consists of the horizontal translations z↦z+c. Thus H is a discrete subgroup of the translation plane C≅R², and is Z or Z². There are no nontrivial elliptic elements: discreteness makes such an element finite-order, contrary to the torsion-free action. This proves the list.

### Explicit topological models

For trivial H, the cover H³/H is R³.

For a loxodromic cyclic H, use a signed coordinate t along the invariant axis and normal-plane coordinates v∈R². The generator has the form

    (t,v) ↦ (t+L, R_θ v),  L>0.

The coordinate change w=R_{−θt/L}v makes the action (t,w)↦(t+L,w). Hence the quotient is smoothly S¹×R².

For a parabolic cyclic H, conjugate the generator to (x,y,u)↦(x+1,y,u) on R²×(0,∞). The quotient is S¹×R×(0,∞)≅S¹×R².

For a rank-two parabolic H with lattice Λ⊂R², the quotient is (R²/Λ)×(0,∞)≅T²×R.

All three resulting manifolds smoothly embed in S³: R³ is the complement of a point, S¹×R² is the interior of a standard solid torus, and T²×R is the interior of a tubular neighborhood of a standard embedded two-torus. Reverse the target orientation if necessary. The image-cover criterion proves the restricted theorem. ∎

## Consequence: genus-one savings cannot occur in hyperbolic M

For a once-punctured torus F, choose π_1(F)=⟨a,b⟩ so that the boundary is represented by [a,b] (up to inversion). Its local boundary K is null-homotopic in M. Thus

    [i_*(a),i_*(b)] = 1 in π_1(M).

The image H is abelian, so the theorem gives g_4(K)≤1. If g_4(K)=0, a classical disk already gives product genus zero. If g_4(K)=1, the known genus-zero obstruction prevents improvement. Consequently, any strict genus-saving example in an orientable complete hyperbolic three-manifold must satisfy

    g_{M×I}(K)≥2 and g_4(K)≥3,

and every genus-minimizing saving surface must have nonabelian image.

In particular the OWR example K=trefoil#trefoil, with g_4(K)=2, cannot produce orientable savings in a complete hyperbolic three-manifold. This conclusion does not require the image to be finite and is stronger than approach 1 on this class of ambient manifolds.

## Where the attempted full argument stops

For genus g≥2, the local boundary relation is only

    ∏_{j=1}^g [i_*(a_j),i_*(b_j)] = 1.

It does not imply that the image is abelian. Already for g=2 the assignment a_1=x, b_1=y, a_2=y, b_2=x sends the boundary word to [x,y][y,x]=1 in a nonabelian free group. Thus the genus-one step is not an induction mechanism.

More fundamentally, image-subgroup covers are not automatically standard. If H=π_1(M) for closed hyperbolic M, then M_H=M, which cannot embed in S³. Indeed a same-dimensional embedding of a closed connected three-manifold into S³ has image both open and closed, so would identify M with S³. There is no algebraic rule saying that a local-boundary surface must have proper image subgroup.

One can realize this latter issue with embedded surfaces, although not genus-saving ones. Start with a local disk bounding the unknot in M×I. For a finite set of generators of π_1(M), choose disjoint arcs in the interior of the four-manifold connecting pairs of small interior disks on the surface and following those generators. General position permits the arcs to avoid the surface except at their endpoints and to be pairwise disjoint. Remove each pair of small disks and tube along the corresponding arc. The boundary stays the local unknot, while each tube contributes a generator to the image and a meridional surface loop which is trivial in the ambient group. The resulting genus-r surface can therefore have full π_1-image. It is not minimizing and not a counterexample, but it blocks any claim that locality of the boundary alone makes the image cover embeddable.

Tameness of a subgroup cover, even when available, only identifies it as the interior of a compact core. It does not by itself give an embedding of that core into S³. No unsupported general embedding theorem is used here.

## Disposition

This is substantive approach 2. It settles the abelian-image hyperbolic branch and the genus-one hyperbolic subcase, but does not resolve the full orientable target. The next attempt must genuinely handle a new obstruction, for example an explicit finite-group-equivariant construction exploiting nonadditivity of g_4, or the product-handle constraints in a surgery construction. Review or source searching is not another author attempt.
