## Exact target and classical criterion

[Joshua Cooper, Combinatorial Problems I Like](https://people.math.sc.edu/cooper/combprob.html)
states the Rado threshold question. The recorded complete HTML identity is
73,084 bytes, SHA-256
`ba547f057b00069c9f6492249d285a8a02dda590ec1e7a88718bfc5293631e5a`.

[Ben Green, 100 Open Problems](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf),
Problem 21 and its comments, gives the fixed-variable formulation, credits
the three-variable upper bound 24 to Fox and Kleitman, and describes higher
arities as open. The recorded PDF identity is 839,479 bytes, SHA-256
`e06971245914947f152550dee59bbb29fe0e798f0c51b2bc2557f824c2f9a44a`.
The author review inspected physical pages 13–14; the independent audit
recorded visual inspection of physical page 13. These are historical
inspection statements, not new inspection performed for this edition.

[Fox and Kleitman, On Rado's Boundedness Conjecture](https://math.mit.edu/~fox/paper-FoxKleitman.pdf),
J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 113 (2006), 84–100, was not obtained in full text.
The author review reported HTTP 404. The independent retrieval returned an
internal tool error, which does not independently reproduce HTTP 404.
The bound is credited through Green. No direct full-paper inspection is claimed.

The nonzero-coefficient one-row form of Rado's criterion is also stated
in the Golowich introduction inspected below. The proof handles zero
coefficients separately before applying that criterion.

## Exact-degree families and the quantifiers

[Alexeev and Tsimerman, Equations resolving a conjecture of Rado on partition regularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1314),
[version 2 full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/0812.1314v2), Theorem 1 and proof,
supplies the expressly credited n-variable family with least avoiding number n.
The recorded arXiv version date is 25 January 2009; the journal citation is
J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 117 (2010), 1008–1010.
The elementary proof is presented in authored compressed form in PROOF_PARTIAL.md.

[Golowich, Resolving a Conjecture on Degree of Regularity of Linear Homogeneous Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3384),
[version 1 full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1404.3384v1), introduction,
Theorem 1.1 and conclusion, supplies the nonzero-coefficient criterion,
the simpler powers-of-two family, and the same fixed-arity distinction.
The recorded arXiv date is 13 April 2014.

Both experimental HTML pages rendered a later date; the version records,
not the rendered date, identify the manuscripts. Exact-degree n−1 regularity
with n growing does not provide an unbounded avoiding-number family at
one fixed arity. Neither credited construction is claimed as new.
