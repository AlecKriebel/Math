# Finiteness of automorphism groups of rational VOAs

## 0. Question, status, and conventions

The target is Lam's Conjecture 0.1 in *Vertex operator algebras and finite groups*, within the 2015 Oberwolfach report *Subfactors and Conformal Field Theory*, printed p. 904 [L]. Over the complex numbers, assume that a vertex operator algebra V is rational, C2-cofinite, and of CFT type, and that its weight-one space V1 is zero. Must its conformal automorphism group be finite?

Here rational means complete reducibility of admissible modules, not merely semisimplicity of Zhu's degree-zero algebra. CFT type means V is the direct sum of its nonnegative integral weight spaces, V0 is the vacuum line, and each weight space is finite dimensional. C2(V) is spanned by a_{-2}b, and C2-cofiniteness means V/C2(V) is finite dimensional. Automorphisms fix both the vacuum and the specified conformal vector and preserve every mode product. Derivations annihilate these two vectors and satisfy the mode Leibniz identity. Set T=L(-1), and write o(a)=a_{k-1} for a homogeneous vector of weight k.

The source does not separately state simplicity, self-contragredience, unitarity, weight-two generation, or holomorphicity. Simplicity follows from rationality and CFT type, as proved below. Also L(1)V1=0 follows immediately from V1=0. Thus the invariant-form hypothesis used in some classical treatments is automatic here. No unitary structure or weight-two generation is inferred.

**Result of this investigation:** no proof or counterexample to the full conjecture is established. There are five substantive routes, with complete proofs of their stated partial results. The most important new audit finding concerns the claimed general theorem [H5]. A false limit inference in its displayed argument prevents accepting it here as a verified prior resolution. This does not prove that the theorem is false or that no other proof exists.

The report is an authored mathematical audit. It makes no novelty claim for standard reductions or special cases and no claim of human peer review or formal proof-assistant verification.

## 1. Approach I: algebraicity and infinitesimal rigidity

### 1.1 Rational CFT type already gives simplicity

Regard V as an admissible module over itself. Rationality decomposes it into a direct sum of irreducible submodules. The projections onto summands commute with L(0); their values on the vacuum therefore lie in V0. Since V0 is one dimensional and the summands form a direct sum, exactly one summand contains the vacuum, and every other summand has zero vacuum component. A submodule containing the vacuum contains a=a_{-1}1 for every a in V. Therefore that summand is V and the others vanish. Thus V is simple.

This argument specifically uses semisimplicity of the admissible module V. It does not follow from a finite list of simple modules or from an isolated semisimple finite-dimensional quotient algebra.

### 1.2 C2-cofiniteness gives finite generation

Choose finitely many homogeneous representatives X for a basis of V/C2(V), and include the vacuum and conformal vector if necessary. Let U be the vertex subalgebra generated by X. We prove Vn is contained in U by induction on n.

For homogeneous v of weight n, subtract a linear combination of representatives of weight n. The remainder is a finite sum of homogeneous terms a_{-2}b of weight wt(a)+wt(b)+1=n. If a has weight zero, the term is zero because 1_{-2}=0. Otherwise wt(a)>0. If b has positive weight, both a and b have weight strictly below n and lie in U by induction; so does their mode product. If b is a vacuum multiple, a_{-2}1=Ta has weight n with a of weight n-1, and U is translation-stable. The induction proves finite generation.

Consequently there is an integer N such that W=direct sum of V0,...,VN contains generators. An automorphism or derivation is determined by its restriction to W: the automorphism case follows by evaluating iterated mode words, and the derivation case follows by induction on word length using Leibniz.

### 1.3 Exact infinitesimal criterion

Dong–Griess prove that Aut(V) is an algebraic group for finitely generated V [DG, Theorem 2.4]. For clarity, the finite-type mechanism is as follows. Embed it by restriction into the finite product of GL(Vj), 0<=j<=N. Choose, in each weight, a basis of mode words in W. Substituting a candidate matrix into each word yields polynomial coordinate expressions. Preservation of all linear relations among words and all mode products, together with the fixed vacuum/conformal conditions, gives polynomial equations in matrix entries and inverse determinants. Their intersection is a closed subgroup. The Noetherian coordinate ring reduces the ideal to finitely many equations. Relation preservation gives a well-defined endomorphism on all of V; imposing the same conditions on the inverse gives an automorphism. This describes the faithful finite-dimensional algebraic realization.

The Lie algebra of this group is Der(V). Differentiating product preservation gives the derivation identity. Conversely a weight-preserving derivation is locally finite, so exp(td) is defined separately on every finite-dimensional weight space; the Leibniz identity implies exp(td)(a_m b)=(exp(td)a)_m(exp(td)b). It fixes the vacuum and conformal vector, and its inverse is exp(-td). Thus d occurs as a tangent vector.

For a finite-type reduced algebraic group over C, the group is finite if and only if its Lie algebra is zero. Indeed, a zero tangent space makes the identity zero dimensional; translations give dimension zero everywhere, and a zero-dimensional affine variety of finite type has finitely many points. Conversely a finite reduced group in characteristic zero has zero tangent space. Therefore

**Proposition 1.** Under the target hypotheses, Aut(V) is finite if and only if Der(V)=0.

Weight-one zero modes provide inner derivations, but V1=0 only removes those derivations. It does not remove every tangent direction without a further argument. In particular, the centralizer term in [DG, Theorem 2.11] cannot be discarded: when V1=0 its defining centralization condition is vacuous.

### 1.4 What finite equations can and cannot certify

The algebraic realization gives a finite-dimensional ambient matrix space for tangent vectors. Linearizing all defining equations produces a family of linear equations. If their common kernel is zero, some finite subfamily already has zero kernel: start with the whole ambient space and repeatedly choose an equation that strictly lowers the current kernel dimension. This can occur at most the ambient dimension many times.

Hence a concrete VOA with certified multiplication data admits a finite full-rank witness whenever its derivation algebra is zero. Failure of one truncation to have full rank says nothing definitive: higher-weight relations may still remove its spurious tangent vectors. This investigation supplies no universal degree cutoff or rank argument for all rational C2-cofinite VOAs. That is the precise gap in this route.

## 2. Approach II: local Zhu interpolation and audit of the 2026 claim

### 2.1 A valid local-to-completed representation

For a simple rational VOA, the standard higher Zhu algebra decomposition realizes arbitrary linear maps on finitely many of the homogeneous pieces of an irreducible module; its V-summands are End(Vj), 0<=j<=n [H5, Theorem 2.2, crediting Dong–Li–Mason]. Thus a derivation d can be matched on each finite weight truncation by o(a), for some a in V. This a need not be a weight-one vector, and o(a) need not be a derivation globally.

The following direct construction identifies what local interpolation actually gives. Choose successive corrections c_n whose zero modes vanish on V0,...,V_{n-1} and match the remaining discrepancy on Vn. Since the total correction acts as zero on the vacuum, its weight-zero component is zero. For a vector a of positive weight k, the translation identity gives

    o(Ta) = -k o(a),
    o(T^r a) = (-1)^r k(k+1)...(k+r-1) o(a).

Consequently each correction can be replaced by a homogeneous vector of any sufficiently large weight H_n without changing its zero mode. Choose the H_n strictly increasing. This avoids needing any assertion that minimal truncation bounds themselves increase strictly. The formal sum of the homogeneous corrections is pointwise o-additive: on any fixed weight space all but finitely many corrections vanish. Its zero-mode sum equals d on V.

The same construction works for any grade-preserving linear map that kills the vacuum, using the same finite-level interpolation. Existence of such a completed representative is therefore much weaker than the derivation property. Rationality alone does not make the formal representative a finite vector or a weight-one vector.

### 2.2 Public status of Han's manuscript

[H1] was submitted June 4, 2026. Version v2 was withdrawn June 6 with a stated missing infinite-supremum case. Versions v3 and v4 followed June 10 and June 16. The current arXiv listing, inspected October 7, identifies v5 dated July 9. Its public comments describe a repair and added detail; its Theorem 2.7 claims every derivation of a simple rational CFT-type VOA is inner. No journal publication was displayed on that listing. Earlier withdrawal is historical information, not evidence that v5 must be wrong.

If the claimed theorem is valid, Proposition 1 proves the target conjecture immediately, with simplicity from Section 1.1 and inner derivations equal to o(V1). However, the following audit does not verify the proof of that theorem.

### 2.3 The exact problematic inference

For a derivation d, let E_d consist of formal positive-weight sums b=(b^(i)) that are pointwise o-additive and satisfy sum_i o(b^(i))=d. For such a representative define t(b) as the least i>=2 for which L(1)b^(i) is nonzero, or infinity if none exists.

The finite-supremum reduction uses the standard decomposition into a quasi-primary component and a translation descendant. Even granting all those reductions, the final page of [H5, proof of Theorem 2.7, p. 9] uses representatives with progressively vanishing low-weight components. It passes from convergence of each fixed component's zero mode to convergence of the sum over all higher weights. It supplies no uniform tail condition for this step. Continuity on each individual finite-dimensional Vi does not justify this interchange, and componentwise convergence is not convergence in the direct-sum topology.

### 2.4 A derivation-valued descendant-escape example

Let V be the simple affine VOA L_1(sl2). Positive integral-level affine VOAs are rational [R, Theorem 3.7 and its setup]; this example is simple and of CFT type. Choose a weight-one Cartan current h normalized by [h,e]=2e, where e is a nonzero positive-root current. The Sugawara conformal structure makes h primary, so L(1)h=0. Its zero mode d=h_0 is a conformal derivation and d(e)=2e, hence d is nonzero.

For each N>=2 define the weight-N vector

    c_N = (-1)^(N-1) T^(N-1)h / (N-1)!.

The translation formula above proves o(c_N)=h_0=d. Hence the formal vector with its sole nonzero component c_N belongs to E_d. It has zero weight-one component. Every fixed weight component of this sequence is eventually zero, yet its total zero-mode sum is the same nonzero derivation d for every N.

The sl2 commutators [L(1),T]=2L(0) and [L(0),T]=T give, for a primary vector a of weight k,

    L(1)T^r a = r(2k+r-1) T^(r-1)a.

Proof: expand [L(1),T^r] as sum_{j=0}^{r-1} T^j (2L(0)) T^(r-1-j); the coefficient is 2 sum_{s=0}^{r-1}(k+s)=r(2k+r-1). For k=1 this is r(r+1). Every T^r h is nonzero, since repeated application of L(1) would otherwise imply h=0. It follows that L(1)c_N=-N c_{N-1} for N>=2, where c_1=h. Thus t(c_N)=N.

This example also fits the particular displayed construction in [H5]. Set N=n+2 and choose b_n=c_N. Then t(b_n)>=n+2. The prescribed operation leaves components below N unchanged, sets the degree-N component to zero, and replaces the upper tail by its normalized translation. Here it gives precisely

    hat b_n = -T c_N/N = c_(N+1).

All components in the intended elimination range 2,...,N-1 are already zero. The eliminations described via Proposition 2.5 are conditional on a nonzero component to remove, so no elimination is needed. Taking tilde b_n=hat b_n satisfies the final displayed support condition (its tail starts even later than required), belongs to E_d, and has the required fixed-component convergence. Nevertheless,

    o(tilde b_n^(1)) = 0 for every n,
    sum_(i>=2) o(tilde b_n^(i)) = d != 0 for every n.

The claimed limiting conclusion would assert d=0. There is no stated coherence, bounded-support condition, norm estimate, or distinguished choice of b_n that excludes this allowed sequence. If a different selection is intended, proving that it has a vanishing tail remains necessary. The example does not contradict innerness, since d=h_0 is already inner; it contradicts the inference used to infer it from the constructed representatives.

The affine example has V1 nonzero. It tests the full scope of [H5, Theorem 2.7], not the hypotheses of the target conjecture. No counterexample to the target is claimed.

For a separate check within a familiar V1=0 setting, take a nontrivial Virasoro minimal model and its conformal vector omega. The vectors

    a_N = (-1)^(N-2) T^(N-2)omega / (N-1)!,  N>=2,

satisfy o(a_N)=L(0). Thus even there degree-by-degree convergence does not imply convergence of zero-mode sums. L(0) is not a conformal derivation: L(0)omega=2omega. This second example proves discontinuity only, not failure of a derivation-specific theorem.

### 2.5 Arbitrarily high support is available for every completed representative

There is also a general identity behind the escape. Given any b=(b^(i)) in E_d and any positive integer m, replace b^(i) by

    (-1)^m T^m b^(i) / [i(i+1)...(i+m-1)]

in weight i+m. The resulting representative remains in E_d: each corresponding zero mode is unchanged, and the same finite support bound on each test vector is merely shifted by m. All of its components of weights at most m are zero. In particular its t-index is greater than m or infinite.

Thus high t-index and a long vanishing initial segment can be obtained by pure translation, with no progress toward making the total tail small. This argument applies to any d for which E_d is nonempty. It does not assume d is inner. If an outer derivation existed, the same high-support construction would apply to it; this is a conditional observation, not an existence claim. An intended preliminary split handling a representative with t=infinity would not itself supply the missing uniform tail estimate in the other case.

**Disposition of this route:** claimed prior resolution located, but not verified. The exact missing condition is control of the zero-mode tail, not simply existence of local representatives. The finite coefficient computations are checked in `verify_math.py`; the argument above supplies the all-N proof.

## 3. Approach III: the weight-two algebra and a fully settled subclass

When V1=0, the weight-two product a*b=a_1 b is commutative. This follows from skew symmetry: the difference a_1 b-b_1 a is a sum of translations of terms a_j b of lower weights; the possible weight-one term vanishes and translations of vacuum terms vanish. Automorphisms preserve this product and the scalar form defined by a_3 b. If V is generated by V2, restriction is injective into the automorphism group of this finite-dimensional algebra with form.

### 3.1 Tensor products of Virasoro minimal models

Let V be a finite tensor product of nontrivial simple Virasoro minimal-model VOAs L(c_i,0), with conformal vectors omega_i. Their positive-weight spaces begin at weight two, and each weight-two space is one dimensional. Each factor is generated by its conformal vector, so V is generated by V2=span{omega_i}. Their familiar rationality is recorded in [R]; the argument that follows only needs the stated grading and generation facts.

The products and scalar form are

    omega_i * omega_j = 2 delta_ij omega_i,
    (omega_i)_3 omega_j = delta_ij (c_i/2) 1.

Put f_i=omega_i/2. Then f_i*f_j=delta_ij f_i. This is the coordinatewise algebra C^r, whose primitive idempotents are exactly the f_i: an idempotent has coordinates in {0,1}, and it is primitive precisely when exactly one coordinate is 1. An algebra automorphism therefore permutes the f_i. Preservation of the scalar form requires c_i=c_j for any two factors interchanged. Conversely every permutation of factors with identical c_i defines a conformal VOA automorphism. Simple Virasoro vacuum VOAs with the same c are the same isomorphism type. Thus

**Proposition 2.** For this tensor-product subclass,

    Aut(V) is the product, over distinct central charges c, of S_(multiplicity of c).

In particular it is finite, and Der(V)=0. One can also see the latter directly: for any derivation D of C^r, applying D to f_i^2=f_i gives D(f_i)=2f_i D(f_i); comparison of the i-th and all other coordinates forces D(f_i)=0.

The proof is an explicit subclass result, not a new general finiteness theorem. It does not require every target VOA to be framed or to have such a tensor decomposition.

### 3.2 Why commutativity and a nondegenerate form are insufficient

Let W=C^s with its standard nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form B, s>=2. Define the commutative algebra A=C e plus W by

    e*x=x,   w*v=B(w,v)e.

The form <ae+w,be+v>=ab+B(w,v) is nondegenerate and associative with respect to this product. Checking triples of elements from {e} union W proves associativity of the form: triples with one e reduce to B, and triples of three W give zero on both sides. The algebra itself is generally nonassociative.

Every orthogonal transformation R of W fixes e and preserves both the product and this form. Hence O(s,C) embeds in its automorphism group, which is positive dimensional. Every B-skew map K on W extends by K(e)=0 to an algebra derivation, since the only nontrivial Leibniz condition is B(Kw,v)+B(w,Kv)=0. There are s(s-1)/2 independent such maps.

Conversely a derivation D has D(e)=0. Write D(w)=ell(w)e+Kw. For w,v in W, applying Leibniz to wv=B(w,v)e yields ell(w)v+ell(v)w=0 and the skew condition on K. Setting v=w!=0 gives ell(w)=0; hence every derivation is of the displayed skew form. The derivation dimension is exactly s(s-1)/2.

This is not a VOA realization or a counterexample to the conjecture. It disproves the proposed shortcut from a finite-dimensional commutative algebra with an invariant nondegenerate form to finite automorphism group. The target hypotheses do not, in this investigation, provide either V2-generation or a rigidity condition on its weight-two algebra. Those are this route's two gaps.

## 4. Approach IV: a precise tail-control repair

Let D_inner=o(V1), a finite-dimensional subspace of the grade-preserving endomorphisms of V. Give the latter the topology of pointwise convergence on each finite-dimensional weight space.

### 4.1 Closedness of inner derivations

**Lemma 3.** D_inner is closed in that topology.

Proof. For M>=0 let R_M restrict an element of D_inner to V0 plus ... plus VM. The kernels form a decreasing sequence of subspaces of the finite-dimensional space D_inner, and their intersection is zero, since an operator vanishing on every weight space is zero. The sequence therefore reaches zero at some finite M. The map R_M is injective. Its image is a finite-dimensional subspace of the finite-dimensional matrix space End(V0 plus ... plus VM), hence closed.

Suppose A_n in D_inner converges pointwise to A. Then R_M A_n tends to R_M A, so R_M A belongs to the image. Let B be its unique preimage. The inverse of the injective finite-dimensional map R_M on its image is continuous, so A_n tends to B in D_inner; every evaluation on a further weight space is a continuous linear map, so A_n also tends pointwise to B. Uniqueness of limits gives A=B. The same argument works for nets and proves closedness.

### 4.2 Sufficient repairs, with proof

Suppose b_n is in E_d, and let H_n(v)=sum_(i>=2) o(b_n^(i))v. If H_n(v) tends to zero for every v in V, then o(b_n^(1)) tends pointwise to d. Lemma 3 gives d in D_inner. When V1=0 this implies d=0 and, for the target class, finite automorphism group.

This is an exact sufficient repair of the limiting argument. The needed hypothesis is about the whole tail, not the convergence of each of its summands. The descendant example has H_n=d identically and fails it maximally.

A more algebraic sufficient condition avoids taking limits. Let W be a finite generating truncation. Suppose representatives b_n have b_n^(i)=0 for 2<=i<=n, and there exists a single M such that

    o(b_n^(i))|W=0 for every n and every i>M.

Taking n>=M gives d|W=o(b_n^(1))|W. Both sides are derivations, so equality on generators gives equality on all V. Thus d is inner. Notice the quantifiers: the upper bound M is uniform in n. Pointwise o-additivity supplies an upper bound for each fixed n and test vector, which is a strictly weaker assertion.

No such uniform tail property is proved from rationality or C2-cofiniteness here. Finite generation reduces how many test vectors are needed, but does not bound where the nonzero zero-mode action can hide in the representative's weight. Section 2.4 shows that it can hide in arbitrarily high weight even on one fixed generator.

## 5. Approach V: quasi-primary spectral separation

This route gives a rigorous replacement for a portion of the completed-space argument without any unjustified limit interchange.

**Theorem 4.** Let V be a CFT-type VOA. Let q_i be in Vi for i>=1, suppose L(1)q_i=0 for i>=2, and suppose the zero modes A_i=o(q_i) are pointwise locally finite: for every v in V, A_i v=0 for all sufficiently large i. If d=sum_i A_i is a conformal derivation, then d=o(q_1). Neither rationality nor finite generation is needed in this assertion.

Proof. Let C be the linear operation on endomorphisms defined by

    C(A)=[L(-1),[L(1),A]].

For a quasi-primary vector q_i of weight i, the Virasoro mode commutator gives

    [L(1),(q_i)_(i-1)] = (i-1)(q_i)_i,
    [L(-1),(q_i)_i] = -i(q_i)_(i-1).

Hence C(A_i)=-i(i-1)A_i for i>=2. For i=1, L(1)q_1 is a vacuum multiple, whose zero mode with index 0 is zero; the same formula yields C(A_1)=0. Since d commutes with both L(-1) and L(1), C(d)=0.

All commutator operations can be interchanged with the pointwise locally finite sum. To check one operation on v, one only needs the finitely many sums evaluated at v, L(1)v, L(-1)v, and their indicated products. For any fixed number of iterated commutators there are still finitely many such test vectors. Taking the maximum of their finite support bounds justifies every rearrangement. Induction therefore gives

    C^r(d)=sum_i (-i(i-1))^r A_i = 0,   r>=1.

Fix v. Let J be the finite set of i>=2 for which A_i v is nonzero and set lambda_i=i(i-1). Define the polynomial

    P(x)=product_(i in J) (1+x/lambda_i),

with the empty product equal to 1. It has P(0)=1 and P(-lambda_i)=0 for every i in J. Since C(d)=0, P(C)d=d. The justified termwise identities imply

    d(v)=sum_i P(-lambda_i)A_i v=A_1 v.

Here lambda_1=0, and indices not in J contribute zero when i>=2. This proves d=A_1 on every v. All sums used at the final evaluation are finite. The polynomial is allowed to depend on v; the conclusion is still equality of the two fixed operators on every v. This proves the theorem.

The familiar finite-sum spectral argument appears in the radical and automorphism literature [Rad, Section 2; DG, Section 3]. The pointwise-local-finiteness justification above is supplied explicitly to delimit the completed-space extension; no claim of novelty is made.

### 5.1 The missing existence theorem is not automatic

A finite homogeneous vector can be normalized into weight one plus quasi-primary components by repeatedly using

    Vk = ker(L(1):Vk -> V_(k-1)) plus T V_(k-1),   k>=2,

and replacing a translated component by its lower-weight zero-mode equivalent [Rad, Lemma 3.3]. The process ends for a finite vector. For an infinite o-additive representative, infinitely many high components may contribute to the same lower weight. There is no automatic finite or convergent sum at that lower weight, and local o-additivity need not survive normalization.

The normalized descendants c_N of Section 2 all reduce to the same h in degree one. Thus normalizing each finite representative does not preserve the vanishing low-degree pattern used for the limit; it transfers the hidden action back into degree one. Taking an infinite collection before normalizing is not interchangeable with this reduction.

The exact remaining hypothesis for this route is:

    Every derivation of the target VOA admits a pointwise o-additive
    representative whose components of weights >=2 are quasi-primary.

Theorem 4 would then give d=o(q_1)=0 because V1=0; Proposition 1 would finish finiteness. This existence statement is not proved by local Zhu interpolation, finite generation, the finite-supremum argument, or a componentwise limit. No independent proof of it was obtained.

## 6. Acceptance boundary

Established with complete arguments here:

- Rational CFT type implies simplicity; C2-cofiniteness supplies finite generation.
- For the target class, finite Aut(V) is equivalent to zero conformal derivation algebra, using the credited algebraicity theorem.
- Tensor products of nontrivial Virasoro minimal-model vacuum VOAs have precisely the central-charge-preserving permutation automorphisms.
- The normalized descendant sequence invalidates the stated sum-limit inference even within the representative construction used in [H5].
- Inner derivations form a closed space; a vanishing total tail or a uniform generator-tail cutoff would repair the inference.
- A pointwise o-additive quasi-primary representative forces a derivation to be inner, with no finite-generation assumption needed for this conditional theorem.

Not established:

- A counterexample to the target conjecture or to Han's theorem.
- A verified general proof, a credited accepted prior resolution, or an exhaustive assertion about all literature.
- A universal quasi-primary normalization, uniform tail estimate, or finite matrix cutoff for the target class.
- Novelty, peer review, or formal verification of the full mathematical arguments.

Recommendation: retain an unsolved/partial disposition with five approaches recorded, and flag the exact v5 limit gap for independent specialist audit. Do not upgrade to already solved solely on the manuscript's abstract or on the formal implication from its claimed theorem.
