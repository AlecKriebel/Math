# The signed area centroid of the outer-side pedal polygon

Problem 5100017 / AMR-050-0017, invariant k307. Complete domain-qualified candidate, substantive author turn1. Independent review is pending. The invariant was proposed by Reznik–Garcia–Koiller; no priority claim for the proof below is made.

## 1. Exact theorem and the undefined-point qualification

Put the center of the billiard ellipse at the origin and its axes along the coordinate axes. Write

\[
 E:\ x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2=1,\quad a>b>0,
 \qquad E_c:\ x^2/(a^2-\lambda)+y^2/(b^2-\lambda)=1,
 \quad 0<\lambda<b^2.
\]

Assume this fixed pair supports a Poncelet billiard family of **least even period** N. Let the orbit vertices be P_i in trajectory order. The outer polygon's side line at P_i is the tangent to E at P_i. For each fixed M, let Q_i be its perpendicular foot on that line. Areas and centroids below are the source's signed shoelace area and first-area-moment quotient, not a centroid of an unsigned union of regions.

Define constants

\[
 H=\frac{a^2+b^2}{2},\quad e=\frac{a^2-b^2}{2},\quad
 d=1-\lambda\left(\frac1{a^2}+\frac1{b^2}\right),\quad
 F=Hd+2\lambda,
\]
\[
 v=\frac{-4e\lambda d}{F^2-e^2d^2},\qquad
 z=M_x+iM_y,\quad R^2=|z|^2,\quad G=2F+dR^2.
\]

Here F>0 and F²−e²d²>0. Let S(M) be the signed area of Q(M), and orient the orbit counterclockwise around its caustic. Then S(0)>0 and

\[
 \boxed{\ S(M)=S(0)\frac{G}{2F}.\ }                         \tag{1}
\]

Whenever G is nonzero, the signed area centroid is

\[
\boxed{\quad
 C'_2(M)=\frac z2-
 \frac{z(F+2Hd+evd)+\overline z\bigl(2Fv+3ed+\tfrac12vdR^2\bigr)}
 {3(2F+dR^2)}.
 \quad}                                                    \tag{2}
\]

The right side depends only on the ellipse, its fixed caustic and M. It is independent of the orbit phase. Reversing the orientation changes both signed area and moment by a sign and leaves (2) unchanged.

- If d≥0, G>0 for every M, so the centroid exists everywhere. For d=0 the least period is4 and (2) reduces to M/3.
- If d<0, the precise undefined locus is the circle |M|²=−2F/d. On that circle **every** member of the family has zero pedal signed area. The source's centroid quotient is not defined there. Away from it, (2) is invariant.
- Every simple winding-one even family has d≥0; hence the literal all-M conclusion is valid in that class.

A repeated traversal of an even least-period orbit multiplies area and first moment equally, so the same conclusion holds. Relabeling an odd least-period orbit as even by repeated traversal is not the usual least-period convention of an N-periodic family and does not create the antipodal hypothesis. The source's surrounding model uses a confocal **elliptic** caustic; no assertion about hyperbolic caustics or degenerate caustics is made.

## 2. Antipodal pairing and positivity

Let T be the counterclockwise Poncelet map on E, choosing the tangent to the inner ellipse on the specified side. The porism gives T^N=id for the whole family. The half-turn R:P↦−P commutes with T. The finite group generated by T and R consists of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms and is cyclic: a nonidentity finite-order orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism cannot fix a point, and the action on a finite orbit embeds such a group into the cyclic permutations preserving that orbit's circular order. Since N is even and is the order of T, its unique involution is T^(N/2)=R. Thus

\[
 P_{i+N/2}=-P_i.                                             \tag{3}
\]

This is also the classical even-period symmetry recalled in Bialy–Tabachnikov Theorem4.3. The finite-circle argument explains the exact least-period hypothesis.

Let n_i=(cosψ_i,sinψ_i) be the outward unit normal to E at P_i, and let h_i>0 be its support number. Then

\[
 h_i^2=H+e\cos2\psi_i,\qquad
 n_{i+N/2}=-n_i,\quad h_{i+N/2}=h_i.
\]

Choose successive lifted angles with Δ_i=ψ_{i+1}−ψ_i in (0,π). This is possible because each chord is tangent to an ellipse containing the origin: its counterclockwise endpoints lie less than a half-turn apart. The ellipse's increasing Gauss map commutes with the half-turn, so the same is true of their outward normals. In particular adjacent tangent lines intersect finitely, as required for the outer polygon. The origin-pedal vertices are w_i=h_i n_i and

\[
 S(0)=\frac12\sum_i h_i h_{i+1}\sin\Delta_i>0.                \tag{4}
\]

This positivity holds for star trajectories too. It is a sum in trajectory order, not a claim that their polygon is simple.

## 3. A bilinear identity for successive normals

Write P_i=(a cosu,b sinu), P_{i+1}=(a cosw,b sinw), and put t=(u+w)/2, q=(w−u)/2 with 0<q<π/2. Their chord has equation

\[
 (\cos t/a)x+(\sin t/b)y=\cos q.
\]

Tangency to E_c gives sin²q=λ(cos²t/a²+sin²t/b²). Equivalently,

\[
 d=[1+\lambda(a^{-2}-b^{-2})]\cos u\cos w
   +[1-\lambda(a^{-2}-b^{-2})]\sin u\sin w.
\]

The outward normal is h_i(cos u/a,sin u/b). Multiplying by h_i h_{i+1} and collecting its coordinate products yields

\[
 d h_i h_{i+1}=F\cos\Delta_i+ed\cos(\psi_i+\psi_{i+1}).       \tag{5}
\]

If d=0, F=2λ>0, so cosΔ_i=0. Every normal advances by π/2, giving a least-period4 orbit. Its outer polygon is a rectangle. In an orthonormal frame of its sides, the pedal vertices are

\[
 (A,M_y),\ (M_x,B),\ (-A,M_y),\ (M_x,-B),\qquad A,B>0.
\]

Their area is2AB and their centroid is M/3 by direct shoelace calculation. This proves (1)–(2) when d=0.

For the rest suppose d≠0 and put D=F/d. Equation (5) becomes

\[
 h_i h_{i+1}=D\cos\Delta_i+e\cos(\psi_i+\psi_{i+1}).         \tag{6}
\]

All denominators are controlled. In fact

\[
 F+ed=a^2-\lambda(a^2/b^2-1)>b^2>0,
\]
\[
 F-ed=b^2+\lambda(1-b^2/a^2)>0.
\]

Thus F>0 and F²−e²d²>0, including at d=0. In particular D²−e²>0 when d≠0.

## 4. The doubled-normal polygon and its centroid

Regard n_i as a unit complex number and put Z_i=n_i²=e^(2iψ_i). Write S_Z for the signed area of the ordered Z-polygon. Squaring (6), and using h_i²=H+e cos2ψ_i, gives

\[
 (D^2-e^2)\cos^2\Delta_i
 =H^2-e^2+e(H-D)(\cos2\psi_i+\cos2\psi_{i+1}).
\]

Hence, with constants u and v,

\[
 \cos(2\Delta_i)=u+v(\Re Z_i+\Re Z_{i+1}),\qquad
 u=\frac{2(H^2-e^2)}{D^2-e^2}-1,
 \quad v=\frac{2e(H-D)}{D^2-e^2}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

The latter v is exactly the value in Section1.

**Cyclic-polygon lemma.** If unit complex vertices Z_i satisfy
cos(φ_{i+1}−φ_i)=u+v(X_i+X_{i+1}), where X_i=Re Z_i and u,v are real constants, their unnormalized first moment obeys

\[
 \mathcal T_Z:=\sum_i [Z_i,Z_{i+1}](Z_i+Z_{i+1})=2vS_Z.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]

Here [U,V]=Im(\overline U V); the right side is a real complex number. No simplicity or nonzero-area assumption is needed for this identity.

To prove it, use

\[
 \sin(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)(Z_i+Z_{i+1})
 =-i[1+\cos(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)](Z_{i+1}-Z_i).
\]

The constant part telescopes. The remaining sum is −ivΣ(X_i+X_{i+1})(Z_{i+1}−Z_i). Its imaginary part telescopes through X_{i+1}²−X_i²; its real part is vΣ[Z_i,Z_{i+1}], after a second telescoping term X_{i+1}Y_{i+1}−X_iY_i is removed. This proves (8). When S_Z≠0, its centroid is the fixed point v/3.

Meanwhile, (6) and a simple telescoping sum give

\[
 S(0)=\frac D4\sum_i\sin2\Delta_i=\frac D2 S_Z.              \tag{9}
\]

Indeed cos(ψ_i+ψ_{i+1})sinΔ_i=(sin2ψ_{i+1}−sin2ψ_i)/2. By (4), S_Z is nonzero whenever d≠0, even for star orbits.

## 5. The area and cubic first moment

The perpendicular projection onto the tangent line n_i·x=h_i is

\[
 Q_i=M+(h_i-M\cdot n_i)n_i.
\]

In complex notation put t=\overline z/2 and w_i=h_i n_i. Then

\[
 Q_i=\frac z2+w_i-tZ_i.                                     \tag{10}
\]

Opposite indices have w_{i+N/2}=−w_i and Z_{i+N/2}=Z_i. Thus odd-in-w terms cancel in cyclic sums. Translation by z/2 does not change area, so (9) gives

\[
 S(M)=S(0)+|t|^2 S_Z
      =\left(\frac D2+\frac{|z|^2}{4}\right)S_Z,
                                                               \tag{11}
\]

which is (1).

For completeness, the cubic-moment calculation is recorded explicitly. Put V_i=w_i−tZ_i and \mathcal T_V=Σ[V_i,V_{i+1}](V_i+V_{i+1}). Its pure-w and quadratic-in-t parts cancel by antipodal pairing. Its cubic part is −|t|²t\mathcal T_Z. To compute the remaining linear terms, set x=n_i, y=n_{i+1} as unit complex numbers and use

\[
 h_i^2=H+\tfrac e2(x^2+x^{-2}),\quad
 h_{i+1}^2=H+\tfrac e2(y^2+y^{-2}),
\]
\[
 h_i h_{i+1}=\tfrac D2(y/x+x/y)+\tfrac e2(xy+(xy)^{-1}).
\]

In 2i times the edge moment, the coefficients of t and \overline t are respectively

\[
 c_i=h_{i+1}^2x^2-h_i^2y^2+2h_ih_{i+1}(x^3/y-y^3/x),
\]
\[
 b_i=h_i^2x^2/y^2-h_{i+1}^2y^2/x^2+h_ih_{i+1}(x/y-y/x).
\]

Define the cyclic sums

\[
 R_1=\sum_i(Z_i/Z_{i+1}-Z_{i+1}/Z_i)=-4iS_Z,
\]
\[
 R_2=\sum_i(Z_i^2/Z_{i+1}-Z_{i+1}^2/Z_i)
     =-2i\mathcal T_Z=-4ivS_Z.
\]

Substitution in c_i,b_i and removal of differences of successive powers yields

\[
 \sum c_i=DR_2+\tfrac{3e}{2}R_1,\qquad
 \sum b_i=(D/2+H)R_1+\tfrac e2R_2.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \mathcal T_V=-2S_Z\left[
 t(Dv+3e/2+v|t|^2)+\overline t(D/2+H+ev/2)\right].          \tag{12}
\]

Divide (12) by6S(M) and translate back by z/2. This gives

\[
 C'_2=\frac z2-
 \frac{z(D+2H+ev)+\overline z(2Dv+3e+v|z|^2/2)}
 {3(2D+|z|^2)}.
\]

Multiplying numerator and denominator by d is precisely (2). This is an identity of signed moments for arbitrary fixed M wherever the centroid quotient exists.

## 6. Domain, winding, and an exact noncircular star control

Equation (4) makes S(0) nonzero, and (1) therefore gives the entire zero-area locus. If d<0, it is a fixed circle of pedal points, independent of the orbit phase. The claim that C'_2 is a defined point for **every** M is consequently false if star families with d<0 are included without the source formula's nonzero-area qualification. This is a definedness issue; it is not a varying-centroid counterexample away from the zero circle.

For a simple winding-one family with N≥6, the first N/2 normal angles run once through an interval of lengthπ, in circular order. Their squares give N/2≥3 distinct points in circular order on the unit circle, and the full Z-polygon traverses that convex polygon twice. Therefore S_Z>0. Equation (9) gives D>0, and F>0 implies d>0. The N=4 case was already treated. Hence all M are valid for every simple even family.

An exact admissible star demonstrates that the exceptional case is real. Take

\[
 a^2=221/96,\quad b^2=27625/20736,\quad \lambda=125/96.
\]

The confocal caustic has semiaxes1 and25/144. Use the eight normalized vertex pairs

\[
 (0,1),\ (-12/13,-5/13),\ (1,0),\ (-12/13,5/13),
 (0,-1),\ (12/13,5/13),\ (-1,0),\ (12/13,-5/13),
\]

and multiply coordinates by (a,b). These are eight distinct points in least-period8 trajectory order, of winding3. The checker verifies tangent-caustic equations, positive orientation, and the reflection law exactly, rather than inferring a billiard merely from closure. This is the same exact 8/3 ellipse previously used in campaign problem5100065; it is reused as a diagnostic, not a new construction. Here d=−120/221, F=2795/1728 and v=7/13. The exceptional radius squared is exactly123539/20736>0; one zero-area pedal point is M=(13√731/144,0). At a pedal point on that circle every signed area is zero by (1).
