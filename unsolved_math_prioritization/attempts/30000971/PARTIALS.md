# Verified partial results and exact residual problem

Throughout this note, k is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, X is a smooth projective n-fold in P^r, c is a positive integer, and the projection X -> P^(n+c) is general with center disjoint from X. Statements about regularity use the ideal-sheaf convention. Empty fibers cause no difficulty and are excluded when a positive length is used.

The classical inputs below are credited to Beheshti--Eisenbud, arXiv:0806.1928v3, and Eisenbud--Harris, arXiv:0807.4243. The elementary arguments are included to make the deductions checkable. These partials are not claimed as new theorems.

## P1. Interpolation by degree at most length minus one

Let Z be a nonempty finite subscheme of projective space, of length l. Choose a hyperplane missing its finite support and dehomogenize. Let A=H^0(O_Z), and let F_t be the subspace of A represented by affine polynomials of degree at most t. The affine coordinate images generate A as an algebra. If F_t=F_(t+1), multiplication by every affine coordinate preserves F_t; since F_t contains 1, this forces F_t=A. Thus, before reaching A, the dimension of F_t strictly increases at each step. Starting with dim(F_0)=1, one must reach A by t=l-1.

Consequently ambient forms of degree l-1 interpolate every section on Z. The ideal-sheaf exact sequence and elementary projective-space cohomology give reg(Z)<=l. For a nonempty finite scheme, reg(Z) is one plus the first nonnegative interpolation degree, except that a single reduced point has regularity one, which follows from the same formula.

## P2. Every all-licci fiber satisfies both original inequalities

The Q module is a direct sum of its local contributions. Beheshti--Eisenbud Theorem 4.4(1) identifies each contribution's length as c times the local fiber length when that local fiber ideal is in the linkage class of a complete intersection (licci). Their Theorem 1.1 gives length(Q)<=n+c for a general projection. Therefore, if every local component of Z is licci,

    reg(Z) <= length(Z) = length(Q)/c <= n/c+1.

This proves both questions for that class. It includes local complete intersections and all fibers of local embedding dimension at most two, using their Proposition 4.5. It also includes fibers lying in a projective line or plane. The general conjecture cannot be reduced to this class: the square-zero algebra of embedding dimension four in PROOF.md has length 5 but q=5/3.

The precise remaining gap in this approach is the behavior of non-licci components. One cannot replace q by length there. The candidate counterexample in PROOF.md goes further and prevents replacing length by regularity in a universal Q comparison.

## P3. A low-corank range

We use the corank inequality stated as Beheshti--Eisenbud Theorem 2.1, attributed there to Mather. In particular, if a point of a fiber has tangential corank e, then

    e(e+c) <= n.

The tangential corank is the local embedding dimension of the fiber. If n<3(c+3), every point has e<=2; P2 therefore applies to every fiber, proving both questions in this range.

When c=1, the range improves to n<20. The inequality excludes e>=4. For a local fiber of embedding dimension e<=3, its ideal in the smooth n-dimensional source is generated by n+1 projection equations. Eliminate n-e independent linear equations. The remaining finite-colength ideal in a regular local ring of dimension e has at most e+1<=4 generators. Beheshti--Eisenbud Proposition 4.5 makes it licci. Hence P2 again proves both inequalities for every fiber. These are deductions from their published corank and linkage results, not new uniform statements.

At the boundary n=3(c+3), corank three is no longer excluded; for c=1, corank four becomes allowed at n=20. The argument has no additional mechanism beyond these inequalities. Moreover the counterexample in PROOF.md occurs at n=40, c=6, e=4; its corank inequality reads 4*(4+6)=40, exactly at equality. Thus that construction is consistent with the cited corank theorem.

For c>n, the multigerm version of the same theorem permits at most one support point and permits no positive corank. Every nonempty fiber is then a reduced point, so both inequalities hold with regularity one.

## P4. A Loewy-length interpolation bound

Let Z be any finite projective scheme, with local algebras A_i at its distinct support points p_i. Put nu_i=min{v>=1: m_i^v=0}. Then

    reg(Z) <= sum_i nu_i.

Proof. Work on a common affine chart. For each i and j!=i choose an affine linear form h_ij vanishing at p_j but not at p_i. The product

    H_i = product_(j!=i) h_ij^(nu_j)

vanishes in A_j for every j!=i and is a unit in A_i. Centered affine coordinates generate m_i, and products of nu_i of them vanish. Hence every element of A_i is represented by a polynomial of degree <=nu_i-1. Given an arbitrary tuple a_i in the product of the A_i, choose P_i of degree <=nu_i-1 representing H_i^(-1)*a_i in A_i. Then sum_i H_i*P_i represents that tuple and has degree <=sum_i nu_i-1. Homogenization and the same interpolation criterion used in P1 prove the assertion.

This is a genuinely different route from total length: a single square-zero component has arbitrarily large length as its embedding dimension grows, but nu=2 and regularity two.

A sufficient condition for both original inequalities would be q_i>=nu_i at every local component. PROOF.md disproves that condition, even for a component that is the entire fiber of a general projection. For the weaker bound, it would instead suffice to prove sum_i nu_i<=n/c+1 directly using genericity. No such all-dimensional inequality is established here. Tangential coranks by themselves do not control Loewy lengths: the abstract algebras k[t]/(t^M) have fixed embedding dimension one and unbounded nu=M. This is an obstruction to a corank-only local argument, not a claim that arbitrary M occurs in a fixed-dimensional general projection.

## P5. The asymptotic powers reformulation of the weaker bound

Let R be the homogeneous coordinate ring of X in its given embedding, m_R its irrelevant ideal, and I the ideal generated by the n+c+1 linear forms defining the projection. Its basepoint-free hypothesis makes I m_R-primary. Eisenbud--Harris Theorem 0.1 identifies the maximum fiber regularity as epsilon+1, where epsilon is the least integer for which

    m_R^(t+epsilon) subset I^t

holds for every sufficiently large t. Hence the weaker conjecture is equivalent to the eventual containment with epsilon=floor(n/c).

The equivalence is useful because it gives an exact algebraic success test; it is not a proof of the required bound on epsilon. Eventual linear growth of regularity determines the existence of an intercept, but supplies no bound epsilon<=floor(n/c). The smoothness hypothesis on Proj(R) and the generic choice of I must do the missing work. Replacing R by a polynomial ring or assuming a linear presentation would change the original target. In particular, this is not the separate primary-powers question in rank 600 (30000080).

This approach remains blocked at the sharp uniform intercept bound. The strong Q comparison is neither needed in this equivalence nor rescued by it.

## Final residual target

The first original question remains: for every smooth projective n-fold in its given embedding over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, must every fiber of a general linear projection to P^(n+c) satisfy reg(Z)<=floor(n/c)+1?

The supplied candidate settles only the second comparison negatively. The original bundled record must stay unsolved, with five substantive approaches recorded, regardless of whether that partial counterexample passes independent audit.
