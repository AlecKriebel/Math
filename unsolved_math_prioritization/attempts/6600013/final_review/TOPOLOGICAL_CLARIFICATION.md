# Unit tile lengths and the stationary proper-substitution model

This additive clarification supplies the metric convention for the variable-length substitution in turn4. It leaves all frozen author statements, complexity counts and conclusions unchanged.

For tau(a)=aab,tau(b)=ab, choose Perron–Frobenius lengths ell(a)=(1+sqrt5)/2 and ell(b)=1. The expansion is lambda=(3+sqrt5)/2: the lengths of aab and ab are respectively lambda ell(a) and lambda ell(b). Equip the two-loop graph with these edge lengths and parameterize the substitution edge map by this constant expansion. The recognizable proper substitution then has the stationary inverse-limit model described in the author proof. The combinatorial cellular map and free-group automorphism are exactly the displayed words, independent of the length convention.

Let X be the symbolic substitution subshift. Its unit-roof suspension is (X×[0,1])/((x,1)~(shift x,0)). Its PF-roof suspension is the corresponding quotient with interval length ell(x_0). The map

    [x,t] → [x,t ell(x_0)]

is a homeomorphism: the roof is a positive locally constant function, endpoints are identified consistently, and the inverse divides by the same roof length. This is a topological homeomorphism (and an orbit reparameterization), not a conjugacy preserving the translation time parameter. Products give the analogous bridge in two dimensions.

The finite cyclic decorations record symbolic edge crossings. The same tilewise map, keeping the fiber state, therefore lifts to the decorated suspensions and commutes with reduction of their fiber states. Consequently the stationary finite-cover models compute the cohomology and connectedness of the unit-square symbolic covers used in the complexity claims. The unit-box language counts themselves are unchanged; no numerical complexity coefficient is transferred through an unstated geometric rescaling.
