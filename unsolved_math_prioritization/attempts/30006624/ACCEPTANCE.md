## Exact full target

For a complete, simply connected path-metric space, suppose every point has a neighborhood all of whose triples have exactly one ambient interval median. Must every triple in the whole space have exactly one median? The metric intervals are computed in the original ambient metric. No uniform radius, local compactness, properness, finite rank, or global bicombing is assumed. Path metric does not assume attained minimizing geodesics. ## Accepted mathematics and exact gaps

1. The capped good-radius function is positive and 1-Lipschitz; every compact set has a uniformly good neighborhood at a controlled radius. Iterative insertion/filling points are not shown to remain in a compact region or have summable travel. Boundedness cannot replace compactness.
2. **Every complete almost modular metric space is modular**, where almost modular means arbitrary additive accuracy for every triple and all three distinct pair intervals. For the target defect f, choose a largest pair defect 2s, so f/3 <= s <= f; request an approximate median with eta=s/10. The descent gives f(new) <= 3f/4 and travel <= (19/15)(f-f(new)). Summable travel gives a Cauchy sequence, completeness gives its limit, and the three nonnegative defects vanish. Connectedness, local medianity, path-metric structure, uniqueness and finite rank are not premises of this reduction lemma. The missing premise for the full problem is **global approximate-median existence**. It is not supplied by local stationarity or compactness of one chosen nullhomotopy. Median uniqueness after modularity is credited to Bowditch's Proposition 9.1 under its hypotheses; modularity alone does not give uniqueness.
3. A globally uniformly bilipschitz CAT(0) auxiliary metric provides uniform short-path homotopy control by geodesic interpolation and hence a conditional medianity route through Bowditch's Proposition 9.4. No such global metric is constructed. Good balls need not be median-closed; median-subalgebra overlaps need not be interval-convex; canonical auxiliary metrics need not agree on such overlaps. Global distortion/completeness control is also missing.
4. The bounded smooth weight 1+x on an l1 strip destroys local medianity while preserving completeness and simple connectivity. The exact interval calculation blocks automatic conformal radius normalization; it is not a target counterexample.
5. A bounded complete shrinking-circle construction is locally median but nonuniform and not simply connected. Filling by l1 squares changes the boundary metric and restores global medianity. A simply connected locally median slit plane has a bad triple but is incomplete. No counterexample satisfying all target hypotheses is supplied.
