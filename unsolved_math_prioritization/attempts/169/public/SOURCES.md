# Sources and scholarly dependencies

The problem statement and published baseline results below are distinct from the authored reductions in this package. The listed source checks were made during the study on 14 October 2026. A dated source-status inspection is not a proof that no other work or later version resolves the problem. No priority or exhaustive literature claim is made. Source documents themselves are not included.

1. Ben Green, *100 Open Problems*, Problem 39, PDF page 20. [Author PDF](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf#page=20). This supplies the square-root random-set translate-covering question. Exact floor conventions and the uniform fixed-cardinality operational model are stated explicitly in the reader. The discussion of alternative constants does not determine the answer.

2. Béla Bollobás, Svante Janson, and Oliver Riordan, *On covering by translates of a set*, arXiv:0910.3815v2. [Versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.3815v2). The study checked the fixed-centre Janson and read-family calculations, including equation (4.5) and Remark 4.3. Its fixed-k efficient-covering limit and near-linear n-versus-k inefficient regime are not substituted for k≈√p. No result from this source is claimed to settle the present target.

3. Ben Green and Imre Z. Ruzsa, *Freiman's theorem in an arbitrary abelian group*, Theorem 1.1 and its proper coset-progression definition. [Versioned author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505198v2), [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdl021). This is the structural input for excluding bounded-doubling translating sets through deterministic progression envelopes. It does not say every successful translating set has small doubling.

4. Giorgis Petridis, *New Proofs of Plünnecke-type Estimates for Product Sets in Groups*, Theorem 3.1. [Versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3507v3). Used to pass from a small-difference hypothesis to a sum-doubling bound in the restricted structural argument.

5. Noga Alon, Béla Bollobás, Jeong Han Kim, and Van H. Vu, *Economical covers with geometric applications*, Theorem 1.2. [Author PDF](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/abkv4.pdf), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611502013886). The growing-uniformity hypothesis is checked and found unsuitable at uniformity and degree both of order √p. This is an applicability check, not a claim that the theorem fails.

6. Dmitry Gavinsky, Shachar Lovett, Michael Saks, and Srikanth Srinivasan, *A Tail Bound for Read-k Families of Functions*, Theorem 1.2 and the entropy discussion. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.1478). Provides a primary-source check on the read-family inequality; it does not control unrestricted adaptive translate selection.

7. R. C. Baker, G. Harman, and J. Pintz, *The difference between consecutive primes II*, Theorem 1. [Public paper copy](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/BakerHarmanPintz.pdf). Used only to move a deterministic Singer construction to nearby primes. The construction supplies exceptional finite sets and method obstructions, not a random-model answer.

The study also gives self-contained arguments for its finite-field Singer property, exposure couplings, seed conditioning, same-A amplification, cylinder conditioning, Charlier identities, exchange flux, collision partitions, and normalized representation counting. Standard Azuma, Harris correlation, Hoeffding, Markov, Chebyshev, Jensen, elementary finite-field rank, and finite inclusion-exclusion are used with the hypotheses made explicit in the supplements.
