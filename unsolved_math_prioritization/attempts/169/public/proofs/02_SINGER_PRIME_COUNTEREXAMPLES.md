## The theorem

There are infinitely many primes p for which, with k=floor(sqrt(p)), there are sets A,T subset Z/pZ such that:

1. |A|=k and A+T=Z/pZ.
2. |T|<=6k+3, hence |T|<=floor(100 sqrt(p)).
3. max_{d!=0}|A intersect(A+d)|<=7, so E(A)<=8k^2.
4. E(T)<=15552k^2.
5. Every nontrivial unnormalized Fourier coefficient of A and of T is O(sqrt(k) log p), with absolute implied constants.
6. Both |A+A|/|A| and |T+T|/|T| are Omega(k); the same is true for the difference-set ratios.
7. Outside a set of at most eight positions, 1<=(1_A*1_T)(x)<=40.

Here E(B)=#{(b1,b2,b3,b4) in B^4:b1-b2=b3-b4}. Thus small additive energy, large doubling, and small nontrivial Fourier coefficients, even for both members of the covering pair, do not deterministically prevent a constant-multiplicity cover. The example is in the exact prime group and exact floor-size model; no unproved infinitude of prime Singer moduli is assumed.

The Fourier bound proved here is O(sqrt(k) log p), which is weaker by a factor sqrt(log p) than the customary random-set bound O(sqrt(k log p)). No claim is made that these examples satisfy that sharper threshold.

The theorem produces exceptional deterministic A. It proves no positive probability for these A under the contract's random model. It also does not establish that these A lack some other low-complexity cover. In fact their displayed cover has a short special-purpose description. A joint inverse/compression theorem is therefore not refuted merely by this construction; a theorem claiming that efficient covers force small ordinary doubling, high individual energy, or a large individual Fourier coefficient would be refuted.

## 1. The cyclic difference set

For a prime power q, put n=q^2+q+1. There is a set D subset Z/nZ with |D|=q+1 and

r_D(d)=1 for every d!=0.                                           (1.1)

For completeness, one construction uses F=F_(q^3) and the cyclic quotient F^*/F_q^*, of order n. Let D consist of the one-dimensional F_q-spaces generated by nonzero elements having trace zero. The trace kernel has dimension two, so it gives (q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1 quotient elements. For any multiplier gamma outside F_q^*, the conditions Tr(x)=Tr(gamma x)=0 are independent F_q-linear conditions. Their common kernel has dimension one and hence gives exactly one quotient element. Independence follows from nondegeneracy of the trace pairing; the latter follows because the trace polynomial is nonzero of degree q^2<q^3, and multiplication by any nonzero field element is bijective. This proves (1.1) after identifying the cyclic quotient with Z/nZ.

The construction is the classical Singer difference set. The inspected contemporary primary paper of Meszaros, Ronyai and Szabo records its exact parameters and difference property in Section 1.2; the argument above also supplies the property used here.

Its Fourier coefficients satisfy, for every nonzero j modulo n,

|D_hat_n(j)|^2 = (q+1)+sum_{d!=0}exp(-2pi i jd/n)=q.                (1.2)

Also E_n(D)=(q+1)^2+(n-1)=2q^2+3q+1.

## 2. A nearby prime and exact cardinality

Baker--Harman--Pintz, Theorem 1, states that every sufficiently large interval [x-x^0.525,x] contains a prime. The primary PDF's theorem was inspected. Set x=n+2n^0.525. Since x^0.525<2n^0.525 for sufficiently large n, the interval lies strictly above n at its left endpoint. Consequently one can choose a prime

p=n+h,  0<h<=2n^0.525.                                           (2.1)

In particular p<2n and 2h<n eventually. Taking any unbounded sequence of prime powers q gives an unbounded sequence of p, hence infinitely many distinct primes after removing repetitions.

Let k=floor(sqrt(p)). Delete one point d0 from D, and regard the remaining q points D0 as integers in [0,n-1], then as residues modulo p. Put u=k-q. Since p>q^2+q+1,

0<=u<=(q+1+h)/(2q)=O(p^0.025).                                   (2.2)

Adding u points outside D0 therefore reaches the exact required cardinality. Both the absolute gap bound and the padding estimate are necessary below. A bare assertion p/n->1 would not give the stated square-root-scale Fourier bound.

## 3. The exact cover and its modulus transfer

Choose a0 in D0 and put c=d0-a0 modulo n. For sufficiently large q, choose a0 so

c notin {h,-h,2h,-2h} modulo n.                                   (3.1)

There are q choices of a0 and at most four forbidden choices. Define

S=(-D) union(c-D) subset Z/nZ,

and use its representatives in [0,n-1]. Because c!=0, (1.1) says the two component sets meet in exactly one point, so |S|=2q+1. Since D is contained in D0 union(a0+c), every difference of elements of D lies in D0+S. Therefore

D0+S=Z/nZ.                                                       (3.2)

The following lift is elementary and does not preserve models by assumption. If B,S subset [0,n-1] satisfy B+S=Z/nZ, then every integer y in [0,n-1] has a representation b+s=y or y+n. Hence [0,n-1] is contained in B+(S union(S-n)). For n<=x<p<2n, apply this to y=x-n to see x belongs to B+((S+n) union S). Thus

T=S union(S-n) union(S+n) modulo p                                (3.3)

satisfies D0+T=Z/pZ. Every padded A containing D0 has the same cover. Moreover

|T|<=3(2q+1)<=6k+3<=floor(100 sqrt(p))

for the large primes under consideration. This checks both the prime-group target and the exact cap with a wide margin.

## 4. Energy survives the transfer

For any B subset [0,n-1] and p>=n, each residue modulo p receives at most two integer differences in [-(n-1),n-1]. Cauchy-Schwarz within each fiber gives

E_p(B)<=2 E_Z(B)<=2 E_n(B).                                      (4.1)

The second inequality holds because reduction modulo n only merges nonnegative difference counts. If B is a union of r translates of a set D in a common group, then

E(B)<=r^4 E(D).                                                  (4.2)

To verify (4.2), dominate 1_B by the sum of the r translated indicators. Energy is monotone for nonnegative functions, and the L4 triangle inequality for their Fourier transforms gives the factor r^4.

Applying (4.2) in Z/n, (4.1), and then (4.2) in Z/p yields

E_p(T)<=3^4 E_p(S)<=3^4*2*2^4 E_n(D)
        =2592(2q^2+3q+1)<=15552q^2<=15552k^2.                     (4.3)

No unproved random-energy estimate is used here.

## 5. Padding with bounded autocorrelation

For each nonzero residue d modulo p,

r_(D0,p)(d)<=2.                                                  (5.1)

Indeed, there are at most two corresponding nonzero integer differences. Each has multiplicity at most one by the perfect-difference property modulo n. Differences equal to +/-n do not occur inside [0,n-1].

Choose R uniformly among the u-subsets of G\D0. For every fixed d, a union bound over three-element subsets gives

Pr[|R intersect(D0+d)|>=3]
 <= binom(q,3)*(u)_3/(p-q)_3.

Summing over d gives O(p q^3 u^3/p^3)=O(p^-0.425), using (2.2); if u<3 the event is empty.

Also R is Sidon in the directed-difference sense with probability 1-o(1). A repeated nonzero ordered difference among distinct ordered pairs involves either four distinct elements (at most p^3 choices) or three distinct elements (O(p^2) choices). The probability all selected elements lie in R is at most (u/(p-q-3))^s for s distinct elements. The resulting union bound is

O(u^4/p+u^3/p)=o(1).

Two distinct elements cannot produce a repeated nonzero ordered difference in an odd prime group. Thus, with positive probability, both all shifted intersections above are at most two and r_R(d)<=1 for every d!=0. Fix one such R and put A=D0 union R. Decomposing an A-difference by the two component sets gives

r_A(d)<=2+1+2+2=7  (d!=0).

Consequently E(A)<=k^2+7k(k-1)<=8k^2. This is an existence argument for a good padding; arbitrary padding need not preserve the pointwise bound.

## 6. Fourier interpolation and the zero-mode leakage

Let B subset [0,n-1], and suppose its nonzero Fourier coefficients modulo n have magnitude at most M. Define B_hat_n(j)=sum_{b in B}exp(-2pi i jb/n). For theta=r/p with r!=0 modulo p, Fourier expansion on Z/n gives

sum_{b in B}exp(-2pi i b theta)=sum_{j=0}^{n-1} B_hat_n(j) K_j(theta),

where K_j(theta)=n^-1 sum_{a=0}^{n-1}exp(-2pi i a(theta-j/n)). The Dirichlet-kernel estimate

sum_j |K_j(theta)|=O(log n)                                     (6.1)

is uniform in theta. To see it, choose the closest j/n on the circle; its term is at most one, and terms at cyclic index-distance m are O(1/(m-1/2)), giving a harmonic sum.

The zero-mode term is much smaller than the bound one. Since r is nonzero modulo p, the full sum of p roots is zero, so

|sum_{a=0}^{n-1}exp(2pi i ar/p)|
 =|sum_{a=n}^{p-1}exp(2pi i ar/p)|<=h.

Therefore

|B_hat_p(r)|<=O(M log n)+|B|h/n.                                (6.2)

For D, M=sqrt(q). The second term is O(q h/n)=O(p^0.025). Removing one point and adding u=O(p^0.025) points changes each Fourier coefficient by at most 1+u. It follows that

max_{r!=0}|A_hat_p(r)|=O(sqrt(k) log p).                          (6.3)

For S, its two shifted copies of -D have a one-point intersection, so every nonzero Fourier coefficient modulo n has magnitude at most 2sqrt(q)+1. Equation (6.2) gives the same O(sqrt(k)log p) bound for S embedded modulo p.

To pass from S to the actual set-union T, repeated points must be controlled; simply summing Fourier coefficients of the three indicators would be wrong without this step. From (1.1),

r_(S,n)(d)<=4 for d notin {0,c,-c}.

As p=n+h and 2h<n, integer differences show

r_(S,p)(h)<=r_(S,n)(h)<=4,
r_(S,p)(2h)<=r_(S,n)(2h)+r_(S,n)(h)<=8,

where (3.1) is used. The three sets in (3.3) are S,S-h,S+h modulo p. Their three pair intersections have total size at most 4+4+8=16. Thus the nonnegative multiplicity correction between the sum of their indicators and 1_T has total mass at most 16. Consequently

|T_hat_p(r)|<=3|S_hat_p(r)|+16=O(sqrt(k)log p).                    (6.4)

In fact 6q-13<=|T|<=6q+3, so the covering multiplicity tends to 6.

## 7. Large doubling and the precise method obstruction

Cauchy-Schwarz gives |B+B|>=|B|^4/E(B), and the same bound for |B-B|. For A this gives |A+A|>=k^2/8. For T, the covering lower bound |T|>=p/k together with (4.3) and |T|<=6k+3 gives |T+T|/|T|=Omega(k). This proves all parts of the theorem.

The examples defeat implications of the form:

- low individual energy and large doubling force holes in every constant-multiplicity sumset;
- small nontrivial Fourier coefficients of both factors force a Poisson-like convolution distribution near zero;
- bounded autocorrelation of A alone forces a superconstant covering multiplicity.

They do not defeat every possible typical-A truncation. Indeed there is an explicit stronger boundary. Expanding the cyclic representatives in (3.3) gives T subset X-D, where

X={-n,0,n,2n,c-n,c,c+n,c+2n} modulo p.

Since D=D0 union {d0} and D0 subset A, one has T subset (X-A) union (X-d0), with at most eight exceptional centres outside X-A. Thus |G\(A+(X-A))|<=8k. These A explicitly violate the Stage 1 high-probability bounded-affine-envelope hole condition, which gives a positive proportion of holes for every such X. The construction cannot defeat a truncation incorporating that condition. No claim about absence of alternative small-doubling covers is made.

There is a useful additional moment warning. For x outside X, each term from a centre family xi-D contributes at most two representations from D0, by the nonzero prime-group difference bound for full D, and at most three from R, because |R intersect(d+D0)|<=2 and D adds only d0. Hence (1_A*1_T)(x)<=8*(2+3)=40 outside X. Coverage gives the lower bound one. Thus discarding the at most eight exceptionally high-multiplicity positions does not restore a zero in this example; joint shifted-copy structure remains essential. Nor are the displayed T claimed to have uniformly small nonzero autocorrelations: finite repeated-shift structure remains, despite small total energy and small Fourier coefficients. That distinction is important for any proposed stronger inverse theorem.

A direct first-moment consequence is available. If a predicate Good_p(A) includes even one of these A, and N_c(A) counts all covers of exact cap size c, then

E[1_Good_p(A) N_c(A)] >= binom(p-|T|,c-|T|)/binom(p,k)
                     >= exp((93+o(1)) k log k)

along the constructed prime sequence. Thus truncating only by conditions that admit these examples cannot repair the unrestricted cap-witness first moment. This observation concerns all witnesses, including redundant supersets; no claim about minimal-witness counts follows from it.
