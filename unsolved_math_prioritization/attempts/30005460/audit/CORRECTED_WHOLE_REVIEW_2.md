## Analytic proof and hidden-assumption checks

The coefficient field is real, the degree m is positive and even, and q is positive and odd. Nonnegativity follows from an SOS odd power. Allowing SOS summands that are arbitrary real polynomials does not create an escape: a highest-degree homogeneous sum of real squares cannot cancel to a lower-degree polynomial. In particular a degree-18 polynomial represented as SOS has every factor of degree at most nine. For homogeneous forms, extracting the homogeneous factor part at half the form degree gives the usual homogeneous SOS definition. Zero polynomials and unused variables cause no difficulty.

The closure proof is valid in finite coefficient topology. A nonzero linear combination of the finitely many monomials has strictly positive squared Gaussian integral, so G is positive definite. The bound tr(QG)≥λ_min(G) tr(Q), with Q positive semidefinite, bounds all Gram entries when coefficients converge. A convergent Gram subsequence therefore supplies an SOS limit. This is a proof of closure of the bounded-degree cone, not an assumption that every linear image of a closed cone is closed.

Separation applies to p itself in the finite-dimensional degree≤qm polynomial space. A non-SOS p cannot enter its enlarged SOS cone through high-degree cancellation. A closed convex cone and a point outside it admit a functional nonnegative on the cone and strictly negative on the point. The proof does not infer such separation merely from p^q being SOS. Adding sufficiently small εΓ keeps L(p)<0, makes its moment matrix positive definite, and makes L(1)>0 because 1 is an SOS and Γ(1)=1. Normalization is legitimate. There is no appeal to a representing measure.

For every finite s the product functional is defined on a finite monomial basis with degree at most 2d in each block. If H has total degree at most d, each of its monomials has degree at most d in every block. Consequently the Gram matrix for every coupled H, not merely products of local polynomials, is a principal submatrix of the finite tensor power M^⊗s. A tensor power of a positive semidefinite matrix is positive semidefinite. Every entry needed by H² and S_s^q lies in the specified blockwise domain. The product uses L(1)=1 for unused blocks. No infinite product measure, limiting matrix, convergence exchange or gigantic numerical object is needed.

Expansion is by partitions of q labelled factors. The falling factorial counts distinct assignments of partition blocks to variable copies; there is no extra factorial because the blocks of a set partition are already distinct subsets. The only partition with q blocks is the singleton partition, giving the sole degree-q term a1^q. All collisions have lower degree. For odd q and a1<0 the leading coefficient is negative, which implies strict negativity for all sufficiently large finite integer s. Small s can have positive values; the proof makes no claim that a specified small prefix already fails. Exact counting controls independently enumerate q=1,2,3,5,7 and s=1,2,4. The even-q control has positive leading coefficient, correctly preventing the odd-power argument from silently extending to even q.

A finite average outside the same ambient C is sufficient to refute convexity, since binary convexity implies closure under all finite convex combinations by induction. Scalar closure converts sums and averages. The least failing prefix gives existence of a violating pair in that same ambient ring; this does not assert membership of a chosen untested prefix. Adding unused variables preserves the obstruction by restriction back to zero. q=1 is consistent with classical SOS convexity, because the non-SOS seed then fails the required membership premise.

The seed is nonnegative by AM–GM. The exact coefficient-1 cube identity has 16 positively weighted degree-nine square factors.
