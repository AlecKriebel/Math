# Kirby 3.70 surgery generation audit

## Result

The unrestricted generation question is unresolved by this investigation. The disposition is `stalled_partial`, with three approaches used out of five. The rigorous output is a clarification of three failed implications, including an explicit calculation showing that the Nozaki--Sato--Taniguchi examples lie in the subgroup generated by genus-one knot surgeries. These are deductions from credited literature and elementary topology, with no novelty claim.

## Exact target and conventions

Let Theta denote the smooth, oriented integral homology cobordism group of integral homology 3-spheres. Addition is connected sum, the identity is [S^3], and -[Y]=[-Y]. An integral homology cobordism from Y0 to Y1 is a compact smooth oriented W with boundary -Y0 disjoint union Y1 for which both boundary inclusions induce integral homology isomorphisms.

The actual source is Baykur--Kirby--Ruberman, K3, Section 3.8, printed pages 181--182, Problem 3.70 [K3]. Its coefficient ring is Z, its cobordisms are smooth, and it asks whether the subgroup

G = < [S^3_{1/n}(K)] : n in Z, K a smooth knot in S^3 >

is all of Theta. Surgery uses the Seifert longitude and meridian convention: slope p/q kills p*mu+q*lambda. The convention 1/0 is meridional filling, giving S^3; it is not zero surgery. Deleting n=0 therefore leaves G unchanged. Coefficients 1/n for n positive and negative are included. Rational coefficients in the surgery notation do not replace integral homology cobordism by rational homology cobordism.

A positive solution requires a finite expression for every [Y], with no uniform genus bound or bound on the number of summands. A negative solution requires one [Y] outside G. Failure to be represented by a single surgery is a different statement.

For clarity, the generating set is closed under inverses: orientation reversal and mirroring give

-S^3_{1/n}(K) = S^3_{-1/n}(mirror(K)) = S^3_{1/(-n)}(mirror(K))

as oriented manifolds for n nonzero. Thus signed sums can also be written as connected sums of members of the displayed set.

## Approach 1  Bounded genus does not settle the union

For each positive integer N, let G_N be generated by surgeries on knots of Seifert genus less than N. Hendricks--Hom--Lidman, Theorem 1.5 [HHL], proves G_N is a proper subgroup of Theta for every N, allowing all integer denominators.

Proposition. G is the increasing union of the G_N.

Proof. Every G_N is contained in G. Conversely, an element of G is a finite integer linear combination of surgery classes. The finitely many knots in that expression have finite genera. Choose N strictly greater than their maximum genus. The entire expression belongs to G_N. The zero element belongs to every G_N. This proves equality.

The quantifiers proved by [HHL] are

for every N there exists x_N in Theta outside G_N.

The desired obstruction would be

there exists x in Theta such that for every N, x is outside G_N.

The former does not imply the latter. For an explicit countermodel, take A to be the direct sum over positive integers i of Z*e_i, and A_N to be the span of e_1,...,e_N. Then e_{N+1} witnesses that A_N is proper, while every element of A has finite support and lies in some A_N. Hence the union equals A.

The exact missing step is a single class excluded at every genus bound, or another obstruction to the union. This argument supplies neither. The algebraic countermodel is not a model of the homology cobordism group; it only disproves the proposed logical implication.

## Approach 2  The known nonrepresentable examples are already in G

Write T for the positive (2,3)-torus knot and A_n=[S^3_{1/n}(T)] for n positive. Nozaki--Sato--Taniguchi [NST], printed page 4703, records the oriented identity

Sigma(p,q,p*q*n-1) = -S^3_{1/n}(T_{p,q}).

Their Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.7 give, for k positive,

Y_k = Sigma(2,3,5) # Sigma(2,3,5) # -Sigma(2,3,6*k+5),

whose homology cobordism class has no representative obtained by one knot surgery. These statements concern exactly the smooth oriented category used here.

Proposition. [Y_k] = -2*A_1 + A_{k+1}, so [Y_k] belongs to G_2 for every positive k.

Proof. Substitute p=2, q=3, n=1 into the cited identity to obtain [Sigma(2,3,5)]=-A_1. With n=k+1, the last Seifert multiplicity is 6*(k+1)-1=6*k+5, so [Sigma(2,3,6*k+5)]=-A_{k+1}. Taking connected sum and reversing the last orientation gives the formula. All knots used are trefoils, which have genus one; the mirrors used to express inverses also have genus one. Hence every term lies in G_2.

This proves concretely that the set of classes represented by a single knot surgery is not closed under addition. It also shows that the absence of any definite bounding 4-manifold, which [NST] establishes for these Y_k, cannot by itself separate a class from G: these particular examples have that property and belong to G_2. There is no contradiction with [HHL]; G_2 is proper but contains these examples.

The exact missing step is a different obstruction vanishing on every surgery generator and remaining valid under all finite sums and inverses, or a direct proof of nonmembership in G. The existing nonrepresentability theorem does not provide it.

## Approach 3  A surgery trace is not a homology cobordism

A possible constructive strategy tries to replace a surgery presentation by a sequence of single-component surgeries and then telescope the changes in Theta. It requires justification both for the cobordisms and for the ambient manifold of each knot.

Lemma. Let Y be an integral homology sphere, and let W be Y times an interval with one 2-handle attached along a framed knot at the outgoing end. Then H_2(W;Z) is Z. In particular this particular surgery trace is not an integral homology cobordism.

Proof. The relative handle chain complex of (W,Y) has one copy of Z in degree 2 and zero in every other degree. Thus H_2(W,Y;Z)=Z. The exact sequence of the pair contains

H_2(Y;Z) -> H_2(W;Z) -> H_2(W,Y;Z) -> H_1(Y;Z).

Both outside groups vanish, so the middle arrow is an isomorphism. This applies even when the other boundary is also an integral homology sphere, for example for framing +1 or -1. It says nothing about the existence of some other homology cobordism.

The same calculation gives H_2(W;Z)=Z^m for a trace containing m 2-handles and no other relative handles. Unimodularity of a link surgery matrix may establish the homology of the outgoing boundary; it does not remove the second homology of this trace.

Also, after the first surgery, later components generally become knots in the surgered manifold rather than in S^3. The definition of G allows only the latter ambient manifold. Even if a presentation passes through integral homology spheres Y_i, writing the formal telescoping identity [Y_m]-[Y_0] as a sum of differences does not prove that those differences lie in G.

The missing input is a valid smooth integral homology cobordism construction yielding the required S^3 surgery summands. Neither sequential surgery nor an algebraic diagonalization of a linking matrix supplies that construction. No such construction was obtained here.
