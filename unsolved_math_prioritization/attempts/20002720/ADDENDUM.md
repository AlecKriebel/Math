# Post-audit addendum: scope and an additional source correction

The independent audit accepts **already_solved**, with five substantive approaches, for the original **formal scalar algebraic** problem. The outcome is an attribution to existing Hopf-character and faithful-matrix theory, not a new solution. The historical author status `independent_audit=pending` is preserved unchanged; RELEASE_ASSESSMENT.json records the later completed audit.

For every finite number of noncommuting variables, the scalar formal unit group consists exactly of series with nonzero linear coefficients over C. Over a general commutative unital ring, the coefficients must be units. A faithful matrix-valued multiplicative transform is defined on the associated formal distributions with invertible means. No claim of a canonical or minimal representation, literal equality of these matrices with the classical one-variable scalar S-series, analytic universality, or complete center classification is made.

## Additional correction to the 2013 source

Friedrich--McKay, *Almost Commutative Probability Theory*, Example 6.1 on printed page 32, identifies the derived subgroup of the normalized two-variable group U_2 with F^3, the subgroup having zero degree-two coefficients. This equality fails.

Fix a letter i. Projection P_i onto coefficients of the pure words i^n is a homomorphism to the abelian one-variable boxed group. It sends every commutator, and therefore every product of commutators, to the identity z. But e+z_i^3 is in F^3 and has projection z+z^3. Thus only the safe inclusion

    [U_s,U_s] is contained in F^3 U_s intersected with every kernel of P_i

is recorded here; equality is not asserted. This is valid over every nonzero commutative coefficient ring. The frozen author proof never used the source's false equality, so its result is unaffected.

See the preserved [independent correction note](independent-audit/CORRECTIONS.md) and [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6194v1). The broader source errors already documented in the author packet are likewise excluded from the accepted proof.
