# Literature status

## Original question

AIM, *Braid groups, clusters and free probability*, Problem 5.2, printed pp. 10--11:
https://aimath.org/WWN/braidgroups/braidgroups.pdf

The original question asks for an algebraic structure description and a higher S-transform. Its statement does not specify a unique minimal matrix representation or analytic universality.

## Verified normalized-group description

Mitja Mastnak and Alexandru Nica, *Hopf algebras and the logarithm of the S-transform in free probability*, arXiv:0807.4169v2; Transactions of the AMS 362 (2010), 3705--3743:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4169v2
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-10-04995-0

Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 3.7 identify normalized distributions with characters of the explicit connected graded Hopf algebra; Section 3 proves its structure. Pages 2--5 and 12--16 were inspected, including the coassociativity argument. The LS-transform linearizes commuting products, not every product. It recovers the logarithm of the scalar S-transform for one variable. The theorem page was visually checked. The publisher page timed out; the author's 2013 talk independently lists the journal reference:
https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anica/Boxtimes2013.pdf

## Full-group and matrix-transform interpretation

Roland Friedrich and John McKay, *The S-transform in arbitrary dimensions*, arXiv:1308.0733v1, and *Almost Commutative Probability Theory*, arXiv:1309.6194v1 (2013):
https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0733v1
https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6194v1

The longer manuscript's Sections 6.1--6.4 give unit-group coordinates, quotient structure, Hopf algebras, and faithful triangular representations. Theorem 6.24 supplies the multiplicativity interpretation. Relevant proofs on pp. 27--43 were inspected; p. 43 was visually checked. Both were found as preprints, not established here as peer-reviewed publications. Their abstract claims alone are not the classification basis: the explicit regular representation in STRUCTURE.md verifies the usable algebraic construction. Several printed overstatements fail elementary tests and are excluded there. Minimality/canonicity is not certified.

## Scalar normalization check

Alexandru Nica, *R-transforms in free probability*, Section 14, pp. 1--2:
https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anica/NOTES/section14.pdf

Notation 14.2 and Theorem 14.3 specify the scalar transform and its correct ordinary-series target. This fixes a typo in the AIM remark.

## Decision

Classify the original formal algebraic problem as already solved by existing Hopf and representation theory. The packet supplies verification and clarification, with no first-resolution claim. It does not endorse every statement in the 2013 manuscripts, nor infer a uniquely preferred analytic S-transform. The exact center remains outside this classification. Literature searching was bounded and cannot certify exhaustive priority or absence of later refinements.
