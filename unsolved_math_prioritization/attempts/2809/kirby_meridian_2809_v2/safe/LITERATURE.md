# Sources and bounded literature assessment

Checked 5 October 2026. No universal proof or knot counterexample was located in the searches described in the metadata. This is a bounded search, not proof of the absence of newer or unindexed work.

1. Baykur, Kirby, and Ruberman, editors, *K3: A New Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topology*, Problem 3.11, printed p.139. [Author-hosted preliminary PDF](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf). The precise target is the maximal-cusp meridian of a hyperbolic knot in S³; the inequality is non-strict, ≤4. The downloaded page spells “every” as “ever,” an apparent typographical error with no mathematical effect. Its four remarks distinguish knots from links. The PDF calls itself a preliminary version of an AMS-published book; the publisher storefront was inaccessible in this check. The inspected file is not presented as a independently verified final publisher edition.

2. Ian Agol, *Bounds on exceptional Dehn filling*, Geometry & Topology 4 (2000), 431–449. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906183v2), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2140/gt.2000.4.431). Theorem 6.2 gives the strict length>6 filling criterion; Section 7 supplies knot meridians approaching 4. The source's conclusion is “hyperbolike”; infinitude of the fundamental group already excludes S³ without requiring a modern reformulation. The entire proof of the filling theorem was not independently re-proved.

3. C. Adams, A. Colestock, J. Fowler, W. Gillam, and E. Katerman, *Cusp size bounds from singular surfaces in hyperbolic 3-manifolds*, Transactions of the AMS 358 (2006), 727–741. [Coauthor-hosted article](https://kisonecat.github.io/research/cusp-size-bounds.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-05-03662-7). Theorem 3.1 supplies m≤6−7/c; the proof and its boundary-compression qualification were inspected. This improves 6 strictly for each knot but does not reach 4 uniformly. The numerical-model discussion only claims satisfaction of specified inequalities, not a realization theorem.

4. Stephan D. Burton and Efstratia Kalfagianni, *Geometric estimates from spanning surfaces*, Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 49 (2017), 694–708. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05035v2), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12063). Theorem 2.4 supplies the pleated-surface cusp-length input; Theorems 4.1 and 1.2 give the two-surface and adequate-diagram estimates. The normal-surface algorithm tests existence of a sufficient pair. A negative answer to that pair-existence test is not a certificate that m>4. Essentiality and the special class must not be dropped. The low-crossing numerical aside in Corollary 1.3 is not used here; no census calculation was reproduced. Its §5 explicitly identifies the Dunfield obstruction to universal distinct-slope essential spanning pairs; see item 7 below.

5. Jessica S. Purcell, *Slope lengths and generalized augmented links*, Communications in Analysis and Geometry 16 (2008), 883–905. [Author-hosted preprint](https://users.monash.edu/~jpurcell/papers/jpurcell-slope.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4310/CAG.2008.v16.n4.a7). Theorem 3.4 bounds the knotting-strand meridian in the augmented link strictly below 4. Section 4 gives families approaching 4, including Corollary 4.4. These statements concern a specific class and long-filling limits; they do not prove the desired bound for every filling of every augmented link.

6. Matthias Görner, *Regular Tessellation Link Complements*, Experimental Mathematics 24 (2015), 225–246. [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2827v3), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2014.986310). Theorem 2.4, Table 1, cusp-modulus definitions, and the discussion of link-complement certificates and exceptional fillings were inspected. K3 specifically cites these link examples for lengths √21>4. No full rerun of the author's computational certificates or independent geometric reconstruction of that numerical example was performed. This link fact is background, not a premise needed in any unconditional proof here. It supplies no knot counterexample.

7. Nathan M. Dunfield, *A knot without a nonorientable essential spanning surface*, Illinois J. Math. 60 (2016), 179–184. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06653v2), Theorem 1.3. This is the obstruction discussed in Burton–Kalfagianni §5; its published computations were not independently rerun here.

The primary statement was reopened, and independent searches included the exact question, “meridian length conjecture,” “meridian length” with knot/hyperbolic and 2024–2026, the named primary papers, and possible counterexamples. A 2024 conference-program description of selected m-almost-alternating classes was located but is not treated as a proved universal theorem. Search-result timestamps were not treated as publication dates.
