# Refined slippery bound: five-route partial investigation

Problem 30002129, OWR-12007-009, rank 714. Author freeze: 2026-10-05 UTC.

**Disposition: unresolved, five substantive approach families.** There is no complete proof or counterexample here, and no claim of novelty, priority, exhaustive literature coverage, formal verification, or human peer review. Independent review is pending. The sharp equal-denominator case below is prior work of Subhadip Chowdhury, not a resolution of the general question.

## 1. Exact question and normalization

Let H be the group of increasing homeomorphisms f of R satisfying f(x+1)=f(x)+1. Write T(x)=x+1 and

τ(f)=lim(n→∞)(f^n(x)-x)/n.

This is the real translation number of a specified lift, not its class modulo Z. For the free group generated by a,b, let R(w,r,s) be the supremum of τ(w(f,g)) over f,g∈H with τ(f)=r, τ(g)=s. Composition acts on the left, so the rightmost letter is applied first.

For a positive cyclic word containing both letters, write its alternating blocks as a^{α1}b^{β1}⋯a^{αm}b^{βm}, with every exponent a positive integer. Set A=Σαi and B=Σβi. There are m block pairs, or 2m individual blocks. The conjecture is:

If R(w,r,s)=p/q with gcd(p,q)=1 and q>0, then

0 ≤ R(w,r,s)−Ar−Bs ≤ m/q.                         (RC)

The lower bound follows by using rigid translations. The q in RC is the reduced denominator of the extremal value, not either input denominator and not an unreduced denominator. For pure powers the defect vanishes identically.

The primary workshop source is Calegari's joint report with Walker, pp. 2173–2174 of Oberwolfach Report 35/2012, DOI 10.4171/OWR/2012/35. The page labelled 2174 displays the refined inequality; the corresponding §3.7 of Calegari–Walker explicitly supplies the reduced-fraction convention. The exact live catalogue URL was attempted but returned 403/unavailable; its statement was not inspected. The descriptor's title/ID/source mapping, rather than its guessed mathematical content, was used to identify the primary text. Raw AI corpus records were absent and uninspected.

Integer shifts cause R(w,r+j,s+k)=R(w,r,s)+Aj+Bk, since T is central. Both sides of the defect and the reduced output denominator are unchanged. Thus input normalization to [0,1) is legitimate; reducing only the final number modulo 1 is not. Cyclic conjugation of w preserves every translation number and its supremum.

## 2. Approach 1: conjugacy averaging and a global defect estimate

This route proves the known weaker estimate R−Ar−Bs≤m (hence RC when q=1). Calegari–Walker Lemma 2.11 states this estimate; the following direct proof is retained to show exactly what it supplies.

For any F∈H and n≥1, put D_n(x)=F^n(x)−x. This is periodic. If x<y<x+1, then 0<F^n(y)−F^n(x)<1; consequently the oscillation of D_n is at most 1. If d_n=inf D_n and e_n=sup D_n, iteration of F^n shows d_n≤nτ(F)≤e_n. Therefore

|F^n(x)−x−nτ(F)|≤1 for all x,n.                  (2.1)

Fix an integer K≥1 and define h_K(x)=K^{-1}Σ_{i=0}^{K−1}(F^i(x)−iτ(F)). It is an increasing onto homeomorphism commuting with T. Telescoping gives

h_K(F(x))−h_K(x)=(F^K(x)−x)/K.

By (2.1), the conjugate F_K=h_K F h_K^{-1} has displacement between r−1/K and r+1/K. Conjugate G by the same h_K, obtaining G_K; its translation number stays s because h_K(x)−x is bounded. Applied to G_K^{β}, (2.1) gives G_K^{β}(x)−x≤βs+1. Each a block contributes at most α(r+1/K), and each b block contributes at most βs+1. Composing these pointwise displacement bounds yields

τ(w(F,G))=τ(w(F_K,G_K))≤Ar+Bs+m+A/K.

Letting K tend to infinity and taking the supremum proves the estimate. Rigid translations prove its lower bound.

**Obstacle.** This argument spends one whole unit at each b block. It does not use the periodic orbit of the extremal word and has no mechanism to divide those errors by q. Applying it to w^q gives qm block pairs and, after division by q, the same m bound. That operation cannot prove RC.

## 3. Approach 2: the exact two-letter formula and word powers

The classical Jankins–Neumann–Naimi result, in Calegari–Walker's Theorem 3.9 formulation, says for 0≤r,s<1 that

R(ab,r,s)=sup_{k≥1}(floor(kr)+floor(ks)+1)/k.       (3.1)

This is a credited input, not proved by finite experiments below. We give the full deduction of RC for ab and its powers.

Let C=R(ab,r,s)=c/d in reduced form and E=C−r−s≥0. If E=0 the desired inequality holds. If E>0, every candidate with k>2/E is less than r+s+E/2, so the supremum in (3.1) is attained among a finite set of k. Choose a maximizing k. Then d divides k, and

E=(floor(kr)+floor(ks)+1−k(r+s))/k≤1/k≤1/d.

For w=(ab)^m, homogeneity τ(h^m)=mτ(h) gives R(w,r,s)=mC. If this value is rational, C is rational. Its reduced denominator is q=d/gcd(d,m). Thus

R(w,r,s)−mr−ms=mE≤m/d≤m/q.

Integer shifts extend this to every real r,s. Cyclic conjugates have the same result.

**Obstacle.** A word with unequal block exponents is not generally a power of ab. Replacing repeated occurrences of a or b by unrelated maps changes the feasible representation set. Nor is the reduced denominator of a sum or product-word translation number controlled by denominators of individual block values. Formula (3.1) does not provide a valid induction over arbitrary blocks.

## 4. Approach 3: a full-period orbit average

**Credited special case.** Chowdhury, 2018 dissertation, Proposition 6.2.2 (printed pp. 39–40), proves RC when one input and R have the same reduced denominator. Its hypothesis is essential. Here is a direct proof for rational inputs, avoiding an assumption that all gap sizes are equal. The extension to real values of the other input follows from the fixed-rational-input right continuity stated in Calegari–Walker Lemmas 3.3–3.4 and the proof of Theorem 3.7.

Let r=P/N and s=U/V be reduced rationals normalized to [0,1), and assume R(w,r,s)=C/N with gcd(C,N)=1. We use Calegari–Walker's exact positive-word model (§§3.1–3.2): choose a cyclic order of N points X and V disjoint points Y; a sends a point to the first X weakly to its right, followed by P further X positions, and b does the analogous operation with U Y positions. The supremum over homeomorphisms equals the maximum over these finitely many cyclic orders. These monotone step maps are a computation device; they are not asserted to be homeomorphisms themselves.

Index lifted X points by integers, X_{i+N}=X_i+1. By a cyclic conjugation of w, start just after an a block. The extremal model has a w-cycle of length N, hence this cycle uses all X points. At every intermediate phase of the word its N images are distinct modulo 1: otherwise applying the remaining suffix would identify two points of the w-cycle. After any a block, therefore, the image set again consists of all X points.

Let t_i be the number of Y points strictly between X_i and X_{i+1}; these are periodic nonnegative integers with Σ_{i=0}^{N−1}t_i=V. Consider a phase b^{β}, followed by a^{α}. The b outputs must lie in distinct X gaps, since otherwise the next a map would collapse two images. The increasing map from all N input X indices to all N output gap indices is consequently i↦i+l for a fixed integer l≥0.

Starting at X_i, b^{β} lands on the (βU+1)-st subsequent Y. Since that point is in the gap after X_{i+l}, the preceding l full gaps contain at most βU Y points:

Σ_{h=0}^{l−1}t_{i+h}≤βU for every i.

Summing over all N starts counts each gap exactly l times, giving lV≤NβU, or l/N≤βs. The following a^{α} lands at X_{i+l+1+αP}. Summing the m phase shifts over one word gives

C=AP+Σ_{j=1}^m(l_j+1).

It follows that C/N−Ar−Bs=m/N+Σ_j(l_j/N−β_js)≤m/N, as required. The lower bound is the rigid-translation representation.

If s is real, keep r=P/N fixed and choose rational s_k decreasing to s in the right-hand constancy interval for R. Apply the rational result to each s_k and pass to the limit. Exchanging a and b gives the symmetric case.

**Obstacle.** When the output has q<N points, a phase sees a proper subset of X. Its endpoints can be distinct without occupying all X gaps. The constant shift i↦i+l and the uniform multiplicity l of the gap sum no longer follow. This is exactly the hypothesis missing from the general question; neither source asserts otherwise.

## 5. Approach 4: non-full orbit combinatorics and a failed local extension

Keep r=P/N, s=U/V rational but let the extremal cycle have q≤N points and translation c/q reduced. For each of the mq b phases in w^q, denote its starting index by i and the gap of its endpoint by i+l. The following a block moves to index i+l+1+αP. Total displacement of w^q in X indices gives the exact identity

cN=qAP+mq+Σ l.                                    (5.1)

If β is that phase's exponent, the full gaps before its endpoint satisfy Σ_{h=0}^{l−1}t_{i+h}≤βU. Thus the desired bound is equivalent, for a maximizing model, to

Σ l ≤ qNB(U/V)+m(N−q).                            (5.2)

This is a reduction, not a proof of (5.2). The weighted inequalities sum to Σ_h M_h t_h≤qBU, where M_h is the number of times gap h occurs. They do not directly bound the unweighted sum Σ_h M_h=Σ l.

A tempting repair would bound each phase separately for q distinct starts and distinct endpoint gaps:

Σ_{selected starts}l ≤ qNβ(U/V)+(N−q).             (false)

Here is an exact counterexample to that auxiliary assertion. Take N=5, V=100, U=1, β=1 and cyclic order

X Y X Y X Y X Y X Y^{96}.

Start b at X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3. Each output is in the next X gap, so the four l values are 1 and their endpoint gaps are distinct. Their sum is 4, while the proposed right side is 4·5/100+1=6/5. With P=1 the next a map sends these points to X indices {3,4,0,1}, which is not the starting set {0,1,2,3}. Consequently this example is not a closed four-point w-cycle and is not a counterexample to RC. It pinpoints why global orbit closure cannot be discarded in a phase-by-phase proof.

The exact oracle in verify_math.py enumerates all cyclic X/Y orders (rotated to begin with X), forms integer lift maps, and follows each composed map until a residue repeats. If the residue repeats after k steps with integer-index displacement h(N+V), its translation number is h/k. Monotonicity and degree one make this number independent of starting point; a direct comparison sandwiches any two lift orbits within a fixed integer translate. Maximizing these values uses the cited positive-word model theorem.

It tests the 10 distinct rational numbers in [0,1) with reduced denominator at most 5 and twelve specified words, giving 1,200 word/parameter cases and 47,700 configurations. RC holds in this bounded list. The two-letter oracle is cross-checked against (3.1): k up to L=lcm(N,V) suffices, since k=L gives r+s+1/L, while every k>L gives at most r+s+1/k. Reversal and power identities are also checked. No floating-point simulation is used.

The supremum condition is also essential. Define a lift f on [0,1] by f(x)=5x for 0≤x≤1/6 and f(x)=5/6+(x−1/6)/5 for 1/6≤x≤1, and extend by integer translation. It is an increasing homeomorphism fixing 0. Put g=f^{-1} composed with translation by 2/3. Since f(1/6)=5/6, g fixes 1/6. Thus τ(f)=τ(g)=0 but fg is exactly translation by 2/3. Substituting the translation number of this nonextremal representation into RC would incorrectly demand 2/3≤1/3. The actual supremum is R(ab,0,0)=1. This is a counterexample only to a variant that drops extremality. Exact piecewise-linear identities are checked in the script.

Another negative control shows that replacing the output denominator by an input denominator is wrong: R(ab,1/5,1/5)=1 by (3.1), so the defect is 3/5, exceeding the falsely substituted 1/5.

**Obstacle.** No all-N,V,q proof of (5.2) was obtained. Increasing a finite enumeration limit does not prove it, and the false auxiliary inequality cannot substitute for it. No original-conjecture counterexample was found.

## 6. Approach 5: lower-left limits and dihedral structure

Define L(w;r,s)=sup_{r'<r,s'<s}R(w,r',s'). This is R(w,r−,s−), distinct from the pointwise value R(w,r,s). A pair is slippery if every value in that strict lower-left quadrant is less than L. In the workshop formulation this definition is given without restricting r,s to rationals; the paper's Definition 3.19 singles out rational pairs.

For a slippery pair, take increasing rational r_j→r and s_j→s. Positivity makes R_j=R(w,r_j,s_j) increase to L: any point in the strict quadrant is eventually dominated. Every R_j is rational by the positive-word theorem. The values lie in a bounded interval by §2, and no R_j equals L. Their reduced denominators q_j tend to infinity. Indeed, if q_j≤M infinitely often, those bounded rationals form a finite set; an infinite constant subsequence would force the increasing sequence's limit to be that attained value, contradicting slipperiness.

Consequently, **if RC were proved at all rational inputs**, then

0≤R_j−Ar_j−Bs_j≤m/q_j→0,

and L=Ar+Bs. This only proves a conditional implication. At a non-slippery rational pair, the supremum is attained somewhere strictly below; replacing that lower pair by a larger rational pair still strictly below the target preserves the value. Rationality of R at rational inputs then makes L rational. These observations recover the source's qualitative consequence without identifying it with RC.

One unconditional lower-left value has a short algebraic proof. The source's rotation-conjugacy interpretation identifies L(w;1/2,1/2) with the supremum over lifts f,g conjugate to translation by 1/2. Such lifts satisfy f²=g²=T. Put c=fg^{-1}. Then gcg^{-1}=c^{-1}, so conjugacy invariance and inversion of τ give τ(c)=0. Since f=cg, every positive word of length ℓ=A+B reduces using gc=c^{-1}g to c^k g^ℓ for an integer k. If ℓ is even, g^ℓ=T^{ℓ/2} and its translation number is ℓ/2. If ℓ is odd, (c^kg)^2=T, so τ(c^kg)=1/2 and again the total is ℓ/2. Thus every such representation has the same value, and

L(w;1/2,1/2)=(A+B)/2.                             (6.1)

This is the dihedral special case already noted by Calegari–Walker. It does not require f and g to commute. For comparison, the elementary commuting case gives τ(w(f,g))=Ar+Bs: write w=f^A g^B, apply (2.1) to f^{An},g^{Bn}, and divide their combined error of at most 2 by n.

**Obstacle.** Formula (6.1) concerns a restricted, rotation-conjugate class and a lower-left limit. It does not assert that (1/2,1/2) is slippery for every word, nor control the pointwise R there. Even proving the qualitative slippery equality everywhere would not yield the quantitative finite-q inequality RC.

## 7. Outcome and remaining target

The retained partials are: a proved global m bound; RC for powers of ab; the credited equal-input/output-denominator theorem with a direct orbit proof; an exact non-full-orbit reduction and a counterexample to an overstrong local step; and the conditional slippery-limit implication with an unconditional dihedral value. The finite checks are supplementary evidence only.

The unresolved core is the sharp m/q estimate when an extremal orbit occupies a proper subset of both generator orbits, together with the appropriate passage to all real parameters in the original pointwise formulation. No source inspected proves the general statement. The literature search is bounded and does not certify that no later solution exists.
