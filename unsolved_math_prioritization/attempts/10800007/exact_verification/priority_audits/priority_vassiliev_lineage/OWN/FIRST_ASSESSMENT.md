Along the circle ζ=r exp(it), its quotient by sqrt(r) exp(it/2) is a continuous ±1-valued function, but the endpoint denominator changes sign while the section value repeats. Thus no such neighborhood has a section. Any open cover contains a member through zero, so no admissible cover exists at any cardinality. This is an elementary deduction from the classical square-root obstruction and the source's explicitly acknowledged embedding. The deduction is mathematically clear, but it does not by itself establish that the exact full local/no-cover conclusion had previously been published, nor establish historical novelty.

The source's square-root covering-number-two claim is compatible with the nonzero complex parameter domain; it is incompatible with an exact open-cover interpretation over all C. Possible intended deletions or alternative covers remain hypotheses until supported by primary documentation.
