# Spectrality via countable-field descent

Let K be an algebraically closed uncountable field, let 1 <= n < infinity,
let S = K[x_ij : i in N, j in [n]], and let G = union_m Sym(m) act by
permuting the first index. Let X = Spec(S) with all prime ideals as points,
and let Q = KQ(X/G), using the topological orbit quotient followed by
identification of topologically indistinguishable points.

**Claim. Q is spectral.**

The sole specialized input is the known equivariant Noetherianity theorem for
S: every ascending chain of G-stable ideals stabilizes. The directly inspected
source is Hillar–Sullivant, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 3.5 (arXiv:0908.1777v2,
PDF pp. 1–2 and 11–12); the corollary’s proof explicitly uses permutations
fixing all sufficiently large indices. Cohen 1987, Theorem 7, is the historical
attribution given by Kummer–Riener; Cohen’s original paper was not retrieved. All generic-point existence steps are proved below. The
uncountability of K is part of the requested setting, but the proof only needs
algebraic closedness.

## 1. Closed subsets and equivariant primality

Write q: X -> Q for the composite quotient. Closed subsets of X/G correspond
under inverse image to G-stable closed subsets of X. All closed subsets of any
topological space are saturated for topological indistinguishability, so the
Kolmogorov quotient does not change this lattice of closed subsets. Thus closed
subsets of Q correspond to G-stable radical ideals I of S, with q^{-1}(C)=V(I).

For an ideal P put core_G(P) = intersection_{g in G} gP. For P prime,
closure_X(GP) = V(core_G(P)). This closure is G-stable and is the smallest
G-stable closed set containing P. Consequently

  q^{-1}(closure_Q({q(P)})) = V(core_G(P)).                 (1)

A proper G-stable ideal I is called G-prime if for all G-stable ideals A,B,
AB subset I implies A subset I or B subset I. If C is a nonempty irreducible
closed subset of Q and q^{-1}(C)=V(I), then I is radical and G-prime: from
AB subset I we obtain V(I) subset V(A) union V(B), a union of G-stable closed
subsets. Descending to Q and using irreducibility shows V(I) subset V(A)
or V(I) subset V(B), hence A subset I or B subset I because I is radical.
This does not assert that V(I) is ordinarily irreducible, or that I is prime.

## 2. A countable reduced ring lemma

**Lemma 1.** Let R be a nonzero countable commutative reduced ring with a group
H acting by ring automorphisms. Suppose the product of any two nonzero
H-stable ideals is nonzero. Then a prime ideal p of R satisfies
intersection_{h in H} hp = 0.

**Proof.** For any nonzero a,b in R there is h in H with a h(b) != 0. Otherwise,
for every u,v in H,

  u(a) v(b) = u(a (u^{-1}v)(b)) = 0,

so the nonzero H-stable ideals generated by Ha and Hb have zero product,
contradicting the hypothesis.

Enumerate all nonzero elements as a_1,a_2,... (repeat elements if R is finite).
Inductively choose h_i so that t_m = product_{i=1}^m h_i(a_i) is nonzero for
every m, starting from t_0=1 and using the preceding observation.

Let T be the multiplicative set generated by the h_i(a_i). Then 0 is not in T.
Indeed, a monomial z = product_{i=1}^m h_i(a_i)^{e_i}, with e_i >= 0,
divides t_m^d for d=max(1,e_1,...,e_m). If z=0 then t_m^d=0, contrary to
reducedness and t_m != 0. This explicitly checks repeated powers; nonzero
squarefree partial products alone would not suffice in a nonreduced ring.

By the prime ideal theorem there is a prime p disjoint from T (equivalently,
contract a maximal ideal of the nonzero localization T^{-1}R). For every
nonzero a_i, h_i(a_i) is not in p, so a_i is not in h_i^{-1}p. Thus no nonzero
element belongs to intersection_h hp. QED.

## 3. Two field-extension facts

**Lemma 2.** For a field extension k subset L, a k-vector space V, and any
family of subspaces V_alpha,

  (intersection_alpha V_alpha) tensor_k L
    = intersection_alpha (V_alpha tensor_k L)

as subspaces of V tensor_k L.

**Proof.** Choose a k-basis E of L. Each tensor has a unique expression as a
finite sum sum_{e in F} v_e tensor e. Membership in V_alpha tensor_k L is
equivalent to v_e in V_alpha for every e in F, as one sees by choosing a basis
of V_alpha and extending it to a basis of V. Hence simultaneous membership
for all alpha is exactly v_e in intersection_alpha V_alpha for every e. QED.

This statement uses scalar extension of vector spaces. It does not make the
false assertion that arbitrary flat ring extensions commute with arbitrary
intersections of ideals.

**Lemma 3.** If k is algebraically closed, A is a commutative k-domain (not
necessarily finitely generated), and L/k is a field extension, then A tensor_k L
is a domain.

**Proof.** Suppose u,v are nonzero tensors with uv=0. Write u=sum_i a_i tensor
lambda_i and v=sum_j b_j tensor mu_j with the a_i linearly independent over k,
the b_j linearly independent over k, and lambda_1,mu_1 nonzero. Let B be the
finitely generated k-subalgebra of L containing all lambda_i and mu_j. The
map A tensor_k B -> A tensor_k L is injective since tensoring k-vector spaces
is exact, so uv=0 already in A tensor_k B. The nonzero finitely generated
k-algebra B[(lambda_1 mu_1)^{-1}] has a k-valued point by the ordinary weak
Nullstellensatz. Specialize coefficients by the resulting homomorphism B -> k.
The specialized u and v remain nonzero by the two linear independence conditions
and the nonvanishing of lambda_1,mu_1. Their product in A is zero, contradicting
that A is a domain. QED.

This is also the standard geometric-integrality argument for domains over an
algebraically closed field. It covers all characteristics and needs no
finite-type assumption on A.

## 4. Descent and a prime with the required core

**Lemma 4.** Every proper G-stable radical G-prime ideal I of S is core_G(P)
for some ordinary prime ideal P of S.

**Proof.** The monomials in the countable set of variables form a countable
K-basis of S. Therefore I has a finite or countable K-vector-space basis.
Collect all coefficients occurring in its basis polynomials. This is a
countable set. Let k be the algebraic closure, inside K, of the subfield
it generates over the prime field. Then k is countable and algebraically
closed. (A field generated by countably many elements over a countable prime
field is countable; the set of elements algebraic over it in K is countable
and is algebraically closed because K is.)

Set S_0=k[x_ij] and I_0=I intersection S_0. Every chosen basis polynomial of
I belongs to I_0, so I=I_0 S. Contraction shows that I_0 is proper and radical.
The given permutation action fixes k, hence preserves S_0 and I_0.

The ideal I_0 is G-prime. Namely, for G-stable ideals A_0,B_0 of S_0 with
A_0 B_0 subset I_0, their extended ideals A_0 S and B_0 S are G-stable and
have product in I. Since I is G-prime, one is contained in I. Contracting
shows A_0 subset I_0 or B_0 subset I_0.

Now R_0=S_0/I_0 is a nonzero countable reduced G-prime ring. By Lemma 1 it
has a prime p_0 with zero core. Let P_0 be its inverse image in S_0. Then
P_0 is prime and intersection_g gP_0 = I_0.

Put P=P_0 S. There is a natural isomorphism

  S/P = (S_0/P_0) tensor_k K.

It is a domain by Lemma 3, so P is an ordinary prime of S. Because the group
action commutes with scalar extension, Lemma 2 gives

  core_G(P)
    = intersection_g ((gP_0) tensor_k K)
    = (intersection_g gP_0) tensor_k K
    = I_0 tensor_k K
    = I.

The tensor equalities are inside S=S_0 tensor_k K. QED.

## 5. Sobriety and every spectral hypothesis

Let C be any nonempty irreducible closed subset of Q. By Section 1 its
inverse-image ideal I is radical and G-prime. Lemma 4 gives P with core_G(P)=I.
Equation (1) and surjectivity of q show closure_Q({q(P)})=C. Thus every such
C has a generic point. Q is T0 by its definition, so that generic point is
unique. This proves sobriety; no generic point is required to be closed.

The equivariant Noetherianity theorem for the finite-width polynomial
ring implies that ascending chains of G-stable radical ideals stabilize.
The closed-subset correspondence in Section 1 therefore proves Q is
Noetherian. Every open subset of a Noetherian space is quasi-compact:
otherwise the directed set of finite unions from an open cover would have
no maximal member, yielding a strictly increasing chain of opens. In
particular Q is quasi-compact. Its quasi-compact opens form a basis, since
all opens are quasi-compact. Intersections of two quasi-compact opens are
open and hence quasi-compact. Together with sobriety these are all spectral
space axioms (Hochster; Stacks Project, Tag 08YF). Hence Q is spectral. QED.

The same argument proves, in the exact original setting, the conclusion of
Kummer--Riener Lemma 3.25 without its conditional hypothesis: every G-stable
closed subset of X is a finite union of orbit closures. Indeed its image in
the Noetherian sober space Q has finitely many irreducible components; take
a generic point for each, lift to Spec(S), and use (1).

## 6. Scope checks and limitations

- G is exactly the finite-support symmetric group. All translates selected
  in Lemma 1 lie in this G. No infinite permutation is substituted.
- Points are ordinary prime ideals, including nonclosed points. For example,
  the diagonal ideal generated by x_i1-x_j1 (and x_ij for j>1) yields a
  G-fixed copy of Spec(K[t]); its generic point is not a K-rational point.
  Rational points of that diagonal alone have no generic point. This guards
  against the wrong rational-points-only interpretation.
- No invariant-ring or GIT quotient occurs. In fact S^G=K, whereas Q has many
  distinct points; replacing Q by Spec(S^G) would erase the target.
- Ordinary irreducibility of V(I) is not used. The relevant property is
  irreducibility in the G-stable closed-set lattice, encoded by radical
  G-primality.
- No ordinary Noetherianity of S is assumed. Its orbit ideal (G x_11) is not
  finitely generated as an ordinary ideal, so a standard locally-ideal-finite
  core-surjectivity theorem cannot simply be substituted for Lemma 4.
- The countable field is chosen separately for each I; a single field of
  definition for all ideals is not claimed. Countability of the monomial
  basis makes the descent valid even if I needs infinitely many ordinary
  generators.
- No transfinite enumeration of elements of K is used.
- The mathematical argument passed an internal AI mathematical audit. This is
  not journal peer review or publication acceptance. Schnieper’s announced
  2024 proof is credited above; its detailed
  assumptions and argument have not been inspected. The n=1 case was already
  credited prior work; the argument here covers every finite positive n.
