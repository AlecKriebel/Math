# Source and current status: uniform sums

The exact source is Ben Green's current Problem 28, printed page 16 of the December 2025 update to 100 Open Problems. The imported identifier GREEN-071 is a dataset alias; the underlying statement is identical. X and Y are independent, integer-valued, finitely supported, and the sum is uniform on its positive-probability support, which need not be an interval. Arbitrary real probabilities are allowed. The full statement and comments were read and the page visually checked. The complete pinned imported record has no prior research result. The target's live UnsolvedMath search result agrees; a direct open failed.

After translating the supports and dividing generating polynomials by their leading coefficients, this is the unfair 0–1 polynomial conjecture. Nonnegativity and finiteness are essential. This is not the continuous dependent-uniform/mixability problem, and signed factors are not counterexamples.

Primary literature checked through 2026-10-02:

- Ghidelli (2022) states the exact equivalence and credits the interval and palindromic-product cases; he proves the factor family 1+a x²+x⁵. The long proof is not fully re-audited here.
- Hare (2023 preprint; 2025 publication) treats candidate factor patterns through degree 15, with 975 residual patterns. This is not a complete arbitrary-degree theorem or a complete degree-15 factor classification.
- Dvorsky's September 2026 v2 proves a high-degree trinomial result and states the full family 1+a x²+x^k using a finite companion manuscript. The high-degree theorem and dependency boundary were checked. The companion manuscript was not retrieved and the 53-page analytic proof is not certified by this packet.
- Nguyen Xuan, Pham and Pham Lan (September 2026) address Hare's epsilon-unfair relaxation. Their quotient has coefficient −t_n<0. Thus the paper does not refute or solve the nonnegative conjecture and explicitly still calls it open.
- Zhang's primary MathOverflow answer reports exact Positivstellensatz verification through total degree 66. The live repository README states degree 45. These are distinct reported computational scopes, not computations replayed in this effort, and neither settles arbitrary degree.
