# Exact theorem and scope

The theorem is an equivalence on every locally compact second countable Hausdorff Abelian group G, every arbitrary measurable ambient space with jointly measurable group action, every sigma-finite Q, and every covariant locally finite Radon xi with Q(xi(G)=0)=0. The sufficient hypothesis tests the full ambient Q law under every invariant, xi-measurable, xi-preserving **Markov** transport kernel. The conclusion is full joint Campbell reversal, hence joint mass-stationarity by the credited ambient Mecke/Palm and Palm/mass-stationarity equivalences. Necessity is credited transport invariance.

Constructed kernels depend only on the measure and spatial starting point and preserve every locally finite Radon measure pointwise. Arbitrary ambient information enters only through permitted test functions. Off-period proof gates use a countable separating family on the canonical Radon-measure space, never on Omega. On periods, normalized Haar restrictions may change the ambient state even when xi is fixed. No disintegration over xi or measurable Haar normalization is used.

Formal source target: Last–Thorisson, Annals of Probability 37 (2009), 790–813, Problem 7.3, checked in official electronic reprint arXiv:0906.2062v1, PDF/reprint p.23. Theorem 7.2 is the credited weighted predecessor, not the hypothesis of this note. The formal ambient definitions are in Sections 2–3; mass-stationarity is Definition 6.1 and Theorem 6.3. The precise literal import 30001080 names invariant mass-preserving transport kernels and supplies no Cox/allocation restriction; its all-Markov interpretation is also represented in Thorisson's original OWR contribution, pp.2692/2694.

The note does not claim the smaller-test-class deterministic Cox allocation converse in Last's OWR closing paragraph, p.2673. An implication from invariance under all Markov tests does not establish an implication from invariance under a smaller class of tests. Original TURN_2's unqualified identification of its background-retaining matching theorem with that narrower question is withdrawn from the public claims here. The manuscript contains only the all-Markov theorem. The separately verified B/C/D Cox findings are retained in the corrected PR as scoped ancillary results, without a novelty claim.

Outside scope: non-Abelian groups, non-locally-finite measures, zero measures under the source definition, deterministic-allocation-only tests for general measures, every OWR question as a collective target, arbitrary-X strict Cox converses, and a general sigma-finite strict Cox extension. Root support is a conclusion, not an assumed condition. Atomic, diffuse, mixed, periodic, and zero period-restriction cases are addressed within the proof.

The analytical proof is the manuscript's deduction of full joint Mecke reversal. Classical equivalences and transport invariance are credited. Finite control programs provide examples and boundary checks; they are not a formal proof or an exhaustive quantifier check. The source compiled successfully and all seven pages were visually inspected. AI tools were used extensively; no human peer review or proof-assistant certificate is claimed.
