The standard block grading of the polynomial ring is nonnegative, but the **ambient degree lattice for regularity is Z^r**. Restricting sheaf degrees to N^r would change the question and invalidate the purported unrestricted comparison. In particular, the source's finite-box wording cannot be applied literally to all coherent sheaves under the stated sheaf definition.

The distinction in the frozen proof is correct. Module regularity tests H_B^0 in the union of d+e_j+N^r and H_B^i, for i≥1, in all d−u+N^r with |u|=i−1. ## 2. Independent diagonal verification

On the diagonal D⊂P¹×P¹, the twist O(a,b) restricts to O_{P¹}(a+b). The two required first-cohomology twists have degree a+b−1; all upward translates have greater degree. Since H¹(P¹,O(k)) has dimension max(−k−1,0), the sheaf is regular exactly when a+b≥0. Its minimal lattice degrees are all (t,−t), with no finite containing box. This refutes an unrestricted finite-frontier sheaf formulation, not algorithms returning symbolic descriptions such as this half-space.

For the finitely generated Cox quotient S/(x₀y₁−x₁y₀), its domain property and nonvanishing irrelevant ideal give H_B^0=0. In nonnegative bidegrees, products of degree-a and degree-b binary forms span all degree-(a+b) forms, so the section map is surjective. In any degree d with a negative coordinate one can retain that negative coordinate and increase the other to obtain e≥d with e₁+e₂≥0. Then M_e=0 while H⁰(D,O_D(e₁+e₂))≠0. The H_B^1 obstruction excludes d. Thus its module regularity is exactly N².

This verification does not infer a negative-coordinate module component from the geometry. The ambient polynomial grading makes that component zero; the nonzero sheaf sections instead create H_B^1.

The independent numerical control uses the two-chart Laurent description of P¹ cohomology and explicit surjectivity of monomial restrictions. It verifies both conclusions at 1,681 lattice degrees. These checks support, rather than replace, the preceding proof.

## 3. Audit of the complete finite-cell theorem

### Localization and negative coordinates

A fine-degree component of a localized monomial quotient is either zero or generated by the single surviving Laurent monomial. Outside the inverted variables, a negative coordinate makes that component zero. For each ideal generator, all inequalities against noninverted coordinates decide ideal membership. The negative interval, the finite singleton intervals, and the terminal interval in the proposed cell partition preserve every such decision, including coordinates whose exponent cap is zero.

The localization maps are identities on surviving monomial bases, with the Čech incidence signs. Consequently the *entire differentials*, not merely the term dimensions, are constant on a cell. Computing matrix ranks handles cancellations inside the complex. The frozen code does so over Q or F_p with exact arithmetic.

### Coarsening and support

The block-sum image of a fine cell is a product of integer intervals, including unbounded intervals. Every integer between its endpoints is realized. Graded coarsening is a direct sum over fine degrees and commutes with kernels and cokernels here. Distinct fine degrees cannot cancel each other. Therefore the support rectangle contributes a regularity obstruction precisely when all finite upper endpoints are at least the tested lower corner; the rectangle's lower endpoints impose no further restriction.

This validates the finite clauses. For i≥1 the threshold in coordinate j is U_j+u_j+1. For i=0 it is U_j+1−δ_jk. An absent finite upper endpoint contributes no literal. Empty conjunctions and disjunctions have the correct all-space and empty-space interpretations.

### Minimal points, full region and termination

Every coordinate of a minimal satisfying degree must be a literal threshold: otherwise decreasing it by one changes no comparison. Testing every threshold tuple, and rejecting a point whenever an immediate predecessor still satisfies the formula, therefore finds exactly all actual minimal points. If a coordinate has no threshold, there are no minimal points. That outcome does not mean the region is empty.

Distributing the finite clauses into generalized orthants, with −∞ allowed in coordinates that receive no selected literal, gives the complete region. The containment-pruning direction in the implementation is correct. The unit ideal, zero associated sheaves, and B-torsion examples need this complete representation; minima alone are insufficient.

For a block E_j of size m_j, a finite cell-image upper endpoint lies between −m_j and Σ(c_l−1). The largest possible Čech index is q=∏m_j. Every literal threshold lies in [−m_j, q+Σ(c_l−1)]. Hence the proposed finite box contains every minimal point. It is a bound on minima, **not** a box containing the full regularity region.

Every loop is finite: finite cells, finite Čech terms, exact finite elimination, finite compositions, finite threshold products and finite orthant distribution. Exponential cost affects practicality, not termination. No arbitrary nonmonomial presentation is silently passed through this fine-grading argument.

## 4. Independent computation

The new checker does not reuse the author's ranks, cells, membership function or Koszul oracle in its reference calculations. It computes local cohomology using a different complex: the total complex of the standard affine covers for each projective factor, augmented by M. A term chooses a nonempty subset of variables in each block. Its local-cohomology degree is 1+Σ(|A_j|−1). Differential signs combine within-block incidence with total-complex signs. Iterated affine-cover descent identifies this with the same augmented Čech cohomology. Column-space reduction supplies an independent exact rank implementation.
