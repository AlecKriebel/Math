# Equality of the involutive and Arf invariants on smooth torsion

## Scope

The target concerns the torsion subgroup T of the smooth knot concordance group C. A knot belongs to T when some positive connected-sum multiple bounds a smooth disk in the standard four-ball. Rational sliceness and finite algebraic concordance order are different hypotheses. The identity class is included.

Write F(K) for the class of the pair consisting of CFK^-(K)=CFK_UV(K)/(V) and the induced involution on its U=0 truncation. Equivalence requires grading-preserving F_2[U]-linear maps both ways, each a homotopy equivalence after inverting U and intertwining the involutions up to homotopy after setting U=0. For torsion K, F(K) is either the unknot class O or the figure-eight class E. The invariant A assigns 0 and 1 to those two classes. This is not ordinary, non-involutive local equivalence.

The question is A(K)=Arf(K) for every K in T. The zero class has both values zero. The primary references are Kang–Park, Definitions 2.3 and 2.5, Corollary 2.12, Example 2.14 and Question 1.6, and OWR 4/2023, Question 4. The quasi-alternating torsion case is already in that literature. No novelty claim is made here.

## Outcome and contents

The exact equality is unresolved in this work. Five approaches were completed as partial audits and the proof-search budget is exhausted (5/5); there is no full candidate. Sections 2–3 are in FORMAL_COMPLEX_AUDIT.md and Sections 4–5 in GEOMETRIC_ROUTE_AUDIT.md. The accompanying verify.py checks only the explicitly described algebra.

## 1. Additive-character reduction

Let Q be the subgroup of T generated by concordance classes represented by quasi-alternating torsion knots. Since A and Arf are homomorphisms T→F_2, their difference (equivalently their sum) D=A+Arf is a homomorphism. It vanishes on Q and on 2T. Thus there is a unique induced character

    D_bar: T/(2T+Q) → F_2.

Consequently the requested equality holds if and only if D_bar is zero. If x has odd order m, then A(x)=mA(x)=A(mx)=0, and likewise Arf(x)=0. Thus odd-order torsion cannot be a counterexample, even if such classes should exist. This reasoning does not assert their existence or nonexistence.

There is also an exact kernel formulation. Let e be the figure-eight class, so A(e)=Arf(e)=1. Given x∈T, the element y=x−A(x)e lies in ker A, and Arf(y)=Arf(x)+A(x)=D(x). Therefore

    A=Arf on T  ⇔  Arf vanishes on ker(A|T).

This can focus a search, but it does not establish the vanishing. In particular, the classification of torsion in the target group of F does not classify the kernel of F on actual knots.

An elementary countermodel rules out a purely formal shortcut. Let G=F_2^2, put e=(1,0), and define a(u,v)=u and b(u,v)=u+v. Both characters are nonzero and agree on e, but differ on (0,1). Their values on every even multiple agree because every even multiple is zero. Hence additivity, normalization at e and the existence of a two-element image do not force equality on the source group.
