# Harmonic circle fibrations under an unrestricted metric choice

## Conclusion and exact scope

Let M be any closed connected smooth 7-manifold homeomorphic to S¹ × CP³, and let u be either primitive generator of H¹(M; Z). There is a smooth Riemannian metric g on M such that the harmonic map M → R/Z in class u is a smooth bundle projection. The assertion holds in particular when the smooth total space M is exotic, meaning that it is not diffeomorphic to the standard product.

This answers the following expressly adopted formulation, called E here: for every such M and primitive u, some unrestricted smooth metric has a harmonic representative in class u with no critical points. It is a transfer of classical results, with the necessary metric construction proved below. It is not claimed as a new existence theorem.

The source is F. Thomas Farrell's Day 5 question in the discussion of the Oberwolfach report *Mini-Workshop: Spaces and Moduli Spaces of Riemannian Metrics*, printed p.164. Its final sentence is: “Can this map be a fibration in the exotic cases?” The preceding sentence introduces a Riemannian metric and a harmonic circle map but supplies neither a metric quantifier nor a homotopy class. The preceding discussion concerns exotic smooth total spaces homeomorphic to S¹ × CP³ and also asks curvature questions. Thus E and the primitive-class normalization are explicit editorial choices, not authenticated author intent. [1]

The proof does not answer the assertion for every metric, decide a supplied fixed metric, or impose nonnegative sectional curvature on the total metric or positive sectional curvature on the fibres. No particular metric is supplied in the source. Those variants remain outside this conclusion.

## Definitions

Take S¹ = R/Z, with the flat metric dθ² and ∫S¹ dθ = 1. A map represents u when the integral class of its pullback of dθ is u. Since S¹ is a K(Z,1), this also specifies its homotopy class. The primitive classes in H¹(M; Z) ≅ Z are its two generators.

A smooth fibration means a smooth locally trivial bundle projection. On a closed domain a submersion onto S¹ is such a fibration, by the proper-submersion theorem. Its differential has no zeros. No assertion of isometric monodromy or a global product metric is part of this definition.

For any metric g, the harmonic representative h(g,u) exists and is unique up to a rotation of the target. More explicitly, the Hodge representative α of the image of u in H¹(M; R) has integral periods, and

    h(g,u)(x) = c + ∫ from x₀ to x of α   (mod Z)

is well-defined. Locally its real lifts have differential α and zero Laplacian. Conversely, a harmonic circle map pulls dθ back to a harmonic one-form. Two such maps in class u have the same pullback one-form, by Hodge uniqueness, and differ by a constant circle translation. Equivalently, their difference lifts to a real harmonic function on connected closed M and is constant. In particular, the critical-point question is independent of this translation.

## The topological input applies to every smooth total space

Browder and Levine's Theorem 1.4, in the closed-manifold case, gives a smooth submersion p: M → S¹ in any prescribed nonzero integral class if M is smooth and compact, dim M > 5, π₁(M) ≅ Z, and all its homotopy groups are finitely generated. Their definitions 1.1–1.3 specify both the smooth-submersion meaning and the prescribed cohomology class. The original theorem allows a boundary extension problem; that condition is vacuous here. [2]

All these hypotheses hold for the present M:

1. It is smooth, closed, and 7-dimensional by assumption. It is orientable because it is homeomorphic to the orientable product; fix an orientation.
2. A homeomorphism to S¹ × CP³ gives π₁(M) ≅ Z.
3. For i ≥ 2, πᵢ(M) ≅ πᵢ(CP³). CP³ is a simply connected finite CW complex, so these groups are finitely generated by Serre's finiteness theorem. Serre's Proposition 1 on printed p.491 states the needed implication from finitely generated integral homology in the simply connected case. [3]
4. A primitive class u is nonzero.

Consequently there is a smooth bundle p with p*[dθ] = u. Primitivity says that the induced map p_*: π₁(M) → π₁(S¹) is onto. The fibre is connected: for a bundle with connected total space, monodromy acts transitively on the set of fibre components, and the index of the image of π₁(M) in Z is the number of components. That index is one here. The homotopy exact sequence then shows that the fibre F is simply connected, since π₂(S¹) = 0 and p_*: Z → Z is an isomorphism.

The proof below only uses that F is a closed connected oriented smooth 6-manifold. It does not need to classify F as a smooth CP³, classify smooth structures on M, identify exotic-sphere representatives, or assume that a nonzero sphere summand necessarily produces an exotic total space. If the bundle p is already the smooth CP³-bundle projection used in the source's preceding discussion, the construction applies to that very projection.

For comparison with that discussion, the universal cover of M is homeomorphic to R × CP³, so H₃ of the universal cover is zero. This is the universal-cover hypothesis in Su's reported fibering statement on printed p.157. It is not the statement H₃(M) = 0; indeed H₃(S¹ × CP³; Z) ≅ Z. The direct Browder–Levine application above avoids needing the later classification part of Su's discussion. [1]

## A metric construction for an oriented circle bundle

We prove a more general fact.

**Proposition.** If p: M → S¹ is a smooth bundle with closed connected positive-dimensional fibre and oriented closed total space, then there is a smooth metric g on M for which p*dθ is everywhere nonzero and harmonic. In fact it can be made a unit one-form.

**Proof.** Orient the base by dθ. Orient the vertical tangent bundle ker(dp) so that the base direction followed by an oriented vertical basis gives the orientation of M. Therefore the fibre F is oriented and its monodromy φ: F → F preserves orientation. Write the bundle as the mapping torus

    Tφ = (F × [0,1]) / ((x,1) ∼ (φ(x),0)),

with projection t mod Z. Let m = dim F; here m = 6.

### Step 1  Preserve a fibre volume form by changing the monodromy within its isotopy class

Choose a metric h₀ on F and its positive volume form μ. Orientation preservation gives ∫F φ*μ = ∫F μ. Thus φ*μ − μ = dη for a smooth (m−1)-form η, by top-degree de Rham cohomology. Set μs = (1−s)μ + sφ*μ, a positive volume form for every s ∈ [0,1]. Define Xs uniquely by

    ιXs μs = −η.

Contraction with a volume form is an isomorphism from tangent vectors to (m−1)-covectors. Compactness gives a smooth flow as for 0 ≤ s ≤ 1, with a₀ = id. Cartan's formula yields

    d/ds (as*μs) = as*(dη + d(ιXs μs)) = 0.

Hence a₁*φ*μ = μ. Set ψ = φ ∘ a₁. Then ψ*μ = μ, and ψ is isotopic to φ. This is the standard volume-form argument associated with Moser; the needed special case has just been proved. [4]

Changing to ψ does not change the bundle or its specified projection. To check this explicitly, choose a smooth function χ: [0,1] → [0,1] that is 0 near 0 and 1 near 1. The map

    D: Tψ → Tφ,     [x,t] ↦ [aχ(t)(x),t]

is a diffeomorphism over S¹. At the seam its first expression gives

    [a₁(x),1] = [φ(a₁(x)),0] = [ψ(x),0],

which is its value on the identified point [ψ(x),0]. It is smooth across the seam because the isotopy is constant in t near both ends. The inverse uses aχ(t)⁻¹ and is smooth for the same reason. Thus neither the total smooth structure nor the circle class has been altered.

### Step 2  Join the endpoint metrics while fixing volume pointwise

Set h₁ = ψ*h₀, so both h₀ and h₁ have volume form μ. Define the positive-definite convex interpolation

    kt = (1−χ(t))h₀ + χ(t)h₁,
    rt = vol(kt)/μ,
    ht = rt^(−2/m) kt.

The ratio rt is a smooth positive function on F × [0,1]. Multiplying an m-dimensional metric by λ multiplies its volume form by λ^(m/2), so vol(ht) = μ at every point. Near t=0, ht=h₀; near t=1, ht=h₁=ψ*h₀. There are constant collars at both ends, so these endpoint identities include all required t-jets, rather than merely a C⁰ match.

### Step 3  Descend the metric and verify harmonicity

On F × [0,1] define

    G = dt² + ht,

with no mixed terms. The gluing derivative is (v,b) ↦ (Dψ(v),b). The equality h₁=ψ*h₀, together with the constant collars, proves that G descends to a smooth positive-definite metric on Tψ. This remains true even if ψ is not an isometry of any one fixed metric: the fibre metric is allowed to vary with t.

The form dt descends, has norm one, and is nowhere zero. With the orientation dt ∧ μ, its Hodge star is

    *G dt = μ.

Here μ is pulled back from F on the cylinder. It descends to Tψ because ψ*μ = μ. It is closed: it is independent of t and its fibrewise exterior derivative is zero for dimensional reasons. Therefore d(dt)=0 and d(*G dt)=0, so dt is harmonic. Equivalently, the volume form dt ∧ μ is invariant under ∂t, and divG(∂t)=0.

Finally transfer G along D and the original bundle identification Tφ → M. Their composite J: Tψ → M covers the identity of S¹; put g=(J⁻¹)*G. Then J*(p*dθ)=dt, so p*dθ is g-harmonic and everywhere nonzero. This proves the proposition. ∎

## Apply the construction and identify the harmonic representative

For each M and primitive u, choose the Browder–Levine projection p in that class and apply the proposition. The map p is harmonic because its locally real-valued lifts have harmonic differential p*dθ. By the uniqueness established above, h(g,u) is p followed by a target rotation. It is consequently a smooth fibration with connected fibres.

The same metric also works for −u: compose p with θ ↦ −θ, an isometry of the flat circle, and then optionally rotate. This proves E for every such smooth M, including every exotic total space in the stated family.

## What has and has not been transferred

The abstract mechanism is classical: once there is a nowhere-vanishing closed one-form, Calabi's intrinsic-harmonicity result provides an appropriate metric. Katz explicitly recalls this nonsingular case on printed p.39 of *Harmonic forms and near-minimal singular foliations*. [5, 6] The present proof does not rely on inspecting Calabi's original proof or on extending any singular-form criterion; the necessary bundle case is constructed directly above. Browder–Levine supplies the separate smooth-topological step. [2]

Several shortcuts would be invalid:

- An arbitrary mapping-torus monodromy need not preserve an arbitrarily chosen volume form. The Moser isotopy and the bundle isomorphism D supply precisely the missing adjustment.
- Equal endpoint total volumes do not make every interpolation harmonic. The construction fixes the entire fibre volume form pointwise for all t.
- Endpoint metric equality alone would not ensure a smooth seam. The constant collars match all transverse derivatives.
- A fibrewise family of metrics does not by itself specify the total metric. The orthogonal splitting and dt² term above do specify one.
- A harmonic representative in a nonzero class need not follow merely from choosing a harmonic scalar function; nonconstant globally real-valued harmonic functions cannot exist on a closed connected M. The circle-valued integral-period construction is essential.
- The exoticity of a connected sum is a claim about the total smooth manifold, not automatically a consequence of choosing a nonstandard homotopy sphere.

For a general cylinder metric dt²+ht, the local coordinate expression is

    Δt = ∂t log(vol(ht)) = (1/2) tr(ht⁻¹ ∂t ht),

up to the overall sign convention for the Laplacian. The fixed-volume construction makes this zero. Merely interpolating h₀ and φ*h₀ generally does not. For instance ht=e^(2a(t))h₀ gives Δt=m a′(t). This example only shows that such a prescribed projection need not be harmonic for an arbitrary metric; it is not a counterexample to the stronger assertion that the harmonic representative itself is always nonsingular.

The conformal normalization and changing monodromy trivialization impose no sectional-curvature bounds. They do not establish a nonnegative-sectionally-curved total metric or a positive-sectionally-curved fibre-metric family. A separately assumed Ricci or sectional curvature condition would require a separate argument. No classification of the possible critical sets for arbitrary metrics is asserted here.

## Verification and dependency boundaries

The only substantial imported existence theorem for M is Browder–Levine's smooth fibering theorem. Its stated hypotheses and application have been checked; its original surgery proof is not independently reproved here. Serre's finite-generation theorem and standard de Rham, Hodge, and smooth-bundle facts are also used. The metric construction, its seam compatibility, the fibre-preserving change of monodromy, and the harmonicity calculation are given explicitly.

The accompanying check_metric_identities.py performs exact rational matrix checks of the pointwise determinant and logarithmic-volume identities in fibre dimension six, plus the endpoint pullback determinant identity for volume-preserving changes of frame. These checks are reproducible algebraic sanity checks. They do not verify Browder–Levine, construct an exotic manifold numerically, prove an infinite-dimensional existence theorem, or substitute for the proof.

**Qualified mathematical disposition:** E is affirmative by credited prior results and the explicit construction. The original source's intended metric quantifier is not established. Arbitrary-metric, prescribed-metric, and curvature-constrained variants are not resolved by this report. There is no novelty claim and no claim that all plausible readings of the source have been solved.
