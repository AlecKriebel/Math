# Primary source and classical-input review

The official OWR14/2016 PDF was inspected at printed p694. Problem3 is the complex-plane analogue of the preceding real Wiseman–Wilson theorem; the definition immediately above requires unique determination. It is distinct from the neighboring counting and higher-degree questions. Source: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46618

The full published Czapliński et al. article was checked at Theorem1.4/Remark1.5 and Problem4.4. Singular and reducible conics count; the five-point uniqueness condition is explicit. Its finite-field example is not a complex example. Source: https://numdam.org/item/10.4171/RSMUP/136-13.pdf

The full Boys–Valculescu–de Zeeuw preprint was inspected at its real-plane hypotheses and section6. It already uses conic/group-law relations for real constructions. Those methods receive credit, while its real theorem is not imported as a complex theorem. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03588

Milne's second-edition text was checked at Proposition I.4.10 and Remark I.4.11, the flex discussion, and ChapterIII Theorem3.10 with its preceding analytic parametrization. These support the Picard interpretation, line-section class at a flex and complex torus uniformization. The reviewed proof uses the necessary intersection-divisor sum, including multiplicities, rather than assuming a converse interpolation statement. Source: https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/EC2.pdf
