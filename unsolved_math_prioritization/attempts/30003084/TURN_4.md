# Author turn 4: every irreducible cubic supports ordinary conics

**Partial theorem, pending independent review.** Let P be a finite set of distinct points on an irreducible complex projective plane cubic. If P is not contained in a conic, then P has an ordinary conic, allowing reducible conics and requiring unique determination by its five points. This is a subclass of the original question, not its full resolution or a claim of novelty.

The proof below rules out the entire smooth-cubic route, not only finite subgroups or torsion sets. It also treats singular irreducible cubics. The elliptic group/divisor inputs are classical; Boys–Valculescu–de Zeeuw, Section 6, already uses the conic/group-law relation in the real setting. Here the complex argument is supplied explicitly rather than importing their real ordinary-conic theorem.

## 1. Five points and the sixth intersection

Any five distinct points with no four collinear impose five independent conditions on quadrics. For each selected point p, partition the other four into two pairs whose joining lines avoid p. This is possible because the groups defined by lines through p have size at most two. The product of those two lines vanishes at the other four points but not at p. These five separating quadrics prove independence and hence uniqueness of the conic through the five points.

An irreducible cubic has no four distinct collinear points, by Bezout. A conic and the cubic share no component, so their intersection divisor has degree six. Thus a conic through five distinct cubic points either meets a sixth distinct point or has extra intersection multiplicity at one of the five. In the second case it is automatically ordinary for every subset of the cubic containing those five points. A reducible conic causes no exception to this argument.

For a smooth cubic E choose a flex O as group identity. A line section is linearly equivalent to 3O and a conic section to 6O. The classical identification E with Pic^0(E) therefore says that the sixth intersection, counted with multiplicity, is

r = -(p_1+p_2+p_3+p_4+p_5).

For these standard inputs see Milne, *Elliptic Curves*, second edition, Proposition I.4.10 and Remark I.4.11; existence of flexes and their relation to plane embeddings is discussed in I.5. No converse interpolation assertion or continuity at multiple intersections is needed here.

Assume there is no ordinary conic. For every four-element subset B of P, the map

T_B(x) = -sum(B)-x

is a fixed-point-free involution of P minus B. Indeed, the unique conic through B together with x has exactly one residual intersection; absence of an ordinary conic forces this residual point to be a distinct member of P outside B and x. In particular, |P| is even. We use a stronger consequence next.

## 2. A reflection lemma for abelian groups

Let A be a finite subset of an abelian group H, with |A| at least seven. Suppose T_B(x)=-sum(B)-x permutes A minus B for every four-element B. Then every multiplicative character chi:H to C* takes at most three distinct values on A. Fixed-point-freeness is not needed for this lemma.

Write z_a=chi(a), F=sum_{a in A} z_a and G=sum_{a in A} z_a^(-1). Since T_B permutes the complement,

(product_{b in B} z_b) (F-sum_{b in B} z_b)
  = G-sum_{b in B} z_b^(-1).                         (1)

Fix three distinct elements C of A and put Q=product_C z_c and S=sum_C z_c. Apply (1) to C together with d, then to C together with e, where d,e are outside C. If z_d differs from z_e, subtracting and dividing gives

F-S = z_d+z_e+1/(Q z_d z_e).                         (2)

If four elements d,e,f,g outside C have pairwise distinct character values, comparison of (2) for (d,e) and (d,f) gives Q z_d z_e z_f=1. Comparison with (d,g) similarly gives Q z_d z_e z_g=1. This contradicts z_f differing from z_g. Consequently no complement of a three-element C has four distinct character values.

If chi(A) had at least four values, protect one representative of each of four values and choose C among three other elements, possible since |A|>=7. Those four values survive in A minus C, a contradiction. This proves the lemma. The argument permits repeated character values and arbitrary torsion in H.

## 3. Application to any smooth complex cubic

The subgroup H generated by P is finitely generated. Classical complex uniformization E(C) is isomorphic to C/Lambda, with Lambda of rank two. Its finite torsion subgroups therefore have at most two cyclic factors: if their exponent is m, they lie in E[m], isomorphic to (Z/m)^2. Thus

H is isomorphic to Z^r direct-sum Z/m direct-sum Z/n

with trivial factors allowed. Uniformization is the classical input in Milne, Chapter III, especially Theorem 3.10 and the preceding analytic parametrization.

There are two characters whose combined map H to (C*)^2 is injective. In the displayed decomposition take distinct ordinary positive primes l_1,...,l_r and define

chi_1(k_1,...,k_r,a,b) = (product_j l_j^(k_j)) exp(2 pi i a/m),
chi_2(k_1,...,k_r,a,b) = exp(2 pi i b/n).

If both values equal one, absolute values and unique prime factorization first force all k_j=0, then the two roots of unity force a=b=0. These characters are abstract group characters; continuity in the elliptic topology is neither asserted nor required.

If |P|>=7 and no ordinary conic exists, the reflection lemma bounds each character image by three, so injectivity gives |P|<=9. TURN_2 already proves the required ordinary-conic theorem for every complex projective set of at most nine points not on a conic. This is a contradiction. The cases |P|<=6 are covered by the same earlier theorem. This establishes the smooth-cubic result for arbitrary finite P, not just subgroups, cosets, or real points.

## 4. Singular irreducible cubics

If the singular point lies in P, choose it and any four other points of P. Such a choice is possible because a set not on a conic has at least six points. The unique conic through the five points has intersection multiplicity at least two at the singular point, and at least one at each of the four smooth points. All six intersection multiplicities are thereby used, so there is no other point of P on the conic. It is ordinary.

Suppose the singular point is absent. The following explicit parametrizations give the needed group-type residual law without assuming a smooth group structure at the singular point.

For the nodal normal form xy z+x^3+y^3=0, all smooth points are

[u^2:u:-(u^3+1)], u in C*.

Pulling a homogeneous quadric back gives a polynomial of degree at most six whose constant and leading coefficients are equal, both being the coefficient of z^2. The conic through five distinct smooth points cannot pass through the node: that would give total intersection multiplicity at least seven. Hence both coefficients are nonzero, the polynomial has six nonzero finite roots counted with multiplicity, and their product is one. The residual parameter is therefore the inverse of the product of the five parameters. The reflection lemma applies multiplicatively, with the identity character of C*. For |P|>=7 it would give |P|<=3, a contradiction; smaller cases were already settled.

For the cuspidal normal form y^2 z=x^3, all smooth points are

[v:1:v^3], v in C.

The pulled-back quadrics span the monomials 1,v,v^2,v^3,v^4,v^6. Again the conic through five distinct smooth points cannot pass through the cusp. Its pullback has degree six, with vanishing degree-five coefficient, so its six roots sum to zero. The reflection lemma applies in the additive subgroup generated by the parameters. This finitely generated subgroup of (C,+) is free abelian and has an injective character into the positive real numbers, constructed with independent primes as above. Once again |P|>=7 is impossible under the no-ordinary-conic assumption.

For completeness, these normal forms cover the singular irreducible plane cubics over C. Put the singular point at [0:0:1], so its equation is z q_2(x,y)+q_3(x,y). If the quadratic tangent cone has two distinct factors, take q_2=xy and absorb the mixed cubic terms into z; irreducibility forces the two remaining pure-cubic coefficients nonzero, and scaling gives xy z+x^3+y^3. If it is a square, take q_2=x^2. Absorb terms divisible by x^2 into z; the remaining equation is x^2 z+cxy^2+dy^3, with d nonzero by irreducibility. A shear of y removes the xy^2 term, newly created x^2 terms are absorbed into z, and scaling/permutation gives the stated cusp. A vanishing quadratic cone would make the cubic a product of lines over C. Thus no singular irreducible case is omitted.

## 5. Scope and checks

`verify_cubic_controls.py` checks the reflection-character identities symbolically, the nodal and cuspidal pullback constraints, exact finite abelian-group controls, and interpolation on fixed rational singular cubics. The universal argument is the proof above, not a finite census.

The theorem closes the irreducible-cubic counterexample route. Reducible cubics and general higher-degree point configurations remain outside it. The general complex Wiseman–Wilson question is still unresolved after four substantive author turns; one turn remains. No priority or novelty is asserted for the elementary and classical consequences proved here.
