# Acceptance and exact mathematical scope

12 October 2026. Three complete mathematical packets passed independent AI-assisted mathematical review within the scopes below. The zero/common/ternary packet is accepted only in its corrected version. The hybrid/prefix acceptance includes the final two-step section. A later accepted theorem excludes the full ternary family; the earlier finite-budget arguments and their historical scopes are preserved separately. This is not external human peer review, journal acceptance, proof-assistant certification or a novelty determination.

## Accepted analytic and finite-prefix mathematics

- For unrestricted independent original-factor signs, the exact Jensen zero inequality, its finite-radius counting consequence, and the limsup constant \(1/(2\log(1/\rho))\) for fixed \(\rho\) and fixed coefficient bound \(M\). The reviews check local uniform convergence, formal/analytic agreement, nonvanishing tails, multiplicity including coincident zeros, lifting under \(q^m\), boundary radii, finite positive cutoffs and the order of limits.
- For ternary digit blocks, the exact contribution \((\log\varphi-nt)_+\) of each noncyclotomic block of exponent \(n\), including all labelled multiplicities. The resulting logarithmic upper bound permits sparse infinitely many defects.
- For unrestricted prefixes, the exact one-step criterion \(|A_n|\le d(n)+(p(n)\bmod2)\), its binomial labelled extension count, and the algebraic proof that attempted degree 7 is the earliest possible dead end.
- The exact sign-constrained linear system from a fixed degree-\(M\) prefix through every \(N\le2M+1\), the exact finite-horizon identical-column grouping and its binomial multiplicities, and the necessary weighted real relaxation. The real relaxation is not sufficient for the integer sign problem.
- The exact two-step test for \(n\ge2\), requiring at most four target/sign pairs. Label compression is only valid for the stated two-step horizon.
- Finite branching gives an infinite solution if and only if every degree has at least one feasible prefix. Neither finite tests nor the one-step criterion establish that premise.

## Later accepted full-ternary theorem

No assignment with every original factor ternary-digit multiplicative makes all total coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). All linear and quadratic digit signs are freely selectable, including infinitely many defects. This is a theorem for the entire restricted ternary family, not the unrestricted independent-factor-coefficient model.

The proof retains a hypothetical global coefficient bound exactly one, enumerates every legal deferred quadratic/linear count choice, and rejects only exact coefficient failures or exact necessary Jensen failures. The rational radii and all strict algebraic comparisons are fully specified. Unknown defects can be omitted safely from the nonnegative zero mass, so every hypothetical solution would induce a surviving state at every level. The complete combined degree-59 frontier is empty, giving a contradiction.

Degree 59 is the first empty frontier of this coefficient-plus-global-Jensen search. It is not claimed to be a sharp coefficient-only obstruction or a universal bound on the first bad coefficient of arbitrary finite ternary prefixes. The result does not establish that every ternary product has unbounded coefficients or rule out every larger finite coefficient bound.

## Explicit exclusions

No accepted result resolves the unrestricted problem, supplies a general finite bound on the number of ternary defects under arbitrary coefficient bounds, excludes two or more arbitrary binary-hybrid exceptions, or excludes exception indices 1 and 2. The full ternary family is excluded only for the target global coefficient bound one, by the later separate theorem. No unrestricted search record, novelty, priority, generic finite-active impossibility, or infinite bounded-coefficient construction is claimed. The binary and ternary digit families are not nested. The logarithmic defect upper bound and the three lower cutoffs are compatible.
