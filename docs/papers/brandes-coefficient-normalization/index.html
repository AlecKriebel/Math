Homogeneous forms · Brandes's Oberwolfach Problem 11

A coefficient normalization for positive definite forms

Every positive definite form admits a basis in which the diagonal coefficients are one and every mixed coefficient lies strictly between zero and one.

The result

A basis that bounds every coefficient.

Let p be a real homogeneous polynomial of positive even degree d, positive away from zero. Its symmetric multilinear polarization A satisfies A(x, …, x) = p(x).

There is a basis z1, …, zm such that

p(zi) = 1,
0 < A(zj1, …, zjd) < 1

for every mixed tuple. Pure tuples have value one. This gives the absolute-value bound asked in Problem 11, printed page 3182, of the 2019 Oberwolfach report.

Read the statement precisely

Symmetric ordered coefficients

The entries are those of A. An ordinary monomial coefficient includes a multinomial multiplicity, which must be divided out.

An invertible real change

The basis may be nearly dependent, but its determinant is nonzero. The problem places no bound on its condition number.

Catalogue reference: OWR-17293-016. The publisher report is the authenticated source.

The proof in three steps

01

Minimize on a sphere

A sphere-minimizing direction has a strictly positive transverse quadratic slice of the polarization.

02

Cluster a basis nearby

Take the minimizing vector and small independent transverse perturbations. They form a basis for every nonzero perturbation size.

03

Compare a polynomial gap

For each mixed tuple, the desired gap has a strictly positive quadratic term. Finitely many tuples share one sufficiently small perturbation.

Check the result

A short proof, with exact checks.

The universal statement follows from the analytic proof. A separate derivation uses positive definite bilinear slices, Cauchy–Schwarz and a finite maximum argument.

The Python companion checks 12 rational examples, covering 120 coefficient classes, with 110 independent polarization cross-checks. It also checks the quadratic identity in degrees 2–24 and rejects two deliberately invalid certificates.

python3 verification/verify.py

Python 3.9+; no extra dependencies. These finite checks are not a formal proof of the universal theorem.

Context & attribution

An answer to the stated question.

Brandes's 2015 paper discusses pseudo-diagonal forms and proves the quadratic case. The 2019 problem asks about general positive definite forms after a change of variables.

The literature audit found no earlier full resolution. It cannot rule out unpublished or differently formulated antecedents.

Generative AI supplied the initial candidate and assisted the independent internal checks, research and writing.
