An explicit odd-order p-group with as many automorphisms as elements

An affirmative answer

There is a nontrivial finite group of odd prime-power order whose full automorphism group has the same order.

Theorem. The explicit construction in the paper gives a finite group \(G\) with

\[|G|=|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|=1009^{52359}.\]

This answers D. MacHale’s existence question in Kourovka Notebook 16.63.

The group is specified by 31 coordinates modulo \(1009^{1689}\), an explicit integer Lie bracket, and the finite Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff multiplication rule. Its nilpotency class is at most 846, below the prime 1009. No minimality claim is made for its prime or order.

Why the count includes every automorphism

The construction starts with a 31-dimensional Lie algebra based on an Omirov–Ruan template. A choice of lattice forces every automorphism of the finite Lie ring to preserve a rigid flag after reduction modulo 1009. Integral logarithms then turn the full automorphism condition into an exact linear equation.

The resulting integer matrix has 14,415 rows, 961 columns, rank 931, and nullity 30. Its nonzero Smith invariants have the following valuations at 1009:

Exact certificate for the derivation matrix
Valuation01234
Multiplicity8755758625

The valuation sum is 1689. At depth \(i\geq7\), the full Lie-ring automorphism count is \(1009^{30i+1689}\). Choosing \(i=1689\) makes this equal to the number of elements, \(1009^{31i}\). The Lazard correspondence preserves the full automorphism group because the class is strictly less than the prime.

Reproduce the checks

Download and extract the source package, then run this command from its folder:

python3 scripts/resume.py

This needs Python 3 and a C++17 compiler supporting 128-bit integers. The verifier reconstructs the brackets and matrix from formulas, replays the exact certificate, checks small-group controls, and confirms that a corrupted certificate is rejected. For full Python regeneration with NumPy and SymPy installed, use python3 scripts/resume.py --full.

Separate AI audit agents independently reviewed the finite-field automorphisms, the congruence and logarithm arguments, and the computation. The package contains their reports and fresh reproduction records. The mathematical argument also uses proofs that are outside the scope of the executable checks.

The remaining developments include the general finite-quotient lifting argument, the complete exponential/logarithm bijection, acceptance of the actual Smith certificate, and the BCH group construction with its full group/Lie automorphism correspondence.
