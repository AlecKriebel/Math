Equality in the Fradelizi–Paouris–Schütt second-moment inequality

Alec Kriebel · ORCID · Version 1.0.0 · 23 September 2026

The equality case for arbitrary convex bodies is exactly the one conjectured: a simplex with every vertex on the sphere.

The complete equality class

Let K be a compact convex body with nonempty interior in ℝn, with n ≥ 1 and r > 0. Suppose every extreme point has Euclidean norm at least r. Write gK for its volume centroid and C2(K) for the average squared Euclidean norm of a point chosen uniformly from K.

C2(K) ≥ [r² + (n + 1) ‖gK‖²] / (n + 2)

Equality holds if and only if K is a nondegenerate simplex whose vertices all have norm r.

The inequality and the polytope equality case are due to Fradelizi, Paouris, and Schütt. This note proves the conjecture following their Theorem 1.1 for general convex bodies, also recorded in the 2009 Oberwolfach report, p. 2908 and catalogued as OWR-4136-011.

Why equality forces one simplex

  1. Partition the body. Add a dense finite or countable collection of extreme points one at a time. Triangulate each visible facet of the previous hull, then cone its simplices to the new point. The pieces have disjoint interiors, use only extreme-point vertices, and cover the body up to a set of volume zero.
  2. Keep the deficit exactly. It splits into two nonnegative quantities: the excess of the vertices' squared norms over r², and the variance of the simplices' centroids.
  3. Use disjoint interiors. Equality makes every simplex centroid equal to gK. A simplex contains its centroid in its interior, so two distinct pieces cannot share that centroid. Only one simplex remains.

Regularity is unnecessary. Even the nonregular triangle with vertices (1, 0), (0, 1), (−1, 0) attains the bound: its centroid is (0, ⅓) and its second moment is ⅓. For the weaker bound C2(K) ≥ r²/(n + 2), equality additionally requires centroid zero.

A short proof, with checkable support

The universal result follows from the written proof. The accompanying script independently integrates simplex polynomials and polygon boundary moments, then checks the finite deficit and shell identities using exact rational arithmetic.

python3 verification/verify.py

Extract the proof package and run this from its project folder. Python 3.9+ is sufficient; no packages or network are needed. 298 checks across 18 cases pass, including dimensions 1–6 and an auxiliary coplanar-facet test. These finite checks do not prove the infinite geometric argument.
