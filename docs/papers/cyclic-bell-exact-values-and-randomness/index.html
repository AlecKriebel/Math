Abstract

Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acín, and Augusiak introduced two cyclic Bell families for efficient certification of maximal global randomness. For the first reduced operator they proved the general bound \(d\sqrt2\), supplied a strategy of value \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))\), and conjectured that the latter value is exact. We prove that formula in every dimension and in the commuting-operator model. Every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family has all equality roots with equal multiplicity on Alice’s support. Yet conditional permutations of those roots remain invisible to the first-harmonic score. For every \(d\ge4\), a final-two swap maximizes both augmented families while producing a nonuniform designated joint-output distribution. Thus the intended scalar-value implication of the first-family maximal-violation conjecture fails in these dimensions. This does not challenge certification conditioned on the complete canonical behavior, identify the worst-case guessing probability, or show that the canonical strategy lacks maximal randomness.

Main results

Exact value The finite-dimensional, approximate, and commuting-operator values coincide in every dimension.
Equality structure Every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family has equal supported root multiplicities, while admissible root permutations can change higher Fourier data.
Randomness scope Both augmented families have exact maximum-value behaviors with nonuniform target outputs for every \(d\ge4\).

Sharp operator theorem. For arbitrary unitaries \(A_0,A_1,B_0,\ldots,B_{d-1}\) satisfying cross-party commutation,

\[ \mathcal I_d\le 2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right)\mathbf 1. \]

After imposing the order-\(d\) measurement relations and using the finite attaining strategy,

\[ \beta_q(\mathcal I_d)=\beta_{qa}(\mathcal I_d)=\beta_{qc}(\mathcal I_d) =2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right). \]

The corresponding first augmented family has value \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))+1\). The second reduced and augmented families have values \(d\) and \(d+1\), respectively, with the source-v3 sum-of-squares identity, whose prefactor is \(1/(2d)\), also admitting the commuting-operator reading.

Support-level rigidity. Every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family has all \(d\) scalar equality roots with equal multiplicity on the support of Alice’s reduced state. Hence \(d\) divides that supported dimension. This necessary condition is not a complete maximizing-face classification and is not asserted for \(qa\), \(qc\), approximate maxima, or the second family.

Exact nonuniform maximizers. For every \(d\ge4\), the displayed final-two phase swap maximizes either augmented family, retains uniform local marginals, and with trivial Eve satisfies

\[ G(AB\mid1,d,E)\ge \frac1{d^2}+\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)} >\frac1{d^2}. \]

At \(d=4\), an exact certificate over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})\) gives target probabilities \(1/32\) and \(3/32\), hence \(G=3/32>1/16\) and displayed min-entropy \(5-\log_2 3=3.415037\ldots<4\) bits.

Exact scope of the randomness conclusion

The maximum scalar Bell value alone does not identify the canonical behavior or certify maximal global randomness across the entire maximizing face.

A full-behavior SDP can return \(G=1/d^2\) for the fixed canonical distribution while another distribution attains the same scalar Bell maximum and has \(G>1/d^2\). The root-swapped behavior differs in higher Fourier correlators and therefore does not satisfy the canonical full-behavior constraints.

The construction does not dispute a calculation that fixes the complete canonical behavior, does not show that the canonical strategy lacks maximal randomness, and does not determine the globally worst guessing probability. Passing from a fixed-behavior conclusion to a value-only conclusion would require uniqueness, suitable self-testing, or additional observed correlators.

In particular, the maximum does not determine a unique behavior even modulo local output relabelings: a relabeling can only permute a target table and cannot turn the canonical-order uniform table into the final-two-swap nonuniform table.

Proof architecture

  1. A sharp roots-of-unity extremum bounds the scalar function \(\sum_y|1+\omega^yz|\) and identifies its equality phases.
  2. A kernel-safe polar positive-factor identity and continuous functional calculus lift that scalar inequality to arbitrary commuting Alice–Bob unitaries.
  3. The canonical finite strategy supplies the matching lower bound and proves equality of \(q\), \(qa\), and \(qc\).
  4. For every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family, state-support cancellation and a reflection-product rank bound force every equality root to have the same supported multiplicity.
  5. A weighted-cycle theorem gives exact product conditions under which permutations of the equality phases remain order-\(d\) projective maximizers.
  6. Fourier analysis of the cycle weights shows that a final-two swap preserves all Bell-visible correlators but makes the designated joint table nonuniform.
  7. An exact discrete Fourier calculation transports the same Bob observables into the second family and annihilates every factor of the source-v3 sum-of-squares certificate, whose prefactor is \(1/(2d)\).

A standard one-input locality theorem supplies the universal setting baseline. A self-contained binary benchmark recovers the prior-art value \(3\sqrt3\) and two private bits, while a private-MUB lemma gives a sufficient operator criterion for higher-dimensional designs. More specialized observations about the standard two-input qudit strategy and a separately bounded third-setting repair are confined to an appendix and do not imply a general \(2\times2\) or \(2\times3\) impossibility theorem.

Current priority statement

The family definitions, canonical strategies, first-family \(d\sqrt2\) bound and matching lower value, second-family sum-of-squares identity, and binary \(d=2\) benchmark are prior work. The candidate contributions here are the all-dimensional sharp first-family upper bound, its commuting-operator strengthening, the phase-permutation mechanism, the explicit nonuniform maximizers, and their second-family extension. The restored support-rigidity and private-MUB statements are retained for completeness and are not promoted as lead novelty claims.

The accompanying audit compares these claims against current primary sources. The manuscript uses qualified language—“to our knowledge”—and makes no claim of absolute or definitive priority. Concurrent, unindexed, or unpublished work may exist.
