Abstract

Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acín, and Augusiak introduced two cyclic Bell families designed for efficient certification of maximal global randomness and conjectured the exact value of the reduced operator underlying the first family. We prove the conjectured value in every dimension and strengthen the upper bound to the commuting-operator model. A polar-decomposition argument identifies an equality-phase permutation freedom invisible to the first-harmonic Bell score. For every \(d\ge4\), a final-two phase swap gives admissible rank-one projective measurements that maximize both augmented cyclic families while producing a nonuniform designated joint-output distribution. Thus the maximum scalar Bell value alone does not certify \(2\log_2 d\) bits across the whole maximizing face. This does not challenge certification conditioned on the complete canonical behavior or show that the canonical strategy lacks maximal randomness.

Main results

Exact value The finite-dimensional, approximate, and commuting-operator values coincide in every dimension.
Equality structure Admissible phase permutations preserve the Bell-visible first harmonics while higher Fourier data can change.
Randomness scope Both augmented families have exact maximum-value behaviors with nonuniform target outputs for every \(d\ge4\).

Sharp operator theorem. For arbitrary unitaries \(A_0,A_1,B_0,\ldots,B_{d-1}\) satisfying cross-party commutation,

\[ \mathcal I_d\le 2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right)\mathbf 1. \]

After imposing the order-\(d\) measurement relations and using the finite attaining strategy,

\[ \beta_q(\mathcal I_d)=\beta_{qa}(\mathcal I_d)=\beta_{qc}(\mathcal I_d) =2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right). \]

The corresponding first augmented family has value \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))+1\). The second reduced and augmented families have values \(d\) and \(d+1\), respectively, with the source sum-of-squares identity also admitting the commuting-operator reading.

Exact nonuniform maximizers. For every \(d\ge4\), the displayed final-two phase swap maximizes either augmented family, retains uniform local marginals, and with trivial Eve satisfies

\[ G(AB\mid1,d,E)\ge \frac1{d^2}+\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)} >\frac1{d^2}. \]

At \(d=4\), an exact certificate over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})\) gives target probabilities \(1/32\) and \(3/32\), hence \(G=3/32>1/16\).

Exact scope of the randomness conclusion

The maximum scalar Bell value alone does not identify the canonical behavior or certify maximal global randomness across the entire maximizing face.

A full-behavior SDP can return \(G=1/d^2\) for the fixed canonical distribution while another distribution attains the same scalar Bell maximum and has \(G>1/d^2\). The root-swapped behavior differs in higher Fourier correlators and therefore does not satisfy the canonical full-behavior constraints.

The construction does not dispute a calculation that fixes the complete canonical behavior, does not show that the canonical strategy lacks maximal randomness, and does not determine the globally worst guessing probability. Passing from a fixed-behavior conclusion to a value-only conclusion would require uniqueness, suitable self-testing, or additional observed correlators.

Proof architecture

  1. A sharp roots-of-unity extremum bounds the scalar function \(\sum_y|1+\omega^yz|\) and identifies its equality phases.
  2. A kernel-safe polar positive-factor identity and continuous functional calculus lift that scalar inequality to arbitrary commuting Alice–Bob unitaries.
  3. The canonical finite strategy supplies the matching lower bound and proves equality of \(q\), \(qa\), and \(qc\).
  4. A weighted-cycle theorem gives exact product conditions under which permutations of the equality phases remain order-\(d\) projective maximizers.
  5. Fourier analysis of the cycle weights shows that a final-two swap preserves all Bell-visible correlators but makes the designated joint table nonuniform.
  6. An exact discrete Fourier calculation transports the same Bob observables into the second family and annihilates every factor of its global sum-of-squares certificate.

A standard one-input locality theorem supplies the universal setting baseline. More specialized observations about the standard two-input qudit strategy and a separately bounded third-setting repair are confined to an appendix and do not imply a general \(2\times2\) or \(2\times3\) impossibility theorem.

Current priority statement

The family definitions, canonical strategies, matching lower bounds, second-family sum-of-squares identity, and binary \(d=2\) setting calibration are prior work. The candidate contributions here are the all-dimensional first-family upper bound, its commuting-operator strengthening, the equality-phase permutation mechanism, the explicit nonuniform maximizers, and their second-family extension.

The accompanying audit compares these claims against current primary sources. The manuscript uses qualified language—“to our knowledge”—and makes no claim of absolute or definitive priority. Concurrent, unindexed, or unpublished work may exist.
