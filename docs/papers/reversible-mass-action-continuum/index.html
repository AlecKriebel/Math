Abstract

We give an exact reversible mass-action system on three species with one linkage class, ten complexes, ten reversible reaction pairs, and positive integer rates. Its stoichiometric subspace is all of ℝ3, and its positive orthant contains a compact algebraic ellipse of equilibria. Nevertheless, the three coordinate polynomials of the mass-action vector field have greatest common divisor one. The equilibrium continuum is therefore a genuine height-two steady-state component, not a common-factor hypersurface.

The exact theorem

There is a reversible mass-action system on \(X,Y,Z\), with one linkage class and positive integer rate constants, whose stoichiometric subspace is ℝ3 and whose steady-state set contains the positive conic

\[z-x-y+1=0,\qquad 7x^2-2xy-16x+7y^2-16y+16=0.\]

The coordinate vector-field polynomials satisfy \(\gcd(F_1,F_2,F_3)=1\) over ℚ, and hence over ℝ and ℂ. The full steady ideal is radical: over the algebraic closure its variety is this conic, disjoint from fifteen reduced isolated points.

An exact positive parametrization is

\[ x(t)=\frac{t^2+3}{2(t^2-t+1)},\quad y(t)=\frac{3t^2+1}{2(t^2-t+1)},\quad z(t)=\frac{t^2+t+1}{t^2-t+1}. \]

On \(t\in(-1,1)\), these points are positive and pairwise distinct. Because the stoichiometric class is the whole positive orthant, they all lie in one compatibility class.

Two independent exact verifiers

The archive supplies a one-command replay:

./reproduce.sh
  • Original verifier. Reconstructs the mass-action field, graph, stoichiometry, ideal membership, parametrization, gcd, Jacobian rank, and full radical decomposition.
  • Clean-room verifier. Starts from the raw complexes, directed reactions, rates, \(L\), \(Q\), and parametrization. It independently derives the field and ideal quotients, then rediscovers the residual component by saturation and ideal-intersection elimination.
  • Proof audit. Separately checks the absolute-prime and scalar-extension arguments, three-species minimality, the one-linkage rank-two obstruction, and the four-complex impossibility proof.

No floating-point computation is used as evidence for the theorem.
