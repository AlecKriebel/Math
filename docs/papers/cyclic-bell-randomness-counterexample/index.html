Abstract

Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acín, and Augusiak conjectured that maximal violation of their first augmented cyclic Bell family certifies \(2\log_2d\) bits of global randomness. This note disproves that Bell-value implication by giving exact maximizing strategies with nonuniform designated joint outputs for every \(d\ge4\). Eve can be trivial and both local marginals remain uniform. The separate numerical certificate for their fixed canonical full behavior is not challenged.

1. Result

For the designated settings \(x=1,y=d\), the explicit family satisfies

\[ G(AB\mid1,d,E) \ge \frac1{d^2} +\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)} >\frac1{d^2}. \]

Theorem. For every \(d\ge4\), admissible order-\(d\) observables on \(\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d\) and the state \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\), with one-dimensional Eve, attain the global operator maximum while violating maximal joint uniformity. Both one-party marginals are exactly \(1/d\).

Equivalently, if \(G_{\mathrm{val}}(d)\) is the supremum of Eve’s guessing probability over all strategies attaining this Bell value, then the displayed strict lower bound applies to \(G_{\mathrm{val}}(d)\). This refutes Bell-value-based maximal randomness more directly than an Eve-flag construction would: the observed output table itself is nonuniform.

Precisely what is refuted

Conjecture 2 prints the maximum as \(2[d\sin(\pi/(2d))]^{-1}+1\). The factor \(d\) is internally inconsistent with the displayed operator, Eq. (17), and the paper’s stated \(d=3\) value \(5\); the evident intended maximum is \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))+1\). The result refutes the implication

\[ \langle\overline{\mathcal I}_d\rangle=2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right)+1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G(AB\mid1,d,E)=\frac1{d^2}. \]

The source’s abstract, introduction, and uniqueness-based symmetry argument all support this Bell-value reading. If Conjecture 2 is instead read only as a claim about the canonical full behavior, that narrower claim is not refuted.

The apparent \(d=4\) conflict

Appendix B.1 fixes the canonical full probability behavior before computing its guessing bound. The cyclic and root-swapped strategies have exactly the same first-harmonic correlators seen by the Bell functional, but different higher Fourier data. At \(d=4\), the canonical target table is uniformly \(1/16\), while the root-swapped table alternates between \(1/32\) and \(3/32\). Thus both statements can hold: the fixed canonical behavior may certify four bits, while the maximal Bell value alone does not.

2. One sparse construction

Let \(X\lvert j\rangle=\lvert j+1\bmod d\rangle\), and let \(z_k\) be the \(d\) roots of \(z^d=(-1)^{d-1}\). For any ordering \(\kappa\), set

\[ \begin{gathered} A_0=X,\qquad A_1=X\,\mathrm{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),\\ V_y=X\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{1+\omega^yz_{\kappa_j}}{|1+\omega^yz_{\kappa_j}|}\right),\qquad B_y=\overline{V_y}\ (y<d),\qquad B_d=X. \end{gathered} \]

The phase products equal one, so the weighted-shift power identity proves every order-\(d\) relation. Also \(A_0+\omega^yA_1=V_yH_y\) with \(H_y>0\), making \(V_y\) the exact polar unitary. This saturates every term of the sharp Bell certificate.

Why the outputs change

Define \(q_0=1\), \(q_{j+1}=z_{\kappa_j}q_j\), and \(\widehat q_m=\sum_jq_j\omega^{mj}\). Then

\[ p(a,b\mid1,d)=\frac{|\widehat q_{-(a+b)}|^2}{d^3}. \]

Choosing \(\kappa=(0,1,\ldots,d-3,d-1,d-2)\) makes the second cyclic autocorrelation

\[ C_2=(z_{d-1}-z_{d-2})(z_{d-3}-z_0),\qquad |C_2|=4\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)>0. \]

Fourier inversion, Parseval, and a mean-zero \(\ell_1\) estimate produce the displayed strict guessing gap.

3. Exact \(d=4\) certificate

With \(\zeta=e^{\pi i/8}\) and \(\kappa=(0,1,3,2)\), the entire witness consists of sparse monomial weighted shifts over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})\). Its Fourier sequence has

\[ \bigl(|\widehat q_m|^2\bigr)_{m=0}^3=(2,6,2,6), \]

and therefore

\[ p(a,b\mid1,4)= \begin{cases} 1/32,&a+b\ \text{even},\\ 3/32,&a+b\ \text{odd}, \end{cases} \qquad G=\frac3{32}>\frac1{16}. \]

  • generate_certificate.py — independently regenerates the compact certificate byte for byte.
  • test_cases.py — regression checks through \(d=12\), secondary to the proof.
  • compare_reference_behavior.py — directly implements the originating paper’s Eqs. (13), (15), and (45), confirming the canonical full behavior and the first-harmonic/full-behavior distinction through \(d=12\).
  • family_certificate.json — symbolic all-dimensional specification, not a formal proof object.

4. Source conditioning and numerical evidence

The general SDP discussion in the originating preprint permits either a full target distribution or a Bell-value constraint. Appendix B.1 uses the former. The exact comparison above concerns the latter and proves nonuniqueness of the maximizing behavior. No claim is made that the originating fixed-behavior SDP is erroneous.

Every root ordering in this particular weighted-shift family has a uniform target table for \(d=2,3\). The nonuniform mechanism begins at \(d=4\), and the complete maximizing faces at \(d=2,3\) remain unresolved.

5. Novelty and unresolved questions

A targeted search found no prior public equivalent of this weighted-shift counterexample. The defensible statement is only “to the best of our knowledge”; unindexed, unpublished, or concurrent work may exist.

The result does not settle \(d=2,3\), classify the complete maximizing face, or determine its worst guessing probability. A supplementary appendix proves equal multiplicities of the allowed relative-unitary eigenvalues on the state-supported Alice space, but this is not a self-testing theorem.
