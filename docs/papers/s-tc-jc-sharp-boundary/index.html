Abstract

Phylogenetic networks extend trees by allowing reticulation events, but a topology is scientifically meaningful only to the extent that it is identifiable from the distribution induced at the observed taxa. We study the displayed-tree Jukes–Cantor model on binary standard semi-directed level-2 networks. A semi-directed topology is called strongly tree-child here when every admissible rooting is tree-child. We first prove a structural fact: any binary level-2 semi-directed topology that admits even one tree-child rooting automatically has at most one triangle in each blob. We then show that the exact generic leaf-pattern distribution determines every strongly tree-child level-2 topology up to ordinary triangle redirection, the only topology change that can remain observationally compatible on a full-dimensional model region. More precisely, one-sided full-dimensional stochastic containment occurs exactly when the labelled bridge trees agree and corresponding blobs are isomorphic or triangle-redirected. The proof combines Fourier cut factorization, pointwise cut-split recovery, projective extraction of local boundary tensors, bounded-support reconstruction of cycle and theta blobs, and exact finite atlases. The final atlas case consists of 192 seven-outgoing-port cycle-to-theta completions; established Jukes–Cantor trinet inequalities, applied to restored support labels, separate all of them. Sharpness holds at weak tree-childness: for every number of leaves at least four, two nonisomorphic and non-triangle-equivalent weakly but not strongly tree-child networks share a full-dimensional regular Jukes–Cantor model region. Exact graph, polynomial, sign, and Jacobian certificates accompany the proof.

1. Main theorems

Three tree-child classes are kept distinct, over the open JC parameter domain \(0<x_e<1\), \(0<\lambda_r<1\): \(R_{\mathrm{TC}}\) (the supplied rooted DAG is tree-child), \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) (the standard semi-directed topology has at least one tree-child rooted partner), and \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) (every admissible rooted partner is tree-child).

Theorem (automatic triangle bound). Every binary standard semi-directed level-2 topology in \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) has at most one triangle in each blob. In particular, the conclusion holds for every topology in \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\).

The proof is purely graph-theoretic: it reduces a hypothetical two-triangle blob to the unique \((1,2,2)\) theta core (\(K_4\) minus an edge) and excludes every possible tree-child rooting. This removes the one-triangle-per-blob hypothesis that the earlier release imposed by assumption — it is now a proved consequence, so the classification below covers all binary standard semi-directed \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) level-2 networks, not a triangle-count-restricted subclass.

Theorem (one-sided observational classification). Let \(N\) and \(N'\) be binary standard semi-directed \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) level-2 topologies on the same leaf set. Then \(N\preceq_{\mathrm{JC}}N'\) if and only if (a) their labelled homeomorphism-reduced bridge trees are isomorphic, and (b) under the induced correspondence of ports, every pair of corresponding local factors is port-labelled isomorphic or related by ordinary triangle redirection \(T\). Every surviving one-sided relation is a symmetric full-dimensional regular overlap; there are no proper one-sided generic containments between distinct local generator types or port placements.

Corollary (generic identifiability modulo triangles). For every fixed topology \(N\) in the stated class, there is a proper algebraic subset \(Z_N\) of its parameter space such that the exact JC distribution at every parameter outside \(Z_N\) determines the standard semi-directed topology up to labelled isomorphism and valid ordinary triangle redirection.

At a generic parameter point the exact JC distribution recovers: the labelled homeomorphism-reduced bridge tree; whether each blob is a cycle or a theta blob; the oriented theta core modulo the local triangle move; the labelled descendant block attached at every port; and the directed core segment and total order of ports along it. Ordinary triangle redirection is the only topology change that can remain observationally compatible on a full-dimensional model region — rooted presentations remain nonidentifiable under the stationary reversible JC model, and \(T\)-related endpoints are proved to share a full-dimensional regular stochastic germ, not necessarily the same complete open stochastic image.

Theorem (sharpness of strong tree-childness). For every \(n\ge4\) there are two nonisomorphic, non-\(T\)-equivalent binary level-2 standard semi-directed networks \(N_n,N_n'\in W_{\mathrm{TC}}\setminus S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) with \(N_n\bowtie_{\mathrm{JC}}N_n'\): both have full-dimensional regular overlapping JC images, common local model dimension \(2n\), and both have rooted tree-child representatives, but each also has an admissible rooting that is not tree-child.

Together with the automatic triangle bound, this gives the boxed sharp boundary: \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) is generically JC-identifiable modulo \(T\); \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) is not — for all binary standard semi-directed level-2 networks, with no triangle-count hypothesis left over. The theorem does not address level greater than two, substitution models richer than JC, a positive classification of the full weakly tree-child class, or equality of complete stochastic images under \(T\); multi-triangle blobs are not an omitted case — the automatic triangle bound proves no binary \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) topology can contain one.

2. Relation to prior work

Ardiyansyah (2021) develops Fourier-invariant tools and proves several algebraic distinguishability and noncontainment results for simple and semisimple level-2 network varieties under JC, K2P, and K3P, including four-leaf cases; it does not give a global open-stochastic classification of all strongly tree-child level-2 networks. Englander et al. (Proposition 2.26, April 2025 version) introduce the trinet polynomial \(F_{abc}=r_{ab}r_{ac}r_{bc}-u_{abc}^2\) to prove a pointwise strict inequality distinguishing a three-leaf tree from strict level-1/2 trinets under JC; a level-\(k\) extension appears in Brits et al. (2026, Lemma 5.5). \(F_{abc}\) itself is not new here — what is new is that, after restoring the labels required by a rigid core-3 support, suitable trinet restrictions make \(F_{abc}\) a strict separator for every one of the 192 residual seven-port completions, closing the final atlas case of the classification.

The comparison distinguishes algebraic, generic, and pointwise notions of identifiability, which are not interchangeable: equality of Zariski closures does not imply equality, or even nonempty intersection, of open stochastic images. Full detail, including the closest-prior-result table, is in docs/PRIOR_WORK_AND_NOVELTY_AUDIT.md.

3. Computer-assisted proof and reproducibility

The proof separates entirely symbolic arguments — Fourier bridge factorization and its reciprocal gauge group, analytic bridge peeling, the two-active-endpoint cut contradiction, reduction of level-2 blobs to a cycle or theta core, the automatic exclusion of multi-triangle weakly tree-child blobs, rigid-support and ordered-word reconstruction, root reduction, semialgebraic localization, and the local-to-global product theorem — from five finite computational tasks: independently enumerating every binary acyclic rooting of the sole two-triangle level-2 core; enumerating all oriented cycle and theta cores and bounded port placements; evaluating exact JC Fourier tensors and polynomial invariants on every canonical type; proving strict open-cube signs by sparse factorization or tensor-product Bernstein coefficients; and certifying generic model dimensions by exact nonzero Jacobian minors. No floating-point fit is used to certify an overlap, containment, separation, sign, dimension, or graph count.

The automatic-triangle census independently enumerates all 25 valid binary acyclic rootings of \(K_4\) minus an edge in Python and in a second, independently written C++ program, finding zero tree-child presentations in either. The seven-port residual atlas regenerates 192 role records as eight free \(S_4\)-orbits and 1,686 canonical completions; the pointwise-cut calculation regenerates 177 endpoint types, 453 one-active crossing types, and 547 sparse Bernstein expansions, independently re-derived by a separate reviewer that does not import the primary sign routine. Jacobian ranks are certified by exact nonzero minors, some first located modulo the prime 1,000,003 and then replayed exactly over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

5. Scope and limitations

Generic identifiability is an infinite-data property; it does not imply that all identifiable features can be estimated accurately from finite sequences, especially near the algebraic exceptional locus or a triangle-model intersection. The reconstruction theorem is constructive but not an efficient implementation: its output is the structural \(T\)-equivalence class, and deciding which redirected orientations contain one particular observed distribution is a separate semialgebraic membership problem, not resolved here.

The theorem is specific to the displayed-tree JC model with uniform root distribution, strictly positive finite branch lengths, and inheritance probabilities strictly between zero and one; boundary parameters, coalescent processes, site-rate variation, nonstationary substitution, richer group-based models, and level greater than two all require separate analysis. No claim is made about efficient inference, statistically stable estimators near the exceptional locus, or a positive classification of the full weakly tree-child class. No external referee or subject-matter specialist has reviewed this release, and no journal submission has been made; the submission package (manuscript, cover letters, referee guide, reproducibility archive) is prepared for that step.
