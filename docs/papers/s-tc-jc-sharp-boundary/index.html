Abstract

Binary standard semi-directed level-2 phylogenetic networks with at most one triangle per blob, restricted to the class \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) where every admissible rooted partner is tree-child, are generically identifiable under the four-state Jukes–Cantor (JC) model modulo ordinary triangle redirection \(T\): a complete one-sided observational classification (Theorem A) shows two such networks share a JC preordering exactly when their reduced bridge trees agree and every pair of corresponding blobs is either labelled-isomorphic or a \(T\)-redirection of a triangle. Generic parameters are identified up to \(T\) and labelled isomorphism (Corollary B). This is sharp: replacing \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) by the strictly larger class \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) (some admissible rooted partner, not necessarily every one, is tree-child) admits, for every \(n\ge4\), a Theta-construction pair of non-isomorphic, non-\(T\)-equivalent \(2n\)-dimensional networks whose open JC images share a full-dimensional regular region (Theorem C).

1. Locked class names

Three tree-child classes are kept distinct throughout, over the open JC parameter domain \(0<x_e<1\), \(0<\lambda_r<1\):

  • \(R_{\mathrm{TC}}\): the supplied rooted DAG is tree-child;
  • \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\): the standard semi-directed topology has at least one tree-child rooted partner;
  • \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\): every admissible rooted partner is tree-child.

The network class under study is binary standard semi-directed \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) level-2 networks with at most one triangle in each blob.

2. Main theorems

Theorem A (complete one-sided observational classification). For leaf-labelled \(N,N'\) in the stated class, \(N\preceq_{\mathrm{JC}}N'\) if and only if their labelled homeomorphism-reduced bridge trees agree and, under the induced correspondence of ports, every pair of corresponding blobs is either labelled-isomorphic or differs only by ordinary triangle redirection \(T\). Every surviving local relation is a symmetric regular overlap, so \(T\) is the complete observational ambiguity in \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\).

Corollary B (generic identifiability modulo T). For each fixed \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) topology \(N\), there is a proper algebraic exceptional set in its parameter space outside of which the exact JC distribution identifies the parameter up to ordinary triangle redirection and labelled isomorphism.

Theorem C (sharpness). The conclusion fails on replacing \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) by \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\). For every \(n\ge4\), the inherited Theta construction gives nonisomorphic, non-\(T\)-equivalent networks \(N_n,N_n'\in W_{\mathrm{TC}}\setminus S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) of model dimension \(2n\) whose open JC model images share a full-dimensional regular region.

Consequently: \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) is generically JC-identifiable modulo \(T\); \(W_{\mathrm{TC}}\) is not. The strong tree-child requirement on every admissible rooted partner is exactly where identifiability breaks.

3. Method

The proof enumerates a final seven-port universe of 192 residual mixed-graph records across eight free \(S_4\)-orbits, giving 1,686 canonical conservative completions. A universal three-port separator \(F_{abc}=r_{ab}r_{ac}r_{bc}-u_{abc}^2\) built from Fourier pair and all-distinct zero-sum coordinates distinguishes cycle- from theta-sourced records: six orbits are separated before completion, and the two genuine completion cases (orbits 649 and 705) are each resolved by an exact strict-positivity identity after marginalization. Exact Jacobian minors modulo the prime \(1{,}000{,}003\) certify that these structural dimension bounds are met exactly for every occurring count vector.

A bounded-support reconstruction theorem promotes the closed six-outgoing-port atlas to arbitrary subdivisions via rigid supports and one-/two-port probes, giving a nonroot local theorem for arbitrary finite port-labelled \(S_{\mathrm{TC}}\) cycle or theta factors. A root-reduction argument (reversing a tree-child root-to-leaf path) shows every rooted local factor reduces to the nonroot case regardless of which admissible pendant root site was used. A separate pointwise-cut analysis over 177 canonical endpoint tensor types and four exact wrong-split minors shows a flattening has rank at most four exactly at a cut split, so cuts are preserved in both containment directions. Peeling the common bridge tree's Fourier rank-one blocks and gauge-fixing then reduces global containment to local containment factor by factor, giving Theorem A; a canonical reconstruction procedure (Fourier transform, split test, block factorization, port-role determination, ordered-word reconstruction, lexicographically-least re-rooting) recovers the full observational-equivalence class from an exact distribution.

5. Scope and limitations

The result concerns exact, infinite-data JC observations on binary standard semi-directed level-2 networks with at most one triangle per blob; it does not address finite-sample estimation, model classes with more than one triangle per blob or higher-level blobs, non-JC substitution models, or numerical conditioning of the inversion. The exceptional set in Corollary B is shown proper by a Zariski-closure and dominance argument but is not given an explicit description or codimension bound. Theorem C's sharpness construction is specific to the Theta family inherited from prior work; it is not claimed to characterize every source of failure once the "every admissible rooted partner" requirement is dropped.

No external expert or author has been contacted, and no external review has occurred. The natural collaborative follow-ups are an explicit description of the exceptional set, extension to more than one triangle per blob, and a finite-sample identifiability analysis.
