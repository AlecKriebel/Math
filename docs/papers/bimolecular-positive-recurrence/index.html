Abstract

Every finite bimolecular weakly reversible stochastic mass-action network with one linkage class is positive recurrent on every closed communicating class, for every positive rate vector. No pure-species complex assumption is imposed — the theorem covers coordinate-face, parity, lattice-restricted, siphon, conservative, and boundary classes alike. The proof augments the embedded jump chain by the target complex of the most recent reaction and removes that target from the population. The resulting residual factorial potential \(V(x,t)=\sum_i\log((x_i-t_i)!)\) has the exact jump increment \(\log((x)_t/(x)_s)\) when the next reaction source is \(s\); following the carried target costs exactly zero, so all Foster drift comes from source switching. A finite library of target-following episodes reduces every possible hierarchy of source propensities to a scalar concave recursion, and a normalized-log compactification of an arbitrary divergent state sequence forces either an enabled source of strictly greater asymptotic weight at a lower terminal complex, or an exact reaction-wise conservation law contradicting divergence in the fixed communicating class. This yields one common proper random-time Foster function, a finite exceptional set, and bounded jump-count episodes; a self-contained finite trace-chain argument then converts the certificate to finite mean return time for the original continuous-time chain.

1. The residual-factorial mechanism

The embedded jump chain is augmented by the target complex \(t\) of the most recent reaction. For \(x\ge t\), the potential \(V(x,t)=\sum_i\log((x_i-t_i)!)\) records the log-factorial of the residual population \(r=x-t\) left after removing the currently carried target. If the next reaction has source \(s\) and target \(t'\), the exact increment factors as \(\Delta V=\log((x)_t/(x)_s)\): moving from residual \(r\) forward by consuming \(s\) and re-crediting \(t'\) leaves a term that depends only on the source, not on which target is eventually produced. Consequently, a chain of reactions that keeps re-selecting the same currently carried target as its own source contributes zero net drift — all reward for the Foster argument comes from the (finitely many) moments the chain switches which complex it is sourcing from.

For every ordered pair of complexes, a designated finite target-following episode of at most \(|\mathcal C|\) jumps is constructed. A sharp scalar recursion shows that if the terminal complex of an episode has vanishing source probability along a divergent direction, the entire episode's expected drift tends to \(-\infty\), regardless of how many distinct propensity scales are present among the intermediate reactions. Bimolecularity is what makes this dichotomy exhaustive: writing an arbitrary divergent state sequence's directions in normalized-log form \(w\), either a lower terminal complex exposes an enabled source of strictly larger \(w\)-weight, or a species-linear conservation law contradicts divergence inside the fixed closed communicating class. The first alternative forces the relevant terminal source probability to zero, and a finite bad-sequence contradiction then makes the finite episode family uniformly negative outside one finite exceptional set \(K\).

2. Theorem and Foster certificate

Theorem. Let \(G=(\mathcal S,\mathcal C,\mathcal R)\) be a finite stochastic mass-action reaction network such that (1) every complex has molecularity at most two, (2) the reaction graph has one linkage class and is strongly connected, and (3) every nontrivial reaction has a positive rate constant. Then every closed communicating class of the associated nonexplosive continuous-time Markov chain is positive recurrent.

The stronger Foster statement used in the proof: augment the embedded jump chain by the target complex \(t\) of the most recent reaction, and for \(x\ge t\) put \(V(x,t)=\sum_i\log((x_i-t_i)!)\). There is a finite set \(K\) of augmented states, one finite target-following episode for every ordered pair of complexes, and a deterministic state-dependent choice among those episodes, such that every selected episode has at most \(|\mathcal C|\) jumps and, outside \(K\), \(\mathbb E_{(x,t)}[V(Z_\tau)-V(x,t)]\le-1\). Because the current target is always enabled, if \(\kappa_*\) is the smallest nontrivial reaction rate, the physical duration of an episode is stochastically dominated by a sum of \(|\mathcal C|\) independent \(\mathrm{Exp}(\kappa_*)\) variables. This finite-family random-time Foster certificate gives finite expected hitting time of \(K\), and a finite trace-chain argument gives finite expected return time to one state — positive recurrence.

The theorem is restricted to one linkage class: the proof uses a directed path from every carried target to every chosen terminal complex, which is not available across distinct linkage classes. Nonexplosion of bimolecular weakly reversible stochastic mass-action systems is inherited rather than reproved.

3. Discovery phases and what Phase V newly proves

The repository preserves the full discovery history, including recorded failed lemmas (an uncorrected one-step entropy drift, a one-step total-count drift, a coercive quadratic correction, and a finite-degree factorial-polynomial stationary ansatz all fail on specific boundary calibrations — see failed_approaches/ and WORKLOG.md). Phases II–IV (phase2_trigger_drain/, phase3_defect_credit/, phase4_critical_lamperti/) develop the trigger-drain stopping-time lemma, defect-credit tier induction, and a critical Lamperti-type bounded-defect theorem; the Phase-IV bounded-defect theorem is not used as a black box in the final proof because its last target-retention interface had not been fully certified.

Phase V, in phase5_source_flag_closure/, is the final self-contained resolution and the source of the theorem and manuscript on this page. It newly proves: the exact target/source residual-factorial identity; a source-probability entropy bound; the finite target-following episode recursion; complete-credit scalar elimination; the normalized-log top-availability-or-global-conservation dichotomy; the finite-family sequence-to-uniform Foster closure; deterministic overshoot and physical-duration bounds; and the finite trace-chain conversion to positive recurrence. What it inherits is limited to nonexplosion, Phase-I target-lifting intuition, and exact finite graph/conservation utilities from earlier phases.

5. Scope and limitations

The theorem is restricted to one linkage class; the proof's directed target-following paths are not available across distinct linkage classes, and no claim is made about the multiple-linkage-class case. No claim of exponential ergodicity, product-form stationarity, or an explicit stationary tail exponent is made. The finite exceptional set \(K\) is defined through episode expectations and proved finite by a compactness argument; the current implementation does not give a polynomial-time bound on its radius. Nonexplosion of bimolecular weakly reversible stochastic mass-action systems is inherited from prior literature rather than reproved here.

No external expert or author has been contacted, and no external review has occurred. The natural collaborative follow-ups are extending the argument across multiple linkage classes, an explicit bound on the exceptional set, and quantitative (rather than qualitative) ergodicity.
