Abstract

We prove that every finite bimolecular weakly reversible stochastic mass-action network with one linkage class is positive recurrent on each closed communicating class, for every positive choice of rate constants. This removes the hypothesis in the 2020 theorem of Anderson, Cappelletti, and Kim that each species occurs in a unary or double complex. The proof marks the target complex of the actual reaction channel that most recently fired and applies a log-factorial potential to the population remaining after that target is removed. Its increment is exactly a target/source falling-factorial ratio. Finite target-following paths propagate the negative reward of a rare terminal source, while a normalized-log compactification and an exhaustive bimolecular top-complex alternative make that terminal source available along every divergent sequence unless a linear stoichiometric invariant already bounds the sequence.

1. The marked-target mechanism

The embedded chain is augmented by the target complex \(t\) of the actual reaction channel that just fired — not a target inferred from the population displacement, which matters once several channels share a source or a displacement. If the post-jump population is \(x\), then \(x-t\ge0\). The residual factorial potential \(V(x,t)=\sum_i\log((x_i-t_i)!)\) has the exact increment \(V(x-s+u,u)-V(x,t)=\log((x)_t/(x)_s)\) when the next channel has source \(s\) and target \(u\): following the carried target as the next source costs exactly zero, so all reward comes from source switching.

For every ordered pair of complexes, a fixed directed path from the carried target to a selected terminal complex is followed only while its exact marked channels fire — a finite target-following episode. A one-dimensional scalar envelope propagates the negative drift of a rare terminal source backward through the whole path without comparing unrelated rate monomials. Every divergent residual sequence yields normalized logarithmic weights on species, and molecularity at most two gives an exhaustive top-complex split: either a higher-weight source is enabled over a lower terminal complex, forcing that terminal's source probability to vanish, or a reaction-wise linear invariant contradicts divergence inside one communicating class. Together these give a finite nonempty exceptional set, a state-selected random-time drift of at most \(-1\) outside it, and a finite trace-chain return argument; nonexplosion is then proved directly from recurrent visits to a marked state and its repeated positive exponential holding times, rather than inherited from prior literature.

2. Theorem and stationary corollary

Theorem (binary one-linkage positive recurrence). Let a finite stochastic mass-action reaction network be weakly reversible, have one linkage class, and satisfy molecularity at most two at every complex. For every positive rate vector, every closed communicating class of its population continuous-time Markov chain is positive recurrent.

Corollary (stationary probability). Under the hypotheses of the theorem, every closed communicating class \(\Gamma\) admits a stationary probability distribution \(\pi_\Gamma\), unique on \(\Gamma\).

The result is class-wise: coordinate faces, siphons, parity restrictions, and other lattice constraints require no separate interior argument, since every constructed target-following path consists of actual enabled channels and stays inside the fixed closed class. The one-linkage hypothesis is used at one decisive point — a directed path from every carried target to every chosen terminal complex must exist, which is not guaranteed across distinct linkage classes — and bimolecularity is used in the exhaustive top-complex case split; neither extension is claimed here.

3. Relation to prior work

Anderson and Kim (2018) formulated the positive-recurrence conjecture for weakly reversible stochastic mass-action systems and proved several structural sufficient conditions. Anderson, Cappelletti, and Kim (2020) proved the binary, one-linkage case under the additional condition that, for every species, the complex set contains a multiple of that species — i.e. every species occurs in a pure unary or pure double complex. This paper removes that condition: the theorem covers every binary single-linkage weakly reversible network, including species that never occur alone or doubled.

4. From discovery to author-ready release

The repository preserves the full discovery history unmodified: recorded failed lemmas (an uncorrected one-step entropy drift, a one-step total-count drift, a coercive quadratic correction, and a finite-degree factorial-polynomial stationary ansatz all fail on specific boundary calibrations — see failed_approaches/ and WORKLOG.md), Phases II–IV (phase2_trigger_drain/, phase3_defect_credit/, phase4_critical_lamperti/), and the Phase V discovery manuscript (phase5_source_flag_closure/).

Version 0.2 is an independent adversarial reconstruction of the Phase V proof from the marked-target manuscript alone, deliberately without reusing the discarded Phase III/IV hierarchy arguments. It completed Gates A1–A12 — marked-channel irreducibility, properness of the residual factorial potential, the exact one-jump entropy identity, the full scalar-envelope branches, the exhaustive bimolecular top-complex split, finiteness and nonemptiness of the exceptional set, finite trace-chain closure, and direct nonexplosion — tested fifteen mandatory adversarial examples, and found no substantive theorem defect. It then rewrote the discovery note as a self-contained archival article with full declarations and appendices, prepared arXiv and Journal of Applied Probability initial-submission wrappers, and replaced the project-dependent verifier with a dependency-free standalone package. Full detail is in revision_log.md and audit/independent_proof_reconstruction.md.

6. Scope and limitations

The theorem is restricted to one linkage class: the proof's directed target-following paths are not available across distinct linkage classes, and no claim is made about the multiple-linkage-class case. Complexes of molecularity three or higher admit top-complex configurations the argument does not cover, so no claim is made for them either. The proof establishes positive recurrence and existence/uniqueness of the stationary distribution on each closed communicating class; it does not provide a product form, explicit tail bounds, exponential ergodicity, quantitative mixing rates, or the full Anderson–Kim positive-recurrence conjecture.

No external subject-matter expert or journal referee has reviewed this release; the JAP submission wrapper is prepared but no submission has been made. The natural collaborative follow-ups are extending the argument across multiple linkage classes, molecularity above two, and quantitative (rather than qualitative) ergodicity.
