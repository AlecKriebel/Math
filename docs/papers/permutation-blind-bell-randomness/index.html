Abstract

How many measurement settings are needed for one Bell score to certify the maximum \(2\log_2d\) private joint bits from projective \(d\)-outcome measurements? This note does not determine that minimum. It instead proves a general permutation-blindness theorem for a broad polar-linear design class, applies the same explicit biased maximizers to both cyclic Bell families of Perito et al., and gives exact obstructions to two natural low-setting repair strategies.

1. Main results

Permutation-blindness theorem. For a polar-linear Bell score

\[ \mathcal J=\sum_r\operatorname{Re}\!\left[(\alpha_rA_0+\beta_rA_1)\otimes B_r\right] +\operatorname{Re}(A_0\otimes B_*), \]

an exact positive-factor certificate reduces the quantum bound to the scalar maximum of \(f(z)=\sum_r|\alpha_r+\beta_rz|\). Under explicit phase-product hypotheses, every cyclic ordering of the same \(d\) scalar equality points yields an admissible order-\(d\) maximizer with identical first-harmonic Bell data, although its full target distribution may change.

The theorem is conditional on fully stated weighted-cycle hypotheses; it is not a no-go theorem for arbitrary Bell functionals. It isolates a precise design failure: an unordered relative-unitary spectrum need not determine the ordered products that control output probabilities.

Two cyclic families

The final-two root swap produces exact nonuniform maximizers for both augmented cyclic families in the originating preprint. For every \(d\ge4\), Eve may be one-dimensional and

\[ G(AB\mid E)\ge \frac1{d^2} \frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)} >\frac1{d^2}. \]

For the first family, the augmented value remains \(2\csc(\pi/(2d))+1\). For the second SOS-certified family, the value remains \(d+1\); the construction annihilates every published SOS factor after an exact discrete-Fourier phase calculation. Thus the maximal score alone does not certify maximal global randomness for either family.

2. Why the score misses the bias

Let \(z_0,\ldots,z_{d-1}\) be scalar equality phases and let \(\kappa\) order them around a weighted shift. Set \(q_0=1\) and \(q_{j+1}=z_{\kappa_j}q_j\). The target table is

\[ p_\kappa(a,b)=\frac{|\widehat q_{-(a+b)}|^2}{d^3}. \]

The Bell-visible first harmonics are symmetric sums over the same unordered roots; the Fourier magnitudes above depend on their order. Swapping the final two natural roots gives the exact cyclic autocorrelation

\[ C_2=(z_{d-1}-z_{d-2})(z_{d-3}-z_0),\qquad |C_2|=4\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)>0 \]

for every \(d\ge4\), proving that the target table is not flat.

Exact \(d=4\) witness

The independent verifier works entirely in \(\mathbb Q[x]/(x^8+1)\), with \(x=\zeta_{16}\). It checks both Bell families, their order-four observables, the full second-family SOS identity, and

\[ p(a,b)= \begin{cases} 1/32,&a+b\ \text{even},\\ 3/32,&a+b\ \text{odd}, \end{cases} \qquad G=\frac3{32}>\frac1{16}. \]

3. Setting-complexity consequences

A standard locality argument proves that if either party has only one input, every nonsignalling behavior admits a compatible pure classical-flag realization in which Eve guesses the complete target pair perfectly. Therefore every private-randomness protocol needs at least two inputs on each wing. At \(d=2\), a known \(2\times2\) binary construction attains that lower bound.

For \(d\ge3\), the paper proves two narrower obstructions:

  • none of the four ideal input pairs in the standard two-setting Fourier-phase qudit self-test is uniformly distributed over \(d^2\) outputs;
  • a separately bounded third-setting term drawn from the real span of those two Alice PVM algebras cannot expose their common computational mutually unbiased basis, except trivially.

These statements rule out named architectures, not the full \(2\times2\) or \(2\times3\) scenarios. A coupled SOS, another target basis, higher harmonics, or a different self-test may still work.

4. Verification package

  • verify_second_family_d4_exact.py — exact \(d=4\) verifier for the coefficients, Fourier selection, order relations, SOS identity, Bell values, and biased target table.
  • test_cases.py — dependency-free regression suite for \(d=2,\ldots,6\).
  • second_family_discovery.py — independent matrix regression through \(d=12\).
  • satwap_ideal_audit.py — exact ideal-table formulas and finite checks of the naive third-setting repair.
  • family_certificate.json — machine-readable all-dimensional formulas.
  • MANIFEST.sha256 — integrity hashes for the frozen package.

The all-dimensional statements rest on the written analytic proofs. The programs independently test exact finite witnesses and symbolic formulas; they are not a formal proof of every general theorem.

5. Scope and unresolved questions

The note does not classify all maximizers, determine the worst guessing probability on either cyclic maximizing face, solve the \(d=2,3\) boundary cases for those faces, or prove robustness from additional observed data. Because biased strategies already attain the exact cyclic maxima, no Bell-value-only bound converging to \(1/d^2\) can hold for those two scores.

A targeted primary-source audit found no all-dimensional \(2\times2\) or \(2\times3\) projective, single-score construction and no universal impossibility theorem for those scenarios. This search result is not proof of novelty or priority. The minimum-setting law requested by the research program remains open.
