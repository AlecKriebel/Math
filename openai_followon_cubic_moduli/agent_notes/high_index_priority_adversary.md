## Mechanism assessment

The numerical ingredient is a familiar Fano index bound, with an explicit checkable singular proof: if -K_Z=rA and A is ample Cartier, Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing and antiample-section vanishing force the Hilbert polynomial to vanish at -1,...,-(r-1). Thus dim Z>=r-1. This should be presented as an elementary lemma/deduction, not a new general index theorem. The same statement holds without choosing a KE metric.

The additional use is to retain the Cartier root on every finite quasi-etale cover and on every product factor; this forces n>=2(r-1) for any nontrivial product. Applying DGP then forces one stable factor. Stability downstairs still needs the actual determinant-degree/reflexive-pullback argument, rather than saying stable automatically descends. Reapplying this argument to every cover gives the asserted stability on all covers. These are finite, explicit obligations and do not replace a central unknown with another conjecture.

For metric fibers, the local positive-Ricci curvature contraction that kills parallel (2,0)-forms is classical differential geometry. Combining it with stable-sheaf simplicity is an additional route around incomplete de Rham theory. The mathematical contribution, if all extensions are proved, is the singular criterion/application, not a new curvature identity. It also requires that real metric isometries preserve the exact smooth algebraic locus, and that a (anti)holomorphic map there extends across the normal singular spaces with polarization preserved. My priority audit does not independently certify those parts.

The equality example P^(r-1) x P^(r-1) checks sharpness of the **general numerical sufficient threshold**: it has dimension 2(r-1), root L=O(1,1), product KE metric, and mixed factor conjugation is neither globally holomorphic nor antiholomorphic. This is an explicit boundary counterexample to including equality in the general criterion. It is not a cubic or a counterexample to cubic-fourfold sign-rigidity. The ordinary numerical observation and product example alone would be too slight to justify relabeling the established complex comparison as a new solution.
