## Target A: prior formulation and construction framework

[Bradford–Fournier-Facio](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-024-03589-3), *Hopfian wreath products and the stable finiteness conjecture*, is available as [arXiv:2211.01510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01510). The first submission was 2 November 2022; version 2 was submitted 2 September 2024; the journal article appeared online 21 October 2024 as *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 308, article 58. Exact PDF passages were compared between arXiv v1 and the published [primary PDF](https://d-nb.info/1355447615/34).

The precise existence question is already Question 1.13 in arXiv v1 and Question 1.14 in the published article. Published Theorems 1.3 and 4.11 connect Hopficity for all finitely generated abelian lamp groups and finitely generated Hopfian acting groups to Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture. Published Theorem 1.5 gives the prime-rank matrix-ring criterion. Lemma 4.1 treats Hopficity of the regular module; Theorem 4.2 explicitly extends an equivariant surjective noninjective module map to the wreath product. Consequently, right multiplication by b and the kernel lamp 1−ba specialize their existing mechanism. They are valuable explicit bookkeeping, but not a new equivalence or a newly invented way to transfer a one-sided inverse defect.

The embedding input used in their proof is [Miller–Schupp, *Embeddings into hopfian groups*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(71)90028-7), *Journal of Algebra* 17(2) (1971), 171–176. Crossref metadata was freshly retrieved; the original full paper could not be retrieved in this audit. Credit this source for the original framework without asserting that this priority audit checked its full proof. The accessible replacement input [Bridson–Short, *Complete embeddings of groups*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972723001442), *Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society* 110(1) (2024), 136–144, Theorem A, also supplies the required finitely generated Hopfian ambient group. The separate mathematical audit determines whether that replacement is proved adequately here. Neither source makes the original OpenAI G Hopfian automatically.

The current primary Kourovka notebook retains Bradford–Fournier-Facio's Problem 21.22 without a resolution marker; the current update file contains no 21.22 entry. These observations corroborate its current presentation, but do not prove that no earlier resolution has circulated. The exact question predates that notebook entry through the 2022 Bradford–Fournier-Facio preprint.

Two nearby lines of work do not establish Target A:

- [Kochloukova, *Hopfian combinatorial wreath products*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19235), v1 of 22 February 2026, Proposition A(b), constructs a non-Hopfian abelian-lamp wreath over a Hopfian acting group using B/H and a nontrivial stabilizer. This is not the regular action. Its result must not be relabeled a resolution of 21.22.
- [Fournier-Facio, *A torsion-free non-sofic group*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02025), and [Kun–Thom, *Nonsofic wreath products of residually finite groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06222), concern nonsoficity. Their first-version texts and current abstract/version records were inspected: Fournier-Facio's v2 of 14 August 2026 notes small changes, and Kun–Thom's v3 of 20 August 2026 notes updates to Theorem A. Inspection did not identify the exact restricted-regular Hopfian-factor conclusion there. This is a narrowly scoped source comparison, not a claim of exhaustive priority over everything announced in August.

## Target B: prior sharpness statements and product literature

[Cohn–Elkies, *New upper bounds on sphere packings I*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2003.157.689), *Annals of Mathematics* 157(2) (2003), 689–714, develops the two-point method. Its Conjecture 7.3 includes sharpness in dimension 2. Conjecture 8.1 additionally specifies a stronger root pattern; the two targets here do not establish that stronger claim. The journal's 2009 online archive date must not replace the 2003 publication date.

[Cohn–de Laat–Salmon, *Three-point bounds for sphere packing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15373), arXiv v1 of 30 June 2022, proves the product-program identity in Proposition 4.7 and a lattice-versus-two-point comparison in Theorem 4.2. Its construction is the substantive input to the triangle upper-bound route. A naive product of sign-changing primal two-point functions is not an independently justified replacement. The corrected [de Laat–Vallentin arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3789v3), submitted 24 August 2021, explicitly repairs the proof of Lemma 5; any retained compact duality dependency must use the corrected statement/proof.

[AIM Problem 1.32](http://aimpl.org/discreteaf/1/) attributes the question to de Laat. The source asks for numerical equality, observed O(n)×O(n) invariance, and conversion in both directions. Showing the exact planar values alone addresses its dimension-two numerical subcase. It does not justify marking the general PR #487 problem solved.
