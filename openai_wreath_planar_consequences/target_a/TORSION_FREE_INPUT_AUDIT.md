## Independent algebraic reconstruction

Let Γ_A and Γ_B be finite loopless graphs immersed in one signed rose. For a vertex x, S_x is its set of outgoing signed labels. Kill every closed graph path by attaching an abstract cone on each graph component. If A′ and B′ are the rooted components, path labels from the roots give well-defined elements g_x and h_y, since any two chosen paths differ by a loop killed by a cone. A directed t-edge gives g_{x·t}=g_x t and h_{y·t}=h_y t.

On A′×A′, connect two vertices by each simultaneous outgoing t-step. The reverse step is the same undirected edge; parallel steps remain separate edges. Looplessness of the factors implies looplessness of this product graph. Its degree is |S_x∩S_x′|. Along an edge, (g_x t)(g_x′ t)⁻¹=g_xg_x′⁻¹, so each connected component contributes one group element multiplied by its cardinality to ab, where a=Σg_x and b=Σg_x⁻¹. If all degrees are odd except the even degree at the root pair, the handshake lemma says every other component has even cardinality and the root-pair component has odd cardinality. Thus ab=1 in characteristic two.

The same product graph on A′×B′ has all degrees odd, hence all components even. The edge-invariant g_xh_y⁻¹ gives ac=0 for c=Σh_y⁻¹. If every root-to-different-vertex path in the B-component has nontrivial label, exactly the B-root contributes the identity basis element to c. Therefore c has identity coefficient one and is nonzero. No pairwise distinctness of all h_y is assumed or needed: nonidentity basis contributions may collide without altering this identity coefficient. Finally ba=1 would give c=(ba)c=b(ac)=0.

Falsification checks: unrestricted graph products have loops and wrong degree counts if factor loops are allowed; the eventual girth L≥3 excludes them. Choosing b on the wrong side would destroy the displayed invariant. Protecting only nonroot/nonroot pairs is not a replacement for protection of the B-root. The source uses the required orientations and exact identity coefficient.

## The central bounded-pattern proof

Fix K0,C,I first and let n grow; ε has already been fixed from word decay. Let H be total traversal length with L≤H≤CL. The image graph Δ has at most CL distinct edges and girth≥L. Its cycle rank is bounded in terms of C: remove trees and circle components, suppress degree-two vertices, and assign inherited lengths to the remaining minimum-degree-three multigraph with r edges. A stationary random nonbacktracking walk of k edges has expected inherited length ≤kCL/r; at least half its mass lies at length ≤2kCL/r. Every walk's probability is at most (2r)⁻¹2^{−(k−1)}, so for k=ceil(log₂(8r))+1 there are more short walks than endpoint pairs. Two distinct such walks have the same endpoints; their expanded union contains a cycle of length ≤4kCL/r. Girth forces r≤4Ck, bounding r. Circle components each use at least L edges, so number≤C.

Mark vertices of non-two degree and every indexed path endpoint. Closed immersed paths create no leaves; the sole possible interval gives at most two leaves. The bounded rank bounds total branching degree. The resulting bounded number of chains have degree-two interiors. Every path traverses whole chains. Uses of the same oriented edge by one immersed path are separated by at least L, since the intervening reduced closed walk contains a cycle. Thus each chain multiplicity m_e≤2(C+K0)=:M. The marked graph, chain lengths, traversal lists and comparison endpoints have only polynomially many unlabelled patterns in L. Their image embeddings are injective; repeated traversals are not distinct edges.

At stage j include each edge with multiplicity at least j. Let V_j,E_j be its vertices and edges. Then ΣE_j=H. At a vertex z every nonendpoint incidence pairs by an immersed path turn with a different half-edge. If a(z) is the number (0,1,2) of interval endpoints there, then 2 max_{e∋z}m_e≤Σ_{e∋z}m_e+a(z). Summing proves ΣV_j≤H+ι, where ι is one if an interval exists. Stage one fixes its prescribed initial root, k1=ι; other k_j=0. Hence Σ(V_j−E_j−k_j)≤0. This exact cancellation is the essential correction to any naive repeated-edge first moment.

Split comparisons at chain ends. Each chain-coordinate comparison is x↦±x+a with integral offset a. There are boundedly many offsets. A pigeonhole simultaneous approximation gives u≤N^{d1}, with dist(ua/L,Z)≤1/N for all offsets, N=floor(L^{1/(2(d1+1))}). Put R=floor(sqrt N), s=L/(uR). Then s→∞, s=o(L), and dist(a/s,Z)≤R/N→0, uniformly over fixed patterns. The lower bound s≥L^{(d1+3/2)/(2(d1+1))} makes every entropy/error term below o(L).

Subdivide all chain traversals on this one grid. Away from boundedly many chain/comparison endpoint blocks, a block occurrence is either entirely unpaired or has a matching partner block with an overlapping compared substring of length (1−κ)s, κ→0. The comparisons originally use disjoint positions; overlaps longer than half a block make the resulting occurrence matching partial, with no shared occurrences. If b_* is the number of unmatched full-block occurrences, s b_*≤b0+O(s+L/s)=b0+o(L).

Forget occurrence indices to get a multigraph of links on underlying blocks. A link determines all but κs+O(1) letters of its partner. Given one word, a spanning tree propagates to all words at cost exp(ρ_L s|U|), ρ_L→0. A self-link translation has a nonzero integral offset (zero would pair the same underlying edge), so its long equality substring leaves at most κs+O(1) components in the equality graph on positions. It costs only exp(ρ_L s|U|), without a free long initial word. A self-link reflection would either fix a letter equal to its inverse or force adjacent inverse letters at the overlap's center, impossible for a reduced word. If there is no self-link, a block of maximal multiplicity z_U has at least z_U−b_U matched occurrences, each requiring a distinct occurrence on another block. Hence 2z_U≤S_U+b_U, with S_U=Σm_i.

This is valid and does not assume an extension embeds in the random graph. A self-linked component has only the propagation cost.

## From arbitrary arrangements to one bounded system

A reduced spherical arrangement consists of positive cyclically immersed graph boundary paths in disjoint disks on a sphere, with at most one rooted interval boundary whose endpoints are placed at one marked break. There is at least one ordinary closed boundary. Disjoint arcs pair all edge occurrences with inverse letters and distinct underlying graph edges.

Short closure: delete at most two incident edges at the endpoint of a segment. Remaining minimum degree≥127≥3 produces more than n nonbacktracking walks of length k=ceil(log₂n)+1 from that endpoint. Two end together; cancel their common terminal part to get a nonempty immersed based loop of length≤2k avoiding the deleted edges. Insert such loops at both ends of a geodesic return path of length≤dL. The joins do not reverse; the original segment stays unchanged. Added length≤dL+4k≤D0L.

Interval organization: thicken disks and pairing arcs to a regular neighborhood. For each complementary region Q, let ℓ_Q be its band-side/gap count. Euler gives Σ(ℓ_Q−2χ(Q))=2N0−4, also for disconnected neighborhoods and regions with multiple boundary components. A disk monogon would create consecutive inverse letters; immersion excludes it except at the one break. A same-band disk digon uses degree-one boundary gaps, at most 2N0 such gaps. Other disk regions have ℓ≤3(ℓ−2), and nondisk planar regions have ℓ≤ℓ−2χ. Thus at most 8N0 gaps fail to be good distinct-band digons. Cut them, indexing gaps, and designated piece cuts, and also cut opposite good gaps. There are at most 12N′ cuts. Every resulting linear run maps in reverse order to a whole partner run. A self-paired run would fix a middle occurrence or create adjacent inverse middle letters; both are excluded. Hence at most 6N′ interval pairs suffice.

Choose U large compared with D0/ε, then η small compared with ε/U, then K0 large compared with η⁻², and only afterward C=K0(U+D0), I≥192C. Split paths longer than UL into segments with original lengths between UL/3 and UL, close them, and leave closures unpaired. Closure total≤(3D0/U)H0. Every piece has at least L original occurrences except possibly the one intact exceptional interval; hence N′L≤H0+L≤2H0.

Splitting a collapsed disk vertex into vertices for consecutive boundary blocks preserves planarity by separate sectors. Apply the recursive Lipton–Tarjan separator: remove at most C_sep N′/sqrt K0≤ηN′ pieces. The deleted original length is at most 2ηUH0. Each deleted occurrence destroys at most one partner in surviving pieces. Surviving components have ≤K0 paths. Their total length≥15H0/16, total unpaired length≤εH0/16, total interval count≤12H0/L. Discard components failing the relative unpaired threshold or having >192H_Z/L intervals; each discarding costs at most H0/16. A remaining component shorter than L can consist only of the one intact exceptional interval. Because an ordinary boundary was also present, its length is less than H0/2. ## Cone pictures, root protection, and torsion

An abstract cone can be replaced relative to its base graph by the graph with disks attached to a free basis of its fundamental group. This replacement is contractible: it is simply connected and its two-cell boundary map is an isomorphism onto the integral cycle group. Both replacements are contractible CW complexes containing the same graph; extending cells and cylinder homotopies gives equivalences relative to that graph. After gluing to the rose they give an equivalence relative to the rose with an ordinary finite presentation complex. Simplicial approximation and shrinking/expanding interiors of relator disks produce cone pictures for any sphere map or any rooted null-homotopy, relative to a prescribed boundary. This construction uses actual abstract cones; it does not assume their images are embedded.

For an essential sphere choose a cone picture minimizing total inner path length. For a collapsed root-to-fixed-distinct-vertex path choose a null-homotopy picture minimizing both inner and outer lengths. Tighten inner paths cyclically and the outer path linearly with graph endpoints fixed, and remove constant inner disks. Every ordinary boundary is positive cyclically immersed; the rooted outer interval stays positive because its graph endpoints differ. There is at least one inner disk: otherwise an essential sphere would factor through a rose, or a nonempty freely reduced word would bound in a rose.

A transverse inverse-image arc in the complementary rose map pairs inverse letters. If the letters traverse the same actual graph edge e, a thin band lifts to that edge and agrees with both boundary lifts. The incident cone disks therefore belong to the same graph component, enabling four length-decreasing surgeries:

1. Two different inner disks: (eP,e⁻¹Q) becomes one disk PQ through their common abstract cone; this is homotopic relative to the rest and removes two occurrences.
2. Inner disk and rooted exterior disk: (PeR,e⁻¹Q) becomes exterior path PQR; restrict the old map to the complementary disk. Graph endpoints are unchanged.
3. Two exterior ends: PeQe⁻¹R becomes PR; retain the complementary disk with the marked break. Q is a graph loop at the endpoint of e, but no independent filling of Q is assumed or needed.
4. Two ends on one inner disk: ePe⁻¹Q splits into caps P and Q. In a rooted picture retain the component containing the exterior break. In an essential sphere, lift to the universal cover and fill both caps through the *same lift* of the abstract cone as the old annulus. The old sphere's H₂ class is the sum of the two capped classes, since their difference factors through a contractible cone. Hurewicz identifies π₂ and H₂ in this simply connected cover, so at least one capped sphere remains essential with smaller length.

Further tightening cannot increase length. All four operations contradict the minimized failure, so paired positions must use distinct graph edges. The minimized failure therefore produces precisely a reduced spherical arrangement, excluded above. Thus π₂=0 and root protection follow without an unsupported injectivity or cancellation assertion.

A two-dimensional universal cover with π₁=π₂=0 is acyclic by cellular homology and Hurewicz. Its first hypothetical nonzero higher homotopy group would supply nonzero homology, so all homotopy vanishes and Whitehead gives contractibility. Finite X gives a finite presentation. Its length-two free ZG cellular resolution, restricted to a prime-order cyclic subgroup C_ℓ if torsion existed, would force H^k(C_ℓ;F_ℓ)=0 for k>2. The explicit periodic resolution alternating u−1 and 1+u+⋯+u^{ℓ−1} has zero cochain differentials with trivial F_ℓ coefficients and gives nonzero H^k in every degree. Contradiction. This establishes torsion freedom as a consequence of the verified asphericity mechanism.
