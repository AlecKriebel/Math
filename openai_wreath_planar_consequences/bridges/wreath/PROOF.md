# The conditional wreath-product bridge

Status: the algebraic implication below is proved independently. It does **not**
establish the existence of its group-ring premise. In particular, this document
does not validate OpenAI family 197.

## Exact statement

Let G be a finitely generated group. Suppose a,b are elements of the ordinary
group algebra F_2[G] satisfying ab=1 and ba!=1. Then there is a finitely
generated Hopfian group H such that the standard restricted regular wreath
product C_2 wr H is finitely generated and non-Hopfian. Its specified
noninjective epimorphism fixes the acting group pointwise and has a nontrivial
elementary abelian kernel contained in the restricted base.

The overgroup assertion used here is the Miller--Schupp embedding theorem
(J. Algebra 17 (1971), 171--176, DOI 10.1016/0021-8693(71)90028-7), as used
explicitly by Bradford--Fournier-Facio in the proof of Theorem 4.11. An
independent published alternative is Bridson--Short, Theorem A (2024), DOI
10.1017/S0004972723001442. Verification limits are in EMBEDDING_AUDIT.md.
Neither theorem assumes that G is Hopfian.

## Proof

Choose an injective homomorphism i:G->H with H finitely generated and Hopfian.
The induced map on group algebras sends the basis symbol [g] to [i(g)],
preserves addition, multiplication and the unit, and is injective: distinct
elements of G have distinct images in H, so no new linear cancellation occurs.
Hence the images, still denoted a,b, satisfy ab=1 and ba!=1 in R=F_2[H].

Put A=C_2, identified with the additive group of F_2. The entire restricted
base A^(H), rather than the single lamp factor A, is identified with the
additive group of R by

    f <-> sum_{h in H} f(h)[h].

The sum is finite. Under this identification the regular left action
(h.f)(x)=f(h^{-1}x) becomes left multiplication by [h]. Thus the standard
restricted regular wreath product is W=R_add semidirect H with multiplication

    (r,h)(s,k)=(r+[h]s,hk).

Define Phi(r,h)=(rb,h). If U,V are finite supports then the support of their
group-ring product is contained in UV={uv:u in U,v in V}. Consequently rb
and ra have finite support for every r in R; neither operation leaves the
restricted base. Right multiplication by b is additive and commutes with the
left action because ([h]s)b=[h](sb). Therefore

    Phi((r,h)(s,k))=((r+[h]s)b,hk)
                     =(rb+[h](sb),hk)
                     =Phi(r,h)Phi(s,k).

For every (r,h), Phi(ra,h)=((ra)b,h)=(r(ab),h)=(r,h). Thus Phi is onto. Put
e=1-ba. It is nonzero by the premise and

    eb=b-bab=b-b(ab)=0.

The element (e,1_H) is nonidentity and lies in the kernel, proving that W is
non-Hopfian.

The factor A is generated by its nonidentity element and is Hopfian because
it is finite. The factor H is finitely generated and Hopfian by the stated
embedding input. If S is a finite generating set for H, then W is generated
by {(0,s):s in S} together with (1_R,1_H): conjugating this lamp by (0,h)
gives ([h],1_H), and all finite-support lamps are finite sums of such basis
lamps. This proves finite generation of W and completes the implication.

## Exact kernel and a split epimorphism

The same proof gives the additional checkable description

    e^2=e,   ker Phi=Re times {1_H}.

Indeed (ba)^2=b(ab)a=ba, so (1-ba)^2=1-ba over any unital ring. The equation
eb=0 gives Re <= ker(r->rb). Conversely rb=0 implies re=r-rba=r, so every
kernel element belongs to Re. The section Sigma(r,h)=(ra,h) is a group
homomorphism for the same left-action reason, and Phi Sigma=id. Thus

    R_add = Ra direct-sum Re

as left R-modules: r=rba+re, and if xa lies in the kernel then
(xa)b=x(ab)=x=0. In characteristic two every nonzero kernel lamp has order
two. These are standard consequences of a one-sided inverse, not a claimed
new group-ring counterexample or an independent historical breakthrough.

## Boundary and orientation checks

- Using left multiplication by b would not generally commute with the left
  H action. The stated map uses **right** multiplication.
- The preimage uses ra because ab=1. Reversing this order uses the false
  relation ba=1.
- The kernel witness is 1-ba, not 1-ab; the latter is zero.
- H must be infinite under the premise. If H were finite then F_2[H] would
  be a finite ring: right multiplication by b has right inverse as a map,
  hence is bijective, forcing ba=1. No finite-H exception is hidden.
- Neither H nor W is claimed finitely presented by this implication.
- H need not be torsion-free, residually finite, simple, or complete. Extra
  properties of available embedding theorems are unused.
- A is C_2. The additive group of R is the base, generally not a finitely
  generated abstract lamp factor.
- This is the regular action on H itself. Replacing it by a nonfree coset
  action changes the module and is not this result.
- The argument proves no result for a prescribed nonabelian free lamp group.

## Attribution and logical gap

Bradford--Fournier-Facio, Theorem 1.3/4.11, already proves the equivalence
between the universal Hopficity assertion for finitely generated abelian
lamps and Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture. Its Theorem 1.5 specializes
at A=C_2 to: C_2 wr H is Hopfian iff H is Hopfian and F_2[H] is directly
finite. The construction above explicitly proves the counterexample
direction in this special case without depending on their reductions for
arbitrary epimorphisms.

The conditional implication is already present in that literature and in
the existing PR389 turn-4 packet. It is new neither in mechanism nor as a
conditional consequence. Establishing the requested unconditional answer
still requires a validated actual pair a,b in an actual group algebra with
ab=1 and ba!=1. No finite symbolic computation in this folder supplies it.
