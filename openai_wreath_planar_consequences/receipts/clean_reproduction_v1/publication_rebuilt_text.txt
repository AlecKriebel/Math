                                           Abstract
         We derive two separately motivated consequences of precisely identiﬁed input theo-
     rems in OpenAI’s mathematics collection. The torsion-free group-algebra counterexam-
     ple, combined with the established Hopﬁan embedding framework, yields a ﬁnitely gener-
     ated Hopﬁan group H for which the standard restricted regular wreath product C2 wr H
     is non-Hopﬁan. We give its split epimorphism and kernel explicitly. The sharp planar
     two-point Fourier certiﬁcate, combined with the Cohn–de Laat–Salmon
                                                                       √ product-program
     comparison and triangular-lattice Poisson bounds, yields L2 = 2/ 3 and the exact pla-
     nar triangle inﬁmum P2 = 4/3. We reconstruct the deductions, audit the substantive
     inputs, repair product-proof bookkeeping, and provide independently implemented vali-
     dated computations and source records. These consequences use credited literature; no
     ﬁrst-priority or independent-breakthrough claim is made. The planar equality does not
     settle the all-dimensional triangle problem or assert attainment of its inﬁmum.


1    Provenance, contribution, and veriﬁcation status
Problem 21.22 of the twenty-ﬁrst Kourovka Notebook asks whether the standard restricted
wreath product of two ﬁnitely generated Hopﬁan groups is Hopﬁan [1]. The same existence
question is Question 1.14 of the published Bradford–Fournier-Facio paper [4]. Our repos-
itory’s draft PR 389, problem 2531, retains this question as unsolved and records partial
results [2]. The acting group in the question acts regularly on itself, and the base consists of
ﬁnite-support lamps. A counterexample for another permutational action does not answer
this question.
     Problem 1.32 in de Laat’s AIM list [9] concerns a triangle Fourier program and its com-
parison with the Cohn–Elkies two-point program, including questions about symmetry and
conversion of optimizers. Draft PR 487, problem 20001754, retains the general question
as unsolved [3]. Our second target concerns only dimension two and the three lengths
|x|, |y|, |x − y|. It adds no condition on |x + y| and makes no all-dimensional claim.
     The wreath reduction is already part of Bradford–Fournier-Facio’s equivalence framework
[4]; the product-program comparison is due to Cohn–de Laat–Salmon [10]; the lattice argu-
ments are standard Poisson arguments. We do not claim these deductions as independent
discoveries. The substantive central inputs are OpenAI’s Theorem 1.1 in [7] and Theorem 1.1
in [8]. All OpenAI sources used here are pinned to commit adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab
7e4b4c146bf78a. Manuscript title dates are not treated as public-priority dates.
     The accompanying ledgers distinguish written proof coverage from ﬁnite computations
and formal artifacts. Two independent source audits reconstructed the central manuscript

2    Part I: a non-Hopﬁan restricted regular wreath product
A group is Hopﬁan if every surjective endomorphism is injective. For groups A, H, their
standard restricted regular wreath product is

                      A wr H = A(H) ⋊ H,          (h · f )(x) = f (h−1 x),

where A(H) denotes ﬁnite-support functions with pointwise multiplication.
Theorem 1 (Conditional wreath consequence). Suppose a ﬁnitely generated group G has
elements a, b ∈ F2 [G] with ab = 1 and ba 6= 1. There is a ﬁnitely generated Hopﬁan
group H such that C2 wr H is ﬁnitely generated and non-Hopﬁan. Its speciﬁed noninjective
epimorphism ﬁxes H pointwise and splits.
    The needed established embedding input is: every ﬁnitely generated group embeds in
a ﬁnitely generated Hopﬁan group. Bradford–Fournier-Facio use Miller–Schupp [5] in their
proof of Theorem 4.11. The stronger countable-group statement also follows from the acces-
sible independent construction of Bridson–Short, Theorem A [6]. Our source audit records
that the original Miller–Schupp proof was not retrievable, and checks the accessible alter-
native’s embedding and epimorphism-rigidity mechanism. Neither embedding statement
assumes G itself is Hopﬁan.

Proof. Choose an injective homomorphism G ,→ H with H ﬁnitely generated and Hop-
ﬁan. Its induced group-algebra homomorphism is injective: distinct group elements remain
distinct basis elements. Thus the same equations hold in R = F2 [H].

    Bradford–Fournier-Facio’s Theorem 1.3/4.11 equates the universal abelian-lamp Hop-

criterion that C2 wr H is Hopﬁan exactly when H is Hopﬁan and F2 [H] is directly ﬁnite.
The proof above veriﬁes the scalar counterexample direction directly; it does not need their
classiﬁcation of arbitrary epimorphisms.
    Theorem 1.1 of [7] supplies a ﬁnitely presented torsion-free G and a, b, c ∈ F2 [G] with
ab = 1, ac = 0 and c 6= 0. If ba = 1, then c = b(ac) = 0, a contradiction; hence it
supplies the hypothesis of Theorem 1. Its manuscript-level chain consists of a parity criterion,
random high-girth immersed graphs, a multiplicity-stage bounded-pattern estimate, planar
extraction, and cone-picture surgeries protecting a nonzero coeﬃcient. The supporting audit
reconstructs each of these steps, including the uniform stage estimates and essentiality-
preserving cone surgeries. Root protection proves that the identity coeﬃcient of c is one;
it does not assert pairwise distinct images of every graph vertex. Exact ﬁnite arithmetic
veriﬁes the weighted-word contraction. No particular matching outcome is enumerated:
the retained construction uses a proved probabilistic existence argument. The fresh source
adversary found no substantive defect in this proof chain. The related Lean declaration
inspected in the collection instead concerns a ﬁnite characteristic-two coeﬃcient ﬁeld and a
group with odd-prime torsion. It is not formal veriﬁcation of this torsion-free F2 input, and
no proof-assistant veriﬁcation is claimed here.

Theorem 2 (Negative wreath-product answer). There exist ﬁnitely generated Hopﬁan
groups A, H whose standard restricted regular wreath product A wr H is not Hopﬁan. One
may take A = C2 , and the noninjective epimorphism in Theorem 1 splits with kernel
(R(1 − ba)) × {1H }.

Proof. Apply Theorem 1 to the ﬁnitely presented, hence ﬁnitely generated, group supplied
by OpenAI’s credited input theorem. The preceding argument supplies its one-sided inverse
defect. All claimed factor and wreath ﬁniteness properties were veriﬁed in that theorem.

5    Attribution, scope, and reproducibility
Theorems 2 and 4 are consequences of the explicitly credited external inputs and the dis-
played deductions. The independent checks and audits have the exact scopes described
above; neither target is claimed as formally veriﬁed. The two parts do not settle other
wreath actions, a prescribed nonabelian free lamp, all-dimensional equality, characterization
of triangle optimizers, or a universal conversion theorem.
    An institutional 2026 thesis abstract by Daria Zhitniaia [12] announces a planar magic-
function representation. Its public-posting date is undetermined, and the full thesis was
unavailable in the inspected institutional record; the abstract alone does not permit a com-
parison of complete sign, regularity, and normalization claims. An April 2026 preprint of
Zhou [14] already asserts planar linear-programming sharpness in its introduction, but does
not provide a planar certiﬁcate in the inspected proof. These records limit any priority con-
clusion. No outreach was undertaken. Our short deductions and standard lattice arguments
are derivative, and the veriﬁcation package must be assessed on its actual checkable coverage.
    The editable source, bibliography, dependency and theorem ledgers, exact source ver-
sions, independent code, computational receipts, review records and reproduction instruc-
tions are maintained together. Third-party manuscripts and runtime libraries are local audit
inputs, excluded from the intended distributable package. The package manifest binds each
distributed ﬁle to a SHA-256 identity. Reproduction checks all decisive ﬁnite gates with as-
sertions enabled, rebuilds the PDF, and compares the extracted text on every page; binary
PDF identity may diﬀer through build metadata. Computational receipts do not certify the
probabilistic/topological proof, which must be checked from the written source audit and its
pinned input.
