# Positive-circuit packing from provisional hemisphere depth

## Conditional theorem

Assume the exact one-sided bound
\[
B(5)\leq34                                                   \tag{1}
\]
has been independently certified.  Then every hypothetical 41-point
kissing code in \(S^4\) contains two disjoint inclusion-minimal positive
circuits, each having between two and six points.

This note treats (1) as a conditional input.  It does not audit the cap
certificate proving (1).

## From the cap bound to deletion-interiority

Let \(C\subset S^4\) have 41 points.  For every nonzero \(u\), the closed
hemisphere
\[
\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\leq0\}
\]
contains at most 34 code points by (1), applied with axis \(-u/\|u\|\).
Therefore its complementary open hemisphere contains at least seven:
\[
\#\{x\in C:\langle u,x\rangle>0\}\geq7.                \tag{2}
\]
Replacing \(u\) by \(-u\) gives seven points on the other open side as
well.

After deleting any \(D\subset C\) with \(|D|\leq6\), both open sides of
every hyperplane through the origin remain nonempty.  Hence
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}(C\setminus D).       \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if (3) failed, the separating/supporting-hyperplane theorem would
give a nonzero \(u\) with all remaining points in
\(\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\geq0\}\), leaving the opposite open side empty.
No spanning, genericity, or strict-position assumption is used.

## Two disjoint minimal circuits

Choose an inclusion-minimal \(A\subset C\) with
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}A.
\]
Carathéodory's theorem gives \(|A|\leq6\), and unit vectors exclude
\(|A|=1\).  Minimality gives coefficients
\[
\alpha_a>0,\qquad \sum_{a\in A}\alpha_a=1,\qquad
\sum_{a\in A}\alpha_a a=0.                             \tag{4}
\]
Moreover the vector family \(A\) has rank \(|A|-1\).  If its kernel had
dimension at least two, one could perturb the strictly positive kernel
vector in an independent kernel direction until one coefficient first
became zero, producing a smaller nonnegative dependence and contradicting
minimality.

Delete \(A\).  Since \(|A|\leq6\), equation (3) applies to \(C\setminus A\).
Choose an inclusion-minimal \(B\subset C\setminus A\) with zero in its
convex hull.  The same argument gives
\[
2\leq|B|\leq6
\]
and strictly positive coefficients \(\beta_b\).  Thus \(A,B\) are disjoint
positive circuits.

If \(X\) is the coordinate matrix and \(G=X^{\mathsf T}X\), extending
\(\alpha,\beta\) by zero outside their supports gives two disjointly
supported nonnegative vectors in \(\ker X=\ker G\).

## Universal cross-block constraints

Write \(m=|A|\), \(n=|B|\), and
\[
H=(\langle a,b\rangle)_{a\in A,b\in B}.
\]
Every such pair obeys
\[
H\beta=0,\qquad \alpha^{\mathsf T}H=0,\qquad
H_{ab}\leq\frac12.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Each principal circuit Gram matrix has rank \(m-1\) or \(n-1\), a
one-dimensional kernel generated by its positive weight vector, diagonal
one, and off-diagonal entries at most \(1/2\).  If \(U,V\) are the two
linear spans, then
\[
\dim(U\cap V)
=(m-1)+(n-1)-\dim(U+V)
\geq\max(0,m+n-7).                                    \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6), full block PSD, and total rank at most five are the
basic classification-free restrictions.  A nonzero row or column of \(H\)
must contain both signs because its positive weighted mean is zero.

The exact common-neighbor capacity bounds and the provisional positive and
negative degree bounds can impose further restrictions when particular
entries of \(H\) cross their thresholds.  They cannot exclude a
circuit-size pair universally, as the following exact catalog shows.

## Exact obstruction to a size-pair classification

The normalized \(D_5\) root code has 40 points and rank five.  The
certificate `positive_circuit_pair_catalog.json` gives, for every
\[
2\leq m\leq n\leq6,                                   \tag{7}
\]
two disjoint subsets of sizes \(m,n\).  The standard-library verifier
reconstructs the roots, computes each kernel by exact rational row
reduction, and proves:

- each subset has rank one less than its cardinality;
- its unique kernel vector is strictly positive, hence it is
  inclusion-minimal;
- the two supports are disjoint;
- the cross block satisfies (5);
- the union has rank at most five and satisfies (6); and
- every inner product between distinct roots is at most \(1/2\).

Thus all 15 unordered size pairs in (7) occur in genuine
five-dimensional kissing geometry.  In particular, one must not assume
full circuit size six, antipodality, genericity, or transverse circuit
spans.

This is not merely an artifact of a shallow origin.  The 20 antipodal
root lines of \(D_5\) meet every open origin hemisphere in at least eight
points.  To see this, write a nonzero normal as
\(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_5)\).  A root line on coordinates \(i,j\) is orthogonal
when \(u_i=\pm u_j\).  If \(z\) coordinates vanish and the nonzero
coordinates are partitioned into equal-absolute-value groups of sizes
\(k_1,k_2,\ldots\), the number of orthogonal root lines is
\[
2\binom z2+\sum_r\binom{k_r}{2}.                       \tag{8}
\]
Over all \(z\leq4\) and all partitions of \(5-z\), (8) is at most 12,
attained at \(z=4\).  Hence at least \(20-12=8\) antipodal lines are
nonorthogonal, and each contributes exactly one point to either open
hemisphere.  The \(D_5\) code therefore retains the origin in the interior
after any seven deletions, stronger than the conditional six-deletion
property above.

## Rank-six near-counterexample at cardinality 41

There is also an exact 41-point warning that misses the target conditions
by only one rank.  Take the first 41 normalized roots of \(D_6\) in the
deterministic ordering recorded in the certificate.  Their Gram matrix has
\[
G\succeq0,\qquad G_{ii}=1,\qquad G_{ij}\leq\frac12,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}G=6,
\]
and contains two disjoint antipodal positive circuits.  The verifier checks
all statements exactly.

Consequently, the existence of two disjoint positive kernel vectors, their
support sizes, positivity, cross annihilation (5), and the kissing entry
bound do not by themselves distinguish a hypothetical 41-code.  Any
successful oriented-matroid argument must exploit genuinely global
rank-five compatibility beyond these two circuits.  The \(D_5\) catalog
rules out a finite case split based only on \((m,n)\), while the \(D_6\)
example shows how sharply the total-rank condition must enter.
