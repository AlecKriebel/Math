# An exact quarter-grid obstruction for 14--16 antipodal pairs

## Theorem

There is no 41-point spherical code in \(S^4\) whose off-diagonal inner
products all belong to

\[
\{-1,-3/4,-1/2,-1/4,0,1/4,1/2\}
\tag{1}
\]

and which has exactly \(r\) antipodal pairs for any
\(r\in\{14,15,16\}\).

This is a finite-support theorem.  It does not assert that an arbitrary
41-point code can be discretized to (1).

## Antipodal pair decomposition

Fix the branch with exactly \(r\) antipodal pairs and put
\(n=41-2r\).  The \(n\) points outside those pairs form the *core*.  No
two core points are antipodal, so their possible inner-product numerators
are

\[
m\in\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2\}.
\]

Let \(c_m\) count unordered core pairs at inner product \(m/4\).  Then

\[
\sum_m c_m=C:={n\choose2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Every unordered pair involving a paired point, other than the \(r\)
antipodal edges themselves, occurs together with its sign-reversed mate.
Moreover, its absolute inner product is at most \(1/2\): if
\(\langle x,y\rangle<-1/2\), then
\(\langle-x,y\rangle>1/2\).  Let \(b_2,b_1\) be the number per sign at
\(\pm1/2,\pm1/4\), and let \(b_0\) count the zero pairs.  Hence

\[
2b_2+2b_1+b_0=B:=820-r-C.
\tag{3}
\]

All nine variables \(c_{-3},\ldots,c_2,b_2,b_1,b_0\) are nonnegative.

Define the integer squared-inner-product numerator

\[
Q=\sum_{\{x,y\}}(4\langle x,y\rangle)^2.
\]

The antipodal edges contribute \(16r\), while the remaining contribution is

\[
R:=\sum_{m=-3}^{2}m^2c_m+8b_2+2b_1,
\qquad Q=16r+R.
\tag{4}
\]

## Three positive harmonic moments

Let \(P_k\) be the normalized dimension-five Gegenbauer polynomial,
\(P_k(1)=1\), and put

\[
M_k=\sum_{x,y}P_k(\langle x,y\rangle).
\]

The addition theorem gives \(M_k\ge0\).  Sign-reversed background pairs
cancel in odd degree.  Since \(P_k(-1)=-1\) for odd \(k\),

\[
M_k=n+2\sum_{m=-3}^{2}c_mP_k(m/4)
\quad(k=1,3).
\tag{5}
\]

In degree four the diagonal and antipodal edges contribute \(41+2r\):

\[
\begin{split}
M_4={}&41+2r+2\sum_{m=-3}^{2}c_mP_4(m/4)\\
&+4b_2P_4(1/2)+4b_1P_4(1/4)+2b_0P_4(0).
\end{split}
\tag{6}
\]

The needed values, generated by the exact recurrence

\[
P_k(t)=\frac{(2k+1)tP_{k-1}(t)-(k-1)P_{k-2}(t)}{k+2},
\]

are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
t&-1&-3/4&-1/2&-1/4&0&1/4&1/2\\ \hline
P_1&-1&-3/4&-1/2&-1/4&0&1/4&1/2\\
P_3&-1&-45/256&5/32&41/256&0&-41/256&-5/32\\
P_4&1&-59/2048&-19/128&53/2048&1/8&53/2048&-19/128.
\end{array}
\]

## Exact Farkas identity

Direct rational expansion gives

\[
\begin{split}
&363M_1+960M_3+1024M_4
-319\left(\sum_m c_m-C\right)\\
&\qquad
-256(2b_2+2b_1+b_0-B)+140R\\
&=K(r)-126c_{-2}-63c_0-252c_1-126b_1,
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]

where

\[
K(r)=1323n+1024(41+2r)+319C+256B.
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (2)--(3), nonnegativity of the count variables, and
\(M_1,M_3,M_4\ge0\) therefore imply

\[
140R\le K(r),\qquad
Q\le16r+\frac{K(r)}{140}.
\tag{9}
\]

No numerical solver is used in (7); the verifier reconstructs every
coefficient over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Rank contradiction

Let \(G\) be the \(41\times41\) Gram matrix.  Since
\(G\succeq0\), \(\operatorname{rank}G\le5\), and
\(\operatorname{tr}G=41\),

\[
\operatorname{tr}(G^2)\ge\frac{41^2}{5}.
\]

On the quarter grid,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(G^2)=41+\frac Q8,
\]

so

\[
Q\ge\frac{11808}{5}.
\tag{10}
\]

The upper bounds from (9) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&Q_{\rm upper}&11808/5-Q_{\rm upper}\\ \hline
14&66093/28&159/140\\
15&165201/70&111/70\\
16&330591/140&33/140.
\end{array}
\]

Every gap is strictly positive, contradicting (10).  This proves the
theorem.

## Boundary and scope audit

- Inner product \(1/2\) is included throughout.
- A background pair cannot have absolute inner product greater than
  \(1/2\), because its sign-reversed mate is also present.
- “Exactly \(r\) antipodal pairs” ensures that the core contains no
  inner product \(-1\).
- Rank below five is covered by the trace Cauchy inequality.
- Counts are allowed to be arbitrary nonnegative reals in (7), so the
  contradiction does not rely on a hidden integrality rounding.
- The quarter-grid assumption remains essential.  No discretization
  theorem is claimed.
