# Research log

## 2026-07-23

- Imported the exact universal one-sided bound \(B(5)\leq34\).  Hence any
  hypothetical 41-code has at least seven points in each open origin
  hemisphere and remains origin-interior after deleting any six points.
- Derived existence of a full-support positive barycentric vector and an
  exact formula for the best possible minimum coordinate in terms of the
  radial reach of the convex hull opposite the unweighted centroid.
- Combined two disjoint positive circuits with deletion-interiority to
  obtain a universal full-support choice with the sharp strict control
  \(p_i<1/4\).  The only coordinates initially at \(1/4\) come from
  antipodal two-circuits; deleting those at most four points lets a second
  dependence perturb every coordinate into the open interval.
- Derived weighted Gram, covariance, Lorentzian, reversible-Markov, and
  entrywise-quadratic identities that require neither ordinary centering nor
  tightness.
- Constructed exact \(D_5\) counterexamples showing that an arbitrary
  full-support centering vector can have minimum coordinate tending to zero,
  maximum coordinate tending to \(1/2\), and covariance minimum eigenvalue
  tending to zero, despite open-hemisphere depth eight.
- Strengthened the reversible-chain analysis.  The rank-six perturbation
  \(S-A\) has exact inertia \((1,5)\), and entrywise nonnegativity together
  with \(\operatorname{tr}S^3\geq0\) forces the sum of all non-Perron
  positive eigenvalues of \(S\) to be strictly less than two.  Available
  interlacing lower bounds are far too small to contradict this.
- Constructed an exact 41-point unit-vector family with open-hemisphere
  depth at least sixteen and origin-interiority surviving any six
  deletions, but whose best minimum barycentric weight tends to zero.  The
  family is not a kissing code, so it refutes a deletion-depth-only
  quantitative claim while leaving code-sensitive selection claims open.
- Identified the current bottleneck: sign-count and cap-capacity information
  is unweighted, while no explicit universal lower bound is known for the
  mass assigned by the selected barycentric vector to those neighborhoods.
