\subsection{Scalar estimates for the analytic bound}

For the alternative bound \(U\) in \cref{sec:separation}, consider
a pair involving auxiliary label \(2\). Let
\[
K_{\bm n}=\frac35D \Pi(\bm n)D,\qquad
K_{\bm m}=\frac45D \Pi(\bm m)D,
\]
where \(x=n_z\), \(y=m_z\), \(r=\bm n\cdot\bm m\), and
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\sqrt{\frac{1+\eta}{2}},\sqrt{\frac{1-\eta}{2}}\right).
\]
Direct expansion, using \(\|\bm n\|=\|\bm m\|=1\), gives
\[
\det(K_{\bm n}-K_{\bm m})
=-\frac3{50}(1-\eta^2)(1-r)\le0.
\]
Thus \(K_{\bm n}-K_{\bm m}\) has eigenvalues of opposite signs, including a
possible zero boundary eigenvalue, and the optimized complementary-projector
score is
\[
F_\eta=
\frac{\Tr K_{\bm n}+\Tr K_{\bm m}
      +\|K_{\bm n}-K_{\bm m}\|_1}{2}.
\]
Its discriminant yields
\[
20F_\eta=
7+\eta(3x+4y)+
\sqrt{[-1+\eta(3x-4y)]^2+24(1-\eta^2)(1-r)}.
\]

At \(\eta=0\),
\[
F_0(r)=\frac7{20}+\frac1{20}\sqrt{25-24r}.
\]
Set
\[
q=\sqrt{25-24r}\ge1,\qquad H=8-3x-4y\ge1.
\]
To prove \(F_\eta\le F_0(r)+2\eta/5\), it is enough to prove
\[
\sqrt{[-1+\eta(3x-4y)]^2+24(1-\eta^2)(1-r)}
\le q+\eta H.
\]
The right side is at least one, so squaring is valid. Using
\(q^2=25-24r\), the squared difference is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\eta\{&
\eta[8(1-y)(4-3x)+12(1-r)]\\
&+(q-1)(8-3x-4y)+8(1-y)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every factor is nonnegative on
\[
0\le\eta\le1,\qquad -1\le x,y,r\le1.
\]
No omitted compatibility condition among \(x,y,r\) can invalidate the
estimate because it holds on the larger box.

For \(u=|\bm b_0\cdot\bm b_1|\), both relevant pairs satisfy \(r\ge-u\).
Moreover,
\[
(5+8u)^2-(25+24u)=8u(7+8u)\ge0,
\]
so
\[
F_0(r)\le\frac7{20}+\frac1{20}\sqrt{25+24u}
\le\frac35+\frac{2u}{5}.
\]

For completeness, the scalar inequalities in the CHSH-deficit estimate have
nonnegative squared differences
\[
\left(1-\frac{u^2}{2}\right)^2-(1-u^2)=\frac{u^4}{4},
\]
\[
\left(2-\frac{u^2}{4}\right)^2-(4-u^2)=\frac{u^4}{16}.
\]
Their right sides are nonnegative on \(0\le u\le1\), so no sign reversal is
introduced by squaring.

\section{The strengthened algebraic strategy}
\label{app:strengthened}

\begin{proposition}[Strengthened attained lower bound]
\label{prop:strengthened-strategy}
There is a fixed-qubit POVM strategy attaining
\[
L_1=\frac{16+8\sqrt{7813}}{25}>U.
\]
Thus \(L_1\) is an attained POVM lower bound. No global POVM optimality is
asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
q=\sqrt{7813},\qquad \eta=\frac1q,\qquad
s=\sqrt{1-\eta^2}=\frac{6\sqrt{217}}q.
\]
Use
\[
|\psi_\eta\rangle=
\sqrt{\frac{1+\eta}{2}}|00\rangle+
\sqrt{\frac{1-\eta}{2}}|11\rangle,
\]
Bob observables \(B_0=X,B_1=Z\), and Alice observables
\[
A_0=\frac{6\sqrt{217}\,X+qZ}{125},\qquad
A_1=\frac{6\sqrt{217}\,X-qZ}{125}.
\]
They square to the identity because
\[
36\cdot217+7813=125^2.
\]
The CHSH value is \(S=250/q\).

Set
\[
k=\frac35\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{q+1}}
\]
and
\[
M_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}k^2&k\\k&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_1=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}k^2&-k\\-k&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_2=\begin{pmatrix}1-k^2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are positive rank-one effects and sum to the identity. The diagonal
dual operator
\[
\Gamma=
\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\frac25(1+\eta),\frac6{25}(1-\eta)\right)
\]
dominates the three weighted steered score operators \(K_j\) from
\cref{sec:separation}. Indeed, for \(v_\pm=(1,\pm k)^T\) the slacks are
\[
\Gamma-K_0=\frac{1+\eta}{4}v_-v_-^T,\qquad
\Gamma-K_1=\frac{1+\eta}{4}v_+v_+^T,\qquad
\Gamma-K_2=\frac6{25}(1-\eta)|1\rangle\langle1|.
\]
Each slack annihilates the range of its matching effect \(M_j\).
Consequently \(T=\sum_j\Tr(K_jM_j)=\sum_j\Tr(\Gamma M_j)\), giving
\[
T=\Tr\Gamma=\frac{16+4\eta}{25},
\qquad
10S+T=\frac{16+8q}{25}.
\]

For the strict comparison with \(U\),
\[
(6+80\sqrt{7813})^2-2\cdot5003^2
=-56782+960\sqrt{7813},
\]
and
\[
960^2\cdot7813-56782^2=3\,976\,265\,276>0.
\]
Both sides being compared are positive, these two exact square comparisons
prove \(L_1>U\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the strengthening]
The attained value in \cref{prop:strengthened-strategy} is a formal endpoint.
The particular dual-slack calculation above and the differentiation and
uniqueness argument below are retained manuscript calculations.
Vary \(\eta\in[0,1]\) in the construction above, keeping Bob's observables,
taking \(k(\eta)=\frac35\sqrt{(1-\eta)/(1+\eta)}\), and choosing Alice's
first two observables to maximize the CHSH value, which is then
\(2\sqrt{2-\eta^2}\). The dual-slack identities hold for every such
\(\eta\). Within this one-parameter family the Bell value
\[
f(\eta)=20\sqrt{2-\eta^2}+\frac{16+4\eta}{25}
\]
has
\[
f'(\eta)=-\frac{20\eta}{\sqrt{2-\eta^2}}+\frac4{25},\qquad
f''(\eta)=-\frac{40}{(2-\eta^2)^{3/2}}<0.
\]
Its unique critical point is \(\eta=1/\sqrt{7813}\). This proves optimality
only within that one-parameter family and is not a global POVM optimality
claim.
\end{remark}

\section{Degenerate cases in the one-binary-party theorem}
\label{app:binary-degeneracies}

If \(B_0,B_1\) are nondegenerate and have linearly independent Bloch axes,
\(\Phi_B\) maps the positive cone onto a three-dimensional Lorentz cone.
An extreme ray of the image lies on the boundary ellipse. Its minimum-norm
Bloch lift in the plane of the two axes has norm one; a perpendicular
component would exceed the Bloch ball. Thus the positive lift in
\(\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{\id,B_0,B_1\}\) is unique and rank one.

Parallel or antiparallel axes and scalar observables all give commuting
\(B_0,B_1\). Choose a common eigenbasis. Measuring in this basis gives
a hidden variable whose value fixes Bob's outcome for each input. Alice
uses her response distribution conditional on that value. Sampling a
response for each of Alice's two inputs gives a finite mixture of
deterministic behaviors, including the case of two scalar observables.
In the noncommuting case, a rank-one circuit sum \(\Omega\) forces its
nonzero positive summands to lie on the same ray and admits a product
purification. A circuit of zero weight can be omitted.

\section{Extremal qubit POVMs and the rank-square bound}
\label{app:extremal-povm}

Let \(M_a\) be the nonzero effects of a POVM. For each \(a\), let
\(\mathcal V_a\) be the real vector space of Hermitian operators supported on
\(\operatorname{ran}M_a\). It has dimension \(\rank(M_a)^2\).
Consider the summation map
\[
\bigoplus_a\mathcal V_a\longrightarrow\Herm(2),\qquad
(H_a)_a\longmapsto\sum_aH_a.
\]
If it has a nonzero kernel, then for sufficiently small \(\eps>0\),
\(M_a\pm\eps H_a\ge0\), and the two perturbed tuples are distinct POVMs whose
average is \(M\). Extremality therefore requires injectivity, giving
\[
\sum_a\rank(M_a)^2\le\dim_{\R}\Herm(2)=4.
\]
This necessary condition is all that is used in
\cref{thm:residual-reduction}. Together with linear independence in a
three-dimensional span, it leaves exactly three rank-one effects.

\section{Finite-dimensional POVM duality}
\label{app:duality}

At a fixed input the POVM optimization has a dual formulation.
The strict sign in the main proof follows from the deterministic
replacement identity \eqref{eq:deterministic-score-gap}.

\begin{lemma}[Finite POVM duality]
\label{lem:povm-duality}
Let \(K_j\) be Hermitian operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\),
indexed by a nonempty finite set. If a POVM \((N_j)_j\) attains
\[
V=\max_{\substack{N'_j\ge0\\\sum_jN'_j=\id}}\sum_j\Tr(K_jN'_j),
\]
there exists a Hermitian \(\Gamma\) such that \(\Tr\Gamma=V\),
\(\Gamma\ge K_j\), and \((\Gamma-K_j)N_j=0\) for every \(j\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weak duality says that every \(\Gamma\ge K_j\) obeys
\[
\sum_j\Tr(K_jN_j)\le\Tr\Gamma.
\]
For the reverse direction, consider the closed cone
\[
\mathcal C=
\left\{\left(\sum_jX_j,\sum_j\Tr(K_jX_j)-t\right):
X_j\ge0,\ t\ge0\right\}
\subset\Herm(\mathcal H)\times\R.
\]
Closedness follows by passing to a convergent subsequence of the
finitely many \(X_j\). Convergence of \(\sum_j X_j\) bounds each
\(0\le X_j\le\sum_kX_k\), and convergence of the second coordinate then
determines the limit of \(t\).

The point \((\id,V+\eps)\) is outside \(\mathcal C\). Nearest-point
separation provides a nonzero functional
\[
\ell(A,s)=\Tr(\Gamma_\eps A)+\gamma s
\]
nonnegative on \(\mathcal C\) and negative at that point. Since
\((0,-t)\in\mathcal C\), \(\gamma\le0\). If \(\gamma=0\), positivity on all
\((X,\Tr(K_jX))\) would give \(\Gamma_\eps\ge0\), contradicting
\(\Tr\Gamma_\eps<0\). Normalize \(\gamma=-1\). Taking one nonzero \(X_j=X\)
gives \(\Gamma_\eps\ge K_j\), while separation and weak duality give
\[
V\le\Tr\Gamma_\eps<V+\eps.
\]
Fix one index \(j_0\). Since
\(\Gamma_\eps-K_{j_0}\ge0\) and \(\Tr\Gamma_\eps<V+\eps\), the positive
matrix \(\Gamma_\eps-K_{j_0}\) has uniformly bounded trace and hence uniformly
bounded operator norm. Thus \(\Gamma_\eps\) is bounded. A convergent
subsequence as \(\eps\downarrow0\) yields dual attainment with
\(\Tr\Gamma=V\).

At a primal optimum,
\[
0=\Tr\Gamma-\sum_j\Tr(K_jN_j)
=\sum_j\Tr[(\Gamma-K_j)N_j].
\]
Each term is nonnegative, hence zero. For \(A,B\ge0\),
\(\Tr(AB)=0\) implies \(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}=0\), and therefore \(AB=0\).
This proves complementary slackness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Determinant pullback for one input]
\label{lem:determinant-pullback}
At a stationary residual strategy, fix one Bob input \(y\), write its effects
as \(N_{j|y}\), and let \(K_{j|y}\) be their Bell score operators. There is a
Hermitian normalization multiplier \(\widehat\Gamma_y\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effect-kkt}
K_{j|y}-\widehat\Gamma_y+
4|\det\Xi|^2\lambda_{j|y}\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y})=0
\end{equation}
for every outcome \(j\). Here, for
\(N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\[
\operatorname{adj}(N):=
\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}
\]
is the matrix adjugate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:lorentz-representation},
\[
F_j=(Pr_j)^Tg^{-1}(Pr_j)
=4|\det\Xi|^2r_j^Thr_j
=4|\det\Xi|^2\det N_{j|y}.
\]
For an arbitrary Hermitian variation \(\Delta N\),
\[
d(\det)_N[\Delta N]=\Tr(\operatorname{adj}(N)\Delta N),
\]
as follows by differentiating \(ad-bc\). Hold the state, Alice's
measurements, and Bob's other input fixed. The first variation of the Bell
objective is \(\sum_j\Tr(K_{j|y}\Delta N_{j|y})\). For variations
satisfying \(\sum_j\Delta N_{j|y}=0\), normalization contributes zero to
the pullback of \eqref{eq:lagrange}. It follows that
\[
\sum_j\Tr\!\left[
\left(K_{j|y}+4|\det\Xi|^2\lambda_{j|y}\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y})\right)
\Delta N_{j|y}\right]=0 .
\]
Vary any two outcomes by opposite arbitrary Hermitian matrices. The trace
pairing shows that the two coefficients coincide. Their common value is
the required \(\widehat\Gamma_y\).
\end{proof}

\section{Square completion for the incidence Hessian}
\label{app:square-completion}

This is the manuscript inverse-metric Hessian calculation. The formal
rank closure uses feasible curves and the exact score-gap identity instead
of a standalone theorem for this full Hessian or its inertia.

Let \(x_j=Pr_j\), \(Q=g^{-1}\), and vary
\[
P(t)=P+t\delta P,\qquad g(t)=g+tH
\]
to first order. The inverse expansion is
\[
(g+tH)^{-1}
=Q-tQHQ+t^2QHQHQ+O(t^3).
\]
Substitution in \(F_j=(P r_j)^TQ(P r_j)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12D^2F_j
={}&(\delta P r_j)^TQ(\delta P r_j)
-2(\delta P r_j)^TQHQ(Pr_j)\\
&+(Pr_j)^TQHQHQ(Pr_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing
\[
\delta P=PW+HQ P
\]
turns the right side into
\[
(PWr_j)^TQ(PWr_j).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_j\lambda_jD^2F_j
=2\sum_j\lambda_jr_j^TW^TP^TQPW r_j
=2\Tr(SW\Lambda W^T).
\]
This expansion also fixes the sign convention used at a Bell maximum.

\section{Projective fibers of the quadratic null-ray map}
\label{app:fibers}

Fix a strict metric \(g\) of the form \eqref{eq:metric}. We prove
\cref{prop:fiber} on its real projective null quadric. Write
\(R=[r_1\ \cdots\ r_5]\). Any four columns are independent because
the first four are the standard basis and \(r_5=(1,1,-1,-1)^T\)
has all coordinates nonzero.

Consider the space \(\mathcal V\) of real symmetric \(4\times4\)
matrices \(D\) such that \(r_i^TDr_i=0\) for all \(i\). Under the trace
pairing, \(\mathcal V^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{r_i r_i^T:1\le i\le5\}\).
The first four matrices span the diagonal and the fifth has nonzero
off-diagonal entries, so the five are independent. Hence \(\dim\mathcal V=5\).
A basis is \(g,H_a,H_b,H_c,H_d\), where \(H_p=\partial g/\partial p\).
Indeed, \(r_i^Tg r_i=0\) holds identically in the parameters, so all five
forms vanish on the frame. The four derivatives are independent by
inspection of the upper-right \(2\times2\) block of \eqref{eq:metric}.
The binary--binary off-diagonal entry is \(1/2\) in \(g\) and zero in
each derivative. In the order \(a,b,c,d\), the four values \(x^T H_p x\)
are precisely the entries of \(2\Phi(x)\).

For \(D=\sum_i c_i r_i r_i^T\), let \(S=\{i:c_i\ne0\}\) and \(s=|S|\).
If \(s\le4\), then \(D=R_S\operatorname{diag}(c_i)_{i\in S}R_S^T\)
has rank \(s\). The transpose \(R_S^T\) is surjective and \(R_S\) is
injective, regardless of the signs of the \(c_i\). If \(s=5\),
Sylvester's rank inequality gives \(\rank D\ge4+4-5=3\).

For a nonzero null vector \(x\) with \(\Phi(x)=0\), the rank-one matrix
\(xx^T\) is orthogonal to every basis element of \(\mathcal V\). The
rank bound forces \(s=1\), so its image is a frame ray's linear span.
The converse follows by evaluation. This proves the base-locus assertion.

Now suppose distinct null rays have representatives \(x,y\) with
\(\Phi(x)=t\Phi(y)\ne0\). Then \(t\ne0\) and
\(D=xx^T-t yy^T\) lies in \(\mathcal V^\perp\).
Since \(x,y\) are independent, \(D\) has rank two and image
\(\operatorname{span}\{x,y\}\). The same rank bound forces \(s=2\).
Thus the image is also \(\operatorname{span}\{r_i,r_j\}\) for some
\(i\ne j\). On this plane,
\[
 (\alpha r_i+\beta r_j)^Tg(\alpha r_i+\beta r_j)
 =2\alpha\beta\,r_i^Tg r_j .
\]
Strictness gives \(r_i^Tg r_j>0\). The only null directions of the
plane are therefore the two frame rays. Both have zero image under
\(\Phi\), a contradiction. This proves projective injectivity everywhere
outside the base locus.

\section{Bounded transportation and the rank-zero simulation}
\label{app:transport}

For three ternary labels, let \(a_i,b_i\ge0\), let
\(c_{ij}=c_{ji}\ge0\) with \(c_{ii}=0\), and assume
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}=a_i+b_i,\qquad
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=\frac12.
\]
Seek \(q_{ij}\) with row sums \(b_i\), column sums \(a_j\), and
\(0\le q_{ij}\le c_{ij}\). Put
\[
q_{ij}=\frac{c_{ij}}2+f_{ij},\qquad f_{ij}=-f_{ji},
\]
\[
d_i=\frac{b_i-a_i}{2},\qquad d_1+d_2+d_3=0.
\]
Set
\[
f_{12}=t,\qquad f_{13}=d_1-t,\qquad f_{23}=d_2+t.
\]
The row and column equations are automatic. Capacity is equivalent to
\[
t\in I_{12}\cap I_{13}\cap I_{23},
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{12}&=[-c_{12}/2,c_{12}/2],\\
I_{13}&=[d_1-c_{13}/2,d_1+c_{13}/2],\\
I_{23}&=[-d_2-c_{23}/2,-d_2+c_{23}/2].
\end{aligned}
\]
The inequalities
\[
|d_i|\le\frac{a_i+b_i}{2}
=\frac{c_{ij}+c_{ik}}2
\]
show that every pair of intervals intersects, including zero-capacity
endpoints. Three closed intervals on the real line with pairwise
intersections have a common point: the largest left endpoint does not exceed
the smallest right endpoint. Any common \(t\) gives the required bounded
transportation matrix.

The deterministic construction in \cref{prop:rank-zero} now reproduces all
four input blocks simultaneously, including zero-capacity cases.

\paragraph{Reproducing the checks.}
The verification snapshot~\cite{kriebel2026verification} specifies
Lean~4.19.0 and pins the dependency revisions in \path{lake-manifest.json}.
With the pinned toolchain installed, run the following command from
\path{qubit_povm_pvm_minimum_settings/bell_lean} in that checkout:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{bash scripts/check.sh --bootstrap --serial}
\end{quote}
The bootstrap option downloads pinned dependencies and their build cache.
It may be omitted with the prepared dependencies installed.
The November 2026 run rebuilt all 68 project modules, checked 78
statement examples, and audited the axiom dependencies of 826 public
theorems. These use only \path{propext}, \path{Classical.choice}, and
\path{Quot.sound}. A further replay checked 33,644 declarations across
1,271 compiled modules in a fresh environment of the same Lean kernel for
the fixed-matrix two-input equality, the Hilbert-space finite-label
equality, and its complete-strategy simulation theorem. The compiler/runtime and the
correspondence between the cached Mathlib artifacts and their pinned
sources remain trusted.

The separate symbolic checks reproduce the Bell coefficients, exact
