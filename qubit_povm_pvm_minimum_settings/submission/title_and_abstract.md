# Title decision

## Selected title

**Minimum Bell-Setting Complexity for Qubit POVM--PVM Separation**

This names the classified resource directly while avoiding any suggestion of
operator-level POVM/PVM equality.

## Alternatives

1. **Qubit POVMs Give No Bell-Correlation Advantage with Two Inputs per Party**
2. **Two-Input Qubit Bell Behaviors Are Projectively Simulable**

The first alternative is more immediately readable but requires the
shared-randomness qualifier in the abstract. The second is concise but may be
misread as same-state or operator-level simulation.

## Abstract

Vértesi and Bene exhibited a \(3\times2\) Bell scenario in which qubit
positive-operator-valued measurements (POVMs) outperform qubit
projection-valued measurements (PVMs). We prove the matching setting lower
bound. For arbitrary finite, input-dependent output alphabets, the
shared-randomness convex hulls of bipartite qubit-POVM and qubit-PVM behaviors
are equal when both parties have two inputs. Equivalently, no two-input
linear Bell functional separates the two convexified sets. We also give a
rational \(3\times2\) functional, an exact qubit-POVM strategy, and a global
analytic qubit-PVM upper bound with a strict exact gap. Hence \(3\times2\) is
the minimum input architecture, up to exchanging the parties. The proof
combines a one-binary-party Lorentz-cone simulation, an extremal
binary--ternary reduction, a physically exact Lorentz incidence manifold,
finite-POVM duality, and a rank trichotomy based on the incidence second
variation. The result concerns convexified behavior sets; it does not assert
equality of the raw nonconvex strategy images or same-state simulation.
