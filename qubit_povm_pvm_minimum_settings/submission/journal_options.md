# Journal, editor, and referee options

**Checked:** 2026-07-29  
**Purpose:** options for the human author at submission time. This document
does not authorize outreach to any editor, referee, or other researcher.

## Recommendation

**Default first submission: _Quantum_.** The paper is a proof-heavy quantum
information result with a clean operational classification, an exact
certificate, and reproducibility artifacts. That combination fits
_Quantum_'s stated criteria of correctness, significance, and clarity and its
broad theoretical quantum-science remit. Its community-run, open-access model
also fits a priority-sensitive public release.

**Stretch option: _PRX Quantum_.** Submit there first only if the title,
abstract, introduction, and cover letter convincingly sell the result as a
broad resource threshold—rather than as a technically impressive closure of a
small Bell scenario. The theorem is conceptually sharp enough to try, but the
journal's exceptional-impact threshold creates a material desk-rejection
risk.

**Strong conventional option: _Physical Review A_.** This is the safest
physics-journal fit. It is the venue of the closest \(3\times2\) prior work and
of major finite-dimensional Bell-correlation work. It should be preferred
over a prestige stretch if rapid specialist review matters more.

Suggested sequential order:

1. _Quantum_;
2. _Physical Review A_;
3. _Journal of Mathematical Physics_;
4. _Communications in Mathematical Physics_.

Alternatively, place _PRX Quantum_ before _Quantum_ as one time-limited
stretch attempt. Do not submit to more than one journal simultaneously.

## Submission framing

Across all venues, lead with the result whose priority is strongest:

> With two inputs per party and arbitrary finite output alphabets, every
> fixed-qubit POVM Bell behavior is a shared-randomness mixture of
> fixed-qubit PVM behaviors.

Then state that the independent exact \(3\times2\) separator matches the
lower bound and proves setting-minimality. On the first manuscript page,
credit Vértesi and Bene for the known \(3\times2\) separation architecture.
Describe the present separator as an **exact rational certificate with a
global analytic PVM upper bound**, not as the first \(3\times2\) separation.

Every pitch should keep the scope visible:

- behavior-set equality, not operator-level POVM simulability;
- branchwise local dimension at most two;
- arbitrary finite, input-dependent output alphabets;
- arbitrary shared classical randomness and mixed states;
- zero projectors and stochastic output postprocessing included;
- no dimension-increasing Naimark dilation;
- no claim that either displayed \(3\times2\) lower value or the PVM upper
  certificate is the exact global optimum.

The cover letter should avoid “first,” “longstanding open problem,” and
“POVMs equal PVMs.” A defensible priority sentence is:

> To our knowledge, no previous work established this arbitrary-output
> two-input behavior-set equality or the resulting setting-minimality
> classification.

## Final checks immediately before submission

1. Repeat the 2025–2026 arXiv and citation search; priority status can change.
2. Recheck the target journal's official scope, board, article type, length,
   data/code, AI-use, conflict, and preprint policies.
3. Confirm that every suggested editor/referee is current and conflict-free.
4. Build the submission archive in a clean directory and run the exact
   verifier from that archive.
5. Ensure the public repository/arXiv record contains all artifacts cited by
   the paper, but do not describe a timestamp as peer review or conclusive
   legal priority.
