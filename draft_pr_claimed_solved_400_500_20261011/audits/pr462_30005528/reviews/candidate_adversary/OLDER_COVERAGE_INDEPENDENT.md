## Decisive route A

The full publisher PDF of Jenkinson, *Every ergodic measure is uniquely maximizing*, DCDS 16 (2006), 383–392, DOI10.3934/dcds.2006.16.383, was independently retrieved. Complete journal pp384–385 were rendered and visually inspected. Theorem1 applies to any ergodic invariant Borel probability on a compact metrizable topological action. Corollary1 specifically supplies continuous f on the circle X=R/Z for doubling D(x)=2x mod1 whose unique maximizing invariant measure is Lebesgue m. The source gives an existence theorem; explicit construction of such f remains a distinct problem there.

Choose C=1+max_X f and s=C-f. Then X is infinite compact connected metric, s is continuous and >=1, and graph(D) is closed. Every legal tile sequence is precisely the forward D-orbit of its first symbol, despite D not being invertible. Define b_N(x)=N^-1 sum_{j=1}^N f(D^j x) and beta=integral f dm. The source normalization gives A(x)=C-liminf b_N(x): the initial cost s(x)/N vanishes.

For every x, every weak-star accumulation point of empirical probabilities nu_N=N^-1 sum_{j=1}^N delta_{D^j x} is D-invariant. Indeed, for every continuous h, integral(h o D-h)dnu_N=[h(D^{N+1}x)-h(Dx)]/N ->0. Compactness provides such subsequential limits; continuity of f makes every scalar accumulation value of b_N equal an invariant integral <=beta. Consequently limsup b_N<=beta and hence A(x)>=C-beta for every legal sequence, including all irregular paths. A Birkhoff-generic point for the ergodic maximizing measure m has b_N->beta, so the infimum C-beta is actually attained.

If the tile sequence has period p, equality of first endpoints implies D^p x=x; conversely the return yields tile periodicity. Its invariant orbit measure is supported on finitely many points and therefore differs from m. Unique maximization gives integral f dnu_periodic<beta, and its ordinary cost mean is C-integral f dnu_periodic>C-beta. This establishes exactly the old compact-domino existence target. It uses atomlessness only to distinguish m from a finite orbit measure, never to infer that supp(m) has no periodic points; in fact supp(m)=X has many periodic points. Doubling has a fixed point and dense periodic points, so exclusion is nonvacuous. Invertibility and eventual-period arguments from the stronger rotation family are not imported into this route.

## Supplemental route B

Bousch, *Le poisson n'a pas d'arêtes*, Ann.IHP36(2000),489–508, DOI10.1016/S0246-0203(00)00132-1, NUMDAM primary scan, gives the irrational rotation coding of Sturm measures and states compact invariant uniquely ergodic support on p492. Complete p492 was visually inspected; extracted p493 was also read. These are older mechanisms. Source uniqueness or atomlessness alone is not used to infer absence of periodic support points.

A direct coding check supplies the needed aperiodicity: take irrational rho in(0,1), code rotation theta->theta+rho using the partition [0,rho), and take the closure Y of its one-sided codes in {0,1}^N. For each code, the number of ones in an n-block is floor(theta+(n-1)rho)-floor(theta-rho), so differs from n rho by less than1. The closed inequality <=1 persists under closure and forces limiting one-frequency rho for every y in Y. Thus Y contains no periodic or eventually constant sequence. The binary evaluation pi(y)=sum_{j>=0}y_j/2^{j+1} mod1 is continuous and semiconjugates shift to doubling. A periodic circle point has a periodic binary expansion, except dyadic ambiguity yields eventually constant expansions; both possibilities contradict irrational frequency. Thus K=pi(Y) is nonempty compact forward invariant and has no periodic doubling point.

Now the old distance-to-support mechanism, explicitly noted by Jenkinson on journal p384 and in the survey p211, gives s(x)=1+d(x,K). Every graph(D) path has A>=1 and every K path has cost identically1. A finite periodic orbit cannot intersect K by forward invariance, so its finite average distance is strictly positive. This also covers the base target with continuous positive cost, connected compact circle alphabet, closed graph and nonvacuous dense periodic competitors.
