## A. Explicit distance construction from the older literature

Take X=R/Z with the circular metric d(x,y)=min over integers k of |x-y-k|, and take G(x)=2x modulo1. Fix rho=sqrt(2)-1, which lies in(0,1) and is irrational. Let mu_rho be the circle Sturmian measure defined by Bousch's Section2 and put K=supp(mu_rho).

For specificity, the source coding can be implemented as follows. For u in R/Z set c_n(u)=1 if u+n rho modulo1 lies in[0,rho), and c_n(u)=0 otherwise, n>=0. Push Lebesgue measure forward by u -> sum over n>=0 of c_n(u)2^(-n-1), modulo1. This is the circle version of the specified symbolic measure. Shift of the digits intertwines this coding with G, so the measure is G-invariant. Its support K is the published irrational Sturmian support. Thus K is nonempty, compact, infinite, G-invariant, minimal, and uniquely ergodic. The support of a probability is nonempty; being a Cantor set makes it infinite. Minimality excludes periodic points: if y in K had a finite G-orbit, that orbit would be a nonempty closed invariant proper subset of the infinite K.

Now define the domino game

\[
\Sigma=X,\qquad T=\{[x,Gx]:x\in X\},\qquad s(x)=1+d(x,K).
\]

This is a completely specified continuous positive cost from the distance potential already displayed by Jenkinson, with its sign reversed and one added. It uses no unknown f or unknown packing realization. The circle is infinite, compact, connected, and metrizable. Distance to a nonempty closed set is continuous (in fact1-Lipschitz); 1<=s<=3/2. G is continuous, so its graph is compact and closed in X times X.

### Every legal sequence, including nongeneric ones

Let w=[a_1,b_1][a_2,b_2]... be any legal sequence. A tile forces b_i=G a_i, and matching forces a_(i+1)=b_i. Induction therefore gives a_i=G^(i-1)x and b_i=G^i x for the unique x=a_1. Conversely every x gives a legal sequence. No extra paths exist.

For every x and N,

\[
\frac{s(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}s(G^j x)}{N}
=1+\frac1N+\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N}d(G^j x,K)\ \ge\ 1+\frac1N.
\]

Hence A(w)>=1 for every legal sequence, without assuming an ordinary average, ergodicity, a generic point, or any measure approximation. If x belongs to K, invariance puts every G^j x in K, so the displayed expression is exactly(N+1)/N. Its limit is1. K is nonempty, so the infimum is attained and equals1.

Every point of K is also generic for mu_rho in the usual continuous-observable sense: empirical measures have convergent subsequences by compactness, every limit is invariant and supported on K, and unique ergodicity makes every such limit mu_rho. Therefore the whole empirical sequence converges. This supplies a generic orbit if one is desired, although attainment here follows directly from constant cost on K.

### Every periodic tile sequence is strictly more expensive

A tile sequence has period p exactly when G^p x=x: the first coordinates prove the forward implication, and iteration proves the converse. In that event its ordinary average is

\[
A(w)=\frac1p\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}s(G^j x)
=1+\frac1p\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}d(G^j x,K).
\]

No periodic point is in K. Thus every point of this finite orbit is outside the closed K and has strictly positive distance from it. The last average is strictly greater than1. This proves exactly the inequality in the printed question for all p, and not only for a sampled set of periods. An eventually periodic sequence has the same limiting average as its eventual periodic cycle, also greater than1.

### Extra robustness that already follows

Periodic starting points are dense in X: every k/(2^p-1) is fixed by G^p, and the mesh1/(2^p-1) tends to0. This is a nonvacuous answer even with connected alphabet and closed relation.

For any fixed period bound Q the set of points fixed by some G^p,1<=p<=Q, is finite. All corresponding average costs exceed1. Taking the minimum of finitely many positive excesses gives a positive bounded-period gap. This is a qualitative gap, not the submission's displayed Diophantine Q^-2 estimate.

Periodic averages can in fact approach1. Since K is invariant and contains no periodic point, it excludes0 and all dyadic points whose forward orbit reaches0. Choose any x in K and its unique binary expansion c_0c_1... . Let y_N be the point whose binary expansion repeats c_0...c_(N-1). Then G^N y_N=y_N. For j<N, G^j x and G^j y_N share their first N-j binary digits, so their circular distance is at most2^(-(N-j)). Since G^j x is in K,

\[
\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}d(G^j y_N,K)
\le\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N}2^{-r}<\frac1N.
\]

Thus 1<A(w_(y_N))<1+1/N. For sufficiently large N the block contains both symbols, because the expansion of x is not eventually constant. The same estimate is valid with circular distance if a short constant repeated block represents the circle endpoint0. Least periods need not equal N and are not assumed to. They must tend to infinity along any near-optimal sequence by the finite-period-gap argument.

The construction does not establish that every legal orbit has an ordinary cost limit, nor that G is a homeomorphism; circle doubling is two-to-one. Those extra features of the submitted annulus family are distinct from the original target.

## B. Independent coverage from the specific published Lebesgue corollary

Jenkinson's Corollary1 printed385 asserts existence of f in C(X) for the same circle doubling G such that Lebesgue probability m is uniquely f-maximizing. Let beta=integral f dm and C=1+||f||_infinity. Set s=C-f and keep the graph tiles above. Then s>=1 and is continuous; X is compact connected and the graph closed. This second route is an existence theorem, not an explicit formula for f.

For any x, write u_N(x)=N^(-1) sum_(j=0)^(N-1) f(G^j x). Every weak-star accumulation point of the empirical probabilities is G-invariant: for each continuous h the difference between its integral after G and before G is(h(G^N x)-h(x))/N, which tends to0. Along any subsequence on which u_N converges, continuity of f gives a limit equal to integral f dnu for such an invariant nu, and therefore at most beta. In particular limsup u_N<=beta, for every x.

The original source's normalization has exactly one extra bounded terminal term:

\[
\frac{s(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}s(G^j x)}N
=C-u_N(x)+\frac{s(G^N x)}N.
\]

Hence A(w_x)=C-liminf u_N(x)>=C-beta. It is not legitimate to replace this by C-limsup u_N(x). The proof above uses the correct liminf identity.

The ergodic theorem, as used in Jenkinson Proposition2.1, gives an m-generic x with u_N(x)->beta, since doubling preserves ergodic Lebesgue probability. Thus some legal sequence has A=C-beta; the infimum is attained. For a periodic x, its finite-orbit invariant probability nu_x is different from non-atomic m, so uniqueness gives integral f dnu_x<beta. Its cost is C-integral f dnu_x>C-beta. Again every periodic legal sequence is excluded. All application hypotheses are satisfied by the actual semigroup action generated by G; no theorem about arbitrary discontinuous maps or unverified alphabet embedding is being substituted.

This chain alone certifies older existence-theorem coverage of the literal question even if one declines to use any recalled Sturmian support property. Its f is nonconstructive, which is why chainA was supplied as an explicit construction as well.

## C. Earlier smooth optimizing family, checked but unnecessary

Bousch's TheoremA together with TheoremB/Corollary1 lets one select omega whose unique maximizing Sturmian measure has the fixed irrational rho. Taking f(x)=cos(2pi(x-omega)) and s=2-f, one may repeat chainB with that unique aperiodic maximizing measure. This gives positive real analytic size on a connected compact circle with dense periodic competitors. The choice of omega is characterized by the published parameter map; a numerical value has not been asserted. ChainA avoids dependence on this parameter selection or the proof's numerical estimates. This third coverage route is corroborating evidence and is not needed for the recommendation.

## Scope of the coverage conclusion

The exact existential compact-alphabet inequality of OWR Question1 is a checked consequence of published results from2000 and2006. The graph conversion and positive normalization written here are audit deductions; an earlier explicit use of the name 'linear domino' or the later numbered question has not been located. The 2026 annulus example may be a convenient elementary presentation, and it has additional invertibility and every-orbit limit properties. No historical first-presentation claim for that precise package is certified. No route here constructs the constrained distance set K of Goncalves-Vedana's packing Conjecture4; the invariant set K in chainA is a subset of the symbol circle, not an allowed-distance set.
