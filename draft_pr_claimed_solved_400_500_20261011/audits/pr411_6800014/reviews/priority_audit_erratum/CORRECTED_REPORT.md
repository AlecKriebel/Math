## Exact comparison and independence

[Morgan–Pansu, Question 14, §11, printed PDF p. 12](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~pierre.pansu/problems_MTDG.pdf) asks whether local maxima of `F=scal²/|Ric|²` on a fixed solvable group satisfy `Ric=cI+D`, with `D` a derivation of that group's Lie algebra. There is no strictness or complete-solvability hypothesis. The counterexample being compared is the standard metric for `D_u=J⊕uE₁₂`, for every `0<u<1/2`, a non-strict local maximum among all nearby left-invariant metrics on that same five-dimensional simply connected group, with `F=1/3`, failing the group's derivation equation for every `c`.

The metric is a Ricci soliton and is isometric to a product of the Euclidean plane and the three-dimensional Heisenberg group. It can consequently have a solvsoliton presentation using another transitive group. This does not imply the original group's derivation equation. [Jablonski, arXiv:1109.6556v2, 25 April 2013, p. 2, Theorem 1.1 and preceding definition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.6556v2) explicitly distinguishes algebraic solitons according to the transitive group and proves the isometric solvsoliton presentation. [Gordon–Jablonski, arXiv:2112.09733v2, 13 March 2023, pp. 4–5, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09733v2) independently confirms the isometric-presentation and automorphism distinction. Neither source supplies the required local optimization proof.

## Source formula discrepancy: correction and independent check

The displayed `E_t` matrix in I v2, §6.4, printed p. 20, is

`[[t,-sqrt(1+t²)],[sqrt(1+t²),-t]] ⊕ [[0,t],[0,0]]`.

The printed expression `4t⁴/(3t⁴+2t²)` is inconsistent with that matrix. In an earlier raw internal message I provisionally used the printed expression to say `F(E₁)=4/5` and to support nonglobality. The parent challenged that inference. I then inspected the actual PDF page and performed an independent exact calculation, without importing the author or parent checkers. The provisional `E₁=4/5` claim is withdrawn and its provenance is retained here.

[check_primary_formulas.py](check_primary_formulas.py) computes in `Q[t,r]/(r²−1−t²)` and separately computes five-dimensional Koszul curvature directly from structure constants. Both calculations give

`s=||S(E_t)||²=5t²/2`, `||[E_t,E_tᵀ]||²=32t²+34t⁴`,

`scal=-5t²/2`, `||Ric||²=8t²+59t⁴/4`,

`F(E_t)=25t⁴/(59t⁴+32t²)`.

Consequently `F(E₁)=25/91<1/3`, whereas `F(E₂)=25/67>1/3`. The script checks `H_tD₁=E_tH_t` for `H_t=diag([[1,t],[0,sqrt(1+t²)]],diag(t,1))`, whose determinant is positive for `t>0`, and the `D_u` to `D₁` conjugacy. Its original computation receipt is [PRIMARY_FORMULA_CONSISTENCY.json](PRIMARY_FORMULA_CONSISTENCY.json), completed at `2026-10-13T01:38:50.449299Z`. This supplies a valid same-group nonglobal comparison from the old displayed family. Independently, I's Theorem 6.5(iii), printed pp. 14–15, already rules out a global maximum when the matrix is neither semisimple nor nilpotent. The packet's `9/17` comparison does not rely on the erroneous expression.

The direct check also confirms `F(D_t)=1/3` and the displayed families' distinct limits as `t→0`; the source's continuous-extension example survives. This is source verification, not a new substantive author proof-search turn. No conclusion about whether the journal corrected the expression is made: the full journal text was not fetched.
