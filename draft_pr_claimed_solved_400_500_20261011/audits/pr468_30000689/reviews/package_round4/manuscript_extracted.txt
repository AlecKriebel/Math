                                                 Abstract
         Let S be a ﬁnite simplicial triangulation of the topological sphere S 2d , and let T be a miss-
     ing d-face. We prove that adjoining T to the d-skeleton of S prevents a topological embedding
     in S 2d . For d ≥ 3, a graded Koszul calculation combines with established ambient-completion,
     anisotropy and metastable embedding theorems. For d = 2, we apply the Nevo–Wagner bistellar
     transfer theorem in the PL class supplied by the given triangulation: a contractible complemen-
     tary manifold supplies the required disk map, and a ﬂag subdivision supplies the base. This
     proves the statement of their Conjecture 1.3 without a standard PL structure assumption on
     the source. No claim of ﬁrst priority or a new general method is made.

With Weber’s existence theorem [8, Theorem 1], these inputs give a synthesis for d ≥ 3.

4.1   The source structure and a complement lemma
A ﬁnite triangulation of a topological 4-manifold is a combinatorial 4-manifold [3, p. 797]. To recall
the dimension-speciﬁc point, positive-dimensional face links have sphere homology in dimensions at
most two. Inductively they are PL spheres; vertex links are therefore closed triangulated homology
3-spheres. At a vertex, choose nested coordinate and conical neighborhoods. Place a link loop
radially in a small cone contained in a punctured coordinate 4-ball, itself contained in the punctured
open star. The coordinate ball makes the loop nullhomotopic, and radial retraction puts the
nullhomotopy in the link. The link is simply connected. Three-dimensional Poincaré and PL
uniqueness then make it a PL 3-sphere. This supplies a PL structure on S without identifying its
global PL class with the standard sphere.

The remaining cases can be classiﬁed without a sphere-structure assumption. If a triangle was an
old face and becomes missing, it contains α, while every proper face survives. It must be M = α,
with (p, q) = (2, 2). Otherwise some proper edge of M is new and contains β. If q = 1 this
gives (p, q) = (3, 1) and M = v ∗ β, with v outside the support; a triangle wholly on the support
vertices would be a new face. If q = 0, the new vertex of β is adjacent only to support vertices;
any proposed missing triangle having all three edges is a new support face. For completeness,
(p, q) = (0, 4) removes a vertex and (1, 3) removes an edge, so a triangle involving the removed
simplex cannot have all proper faces present; no new proper edge arises in either case. These
exhaust all ﬁve move types.

4.4   The base in the source’s own PL class
The barycentric subdivision sd S is ﬂag: its vertices are faces of S, and pairwise adjacency means
pairwise comparability, hence a chain. It has no missing triangles, so E(sd S) holds vacuously. The
subdivision and S are closed combinatorial 4-manifolds that are PL homeomorphic for the PL struc-
ture supplied by this triangulation. Pachner’s theorem, as stated and proved in [5, Theorem 5.9],
gives a ﬁnite bistellar sequence between them, allowing simplicial isomorphisms. Every intermedi-
ate complex remains homeomorphic to S 4 in the same PL class. Transfer at every step gives E(S),
completing d = 2. No comparison with the standard PL 4-sphere occurs in this induction.


5     Low dimensions and veriﬁcation scope
For d = 1, a triangulated 2-sphere with n vertices has 3n − 6 edges, by Euler’s formula and the
triangle-edge incidence count. Adding a missing edge gives 3n − 5, exceeding the simple planar
graph bound. Thus the augmented graph does not embed in S 2 . For d = 0 there is no missing
singleton on the actual vertex set. If a convention permits a ghost singleton, its addition gives three
points, which cannot embed in the two-point sphere S 0 . These cases, the metastable argument and
the dimension-four obstruction prove Theorem 1.
