## Source reconstruction

The OWR statement is a geometric conjecture that the displayed homogeneous weak Einstein-tensor identity extends to all spacetimes with its described admissible timelike singularities. The description is a regular zero-area singularity with negative ZAS mass on spacelike slices. The immediately cited BKTZ construction concretely uses the constant-time foliation; it does not quantify over every tilted spacelike hypersurface. The OWR prose also supplies no explicit energy condition, matter equation, prescribed electromagnetic constitutive law, or lapse growth bound.

BKTZ Section 1.3 introduces spatial conformally flat coordinates and the constant-time slices. Section 1.4 gives the regular conformal resolution and negative mass. Theorem 1.1 separately imposes sufficient lapse and Einstein-tensor growth bounds; these are theorem hypotheses rather than part of the ZAS definition. Section 2 Definition 2.1 tests smooth compactly supported vector fields on the blown-up manifold including its inner boundary. Theorem 1.1 footnote 7 says angular independence at the boundary is a convenient restriction, not necessary for its theorem. Normal components need not vanish.

Bray-Jauregui Definitions 4 and 6 concern Riemannian geometry. Their regular-resolution metric is smooth positive definite on a boundary collar; the conformal factor vanishes exactly at the boundary with positive derivative along the inward resolution normal. Definition 10 gives negative regular mass. These definitions impose no spacetime lapse or energy equation.

## Adversarial scope reservation

It would overstate the source to claim that OWR explicitly means ONLY one foliation. It would equally overstate it to add a universal quantifier over all tilted slices as a published requirement. The precise check is whether a proposed metric has the regular negative-mass ZAS on the natural static foliation in the exact cited framework. A counterexample should clearly disclose that interpretation, test nearby admissible tilted graphs where possible, and not claim a theorem under unproved stronger all-slice or physically constrained variants.

The smooth structure of the blown-up cylinder governs the source test fields. Smoothness in collapsed Cartesian coordinates would be a different test class and cannot silently replace Definition 2.1.
