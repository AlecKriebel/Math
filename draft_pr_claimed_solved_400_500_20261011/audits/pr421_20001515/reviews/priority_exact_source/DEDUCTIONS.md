# Original identity deductions for the priority audit

These computations test possible transfer routes; they are not a new geometric-cubulation proof attempt.

Let \(G_p\) have the monodromy \(a\mapsto a,b\mapsto a^p b,c\mapsto bcb\). The target is \(p=1\). The workshop \(b\mapsto aba\) has the same abelianization matrix as \(p=2\), although we do not assert equality of its free-group automorphism with \(b\mapsto a^2b\).

On the basis \(([a],[b],[c])\), the matrix is
\[
M_p=\begin{pmatrix}1&p&0\\0&1&2\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A mapping torus has abelianization \(\mathbb Z\oplus\operatorname{coker}(M_p-I)\). This follows by abelianizing the presentation: the stable letter supplies an independent \(\mathbb Z\), while the conjugacy relations impose exactly \((M_p-I)[F_3]\).

For \(p=1\), the relations kill \([a]\) and \(2[b]\); hence \(H_1=\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2\). For the workshop map, they kill \(2[a]\) and \(2[b]\), so \(H_1=\mathbb Z^2\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2\). These are nonisomorphic groups, not merely distinct representatives in \(\operatorname{Out}(F_3)\).

The index-\(k\) subgroup generated by the fiber and the \(k\)-th power of the stable letter has matrix
\[
M_p^k-I=\begin{pmatrix}0&pk&pk(k-1)\\0&0&2k\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed \(M_p=I+N\), \(N^3=0\), and the binomial expansion gives \(kN+\binom{k}{2}N^2\). The gcd of all entries gives the first Smith invariant. The only nonzero rank-two minor is \(2pk^2\), so the gcd of the rank-two minors gives the product of the first two invariants. Thus the target time cover has Smith invariants \((k,2k,0)\); the workshop time cover has \((2k,2k,0)\).

Consequently, no positive time covers of the target and workshop groups are isomorphic: their torsion orders would require \(2k^2=4\ell^2\), or \(k^2=2\ell^2\), which is impossible for positive integers (the standard parity descent). This excludes the simple “take powers” transfer. It does not rule out arbitrary commensurability through different fibers or more general finite-index subgroups.

Other near examples must also keep their signs. Replacing \(c\mapsto bcb\) by conjugation \(bcb^{-1}\) gives torsion-free abelianization \(\mathbb Z^3\), and the one-sided quadratic example \(a\mapsto a,b\mapsto ba,c\mapsto cb\) in BGGH Example 9.1 gives torsion-free \(\mathbb Z^2\). Neither is the target. The current Leary–Niblo–Wise Proposition 1 uses \(a^t=b,b^t=aba^{-1}\); its abelianization is torsion-free \(\mathbb Z^2\). Search-result text that drops the inverse bar cannot be used to identify that group.

For Wu–Ye's displayed one-vertex tubular family in rank three, both nonfixed basis elements receive words in powers of the fixed \(a_0\). Abelianization only imposes multiples of \([a_0]\), leaving free rank at least three, whereas the target has rank two. Therefore the displayed rank-three tubular family is not isomorphic to this target. The audit does not claim to rule out every other rank or every other fibration of the target.

The script independently checks the power and Smith formulas for \(1\leq k\leq20\) with exact integers. The argument above proves the formulas for every positive \(k\); the finite calculation is a reproducible consistency check.
