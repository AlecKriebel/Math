## Independent algebraic reconstruction

At p use B_p(z)=product_i(1-chi_i(p)z)^(-a_i), the analytic branch equal to one at zero, and write b_p(r) for its coefficients. Parseval at radius p^(-1/2) gives J_p=sum_r |b_p(r)|^2/p^r, equal to the single common-phase average of the product of local absolute-value powers. Distinct local factors must share that phase. The proposed A is the increasing-prime product of (1-1/p)^E J_p. This is a defined arithmetic constant, not an assertion about averaging the actual critical-line L-function.

Coefficient magnitudes are dominated by coefficients of (1-z)^(-sum a_i): triangle inequality and the positive binomial coefficients for a_i>=0 prove this directly. Their growth is polynomial, so J_p is finite and at least one. The first coefficient is sum a_i chi_i(p); thus J_p=1+|sum a_i chi_i(p)|^2/p+O_a(p^-2), uniformly over primes. At primes outside the finite set S of divisors of Q=lcm(q_i), the first coefficient squared equals E+2 sum_{psi<phi} M_psi M_phi Re(psi(p)overline(phi(p))). Multiplication by (1-1/p)^E and by |1-rho(p)/p|^(2 M_psi M_phi) cancels every linear term. The remaining H_p has absolutely summable O(p^-2) logarithms. The ratio rho is nonprincipal when primitive characters are distinct, by uniqueness of primitive induction. Nonprincipal Dirichlet Euler products at one, ordered by increasing prime, then supply A as a finite positive product of local factors, absolutely convergent H_p, and |L^S(1,rho)| powers. The original addendum fixes rho's modulus to lcm of its two conductors or Q, so it introduces no additional deleted primes. This convergence uses standard analytic number theory, not random matrix assumptions.

The exact identity L(s,chi_i)=L(s,psi_i) product_{p|q_i}(1-psi_i(p)p^-s) groups the primitive core exponents into M_psi while retaining each deletion. Changing all q_i to common Q requires inverse local factors at p|Q with p not dividing q_i. They are convergent reciprocal Euler factors, not finite Dirichlet polynomials. The ratio A/A* is product_{p|Q} J_p/J_p*. This identity is rigorous for the defined constants; the corresponding ratio for actual leading moments is part of the model and requires weighted correlation assumptions.

The Haar U(N) moment product is product_{j=1}^N Gamma(j)Gamma(j+2m)/Gamma(j+m)^2 and has leading G(1+m)^2/G(1+2m)*N^(m^2). This gives one matrix factor for each distinct primitive constituent with m=M_psi. The original proposed G is the product of these positive factors. Its exponent agrees with the prime variance E.
