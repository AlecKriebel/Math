The live AIM section 5 assigns Problem 5.3 to C. Turnage-Butterbaugh. Its task is to develop a heuristic for imprimitive L-function moments or products of degree-one factors; the explicit example is a critical-line absolute-square product of Dirichlet L-functions, with differing powers allowed. The statement does not request a proof of the resulting asymptotic, and does not specify a moving-conductor or shifted regime. A complete fixed unshifted Dirichlet-product heuristic can answer the displayed example but cannot be relabeled a theorem, an all-L-functions classification, a shifted formula, or a negative-moment result.

Independently reconstructed expected mechanism: partition factors by identical primitive inducing character psi, aggregate nonnegative real weights kappa_psi=sum k_j, and use Lambda=sum kappa_psi^2. Equal primitive factors share the same zeros, so treating inducing moduli as independent gives a falsifiable wrong exponent. Distinct psi and conjugate psi remain separate unless they are actually equal. All deleted Euler factors must remain inside one common local phase expectation before integration. The arithmetic factor is the increasing-prime product (1-1/p)^Lambda E_theta product_j |1-chi_j(p)p^-1/2 exp(i theta)|^-2k_j. Cross-character first-order terms cancel only conditionally over primes. Finite local algebra is rigorous; independent CUE models and Euler/zero splitting are model assumptions. Nonnegative powers make zeros of original L-functions harmless, with zero-weight factors defined as one. Negative powers may create nonintegrable singularities and are outside this reconstruction.
