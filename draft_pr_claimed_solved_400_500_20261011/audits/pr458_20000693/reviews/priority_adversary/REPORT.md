## Mechanism comparison and attempts to falsify both sides

Write the candidate moment as the integral of the product of `|L(1/2+it, chi_i)|^(2 a_i)`, with fixed `a_i >= 0`. If `psi_i` is the primitive inducer, the candidate combines only **equal** primitive characters: `M_psi = sum_{psi_i = psi} a_i`. Its predicted exponent is `E = sum_psi M_psi^2`. Distinct conjugate primitive characters remain different unless they are actually equal. Conjugacy alone neither duplicates the same function at the same positive t nor generates the principal prime cross term. Equality must be exact, including conductor and character values.

At a prime, its coefficients come from `B_p(z) = product_i (1-chi_i(p) z)^(-a_i)`; the arithmetic local mean is `J_p = sum_r |[z^r]B_p|^2 / p^r`. All factors at that prime use the same phase. The ordered prime product is normalized by `(1-1/p)^E`. Each primitive class contributes the matrix factor `G(1+M_psi)^2/G(1+2M_psi)`. This description comes from the frozen candidate, not from claiming an exact prior full-tuple quotation.

Heap already has the distinctive separation: constituent zero models factor, while arithmetic need not. Multiplicities enter before squaring; distinct primitive conductors are allowed. His worked `zeta^2 L(chi)` example gives the 5 exponent and the corresponding two constituent factors. His modified CFKRS recipe prevents conductor phases from being mixed incorrectly. Thus multiple primitive constituents, differing primitive conductors, multiplicities, and mixed Euler arithmetic are not new mechanisms here. Sahay later treats Dirichlet characters at one ambient modulus explicitly, including the deletion of prime factors absent from an imprimitive character.

The following deductions delimit how far those statements can be transported:

1. **Independent real weights.** Integer multiplicity times one real outer weight covers rational weight ratios, not an arbitrary irrational vector by literal quantification. Matrix moments themselves accept nonnegative real effective weights. The prime-local power series is also well defined for those weights because its arguments lie inside the unit disk. Substituting an independent weight into each constituent's same model is a natural heuristic extension, already supported by the independent-real-weight prediction in the 2014 paper. Density or analytic continuation alone would not prove the actual moment conjecture for every real vector.
2. **Different imprimitive levels.** Passing each primitive factor to an imprimitive level multiplies by finitely many explicit nonzero Euler factors on the critical line. At the heuristic model level these must be inserted into the joint prime mean and replace the relevant local factors, leaving zeros and `E` unchanged. For actual moments, boundedness of these factors gives comparison bounds only. It does not identify the precise weighted leading constant without a joint averaging/splitting assumption. The candidate makes that assumption explicit after clarification.
3. **Common modulus.** At one fixed modulus, a primitive inducer determines a unique induced Dirichlet character as a function on integers. Hence two different characters at that modulus cannot secretly share a primitive inducer. Sahay's indexing needs no separate aggregation rule. With differing levels, repetitions are possible and must be aggregated. One can instead move all characters to their least common multiple and restore inverse finite local factors; that restatement is algebraic, while the finite-factor moment transport remains modeled.
4. **Boundary cases.** Zero weights can be deleted; an all-zero tuple has integral exactly T over `[T,2T]`. Nonnegative weights avoid inverse-moment singularities. Older displayed `k > -1/2` ranges must not be applied to repeated constituents without checking their effective weights and zeros. Conductors, shifts, and family size growing with T are outside the candidate's fixed-data scope.

These observations defeat both adversarial extremes: there is no new central product mechanism identified, and the exact expanded scope is not certified merely by citing integer/common-modulus prior statements.

## A falsified old convergence inference

Heap's preprint (194), and thesis (571), infer absolute convergence of a residual Euler product in `Re s > 1/2` after dividing out constituent diagonal products, using bounded signed prime cross sums. This inference is false even for degree-one characters. The following is an independent check, not a claim to a new moment theorem.

Take `L(s)=zeta(s)L(s,chi_3)` with the real nonprincipal character modulo 3 and outer weight 1. For a good prime put `x=p^(-s)` and `epsilon=chi_3(p)`, which is ±1. The coefficient-square local series is

```
epsilon=+1: F_p(x) = sum_{r>=0} (r+1)^2 x^r = (1+x)/(1-x)^3;
epsilon=-1: F_p(x) = sum_{r>=0} 1_{r even} x^r = 1/(1-x^2).
```

The two individual diagonal series together are `(1-x)^(-2)`. Their quotient is therefore

```
U_p(s) = (1+epsilon*x)/(1-epsilon*x)
       = 1 + 2 epsilon p^(-s) + O(p^(-2 Re s)).
```

At real `s=1`, the sum of `|U_p(1)-1|` diverges like the prime harmonic sum. The signed character sum can converge and still fails to give absolute convergence. Factoring out `L(s,chi_3)^2` removes this linear cross term and leaves a suitable absolutely convergent remainder; the raw cross Euler product remains conditional in increasing-prime order. For arbitrary noninteger powers, “pole” and “residue” also need branch care. Thus this supporting argument cannot be silently transported as a proof of analyticity or a Perron asymptotic. The earlier printed heuristic and its priority remain real evidence despite that flaw.

The candidate's separate nonprincipal `L(1)` cross factors and absolutely convergent remainder address precisely this distinction. Its full normalized arithmetic product is generally conditionally, not absolutely, convergent.
