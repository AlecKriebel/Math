**The original arbitrary-entire question remains unsolved after five substantive author turns.** The strongest explicit positive result is: if just one of the three inputs is R(exp(Q)), with R a nonconstant Laurent polynomial and Q a nonconstant polynomial, the other two inputs may be completely arbitrary nonconstant entire functions and the required constancy follows.

## Exact original scope

For arbitrary nonconstant entire one-variable f_1,f_2,f_3, let V={sum f_i(z_i)=0} in C^3. Does every ambient entire F bounded on all of V take one constant value there? The source adds no growth, smoothness or connectedness assumption. Hayman–Lingham 2018, printed p43, gives the complete question and credits a special case to Demailly. SOURCE_SCOPE.md records primary editions, exact qualifications and the bounded prior-attempt/current-literature gate.

## Proved scoped results

1. **Exact finite polynomial pullback equivalence.** The source property holds before polynomial coordinate precomposition if and only if it holds afterwards. A companion-matrix construction makes the symmetric coefficients ambient entire even at repeated-root fibers. This also gives a positive family with three Laurent-polynomial exponential inputs and polynomial phases, using a credited algebraic-base abelian-cover theorem.
2. **Two such inputs, arbitrary third.** Generic separated Laurent curves have connected algebraic fibers and exponential lifts with at most 2d_1d_2 components. A local lifted isotopy justifies component labels; bounded symmetric coefficients in the third variable and removability cover all exceptional fibers. This result is subsumed in scope by the stronger next theorem but its historical proof is retained unchanged.
3. **One such input, two arbitrary inputs.** The quotient by the first exponential is ultra-Liouville by a finite-fiber maximum argument over the complement of an analytic subset of C^2. The connected cyclic cover is then Liouville. A failed double-exponential quotient iteration is explicitly a method obstruction on an otherwise affirmative example.
4. **Virtually nilpotent inverse-monodromy criterion.** One input admitting a surjective inverse cover off a finite set and virtually nilpotent monodromy suffices. The normal closure and its pullback are treated explicitly. The exact monodromy of exp(exp z) is Z wreath Z, which has no finite-index nilpotent subgroup. This is not a source counterexample.
5. **Character rigidity on the triple-double-exponential test.** Every bounded character-automorphic holomorphic function on V_2={sum exp(exp z_i)=0} is constant, and is zero for a nontrivial character. Every bounded function with finite-dimensional deck orbit is constant there, including every ambient exponential polynomial. Arbitrary entire functions need not have such an orbit.

## Sharp remaining gaps

The unrestricted triple need not contain a polynomial input, an R(exp(Q)) input, or an input with the specified monodromy condition. Infinite fibers do not supply the finite maximum used in turn 3. A merely Liouville quotient need not be ultra-Liouville. Finite singular-value data do not imply virtually nilpotent monodromy. Even on V_2, absence of character eigenfunctions does not eliminate all infinite-dimensional deck representations. No nonconstant bounded ambient entire F has been produced, and no general theorem closes these gaps.
