# Independent function-theory derivations

These derivations were completed from primary sources and mathematical TURN_1–TURN_5 before any candidate code, computations, final/status packet, history, or prior review was read. They are conditional only on the explicitly classical results identified below; finite computations are not part of these proofs.

## A. Finite maps and intrinsic growth

Let p be the coordinatewise polynomial map with degrees d_i and D=product d_i. Its fibers, counted with multiplicity, have exactly D points. For an entire F, the elementary symmetric functions of F on these fibers extend to entire functions in the base. Independently, construct the finite free algebra tensor_i C[w_i,Z_i]/(P_i(Z_i)-w_i), using basis 1,Z_i,...,Z_i^(d_i-1). Multiplication by Z_i has entire matrix entries. The Taylor series of F evaluated at the three commuting multiplication matrices converges locally uniformly: on a compact base the norms are uniformly bounded and the entire scalar power series is absolutely convergent at those bounds. Its characteristic polynomial has the fiber values as roots, with multiplicities. This works at colliding roots, without dividing by a discriminant.

If all fiber values are bounded by M, coefficient k is bounded by binomial(D,k)M^k. Thus bounded-entire constancy on the base hypersurface makes the characteristic polynomial constant there. On the pullback hypersurface the image of F is finite, and connectedness makes that image one point. Conversely, surjectivity gives constancy on the base from constancy of the composition. This independently verifies TURN_1 Theorem A, including repeated roots and unequal degrees; ambient entirety is maintained in both directions.

If a zero-free entire h has finite order, write h=e^q with q entire (integrate h'/h on simply connected C). For every fixed exponent sigma larger than the order, Re q(z)=log|h(z)| is at most C(1+|z|^sigma). The Borel–Caratheodory estimate on discs of radii r and 2r bounds |q(z)-q(0)| on |z|<=r by O(r^sigma). Cauchy's derivative estimate then makes q^(k)(0)=0 for k>sigma; q is polynomial. This gives an independent growth check for the zero-free corollaries. It fails when finite order is dropped: q=e^z is not polynomial and e^q is the stress test.

## B. Finite maxima and cyclic descent

For a connected manifold Y mapping to C^n, with a dense, surjective, finite unramified cover away from a proper analytic set, the maximum of a bounded continuous plurisubharmonic v over each generic fiber is plurisubharmonic. Locally it is the maximum of finitely many branch compositions. Boundedness gives removable extension across the analytic set. A bounded-above plurisubharmonic function on C^n is constant, by applying one-variable subharmonic Liouville on every affine complex line. Generic finite fibers actually attain the constant maximum. Density and continuity put v below that value everywhere on Y; the maximum principle gives constancy. Nonproper exceptional fibers are harmless because the generic degree is finite and the maximum is attained.

For Y={R(u)+g(y)+h(z)=0}, u in C*, the rational map R has fixed finite degree. Removing its branch values and its finite endpoint values at 0 and infinity ensures precisely that finite-cover description. The exceptional preimage is analytic. It cannot contain an open part of Y: at a fixed rational value u has finitely many possible values and the remaining separated equation has complex dimension one, while Y has dimension two. Irreducibility of the separated entire hypersurface V, from the source-attributed Rubel–Squires–Taylor input, descends under exp in the first coordinate to Y and gives connected regular loci. The cyclic logarithmic cover V_reg→Y_reg therefore has ultra-Liouville base and abelian deck group. Lin's Theorem 1.6 makes V_reg Liouville; density gives the exact ambient restriction conclusion. This independently proves TURN_3's one-input family, which subsumes the positive families in turns 1 and 2 without the turn-2 trace argument.

For TURN_1's separate torus proof, exp:C^3→(C*)^3 is a local biholomorphism and surjection. The Laurent hypersurface S is irreducible because its inverse image V is irreducible. Its singularities are finite: all three Laurent derivatives must vanish. Its smooth locus is a connected quasiprojective surface and is ultra-Liouville by Lin–Zaidenberg section 1.3. Its connected logarithmic pullback has deck group Z^3, so the same Lin theorem applies. A discrete gradient-zero set also rules out multiplicities and gives normality to the two-dimensional reduced hypersurfaces by the hypersurface criterion. Normality is not used as a Liouville substitute.

## C. Rechecking the two-input family with inverse singularities retained

For rational Laurent maps R_1,R_2, remove all finite rational branch values and both finite distinguished endpoint values, forming E_i. For c outside E_1+E_2, the two puncture sets E_1 and c-E_2 are disjoint. Rational inverse monodromy around the first acts only on the first root, and around the second only on the second root. Each individual action is transitive since a sphere minus finitely many points is connected. Thus the product action is transitive. The algebraic curve C_c is connected and smooth, not merely generically connected.

Let u,v be meromorphic coordinates on its compact normalization. Every zero and pole is at the boundary. Their orders are bounded by d_2,d_1 respectively, because the coordinate projection degrees are bounded by those rational degrees. If all boundary order vectors spanned rank at most one, some nonzero integral (a,b) would make u^a v^b a meromorphic function with no zero or pole on the compact normalization, hence constant. Neither exponent can be zero because both coordinate functions are nonconstant. The curve lies in one torus coset component, locally parametrized by (alpha t^r,beta t^s) with r,s nonzero. Its separated Laurent identity forces c to be the sum of the constant Laurent coefficients. Away from that additional value two independent boundary vectors exist, and their nonzero determinant has absolute value <=2d_1d_2. The logarithmic lift consequently has finitely many components, bounded by that index. Each component is an abelian cover of a smooth affine algebraic curve and is Liouville.

Pointwise finite component counts alone do not give a trace. To check the actual proposed construction, choose a compactly supported small diffeomorphism h_delta of the t-sphere, identity near E_1 and translation by delta near c_*-E_2. Put k_delta(t)=c_*+delta-h_delta(c_*-t). This fixes each finite E_2 neighborhood; near infinity it is translation by delta and still fixes infinity. Isotopy lifting under each finite branched rational map extends over its finite ramification centers and fixes the source points 0 and infinity separately, since their image paths are constant and their fibers finite. The induced C* isotopies lift under exp. Thus curve-component labels vary continuously locally, including at punctures of the compactification; no Ehresmann properness assumption is needed.

Holomorphic implicit sections through one point of each smooth component then yield the component values of bounded F, and their symmetric coefficients glue under permutations. Boundedness supplies removable extension at the discrete preimages of the finite excluded c-values in the arbitrary third variable. Liouville makes the coefficients constant. The generic portion is dense even at critical x-values: for targets approaching the original value, the local open mapping theorem/Rouche gives roots approaching the original x. Connectedness of the full separated hypersurface makes the finite image one point. This independently checks TURN_2's entire universal argument, including its exceptional fibers, rather than accepting a finite lattice test as proof.

## D. Monodromy criterion and the double exponential

A finite cover of an ultra-Liouville manifold is ultra-Liouville by finite fiber maxima and an attained maximum. For a regular cover with virtually nilpotent deck group, take the finite-index nilpotent core N. The intermediate finite cover is ultra-Liouville, and the N-cover is Liouville by Lin's theorem. No amenable or merely solvable replacement is justified.

For an entire f with the assumed finite-exclusion surjective cover, the kernel of its permutation monodromy defines a normal cover of C minus S that maps onto the original nonnormal cover. Pulling back over U={-g(y)-h(z) not in S}, each connected normal-pullback component has deck group a subgroup of the virtually nilpotent group. U is connected and ultra-Liouville because bounded plurisubharmonic functions extend across its deleted analytic set into C^2. The original pullback V^0 is smooth and connected: it is a dense analytic-set complement in an irreducible separated hypersurface, and f has no critical point over this base. A connected normal-pullback component surjects onto V^0 by path lifting, so bounded functions descend as constants. This verifies TURN_4's criterion while preserving every covering and surjectivity hypothesis.

For f=e^(e^z), over C minus {0,1} all inverse sheets are log(log w+2pi i k)+2pi i n. A loop about 0 moves k by one; the outer-log shifts can be removed by independent integer relabelings on the infinite k-orbit. A loop about 1 winds exactly the single inner branch that vanishes there, increasing its n by one. Conjugation moves this lamp to every k. The faithful group is (direct sum_Z Z) semidirect Z. Any finite-index nilpotent subgroup would contain a positive power of the shift and a positive power of one lamp. Repeated commutators give successive differences s(shift_r-I)^k delta_0; the endpoint coefficient at kr is s for every k. They never vanish, contrary to finite nilpotency class. Hence finite singular-value exclusions do not imply the stated group criterion. This is not a bounded-function counterexample.

## E. Characters and finite orbit spaces

On V_2, a nonzero bounded deck eigenfunction has |chi(e_j)|=1 by considering positive and negative translation powers. Write chi(e_j)=exp(2pi i alpha_j), 0<=alpha_j<1. Twisting by exp(-sum alpha_j z_j) gives a well-defined holomorphic H on Y^* with |H|<=M product |u_j|^(-alpha_j). The coordinate divisors in Y={sum exp u_j=0} are smooth and have only pairwise normal crossings, because one exponential derivative in the remaining coordinate is nonzero and the origin is not on Y. Negative Laurent coefficients at each divisor vanish by the estimate r^(m-alpha_j)→0; iterated Cauchy integrals give joint extension at crossings. H is holomorphic on all Y.

The explicit isomorphism Y=S×C gives common-translation entire lines. On each such line, H has outside-disc bound O(|t|^(-sum alpha_j)). If any alpha_j>0, Liouville and decay make it zero; if all are zero, H is bounded and is constant by the known Liouville property of S and linewise Liouville. Therefore every nontrivial character eigenfunction is zero, and invariant bounded functions are constant.

A finite-dimensional translation orbit space consists of bounded functions. Its commuting generators are invertible isometries for the supremum norm, so all eigenvalues are unitary and all Jordan blocks are trivial (otherwise powers grow polynomially in any finite-dimensional norm). Simultaneous diagonalization reduces to the preceding characters. Ambient exponential polynomials have finite-dimensional translation orbit because shifts preserve the finite span of all smaller polynomial monomials with the same exponent vectors. This verifies all TURN_5 claims for arbitrary complex exponent vectors, including nonunitary ones and polynomial prefactors.

## F. Independent entire-curve falsification of an alternate proof route

Every holomorphic map gamma:C→V_2 is constant. Write its coordinate functions z_j(t), and A_j(t)=exp(exp z_j(t)). Since A_j is zero-free and A_1+A_2+A_3=0, q=A_1/A_3 is an entire function omitting 0 and -1. Little Picard makes q constant, and likewise A_2/A_3 constant. Therefore exp z_1-exp z_3 and exp z_2-exp z_3 are constants c_1,c_2: their exponentials are constant, so each difference takes values in a discrete logarithm fiber and is constant. These constants satisfy exp c_1+exp c_2+1=0; at least one c_j is nonzero. The zero-free entire function U=exp z_3 also has U+c_j=exp z_j zero-free, and hence omits the two distinct values 0,-c_j. Little Picard makes U constant. Then every exp z_j is constant, and every z_j is constant by the same discrete-fiber argument.

This is an independently checkable obstruction to proving V_2 Liouville by a connecting family of entire curves. It does not construct a bounded nonconstant function and does not contradict TURN_5. The only additional classical dependency is Little Picard, verified in Christopher Bishop's Stony Brook Complex Analysis notes, Chapter 10 (primary teaching source at https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~bishop/classes/math536.S24/chap10.pdf). No novelty assertion is made.

## G. Tangent differential equations do not supply complete flows

On the affirmative exponential family exp x+exp y+exp z=0, fix z and use the tangent vector field T=exp(y) partial_x-exp(x) partial_y. Let u=exp x, v=exp y and c=u+v=-exp z, which is nonzero. Its local integral equation is u'=u(c-u), with solution u(t)=c/(1+K exp(-ct)) and K=(c-u(0))/u(0)=v(0)/u(0), nonzero. The denominator has finite complex zeros because exp(-ct) attains every nonzero complex value. Thus u has finite-time poles, and x cannot be an entire coordinate along the flow. This tangent field is not complete even on an already Liouville separated hypersurface. Local tangent directions do not justify global entire trajectories or a Liouville argument. This records a blocked independent mechanism, not a defect in a proof actually asserted by the packet.

## H. Boundary counterexample when a source hypothesis is dropped

For only two inputs f_1(x)=exp x and f_2(y)=-exp y, the zero set is the disjoint union x-y=2pi i k. Ambient entire F=exp((x-y)/2) takes values (-1)^k, hence is bounded and nonconstant there. Adding a constant third summand leaves the same failure if that summand is zero. This demonstrates why three nonconstant inputs and the credited connectedness result matter; it is not admissible for the original problem.
