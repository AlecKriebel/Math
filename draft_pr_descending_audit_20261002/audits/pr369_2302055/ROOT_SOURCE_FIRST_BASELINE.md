## Exact target and success criteria

The authoritative problem is Problem 2.55, printed page 43 of Hayman–Lingham, Research Problems in Function Theory (2018, arXiv:1809.07200v2). I read its surrounding text and visually inspected PDF page 44 (one based). Each f_i, i=1,2,3, is a nonconstant entire function of one complex variable. V is the complete zero set in C^3 of f_1(z_1)+f_2(z_2)+f_3(z_3). F is entire on C^3 and bounded on V. The question asks whether F is necessarily constant on V. Constancy of F on all C^3 is not the claim; the defining function itself is an immediate warning against that substitution. A positive resolution must apply to every such triple, without assuming finite singular values, polynomial growth, algebraicity, smoothness, a covering presentation, or a particular exponential form. A negative resolution needs an admissible triple and an entire F with a rigorously bounded, nonconstant restriction.

The book's update credits Demailly for one very special case. It does not report a universal resolution. An intrinsic Liouville theorem (every bounded holomorphic function on a surface is constant) can imply the required property but must have its hypotheses established for that surface. A special-case theorem or a sufficient condition is useful partial evidence and cannot alone settle the original quantifier.

Demailly's Bulletin des Sciences Mathématiques 103 (1979), 179–191, PDF pages 1–3 and 8–13, were visually read, including the exact Theorem 2 on printed page 186 and its proof continuation to page 190. For S={e^x+e^y=1}, a holomorphic function bounded by C(1+|z|)^n, n real >=0, is a restriction of a polynomial of degree <=n. Taking n=0 gives intrinsic Liouville. The introduction also credits a separate Wakabayashi argument and a prior irreducibility result of Rubel–Squires–Taylor; these credits are not newly proved by our source access. The weighted extension theorem has special surface geometry and explicit growth control. It is not a general assertion that boundedness on an arbitrary hypersurface controls an arbitrary entire extension away from it. The last five PDF pages are a separate appendix; I inspected pages 16–18 and did not mistake them for the original article's final theorem.

Demailly's two-page C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 288 (8 January 1979), 39–40, was visually read in full. Besides the same polynomial-growth theorem, Corollary 2 treats e^x+e^y=f(w) for a nonzero meromorphic f on C^n, including the bounded holomorphic conclusion. This is broader than just the constant right side, but still fixes two exponential summands. The note explicitly states the general separate-variable meromorphic Liouville conjecture rather than resolving it. Corollary 3 supplies specific exponential covers of hyperplane complements. Covering type, connectedness and removal of exceptional fibers need proof in any application. None of these statements substitutes arbitrary entire f_1 and f_2.

Lin–Zaidenberg, Liouville and Carathéodory coverings in Riemannian and complex geometry, arXiv:alg-geom/9611020v2 (1997), introduction and definitions/Theorems 1.1, 1.4, 1.6 and Corollary 1.8 were read. Spaces and manifolds are connected in the paper's convention. Ordinary Liouville means constant bounded holomorphic functions; ultra-Liouville means constant bounded plurisubharmonic functions. These notions are distinct. Theorem 1.6: an ultra-Liouville complex base with a hypernilpotent Galois covering group gives a Liouville cover. Abelian groups are within this class. A connected Zariski-open subset of a compact complex analytic space is ultra-Liouville by bounded-PSH extension and the compact maximum principle, subject to the stated analytic hypotheses. Merely assuming an arbitrary noncompact base is Liouville does not allow this replacement. The paper uses the exponential curve as a maximal abelian cover of C minus {0,1}; it is Liouville but not ultra-Liouville. Non-Kähler or non-hypernilpotent generalizations cannot be silently inferred from compact-base examples.

Rempe-Gillen–Sixsmith, Hyperbolic entire functions and the Eremenko–Lyubich class: class B or not class B?, arXiv:1502.00492v2 (11 October 2016), introduction, Theorems/Definitions 1.1–1.5 and Section 2 definitions 2.1–2.2 were read. For an entire map, S(f) is the closure of finite critical and finite asymptotic values. It is the smallest closed target set outside which restriction of f is a covering; critical values alone are insufficient. Theorem 1.2 says expansion under the explicitly specified metric conditions forces bounded S(f). Theorem 1.3 concerns dynamical hyperbolicity/postsingular sets of a one-variable transcendental entire map, not intrinsic Liouville of our hypersurface. For a covering argument, a disk of regular values supports conformal inverse sheets; infinite critical/asymptotic value accumulation can prevent a finite exceptional set from accomplishing this. Polynomial critical values are finite but general entire functions are not restricted to that situation.

## Independent hypotheses to test before candidate reading

Direct boundedness on a plane or affine graph can be settled by ordinary one-variable Liouville applied successively along complex lines. A polynomial summand can suggest a proper finite projection with ramification; symmetric functions of finitely many bounded values might descend to C^2. This requires properness, multiplicity/branch handling and one common constant across all sheets. Being bounded on each fiber separately does not itself prove the values are the same on different sheets. A holomorphic multiplier depending on the remaining variables must stay nonvanishing for a naive polynomial root estimate.

A whole surface need not be an unramified cover of a finite punctured algebraic base. General entire functions can have infinitely many singular values. If a route merely assumes the original hypersurface already has a Liouville or suitable cover property, it transfers the central difficulty and is blocked as a general solution. Unbounded exceptional sets alone do not construct a negative example; they only obstruct that particular proof mechanism. I will distinguish a valid criterion from any universal implication, and test zero/omitted values, ramification, singular points, disconnected regular loci, degree one, and limiting growth assumptions.
