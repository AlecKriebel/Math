## Original claim and success criteria

Hayman and Lingham, *Research Problems in Function Theory*, arXiv:1809.07200v2, printed p.43, Problem 2.55, attributes the problem to J. M. Anderson and L. A. Rubel. Its exact mathematical content is:

For **every** three nonconstant entire functions `f_i:C -> C`, let

`V = {(z1,z2,z3) in C^3 : f1(z1)+f2(z2)+f3(z3)=0}`.

For **every** entire `F:C^3 -> C` for which `sup_V |F| < infinity`, must there exist a single `c in C` such that `F(z)=c` for **all** `z in V`? The decisive original words are "is F necessarily constant on V?"

A full affirmative resolution must cover arbitrary growth, arbitrary critical and asymptotic behavior, smooth and singular loci, omitted values, and any potential disconnected or irreducible-structure issue; constancy on each component alone is insufficient. A negative resolution needs an admissible triple and an ambient entire function whose bounded restriction takes two different values. An intrinsic bounded holomorphic counterexample would additionally need justified extension to an ambient entire function. A special-case proof, necessary obstruction, reduction, computational check, or conditional theorem is not a full answer.

The original update credits Demailly with a very special case, and bibliography item [189] identifies the 1979 Bull. Sci. Math. paper below. I do not infer modern open/closed status merely from the 2018 update.

## Primary-source coverage established before candidate access

1. J.-P. Demailly, *Fonctions holomorphes a croissance polynomiale sur la surface d'equation e^x+e^y=1*, Bull. Sci. Math. 103 (1979), 179-191. Independently downloaded the author-hosted PDF; SHA256 matches the routed hash. This is a scanned PDF: text extraction produces no usable text, so the initial pages, Theorem 2, its conclusion, and relevant addendum were checked as rendered pixels, with private OCR used only as navigation. Theorem 2 at printed p.186 says an intrinsic holomorphic function on `S={e^x+e^y=1}` with growth `|f| <= C(1+|z|)^n`, real `n>=0`, is the restriction of a polynomial of total degree at most `n`. At `n=0` this proves intrinsic bounded-function constancy. Theorem 1's controlled extension has an explicit `(1+|z|)^5(1+|e^x+e^y|)` factor; an arbitrary ambient extension is not automatically controlled. Printed p.180 states that a rational coordinate is an easy positive case for the higher-dimensional separated equation. Printed p.179 attributes irreducibility for `n>=3` nonconstant meromorphic coordinate functions to Rubel, Squires, and Taylor; that theorem's original proof was not independently fetched here, so it is attribution evidence rather than a replacement for the required Liouville proof.

2. Demailly, the C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 288, 8 January 1979, pp.39-40 announcement, independently downloaded and both scanned pages visually read. Corollary 2 on p.40 proves constancy of intrinsic bounded holomorphic functions on `e^x+e^y=f(w)` for nonzero meromorphic `f` on `C^n`. It also gives a polynomial-growth variant under finite-order hypotheses. Thus two exponential coordinates with a third nonconstant entire coordinate are directly within prior theorem coverage, after signs and affine coordinate changes are handled. It is not a theorem about three arbitrary entire coordinate functions.

3. V. Ya. Lin and M. Zaidenberg, *Liouville and Caratheodory Coverings in Riemannian and Complex Geometry*, arXiv:alg-geom/9611020v2. The routed URL is this paper, not a jet-differential or algebraic-hyperbolicity paper. Definition 1.3 establishes that connected Zariski-open subsets of compact complex spaces are ultra-Liouville, using bounded plurisubharmonic extension. Theorem 1.6 establishes Liouville for a hypernilpotent group action **provided** the action is ultra-Liouville; this includes hypernilpotent coverings of an ultra-Liouville base. The stronger base/action hypothesis cannot be replaced by ordinary Liouville, as their introduction and Remarks 1.9 explicitly warn. Their terminology distinguishes Caratheodory separation from Liouville. I read the introduction and section 1 through Theorem 1.6 and its adjacent qualifications.

4. D. J. Sixsmith and L. Rempe-Gillen, *Hyperbolic entire functions and the Eremenko-Lyubich class: Class B or not class B?*, arXiv:1502.00492v2. Section 2, Definition 2.1, uses regular inverse neighborhoods in which each preimage component maps conformally, and Remark 2.2 identifies the singular set as the closure of critical and finite asymptotic values, equivalently the smallest closed removed set giving a covering. This is a covering criterion after removing the whole singular set, not permission to remove an arbitrary finite subset. Their dynamical hyperbolicity and expansion theorems concern entire-function iteration and are not Liouville theorems for the hypersurface `V`.

## Geometric falsification mechanisms fixed before candidate access

* **Quantifier boundary:** with `f3(z)=z`, `V` is a graph biholomorphic to `C^2`. Its intrinsic Liouville property is immediate but its Kobayashi pseudometric is identically zero and its cotangent bundle is trivial. Nonconstant entire coordinate functions therefore do not imply negative curvature, Brody hyperbolicity, ample cotangent, positive jet bundles, or absence of entire curves.
* **Singularity boundary:** `f1=f2=f3=z^2` gives the quadratic cone with a singular vertex. Smooth metric or cotangent arguments must address the singularity and any resolution's exceptional divisor. An exceptional compact rational curve cannot support strictly negative holomorphic curvature or an ample cotangent restriction. Resolving the hypersurface does not prove the needed positivity.
* **Logical boundary:** the unit disk has a hyperbolic metric and a nonconstant bounded holomorphic identity function, whereas Demailly's exponential curve is Liouville. A curvature or hyperbolicity conclusion alone cannot imply the desired bounded-function constancy. Conversely, absence of bounded holomorphic functions does not imply absence of intrinsic complex-geometric hyperbolicity.
* **Algebraicity boundary:** compactification and bounded extension across a divisor can prove constancy on connected affine algebraic varieties. Arbitrary entire coordinate functions need not define an algebraic or quasi-projective hypersurface. No projective positivity theorem may be imported without constructing an appropriate compactification and checking its exact hypotheses.
* **Covering boundary:** arbitrary nonconstant entire maps can have infinitely many singular values. A pullback of a punctured affine plane may fail to be an unramified covering, or may have a nonnilpotent group. Properness or finite fibers cannot be inferred from holomorphicity. A finite cover or ultra-Liouville action needs proof with the actual quantified maps.
* **Degeneration boundary:** exponential level zero `e^x+e^y=0` is the union of lines `y=x+(2k+1)pi i`. A slice-by-slice argument must show how constants on its different components match through nonzero levels and global analytic continuation. Generic smooth fibers do not by themselves control exceptional fibers.
* **Source-credit boundary:** Demailly's two-exponential theorem already includes bounded intrinsic functions; a rederivation or affine restatement is not novel. Lin-Zaidenberg's covering theorem already packages a broad inherited mechanism. A claim of new coverage must identify a precise verified class outside these hypotheses.
