Independent mechanism: bounded nonnegative continuous entire subharmonic functions have spherical means tending to their global supremum. For v with sup M<infinity let m(R) be its normalized spherical mean about0. The standard subharmonic mean inequalities make m(R) nondecreasing and at most M. Harmonic Poisson extension H_R of the continuous sphere values majorizes v on B_R, by the maximum principle. With rho=|x|/R<1, its normalized kernel is bounded above by (1−rho^2)/(1−rho)^n. Thus v(x)<=H_R(x)<=(1−rho^2)/(1−rho)^n m(R). Taking R→infinity shows v(x)<=lim m(R) for every x; taking the supremum forces lim m(R)=M. This uses only ordinary Poisson formula, mean monotonicity and comparison, not a conclusion about asymptotic paths.

Define an open component to be bad if it supports a nonzero bounded nonnegative continuous entire subharmonic function vanishing off that component. Two disjoint components cannot both be bad: normalize two witnesses to supremum1. Their sum is at most1 pointwise because their supports are disjoint, but its spherical averages tend to2, a contradiction. A component that is not bad passes this property to every subdomain, since the same witness would be supported in the larger domain.

For continuous subharmonic u, let O_k={u>k}. On a component A of O_k, paste u−k inside A to0 outside. This is continuous and subharmonic; the boundary values are0, and the standard subharmonic pasting/comparison proof applies even at critical level sets. If u is bounded above on A, it is a nonzero bounded witness, so A is bad. Consequently a non-bad A has u unbounded above. If every positive integer O_k is connected, their nested components already give a chain. Otherwise at a branching level choose a non-bad component (at most one can be bad) and successively choose a higher-level component inside it. Every descendant remains non-bad and has u unbounded above, so this recursion never stops. There is no unsupported finite-branching compactness or arbitrary high-component selection assumption.

Choose x_k in the k-th nested component, with one level of slack if desired. An open connected subset of R^n is polygonally connected; its proof uses the subset reachable by finite polygons being both open and relatively closed. Connect successive points by finitely many segments inside the lower-level component, parameterize that polygon on [k,k+1], and concatenate. On every later piece u>k. For a compact K, u has finite maximum on K, so all sufficiently late pieces miss K. There are only finitely many earlier pieces and each has finitely many segments. Hence the path is continuous, proper and locally finite, and u tends to infinity on its whole tail. Self-intersections are immaterial.

An entire nonconstant harmonic u must be unbounded above: if u<=M, apply Poisson's lower and upper kernel bounds to nonnegative h=M−u. The normalized sphere mean equals h(0). Both bounds tend to1 as R→infinity, giving h(x)=h(0) for every x, a contradiction. This derives one-sided Liouville without assuming the desired path theorem.

Boundary controls: constant harmonic functions are excluded. The source's bounded subharmonic example max(−1,−r^(2−n)), with value−1 at0, is continuous and bounded above by0; it does not satisfy the unboundedness hypothesis. Both radial pieces are harmonic/constant away from r=1; outward derivative jumps from0 to n−2>0, giving a positive distributional surface Laplacian. It is subharmonic and cannot have u→+infinity on any path. The root mean/component mechanism works for continuous subharmonic functions unbounded above, and therefore covers n>=3 harmonic functions. Claims about discontinuous approximation, arbitrary prescribed rays, monotonicity, rates or injectivity will not be inferred.
