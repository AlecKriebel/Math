## 1. All weights and all numbers of multipartite parts

At fixed second moment q, minimizing S4 on each finite simplex section is
legitimate by compactness. Zeros must first be removed; no stationarity claim is
made at the simplex boundary. For a nonuniform positive support, the constraint
gradients are independent. Stationarity gives 4z³-2lambda z-mu=0, whose missing
quadratic coefficient rules out three distinct positive roots. This does not by
itself distinguish the two branches. The decisive second-order test uses the
tangent vector (1,-1) on two small equal coordinates t. The constrained second
variation is 8(t-u)(2t+u)<0 when the larger coordinate is u. A feasible smooth
curve exists because the two gradients have rank two. Thus a small value can occur
only once. The remaining constraints force exactly r large masses and one small
mass, with 1/(r+1)<q<1/r and r=floor(1/q). The proposed vector fits every feasible
dimension by Cauchy–Schwarz. Reciprocal q is handled separately by Hölder, with
equality only for equal positive masses. This proves the value and classification
for every finite dimension, rather than merely identifying a stationary point.

For countable support summing to one, merge a tail of mass tau into one coordinate.
Its second- and fourth-moment errors are bounded by tau² and tau⁴ respectively,
and tau→0. Apply the finite theorem and the continuity of L to obtain S4≥L(q).
This establishes the use of that bound in countable joins. Sequences whose part
counts grow without bound are separately controlled by the finite count identity:
24N/n⁴=3(S2²-S4)+6(S3-S2)/n+3(1-S2)/n².
The error bound is uniform in part count. At q→0, L≤q²→0; at reciprocal
junctions both adjacent parameterizations give the same fourth moment q³.

## 2. Join replacement, triangle stability and nonunique ties

the two children supplies a nonedge. All other crossing occupancies force a

on each four-vertex outcome, the C4 indicator is at most half the number of present
perfect matchings. Three matching probabilities each equal p², even for an

bipartite kernel preserves that density and attains this bound. The global

comparison without assuming finite support.
