## Direct converse for infinite sigma-finite P

This avoids relying on a source theorem with finite-total-law hypotheses. Assume canonical Mecke on countable discrete G. Its root-zero test at each displacement d shows integral 1_{alpha0=0} alpha{d} dP=0. Countability and nonzero alpha imply P(alpha0=0)=0.

Let K be any invariant alpha-preserving Markov transport, including a Poisson-averaged count-only one. For alpha0>0, preservation implies alpha{d}=0 => K(alpha,0,{d})=0. Define the nonnegative Mecke test

    g(alpha,d)= f(alpha) K(alpha,d,{0})/alpha{0}

on alpha0>0, with zero branch elsewhere. Covariance gives

    g(theta_d alpha,-d)
      = f(theta_d alpha) K(alpha,0,{d})/alpha{d}

on alpha{d}>0. Multiplying by alpha{d} and using the zero-flux observation on the remaining displacements recovers the transported expectation. The unreversed integral is

    integral P(dalpha) f(alpha)
      [sum_d alpha{d} K(alpha,d,{0})]/alpha{0}
      = integral f dP.

Every integral is nonnegative; no finite total P or infinity subtraction is required. Averaged K preserves alpha by ordinary conditional Poisson Campbell-Mecke, covariance and counting-mass preservation (the same elementary identities as final 2011 (4.8)-(4.9)). Thus the discrete statement is an equivalence even for infinite sigma-finite canonical P.
