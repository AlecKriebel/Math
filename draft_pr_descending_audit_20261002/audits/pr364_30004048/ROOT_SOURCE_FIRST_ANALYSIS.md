The exact hypothesis to audit is that the universal finite-unweighted biconstrained guarantee ψ is not symmetric. Every A vertex has ≥x|B| neighbours in B, every B vertex has ≥x|A| neighbours in A and ≥y|C| neighbours in C, every C vertex has ≥y|B| neighbours in B. A,B,C are finite nonempty stable parts; no A–C edges. Output is max over C of its A two-step reach fraction. ψ is the infimum of these outputs over all feasible graphs. The guaranteed maximum is attained as a guarantee (published1.7's rounding argument), which does not establish an extremizing finite graph. A finite graph alone gives an upper bound, and unequal outputs on graph reversal do not by themselves refute universal ψ symmetry.

My mechanism to test is exact complementary neighbourhood rigidity on x+y=1. If a∈A and c∈C have no two-edge path, their B neighbourhoods are disjoint. With their lower bounds summing to |B|, both degree bounds are equalities and the two neighbourhoods are exact complements. For x+y>1 every pair is reachable, so ψ=1; any proposed boundary formula must distinguish strict inequality. On the rational boundary, integer rounding matters: if x|B| is not integral the two lower bounds sum to at least |B|+1, so every pair is again reachable. A nontrivial rational-boundary witness must therefore allow part sizes divisible by the denominator.

With positive weighted B vertices, the same complement identity follows from equality of mass. Such identities can identify twins, and missing A–C blocks can be collected by common B neighbourhood types. A potential rigorous route reduces the original graph to finite weighted support types with three separately normalized mass vectors; however, both reciprocal degree constraints must survive reduction. The B weight vector is coupled to both sides; separate LP reweightings cannot be presumed compatible. Zero-weight B intermediates can create purely graph-theoretic paths despite carrying no mass: they must be deleted before computing a weighted model's intended support paths, or an exact blowup must account for them. An arbitrary deletion can also change two-step reach, so the direction of any inequality must be proved.

For fixed finite support and rational x,y, feasibility and reach bounds form a rational polytope, and rational feasible weights can be chosen without changing exact equality constraints. Positive rational weights admit a common-denominator complete bipartite blowup, preserving all four degree bounds and the Boolean support reach relation. This supplies finite-unweighted witnesses. It does not assert uniform finite support bounds, global infimum attainment, or irrational-boundary rational approximation.
