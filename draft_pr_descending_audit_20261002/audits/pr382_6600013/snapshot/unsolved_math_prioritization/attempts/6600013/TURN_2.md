# Turn 2: a fully aperiodic low-complexity example with unbounded approximant ranks

The source question remains unresolved. This turn constructs a repetitive, fully aperiodic two-dimensional symbolic tiling with O(n²) complexity and finite rational hull cohomology, but whose standard rectangular approximants have unbounded Betti numbers and Euler characteristics of both signs. It is an obstruction to a naive approximant-counting proof, not a counterexample to the original conclusion.

## 1. Rectangular pattern complexes

Let X be a minimal Z² subshift over a finite alphabet. Write P(n,m) for its number of n-column, m-row patterns. Form a finite square complex K_(n,m) with

    vertices: n by m patterns,
    horizontal edges: (n+1) by m patterns,
    vertical edges: n by(m+1) patterns,
    faces: (n+1) by(m+1) patterns.

Edges attach to their prefix/suffix rectangles. A face attaches to the four rectangles obtained by deleting its first or last row/column. Labels distinguish open cells; boundary identifications may identify faces or endpoints and do not require the complex to be regular. These are finite CW complexes. Their1-skeleton is connected, because patterns in a minimal language can be joined by successive unit shifts of an occurrence in one configuration.

Nested centered rectangles give cellular forgetful maps. Their inverse limit is the suspension hull: compatible patches reconstruct a configuration, and the two interval coordinates reconstruct its position in the unit square, with the boundary identifications exactly matching translations across edges. The same compact-to-Hausdorff argument as for the Rauzy graphs proves the identification. Thus rational Čech cohomology is the direct limit of the cohomology of these approximants.

Their Euler characteristic is exactly

    chi(K_(n,m))=P(n,m)−P(n+1,m)−P(n,m+1)+P(n+1,m+1).    (1.1)

Since they are connected and two-dimensional,

    chi=1−beta_1+beta_2.                                  (1.2)

There is no reason for the individual Betti numbers to be controlled by a small alternating sum.

## 2. The one-dimensional building blocks

Let t be the Thue–Morse system generated by the primitive substitution

    mu(0)=01, mu(1)=10.

Primitivity gives a minimal two-sided subshift with the substitution language. More explicitly, any allowed word appears in some mu^k(0), and every sufficiently high substituted letter contains that block; this gives bounded gaps for each fixed word. Let p_t(n) be its complexity.

The system has no000 or111: every three consecutive output symbols contain a complete substituted pair, whose two symbols differ. It also has no alternating word of length5. Such a word, when aligned or misaligned with the substituted pairs, would decode to000 or111. Consequently every factor of length at least5 has a double letter, which determines the parity of its substitution alignment, since doubles occur only across pair boundaries.

For n>=3, a length2n factor aligned with the substitution blocks corresponds bijectively to a length-n preimage factor; a misaligned one corresponds bijectively to a length-(n+1) factor, because its two partial boundary symbols determine their preimage letters. These alignment classes are disjoint. Similarly, each alignment class of a length-(2n+1) factor corresponds bijectively to length n+1. Hence

    p_t(2n)=p_t(n)+p_t(n+1), n>=3,
    p_t(2n+1)=2p_t(n+1), n>=2.                            (2.1)

Direct small-word enumeration gives p_t(1),...,p_t(6)=2,4,6,10,12,16; in particular the first formula also holds at n=2. If s(n)=p_t(n+1)−p_t(n), then

    s(2n)=s(n),  s(2n+1)=s(n+1), n>=2,
    s(1)=s(2)=2, s(3)=4.                                 (2.2)

It follows inductively that s(n) is always2 or4, so p_t(n)<=4n and complexity is unbounded, proving aperiodicity. Moreover, for every integer a>=2,

    s(2^a)=2,             s(2^a+1)=4,
    s(3*2^(a−1))=4,       s(3*2^(a−1)+1)=2.               (2.3)

These follow by repeatedly halving in(2.2), ending at the displayed small cases. The construction uses only these recurrences, not a recalled closed formula for Thue–Morse complexity.

Let y be a Sturmian system, with complexity p_y(m)=m+1. Both systems are minimal and aperiodic, and Turn1 gives finite rational cohomology of each suspension from their linear complexity.

## 3. The sheared, recoded two-dimensional configuration

At site(i,j) put the symbol

    z_(i,j)=(t_(i+j), t_(i+j+1), y_j).                    (3.1)

Both t and y range over their full minimal hulls. The pair of consecutive t symbols is an invertible two-block recoding; the independent translation(i,j) acts on(t,y) by(shift^(i+j)t, shift^j y). The integer change of coordinates(i,j)→(i+j,j) is invertible with determinant1. Therefore this Z² system is minimal, has no nonzero period, and its unit-square tiling is repetitive, FLC and fully aperiodic.

An n by m pattern reads exactly a Thue–Morse word of length n+m and a Sturmian word of length m. Every pair occurs, since these are independent coordinates of the product hull under the invertible integer action. Conversely the pattern recovers both words: the first two components cover every diagonal index and its successor, and the last component gives the row word. Consequently

    P(n,m)=p_t(n+m)(m+1),
    P(n,n)<=8n(n+1)=O(n²).                               (3.2)

The suspension of the sheared action is homeomorphic to the ordinary product of the two one-dimensional suspensions: on the R² suspension coordinate apply the same invertible integer linear map, which respects both lattice identifications. The two-block recoding is a shift conjugacy. Hence the full rational Čech cohomology of this tiling hull is finite-dimensional by Turn1 and rational Künneth. No integral finite-generation assertion is used.

## 4. Unbounded Euler characteristics of both signs

Set t=n+m in(3.2). The mixed difference in(1.1) becomes

    chi(K_(n,m))=(m+2)[s(t+1)−s(t)]+s(t).                 (4.1)

At equal side lengths n=m, use(2.3). Along n=2^(a−1),

    chi(K_(n,n))=2n+6.

Along n=3*2^(a−2),

    chi(K_(n,n))=−2n.

Both subsequences tend to infinity in absolute value. By(1.2), the first forces beta_2(K_(n,n))>=2n+5, and the second forces beta_1(K_(n,n))>=2n+1. Thus the standard approximants have unbounded Betti numbers in both positive degrees, even though the limiting rational cohomology is finite-dimensional and the tiling satisfies the source's low-complexity hypotheses.

This demonstrates two concrete limits of a prospective proof strategy: O(n²) patch complexity does not bound these mixed differences uniformly, and large approximant Betti numbers need not survive to the inverse-limit hull. It does not show that every cofinal subsequence of approximants has large rank, nor rule out a different uniformly bounded model.

## 5. Exact controls and remaining gap

verify_turn2.py obtains complete Thue–Morse finite languages as follows. Choose k with2^k>=n−1. Every length-n word is contained in mu^k(ab) for an allowed adjacent pair ab, and all four binary pairs are allowed. Conversely each such substituted pair occurs in the language. Thus taking factors of those four finite words is exhaustive, rather than sampling a long prefix. The checker verifies(2.1)–(2.3), explicitly builds small rectangular pattern languages from(3.1), and checks the signed cellular boundary identity and Euler counts.

The current example has finite limiting cohomology. A genuine negative answer would require persistent independent classes in a fully aperiodic repetitive low-complexity hull, not merely the unbounded approximant classes displayed here. A positive answer would need a survival or alternate-model argument beyond raw cell and Euler counts. Neither is supplied in this turn.
