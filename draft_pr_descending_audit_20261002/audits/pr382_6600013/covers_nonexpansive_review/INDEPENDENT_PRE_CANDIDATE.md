# Independent pre-candidate reconstruction

Sealed before reading TURN_4, TURN_5, RESULT, candidate code, clarification, or the old adversarial verdict. The assignment disclosed the intended finite-cyclic Betti tuple and 2-adic theme only.

Source reconstructed: Problem 2.5.1, arXiv 1604.06280v2 printed p8, asks whether an aperiodic repetitive d-dimensional tiling with patch complexity O(n^d) has finite total rational cohomology rank. Julien 2017 printed pp545-547 uses finitely many translation prototiles, aperiodicity meaning no nonzero translation period, translational hull, and FLC meaning finitely many fixed-radius patches up to translation. Repetitivity and FLC give compact minimal translation action. His pp550-552 compare pointing/norm conventions by scale and multiplicative constants; they do not preserve a numerical complexity coefficient. Julien arXiv0804.0145v1 Section5 defines the unit-roof suspension of a finite-alphabet subshift, Rauzy inverse limits, and rational Cech cohomology; Lemma5.14/Proposition5.16 prove the one-dimensional implication. The older cut/project terminology permits partial periods elsewhere in that paper; it must not weaken the target's later aperiodicity convention.

Independent mechanisms to test:

1. Finite local extension: a continuous finite-valued cocycle on a compact zero-dimensional finite-alphabet subshift factors through a finite patch collar. Its skew-product has a finite alphabet consisting of old tile symbol and fiber state. Thus its box complexity is at most q times the base complexity at a bounded larger radius (assuming a q-element fiber), FLC follows, and aperiodicity passes from the base. Repetitivity requires the extension action to be minimal; minimal base alone is insufficient. A countercontrol is the zero cocycle to a nontrivial finite fiber, giving q invariant components.

2. Actual cochains: a q-sheet covering of a finite CW approximant has q times each cell count, and the boundary incidences must be lifted according to actual edge monodromy. A Betti formula cannot be inferred from q-sheet degree alone. Connected covers give H0=Q. Transfer makes pullback rationally injective, but does not bound the covering cohomology by q times the base Betti numbers. Example: a connected q-cover of a two-loop rose has first Betti q+1 although the base has Betti2.

3. Metric distinction: for positive continuous roof r on a Cantor Z-system, [x,s] with 0<=s<=r(x) maps to [x,s/r(x)] in the unit roof. This is a homeomorphism, compatible with endpoint identifications. It changes speed along each orbit. Equality of underlying spaces or Cech cohomology does not by itself give a conjugacy that preserves time. For a substitution graph computation one must check that the chosen graph is an actual inverse-limit model of the symbolic suspension (recognizability and border control, or a proper substitution construction) before accepting its direct-limit cohomology.

4. Infinite 2-adic fiber: every continuous map from a compact product X0 x Z2 to a finite discrete alphabet is constant on sufficiently small 2-adic congruence classes uniformly in X0. To prove it, cover by finitely many products U_i x (z_i+2^k_i Z2) on which the map is constant, and take k=max k_i. For a skew action in which the same continuous finite-fiber translation is added to both z and z+2^k, the entire itineraries coincide. Distinct points with identical itineraries exclude a continuous finite-alphabet generator and an equivariant realization as a finite-alphabet Zd subshift. Equivalently, arbitrarily close distinct pairs with equal base point and 2-adic difference are uniformly close under all Zd iterates, proving nonexpansiveness in a standard product metric. This is a statement about the specified discrete action and symbolic generator; it is not by itself an assertion excluding every possible nonequivariant homeomorphism to every FLC tiling space.

5. Infinite rank: inverse limit of finite covers gives a direct limit in Cech cohomology; proof of infinite rational rank requires injective bonding pullbacks and an unbounded sequence of independently computed finite-cover Betti ranks. An abstract inverse limit or repeated fiber degree alone is insufficient. Finite alphabet observations that forget higher digits cannot establish target patch complexity of the whole infinite-fiber system.
