## Turn 1: product theorem and sharp coefficient

Minimality makes the Rauzy graph connected. Its edge-minus-vertex rank is p(n+1)-p(n)+1. Alternating centered window forgetful maps retain the unit-edge parameter; interiors and boundary vertices both reconstruct the symbolic suspension from growing compatible words. Čech continuity gives the direct limit. A cofinal subsequence of stage ranks at most M bounds the limit rank by M, with no injectivity assumption. Conversely, finitely many independent limit classes remain independent at every later stage after their representatives first coexist.

For finite C = liminf p(n)/n, failure of infinitely many increments <= floor(C) would force the ratio to have liminf at least floor(C)+1. Thus r <= floor(C)+1. Persistent r independent classes force eventual increments >= r-1 and hence p(n) >= (r-1)n-B. The argument works for any finite collection of independent classes even when the ultimate rank is infinite.

The product action consists of independent coordinate shifts; its suspension is the product of the one-dimensional suspensions. Repetitivity follows from bounded coordinate word returns. A period must preserve the unit-grid boundaries and then have integer coordinates, each forbidden by the corresponding aperiodic word. The box language has exactly the product of factor complexities. Morse–Hedlund supplies p_i(n) >= n+1, so any common subsequence bounding P(n)/n^d bounds all p_i(n)/n, establishing each finite r_i. Rational Künneth is applied at finite graph stages and passed through filtered vector-space limits; no local contractibility of the hull is assumed.

With a_i = max(1,r_i-1), eventual lower bounds imply product a_i <= C. The integer inequality 1+r_i <= 3a_i proves total rank <= 3^d C. Sturmian systems attain r=2: the constant and letter-indicator classes integrate to 1 and an irrational frequency; invariant edge frequencies kill graph coboundaries and commute with bonding maps, excluding every nonzero rational relation. The d-fold product therefore has total rank 3^d and coefficient C=1. This is a sharp subclass theorem, not a general decomposition theorem.
