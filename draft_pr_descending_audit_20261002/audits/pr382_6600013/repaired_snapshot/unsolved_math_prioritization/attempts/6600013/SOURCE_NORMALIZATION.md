# Exact source, coefficients and literature qualification

The target is A. Julien's Problem2.5.1 in the2016 quasicrystal problem collection, arXiv page8 (published journal page587). Its conclusion is finite total rank of **rational Čech cohomology** of the **translational hull**, under aperiodicity, repetitivity and patch complexity O(n^d). Aperiodic here means no nonzero translational period, as in Julien2017 Section2. It does not mean merely that the period group has rank less than d. Rotation hulls, singular cohomology and integral finite generation are different questions.

Complexity counts finite patches up to translation, with the standard local patch/pointing convention. The polynomial bound implies finite local complexity in this setting. Changing fixed norms or bounded collar conventions changes scale/constants, not the exponent. Explicit symbolic subclasses below use n by ... by n box languages; any quantitative coefficient is for that stated convention, while the big-O exponent agrees with the usual radius convention.

The original source already records the affirmative one-dimensional implication and the canonical cut-and-project equivalence. It also records counterexamples to the converse and to integral finite generation. None resolves the stated higher-dimensional direction. The available Julien arXiv0804.0145 is version1, with different numbering: its Rauzy inverse-limit theorem is5.10 and its linear-complexity proposition is5.16, corresponding to the dimension-one result cited as Proposition6.7 in the published2010 paper. That renumbering is kept explicit.

A later primary correction matters for the background: Koivusalo–Walton, ETDS41 (2021),1431–1463, published online2020, shows that the almost-canonical condition alone does not justify identifying acceptance domains with hyperplane cut regions. Its quasicanonical condition supplies the missing identification; Example4.3 confirms that canonical schemes satisfy it, and Section8 explains the topological issue. Therefore this packet does not invoke an unrestricted almost-canonical cohomology equivalence. The source's narrower canonical background remains distinguished from that extension. The correction is credited published work, not a new error allegation.
