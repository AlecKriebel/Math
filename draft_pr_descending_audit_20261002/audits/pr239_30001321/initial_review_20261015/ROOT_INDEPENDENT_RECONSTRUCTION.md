## Exact hypothesis and success criterion

Fix one iid space-time law of transition probabilities with 0<p<1 almost surely. Let q=E p, a=q(1-q), delta=E[p(1-p)]>0 and v=2q-1. At time s and transverse site x the walk moves to x+1 with probability p_(s,x), otherwise x-1. The claimed result is superpolynomial environmental concentration of the L1 difference between quenched and averaged laws after aggregation into every deterministic equal-interval partition whose lengths M_N tend to infinity. The alignment may vary deterministically with N. For each epsilon,K the eventual bound is C_(epsilon,K)N^-K; the eventual threshold may depend on the scale sequence. The law is fixed, with no uniform constant as delta tends to zero. Real geometric interval lengths are addressed separately in PROOF section 6. No variable-range or time-independent environment is asserted.

Success requires (i) the analytic proof of this exact theorem, with all earlier-turn inputs proved rather than imported from a broader theorem; (ii) agreement with the actual published target; (iii) fresh adversarial checks and reproducible finite controls. These do not establish historical novelty. Falsifiable checks include the replica transition law, parity-sensitive gradients, conditional occupation iteration, entropy/exponent margins, killed-kernel locality under changing one entire strip, and wrong-boundary negative controls.

## Earlier-turn inputs reconstructed

Two conditionally independent replicas in a common environment give D=(X-Y)/2. Away from zero its steps +/-1 have probability a and at zero probability delta; holding is the remainder. The reversible measure is pi(0)=a/delta and pi(d)=1 elsewhere. Because each holding probability is at least 1/2 the reversible operator has spectrum in [0,1], so its return probabilities are nonincreasing. The stated resolvent bound at z=n/(n+1), combined with monotonicity, gives g_n <= C/sqrt(n+1). This does not require inverse moments of p.

The backward profile u_n(d)=P_d(D_n=0) is radial and decreases with |d|: its successive differences evolve with nonnegative coefficients, including the boundary coefficient 1-2delta-a >=0. Thus conditional future overlap is at most g_t for every old same-parity starting law. Expanding powers of the occupation sum, ordering the time indices and conditioning successively gives k! C^k L^(k/2). Repeated indices are harmless because 0<=I_s<=1. The high-moment order may depend on the requested polynomial exponent; there is no asserted uniformity in that order.

Exposing each independent time layer gives the displayed exact martingale increment, not an informal independent-endpoint argument. Independence within the layer makes the predictable variance a sum of mu_s(x)^2 times squared semigroup gradients. The biased binomial law lives on a parity sublattice, so differences must be by two lattice sites. Telescoping and the maximal point-mass bound give gradient bounds C/(t+1) and total absolute gradient C/sqrt(t+1). Summing interval-gradient squares yields the scale-sensitive bound in TURN_1. Restricting to a diffusive window and applying Cauchy-Schwarz gives (F); fixed large R followed by n,M to infinity makes rho small even for nonmonotone and arbitrarily slow divergent scales.

The exponential martingale inequality is applied on the joint event {Q<=w}; conditioning on a future good-occupation event would be invalid and is not needed. In TURN_2 fix gamma in (1/4,1/2), beta=(gamma+1/4)/2 and eta=(gamma-1/4)/4. With ell=N^(2beta)/log^2 N, dyadic occupation controls give Q <= C N^eta/sqrt(ell). The exponent for smoothed sign tests dominates the entropy of O(N^(1/2-gamma)log N) central cells because beta-eta-(1/2-gamma)=5(gamma-1/4)/4>0. The Lipschitz boundary test has variance margin 2beta-1/2-eta=3(gamma-1/4)/4>0. The final displacement error is exp(-c log^2 N). These facts give the auxiliary-scale theorem (G); they alone do not settle arbitrary M_N.

## Final-segment closure reconstructed

Use ell=floor(N^3/4), H=ceil(N^5/16), r=N^3/8, R=ceil(N^3/8 log N), eta=kappa=1/32 and m=N-ell. Deterministic interval majorants of radius r have annealed mean O(N^-1/8 log N). On the common occupation event their martingale variance is O(N^-7/32), with increments O(N^-3/8). An upper deviation N^-1/32 therefore has exponent cN^5/32. A deterministic O(N) collection of intervals of length O(R) consequently has maximum old mass at most N^-1/32 outside a superpolynomial exceptional event.

For two same-parity starts, define a quenched coupling using independent coins until meeting and common coins thereafter. They cannot cross without meeting. Before meeting, the two distinct fresh environment variables average independently, so the half-gap is the homogeneous symmetric lazy walk killed at zero. Reflection, retaining holding steps, gives survival sum from 1-d to d and hence C d/sqrt(ell). This proves the expected L1 coupling bound; no independence is claimed conditional on the fixed environment.

Replace the old law inside each H-cell by the conditional annealed old law while retaining the cell mass. All reachable same-parity sites have positive annealed mass. Convexity of the coupling bound costs CH/sqrt(ell), and the remaining mass discrepancy is exactly L_(m,H). Contracting through the kernel and using the all-scale expectation bound on a translated future walk yields E[F|F_m] <= L_(m,H)+CH/sqrt(ell)+rho(ell,M_N). The translated partition remains deterministic. Here H/sqrt(ell)=O(N^-1/16); only the separate auxiliary scale H uses (G).

Kill a last-segment path at its first all-times departure from the drift tube centered at x+vt with radius R. Under averaging, the increments of the single walk are iid, since every time uses a fresh layer. The maximal exponential inequality bounds expected lost mass by exp(-c log^2 N), uniformly in the old law. This must be a maximal path estimate, not just an endpoint tail.

The environment strips kR <= y-vt < (k+1)R are deterministic even for irrational drift and are disjoint independent inputs. A live path started at x uses only sites with y-vt in [x-R,x+R]. Changing a strip cannot affect a killed transition kernel for a start outside its influence interval: couple identical coins until the first changed used site; a newly dead path does not read its dead-site environment. This argument remains valid when changing a strip also changes which paths die. Only O(N) strips can affect starts in [-m,m]. The enlarged influence intervals have overlap at most six, so sum w_k^2 <=6 max w_k. Replacing one whole strip changes the killed output by at most 2w_k in L1. Conditional independent-input bounded differences now has exponent c t^2/max w_k, hence c_t N^1/32 on the old-mass good event.

The original kernel dominates the killed one, so the norm error is at most lost mass D, with E[D|F_m] <= d_N and P(D>t|F_m) <= d_N/t. Combine these inequalities with the small conditional mean, (G), and the old interval-mass event. For any fixed epsilon the resulting exceptional probability is O(N^-K)+exp(-c_epsilon N^1/32)+O_epsilon(exp(-c log^2 N)). This is the claimed conclusion for every K once rho(ell,M_N) is sufficiently small.
