## Exact source and scope

Section 1 opens: “Let k be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic.” The field convention is not replaced in Section 2 or Future Work. Thus characteristic p is part of the source target.

Definition 1.1: for S=k[x,y,z,w], I^(m)=S intersect (intersection over P in Ass(I) of I^m S_P). For the reduced union L of distinct projective lines, I=intersection I(line), and each line ideal is generated by two independent linear forms. Its ordinary powers are primary, so I^(2)=intersection I(line)^2. Definition 1.2 fattening begins with a reduced subscheme; it is the *double scheme*, not L itself, that is nonreduced. Page 2 defines alpha(I)=min{d:I_d nonzero} for a nonzero homogeneous ideal. Type (d-t,d) means alpha(L)=d-t and alpha(2L)=d.

Question 3.1, physical/printed page 8, asks whether there exists a line configuration of type (d-1,d) that is not ACM. This continues Theorem 2.13's finite union of lines and d>1. The theorem already establishes that an ACM such union is coplanar or a pseudostar, and either of those configurations has the specified type. Pseudostar means pairwise intersections of finitely many planes with no three containing a line; multiple planes may meet at a point. The target is a nonempty finite reduced union in P3, with arbitrary intersections, concurrency, disconnection or skewness allowed before imposing delta=1. There is no source permission to replace this by arbitrary nonreduced thickened lines. Repeated names for the same line do not change its reduced union. An empty union has I=S, alpha(I)=alpha(I^(2))=0 (under the displayed definition), hence difference 0, so cannot meet the target. The ideal of all P3 is zero and alpha undefined, and also is not a line configuration. No missing empty-case counterexample exists.

Question 3.2, page 9, is a *different* question: which reduced, possibly irreducible curves in P3 have type (d-1,d). It cannot be silently substituted for Question 3.1. A theorem about all reduced curves could imply the line statement, but every extra assertion would need independent proof.

## Independent deductions and boundaries

For two skew coordinate lines I=(x,y) intersect (z,w), alpha(I)=2 and alpha(I^(2))=4 by disjoint-variable multidegrees. This is an excluded boundary with delta=2 in every characteristic. One line or any nonempty coplanar finite union has alpha=1, alpha(symbolic square)=2 from the containing plane squared and absence of a degree-one form in a line-ideal square. This establishes target sanity, not general classification. In characteristic p, all zero partial derivatives imply F=G^p over the perfect field k; then every reduced line in V(F) lies in V(G), and minimal-degree constraints may rule out F. The ordinary derivative argument must explicitly handle this case. None of these deductions implies the negative answer to Question 3.1.

Success for a positive answer: exhibit a reduced line union, prove non-ACM and delta=1 over a specified algebraically closed characteristic allowed by the source. Success for a negative answer: establish ACM for *every* such union in every characteristic, or explicitly label a restricted result and leave the missing scope open. A proof that transfers the key obstruction to an unsupported surface/Albanese lemma remains blocked. Finite searches cannot certify the universal theorem.

## Frozen strategy before proof exposure

Family C1: Frobenius/derivative degeneration. Inspect every differentiation, Euler division and extraction of p-th roots; try p=2,3 and degrees divisible by p. Explicitly test inseparable hypersurfaces and whether minimal degree actually eliminates them.

Family C2: singular and nonnormal surfaces. Seek cone, cuspidal normalization, wild purely inseparable cover, conductor and exceptional-curve failures. A general hyperplane may avoid isolated singularities but intersects a conductor curve; regularity/integrality claims must distinguish normalization from the original section. Test the claimed genus comparison with an explicit cuspidal cone rather than generic word-level objections.

Family C3: Picard/Albanese positive-characteristic obstruction. Nonreduced Pic^0 can give h^1(O)>dim Alb even on smooth surfaces. A rational curve forces a map to an abelian variety to be constant, but does not automatically kill infinitesimal Picard directions or all H^1. Determine whether the author proof actually uses this false equality, or has a characteristic-free alternative.

Family C4: Bertini and resolution. Separate generic hyperplane integralness, geometric integralness, smoothness on the smooth locus, regular sections of a resolved surface, and inseparable maps. Surface resolution is a different theorem from resolution in arbitrary dimension. Verify exact cited primary assertions and hypotheses before using them. Check whether the linear system on the resolution is ample versus merely basepoint free and big, and whether any cohomology/vanishing invoked relies on characteristic zero.

Family C5: matrix small-characteristic interface. Hilbert–Burch itself is characteristic free over the polynomial ring, but a determinant/sign/alternating-matrix inference may degenerate at p=2. Inspect only interfaces to the geometric argument rather than duplicate the algebra-control family's work.
