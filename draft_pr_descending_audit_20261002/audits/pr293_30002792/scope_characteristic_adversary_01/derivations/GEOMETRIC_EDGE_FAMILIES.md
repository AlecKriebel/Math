# Geometric falsification families and exact deductions

These are independent constructions after reading PUBLIC_TURN_2. They are neither inherited test outputs nor a finite-search proof of the target. Mathematical statements below have explicit derivations; the separate program checks some finite instances and negative controls.

## 1. A nonnormal cuspidal cubic cone, including characteristics 2 and 3

Let S=V(y^2 z-x^3) in P3_[x:y:z:w]. The polynomial is irreducible: on z=1 its coordinate ring is k[t^2,t^3,w], with x=t^2 and y=t^3, a domain of dimension two, and the homogeneous polynomial has no extra component z=0. Its reduced singular locus is the line x=y=0, in all characteristics. In characteristic 2, its nonzero partials are -x^2 and y^2; in characteristic 3 they are 2yz and y^2; the equation itself in each case still forces x=y=0. On z=1, normalization is k[t,w], and the conductor is (t^2,t^3)=(x,y) as an ideal in the original ring, by the numerical semigroup <2,3>. Thus x is a degree e-2=1 conductor section and an adjoint polynomial for its singular line.

The projective normalization has a vertex, and resolving it gives the Hirzebruch surface F3. Its exceptional negative section E has E^2=-3, a fiber T has E.T=1 and T^2=0; H=E+3T, H^2=3 and H.E=0. This is the ruled surface obtained by resolving the cone over the normalization P1 of the cubic; the pullback of the degree-three hyperplane bundle is O_P1(3). K=-2E-5T, so K+2H=T and h0(K+2H)=2. A plane avoiding the vertex cuts a cuspidal cubic downstairs but a section C~H upstairs is P1. Hence pa(C)=q=0 and the proof's RR expression is 3+0-1-0=2. A general E downstairs is *not* regular where it meets the singular line; that does not contradict the submitted proof, which explicitly allows this and applies Lemma A only upstairs. The example also tests H nef rather than ample: H.E=0.

## 2. Inseparable normalization on a conductor in every characteristic p

For p=char(k)>0 let S_p=V(x^p z-y^p w), degree e=p+1. It is irreducible: considered as a degree-one polynomial in z over the UFD k[x,y,w], its coefficients x^p and -y^p w are coprime, so Gauss's lemma applies. The partials with respect to x,y are zero; those with respect to z,w are x^p,-y^p. Thus its reduced singular locus is L=V(x,y). On w=1 put t=y/x in the function field. Then A=k[x,xt,t^p] inside B=k[x,t], where y=xt and z=t^p. B is finite over A (t satisfies T^p-z), birational (t=y/x) and regular, hence is its normalization. Along L the reduced normalization map is t -> t^p, purely inseparable of degree p. This deliberately violates any unstated separability along the conductor.

An A-monomial x^a t^b is present exactly when b mod p <= a, because the exponent semigroup is generated by (1,0),(1,1),(0,p). Such a monomial belongs to the conductor exactly when a>=p-1: multiplying by t^j for 0<=j<p cycles through every residue, so a<p-1 fails for some j. Therefore the conductor in B is x^(p-1)B, and in A is (x,y)^(p-1). In particular the polynomial x^(p-1) of degree e-2=p-1 is a conductor section and vanishes on L.

The normalization is globally the ruled surface F_(p-1)=P(O_P1(1) directsum O_P1(p)). In fiber notation the map is [x:y:z:w]=[a s:a t:b t^p:b s^p]. With the transition weights of O(1) and O(p), all four sections belong to the tautological line bundle. There is no base point. It is birational where a and s are nonzero, since t/s=y/x. Fibers are finite: off a=0 the base is determined by [x:y]; on a=0 the p-th-power map determines the unique reduced base point. Proper plus quasi-finite gives finiteness. A smooth finite birational domain is the normalization. In the standard negative-section/fiber basis E,T, H=E+pT, E^2=-(p-1), H^2=p+1 and K=-2E-(p+1)T. Thus K+2H=(p-1)T, with exactly p sections and chi=p. This equals e+2pa(C)-1-q with q=pa(C)=0.

A plane with coefficients [A:B:C:D] pulls back to a(As+Bt)+b(Ct^p+Ds^p). Since k is perfect, Ct^p+Ds^p is the p-th power of a linear form. For the nonempty open set where As+Bt and that linear form have distinct zeros, their fiberwise linear equation has no common vanishing base point, so the section is an integral P1. At the conductor its intersection may have multiplicity p; this is an intersection-point multiplicity, not a curve component multiplicity. Affinely the plane x+z=0 pulls back to x+t^p=0, a smooth graph, while downstairs it is the integral cusp y^p+x^(p+1)=0. This checks the key distinction in Lemma B at a purely inseparable conductor.

Negative control: the special plane z=0 gives y^p w=0 downstairs, and t^p=0 on the w=1 normalized chart. Its pullback is nonreduced on that chart. Thus genericity is necessary; the proof does not claim this for all planes. The infinitesimal thickening detected here does not furnish a target configuration, which is reduced and must satisfy the minimal-degree equality.

## 3. Picard-scheme pathology and the pg split

Liedtke original lecture notes printed 13–14 explicitly give T_0 Pic^0=H1(O), duality of Pic^0_red and Alb, the inequality h1(O)>=dim Alb, possible strict inequality, and the implication H2(O)=0 -> Pic^0 reduced. Hence the equality cannot be used for arbitrary smooth surfaces. Printed26 explicitly lists nonclassical Enriques surfaces in characteristic2 with pg=h1(O)=1 and b1=0. These have dim Alb=0 and nonreduced Pic^0. They falsify the unconditional equality q=dim Alb, but do not falsify PUBLIC_TURN_2: its pg>0 branch produces a canonical section directly. Only the pg=0 branch asserts equality, and Serre duality then gives H2(O)=0. No candidate use of analytic Hodge symmetry or vanishing in characteristic zero was found. I did not construct an Enriques equation or compute its Picard scheme; this adversarial input is a precisely read primary-source example, separate from my explicit singular surfaces.

## 4. Hypothesis-sharp surface negative control

If e=1, take X=P2 and H a line. Then C=P1 is integral, H is ample and H^2=1, but K+2H=-H has no sections. The RR lower bound is e+pa(C)-1=0. Thus e>=2 in Lemma A and the nonlinear degree e>=2 application in Lemma B are substantive; the proof respects them. An all-plane reduction need not and does not invoke Lemma B for e=1.
