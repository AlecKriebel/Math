The central statement is sound: if a smooth integral projective surface carries a nef divisor H with square e>=2 and an integral member C of |H|, then it has a nonzero section of omega_X(2H). The derivation recorded before reading the author proof independently found the same mechanism. No prior reviewer proof was read. The initial independence record and its timestamp are preserved; reading the author proof afterward was needed to audit its exact Lemmas A and B.

The apparent positive-characteristic obstruction is handled correctly. If p_g>0, the canonical section can be multiplied by the square of C's defining section. If p_g=0, Serre duality annihilates H^2(O_X), making the Picard scheme smooth; therefore q equals the Albanese dimension. Normalized C generates the Albanese: an ample pullback from a quotient constant on C is perpendicular to a positive-square divisor, so algebraic Hodge index makes it numerically trivial; a nonconstant map would have positive pullback degree on a curve. This argument includes inseparable maps and avoids an invalid tangent-space genus comparison. The normalization sequence gives q<=g(C_normalized)<=p_a(C). Riemann–Roch has the correct sign and gives chi(omega_X(2H))>=e+p_a(C)-1>=1, while h^2=0.

The singular-surface transfer in Lemma B also passes. Surface resolution in arbitrary characteristic can preserve the normalization's regular locus. A general embedded plane section is integral. Its pullback remains integral after avoiding the finitely many images of contracted curves: all components must dominate the same plane section, over whose generic regular point the birational morphism is an isomorphism. Multiplicity one and the Cartier divisor's absence of embedded components finish the check. This does not use smooth Bertini on the pullback system.

Canonical forms on the resolution inject into the normalization's reflexive canonical sheaf. Finite canonical duality identifies its pushforward with the conductor tensored with the hypersurface canonical bundle. The conductor vanishes at every generic singular-curve point because a one-dimensional normal local domain would be regular. The degree-(e-2) section then lifts to a polynomial using H^1(P^3,O(-2))=0. Neither equality of resolution and normalization canonical pushforwards nor rational singularities is assumed.

DERIVATIONS.md writes out the equations, the finite-duality argument, the Albanese curve-existence argument, and explicit boundary cases. It also gives an independent isogeny argument that supplies the needed quotient-killing homomorphism without relying on a quotient construction as a black box. A square-one line on P^2 is a counterexample when e>=2 is omitted; P^1 x P^1 at H=(1,1) attains the bound. A cubic cusp cone illustrates the conductor in characteristics two and three. These are hand derivations; no finite computation is offered as proof of the general statement.
