## Exact claim and topology

X=[-1/2,1/2]; D={u in L1(X,dx):u>=0 a.e., integral u=1}, with relative L1 topology. Local maps have two full increasing branches of
F_r(x)=((r+4)x+r+1)/(2rx+2), split at alpha_r=-r/4, with second branch F_r-1. The derivative is (4-r^2)/(2(1+rx)^2); |r|<2/3 gives uniform expansion. The nonlinear operator is Ptilde u=P_{G(phi(u))}u, phi(u)=integral x u(x)dx. OWR 2009/49, Keller printed pp.2713-2715, explicitly discusses G(x)=A tanh(Bx/A), 0<A<=0.4, 6<B<=16 in its bistable regime. Its conjecture is W0=boundary_D(B+)=boundary_D(B-), where W0 consists of densities converging in L1 to 1 and B± consist of densities converging to u_{±r*}. This is not a finite-particle statement, a weak-topology statement, nor the separate question about zero weight on the unstable thermodynamic limit state.

BKZ dated 19 December 2008, 36 pages, author-hosted PDF: generic G is odd, strictly increasing, concave for positive arguments, with range [-0.4,0.4]. Assumption I: G'(x)<=25-50|G(x)|. Assumption II: H(r)=G(phi(u_r)) is S-shaped (used for its fixed-point dichotomy). B=G'(0)>6 is bistable. Analyticity is required for finite-system density regularity; the other statements are said to remain true for C2 feedback. BKZ Example 1 gives the tanh example up to B<=18, wider than the workshop range. Success for the workshop claim requires every u in W0, including nonsmooth and vanishing densities, to be approximable in L1 from BOTH stable basins. The inclusion boundary_D(B±) subset W0 follows from global trichotomy and openness of B±; the reverse inclusion is the substantive goal.

## Existing results, including complete relevant proofs read

BKZ Theorem2/Proposition3: every density converges to one of the three equilibria in the bistable regime, and B± are L1-open. Read complete proofs in Sections4.1-4.4 and5.1-5.3, including Lemmas5-14 and Propositions2-5. The representation uses w_y(x)=(1-y^2/4)/(1-xy)^2 for Y=[-2/3,2/3], and D0={integral_Y w_y dmu(y)}. The two dual maps are sigma_r(y)=2(y+r)/((r+1)y+r+4) and tau_r(y)=2(y+r)/((r-1)y-r+4), with p_r(y)=1/2-(r+y)/(4+ry). They have common zero -r, equal slope there, slope bounded above by3/4 on Y, and strict stochastic order monotonicity. Lemma10 contracts supports when they straddle that zero, even with approximate straddling. Proposition3 reaches arbitrary D by linear-parameter shadowing and conditional expectations on fine full-branch partitions. That argument proves convergence/openness, not the missing boundary conclusion.

BKZ Proposition4: W0 intersect D0 lies in both boundaries. Lemma12 gives quadratic field/support-radius control for central orbits. Lemma13 excludes two distinct stochastically ordered representing measures both having central limits; perturbing by masses at ±2/3 yields the two-basin approximations. D0 is a compact image in L1 of compact P(Y), by (4.17); it is not L1-dense in D. Its conclusion therefore cannot be extended by a density assertion. BKZ Section5.3 explicitly disclaims Frechet differentiability on L1/BV; it gives directional differentiation at C2 densities and BV-to-L1 differentiation there. The unstable eigenvalue at1 in the x direction is lambda=1/2+B/12>1. An ordinary L1 smooth stable-manifold theorem cannot be invoked without a new argument. The last paragraph in the printed proof of Proposition5 does not establish invariance of ker(phi) under P0: for example phi(P0 f) need not vanish when phi(f)=integral f=0. This observation does not invalidate the trichotomy or Proposition4, and should not be used as a counterexample to the conjecture.

OWR additionally reports density of B+ union B- in D. Density of a union of two disjoint open sets does not imply each complement point touches each one.

## Independent mechanisms and exact unresolved gaps

1. Ordered IFS perturbations: rigorously established on D0 by BKZ. Gap for arbitrary D is absence of representing probability measures; blocked if it merely assumes D0 is dense.
2. Spectral/analytic local stable-manifold approach: the x eigenvalue is explicit. Gap is sufficient regularity and a positivity-preserving perturbation theorem for every L1 density. A BV result alone would not settle the requested topology. Blocked if it assumes Frechet differentiability contradicted by the source.
3. One-step self-consistent right inverse (independently derived): let h_{r,0}=F_r^{-1} on X and h_{r,1}=F_r^{-1} on X+1. For a fixed measurable branch allocation a_0(y)+a_1(y)=1, a_i>=0, define an inverse density as the sum of (h_{r,i})_*(a_i v dx). Its P_r image equals v. Its field m_v(r)=sum_i integral h_{r,i}(y)a_i(y)v(y)dy is decreasing in r because d_r h=(4h^2-1)/(4-r^2)<=0. Therefore r=G(m_v(r)) has a unique scalar solution in the feedback range by monotonicity and endpoint trapping. Given a base u, use its conditional branch allocation relative to P_r u, with arbitrary allocations on its zero set; this gives a continuous local right inverse at u in L1. Strong continuity must be proved for measurable allocation data, by transport approximation, not by pointwise evaluation of an L1 representative. This seems sufficient for finite-step lifting of already-known boundary points, but not for central orbits merely approaching1. Exact gap: establish a control uniform in the number of backward steps (or a different mechanism) so tiny terminal density perturbations can be lifted near an arbitrary central initial density without uncontrolled amplification or an assumed convergence rate.
4. Inverse-path transport: each h is contracting in its y argument (at |r|<=.4 its largest slope is3/4), while r sensitivity is bounded by1/(4-.4^2)=25/96. With full itinerary allocations, varying feedback sequences could be treated by a coupled finite-dimensional fixed point. Existence alone does not give closeness to the original trajectory; a uniform parameter estimate is required. Potential cancellation arises because increasing feedback moves backward positions left. The positive mean-field effect forward becomes negative feedback backward; crude absolute-value bounds may erase this mechanism and produce B-dependent amplification. Gap: derive a parameter estimate or an exact monotonicity/degree argument for simultaneous constraints, without presupposing the desired stable manifold.
