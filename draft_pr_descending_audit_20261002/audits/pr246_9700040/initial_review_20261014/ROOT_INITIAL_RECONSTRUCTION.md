Sorting rectangle points by their second coordinate and using minimum-pile-top updates computes the minimal possible last value of a chain of each length. Points to the rectangle's right never change the smaller pile tops. Thus no global ordering of infinitely many spatial events is needed. At every positive height, any finite chain length can be obtained by using successive height strips and successively larger first coordinates. Finite rectangle counts give local finiteness and distinct, positive pile tops.

Between points, Y_i(s)=exp(s) Z_i(exp(s)) has derivative Y_i, while events replace the first pile top strictly above their transformed x. The first k coordinates are autonomous because an event above the k-th position cannot alter them. Source finite-k dynamics have no immigration, even though the underlying empty-start quadrant construction needs particles from infinity before a finite time. Thus until coalescence the smaller coordinate follows a exp(t), while the common preceding coordinate is at most b exp(t), with a>b. A future event in the expanding strip between these two deterministic curves forces coalescence. Its conditional area diverges.
