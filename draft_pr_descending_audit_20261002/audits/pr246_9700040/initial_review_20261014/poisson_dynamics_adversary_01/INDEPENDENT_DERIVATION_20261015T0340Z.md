## Finite rectangle identity proved directly

For a finite list of distinct spatial values arriving in increasing time order, let a_i be the smallest possible last value of an increasing subsequence of length i. A new value u replaces the first a_i>u, or appends u when none is larger. To prove this, if a_{i-1}<u<a_i then u extends a subsequence of length i-1 and improves the i-th terminal minimum; a subsequence ending in u cannot have length i+1 because every old terminal minimum of length i exceeds u. Other terminal minima do not improve. Ties are excluded. Thus the pile tops equal these minima.

Deleting every arrival above M leaves exactly the same minima at or below M. Such an arrival cannot decrease a top below M. Hence finite rectangles of width M and time horizon T give exactly matching local answers when either bounding rectangle is enlarged. This avoids ordering the infinitely many quadrant events in one time list.

Let L(x,t) be the longest strict two-coordinate chain in (0,x]x(0,t], and Z_i(t)=inf{x:L(x,t)>=i}. The preceding finite identity gives #{i:Z_i(t)<=x}=L(x,t). The infimum is attained: find any finite rectangle containing an i-chain; its finitely many spatial coordinates contain the first crossing.

For fixed t>0, choose i ordered disjoint positive-height time strips. Each strip has points arbitrarily far right almost surely; successively choose a point to the right of its predecessor. Thus Z_i(t)<infinity. In every finite rectangle only finitely many coordinates occur; all are positive and almost surely no two points have the same spatial coordinate. Adding one new rightmost point can raise L by at most one, so all Z_i(t) are strictly increasing and diverge with i. Simultaneity in all t>0 follows by first taking rational q,T with 0<q<t<T: Z_i(t)<=Z_i(q)<infinity, Z_1(t)>=Z_1(T)>0, and local counts at t are bounded by the finite count at T. Globally distinct event coordinates exclude coordinate equality at arbitrary t.

These facts construct a locally finite infinite entrance process for every t>0. At each positive t all finite indices have already appeared, although the process has empty local state at t=0. This is an entrance from spatial infinity, not finite-index immigration after a positive starting time.

## Change of variables, generator, stationarity

Set Y_i(s)=exp(s)Z_i(exp(s)). If no relevant event occurs, Z_i is constant and dY_i/ds=Y_i. An event (u,v) maps to x=uv; multiplication by positive v preserves spatial order, so the same nearest-right particle moves to x. Since all indices are finite at every finite s, no finite-index birth occurs. The first k coordinates are autonomous: events above their maximum do not affect them, and an event below it moves precisely the first coordinate to its right in that projection. ## Coupling at random times with moving lower coordinates

Drive two copies by the same future unit PPP, initially independent of that future field. First k evolution is well defined by the preceding bound. Define tau_i to be the first time coordinates 1,...,i agree and let tau_0=0. These are stopping times because the paths are adapted and have locally finitely many jumps; agreement of an initial segment persists by autonomy and common driving events. The argument below constructs a finite such time, so one may equivalently use the successive constructed coalescing times.

At finite tau=tau_{i-1}, put a=min(X_i(tau),X'_i(tau)), b=X_{i-1}(tau)=X'_{i-1}(tau), with b=0 when i=1. Both copies are strictly ordered, hence a>b. Until coalescence of coordinate i, its smaller current member cannot jump: any effective event below that member but above the common lower neighbor moves both i-th coordinates to exactly the same event. Events below the common lower neighbor affect lower coordinates only. The larger i-th coordinate may jump while staying above the smaller one; it cannot cross it without coalescing. Thus on failure to coalesce the smaller coordinate equals a exp(h), while the common lower neighbor is at most b exp(h), h>=0.

Consequently every future event in A_H={(y,tau+h):0<h<=H, b exp(h)<y<a exp(h)} forces coalescence by that event. Its area is (a-b)(exp(H)-1). Given F_tau, a,b are known. The Poisson strong Markov property implies that future shifted PPP is independent of F_tau, so
P(tau_i-tau>H | F_tau) <= exp(-(a-b)(exp(H)-1)).
The bound tends to zero. This use remains valid when lower coordinates jump, since their jumps only decrease the upper envelope b exp(h). It also remains valid for random a,b and random tau; apply the strong Markov law first, then evaluate the now fixed strip. The PPP strong Markov property can be justified by dyadic stopping-time approximation on each bounded rectangle followed by exhaustion. No boundedness or finite expectation of initial coordinates is needed.

Induction gives finite tau_k almost surely for each fixed k. Couple independent samples from any two invariant laws with the independent common future field. Their time-t laws are the invariant laws, and total variation is at most P(tau_k>t), tending to zero. Hence the finite invariant laws coincide. An arbitrary initial E_k distribution also converges in total variation to the invariant law under this coupling (no uniform rate claimed).

Coordinate-consistent construction defines an E_infinity process; every invariant probability on that exact state space has invariant finite-k projections, hence the same marginals. Cylinder events determine the Borel probability on E_infinity, so the infinite invariant law is unique. This does not require global finite-time agreement of all coordinates.
