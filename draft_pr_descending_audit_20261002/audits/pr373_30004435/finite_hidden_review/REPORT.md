## Falsification attempts and boundary cases

**Stationary support and transients.** A finite stationary chain cannot charge transient states: their occupation probability tends to zero yet is constant by stationarity. An outgoing transition from positive support into a zero-mass destination likewise contradicts the stationary balance equation. The controls include a transient with an exit, an unused absorbing recurrent class, and support-restricted reversal. No zero denominator is used in the reversed kernel or limiting bridge.

**Period and mixtures.** The phase at time zero is recoverable from every remote hidden coordinate by subtracting its time index. An irreducible periodic stationary chain can therefore be ergodic while retaining nontrivial tails. Conditional phase laws are stationary under the common D-step shift; the candidate does not assert one-step conditional stationarity. Nonergodic mixtures retain their class labels when observable. Nonreversible matrices are checked and the proofs require no detailed balance.

**Bilateral upper bound.** The finite Markov proof conditions a central block on two remote endpoint states and factors the full path density. On common-period cutoffs the denominator tends to a strictly positive phase-stationary mass and the ratio tends uniformly to the class/phase-conditioned central-block law. Reverse martingale convergence and cylinder density supply the completed field identification. This extra proof is necessary because general finite-alphabet processes can have much larger bilateral tails.

**Observation degeneracy and full-law quotient.** Constant emissions erase all hidden phases and classes, including a mixture of a two-cycle and a three-cycle. Identical iid emission rows erase disjoint absorbing classes. Different iid biases retain class information. The independently constructed four-state period-two model has identical fair one-letter laws in both phases but probabilities of word 00 equal to 1/2 and 1/4. Thus a quotient based on immediate emission marginals is false; the candidate correctly uses all conditional word laws. Finite stochastic augmentation preserves hidden class periods after null pair removal in the controls.

**Word length and dimension.** The candidate's s-1 bound is a proved dimension bound rather than extrapolation from a scan. Exact controls include generic initial-distribution witnesses first distinguished at length N-1 for N=2 through 7, so a blanket shorter bound would fail. These sharpness witnesses are used only for the linear lemma; their nonstationary initial laws are not misrepresented as stationary tail examples. Equality of right-side prefix laws implies equality of full conditioned observed laws using the common D-shift invariance and cylinder generation.

**Completion and density.** For decreasing fields, representatives A_n equal to a completed event A modulo null sets yield a raw representative liminf A_n in every field. Finitely many positive class/phase weights also make any ambient null set null in every retained conditional law. Cylinder density and conditional-expectation contraction justify extension from finite-coordinate functions to all process-measurable functions. No unproved interchange of a tail intersection and a factor/join is used.

## Remaining gap and disposition recommendation

The finite routes use a finite class/phase variable and geometric forgetting inside a finite skeleton. An arbitrary stationary finite-alphabet process need not have finite hidden state, a finite observable span, or geometric mixing. Increasingly accurate finite-block approximations do not imply continuity of tail fields. Supplying an unsupported finite-label or finite-state reduction would move the central difficulty to an equivalent unsupported claim and would be blocked.
