# Turn 1: exact tail-defect reduction and finite-Markov classification

Original method-restricted question remains unresolved. This turn gives a probability-only reduction, reconstructs the complete finite-Markov case including periodicity and mixtures, and exhibits two barriers to passing from finite models to the general process. These are scoped results with prior credit, not a new proof for arbitrary stationary processes.

## 1. An exact conditional-expectation formulation

Work on the canonical space of the process, with all fields completed. Write

    F_m^- = sigma(X_k:k<=−m),
    F_m^+ = sigma(X_k:k>=m),
    T^- = intersection_m F_m^-,  T^+ = intersection_m F_m^+.

For a bounded finite-coordinate cylinder function f, define

    D_m(f)=E[(E[f|F_m^-]−E[f|F_m^+])²].

Reverse-martingale convergence in L² gives

    lim_m D_m(f)=||E[f|T^-]−E[f|T^+]||²_2.             (1.1)

The desired equality of the two fields is equivalent to lim_m D_m(f)=0 for every cylinder indicator. Indeed, equality implies equal conditional expectations. Conversely cylinder functions are dense in the L² space generated by the process, and conditional expectations are contractions, so equal projections on cylinders imply equal projections everywhere. Applying them to indicators of events in either field gives equality modulo null sets.

For fixed m, the half-line conditional expectations can themselves be obtained as L² limits of conditioning on X_[−N,−m] and X_[m,N] as N→infinity. Thus the exact formulation has an ordered double limit, first N then m. No interchange, finite diagonal test or uniform rate follows from stationarity alone. The finite alphabet does not yet supply the missing estimate in this formulation.

## 2. Finite stationary Markov chains: the actual common field

Let Y be a stationary Markov chain with finite transition matrix P and stationary law pi. Remove states of pi-mass zero. The remaining states decompose into closed irreducible classes C. For a class of positive mass, write pi_C for its normalized stationary law, d_C for its period, and

    C=C_0 union ... union C_(d_C−1)

for cyclic subclasses, labeled so transitions advance the label by1 modulo d_C. All cyclic classes have pi_C-mass1/d_C. Define Z=(C,j), where Y_0 lies in C_j.

**Claim.** The left tail, right tail and bilateral tail of Y all equal sigma(Z) modulo null sets.

The removal of transient states is legitimate. In a finite chain every transient state reaches a closed class almost surely; there is a uniform positive chance of doing so in a bounded number of steps. A stationary distribution therefore assigns zero mass to all transient states. Alternatively, any transition out of positive-pi support would contradict pi P=pi at a zero-mass destination.

The variable Z is measurable in each remote half-line: from Y_k one reads its closed class and subtracts k from its cyclic label. This works for any positive or negative k, so sigma(Z) lies in both one-sided tails.

## 3. Probability-only mixing within cyclic classes

Let D be a common multiple of the finitely many periods. On each C_j, the D-step chain is finite, irreducible and aperiodic. A sufficiently large power Q^a of its transition matrix Q has all entries positive. Set epsilon=|C_j| min_(x,y) Q^a(x,y)>0. Then each row has the common minorization epsilon times the uniform law on C_j.

Couple two copies at every a-th step using the common part. They coalesce with probability at least epsilon at each attempt and can remain together thereafter. Their non-coalescence probability is at most(1−epsilon)^k after k attempts. This proves uniform convergence to the stationary phase law, whose mass at a state y in C_j is d_C pi_C(y). The finite graph period decomposition and this coupling use no entropy.

Conditioning on Z does not make the chain stationary under a single step when d_C>1. It makes the D-step skeleton stationary on its fixed phase. The proof below retains that distinction.

## 4. Two-sided bridges prove the tail upper bound

It suffices to handle the bilateral tail, which contains both one-sided tails. Fix a central path y=(y_−r,...,y_r), and let m>r be a multiple of D. Given the entire outside field sigma(Y_k:k<=−m or k>=m), the conditional probability of this central path depends only on i=Y_−m and j=Y_m, and is

    P^(m−r)(i,y_−r)
      [product_(k=−r)^(r−1) P(y_k,y_(k+1))]
      P^(m−r)(y_r,j) / P^(2m)(i,j).                  (4.1)

This bridge formula follows by factoring finite Markov path probabilities and canceling the outside factors. Conditioning on larger finite outside windows and taking the martingale limit gives the same formula for the entire outside field. Zero-probability endpoint pairs can be discarded.

Fix Z=(C,j_0). Since m is a multiple of D, both endpoints have phase j_0. The mixing just proved gives, uniformly over their possible states,

    P^(m−r)(i,y_−r) → d_C pi_C(y_−r)
       on the compatible phase j_0−r,
    P^(m−r)(y_r,j) → d_C pi_C(j),
    P^(2m)(i,j) → d_C pi_C(j)>0.

Formula(4.1) therefore converges uniformly to

    d_C pi_C(y_−r) product P(y_k,y_(k+1)),

which is precisely the probability of the central path conditional on Z. Incompatible paths have probability zero. There are finitely many endpoints and classes, so the convergence is uniform across every positive-mass Z value.

Reverse-martingale convergence along the cofinal sequence of these m now yields

    E[f | T_bilateral] = E[f | Z]

for every finite-coordinate function f. Density of cylinders extends this identity to every bounded measurable f generated by the chain. In particular an indicator measurable in the bilateral tail equals its conditional expectation given Z, so it is sigma(Z)-measurable. This proves the claim in Section2.

This classification is standard: see Lemańczyk AppendixB.3.2, formula(B.3.3), and its Blackwell–Freedman reference. The bridge argument here makes its probability-only content and the periodic/mixed cases explicit.

## 5. Periodic phase is not the invariant-event field

Take the stationary deterministic binary alternation, with the two phases equally likely. All three tails are sigma(Y_0), since any distant observation reveals the phase. But the only shift-invariant events have probability0 or1: the shift interchanges the two sample sequences. Thus replacing the common tail by the invariant-event field would already be wrong in this elementary finite Markov example.

The common tail sigma algebra is preserved by the shift as a collection of events. That is different from every event in it being individually shift invariant.

## 6. Why finite approximations do not finish the original problem

**Fixed-alphabet weak convergence.** Let P_epsilon have binary transition probabilities1−epsilon to stay and epsilon to flip, with the uniform stationary law. For0<epsilon<1 it is irreducible and aperiodic, so both one-sided tails are trivial. As epsilon decreases to0, the finite-dimensional distributions tend to those of a random constant sequence, whose two tails contain the nontrivial initial bit.

For an n-letter block the total variation distance is exactly

    1−(1−epsilon)^(n−1) <= (n−1)epsilon.

One coupling uses the same initial bit and regards any transition as a possible disagreement with the constant sequence. Thus even arbitrarily accurate finite-block approximation does not make tail fields continuous. Both limiting one-sided fields still coincide; this example blocks an argument, not the source theorem.

**Increasing alphabet/infinite-memory encoding.** Let(B_k)_(k in Z) be independent fair bits and put

    V_k=(B_k,B_(k−1),B_(k−2),...) in {0,1}^N.

This process is stationary. Its remote past field is the ordinary iid past tail and is trivial. Every future half-line determines every B_j: choose k>=max(m,j) and read coordinate k−j of V_k. Hence its future tail is the full bit field. This is the source's excluded infinite-spin phenomenon, also explicitly given by Al-Najjar–Shmaya Example6.1.

Truncate V_k to its first L coordinates. The resulting finite-state shift-register Markov chain has a transition power of length L with every row uniform, so all three tails are trivial. Passing to the increasing coordinate limit changes the future tail to the full field. One cannot commute an increasing coding limit with the remote-tail intersection.

## 7. Exact controls and remaining gap

finite_markov.py computes supported closed classes, graph periods and cyclic subclasses using exact rational transitions. verify_turn1.py checks stationary identities, cyclic masses, uniform-coupling bounds and bridge normalizations on33 finite-chain examples with mixtures, periodic classes and a zero-mass transient state. It also checks the alternation, shift-register and weak-limit formulas exactly. These controls do not prove an infinite tail statement; the proofs above do.

The remaining source problem is still arbitrary finite-alphabet stationarity, with no Markov or mixing assumption. The defect formulation(1.1) isolates a probability-theoretic target, but no general bound forcing it to zero has been established in this turn. No entropy-based theorem is used to fill that gap.
