## Exact target and source scope

Let independent Bernoulli variables X_n have P(X_n=1)=1/n and A={n:X_n=1}. For finite B let r_B(s)=#{C subset B:sum C=s}, with the empty subset included, and M(B)=max_s r_B(s). The target is a deterministic exponent for log M(A intersect [1,D])/log log D as real D tends to infinity, including whether the limit exists. Each probability mode and each one-sided bound must be separately established.

Independently downloaded exact primary sources: Ben Green's `Propinquity of divisors`, OWR 50/2019 printed pp.3164–3167; FGK arXiv:1908.00378v3; actual published FGK Inventiones 232 (2023), 1027–1160, DOI 10.1007/s00222-022-01177-y; Mao–Song arXiv:2609.22296v2 (81 pages, 27 September 2026); de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum author PDF `On the concentration of divisors of powers` (13 pages; 8/9/2026, 10h09).

The OWR model question motivates the prefix target but expressly studies a truncated fixed-k problem instead. OWR says “a.s.” in its informal truncated description. Controlling published FGK Problem 1 defines beta_k through probability tending to one, not eventual probability-one occurrence on a single sample. Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 give zeta_- >= eta=log 2/log(2/rho)=0.35332277270132346711..., rho=0.28121134969637466015.... Published Lemma 2.1, including its complete tensor proof and remark, gives the prefix lower bound in probability and describes probable tightness as belief. This is published credited input, not new independent research.

Published Definitions 3.1–3.6 and Theorem 7 compare tilde-gamma_k <= beta_k <= gamma_k, with nondegenerate cube-spanned flags, supported measures, and entropy inequalities over all subflags. The framework at fixed k does not itself permit a uniform passage to growing k or a new prefix upper bound. Published section 3.4 identifies beta_2=1-1/log 3 via the flag (span(1),Q^2) and its three cosets, with the upper statement attributed to entropy Lemma B.2. This gives a verified published fixed-k threshold, not a claim about M for all D. Corollary 1's printed beta_k <= beta_(k+1) has the wrong direction: having k+1 distinct representations implies having k. The dyadic sandwich remains valid after reversing the sign.

Mao–Song v2 claims alpha_k=beta_k/(1-beta_k), with fixed-k density-zero above threshold, and local FGK corrections. Its introduction and controlling definitions are read; the complete 81-page preprint is not an assumed premise of this audit. The author divisor-power manuscript concerns Delta(n^r) and polynomial values, with slowly varying errors. Neither stated result supplies a prefix exponent for this Bernoulli subset-sum model without an additional proof.

## Candidate-free mechanisms and falsification plan

1. **Annular amplification.** For fixed k and 0<c<beta_k choose a gap parameter c<a<beta_k and intervals I_j=(exp(a^(-j)),exp(a^(-j-1))]. Good events on disjoint intervals are independent and have probability tending to one, by the beta definition at exponent a (rounding costs vanish). For centered bounded indicators, sum Var(Y_j)/j^2<infinity; Kolmogorov convergence plus Kronecker gives average(Y_j-EY_j)->0 a.s., hence good-block density one. Disjoint unions give k^(j-o(j)) distinct equal-sum subsets. Therefore liminf log M/log log D >= log k/log(1/a) a.s., followed by countable parameters and real-D monotonicity. Check interval endpoint ownership, countable exceptional-set intersection, supremum admissibility, and uniform tail over all j. No convergence rate is needed for this independent-block SLLN.
2. **Finite-prefix stability.** If B differs from C in m elements, M(B)<=2^m M(C) after each removal, and adding elements cannot decrease M. This makes liminf and limsup exponents tail measurable. Their determinism follows from Kolmogorov 0–1. Tail determinism alone does not prove equality of liminf and limsup, positivity, or existence.
3. **Signed-relation obstruction.** Two distinct equal-sum subsets yield a nonzero {-1,0,1} relation after cancellation. Positivity forces both signs. If largest support point is x and relation support has h points, second largest is >=x/(h-1). Any counting proof needs second-largest constraints and control of h rather than an unjustified cancellation condition on the largest point alone. A finite union bound is not universal in D.
4. **Growing flags.** For arbitrary affine target coset, a descending rank-increasing pivot assignment has at most one numerical pivot tuple for each free assignment. Diagonal common columns matter. Bound every coset, not only zero, handle rational rank and all cube-generated spaces, replace inclusion probabilities by exact deletion odds 1/(n-1), and control selected free population under a regularity event. Any claimed exponent must dominate number of flags/labels and have a summable failure bound on a scale sequence with adequate interpolation to all real D. Record exact dimension/count growth and how pivot scale is paid; don't transfer the central problem into an unsupported flag bound.
5. **Moments and tilts.** Exact E[z^N]=product(1+(z-1)/n), including the forced n=1 factor. The z-tilted inclusion law is z/(n+z-1). Moments and raw expectations can be dominated by rare N tails. E log M and log E M are different; use pathwise domination/uniform integrability only with proof. Check n=1, z=1, empty subsets, signed sums, and rare tails explicitly.
