## Complete coverage tests

The potentially strongest prior bridges were tested, rather than rejected by title.

1. **Classical smooth sufficiency.** CT2012 allows abstract Banach/Hilbert optimization and ordinary-cone positivity, but also imposes a positive weak-null lower bound. The submitted Hessian violates that bound. Its complete objective also has a nonsmooth group norm. Thus CT's theorem does not imply growth here. Its elementary counterexamples establish the generic mechanism, without constructing this wave/global optimum.

2. **Cited sparse parabolic sufficiency.** CHW2017's j3 uses the same outer-L1/inner-Hilbert geometry after exchanging the grouping variables. The exchange alone is legitimate for the norm calculus. It does not exchange a first-order parabolic state equation with a second-order wave equation, scalar box constraints with pointwise Hilbert balls, or its regularized assumption with beta2=0. Its unregularized results use strengthened cones and state-based growth; its j3 proof still requires the quadratic term. No negative wave/global-minimum theorem appears in the inspected relevant sections.

3. **General nonsmooth second-subderivative framework: complete core coverage.** BW2024 Theorem2.18 is a genuine abstract necessary condition that applies to the submitted reduced objective at zero; BRIDGES.md maps every relevant assumption. KM Theorem4.3 already supplies C² in L1H and S''(0)=0, hence y=z+o(A²). The first-mode kernel gives x<=A/pi+o(A²). The decreasing scalar tracking expression A-pi*x+x²/2 then proves a genuine strict local optimizer, including in L2 and L-infinity. Standard group-threshold algebra gives C={0}, and centered pulses violate NDC and L2 growth. This short specialization contains no unsupported central PDE/optimizer bridge. Under the parent's explicit criterion accepting complete routine specializations, it covers the core negative implication.

4. **Measure-space L1 frameworks.** CW2018 and WW2022 explain why changing the norm and admitting measure directions can restore meaningful second-order conditions. The latter's specific sparse integral theorem requires a transversal threshold. On the submitted time ray, the threshold function sin(pi*t) touches 1 at a quadratic maximum; the transversality and O(delta) level-set assumptions fail. Applying that theorem directly is invalid. The optional L1-squared estimate follows instead from an elementary tracking-square projection bound, as the variational audit shows. It should be framed as a useful estimate derived here using established methods, without a novelty claim.

5. **Prior sparse linear wave theory.** KTV2014/2016 includes terminal displacement and H^-1 velocity tracking and proves optimizer existence. Its controls are spatial vector measures with L2 time profiles, its wave equation is linear, and its terminal-only observation need not give uniqueness. It is substantive wave/sparse-control antecedence, but has neither this temporal group-L1/Hilbert-cap construction nor its nonlinear global-optimality certificate.

The exact formulas in BRIDGES.md distinguish the genuine nonlinear local optimizer, the general NDC obstruction, and the stronger nonlinear-global-minimum step. Core coverage does not require global uniqueness, target-instability or optional L1 counterparts. The source-only local-optimality proof avoids the submitted O(A^5) contraction and bathtub/global comparison entirely. ROUTE_LEDGER.md records the exact coverage and gaps.
