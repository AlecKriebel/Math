# Independent root energy route, before candidate inspection

This is an independently derived mechanism rather than a claim that the candidate already proves it. Standard foundations admitted explicitly: weak compactness of probability measures on a compact set, lower semicontinuity of positive-kernel energy, Borel regularity of finite Radon measures, ordinary Borel Choquet capacitability, and zero Newtonian capacity iff polar. These do not include the Kellogg property, Dirichlet irregularity, fine Choquet property, or Wiener equivalence.

For positive-capacity compact F, minimize probability energy I(mu)=m. Variation toward a normalized restriction mu|A/mu(A), which has finite energy by positivity, gives U(mu)>=m mu-almost everywhere. Its mean is m, so equality holds almost everywhere. This equality set is dense in supp(mu); lower semicontinuity then gives U(mu)<=m on the entire support. For any exterior x choose its nearest support point z. For each support y, |z-y|<=2|x-y|, so U(mu)(x)<=2^(n-2)U(mu)(z)<=2^(n-2)m. This avoids a maximum principle. Setting lambda=mu/m gives mass cap(F), U(lambda)=1 lambda-almost everywhere and U(lambda)<=C=2^(n-2) everywhere.

For a Borel subset A of F and q=lambda(A)>0, I(lambda|A)<=Cq; its normalization is an admissible probability measure, hence q<=C cap(A), interpreting Borel capacity by the admitted ordinary capacitability foundation. In particular lambda(closed B(x,r))<=C cap(F intersect closed B(x,r))<=C cap(E intersect closed B(x,r)). One can instead restrict to compact A for each ball, avoiding arbitrary-set capacity in this bound.

Define J_R(x)=integral_0^R cap(E intersect closed B(x,r))/r^(n-1) dr. The moving compact capacity is upper semicontinuous in (x,r), by compact containment followed by continuity from above of compact capacity. That continuity follows from weak compactness of equilibrium measures and lower semicontinuity of energy; the zero-capacity case follows by the same limiting argument. Consequently J_R and its finiteness set are Borel. For a finite-integral point J_R tends to zero as R tends to zero, by continuity of the integral of a nonnegative integrable function.

If the finite-integral Borel subset had positive capacity, ordinary capacitability would yield a positive-capacity compact subset carrying a finite-energy probability measure. Fix delta=1/(4C(n-2)). The sets where J_(1/k)<delta exhaust that subset. A positive-measure member therefore exists. Inner regularity selects a compact positive-measure subset K therein, still of positive capacity by restriction of the finite-energy measure. This measured exhaustion avoids assuming continuity of capacity under increasing arbitrary sets or incorrectly interchanging a supremum with a limit.

Fix R=1/k and cover K by finitely many balls of radius rho=R/8. At least one closed-ball intersection F has positive measure, hence positive capacity, and J_R(x)<delta for every x in F. Its diameter is at most 2rho; the kernel lower bound gives cap(F)<=(2rho)^(n-2). Extended Tonelli yields, for all x,

 U(lambda)(x) <= R^(2-n) lambda(F) + (n-2) integral_0^R lambda(closed B(x,r))/r^(n-1) dr.

The displayed inequality includes the whole far tail by total mass; coincident-point terms are interpreted in the extended nonnegative sense. For x in F its right side is at most (2rho/R)^(n-2)+C(n-2)J_R(x)<=1/4+1/4=1/2, contradicting U(lambda)=1 lambda-almost everywhere. This establishes the direct theorem subject to the listed foundations and the measurability detail.

Power sharpness: for epsilon>0, cap(E intersect closed B(x,r))<=(2r)^(n-2), so replacing r^(n-1) by r^(n-1-epsilon) gives integrand at most 2^(n-2)r^(epsilon-1), integrable everywhere. A compact set of positive capacity then falsifies the polar conclusion. Multiplication by a nonnegative weight w with integral_0^1 w(r) dr/r finite has the same consequence. This proves precisely those weakenings, not an optimality statement for every nonintegrable gauge.

For an uncountable polar example embed a middle-third Cantor set in a line. Any probability measure has either an atom (already infinite energy) or disjoint basic intervals at level k with masses summing to one. At least the sum of squared masses, >=2^(-k), contributes pairs at distance <=3^(-k). Thus I(mu)>=3^(k(n-2))/2^k tends to infinity for n>=3. Its capacity is zero, and every displayed capacity integral vanishes. The construction is uncountable and compact. A whole segment in n=3 requires a different borderline argument and must not be inferred from this Cantor estimate.

Adversarial gaps to inspect in candidate: lower-semicontinuity direction, restriction first variation without circular quasi-everywhere assumptions, measurable exhaustion and closed ball compactness, tail constants, atom/diagonal layer cake, kernel normalization, borderline line example, and breadth of priority/sharpness language.
