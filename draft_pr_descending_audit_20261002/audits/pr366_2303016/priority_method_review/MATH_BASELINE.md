# Independent mathematical baseline — sealed before candidate inspection

Seal UTC: 2026-10-03 17:25:22 UTC. Built from the primary sources and independent deductions before candidate proof/code/history. This is a falsification baseline, not a novelty claim.

Let s=n−2>0, k(x,y)=|x−y|^(−s), I(ν)=∫∫k dν dν, and C(K)=(inf over probability measures supported on compact K of I)^(-1), with C(empty)=0 and reciprocal infinity zero. For compact E define W_E(x)=∫_0^1 C(E∩closed B(x,r))r^(−s−1)dr and T(E)={x∈E:W_E(x)<∞}. Target: outer Newtonian capacity zero, hence polar, for T(E). All constants may change under a positive dimensional kernel normalization; finiteness and zero capacity are invariant.

Required boundaries: E empty or polar; isolated points; uncountable polar E; n=3 and arbitrary finite n≥3; tiny positive-capacity E; open vs closed balls; T(E) potentially nonclosed; divergent integrals; arbitrary translations and diameters. The claim over all x∈R^n is false: points outside E have positive distance from it and finite integrals, generally an open nonpolar set. No assertion for n=2's logarithmic capacity or n<2 is inherited from this formula.

Independent direct proof mechanism to compare against:

* Compact capacity is continuous along decreasing compact sets. Minimizing probabilities exist when C(K)>0 by weak compactness and lower semicontinuity of positive kernel energy. Moving closed balls give upper semicontinuous capacity in x for each r; truncated positive-radius integrals are continuous by radius-shift inclusions and dominated convergence at the countably many exceptional radii. Therefore W_E and T(E) are Borel.
* If T(E) has positive outer capacity, ordinary Borel capacitability selects compact F⊂T(E) of positive capacity and bounded W_E (first select a finite sublevel). Restricting a finite-energy probability to its support makes every nonempty neighborhood there have positive capacity. This avoids selecting a zero-capacity isolated point in a later localization.
* Put V(x)=W_F(x), M=sup_F V<∞. Choose a∈F with V(a)>M−ε/4; choose γ>0 with its tail ∫_γ^1 within ε/4 of V(a). Tail continuity provides a neighborhood where that tail exceeds M−3ε/4. Thus the small-radius integral is <3ε/4 on that neighborhood. Intersect F with a sufficiently small closed ball centered at a; positive capacity persists, the large-radius part is bounded by C(closed B(a,ρ))∫_γ^1 r^(−s−1)dr, tending to zero as ρ→0. This gives compact K of positive capacity, arbitrarily small diameter/capacity, and uniformly small W_K.
* For a minimizing probability ν on K, first variation implies Uν=1/C(K) ν-a.e. Lower semicontinuity gives Uν≤1/C(K) on suppν. For x outside suppν choose a nearest support point z; |z−y|≤2|x−y| gives Uν(x)≤D/C(K), D=2^s. This weak bound is elementary and does not cite a harmonic maximum principle or boundary regularity.
* Set μ=C(K)ν. Then μ(K)=I(μ)=C(K), Uμ≤D, and for a restriction to compact A, I(μ_A)≤Dμ(A), whereas I(μ_A)≥μ(A)^2/C(A); hence μ(A)≤DC(A). The same estimate for the intersections K∩closed balls controls local mass. Layer-cake gives Uμ(x)=s∫_0^∞ μ(closed B(x,r))r^(−s−1)dr. Splitting at 1 gives Uμ≤DsW_K+μ(K). Taking ε and C(K) sufficiently small makes this <1 on K, contradicting I(μ)=μ(K). Equivalent rescalings by D are acceptable if constants stay consistent.

Potential hidden circularity: assuming Kellogg, fine Choquet, Dirichlet irregular-point polarity, or a Wiener equivalence to conclude the measure exceptional set is polar; assuming equilibrium potential identically 1 everywhere on K; treating finite energy as bounded potential for arbitrary measures; applying compact capacitability to a non-Borel set; confusing Bessel energy with exactly Newtonian energy; forgetting x∈E. Ordinary Borel capacitability and the capacity-zero/polar equivalence are foundations independent of Kellogg if explicitly admitted. A result using them is direct at the classical potential-theory level, not a reconstruction of all capacity foundations.

Independent sharpness deductions: C(E∩closed B(x,r))≤(2r)^s follows from the diameter energy bound. Replacing the denominator r^(n−1) by r^(n−1−ε), ε>0, makes the integral finite at every x for every compact E; a compact ball then falsifies the polarity conclusion. Multiplying the original integrand by nonnegative w with ∫_0^1 w(r)dr/r<∞ has the same consequence. Uncountable compact polar sets can have W identically zero on E; binary Cantor intervals of length 2^(−m²) give covering capacity bounded by 2^m(2r_m)^s→0. These facts do not characterize arbitrary weights whose logarithmic integral diverges, arbitrary gauges, or all critical weakenings.
