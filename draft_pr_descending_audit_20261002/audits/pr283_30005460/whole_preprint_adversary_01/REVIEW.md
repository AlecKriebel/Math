## Analytic falsification attempts

**SOS closure and separation.** The proof avoids the false general inference that a linear image of a closed PSD cone must be closed. For v containing all monomials of degree≤d, G=Γ(vvᵀ) is PD: a nonzero polynomial cannot have zero square integral against a Gaussian density positive everywhere. If Q≥0 then tr(QG)≥λ_min(G)tr(Q), and coefficient convergence bounds Γ(vᵀQv). Thus the chosen PSD Gram matrices are bounded; a subsequence converges to a PSD Gram matrix for the limit. Strict separation from this closed convex cone gives L0(p)<0 and nonnegative values on SOS. L0(1)≥0. Adding εΓ makes the moment matrix PD, gives a positive value on 1, and preserves negativity when ε is sufficiently small. Normalization is consequently valid. The argument is finite-dimensional and does not presume a representing measure.

**Degree control.** If an SOS representation has a square summand of largest degree t, its degree-2t homogeneous component is a sum of squares of nonzero real t-forms. It cannot vanish identically: vanishing at every real point would force each form to vanish. Therefore an SOS polynomial of degree≤2d has only summands of degree≤d. The same argument rules out representing the non-SOS lower-degree seed using larger-degree summands in the separation cone. No hidden cancellation enlarges the domain that must be tested.

**Product positivity, including coupled summands.** For each finite s the product functional is defined on monomials of degree≤2d in each block. Every monomial in H of total degree≤d has local degree≤d, and every monomial of H² has local degree≤2d. The corresponding moment matrix is a principal submatrix of M^{⊗s}; PSD factors give a PSD product and hence a PSD principal submatrix. Equivalently, factor M=RᵀR and use R^{⊗s} on the zero-padded coupled coefficient vector. Arbitrary real coefficients across different blocks are covered. The argument is finite for the enormous N and requires neither an infinite product nor actual random-variable independence. Every monomial in S_s^q is also in the functional's domain because local degree is at most qm=2d.

**Partition counting and leading sign.** A function from the q labelled factors to s copies induces the partition into equal-index classes; assigning those labelled classes to distinct copies gives (s)_r choices. This proves the displayed partition identity without an extra r! factor. The singleton partition alone has q classes and leading coefficient a1^q. All collisions have degree≤q−1 in s, so with a1<0 and odd q the polynomial is strictly negative for sufficiently large finite integers s. The written bijection and finite-degree leading-term argument establish the general result; the portable checker's five small cubic counts are only finite controls.

**Convex-combination inference.** All p_i are in the same finite ambient space. Convexity implies closure under any finite convex combination by induction, so the negative-average certificate suffices. Scalar stability also permits the least failing sum prefix to give a violating two-input pair. This is an existence argument and assigns no SOS representation to any specified untested prefix. Positive rescaling of S_s^q cannot erase its negative functional value.

**Seed and arbitrary odd powers.** AM–GM applies to the three nonnegative monomials and yields a lower bound 3x²y²z²; hence the seed with negative coefficient 1 is nonnegative. The explicit moment functional gives L(p)=1+1+1−4=−1, so p is not SOS independently of the historical citation. The exact credited cube is SOS with positive weights. For odd q≥3, p^q=p³(p^{(q−3)/2})² is therefore SOS. Separation is applied anew with d=3q. The manuscript expressly forbids reusing a degree-18 functional outside its domain; no uniform n(q) follows or is claimed.

**Closedness and boundaries.** The coefficient power map is continuous and the applicable finite-degree SOS cone is closed. For odd q, f^q≥0 implies f≥0 pointwise, so the fixed-power set is that inverse image. Zero and nonnegative scalar multiplication are valid. Padding unused variables preserves the seed's SOS power, while a hypothetical SOS for the padded average would restrict to an SOS after setting the added variables to zero. q=1 and classical parameter cases with PSD=SOS are compatible: a non-SOS seed cannot satisfy the q=1 premise.
