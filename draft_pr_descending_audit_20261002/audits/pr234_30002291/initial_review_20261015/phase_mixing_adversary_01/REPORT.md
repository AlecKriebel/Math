## 1. Exact verified claim

Let N be a simple adapted counting process on (0,T], with predictable compensator

    A_t = integral_0^t lambda_s ds,
    0 <= lambda_s <= C < infinity      (P x ds)-almost everywhere,

where C is deterministic. The ambient sigma field F may contain the entire original randomness and need not be generated by N or by the filtration up to T. Let E_m={N_T=m}. On E_m write S_1<...<S_m for the ordered event times.

For every m>=1 the measure

    mu_m(B) = P(E_m, (S_1,...,S_m) in B)

has a density j_m on D_m={0<s_1<...<s_m<=T}, satisfying

    0 <= j_m(s) <= C^m             almost everywhere.                 (1)

For any deterministic positive sequence delta_n -> 0, every bounded F-measurable H, and every continuous function phi on the m-dimensional torus,

    E[1_E_m H phi({S_1/delta_n},...,{S_m/delta_n})]
      -> E[1_E_m H] integral_[0,1]^m phi(u) du.                      (2)

This is full-F stable convergence on each count event to m independent uniforms, independent of F. It remains valid jointly with every fixed finite collection of F-measurable real marks, whether or not those marks are bounded or independent of the times. Finite random count mixtures inherit this statement; under the displayed assumptions their tails satisfy P(N_T>M)<=CT/(M+1).

Here and below, a statement on E_m is void if P(E_m)=0. For m=0 the phase vector is empty and (2) is an identity. C=0 gives N_T=0 almost surely and is covered by this convention.

## 2. Reconstructing the factorial-measure domination

For nonnegative Borel h on D_m define

    F_m(h) = E sum_(R_1<...<R_m<=T) h(R_1,...,R_m),

where the sum runs over m-element subsets of all event times, in increasing order. There is no extra m! because the arguments already lie in the ordered simplex.

For m>=2 set

    Q_h(t) = sum_(R_1<...<R_(m-1)<t) h(R_1,...,R_(m-1),t).

This is predictable. One direct justification starts with product tests h=product_i f_i(s_i), with bounded nonnegative deterministic Borel f_i. The sum of products over the strictly earlier events is a left-continuous adapted process, and the deterministic Borel function f_m(t) is predictable. A functional monotone-class argument gives bounded Borel h; increasing truncation gives arbitrary nonnegative h. Simplicity and local finiteness make the finite-time sums well defined. For m=1, Q_h(t)=h(t) is deterministic predictable.

Compensate the final event, **before inserting an exact-count event or a future-measurable weight**:

    F_m(h) = E integral_0^T Q_h(t) dN_t
           = E integral_0^T Q_h(t) lambda_t dt
          <= C E integral_0^T Q_h(t) dt.                            (3)

Tonelli converts the last expression to C F_(m-1)(H_h), where

    H_h(s_1,...,s_(m-1))
      = integral_(s_(m-1))^T h(s_1,...,s_(m-1),t) dt.

Induction, beginning with E integral h dN<=C integral h dt, gives

    F_m(h) <= C^m integral_D_m h(s) ds.                            (4)

All quantities may initially be nonnegative extended integrals; the finite right side for bounded h then supplies finiteness. In particular,

    E binom(N_T,m) <= (CT)^m/m!,       E N_T <= CT.                 (5)

For an indicator h=1_B, on E_m there is exactly one ordered m-tuple. Thus

    mu_m(B) <= F_m(1_B) <= C^m Leb_m(B),

which proves (1). No conditional intensity under E_m was used. Conditional on E_m, the density is j_m/P(E_m), not necessarily bounded by C^m.

### Why the order of operations matters

It would generally be invalid to compensate 1_E_m H Q_h(t): E_m and H can depend on the future. Nor does conditioning on N_T=m preserve a bounded intensity. Even a Poisson process conditioned to have exactly one event before T has uniform event time and pre-event hazard 1/(T-t), which is unbounded near T. The frozen proof correctly obtains unconditional measure domination first, then introduces exact-count and full-field weights.

## 3. Full-field weights and the Fourier step

For bounded real or complex F-measurable H define the finite signed or complex measure

    mu_(m,H)(B) = E[1_E_m H 1_{S in B}].

Total-variation domination gives

    |mu_(m,H)|(B) <= ||H||_infinity mu_m(B).

Consequently it has an L1 density a_(m,H), extended by zero outside D_m, with

    |a_(m,H)(s)| <= ||H||_infinity j_m(s),
    ||a_(m,H)||_1 <= ||H||_infinity P(E_m).                       (6)

This is a Radon-Nikodym assertion about weighted measures; it does not require a regular conditional law on the full original space. In particular it does **not** assert that the distribution of S conditional on F has a density. If S is F-measurable, that conditional distribution is a point mass. There is no contradiction: stable convergence tests each fixed H, rather than an n-dependent H chosen to cancel the nth oscillation.

For q in Z^m, the corresponding weighted Fourier coefficient is

    E[1_E_m H exp(2*pi*i*q dot S/delta_n)]
      = integral_R^m a_(m,H)(s) exp(2*pi*i*q dot s/delta_n) ds.    (7)

At q=0 it equals E[1_E_m H]. At q!=0 it tends to zero. For completeness, this form of the Riemann-Lebesgue argument has a direct proof: approximate a_(m,H) in L1 by finite linear combinations of axis-aligned rectangle indicators. For a rectangle, choose j with q_j!=0; its jth one-dimensional oscillatory integral has absolute value at most delta_n/(pi*|q_j|), while all remaining factors are bounded by side lengths. Hence each rectangle integral tends to zero. The L1 approximation error bounds the oscillatory integral error uniformly in n.

Uniform approximation of continuous torus functions by trigonometric polynomials proves (2). The argument uses only delta_n -> 0. It does not require delta_n=1/n, monotonicity, nested meshes, rational meshes, or a rate condition.

### Marks, including unbounded marks

On E_m let R be any finite vector of F-measurable real variables. The weights H f(R), for bounded continuous f, are admissible in (2). Thus the phase vector converges jointly and stably with R to (R,U), where U has product-uniform law independent of all F.

A detailed extension to the random coefficient functions used here is as follows. On {|R|<=L}, continuous tests of (R,u) are uniformly approximable on the compact coefficient set times the phase torus by finite sums f_a(R) phi_a(u). Apply (2) to each term, then let L increase to infinity. The excluded probability tends to zero because R is finite almost surely; no moment assumption is needed for this fixed-count step. In the candidate's application, E sum J_k^2<=KT also gives a quantitative mark-tail bound.

No assumption that P(|J|=epsilon)=0 is required. The process with jumps |J|>epsilon is defined before taking n to infinity, and the coefficients and truncation indicators are fixed original variables throughout this limit.

### Count mixtures

For bounded test psi, truncate the sum over E_m to m<=M. The omitted expectation is bounded by ||H||_infinity ||psi||_infinity P(N_T>M), for both the prelimit and the independent-uniform extension. Equation (5) makes this tail tend to zero as M increases. This justifies the passage from every exact count to the full finite random count.

## 4. Transfer to W_alpha and the finite-jump statistic

For 0<alpha<1/2, the lth summand of W_alpha, for l>=3 and u in [0,1], is bounded by

    alpha^2 (l-2)^(2*alpha-2).

The bound is summable. The first two summands are continuous, so the defining series is uniformly convergent and W_alpha is bounded and continuous on [0,1]. At u=1 the first summand is zero and shifting the remaining index gives exactly W_alpha(1)=W_alpha(0). Thus W_alpha is continuous as a function on the torus, including mesh phases approaching an endpoint from either side. The torus tests required in Section 3 therefore include every bounded continuous test of a finite marked W_alpha sum.

Fix epsilon>0 and T=t_r. Under the candidate's hypotheses,

    lambda_s^epsilon = nu_s(|z|>epsilon) <= K/epsilon^2.

The preceding theorem applies directly to this simple truncated count. Its intensity is bounded in the original filtration; it need not be deterministic or autonomous. Moreover, for every deterministic t,

    E Delta N_t = E integral 1_{s=t} lambda_s^epsilon ds = 0.        (8)

Hence there are almost surely no truncated jumps at any of the finitely many observation horizons, or at time 0.

Take a path with finitely many truncated jumps, distinct times, finite marks, and no time equal to an observation horizon. The deterministic single-impulse formula (3.3) of the frozen proof applies to each impulse before each horizon. An impulse after that horizon contributes zero. The pairwise normalized inner products vanish by the disjoint-neighborhood and tail-energy argument in Section 3 of the candidate. Multiplication by the finitely many fixed values J_i J_j does not change the pathwise limit, even if those random marks have no deterministic bound. Thus the finite-impulse statistic differs almost surely by o(1) from the vector

    f(0)^2 sum_(i=1)^m J_i^2 1_{S_i<=t_j}
                               W_alpha({S_i/delta_n}),  j=1,...,r. (9)

If an absolutely continuous background A with square-integrable derivative is included, its normalized energy tends to zero. The cross terms with the finite impulse sum tend to zero by Cauchy-Schwarz: the normalized impulse energy is pathwise bounded, and the normalized background energy tends to zero. This verifies the addition of the prehistory/drift background to the finite-jump step, conditional on the background facts established elsewhere in the candidate.

Apply the full-field marked phase theorem to the coefficients

    c_(j,i)=J_i^2 1_{S_i<=t_j}.

They are fixed F-measurable variables. The map (c,u) -> sum_i c_(j,i) W_alpha(u_i) is continuous in coefficients and torus phases. This proves the finite-jump stable limit (6.1), including joint horizons. Equation (8) is needed for the impulse approximation at the right boundary: a jump at a horizon need not have accumulated its asymptotic kernel energy by that horizon. The frozen proof explicitly excludes this null event.

Distinct times need not have a deterministic positive separation. Every fixed finite realization has a positive minimum separation; the deterministic impulse result is applied pathwise. Near-diagonal events therefore introduce no unmentioned uniform-gap hypothesis.

### Common uniform marks for different truncations

The full jump collection can be measurably enumerated without repetition; a global chronological listing is not required when activity is infinite. Put independent uniforms U_k on that enumeration, independent of F. For a given truncation, its finite chronological subset corresponds to distinct F-measurable indices. Conditional on F, the selected uniforms have product-uniform law. Therefore this labeling realizes the same finite-truncation stable kernels and can be used consistently across epsilon, exactly as needed by the candidate's later converging-together construction.
