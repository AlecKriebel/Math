The submitted claim is a sufficient-condition fixed-time stable quadratic
variation limit for causal fractional convolutions of pure-jump Itô drivers.
The exact historical source and priority remain to be audited. This document
records the parent's independent analytic reconstruction, not acceptance.

The three mechanisms to verify separately are:

1. Deterministic impulse-energy localization. With `0<alpha<1/2`, the
   scaled single-jump energy has a continuous periodic phase function; tail
   summability and separated jump times eliminate cross terms. Near zero,
   the past derivative uses the distinct integrability exponent `2alpha-1`.
2. General point-process phase mixing. A bounded predictable truncated
   intensity gives factorial-moment measures dominated by Lebesgue measure.
   On each exact finite-count event the ordered-time law is consequently
   absolutely continuous. Bounded full-field weights retain an L1 density,
   so Riemann–Lebesgue gives independent limiting uniform phases. A future
   full-field weight must not be inserted directly into predictable compensation.
3. L2 truncation and prehistory. The past stochastic derivative is controlled
   by `K integral min(x,T)|g'(x)|²dx`. Bounded drift convolutions are AC and
   normalized quadratic variation vanishes like `Delta^(1-2alpha)`. Small
   future jumps have uniform normalized energy bounded by the integrated
   truncated second moment; reverse Euclidean norm triangle and stable
   converging-together remove the cutoff.

Initial inspection finds these mechanisms plausible under the exact stated
global deterministic bounded-characteristic assumptions and diffuse time

Fubini, joint horizons, integrability, and proof/program consistency require
full review. Deterministic jump times, alpha endpoints, singular drifts,
Brownian parts, and uncontrolled prehistory are excluded rather than silently
covered. No claim is made about functional J1 convergence.
