## Two distinct questions that must survive adversarial scrutiny

The original OWR2013 request specifies no maximal driver class. It asks for
an analogous marked-jump limit for a rather general pure-jump semimartingale
under sufficient conditions. A prior theorem can answer that request without
containing every driver in the present candidate. Conversely, a candidate
can enlarge one previously covered class without establishing that the
original open-ended request remained unanswered.

The positive priority test is therefore twofold: (a) was a substantive
semimartingale sufficient-conditions answer already proved, and (b) does an
actual previously stated theorem imply this entire exact candidate? Answers
to these questions may differ. It would be inaccurate to silently replace
(a) by (b), or to say full candidate coverage follows merely from a common
proof method. It would also be inaccurate to treat failure of (b) as proof
that the source question was still open.

Here is a direct derived specialization with a complete hypothesis map.
Take `g(x)=x_+^alpha exp(-x)`, `0<alpha<1/2`, `g0=0`, and a symmetric
pure-jump Levy driver with measure
`nu_L(dz)=exp(-|z|)|z|^-2 dz` on nonzero marks. Its BG index is1 and
`int z^2 nu_L(dz)=2`; it has an exponentially controlled large-jump tail.
Let `sigma` be any bounded adapted cadlag process, with bound `S`, for which
the usual stochastic integral is defined. The process `dY=sigma_- dL`
is a pure-jump semimartingale and is generally not Levy. Choose
`theta=2`, `rho=1`, `beta'=3`, `p=2`, `k=1` in the prior assumptions.

The kernel is bounded and inL2; its derivative is integrable away from
zero with exponential decay, behaves as `O(x^(alpha-1))` near zero, and
its absolute value is decreasing for sufficiently large positive x.
Indeed, for `x>alpha`, the logarithmic derivative of `|g'(x)|` is
`-1+(alpha-1)/x+1/(x-alpha)`, which is negative for all sufficiently
large x. Boundedness of sigma and exponential decay give both tail
integrals in(B1), with powers1 and3. The inequality `p>beta` is `2>1`,
and the rough-regime condition is exactly `alpha<1/2`. The prior
conclusion therefore gives the stable marked-jump quadratic-variation
limit for this whole adapted-volatility class at its stated mesh1/n, with
a functionalM1 statement as well as a fixed-time statement. This direct
specialization does not by itself prove the candidate's arbitrary-mesh
quantifier; that distinction must remain in the final comparison.

Its jumps are `J_T=sigma_(T-) Delta L_T`. Replacing each independent
uniform by `1-U` reindexes the prior `sum_(l>=0)h1(l+U)^2` into the
candidate's `W_alpha(U)`. The observation-independent `g0` offset would
cancel from increments if present. These are direct algebraic identities,

## Exact unresolved implications

state/path-dependent predictable compensator shapes, and some kernels
whose absolute derivative need not eventually decrease. Neither direct
specialization above covers that entire class. Conversely, prior heavy-tail

Consequently the classes are nonnested. No result in this reconstruction
proves that the exact candidate has already appeared, or that it is novel.
