## Tame finite isotropy

Take char(k)=p and the compatible prime-to-p Puiseux union L_tame. Each stage k((s_n)) is a Laurent field over the actual coefficient field k, and K(s_n)=k((s_n)) by grouping exponents modulo n. Temkin §2.2 and BRV Proposition5.6 describe the maximal tame field as L_tame times the maximal unramified extension. Its remaining separable Galois kernel is pro-p, including for imperfect residue fields. This preserves wild inertia; no false equality of absolute Galois groups with Gal(k) is used.

The Laurent coefficient criterion preserves p-linear independence of constants and proves K/k separable. Finite separable stages preserve it in L_tame. Algebraic elements over k in each stage are constants, so restriction Gal(L_tame)→Gal(k) is surjective. A finite étale gerbe with inertia F of order prime to p has a profinite extension 1→F→E→Gal(k)→1. A lift from Gal(L_tame) kills the pro-p kernel since its image in F would have order both a p-power and prime to p. It uniquely factors through Gal(k). Surjectivity also preserves all F-conjugation morphisms. This proves the claimed equivalence of object categories, including nonneutral gerbes, under the exact BRV Proposition5.5 separability hypothesis.

For a smooth homogeneous X under smooth affine G with tame finite geometric stabilizer, [X/G] is this finite étale gerbe. Its prescribed K-object comes from an actual X(K) point and a trivial G-torsor. Descending that whole object over L_tame gives a constant torsor E that becomes trivial over a finite Laurent stage. The credited Florence–Gille theorem gives E(k) nonempty, whose image gives X(k). Mere neutrality would not have supplied this conclusion for arbitrary G.

The Artin–Schreier class z^p−z=t^-1 over L_tame is not constant: at a stage t=s^n with p not dividing n, its pole has order n whereas b^p−b for a polar b has order divisible by p. Integral b cannot produce a pole. This falsifies wild full-object descent, and is not a k-defined homogeneous-space counterexample.

## Wild finite isotropy with H1-trivial acting group

BRV Proposition5.6 supplies a section of the inertia sequence for every henselian DVR; its proof uses tame splitting followed by cd_p(Gal(residue))≤1. Composing a K-object's group-extension lift with a section gives a k-object of any finite étale gerbe, regardless of inertia order. This is existence, not essential surjectivity of all prescribed K-objects. If H1(k,G) is trivial, its resulting G-torsor has a rational point and hence so does X. Hilbert90, determinant surjectivity and classification of alternating forms justify the stated GL,SL,Sp examples; no blanket claim for all split reductive groups follows.

The failure of a more general section-fixed-field argument is fully explicit. Over k=F_p(a,b), P:y^p−a x^p−x=b is a smooth additive-group torsor: the x-derivative is−1, the additive map is faithfully flat onto Ga, and differences of two fiber points lie in its kernel. After adjoining d with d^p=a, it is an affine line under z=y−dx. At the pole-of-b valuation with residue F_p(a), a is not a pth power. A negative leading term of x or y cannot cancel the pth-power terms: equal valuations would require a pth-power residue coefficient, unequal ones give a unique leading term. The surviving valuation is p times a negative integer, never v(b)=−1. Thus P(k) is empty. The analogous polynomial-leading-degree proof shows the smooth unipotent kernel is wound. This supplementary term is not needed for the torsor's nontriviality.

R=k[[t]][v]/(v^p−tv−a) is finite free of rank p. Modulo t its polynomial is irreducible, its unique maximal ideal is(t), and its residue is k(a^(1/p)). It is a one-dimensional regular local ring, thus a complete DVR and domain. Its fraction field has separable degree p over K (derivative−t), ramification index1 and purely inseparable residue degree p. These statements include p=2.

With c=(-b)^p t v, the displayed series delta has successive exponents p^j and coefficients(-a)^((p^j−1)/(p−1)). It converges and satisfies delta+a delta^p=c by termwise cancellation. For x=−b+delta,y=−bv, the defining torsor expression is b+c−delta−a delta^p=b. A section for this DVR field L, composed into Gal(K), is a section of restriction to Gal(k): finite separable constants induce the unramified extensions whose residue fields are their composites with the purely inseparable residue extension. Thus its fixed field contains L and trivializes P, despite P(k) being empty. The usual constant Laurent-torsor theorem is unaffected: P(K) is not asserted nonempty.

## Full Puiseux torus cohomology

For L_all, the union of every finite root stage including p-power roots, constant affine-group torsors retain trivial-kernel descent: points are finite-stage, and every stage is k-isomorphic to k((s)). Full H1 injectivity follows using isomorphism torsors under inner forms. Each stage retains relative algebraic closure of k and separability over k even though its extension over K can be inseparable. Constant finite separable splitting fields remain disjoint.

Let E/k split a torus T and Delta=Gal(E/k), M=X_*(T). Valuation plus the fixed uniformizer split T(E((s))) into T(E[[s]]) and M as Delta-modules. Galois descent works on E[[s]] because split torus torsors have zero Picard obstruction. Specialization H1(k[[s]],T)≅H1(k,T) follows from constant lifts and the smooth affine isomorphism torsor between any two lifts: a residue point lifts successively and completeness gives a section. No averaging by |Delta| is used. Accordingly H1(k((s)),T)≅H1(k,T)⊕H1(Delta,M). A root of ramification degree e multiplies the second summand by e; finite-group cohomology annihilates it by |Delta|. All object/isomorphism data have finite presentation, proving H1(k,T)≅H1(L_all,T), including wild splitting fields.

For multiplicative-type D, a surjection from a finite-rank permutation lattice to X^*(D) has free kernel. Character duality gives 1→D→Q→S→1 with Q quasitrivial and S torus, including torsion character modules such as mu_p. Brauer groups of constant splitting fields inject into their Laurent/root unions by proper Severi–Brauer specialization. Thus H2(k,Q) injects. H1(Q)=0 over both fields, and injectivity for S makes the connecting-image argument prove H2(k,D) injective. All cohomology here is fppf. Confusing this with H1 surjectivity for arbitrary D would be false.

Commutative geometric automorphisms of the quotient gerbe descend canonically to a band because conjugation is trivial. Stacks06NY verifies this descent; the full standard abelian-gerbe H2 classification and H1 twisting interpretation remain credited structural facts (BRV/Giraud), not newly reproved by the short Stacks0CJZ discussion. For a torus band, H2 injectivity gives neutrality and H1 surjectivity descends the difference torsor of a prescribed L_all-object. Descending the entire trivial-underlying-torsor object therefore gives an X(k) point and an orbit relation over L_all, not necessarily over K or k.

The mu_p class1+a t is not constant when a is not a pth power. At t=s^n, a pth-power Laurent expansion of(1+a s^n)/c requires both1/c and a/c to be pth powers (or already fails from exponent n if p does not divide n); their ratio contradicts the choice of a. Artin–Schreier t^-1 similarly remains nonconstant in the full union by removing p-factors from its pole exponent. Both are valid mechanism obstructions; neither supplies a counterexample to the original k-defined point-existence problem.

## Multiplicative-type envelopes

AOV AppendixA TheoremA.1 removes a flat finitely presented normal inertia subgroup, with no requirement that the removed torus be finite or central. Its quotient gerbe has finite tame inertia. Purely inseparable extension from L_tame to L_all preserves finite étale gerbes. Descent of its prescribed finite object followed by the torus-banded fiber's H2/H1 argument gives full-object essential surjectivity for smooth torus-by-tame-finite inertia. It does not identify all positive-dimensional automorphism groups over the two fields. The 2014 AOV correction invalidates a Lie-algebra deformation argument elsewhere; the cited AppendixA theorem is unchanged. The root read the correction in full.

For H of multiplicative type inside smooth affine J, Conrad Lemma2.2.4 makes C=Z_J(H) smooth even for infinitesimal H. Grothendieck's TheoremA.1.1 provides a geometrically maximal k-torus T of C^0 over imperfect k. H^0 is central in C^0 and lies schematically in T: over an algebraic closure it lies in the smooth connected Cartan centralizer of T, whose quotient by its central unique maximal torus is unipotent; a multiplicative-type group has trivial image in a unipotent group. Faithful flat descent then gives the assertion over k.

Because T centralizes H, S=HT is commutative of multiplicative type. S/T is a quotient of the finite prime-to-p étale H/H^0, so S is smooth with S^0=T. S/H=T/(T∩H) is a torus by the free character subgroup criterion, even if the intersection is infinitesimal. This envelope may be disconnected: the Klein-four subgroup of PGL2 in odd characteristic is multiplicative type and is not contained in a one-dimensional torus. The construction retains it rather than falsely forcing torality.

For [X/G] with multiplicative-type band M, H2 injectivity neutralizes the gerbe and gives a possibly nontrivial G-torsor E→X. Its equivariant automorphism group is a smooth affine inner form J. The object stabilizer H lies in J, and X=H\E. Its envelope S gives Y=S\E and a G-equivariant map X→Y. These homogeneous quotients are representable of finite type (after a finite separable trivialization of the smooth torsor they are standard quasiprojective homogeneous quotients). The smooth tame-torus-inertia result carries y=q(x) to y0∈Y(k) over L_all. The carried x lies in the fiber over y0, a torsor under the actual k-torus S/H because S is commutative. Finite-stage torsor descent supplies a fiber k-point. The non-smooth H is preserved throughout; bare mu_p object descent was not assumed.

## Reductive isotropy with tame Weyl/component order

Enhancing each gerbe object by a maximal torus of its connected reductive automorphism group gives an algebraic gerbe locally BN_H(T). Conrad's maximal-torus scheme theorem applies because reductive geometric identity components have self-centralizing maximal tori; it gives étale-local conjugacy, not rational conjugacy. The normalizer is smooth, its identity component is T, and its component quotient fits1→W(H^0)→pi0(N)→pi0(H)→1. Surjectivity follows by adjusting each component's torus by H^0 over an algebraic closure. The exact prime-to-p product assumption therefore puts the enhanced gerbe in the preceding tame-torus class. Grothendieck supplies a maximal torus over the actual K of the given object. Full descent of the enhanced object then descends the underlying G-torsor and gives the claimed rational point/orbit control. Neither the acting group nor its H1 is improperly assumed reductive or trivial.

The auxiliary cyclic algebra D with x^p−x=a/t,y^p=b,yxy^-1=x+1 has the stated degree-p central-simple structure; the generator convention is explicit and can be opposite to the book's. Its Artin–Schreier polynomial is irreducible by the non-p-divisible pole. After t=s^n and a further p-root stage, n=pq, w=s^q x gives w^p−s^(q(p−1))w=a and conjugation shift w→w+s^q. The finite free complete order on w^i y^j has reduction k(a^(1/p),b^(1/p)), a field of degree p² by independent a,b. Every nonzero reduction is a unit by two-sided convergent inversion. Scaling a nonzero basis expansion to least coefficient valuation gives a unit, proving division and a multiplicative valuation. Nonsplitting at every divisible stage precludes splitting at any earlier stage or at the union.

If this Brauer class were constant, equality would be detected at a finite stage. Constant division algebras remain division under Laurent extension, so the constant division representative would also have degree p. Its Laurent residue ring is the noncommutative constant division algebra. In D the residue ring is instead the commutative purely inseparable degree-p² field. These valuations are intrinsic: det(left multiplication)=Nrd^p, and for s^m times an order unit its determinant valuation is m p². An algebra isomorphism thus preserves the valuation and residue ring, a contradiction. The reduced-norm identity is valid in characteristic p as well. This disproves general reductive H1 surjectivity over L_all; it does not disprove point descent for k-defined spaces or show the Weyl hypothesis necessary for that problem.
