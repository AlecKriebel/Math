## Exact original problem and success criteria

Oberwolfach Report 52/2019, Philippe Gille with Mathieu Florence, printed pp. 3264–3265 (PDF pages 22–23), states the established torsor implication: for an affine algebraic k-group G and a G-torsor X, a k((t))-point entails a k-point. It then asks whether this implication generalizes to homogeneous spaces, already over a field; characteristic zero is attributed to Florence. This is a separate question from the adjacent extension of torsor statements over arbitrary rings. A general solution must cover the original homogeneous-space class in the remaining positive-characteristic setting, and cannot follow merely by replacing a homogeneous space by an arbitrary torsor without proving that replacement has the right rational-point information. There is no counterexample or general proof in the original source.

The Florence primary paper was independently read at its introduction, definitions (PDF pp. 1–3), Proposition 4.7 and reduced-stabilizer reduction (pp. 11–12), and its final rational-point results (p. 15). It fixes a perfect base field throughout and proves homogeneous-space descent through an acyclic extension containing k((t)); its introduction states the Laurent-series implication explicitly. Thus the primary paper covers perfect fields, beyond the original workshop's narrower characteristic-zero attribution. It does not justify applying its theorem over imperfect fields by deleting this standing assumption. The 'Lemme 5.5' numbering in the introduction is not identical to the final draft's Lemma 5.5, which is a birational smooth-point-to-Laurent-point construction; the implication comes from Proposition 5.4 plus Proposition 4.7.

Florence–Gille 2019 independently read at PDF pp. 1–3: its torsor result and residues do not themselves give the homogeneous-space extension. Gille's Pisa notes introduction and definitions independently read at pp. 1–2 give the wound/anisotropic group framework, with smooth affine group conventions; no universal homogeneous descent was inferred from them.

## Independent hazards and falsifiable checks

1. A Laurent point can escape a compactification boundary, and specializing a point in a proper model gives a point on the model, not automatically on the original open homogeneous space.
2. Finite or tame stabilizers, neutral gerbes and stabilizer torsors must be distinguished; geometric or local sections do not automatically descend to a k-point.
3. An imperfect base field creates inseparable residue orbits and nonreduced stabilizer hazards. Any alteration may change the base field or acquire residue extensions; a prime-to-p or p-power zero-cycle does not automatically have degree one.
4. A rank-zero residue is a multiplicative homomorphism and a positive rank residue is additive. Each route must justify returning to an integral point rather than merely lowering an invariant on an unrelated object.
5. Smooth group actions may have nonsmooth stabilizers; deleting this possibility requires an explicit restricted conclusion.
6. All finite computational examples must remain controls for formulas or specific constructions, not evidence for universal descent.
7. The remaining general question cannot be replaced by the unsupported claim that the neutralization gerbe has a k-object merely because it has a k((t))-object.
