## 1. Tame finite bands: proof reconstructed

For n prime to p, k((t^(1/n))) is a degree-n separable extension of k((t)), and retains k as its coefficient field. The directed union L_tame is separable over k and has k relatively algebraically closed. Coefficientwise p-independence verifies separability even over an imperfect coefficient field. The valuation argument for relative algebraic closure needs the fact that any nonzero algebraic constant-field element has valuation zero; subtracting the leading constant produces positive valuation and hence forces zero.

Temkin §2.2.3–2.2.6 (PDF p.8) explicitly supplies maximal unramified extension and pro-p wild inertia without a perfect-residue hypothesis. BRV Proposition 5.6 (PDF p.14) supplies K_tame = K_ram K_unramified and the tame splitting fixing chosen uniformizer roots. Therefore Gal(L_tame) surjects onto Gal(k) with pro-p kernel P, rather than being isomorphic to Gal(k). The candidate's retained P is essential.

A finite étale gerbe is described by 1 → F → E → Gal(k) → 1. BRV Proposition 5.5 (p.13) applies to separable extensions without requiring finiteness. An L_tame-object is a lift of restriction; its image on P is simultaneously a p-group and a subgroup of prime-to-p F, hence trivial. It descends uniquely to a continuous section. Surjectivity of restriction also reflects all conjugation identities, yielding full faithfulness. This verifies actual equivalence of finite-gerbe groupoids, including nonneutral ones, rather than a neutrality shortcut.

For [X/G], a Laurent point gives an object with trivial G-torsor. The descended k-object's G-torsor becomes trivial at a finite Laurent stage. Florence–Gille Proposition 5.2(3) (v3 PDF pp.16–17) is precisely the constant affine-group torsor theorem; it gives a k-point on this specific torsor. Evaluation into X proves the restricted conclusion. No arbitrary homogeneous-space theorem is used circularly.

The Artin–Schreier obstruction has pole order n prime to p at a tame stage, while b^p−b at negative valuation has pole divisible by p. Adding a coefficient-field constant cannot change a negative pole. Thus it is an exact failure of object-surjectivity for a wild band, not a k-defined homogeneous counterexample.

## 2. Wild finite inertia and section-fixed-field failure

BRV Proposition 5.6 proves the full inertia sequence splits for every henselian discrete valuation ring. Its proof lifts a tame section using cd_p(Gal(residue field)) ≤ 1. It does not claim functorial compatibility; Remark 5.8 warns of precisely that limitation. Composing a K-object lift with one section gives neutrality of any finite étale gerbe over k. For H1(k,G) = 1 the resulting G-torsor must be trivial. GL_n, SL_n, Sp_2n are valid examples, and the proof does not generalize this assumption to every split reductive group.

The auxiliary imperfect-field torsor is independently checked algebraically. For k=F_p(a,b), U is the kernel of y^p−a x^p−x, a smooth surjective additive map (derivative in x is −1); its fiber at b is a U-torsor P. Over k(a^(1/p)), z=y−a^(1/p)x identifies U with z^p−x=0 and P with z^p−x=b, so both are smooth and geometrically integral. At the b-infinity valuation, leading pth-power terms cannot cancel because a is not a pth power in F_p(a). A negative leading pole of P's left side is therefore divisible by p, contrary to b's pole one; nonnegative poles are impossible too. This proves P(k) empty for every p, including p=2.

R=k[[t]][v]/(v^p−tv−a) is finite free. Reduction is a field, so R is local with maximal ideal (t); t is a nonzerodivisor and t-adic separation makes any nonzero element a power of t times a unit. Hence R is a domain and DVR. Its fraction field L/K is separable degree p (derivative −t), with ramification index one and purely inseparable residue degree p; a perfect-field unramified simplification would be false.

Set c=(-b)^p tv and δ=Σ_j (-a)^((p^j−1)/(p−1)) c^(p^j). T-adic valuation tends to infinity, and adjacent terms cancel in δ+aδ^p=c, including characteristic two. Substituting x=-b+δ and y=-bv gives y^p−ax^p−x=b. Thus P(L) is nonempty. A henselian section for L has residue Galois group canonically Gal(k), since the residue extension is purely inseparable. Constant finite separable extensions identify the section with a section of Gal(K)→Gal(k); its fixed field contains L. P becomes trivial over that fixed field without a k-point. This refutes the claimed extension of constant-torsor injectivity to arbitrary section-fixed fields, while leaving the original constant Laurent theorem intact.

## 3. Tori and multiplicative-type H2

Let L_all contain every root of the parameter, keeping constants unchanged at every finite stage. Constant torsor injectivity follows stagewise; the isomorphism-torsor argument gives full injectivity of H1, using inner forms to reflect equality of two classes.

For a torus split by finite Galois E/k with group Δ, E((s)) is a constant splitting extension. Split torus Hilbert 90 gives H1(k((s)),T)=H1(Δ,T(E((s)))). The coefficient group splits Δ-equivariantly into T(E[[s]]) and the cocharacter lattice by the valuation with section λ↦λ(s). Smooth affine torsors over k[[s]] with the same special fiber are isomorphic by successive lifting and completeness; this establishes H1(k[[s]],T)=H1(k,T) without averaging by |Δ|. Since E[[s]] has Picard group zero, Galois descent identifies the first summand. Thus H1(k((s)),T)=H1(k,T)⊕H1(Δ,X_*(T)). Pullback s=u^e multiplies the second summand by e. Finite-group H1 is annihilated by |Δ|; adjoining all parameter roots kills this part. This remains valid when p divides |Δ|. Finite presentation makes the direct limit argument legitimate.

For any finite-type multiplicative-type D, a permutation-lattice surjection onto X^*(D) has free kernel, yielding 1→D→Q→S→1 with Q quasitrivial and S a torus. This includes torsion character modules such as μ_p. H1(Q)=0 and Brauer injectivity of constant finite separable fields into Laurent stages follows by proper Severi–Brauer specialization. In the H2 long exact sequence, any class killed over L_all first maps to zero in H2(Q), hence comes from H1(k,S); since H1(L_all,Q)=0 it must have zero image in H1(L_all,S), and constant torsor injectivity forces it to vanish. This proves H2 injectivity, without asserting arbitrary H1 surjectivity for D.

Commutative automorphisms descend canonically to a band: the isomorphism identification is independent of choices because conjugation is trivial. Stacks 06NY Lemma 8.11.8 states this exact fact; 0CJZ explains the H1/H2 obstruction mechanism but is not alone a full classification theorem. The standard Giraud/abelian-gerbe framework used here is also credited in Florence and BRV. For torus bands, H2 injectivity yields a k-object and H1 surjectivity matches the prescribed L_all-object. Its underlying G-torsor then descends and is trivialized by the stagewise theorem. The retained isomorphism gives the asserted orbit relation only over L_all.

Two auxiliary nonconstant objects remain nonconstant. The μ_p example 1+at would require both 1/c and a/c to be pth powers at any finite parameter-root stage, forcing a a pth power. Artin–Schreier pole p^e m reduces modulo coboundaries to pole m prime to p; adjoining parameter roots never removes the final wild pole. Neither produces a counterexample to descent of a k-space.

## 4. Smooth envelopes do handle infinitesimal multiplicative type

AOV Appendix A Theorem A.1 (printed pp.1087–1089; PDF pp.32–34) rigidifies flat finitely presented normal inertia without a finiteness or centrality requirement. Its local model BH→B(H/H0) verifies the torus-identity component case. The 2014 corrigendum was read completely: it discards a Lie-algebra method in Lemma 2.14 and corrects deformation text in Proposition 3.6; it leaves Appendix A unchanged. No invalidated proof method is required here.

The full Puiseux extension over the tame union is purely inseparable, so finite étale gerbe object categories are unchanged. Tame finite object matching gives a k-object of the rigidified gerbe with its chosen isomorphism. Its 2-fiber is a torus-banded gerbe; the preceding H2 and H1 arguments match the full lifted object. This verifies essential surjectivity only: positive-dimensional torus automorphisms grow over L and whole-groupoid equivalence would be false.

For H of multiplicative type in a smooth affine J, C=Z_J(H) is smooth by Conrad Lemma 2.2.4, read at pp.64–66 with its scheme-theoretic hypotheses. Grothendieck's theorem A.1.1 (p.267) gives a geometrically maximal k-torus T of C0 even for imperfect k. H0 is central in C0. Over an algebraic closure, a central multiplicative-type subgroup maps trivially to the unipotent quotient of a Cartan group, so it lies schematically in T. This uses absence of a nontrivial multiplicative-type quotient inside a unipotent group, not geometric-points-only containment. Conrad's Cartan definition and discussion (pp.268–269) and classical connected torus centralizer theorem 1.1.19 were checked. H need not be contained in a torus: T centralizes all H, so S=HT is instead a commutative multiplicative-type envelope with finite prime-to-p component group and S/H a torus. Smoothness of S follows from its torus identity component and étale component quotient. The PGL_2 Klein-four example correctly warns against a false torality assumption.

For [X/G] with multiplicative-type stabilizer, H2 injectivity first makes the gerbe neutral, producing E→X. In J=Aut_G(E) embed H into S. X=H\E maps to Y=S\E; Y is smooth with torus-by-tame-finite stabilizer. An L-point of X moves to a fiber over a k-point of Y by the established orbit control. Because S is commutative and H normal, that fiber is an actual k-torus S/H torsor. A finite Laurent stage point specializes by the credited constant-torsor theorem. This supplies the missing object control for μ_p; it does not falsely assert μ_p H1 surjectivity.

## 5. Torus enhancement and the Brauer obstruction

For smooth H with reductive H0, maximal tori form a smooth quasi-affine scheme with local conjugacy, by Conrad Theorem 3.2.6 (pp.94–95), whose hypothesis that connected fiber Cartan subgroups be tori is satisfied by reductivity. Adding a chosen maximal torus to a gerbe object therefore gives a gerbe locally BN_H(T). Normalizer smoothness follows from Proposition 2.1.2; N0=T and π0(N) is an extension of π0(H) by W(H0). Each H-component sends T to a conjugate under H0, which proves surjectivity on components. The Weyl quotient is finite étale by Proposition 3.2.8. The prime-to-p order hypothesis thus genuinely applies the prior torus-by-tame-finite lemma. A K-object can be enhanced using a maximal torus over the actual field K (Grothendieck), without asserting rational conjugacy. Forgetting a descended full enhanced object retains the trivialization of the underlying G-torsor. This proves the restricted theorem and its L-orbit statement.

For k=F_p(a,b), D has degree p by the usual Artin–Schreier cyclic algebra construction, independently checked against Gille–Szamuely §§2.5–2.6 (PDF pp.49–53). Their displayed conjugation convention is opposite to the candidate's; the candidate fixes its own convention explicitly and only uses convention-independent central simplicity and norm facts. At a stage t=s^(pq), w=s^q x gives w^p−s^(q(p−1))w=a and ywy^-1=w+s^q. These relations close the finite free order with basis w^i y^j. Modulo s the ring is F_p(a,b)[a^(1/p),b^(1/p)], a field of degree p² by p-independence. Every nonzero reduction has a lift inverse on both sides by completeness. Hence every nonzero algebra element is s^m times a unit and the algebra is division. Smaller stages cannot split it if a further p-divisible stage remains division. Finite presentation reflects any splitting or equality of Brauer classes over the union to a finite stage.

A constant division algebra A0 remains division over k((s)), with unchanged index and residue A0. If D_L were constant, its degree-p division representative would be isomorphic at a stage to A0((s)). Both least-coefficient valuations are intrinsic: left multiplication has determinant valuation mp², while determinant(left multiplication)=Nrd^p, so valuation equals v_K(Nrd)/p. This identity is Gille–Szamuely Proposition 2.6.3 and has a direct split-matrix proof valid in characteristic p. An algebra isomorphism therefore preserves residue rings. D's residue is commutative purely inseparable degree p²; A0's residue is a noncommutative central division degree-p algebra. They cannot be isomorphic. Thus H1(k,PGL_p)→H1(L_all,PGL_p) is not surjective. This is a nonconstant algebra over a Laurent field, not a constant algebra split by that field. It only blocks the blanket reductive-object-surjectivity shortcut.
