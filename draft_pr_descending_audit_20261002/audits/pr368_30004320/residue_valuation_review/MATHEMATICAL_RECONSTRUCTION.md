## Turn1: tame finite inertia

The union of prime-to-p Laurent root stages has finite stages k-isomorphic to k((s)), so every CONSTANT affine-group torsor trivialized in that union descends by Florence–Gille; being constant is essential. Temkin's tame/wild decomposition works for imperfect residue fields and BRV's chosen-uniformizer splitting identifies Gal(K^t/L_tame) with Gal(k). The kernel in Gal(L_tame) is wild inertia, pro-p, not zero. The separability condition L_tame/k is valid coefficientwise: any finite k^p-independent constants remain independent over k((t))^p by comparing Laurent coefficients, then finite separable stage extensions preserve separability. Relative algebraic closure of k follows because an algebraic nonzero Laurent element has value0 (a constant-coefficient minimal polynomial rules out nonzero value), and subtracting its residue gives another algebraic element of positive value, impossible.

For a finite étale gerbe, BRV Proposition5.5 describes a given object as a lift s:Gal(L_tame)→E of restriction in1→F→E→Gal(k)→1. The pro-p kernel maps to a p-subgroup of finite F, hence trivial if |F| is prime to p. Factorization through Gal(k) is continuous by profinite quotient topology. All morphisms are F-conjugacies, and surjectivity gives full faithfulness. Thus full object descent, not just neutrality, is established. Its forgetful G-torsor is specifically trivialized over L_tame, so finite presentation plus a Laurent stage and the source torsor theorem yields a k-point of that torsor and of X. Nonconnected smooth G is permitted.

The Artin–Schreier t^-1 control is exact: in t=s^n with p∤n, its pole is n while any b^p−b with negative value has pole divisible by p. Adding a constant cannot change that leading pole. It obstructs essential surjectivity for wild inertia; it is a torsor defined over the extension and therefore is not an original-problem counterexample.
