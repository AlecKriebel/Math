## Original quantified problem

Oberwolfach Report52/2019, Philippe Gille's contribution with Mathieu Florence, printed3264–3265, PDF22–23, first states Laurent-point descent for torsors under affine algebraic groups over any field. The distinct open question asks whether this conclusion extends to homogeneous spaces over a field. For a field k, K=k((t)), an affine algebraic k-group G and a homogeneous k-variety X under G, the desired implication is X(K) nonempty implies X(k) nonempty. Success requires a theorem with all specified field/group/space hypotheses or a counterexample satisfying them. A proof just for torsors, perfect fields, proper spaces, a special stabilizer or a special additive equation is a partial result. The adjacent question about arbitrary base rings and torsors is different and must not be substituted.

## Independent mechanisms and falsification boundaries

Proper varieties satisfy the point-existence implication by the valuative criterion for k[[t]] followed by specialization. This says nothing about extending a point inside an affine/open homogeneous space: specialization may hit its boundary. A claimed compactification route needs a proof that the specialized point belongs to the original open set.

The only elements of k((t)) algebraic over k are constants in k. An element algebraic over k cannot have negative valuation (the highest-degree nonzero constant coefficient in its minimal polynomial gives a unique least-valuation term). After integrality, reducing the minimal polynomial at t=0 gives a root in k, hence the irreducible minimal polynomial is linear. This argument includes inseparable algebraic elements. It controls finite reduced points and polynomial equations with constant coefficients, but not all positive-dimensional homogeneous spaces.

For an additive polynomial over characteristic p, Laurent exponents and Frobenius interact: nonzero terms may cancel when exponent pairs satisfy integer ratios by powers of p. Any argument that takes only the most negative term must track all coordinates, coefficient p-dependencies, characteristic and leading exponents, and must explain why cancellation cannot continue. Finite truncated Laurent searches are not proofs for all series. Smooth additive-group kernels require a nonzero linear differential; dropping it can introduce nonreduced or purely inseparable behavior. Imperfect coefficients cannot be silently replaced by their pth roots inside k.

For norm torsors, a constant-coefficient norm equation over L((t)) may reduce to a scalar norm over L, but the valuation of the norm and unit normalization must be justified. The general torsor theorem already credits such descent; checking a new coordinate presentation alone does not resolve the homogeneous-space question.

For homogeneous G/H, surjectivity of G(K) onto X(K) is not automatic. A rational point has an H-torsor obstruction, and even a transporter argument needs scheme-theoretic stabilizer hypotheses. Claims using H1 or central extensions must distinguish pointed-set kernels from global injectivity and justify twisting by every relevant form. Relative smoothness and connectedness are not interchangeable with geometric reducedness over imperfect k.

Falsifiable checks therefore include negative/zero/positive valuations, characteristic2 and odd primes, pth-power coefficient dependence and independence, missing linear coefficients, disconnected/infinitesimal stabilizers, nonproper boundary specialization, exact torsor versus homogeneous-space scope, and all quantifiers in any proposed reduction. A route is blocked if it simply assumes Laurent descent for the same unsupported general stabilizer problem.
