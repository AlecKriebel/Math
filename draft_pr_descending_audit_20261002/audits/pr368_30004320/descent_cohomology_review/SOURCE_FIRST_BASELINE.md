The original problem is the final question in Philippe Gille's joint-with-Mathieu-Florence contribution to Oberwolfach Report 52/2019, printed page 3265 (PDF page 23). Let k be a field, G an affine algebraic k-group (the report's introductory convention is smooth affine), and X a homogeneous G-space. Does X(k((t))) nonempty imply X(k) nonempty? A homogeneous space is not implicitly a torsor. Geometric transitivity allows nontrivial, disconnected or non-smooth stabilizers; the original report does not restrict stabilizers in this sentence. The field may be imperfect and the question does not assume resolution or properness. One must distinguish constant varieties over k from arbitrary objects over k((t)).

A complete affirmative outcome would prove this implication for all original k,G,X, or precisely justify any convention-level narrowing; a complete negative outcome would give such a homogeneous space with a Laurent-series point and no k-point, and verify geometric transitivity and each field/hypothesis. A theorem for torsors alone, perfect fields alone, projective X alone, finite stabilizers alone, or certain p-primary obstructions is partial. A claim of current openness needs a separate contemporary literature audit; a historical question alone is insufficient.

The source itself proves Laurent descent for affine-group torsors and cites the characteristic-zero homogeneous-space result. The independently acquired Florence primary, however, assumes perfect fields globally and gives a larger old result. Its introduction, conventions, Proposition 4.4, Proposition 4.7, Lemma 4.8, Proposition 5.4 and Theorem 5.6 were read directly, including the mechanisms relating cohomology and homogeneous-space points. In particular, it constructs a perfect extension K-infinity containing k((t)) whose absolute Galois group identifies with that of k and whose restriction on H1 for every group is bijective. Nonabelian H2 neutralization plus twisting by bitorsors then descends homogeneous-space points. Its reduced-stabilizer replacement is justified over perfect extensions. The literal introduction's reference to Lemma 5.5 for Laurent descent is compressed: 5.5 produces a Laurent point from a smooth birational point, while 4.7 + 5.4 yields Laurent descent. Thus the mathematical statement for perfect fields, including positive characteristic, is classical; the report's short characteristic-zero parenthesis should not be misread as excluding positive perfect characteristic.

The Florence--Gille 2019 and 2021 primaries separately retain the homogeneous-space question. Their torsor theorem is injectivity, established with twisting, of H1(k,G) -> H1(k((t)),G). Their wound-group equality G(k[[t]])=G(k((t))) controls points of the group itself and is not an automatic properness criterion for every quotient. A rational point of X need not lift to a rational point of G unless its stabilizer torsor is trivial. Conversely, an X-point often permits a twisted-group torsor presentation only after a gerbe neutralizes; overlooking that step would transfer the central difficulty.

Independent boundaries and possible falsifiers:

1. For proper X, every k((t))-point extends over k[[t]] by the valuative criterion and specializes to k; homogeneous transitivity is unnecessary. For arbitrary affine X, an escaping arc does not extend automatically. A compactification can gain rational boundary points which say nothing about X(k).
2. Rational constants, positive valuation, negative valuation, inseparability, and whether the constant field is relatively algebraically closed in k((t)) must be distinguished. Any constant algebraic element of k((t)) is in k by taking its lowest nonconstant coefficient in a polynomial relation after separating the separable and purely inseparable parts. Thus finite constant-scheme points descend; ramified extensions of k((t)) do not manufacture constant-field algebraic elements.
3. Smooth groups over imperfect fields may have non-smooth stabilizers. Galois H1/H2 alone need not represent their fppf torsors or gerbes. Alpha-p and mu-p behave differently: alpha-p has additive inseparable phenomena, whereas mu-p is diagonalizable and linearly reductive despite being nonreduced. Étale finite groups of order divisible by p need not be linearly reductive.
4. For fppf mu-p or alpha-p torsors over k, a Laurent solution of x^p=a or an Artin--Schreier equation with constant right side should be checked coefficient by coefficient; this gives injectivity for specific constant classes but does not settle arbitrary noncommutative homogeneous-space gerbes.
5. A birational smooth-point argument requires a smooth rational point. A singular boundary point cannot be substituted. An alteration of p-power degree can create a residue extension of p-power degree without a rational point downstairs; degree conditions by themselves require a verified restriction/corestriction or point-descent argument.
6. Finite tame gerbes or multiplicative-type stabilizers are a plausible useful subfamily. For non-smooth linearly reductive groups, deformation proofs must incorporate the Abramovich--Olsson--Vistoli 2014 corrigendum: their Lie-algebra lifting proof is withdrawn, while the cotangent-complex proof remains. Nontrivial deformations may exist even when torsor obstructions vanish.
7. Iterated Laurent fields, change of parameter, extension of constants, dimension zero, and trivial group provide limiting tests. A valid universal one-variable theorem can be iterated, but a theorem requiring perfectness cannot be iterated blindly through a positive-characteristic Laurent field, which is imperfect.
