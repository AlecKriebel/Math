## Original scope and strongest positive scope

The original question quantifies over arbitrary fields and homogeneous spaces under affine groups: whether a Laurent point implies a constant-field point. The candidate supplies several restricted affirmative theorems for smooth affine acting groups and smooth homogeneous varieties, and auxiliary counterexamples to object-surjectivity methods. The strongest geometric classes proved are all multiplicative-type stabilizers, and smooth stabilizers with reductive identity component and both geometric Weyl-group order and component-group order prime to the characteristic. Finite étale wild stabilizers are additionally covered when H1(k,G) is trivial. They do not collectively include general wild/noncommutative or unipotent isotropy.

The prior torsor theorem and Florence's perfect-field homogeneous-space theorem remain external, explicitly credited mathematical inputs. Their hypotheses were checked directly. The argument does not silently apply Florence's theorem to an imperfect field. I certify the scoped deductions relative to those standard theorems; I do not independently reprove their entire publications or establish novelty/current openness.

## Turn 1: finite tame isotropy

For positive characteristic p, the prime-to-p Puiseux union is separable over k. Coefficientwise p-linear independence shows k((t))/k is separable as a possibly transcendental extension; a finite separable root extension and a directed union retain separability. Every algebraic constant in a Laurent stage is in k: a nonzero algebraic element has valuation zero, and subtracting its constant coefficient would otherwise give an algebraic element of strictly positive valuation, impossible in a polynomial with nonzero extreme coefficients. Thus constant separable splitting fields remain linearly disjoint.

The actual kernel of Gal(L_tame)->Gal(k) is pro-p, not zero. Temkin §2.2.3--2.2.6 and the complete BRV §5.2--5.3 text verify the imperfect-residue tame/wild decomposition and coefficient-field splitting. A full object of a finite étale gerbe is a lift to an extension with finite kernel F. Its restriction to pro-p inertia has p-group image, hence is zero when p does not divide |F|. Factorization through the profinite quotient is continuous and unique. Morphisms are F-conjugations, and surjectivity of the constants map gives exactly the same conjugation conditions. This proves an equivalence of groupoids, not merely existence of a section.

For [X/G], the object from x has a trivial underlying G-torsor. Descending that specific object and its isomorphism over L_tame makes the descended constant torsor trivial at some finite Laurent stage. The credited constant torsor theorem then trivializes it over k. Sending its rational point to X is legitimate. No claim that G(M)->X(M) is generally surjective is used.

The Artin--Schreier torsor with pole t^-1 is an auxiliary object-surjectivity counterexample. At a prime-to-p stage its pole order is not divisible by p, whereas a negative-valuation b^p-b has pole order divisible by p. Constants cannot alter this contradiction. The torsor is defined over the extension field; it is not a constant homogeneous-space counterexample.

## Turn 2: wild étale inertia and the fixed-section failure

BRV Proposition 5.6 applies to henselian discrete valuation rings without a perfect-residue assumption. A K-object lift composed with a Galois section therefore gives a k-object of every finite étale gerbe. This is neutrality/existence only; preserving the original K-object is not guaranteed. If H1(k,G) is trivial, the resulting G-torsor has a k-point and the conclusion follows. GL_n, SL_n and symplectic groups are valid examples, including alternating forms in characteristic two; all split reductive groups are not covered by this assertion.

I independently checked the constant wound-unipotent torsor construction. The map (x,y)->y^p-a x^p-x is a smooth surjective additive morphism, its derivative in x being -1. After adjoining d^p=a its kernel becomes the affine line via x=z^p, y=z+d z^p, so the kernel and fibers are smooth geometrically integral. At the b-pole valuation, any negative leading term has valuation p m. If x and y have the same negative valuation, cancellation would require a to be a p-th power in the residue field F_p(a), which it is not. If their valuations differ, the smallest term is unique. The linear x term has strictly larger valuation than p m. Consequently the constant fiber b has no k-point.

The monic v-polynomial v^p-t v-a has irreducible reduction; its finite free complete local ring has maximal ideal (t), hence is a regular local domain of dimension one and a DVR. Fraction-field degree is p and separability follows from derivative -t. Residue degree p is purely inseparable; ramification index one does not mean the residue extension is separable or unchanged.

For c=(-b)^p t v, let d_j=(-a)^(1+p+...+p^(j-1)), with d_0=1. The coefficients satisfy d_j=-a d_(j-1)^p. Thus in the convergent series delta=sum d_j c^(p^j), all terms of delta+a delta^p cancel except c. Substituting x=-b+delta, y=-b v gives b. This is a proof of the complete series identity for every p, independent of a finite truncation.

The residue's purely inseparable extension preserves the absolute Galois group, so a section for this DVR embeds into Gal(K), and its fixed field contains the bad residue extension L. Triviality of this particular constant torsor over that fixed field therefore does not reflect to k. The example does not contradict the Laurent torsor theorem: L need not be k-isomorphic to a Laurent field with coefficient k.

## Turn 3: torus objects and multiplicative-type obstruction classes

Each full Puiseux stage, including inseparable roots of t, is k-isomorphic to k((s)). Finite presentation and the torsor theorem imply injectivity of constant affine-group H1 over the union; an isomorphism torsor under the appropriate inner form gives full injectivity rather than just the trivial kernel. Although the extension over K includes inseparability, each stage over k is separable/regular; fppf cohomology is used throughout.

For a finite Galois torus splitting field E/k with group Delta and cocharacter lattice M, split-torus Hilbert 90 identifies H1(k((s)),T) with group cohomology of T(E((s))). Valuation gives a Delta-equivariant direct product T(E[[s]]) times M, since the parameter is fixed. The unit factor's H1 is H1(k[[s]],T): the splitting cover is finite étale and the split-torus Picard group is zero. Completeness and smoothness of the Isom torsor identify that group with H1(k,T). This argument does not divide by |Delta| and covers wild splitting fields. Changing s to u^e multiplies the lattice summand by e while leaving the constant summand fixed. Finite-group H1(Delta,M) is killed by |Delta|, so the direct limit kills that entire summand. Both surjectivity and injectivity of H1(k,T)->H1(L,T) follow.

A finite-type multiplicative-type character module has a surjection from a finite permutation lattice whose kernel is free. Cartier/character duality then embeds the possibly non-smooth D into a quasitrivial torus Q with torus quotient S. The Brauer groups of the finite separable constant factors inject over each Laurent stage by proper Severi--Brauer specialization. In the cohomology sequence, any alpha in the kernel of H2(k,D)->H2(L,D) comes from beta in H1(k,S). Because H1(L,Q)=0, alpha_L=0 forces beta_L=0. Constant-torus H1 injectivity forces beta=0. Thus the H2 assertion is sound in fppf cohomology, including mu_p.

For a torus band, the gerbe class vanishes over k and the H1 isomorphism adjusts a k-object to the prescribed L-object. This retained isomorphism, followed by constant acting-torsor descent, gives a rational point and the specified G(L)-orbit relation. The orientation of the Isom torsor can be chosen consistently; no inversion ambiguity affects existence.

Mu_p does not have the same object-surjectivity property: a hypothetical p-th root of (1+a t)/c at any Laurent stage requires both 1/c and a/c to be p-th powers of constants, contradicting a not in k^p. Allowing ramification degree divisible by p only fixes the exponents, not the coefficient obstruction. For Artin--Schreier poles at full stages, repeatedly removing p-power pole exponents leaves an exponent prime to p and retains the tame-stage obstruction. Neither example is an original-problem counterexample.

## Turn 4: noncentral rigidification and a smooth envelope

I read AOV Appendix A, Theorem A.1 and its complete proof. The removed inertia subgroup must be flat and finitely presented, closed, compatible with all base changes and arrows; it may be positive-dimensional and normal rather than central. Torus identity components satisfy these requirements. The 2014 corrigendum was also read and affects the Lie-algebra proof of a different lemma, not the rigidification theorem. The quotient inertia is finite étale prime-to-p. Purely inseparable passage from L_tame to L changes no finite étale gerbe object category. Descending its chosen quotient object and taking the two-fiber leaves a torus-band gerbe; the previous H2 and H1 statements then preserve the full original object. Full faithfulness is deliberately not claimed for positive-dimensional torus automorphisms.

I checked the envelope schematically. Conrad Lemma 2.2.4 asserts smooth centralizers in smooth affine groups for arbitrary finite-type multiplicative-type subgroups, including non-smooth ones. Conrad Theorem A.1.1 supplies a geometrically maximal k-torus of the centralizer's smooth identity component. Over an algebraic closure, a central multiplicative-type subgroup lies in every maximal torus: it lies in the torus centralizer, whose quotient by the torus is unipotent, and a multiplicative-type image in a unipotent group is trivial, including infinitesimal images. Thus H^0 lies in T. H commutes with T, and S=HT is the quotient of H times T by their anti-diagonal intersection. It is closed and multiplicative type; S/T is a quotient of the prime-to-p étale component group H/H^0, so S is smooth. S/H is a torus because its character module is a sublattice of the free character lattice of T. This does not claim H itself lies in a torus; disconnected diagonalizable groups in PGL_2 are a correct boundary.

After gerbe neutralization for multiplicative-type band M, the G-torsor E has inner automorphism group J containing H. The quotients X=H\E and Y=S\E are obtained by fppf descent of the usual quasi-projective homogeneous quotients. Orbit-controlled descent for Y transports x_L into the fiber over y_0 in Y(k). Its pullback from the S-torsor is an S/H-torus torsor over k. A point of this fiber over the union descends to a finite Laurent stage and then to k. No non-smooth stabilizer is simply discarded, and no invalid mu_p object-surjectivity assumption is introduced.

## Turn 5: enhanced torus objects and a wild cyclic division algebra

Conrad Theorem 3.2.6 is read with its exact hypothesis: maximal tori of the geometric identity components are their own centralizers. It applies here because H^0 is reductive. It does not assert rational conjugacy under rational points. Adding a maximal torus to a gerbe object is locally the classifying gerbe of the normalizer; local conjugacy makes this an actual gerbe. Smooth normalizers follow from Proposition 2.1.2. Its identity component is T, and each component of H can be adjusted by H^0 to normalize T. Hence the finite normalizer component group has order |W(H^0)| times |pi_0(H)|. Prime-to-p order gives precisely the preceding full-object descent. The K-object admits a maximal K-torus by A.1.1. Descending the enhanced object retains the G-torsor isomorphism, so constant-torsor descent finishes.

The auxiliary cyclic algebra is sound. The Artin--Schreier polynomial with pole a/t is irreducible over K; its translations give a cyclic p-extension. Gille--Szamuely §§2.5--2.6 verify the central simple construction and reduced norm; the candidate explicitly permits the opposite generator convention. At a Puiseux stage n=pq, the order generated by w=s^q x and y is finite free with the stated p^2 basis. Its residue is the field k(a^(1/p),b^(1/p)); algebraic independence makes its degree p^2. Every nonzero algebra element has a least coefficient valuation and nonzero residue after rescaling. Lift left and right residue inverses, use completeness for geometric inverses, and obtain an actual inverse. This proves division and multiplicativity of the valuation without a defectlessness assumption. For a stage with p not dividing n, splitting would persist to a later p-divisible stage and contradict division. Thus no finite stage splits the algebra, and finite presentation gives division over the union.

A constant division algebra's leading-coefficient valuation has residue equal to that constant division algebra, and constant indices are preserved over Laurent stages. Equality of Brauer classes at a finite stage yields isomorphism of division representatives, not merely Morita equivalence. For a degree-p division algebra the least-coefficient valuation equals v(Nrd(z))/p: determinant of left multiplication has valuation p^2 times that least coefficient, and det(L_z)=Nrd(z)^p. Consequently a K-stage algebra isomorphism must preserve valuations and residues. The nonconstant algebra has commutative purely inseparable residue field, whereas a constant degree-p central division algebra has noncommutative residue. They cannot agree. This is a valid failure of reductive H1 surjectivity over the union, with PGL_p's wild Weyl order outside the positive theorem. It does not prove the tame Weyl hypothesis is necessary for Laurent point descent.
