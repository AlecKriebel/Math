# Exact source and prior gate: 30004320 / OWR-17295-004

## Target

For a field k, an affine algebraic k-group G and a homogeneous space X of G,
does X(k((t))) nonempty imply X(k) nonempty? The Laurent-series variable is
new and the group, space and action are all defined over the constant field k.
The source imposes no characteristic-zero or perfect-field hypothesis on the
question. Results below must distinguish torsors from general homogeneous
spaces, and keep stabilizer and smoothness restrictions explicit.

Primary: Philippe Gille (joint with Mathieu Florence), “Residues on Affine
Grassmannians,” OWR52/2019, printed pp.3264–3265, physical PDF22–23, in
*Mini-Workshop: Rank One Groups and Exceptional Algebraic Groups*.
https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/17295
https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46832
The full two-page contribution was read and both pages visually inspected.
The report volume is 2019; the publisher gives 19 November 2020 publication.
Theorem 2 is the torsor descent theorem. The following paragraph expressly
asks for its extension to homogeneous spaces.

The report opens with smooth affine groups, while its torsor theorem says
“affine algebraic group.” The full later residues paper allows finite-type
group schemes and separated finite-type schemes as varieties. We will not
manufacture a counterexample by replacing schematic/fppf homogeneity with
mere set-theoretic orbit transitivity, nor silently impose smooth stabilizers.
Any partial theorem states its own model assumptions; a complete claim must
cover the source-level generality under the standard homogeneous-space sense.

## Already known results, including an important correction to triage scope

1. Florence–Gille, *Residues on affine Grassmannians*, J. reine angew. Math.
   776 (2021),119–150, DOI10.1515/crelle-2021-0007. Complete primary versions
   retrieved: the author-hosted 10 November 2019 manuscript and
   arXiv:1910.14509v3 (8 February 2021; PDF dated February9).
   https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~mathieu.florence/residus11nov.pdf
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.14509
   The latter's Proposition5.2(3) and Theorem5.4 prove torsor descent for
   affine algebraic groups over arbitrary fields (indeed Theorem5.4 is
   broader). Its introduction still identifies the homogeneous extension
   as open and mentions characteristic zero as known. The torsor and
   homogeneous statements must not be conflated.
2. Mathieu Florence, *Points rationnels sur les espaces homogènes et leurs
   compactifications*, Transformation Groups11(2006),161–176,
   DOI10.1007/s00031-004-1107-9. The full primary preprint is available at
   https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/lag/man/174.pdf . Its hypotheses are
   **perfect fields in any characteristic**, not just characteristic zero.
   The direct Laurent-descent implication follows from Proposition4.7
   (acyclic extensions and homogeneous spaces) and Proposition5.4 (the
   constructed field K_infinity is acyclic and contains k((t))). Its
   Lemma4.8 handles nonreduced geometric stabilizers over perfect fields.
   Theorem5.6 in this preprint then gives the compactification consequence;
   the published theorem numbering differs. The introduction's reference
   to Lemma5.5 for descent is imprecise: the displayed Lemma5.5 goes from
   a smooth point on a birational model to a Laurent point. We use the
   actual Proposition4.7 + Proposition5.4 chain.
3. Gille's later primary lecture notes *Wound algebraic groups and their
   compactifications*, https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~gille/prenotes/gille_pisa.pdf ,
   p.30 after Theorem12.4, explicitly credit the perfect-field homogeneous
   result and identify the imperfect-field case as open. The complete PDF
   was retrieved and p.30 visually inspected. This is a source-qualified
   status statement, not a certification that no later theorem exists.

The imported catalog's narrower description “beyond characteristic zero”
is therefore corrected in our source gate. Perfect-field proofs are credited
prior work, not counted as new author discoveries or as a solution of the
remaining arbitrary-field problem.
