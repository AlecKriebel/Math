## 1. Claim being compared

The exact accepted construction is the fixed map

\[
L(u,v)=(u,(u^2+1)v),\qquad L^{-1}(u,v)=(u,v/(u^2+1)).
\]

It is a birational map over Q with polynomial forward formula, a rational inverse, and the rational integral I=u. Its generic invariant fibers have genus zero. It permutes the full affine plane over F_p when p ≡ 3 mod 4. For the ordinary point-weighted CDF

\[
D_p(x)=p^{-2}\#\{(u,v)\in\mathbf F_p^2:T_p(u,v)\le px\},
\]

ROOT accepted that, for each fixed 1/2 ≤ x < 1 along this prime class,

\[
\limsup D_p(x)=1,\qquad \liminf D_p(x)\le23/24,
\]

and that the period distributions have no weak limit. This is a fixed-map, full-affine-space, p-normalized, ordinary subsequence claim. It is not a claim about averaging over random maps, a density over primes of one fixed rational orbit, or a map whose coefficients change with p. This audit compares that exact negative result with predecessors; it does not redo ROOT's proof acceptance.

The literal historical target is Conjecture 1 of Vivaldi's joint report with Roberts, *Maps over finite fields: integrability and reversibility*, Oberwolfach Report 54/2004, pp.2944–2945. The workshop was 5–11 December 2004. Its displayed CDF has precisely the point weighting and p scaling above; its broad wording is: “The limit (1) exists for any bi-rational map L.” The official EMS report and complete author copy were both read, with the relevant official pages visually checked. [Official original report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45972), [author copy](https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/f.vivaldi/research/Oberwolfach.pdf).

A broad literal counterexample is a narrower historical claim than disproving every later distribution conjecture in this research program. The source distinctions below must survive any eventual publication description.

## 3. Established predecessor ingredients and attribution

Several ingredients cannot be presented as new.

- Jogia 2008 §2.4.2, printed pp.47–48, explicitly constructs invertible multiplication maps on genus-zero conics. Multiplicative dynamics on rational fibers is therefore an established nearby mechanism, even though that example does not supply the accepted varying-fiber ordinary CDF nonlimit.
- The same thesis, printed pp.182–183, uses denominators z²+1 and primes p ≡ 3 mod 4 to obtain a well-defined permutation. The suitable-prime-class device also predates this submission.
- RV05 §4, printed p.2184, already explains that one should not expect an ordinary distribution limit in its nonsymmetric, nonintegrable p²-scaled setting and formulates a prime-averaged conjecture. Recognition of averaging and arithmetic subsequences is not itself the new contribution.
- Siu 2019 chapter 6 constructs higher-dimensional maps by retaining a parameter as a coordinate. Its linear examples count possible orders over the parameter family, whereas the accepted theorem fixes L once and varies p. Parameter foliation/gluing and linear order statistics require attribution as neighboring constructions.

[Full Jogia thesis record](https://unsworks.unsw.edu.au/entities/publication/3b82422c-bbaa-481d-8b1f-c29bd7f977f1), [full Siu thesis record](https://unsworks.unsw.edu.au/entities/publication/6e0aaf0f-d2d5-4b01-a65f-d08dfd232041).

These predecessors do not establish the quantitative 23/24 gap or the no-weak-limit conclusion for L(u,v)=(u,(u²+1)v). An eventual priority claim would need to isolate that specific theorem and proof, not claim a first discovery of genus-zero multiplicative dynamics, reduction on suitable prime classes, or the general need to average arithmetic statistics.

I found no exact L formula, an equivalent fixed-fiber order-distribution negative theorem, or the accepted limsup/liminf bounds in the relevant bodies read. This is a statement about this audit's coverage, not an inference that none exists anywhere.
