# Turn 2: finite extensions of the metabelian exclusion

Second substantive turn. The original existence problem remains unresolved. This turn proves that every finitely generated virtually metabelian group satisfies both source dichotomies. It uses turn1's quantitative compression and the classical Schur finite-derived-subgroup argument, proved below by transfer. No historical novelty is certified.

## 1. Bounded kernels and quotient word norms

Fix a finite ordinary generating set X of G, its conjugation-invariant word norm, and a normal subgroup N whose diameter in G is at most B. Equip G/N with the corresponding quotient normal word norm. Each shortest quotient factorization lifts to a product of the same number of conjugates of X, so for every g,

    ||gN|| <= ||g|| <= ||gN||+B.                          (1)

The lift may differ from g by an element of N; no subgroup metric is silently identified with its ambient metric. Consequently if (G/N)' is bounded then G' is bounded, since the image of G' is exactly (G/N)'. The same statement with an arbitrarily small error works for quotient infima of general norms, but only integer word norms are needed here.

## 2. Finitely generated virtually abelian groups

Let Q be finitely generated with an abelian normal subgroup A of finite index. A is finitely generated by the Schreier argument. Put K=[Q,A]. Turn1's abelian-normal-subgroup compression, which does not require commuting conjugation operators, makes K bounded in Q's normal word norm. In Q/K, the image C=A/K is central and has finite index.

We recall an elementary proof that a finitely generated group E with central finite-index subgroup C has finite derived subgroup. Choose a finite transversal T for E/C, and write m=[E:C]. Conjugation of any element of E depends only on its coset modulo C. The derived subgroup is therefore generated by the finitely many conjugates, by T, of commutators of a finite generating set. Hence E' is finitely generated. The subgroup E' intersect C is finite index in E', so is finitely generated; it is abelian because C is central.

Define transfer v:E→C as follows. For t in T write

    tg = c_{t,g} t',       c_{t,g} in C, t' in T,

and set v(g)=product_{t in T} c_{t,g}. Multiplication of these equations and permutation of the transversal prove v(gh)=v(g)v(h), using that C is abelian. For c in C, centrality gives c_{t,c}=c, hence v(c)=c^m. As a homomorphism to an abelian group, v kills E'. Thus every element of E' intersect C has m-th power one. A finitely generated abelian group of finite exponent is finite; E' is its finite extension and is finite. This is the classical Schur theorem in the needed setting, not a new input.

Apply this to E=Q/K. Its derived subgroup is finite. Its diameter in the quotient word norm is therefore finite. Equation(1), and boundedness of K, imply Q' is bounded in Q. This conclusion is stronger than saying each individual commutator has bounded powers; it bounds the entire derived subgroup.

## 3. Virtually metabelian groups

Let G be finitely generated and have a metabelian subgroup H of finite index. Replace H by its core, the intersection of its finitely many conjugates. The core is normal and still finite index; as a subgroup of H it is metabelian. It is finitely generated by Schreier. Denote this core by H again.

The subgroup N=H' is characteristic in H, hence normal in G. Apply turn1 with a finite generating set Y of H and the restriction to H of G's normal word norm. All ν(y) are finite, so its explicit bound(4) proves N is bounded in G, even when N is not finitely generated.

The quotient G/N has the abelian normal finite-index subgroup H/N. Section2 therefore makes (G/N)' bounded. Section1 lifts this to boundedness of G'. Thus

    G' has finite diameter in every finite-normal-generator word norm.  (2)

There is a constant B, depending on the specified group and generating set, such that

    |π(g)|_{G_ab} <= ||g|| <= |π(g)|_{G_ab}+B.            (3)

This follows by lifting a shortest abelianization word and using (2), as in turn1. Unlike turn1's metabelian bound d(d+1), no uniform constant independent of the finite extension is claimed here.

The abelianization is finitely generated. A torsion image gives a bounded cyclic subgroup by division of powers into finitely many residues; an infinite-order image is detected by a real homomorphism. Both source dichotomies follow. Every homogeneous quasimorphism is a homomorphism, using boundedness of G' and the coset argument from turn1. The proof does not infer this from amenability alone.

## 4. Why finite-index geometry must be handled in the ambient norm

The infinite dihedral family gives a sharp caution. Let

    G_d=Z^d semidirect {1,-1},

where -1 acts by inversion, generated by translations e_1,...,e_d and a reflection t. This is finitely presented. Its translation subgroup has an unbounded ordinary and intrinsic abelian normal word norm. In the ambient normal word norm, however,

    [v,t]=2v

in additive translation notation, and ||2v||<=2||t||=2 for every v. Every translation is 2v plus a sum of at most d standard basis vectors, so has norm at most d+2. The other coset adds at most one. In fact each nontranslation element is a reflection with a parity vector, giving the slightly sharper overall bound d+2 for d≥1: a reflection with parity vector r is conjugate to r t and costs at most |r|+1≤d+1. Thus G_d is globally bounded, although its index-two subgroup Z^d is not bounded in its intrinsic norm.

This example and its mechanism are classical (the d=1 case appears in Burago–Ivanov–Polterovich's discussion of conjugation-invariant norms). It explains why simply embedding or taking a finite extension of a candidate cannot be assumed to preserve normal-word unboundedness. Our proof above explicitly bounds the relevant kernels instead.

The exact checker verifies the transfer identities in finite central extensions and the dihedral parity decompositions. The unrestricted result is the written group-theoretic proof, not finite enumeration. Original unresolved2/5.
