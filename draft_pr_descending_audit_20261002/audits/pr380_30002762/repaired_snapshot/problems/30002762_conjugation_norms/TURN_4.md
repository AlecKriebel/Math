# Turn 4: effective exact stable norms on the excluded classes

Fourth substantive turn. Original counterexample question remains unresolved. This turn converts the bounded-derived-subgroup theorem into an exact, terminating rational optimization procedure for finitely presented virtually metabelian groups, with the group-class hypothesis supplied. It also applies to any finitely presented group independently known to have bounded derived subgroup. It does not decide whether an arbitrary finite presentation belongs to that class. Stable norms, abelianization and rational linear-programming duality are classical tools; no novelty certification.

## 1. Input and the formula

Let G=<x_1,...,x_d | r_1,...,r_m> be finitely presented, and assume G' is bounded in the normal word norm generated by these generators and their inverses. Turns1–2 prove this hypothesis for finitely generated virtually metabelian groups without requiring a presentation of their derived subgroups.

For each relator let R_j in Z^d be its exponent-sum vector, and let R be the integer matrix of these rows. For a word g let v in Z^d be its exponent-sum vector. Its image in the abelianization is the coset of v in Z^d/L, where L is the integer span of the R_j. Set

    α(v)=min_{a in R^m} ||v+R^T a||_1.

Then the stable normal word norm is exactly

    lim_{n→infinity} ||g^n||/n
       = α(v)
       = max {u·v : Ru=0, |u_i|<=1 for all i}.             (1)

The minimum is attained and has a rational optimizer; the maximum is attained at a rational point. Thus the common value is rational and computable by a finite exact algorithm. All relation rows, including zeros and dependencies, are permitted. The trivial d=0 group is handled separately with value zero.

## 2. Stable lattice norm equals the real relaxation

Write ℓ(nv) for the word norm of the image of nv in Z^d/L. Since words in the images of x_i commute,

    ℓ(nv)=min_{z in L} ||nv+z||_1.

This is at least nα(v). To prove the converse asymptotically, use a rational optimizer a and let N be a positive integer clearing all its denominators. Then NR^T a is an integer combination of the relator rows, and

    ℓ(Nv)<=||Nv+NR^T a||_1=Nα(v).

The reverse inequality shows equality. For n=kN+s,0<=s<N, the triangle inequality gives

    ℓ(nv)<=kNα(v)+max_{0<=s<N}ℓ(sv).

Division by n and the previous lower bound yield ℓ(nv)/n→α(v). Torsion is not discarded illicitly: clearing denominators is exactly what permits a real relation to become an integer relation after a fixed multiple.

Because G' is bounded, lifting shortest abelianization words gives a fixed B with

    ℓ(nv)<=||g^n||<=ℓ(nv)+B.

This proves the first equality in(1). It needs no effective knowledge of B; the supplied group-class theorem establishes its existence.

## 3. Rational optimization and certificates

The L1 minimization is the rational linear program

    minimize sum_i (p_i+q_i),
    p_i,q_i>=0,       p-q=v+R^T a,

with the a_j free (or represented as differences of nonnegative variables). Feasibility follows by taking a=0, and the objective is bounded below by zero. Elementary finite-dimensional linear programming therefore gives attainment and rational optimal data. Equivalently, project the rational cross-polytope conv{±e_i} to R^d/span_R L; its gauge is α. The dual compact polytope is exactly Ru=0 with |u_i|<=1. Standard primal-dual linear programming gives the second equality in(1).

A completely finite exact implementation can row-reduce R to rank r and enumerate candidate vertices of the dual polytope: append d-r equations u_i=±1 at distinct coordinates, solve each resulting d×d rational system when nonsingular, and retain the solutions obeying all inequalities. A vertex has enough linearly independent active coordinate constraints to appear in this enumeration. If r=d, the unique feasible point is0. Taking the largest u·v yields α. Rational Gaussian elimination and a finite list of coordinate/sign choices guarantee termination; no norm ball search or word-problem oracle occurs.

An independently checkable certificate may contain rational primal a and w=v+R^T a, and rational dual u, with Ru=0, |u_i|<=1 and

    ||w||_1=u·v.

Weak duality then proves optimality directly, without trusting an optimizer implementation. A denominator N gives the explicit lattice-multiple equality from Section2. A positive value also provides the detecting homomorphism χ(g)=u·v; its values on generators have absolute value at most1. It is an ordinary homogeneous quasimorphism with zero defect, not a norm-controlled partial quasimorphism.

## 4. Boundedness decision and examples

Under the bounded-derived-subgroup hypothesis, the cyclic subgroup of g is bounded if and only if α(v)=0, equivalently v lies in the real row span of R, equivalently its abelianization image is torsion. Otherwise χ above detects it and the norm grows linearly. These tests are effective on the stated promised input class.

For G=<x,y | [x,y], x²y^{-3}>, the group is infinite cyclic with x=3t,y=2t. For g=y, the exact value is2/3. A dual optimizer is u=(1,2/3); a primal choice a=1/3 for the relation vector (2,-3) gives w=(2/3,0), both value2/3. Three copies of y equal two copies of x in the abelian group, illustrating why a rational stable value need not be an integer.

For the integral Heisenberg presentation <x,y,z | [x,y]z^{-1},[x,z],[y,z]>, the exponent-sum row span contains e_z, and the procedure returns0 for z. It returns |a|+|b| for x^a y^b z^c. This agrees with the bounded-central-commutator identity; the nilpotent bounded-derived input is credited to the classical/BGKM theorem, rather than inferred from the relation matrix alone.

The same relation matrix could occur in a group whose derived subgroup is not bounded. For instance a free group on x,y has zero relation matrix, yet [x,y] has exponent vector0 and positive stable normal norm, detected by a credited Brooks homogeneous quasimorphism. Thus omitting the group-class hypothesis would be a false algorithm. The procedure does not recognize metabelianity or solve the original existence problem.

The exact checker compares independent rational primal and dual vertex certificates across finite integer matrices, including rank defects and torsion cases. This supports the finite algorithm; the stable-norm equality is the unrestricted proof above. Original unresolved4/5.
