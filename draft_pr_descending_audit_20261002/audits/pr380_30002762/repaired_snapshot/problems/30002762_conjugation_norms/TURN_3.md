# Turn 3: split abelian extensions preserve both dichotomies

Third substantive turn; original unresolved. The aim is to test semidirect-product constructions with nonabelian quotient, rather than assume that adding an abelian kernel creates intermediate cyclic growth. We prove that neither source dichotomy can first fail in a finitely generated split extension by an abelian kernel if it holds in the quotient. Classical quotient and module tools are credited; no novelty certification.

## 1. The bounded kernel and exact quotient

Let G=M semidirect Q be finitely generated, with M abelian. Write K=[Q,M]=[G,M]. Choose any finite ordinary generating set X={x_1,...,x_d} of G. Turn1's operator telescoping applies even when the action operators do not commute, and proves

    sup_{k in K} ||k||_X <= 2d.                            (1)

This does not require M to be finitely generated as an abelian group. The quotient is canonically

    G/K = M_Q × Q,       M_Q=M/[Q,M],                     (2)

because the action becomes trivial after taking coinvariants and the given section Q→G remains a section. The homomorphism is (m,q)↦(m mod K,q). Since G is finitely generated, both factors on the right are finitely generated. In particular M_Q is a finitely generated abelian group, even when M has infinite rank.

Equip the quotient with the image normal word norm. The shortest-factorization lifting argument of turn2 gives

    ||gK|| <= ||g|| <= ||gK||+2d.                         (3)

Thus all power sequences are preserved up to a uniformly bounded additive error. Choosing different finite normal generators changes norms only by fixed multiplicative constants.

## 2. Products with finitely generated abelian groups

Let B be finitely generated abelian. For a product generating set of B×Q the normal word norm is exactly

    ||(b,q)|| = |b|_B + ||q||_Q.                           (4)

Every conjugate of a generator in one factor stays in that factor. Any factorization therefore requires at least the minimum number of letters in each projection, and concatenating shortest factorizations attains their sum.

Suppose Q has the weak dichotomy: each cyclic subgroup is bounded or has positive linear lower growth. For (b,q), if either component has linear lower growth then (4) gives it in the product. Otherwise the Q-component is bounded and b is torsion, so both power sequences and hence their sum are bounded. The product has the weak dichotomy.

Suppose Q has the stronger bq-dichotomy. If b has infinite order, a real homomorphism on B detects (b,q). If q is detected by a homogeneous quasimorphism, its pullback under the Q-projection detects (b,q). If neither occurs, b is torsion and the Q-power sequence is bounded, so the product-power sequence is bounded. These exhaust the possibilities, proving the strong dichotomy.

## 3. Lift the conclusion to G

For the weak statement, apply(3) to powers of g: bounded quotient powers give bounded G-powers, and a linear quotient lower bound is also a G lower bound. For the strong statement, a detector on G/K pulls back to a homogeneous quasimorphism on G; otherwise quotient powers are bounded and(3) applies. Therefore

    If Q satisfies either source dichotomy, then so does M semidirect Q,
    provided G is finitely generated and M is abelian.    (5)

No finite presentation of the kernel is assumed or deduced. This includes all finitely presented examples satisfying these hypotheses. It applies iteratively to finite towers of split abelian extensions whose total groups are finitely generated, starting from any credited positive class. For example one may start with a free group, a right-angled Artin group, or a group covered by the strong BGKM theorem. For Coxeter groups without the additional strong-dichotomy hypotheses, only their credited weak dichotomy is inherited.

It also applies to a restricted wreath product B wr Q with B finitely generated abelian and Q finitely generated satisfying a dichotomy: the base is the direct sum of copies of B indexed by Q, and the total group is finitely generated by a generating set of Q and generators at its identity lamp. Its coinvariants identify all translated lamps, so M_Q is B and the quotient in(2) is B×Q. A wreath product need not be finitely presented; this example is an exclusion mechanism, not a solution to the original finite-presentation requirement.

## 4. Stable norms and the necessity of tracking splitting

Equation(3) also proves equality of stable norms with those of the quotient for the specified quotient metric:

    lim_n ||g^n||/n = lim_n ||(gK)^n||/n.

These limits exist by subadditivity. Equation(4) then adds the stable norms of the factors for product generators. This is a metric statement, not merely a vanishing-bounded-cohomology argument.

The splitting hypothesis cannot be discarded from the quotient identification. In the integral Heisenberg group, take central kernel M=<z> and quotient Z² with [x,y]=z. Then [G,M]=1, but G is not M×Z². The center generator maps to zero in G_ab and is not detected by any real homomorphism. Its powers nevertheless satisfy z^n=[x^n,y] and have normal word norm at most2||y||. Hence the nonsplit example is not a counterexample to either dichotomy; it demonstrates exactly why a direct-product conclusion from abelianity of the kernel alone would be false.

The remaining search must therefore investigate genuinely nonsplit or nonabelian-kernel constructions, or a quotient already failing the desired dichotomy. No claim that every extension preserves the dichotomies is made.
