# Turn 1: a bounded derived subgroup for every finitely generated metabelian group

First substantive turn; the original finitely presented counterexample question remains unresolved. This turn excludes all finitely generated metabelian groups by a direct quantitative argument, including those with infinitely generated derived subgroup. Classical commutator identities, augmentation modules and the BGKM bounded-element method are credited; historical novelty is not certified.

## 1. An abelian-normal-subgroup compression lemma

Let G be generated as an ordinary group by x_1,...,x_d, and let M be an abelian normal subgroup. Use additive notation in M. Conjugation defines operators T_i on M. Then

    [G,M] = sum_{i=1}^d (T_i-1)M.                         (1)

Here [G,M] is the subgroup generated by all gmg^{-1}m^{-1}. To verify (1), for operators U,V use UV-1=(U-1)V+(V-1), and T_i^{-1}-1=(T_i-1)(-T_i^{-1}). Repeatedly expanding any word in the generators and their inverses expresses (T_g-1)m as a sum of elements in the displayed d images. Each image is an additive subgroup, so all terms with the same i combine into one. Conversely each displayed image is generated by commutators with x_i. This proof does not require commuting T_i or finite generation of M.

Consequently every a in [G,M] has the form

    a = product_{i=1}^d [x_i,m_i]                         (2)

for some m_i in M. Products commute because all factors belong to M. In any conjugation-invariant norm ν on G,

    ν([x_i,m_i]) <= 2ν(x_i),
    sup_{a in [G,M]} ν(a) <= 2 sum_i ν(x_i).               (3)

The m_i may have arbitrarily large norm; it is the fixed generator side of the commutator that controls the estimate. Merely bounding commutator length without controlling a side would not suffice.

## 2. Quantitative metabelian theorem

Assume now that M=G' is abelian. Set A=[G,M]. In G/A, the image of M is central and the quotient is nilpotent of class at most two. Its derived subgroup is generated as an ordinary abelian group by the finitely many [x_i,x_j], i<j: the normal closure of these commutators is the full derived subgroup of a group generated by the x_i, and centrality removes the conjugates.

For any m in M choose integers n_{ij} such that modulo A,

    m = product_{i<j} [x_i,x_j]^{n_{ij}}.

In a class-two group [x_i^{n},x_j]=[x_i,x_j]^n for every integer n. Thus with

    u=product_{i<j} [x_i^{n_{ij}},x_j]

we have mu^{-1} in A. Each factor of u has norm at most 2ν(x_j), independent of n_{ij}. Applying (3) to the remaining factor proves

    sup_{m in G'} ν(m)
       <= 2 sum_i ν(x_i) + 2 sum_{i<j} ν(x_j).            (4)

For the conjugation-invariant word norm generated by the x_i and their inverses this is at most

    d(d+1).                                               (5)

Identity generators or repeated generators only make this upper bound less efficient, never invalid. The case d=1 has trivial derived subgroup. No finite presentation, finite rank of G', or noetherian module assertion is needed.

## 3. Exact large-scale comparison with abelianization

Write π:G→G_ab. Let |.|_ab be the ordinary word norm for the finite symmetric image of the x_i. Conjugates have the same image, hence

    |π(g)|_ab <= ||g||.

Lift a shortest abelianization word to a word h in the x_i. Then gh^{-1} lies in G' and ||h||<=|π(g)|_ab. Using (5),

    |π(g)|_ab <= ||g|| <= |π(g)|_ab+d(d+1).               (6)

Thus the normal word metric differs from its abelian quotient metric by a uniformly bounded additive error. This statement is for these specified generating norms; another finite normal generating set gives the usual bi-Lipschitz equivalent conclusion, and (4) applies with the finite values ν(x_i).

In particular the stable norm exists by subadditivity and equals the stable word norm of π(g) in the finitely generated abelian group. If π(g) has finite order r, write n=qr+s with 0<=s<r. The elements g^{qr} belong to G', so

    ||g^n|| <= d(d+1)+max_{0<=s<r}||g^s||,

and the cyclic subgroup is bounded. If π(g) has infinite order, some real homomorphism on G_ab is nonzero on π(g), by the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups. Its pullback is a homogeneous quasimorphism (indeed a homomorphism), and gives a linear lower norm bound. Hence every finitely generated metabelian group satisfies both source dichotomies.

Every homogeneous quasimorphism is Lipschitz in this normal word norm: its values on the finite generator representatives and its defect bound control products of their conjugates. Since G' is bounded, homogeneity forces the quasimorphism to vanish on each element of G'. More strongly, it is constant on each coset of G': if g,h have the same image then g^n h^{-n} lies in G', so the Lipschitz bound gives |nq(g)-nq(h)| uniformly bounded; division by n proves equality. The induced homogeneous quasimorphism on the abelian quotient is a homomorphism (apply the defect to nth powers of commuting elements). Thus all homogeneous quasimorphisms here are homomorphisms. This conclusion is proved directly without appealing to amenability.

## 4. What this rules out and what remains

The result excludes finitely presented metabelian candidates, including constructions with a non-finitely-generated abelian derived subgroup. It also shows why ordinary word distortion in a metabelian group cannot be reused as normal-word distortion. In particular no argument may infer a counterexample merely from exponential distortion of a cyclic subgroup in a Baumslag–Solitar or module semidirect-product presentation.

This does not cover every finitely presented solvable group, every central extension, or a non-solvable group with trivial bounded cohomology. Nor does it imply that arbitrary embeddings of non-finitely-presented counterexamples preserve the normal word metric. Those remain possible directions within the remaining turn budget.
