## Strongest verified theorem and its assumptions

Given a finite presentation on ordinary generators x_1,...,x_d, use the normal word norm whose unit letters are conjugates of these generators and inverses. Assume a valid mathematical argument supplies a finite bound B for the whole derived subgroup G', measured in this same norm. The theorem computes the stable norm of a word with exponent vector v as

    α(v) = min_a ||v+R^T a||_1
         = max{u·v : Ru=0, |u_i|≤1},

where the rows of R are the integral exponent vectors of the relators. This is exact rational arithmetic on a promised input class. The algorithm does not test the promise, verify arbitrary group-class certificates, or solve a word problem.

The abelianization is Z^d/L, with L the **integer** relator lattice. For integer n its quotient word norm is min_{z∈L}||nv+z||_1, so it is at least nα(v). A rational optimal a has a denominator N with NR^T a∈L, and thus equality at Nv. Decomposing n=kN+s into finitely many residues yields the full stable equality with an additive error independent of n. The proof explicitly clears coefficients of the relator combination, retaining integral torsion correctly. It never equates an integral one-step length with the rational optimum.

Shortest abelianization words lift with the same length; the residual lies in G'. The supplied bound gives quotient length ≤ group normal length ≤ quotient length+B. Therefore the two stable norms agree. No effective value of B is needed for the numerical stable norm, but its existence is essential for the theorem and boundedness decision.

The value is zero precisely when v belongs to the real row span of R, equivalently when its abelianization class is torsion. This does **not** say that the group element is torsion or is itself in G'. If m times its abelianization class vanishes, all g^{qm} lie in G', and the global derived bound plus finitely many remainders bounds every g^n. This is exactly where the supplied bound is needed.

When α(v)>0, an optimal rational dual u gives a real homomorphism χ:G→R with χ(g)=α(v)≠0, since Ru=0 makes every relator vanish. This is a detector under the original stronger formulation, and its unit-generator values have absolute value at most one. Both source dichotomies therefore hold on the promised class.

## Rational attainment and finite enumeration

The primal LP is feasible and bounded below. It has rational data, an attained rational optimum, and the compact dual is the cube intersected with ker(R). If R has rank r, a dual vertex has d-r independent active coordinate constraints. Enumerating distinct coordinate subsets and sign assignments, solving nonsingular rational systems, and rejecting infeasible points reaches every vertex in finitely many operations. Rank d yields the unique zero vector; d=0 is separately trivial. Zero rows and dependent relators change neither the relation space nor the argument. The procedure cannot return an irrational value from finite integral input.

The sealed independent program uses a different coordinate mechanism: it computes an annihilator basis of the relation space, projects an arbitrary integral generating set to that quotient, and enumerates basic nonnegative supports of signed generators for the primal and signed active generator constraints for the dual. It also independently diagonalizes the **integral** lattice by unimodular Euclidean row/column operations, checks actual integral generation, computes exact quotient word lengths, and produces exact power certificates clearing both generator and relation denominators. It imports no candidate code. These computations corroborate the unrestricted written proof; they do not substitute finite observations for it.

Turn 4's formula is for the specified presentation generators. The independently checked general-generator gauge is a validation extension, not an attribution that Turn 4 claims the same numerical norm for every normally generating set. For arbitrary finite normal representatives their abelianization images must generate the integral quotient, not merely span its rationalization. Normal generation is a group-theoretic premise stronger than the integral quotient test; the new code does not recognize it.

- Generator 2 spans R but does not integrally generate Z.
- A Klein-bottle multiplication model gives an infinite-order element whose abelianization class has order 2; torsion abelianization does not imply finite order in G.

A separate candidate-free post-seal negative control makes omission of the global derived bound falsifiable. In F(a,b), define φ on reduced words by the number of adjacent ab occurrences minus adjacent b^{-1}a^{-1} occurrences. In a maximal cancellation decomposition u c and c^{-1}v, contributions inside c and c^{-1} cancel, leaving at most three boundary terms. Thus its defect is at most 3 for every pair, not just the checked pairs. Its homogenization q has defect at most 6, is conjugacy invariant, vanishes on a and b, and satisfies q([a,b])=1 because φ([a,b]^n)=n. Consequently normal-generator factorizations give ||[a,b]^n||≥n/6, whereas the abelianization LP gives zero. The free derived subgroup is therefore unbounded. The new code passed 61,200 exact assertions, including 30,000 reduced-word pairs and powers 1–200. The infinite defect proof is the cancellation argument; sampling alone is not asserted to prove it.

## Dependent class assumptions in Turns 1–3

Turn 1's augmentation compression is valid for abelian normal M with possibly noncommuting action operators. Right-suffix expansion expresses each difference in the sum of the fixed (T_i−1)M images, and abelianity allows terms for each i to combine. Fixed-side commutator estimates are independent of the other entry's norm. With M=G', the quotient by [G,G'] has central derived image generated by the finitely many basic commutators. Their powers are fixed-side commutators in the class-two quotient; the residual lies in the bounded augmentation subgroup. The resulting bound d(d+1) needs neither finite generation of G' nor a presentation of it.

Turn 2 passes to a finite-index normal metabelian core and applies that bound in the **ambient** norm. The central finite-index Schur argument is complete: finitely many conjugates generate E'; its central finite-index intersection is finitely generated abelian; transfer kills E' and restricts to the index power map on the central subgroup; that intersection has finite exponent and is finite. Bounded-kernel lifting then makes G' bounded. This supplies the promised hypothesis for finitely generated virtually metabelian groups without a recognition algorithm.

Turn 3 does not silently broaden that class hypothesis. It proves a bounded coinvariant kernel for a **split** abelian extension and identifies the quotient as M_Q×Q using the section. It transfers the dichotomies when Q has them. It does not assert that G' is bounded for every such Q or that a nonsplit extension has that direct-product quotient. Its stable-norm statements concern the correct quotient/product generators and do not license applying the abelianization LP when the derived promise is missing.
