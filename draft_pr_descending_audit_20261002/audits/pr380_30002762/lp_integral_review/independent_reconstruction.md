## Hypothesis and deductions

Let G be a group normally generated by a finite S={s_1,...,s_k}; use conjugates of S and their inverses as unit-cost letters. Assume a supplied mathematical certificate that every element of K=[G,G] has norm at most D. This is a genuine extra hypothesis. The algorithm below neither recognizes it nor proves it from a finite presentation.

Let A=G/K, with the quotient word norm from the images of S. If q:G→A, then

    |q(g)|_A ≤ |g|_S ≤ |q(g)|_A + D.                 (1)

For the upper bound lift a shortest abelian word using S, without requiring S to ordinarily generate G. The residual is in K and the hypothesis bounds its norm. Applying (1) to g^n shows that the stable norm equals the stable word norm of q(g).

For a finite presentation on d ordinary generators, let L≤Z^d be the integer lattice generated by exponent-sum vectors of relators, and let v_j∈Z^d be the exponent-sum vectors of the normally generating words s_j. Thus A=Z^d/L. Set V=R^d/span_R(L), let a be the image of the exponent vector of g, and let P=conv(±v_j) in V. Normal generation implies that v_j span A integrally, hence span V over R. Its Minkowski gauge is

    ρ(a)=min{Σ|t_j|: Σ t_j v_j=a in V}.

For integer data the minimum is rational. It is a finite exact rational LP, and duality gives

    ρ(a)=max{ℓ(a): ℓ(L)=0 and |ℓ(v_j)|≤1}.

The positive-dimensional dual polytope is bounded because the generators span V. Enumerating all independent sets of dim(V) signed active inequalities gives all vertices and terminates; the primal basic feasible supports have at most dim(V) positive signed-generator coefficients. Neither enumeration needs to approximate irrational values. V=0 is handled separately with optimum zero and the zero functional. Normal-generation validation is a separate group-theoretic requirement; spanning V alone is weaker.

## Exact powers and integer error

Lower bound: any abelian word for n a has length ≥nρ(a), because all unit generators lie in P.

Upper bound: select a rational optimum t. Choose m clearing denominators of t and expressing m(a-Σt_j v_j) in L **integrally**, not merely in span_R(L). Such an m exists: rational linear algebra writes the residual as a rational combination of the given integral relator vectors, and a common denominator clears both combinations. Then m a=Σ(m t_j)v_j in A; consequently |m a|_A≤mρ(a). For n=qm+r, 0≤r<m, use the exact block expression q times and any fixed expression for r a. This yields

    nρ(a)≤|n a|_A≤nρ(a)+C,

where C=max_{0≤r<m}(|r a|_A-rρ(a)) is finite. No integral-program optimum is equated with a one-step rational optimum. This establishes stable equality. An alternative rounding proof must absorb bounded torsion residuals; ignoring torsion when rounding is invalid.

## Stable zero and boundedness

ρ(a)=0 iff a=0 in V iff q(g) is torsion in the finitely generated abelian A. It does not imply g has finite order or g∈K. If m q(g)=0, then g^m∈K and every g^{qm} lies in K, so |g^n|≤D+max_{0≤r<m}|g^r|. Thus the whole cyclic subgroup is bounded **using the bound on K**. For ρ(a)>0 an optimal dual ℓ gives an ordinary rational homomorphism G→R nonzero on g and with |ℓ(g)|=ρ(a), certifying undistortion and detection. Conversely any homogeneous quasimorphism nonzero on g supplies a linear lower bound for a finite-normal-generation norm, by conjugacy invariance and its defect bound.
