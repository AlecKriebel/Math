## Literal problem and source boundaries

Kędra's complete contribution in OWR3/2015, printed199–200, asks for a finitely presented group violating either dichotomy: every cyclic subgroup is bounded or has positive linear growth in a word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes; or every cyclic subgroup is bounded or detected by an ordinary homogeneous real quasimorphism. For a fixed normally finitely generated group these normal word norms are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. Ordinary finite generation is a stronger hypothesis and is used explicitly in the positive classes here. The symmetric conjugate generating convention is fixed.

## Turn1: bounded derived subgroup without finite generation of it

Let X=(x_1,...,x_d) ordinarily generate G and M be abelian normal. Write its conjugation actions additively as T_i. The subgroup [G,M] equals sum_i(T_i-1)M. For a word of signed actions, use (UV-1)m=(U-1)Vm+(V-1)m and (T_i^-1-1)m=(T_i-1)(-T_i^-1 m). Recursively expanding from the left puts every term in the stated sum. This does not assume the actions commute or that individual summands are invariant under every other action. Conversely each summand is a commutator. Addition within the abelian module combines every coefficient belonging to the same i, so each element is a product of at most d commutators [x_i,m_i], with signs absorbed into m_i. Conjugation invariance and the triangle inequality give norm<=2 sum_i norm(x_i).

For metabelian G, take M=G' and K=[G,M]. Modulo K the commutator subgroup is central. A class-two group on d ordinary generators has derived subgroup generated by their finitely many pair commutators: use [uv,w]=[u,w][v,w] and its second-variable version, centrality and signed power identities. Any element of G' modulo K therefore has a product of [x_i,x_j]^n coordinates. Each coordinate lifts using [x_i^n,x_j], of norm<=2norm(x_j); no bound on n is needed. The difference from the original derived element lies in K and has the preceding uniform bound. With unit generator costs this yields

    sup_{h in G'} norm(h) <= 2d + 2 binomial(d,2) = d(d+1).

There is no assertion that G' is ordinarily finitely generated. The quotient norm is <=norm(g). Lift a shortest quotient normal word and absorb its error in G', giving norm(g)<=norm_ab(g)+d(d+1). For a general finite X with other conjugation invariant norm costs the bound uses those costs instead of d(d+1).

The finitely generated abelian quotient has finite torsion subgroup and a lattice free part. A torsion quotient coordinate makes all powers have bounded quotient word lengths and therefore bounded ambient lengths. A nonzero free coordinate has an ordinary real homomorphism nonzero on g and gives a linear normal-word lower bound. This proves both dichotomies in this class. A homogeneous ordinary quasimorphism is conjugation invariant, additive on commuting elements and satisfies |q(g)|<=C norm(g), using finite generator values and its defect. It vanishes on bounded cyclic subgroups. In fact all such q here are homomorphisms: (gh)^n and g^n h^n differ by a bounded-derived element; their q values differ uniformly by the Lipschitz bound and one defect; divide by n. This is stronger than invoking amenability but is proved directly.

## Turn2: bounded kernels and virtual classes, with ambient norms

If N is uniformly bounded and normal, the norm induced by images of the same normal generators in G/N differs from the ambient norm by at most the bound on N. A shortest quotient factorization lifts term by term to a normal factorization in G; the remaining factor is in N. This needs one bound on ALL of N, not individually bounded cyclic subgroups without a common bound.

For finitely generated virtually abelian Q choose a finitely generated abelian normal finite-index subgroup A. Apply the previous abelian-module compression in Q to K=[Q,A], uniformly bounded in the AMBIENT Q norm. Then A/K is central of finite index in E=Q/K. Here Schur's finite-derived conclusion is established explicitly: the finite quotient transversal gives finitely many pair commutators; every conjugation is determined by its finite quotient representative, so E' is ordinarily finitely generated. Its intersection with central C=A/K has finite index in E', hence is a finitely generated abelian group. Transfer to C is a homomorphism and sends c to c^m where m=[E:C]; because it kills E', the intersection has exponent dividing m and is finite. Thus E' is finite and Q' uniformly bounded after lifting through K. The transfer multiplicativity follows by the permutation of transversal indices and centrality; it is not asserted for a merely abelian noncentral subgroup.

For ordinarily finitely generated virtually metabelian G, take the normal finite-index core H of a metabelian finite-index subgroup. Schreier generation makes H ordinarily finitely generated, and H' is uniformly bounded intrinsically by Turn1. A finite H generating set has finite ambient G norm costs, so the bound remains uniform in G. The subgroup H' is normal in G. Its quotient is virtually abelian, and the preceding argument plus bounded-kernel lifting bounds G'. The same abelianization/detector conclusions follow. No universal d(d+1) ambient constant is asserted for the virtual extension.

The integral dihedral example must retain intrinsic/ambient distinction. In Z^d semidirect inversion, [v,t]=2v has ambient norm<=2. Write any integral translation as 2w+r with r in{0,1}^d; its norm<=d+2. A reflection v t is conjugate to r t and has norm<=d+1. The whole ambient group is bounded, whereas the index-two translation subgroup with its own intrinsic finite normal generators is unbounded. No finite-index norm equivalence is assumed.

## Turn3: split abelian extensions and the nonsplit control

For a split G=M semidirect Q, with M abelian and G ordinarily finitely generated, K=[G,M] is bounded by the module argument applied to a finite ordinary G generating set. The canonical quotient is M_Q times Q, where M_Q=M/K: conjugation acts trivially after quotienting and the section makes the two factors commute and have trivial intersection. Q and M_Q are finitely generated because they are quotients/projections of this finitely generated product.

With the union of finite generating sets for the factors, the product normal word norm is exactly the sum of factor norms: projecting a factorization gives the lower bound, and concatenating shortest factorizations gives equality. The actual quotient generating set need not be this chosen union, but finite ordinary generating sets give bi-Lipschitz normal norms. Positive stable growth or uniform boundedness therefore transfers from factors and then through K; a factor ordinary homogeneous detector pulls back to G. This proves inheritance of each dichotomy only if Q satisfies it, not a decision of the unrestricted original question. Restricted wreath products with a finitely generated abelian lamp and finitely generated base fit the stated split class; no quotient finite-presentation inheritance is needed.

The section is essential. In the integral Heisenberg group, the central kernel has trivial [G,M], yet the extension over Z² is nonsplit, not Z times Z². The central element z^n=[x^n,y] has bounded normal length despite its unbounded intrinsic center length. This rejects replacing a nonsplit extension by the displayed direct product and again distinguishes subgroup and ambient metrics.

## Turn4: exact stable norm under a supplied bounded-derived promise

A finite presentation on d generators supplies an integer relator exponent matrix R and a word exponent vector v. Given an independently established bound B on ALL of G', the quotient normal word length is the ordinary word length in Z^d/L, L the INTEGER row lattice of R, and the ambient norm differs by at most B. The correct stable value is

    alpha(v)=min_a ||v+R^T a||_1
            =max{u.v: Ru=0 and |u_i|<=1}.

The minimum/maximum use real variables; finite LP duality is a credited standard input. Finite rational data give rational primal/dual optimizers, but the finite-power quotient uses the integer lattice. If a rational optimal correction has denominator N, its N-multiple lies in L and provides a representative for Nv of length N alpha. The dual gives the lower bound at every power; decomposing arbitrary n into multiples of N plus finitely many residues gives an additive bounded error and convergence of length(nv)/n to alpha. The ambient B/n vanishes. Real row span is not silently substituted for integer torsion: alpha=0 iff v lies in the rational row span iff some Nv belongs to L, exactly torsion in the abelianization. This implies bounded powers only under the bounded-derived promise.

For exact finite enumeration, reduce to independent rows of R of rank r. The dual feasible set is a bounded polytope in a (d-r)-dimensional linear subspace. At a vertex, d-r independent coordinate constraints u_i=+/-1 combine with the rows to a nonsingular d by d system. Enumerating coordinate choices/signs and checking all constraints finds a maximizer. If r=d, the only vector is0; if d=0, the empty feasible vector is handled. The supplied code's random checks begin at d=1 but the theorem includes the trivial presentation separately. Dependent/zero rows must be pruned exactly, not approximately. Matching primal/dual objective with full feasibility is a certificate, not just an optimizer output.

For relator row(2,-3) and v=(0,1), the stable value2/3 is certified by dual(1,2/3) and primal residual(2/3,0). Negative signed powers follow symmetry. Integer-unsaturated rows can give nontrivial finite torsion even when their real span is full. The LP does not recognize whether the bound B exists. In a free group a commutator has exponent vector0 yet an ordinary Brooks quasimorphism detects it, so removing the promise makes the conclusion false.

## Turn5: actual two-generator finite models and a different infinite group

For K>=2, define a_j=t^j a t^-j and impose the braid relation aa_1a=a_1aa_1 and commutations [a,a_j]=1 for2<=j<=K. With N=K+2 and delta=sigma_1 ... sigma_{N-1}, send a to sigma_1 and t to delta. The conjugation identity delta sigma_i delta^-1=sigma_{i+1} is used only for1<=i<=N-2. It gives a_j=sigma_{j+1} through j=K, including the LAST listed commutation. There is no wraparound identity at i=N-1 or j=K+1. The braid relation maps to the neighboring Artin relation; all distance>=2 listed commutations hold. In the all-distance presentation G_infty, conjugating the infinitely many distance relations gives commutation of a_i,a_j whenever |i-j|>=2. Consequently w^n has some finite commutator length m in H, even if that length grows with n. If H is abelian or w^n=1 the bound is immediate; otherwise use BIP Theorem2.2(i).
