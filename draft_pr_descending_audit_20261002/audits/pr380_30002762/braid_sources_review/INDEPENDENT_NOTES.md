## Independently derived finite-range braid test

For K>=0 define P_K=<a,b,t | aba=bab, [u,t^k v t^-k]=1 (u,v in{a,b},1<=k<=K)>.
Let N=3(K+1), delta= sigma1 ... sigma_(N-1) in B_N. Send a->sigma1,b->sigma2,t->delta^3. The identity delta sigma_i delta^-1 = sigma_(i+1) holds for1<=i<=N-2. Thus for1<=k<=K the shifted generators are sigma_(1+3k),sigma_(2+3k). Their index differences from1,2 are>=2, so every four commuting relators holds, including k=K where the largest index2+3K=N-1. K=0 has no such relators and N=3. By the universal property these maps are genuine homomorphisms, not a test table substituted for a proof. No surjectivity is needed. At omitted distance K+1, delta^(3(K+1))=delta^N is central; [a,t^(K+1)b t^-(K+1)] maps to[sigma1,sigma2]!=1. This is a negative-mutant control against accidentally imposing all distances.

Detector g=a^-4 b a^2 b. Its Artin exponent is0 and its permutation is identity. The identity Z=(ab)^3=a^2 b a^2 b gives g=a^-6 Z. Z is central in B3 and hence commutes with a. Under Gambaudo-Ghys2005 Braids and Signatures convention, homogeneous signature sigma(a)=-1 (two-strand torus formula), sigma(ab)=-4/3 (Prop5.2,n3,theta1/2), so sigma(Z)=-4 and sigma(g)=6-4=2 exactly. Extra trivial strands add a disjoint disc to a Seifert surface (GG2005 p543), so sigma_N(g)=2. Every ordinary homogeneous quasimorphism is additive on commuting elements, justifying the decomposition. This derivation needs no computed signature values at a handful of powers.

GG2005 TheoremA expresses signature defect by Meyer cocycle; a finite-dimensional quadratic form signature is bounded by its dimension, giving a finite ordinary defect for fixed N. Its p575 homogenization statement bounds the difference from the original signature by2N. This gap is not itself a statement of the homogeneous defect; the ordinary defect of the homogenization is finite by the standard homogenization theorem. The lower bound uses the actual finite defectD without identifying it with the2N gap. Let q=sigma_N compose the above homomorphism, S={a,b,t}. Its conjugacy invariance and finite M=max(|q(a)|,|q(b)|,|q(t)|), defectD, imply2|p|<= (M+D)*norm_S(g^p). This is a lower bound for the normal word norm, not an Artin word length computation. It detects g by an ordinary homogeneous quasimorphism; it cannot be a counterexample to the stronger dichotomy at this word.

## Independently derived all-distance comparison

In P_infinity impose the four commuting relators for every positive shift distance. The conjugate base groups H_i=t^i H t^-i then pairwise commute for all unequal i,j. For any finite m one fixed t strongly m-displacesH. BIP2007/2008 Theorem2.2(i) requires x inH' and cl_H(x)=m, and proves norm(x)<=14 e_m(H)<=14 norm(t). Thus all powers of any fixed x inH' are bounded by14 norm(t). If the chosen actual displacerF has word norm<=3, the conservative numerical conclusion is42. The inference applies to powers x^p because they remain inH'; their commutator lengths may vary and the sameF displacesH for every requiredm. It does not assert all ofG is bounded, nor establish finite presentation, nor rule out another word outsideH'. A mere sequence of different displacers with unbounded norms would not give this bound. All-distance relations must be checked for the actual construction; finiteK only gives displacement up toK and cannot be used for arbitrary powers.
