## Exact target and conventions

Kędra's original target asks for a finitely presented group violating either of two cyclic-subgroup dichotomies for a word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes. The second uses an ordinary homogeneous real quasimorphism. Existing examples cited there do not furnish the requested finite presentation. This audit tests obstructions, not a complete answer to the existence problem.

For a finite set S={s_1,...,s_d}, the norm is the minimum number of letters in the union of G-conjugates of S and S^{-1}. Normal generation makes this norm finite; ordinary generation is the stronger requirement G=<S>. Different finite normal generating sets give bi-Lipschitz norms because the norm of each of the finitely many alternate generators has a finite maximum. Here d counts representatives before adjoining their inverses. In any conjugation-invariant norm, [x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1} satisfies ||[x,y]||<=2||y|| and also <=2||x||. This bounds a family uniformly only when one entry has a uniform norm bound. Bounded powers mean sup_{n in Z}||g^n||<infinity. Undistorted powers mean a positive linear lower bound, equivalently positive stable norm lim_{n->infinity}||g^n||/n, whose existence follows from subadditivity.

An ordinary real quasimorphism has a single finite defect D bounding |phi(xy)-phi(x)-phi(y)| on the entire group. Homogeneity means phi(g^n)=n phi(g). Such a function is conjugation invariant; if it is nonzero on g, it supplies a linear lower bound in a finite normal word norm. A norm-dependent defect is insufficient for that argument. All quotient pullbacks below have the same uniform defect as their source functions.

## Family A: arbitrary metabelian derived subgroup

Let G be ordinarily generated by s_1,...,s_d and M=G' abelian. Put C=[G,M]. Conjugation on M factors through the abelian group Q=G/M, so M is a Z[Q]-module. The ordinary-generator commutators c_ij=[s_i,s_j], i<j, normally generate M: their normal closure has abelian quotient. Therefore they generate M as a Z[Q]-module, without any assumption that M is finitely generated as an abelian group.

The augmentation ideal of Z[Q] is generated by the s_i-1. Consequently C=sum_i(s_i-1)M. Each element of C is a sum of at most d differences; in multiplicative notation it is a product of d commutators with the fixed s_i. Thus ||c||<=2d for every c in C. This is a bound on all elements, not merely on a single commutator.

Modulo C, the module action is trivial and the c_ij generate M/C as an abelian group. In G/C the commutator subgroup is central, so [s_i^n,s_j]=c_ij^n there for every integer n. Every m in M is therefore a product of the binomial(d,2) actual commutators [s_i^{n_ij},s_j] and an element of C. Each displayed commutator has norm at most 2. This proves the uniform bound

    sup_{m in M} ||m|| <= 2 binomial(d,2)+2d = d(d+1).

No statement that all elements of M are single commutators is needed. In the free class-two group of rank four, the central element c_12 c_34 is not a single commutator: its skew coefficient matrix has nonzero Pfaffian, whereas any single commutator has decomposable wedge coefficients and Pfaffian zero.

If the image of g in the finitely generated abelian group G/M is torsion, some g^r lies in M and the finitely many residues give ||g^n||<=d(d+1)+(r-1)||g||. Otherwise a real homomorphism on G/M is nonzero on its image and supplies the stronger dichotomy.

Boundary: module generation by the c_ij uses ordinary generation. For G=(direct sum over countably many copies of Z[1/2]) semidirect Z, with the generator t acting by multiplication by 2, t normally generates G and [a,t]=-a. Thus G is normally generated by one element but is not ordinarily finitely generated; its derived group is nonzero although there are no pairwise generator commutators. The claimed bound itself can be reconstructed more generally with normal generation by first passing to the class-two quotient G/C, where normal generation becomes ordinary generation. The simple module-generation proof above must not silently be used with normal generation alone.

## Family B: finite-index transfer in the ambient norm

Let G be ordinarily finitely generated, N a finite-index normal metabelian subgroup, A=N/N', and F=G/N. N is finitely generated by Schreier's argument. Family A bounds N' in an intrinsic finite normal word norm, and hence in the ambient norm: express the finitely many N-generators in G and use the largest ambient length. There is only an upper comparison here; an ambient lower comparison would be false.

Choose a finite set of coset representatives t_f for F, with t_1=1. Since the action on A factors through F,

    [G,N]/N' = sum_{f in F}(t_f-1)A.

Each element of [G,N] is thus a product of at most |F|-1 commutators [t_f,n_f] modulo N'. The entire subgroup [G,N] has finite ambient norm diameter, bounded by the N' bound plus 2 sum_{f!=1}||t_f||. This bound is independent of the n_f.

Put B=N/[G,N]; this is a finitely generated abelian group. For g in G choose k with g^k in N. If the image b of g^k in B is torsion, a further power lies in the bounded subgroup [G,N], so g has bounded powers. If b is nontorsion, choose f:B->R nonzero on b and pull it back to N. This homomorphism is G-invariant. It extends to a real homomorphism on G by the explicit transfer construction: fix left-coset representatives t_i and write t_i g=n_i(g)t_{sigma_g(i)}. Then chi(g)=|F|^{-1}sum_i f(n_i(g)) is additive; reindexing the permutation proves this directly. Invariance gives chi(n)=f(n) on N. Therefore k chi(g)=f(g^k)!=0. This proves the stronger dichotomy in the ambient norm without assuming equivalence to the restricted intrinsic norm.

Negative boundary: D_infinity=<t,r | r^2=1, rtr=t^{-1}> has index-two N=<t>. Its intrinsic normal word norm has ||t^n||_N=|n|, but t^{2m}=[t^m,r] has ambient norm at most 2, and odd powers have norm at most 3. This prevents a naive finite-index inheritance argument. A stronger control below adds a Laurent-polynomial lamp module, making N metabelian with infinitely generated abelian derived subgroup while retaining the same ambient collapse.

## Family C: split abelian extension

Let G=A semidirect Q, with A abelian and Q ordinarily generated by q_1,...,q_d; use generator lifts (0,q_i) whose ambient normal norms are at most 1 (or multiply each term by their individual norm). No abelianity of Q or commuting action on A is assumed.

Write C=[Q,A] additively. The right-factor identity uv-1=(u-1)v+(v-1), including q_i^{-1}-1=-(q_i-1)q_i^{-1}, shows

    C = sum_{i=1}^d (q_i-1)A.

The reverse inclusion is immediate. Because A is abelian, an arbitrary element of C can be grouped into exactly d differences (some zero). Each difference is a commutator with the fixed lift q_i. Hence sup_{c in C}||c||<=2d. In particular, this bound is independent of the size of A or of an expression as a sum of many conjugates. A mistaken left-factor telescoping formula could obstruct grouping when Q acts noncommutatively; the displayed right-factor identity resolves this.

There is a genuine homomorphism pi:G->A_Q x Q, (a,q)->([a],q), with kernel C. If G is finitely generated, A_Q is finitely generated abelian. For the quotient norm induced by the chosen finite normal generators, every x in G satisfies

    ||pi(x)|| <= ||x|| <= ||pi(x)||+2d.

The second inequality is proved by taking a minimum-length quotient word, lifting the same letters with the same conjugating elements, and absorbing the residual element in C. Thus stable norms are exactly equal for these induced norms.

In A_Q x Q, a nontorsion A_Q component is detected by a real homomorphism. A torsion A_Q component has bounded powers. If Q satisfies the bounded-or-undistorted dichotomy, the projection to Q gives the linear lower bound; if Q satisfies the stronger ordinary homogeneous-quasimorphism dichotomy, compose its detecting quasimorphism with the Q projection. Bounded powers of both components give bounded powers in the product by finite-generator comparisons, and then in G by the bounded kernel. These arguments require the same group-wide norm domain as the premise and ordinary quasimorphisms. They make no assertion about nonsplit extensions.

Nonsplit boundary: the integer Heisenberg group is a central extension of Z^2 by Z. Its central generator has bounded powers in the normal word norm even though its proposed central coordinate in Z x Z^2 would have linear powers. The central-coordinate map is not a homomorphism because of the multiplication cocycle. This pinpoints why the split map cannot be supplied for a nonsplit extension.

## Falsifiable controls planned before comparison

1. Execute the full class-two group law in rank four, check commutators, signed powers, central certificates, and the Pfaffian obstruction to a single commutator.
2. Execute the actual Laurent lamp semidirect D_infinity group law, construct bounded certificates for every zero-sum lamp element and its signed powers, and compare intrinsic translation growth with bounded ambient certificates.
3. Execute rational-vector semidirect products with doubling in increasing dimension; certify normal generation by t and expose the failure of pairwise-module generation under merely normal generation.
4. Execute A=Z^2 semidirect the free group on two generators acting by two noncommuting unipotent matrices. Check the exact coinvariant kernel 2Z^2, four-letter conjugation certificates, projection homomorphisms, and pullback of an ordinary homogeneous Brooks-type quasimorphism with a fixed global defect bound.
5. Use the Heisenberg multiplication cocycle to falsify the proposed nonsplit direct-product map.

These finite controls test identities and negative assumptions. The universal results rest on the proofs above, not on sampled counts. Before comparison, the reconstruction, source and Git receipts, control code and full output will receive a SHA-256 seal.

The ordinary quasimorphism control counts adjacent `ab` minus `b^{-1}a^{-1}` pairs in a reduced free-group word. Its raw function has defect at most 3: in a reduced product, the canceled inverse segments have opposite counts; only the two old joining edges and the new joining edge can contribute a discrepancy. Cyclically reduce a word and count the same signed pairs around the resulting cycle to obtain phi. Repeated cyclic cores prove exact signed homogeneity. Removing a conjugating prefix and suffix changes the raw count by at most 2, and closing the cyclic core adds at most 1; hence |phi-f_raw|<=3 and phi has global defect at most 12. This is an ordinary homogeneous quasimorphism on the whole free group, and its pullback to the semidirect product has that same defect. The sampled maximum is only an additional control, not the proof of bounded defect.
