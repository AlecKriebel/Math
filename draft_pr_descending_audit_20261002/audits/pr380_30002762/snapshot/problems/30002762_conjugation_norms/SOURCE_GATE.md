# Exact source and prior-attempt gate: 30002762

The full Kędra contribution, *The conjugation invariant geometry of cyclic subgroups*, OWR3/2015 printed199–200, was read and printed199 visually checked. Official PDF https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46555 ; DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2015/3 . It asks for a finitely presented group violating either: every cyclic subgroup is bounded or undistorted in a word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes; or every cyclic subgroup is bounded or detected by an ordinary homogeneous real quasimorphism. These norms are all bi-Lipschitz equivalent for a normally finitely generated group. Undistorted means positive linear lower growth of powers. Standard symmetric generating conventions are used, as in the full cited paper.

The full source explicitly credits Muranov's two-generator non-finitely-presented example for the first failure, and the infinite braid commutator subgroup for the second. Neither supplies the requested finite presentation. A plain embedding into a finitely presented group need not preserve the induced normal word norm, so no embedding shortcut is assumed.

Primary literature read: BGKM, *The cancellation norm and the geometry of bi-invariant word metrics*, Glasgow Math. J.58(2016)153–176, DOI10.1017/S0017089515000129; author manuscript https://math.bgu.ac.il/~brandens/bgkm.pdf, definitions/introduction and bounded-element, nilpotent and solvable arguments. The known solvable theorem assumes a finitely generated nilpotent commutator subgroup; this assumption is not automatic for a finitely generated metabelian group. Standard free, hyperbolic, braid, Coxeter and related positive classes are credited and not new discoveries here.

Brandenbursky–Kędra, AGT15(2015)2859–2884, https://msp.org/agt/2015/15-5/agt-v15-n5-p11-p.pdf, proves the infinite braid example. Kędra's later https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09373 (downloaded v3,3August2023) detects undistorted elements by antisymmetric homogeneous PARTIAL quasimorphisms, whose defect is norm-controlled rather than uniformly bounded. This does not solve the original ordinary-quasimorphism question. Fournier-Facio–Lodha–Zaremsky, AGT24(2024)1601–1622, https://msp.org/agt/2024/24-3/agt-v24-n3-p10-p.pdf, provides finitely presented braided groups with contrasting bounded-cohomology behavior, but vanishing bounded cohomology alone proves no normal-word unboundedness. No such implication is assumed.
