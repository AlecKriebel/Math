## Target, coefficients, and primary sources

The actual Davis talk jointly with Dymara, Januszkiewicz and Okun asks on printed2590, physical12 of OWR43/2006 whether every virtually typeFP group has finitely generated **total right group-ring cohomology** H*(G;ZG). FP means a finite-length resolution of the trivial left ZG module by finitely generated projectives. It is stronger than FP-infinity plus no dimension bound, and different from finite presentability. The coefficient module is left regular and the surviving action is right regular. Finite generation as an abelian group, field rank, or cup-product algebra does not answer this question. ## Turn1: finite index and the exact algebraic obstacle

Let H have finite index in G, not necessarily normal. With R=ZG and S=ZH, let pi_H keep just the H-supported coefficients. The map a -> (x -> pi_H(xa)) identifies R with Hom_S(R,S), for left G action (k f)(x)=f(xk) and right H multiplication on values. Left-coset representatives and inverse representatives give dual bases, so this map is bijective. Restricting a projective G-resolution remains projective over H; coinduction adjunction gives Res_H H^i(G;R) = H^i(H;S), preserving the native right-H action. It is **not** the usually larger induced module H^i(H;S) tensor_S R. Finite generation of a right R-module is equivalent to finite generation of its restricted S-module: one direction multiplies each R-generator by finitely many coset representatives; the other uses the same S-generators. Hence the target reduces to FP groups without a virtual qualification. FP resolutions have finite length; total finite generation then reduces to finitely many degrees.

Dualizing the finite resolution gives finitely generated projective right modules C^i. The top group H^n is a cokernel of a map between such modules, hence finitely presented (FP1 as module). H^0 is Z for a trivial finite FP group and zero for an infinite group by finite-support invariance. A coherent right ring makes kernels of maps between finite projectives finitely generated, so all cohomology is finitely generated. Assuming coherence of arbitrary group rings is an unsupported route.

For n>=1, put B=im(C^(n-1)->C^n), Z=ker(C^(n-1)->C^n). Both preceding boundary modules are finitely generated. The two exact sequences with finite projective C^n and C^(n-1), plus Schanuel's lemma, imply H^n is FP2 iff B is FP1 iff Z is finitely generated iff H^(n-1) is finitely generated. FP1 of H^n alone is insufficient. In Z<x,y>/(x y^a x: a>=0), normal words are y^a or y^a x y^b. The right-linear map given by left multiplication by x has kernel direct sum_a y^a x Z[y], whose initial a cannot change under right multiplication, so it is not finitely generated. The cokernel is cyclic and FP1, not FP2. This is a ring control, not a group-ring or dual-augmentation example.

## Turn2: conditional ascending HNN and finite free products

For an injective endomorphism phi of a good FP group A, the Bass–Serre tree yields a length-one resolution by induced trivial A-modules. Substituting the finite A-projective resolution yields a finite G-projective mapping cone and hence FP. The cohomological tree map is 1-T, where at bar-cochain level T f(a_1,...,a_q)=t f(phi(a_1),...,phi(a_q)), for t^-1 a t=phi(a). Indeed a t=t phi(a) makes T commute with the differential; left multiplication by t commutes with residual right-G multiplication. The height homomorphism sends t to1 and A to0. Induced cochains split as a direct sum of integer height pieces because the source projectives are finitely generated. Cohomology commutes with this direct sum; T raises height. A nonzero finitely supported height sum has a lowest nonzero component that survives under1-T, so its kernel is zero even if T is not injective. Therefore H^q(G;ZG) is a quotient of H^(q-1)(A;ZA) tensor_ZA ZG and remains finitely generated **assuming the base is good**.

For BS(1,m), m>=1, the cyclic base has H^1 represented by R/(a-1)R. The induced map uses t(1+a+...+a^(m-1)), since t(a^m-1)=(a-1)t. Thus the only nonzero positive degree is the cyclic H^2 with the two displayed right-ideal relations. At m=1 the expression agrees with Z^2 and trivial orientation. No abelian finite-generation conclusion is inferred for m>1. For a finite free product of good FP factors, the tree sequence gives induced direct sums in degree>=2; degree1 is an extension involving a quotient of a finite free R-module and the induced H^1 terms. Its finite generation follows without assuming a kernel in the middle is finitely generated.

**Repair required:** the incidental statement about a general graph of FP vertex/edge groups needs **finite underlying graph**, or an equivalent finite-orbit hypothesis for the tree resolution. A countable bouquet of loops with trivial stabilizers has fundamental group the infinitely generated free group and is not FP1. The single ascending HNN and finite free-product theorems already have finite orbit sets. The corrected broad statement is only finite-length-FP closure; it supplies no automatic regular-cohomology finiteness for arbitrary nonascending maps or amalgams.

## Turn3: integral Kunneth, torsion, and detection

Tensoring the two finite projective resolutions over Z gives a finite resolution over the product group ring: finite projectives are direct summands of finite frees and Z-free, and the underlying exact complexes resolve Z. Their finite dual complexes identify with the tensor product dual. Splitting boundaries over Z, not equivariantly, gives the natural module exact sequence

0 -> direct_sum_(i+j=n) M_i tensor_Z N_j -> H^n(AxB;Z[AxB]) -> direct_sum_(i+j=n+1) Tor_1^Z(M_i,N_j) -> 0.

The Tor term is one degree lower than the tensor of cohomology representatives. All actions are natural, but there is no claimed equivariant splitting. If the two factors are good, tensor terms have generators given by finitely many pairs; the product is good iff all actual Tor terms are finitely generated. Z-flat cohomology for one factor kills them. No general finite-generation theorem for these Tor modules over a nonnoetherian product ring follows from finite generation of the inputs.

Over a field, nonzero finite-dimensional-source dual cohomology exists for an FP group: if the bounded finite-projective dual were acyclic, repeatedly split off its projective last term to contract it; dualizing back would contradict H_0=k of the resolution. If M tensor N is finitely generated over k[A x B] and N!=0, its finite generators use finitely many M coordinates. Let M_0 be their kA-span. The product orbit remains in M_0 tensor N, so (M/M_0) tensor N=0; over a field tensor with nonzero N detects a nonzero vector space. Hence M=M_0. Integral PD factors instead have a single flat orientation module Z_w; tensoring shifts cohomology and changes only the sign action of the other factor, so finite generation is equivalent. Rational finite generation alone cannot detect integral kernels or torsion.

The integral short exact coefficient sequence for multiplication by m gives 0 -> M_i/mM_i -> H^i(G;(Z/m)G) -> M_(i+1)[m] ->0. Reducing the finite Z-free resolution remains exact because it resolves the Z-flat module Z. If M_i is finitely generated, the middle term is finitely generated exactly when the adjacent fixed-m torsion kernel is. For p-killed N, Tor_1^Z(M,N)=M[p] tensor_Fp N naturally, with its residual product action. These fixed-prime/fixed-modulus tests do not control all torsion or an arbitrary Tor module. For the two-term complexes Z --m-> Z and Z --n-> Z in degrees0,1, the tensor total differentials are (m,n) and (-n,m); H^1 and H^2 both equal Z/gcd(m,n), respectively Tor and tensor. This validates the index and falsifies rational-only evidence without pretending these complexes are group resolutions.

## Turn4: nonsplit extension and diagonal right action

For 1->N->G->Q->1, suppose N is good FP and Q is FP with group-ring cohomology concentrated in degree d, dual module D **Z-flat**. The bounded Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for arbitrary coefficients bounds cd G by cd N+cd Q. Cohomology of both N and Q commutes with filtered colimits, since finite projective resolutions do. Naturality, exact filtered colimits and bounded filtrations transfer that property to G. The Davis/Brown criterion then gives FP for G; finite cd truncates an FP-infinity resolution at a finitely generated projective syzygy. No finite-index or finite-presentation substitute is used.

For M=H^q(N;ZN), C_g f(n_1,...)=g f(g^-1 n_1 g,...) g^-1 defines a left G action. The right action m.g=C_(g^-1)m extends native right N multiplication: the inner bar map precomposed with coefficient multiplication is chain-homotopic to identity via the homogeneous prism with vertices n_i and n_i n. Thus C_n(m)=m n^-1, not m. The induced bimodule M tensor_N ZG has left Q action L_g(m tensor h)=C_g(m) tensor gh, well-defined because C_g(mn)=C_g(m)(gng^-1). This commutes with residual right G multiplication.

For a lift tilde q, q tensor m -> (m.tilde q^-1) tensor tilde q identifies ZQ tensor_Z M with M tensor_N ZG. It is independent of the lift by balancing; its inverse is m tensor h -> bar h tensor (m.h). Right G acts diagonally: (q tensor m).g=q bar g tensor (m.g). This construction needs no splitting of the extension. Applying the finite Q-dual complex and the integral cochain UCT gives only D tensor M in degree d, since flat D kills Tor. The one nonzero column collapses the spectral sequence without filtration ambiguity, yielding H^(d+q)(G;ZG)=D tensor M_q with this diagonal action. D is finitely generated over ZQ by splitting off the acyclic finite-projective tail above d. Choose Q-generators z_i and N-generators m_j; every z_i q tensor m equals (z_i tensor m.g^-1).g, and expanding m.g^-1 over the N-generators shows all pairs z_i tensor m_j generate as G-module. Dropping flatness or replacing the diagonal action by only one factor invalidates this proof.

## Turn5: genuine noncoherence, but no target counterexample

Let G=F(a,b)xF(c,d), chi send every generator to1, H=ker chi. In the first factor the height-kernel free basis is u_i=a^i b a^-(i+1), and in the second v_j=c^j d c^-(j+1). This follows from the infinite cyclic rose cover with all a-edges or c-edges chosen as a spanning tree. With t=a c^-1, u=b a^-1, v=d c^-1, H=(K_1xK_2) semidirect Z is generated by t,u,v; conjugation shifts u_i up and v_j down. The mapping torus of the actual shift homeomorphism on the product of two countable roses is a K(H,1). H_1 of the base has no fixed finite-support elements. H_2 has basis e_(i,j), shifted to e_(i+1,j-1), so its coinvariant basis is indexed by i+j. Finite telescoping on each orbit proves the kernel of projection is exactly im(1-T). Consequently H_2(H;Z) is direct_sum_Z Z and H is not FP2: an FP2 resolution tensored with Z would force finitely generated integral H_2.

The right augmentation ideal of S=ZH is generated by t-1,u-1,v-1 using hk-1=(h-1)k+(k-1) and inverse identities. Its presentation kernel K_H in S^3 is not finitely generated, or Z would be FP2. R=ZG is free and faithfully flat as a left S-module, so the same matrix's kernel over R is K_H tensor_S R. If finitely many R-generators existed, finitely many K_H coordinates would span K_0 and give (K_H/K_0) tensor R=0; faithfulness forces K_H=K_0, contradiction. This is an actual non-finitely-generated matrix kernel over an actual group ring of an FP group. Its cokernel is cyclic and finitely presented, not FP2.

Nevertheless G's own product-tree dual resolution has H^0=H^1=0, and H^2=D_A tensor_Z D_B generated by four pairs. Each D_A is Z-torsion-free: if m z=delta r then reduction modulo m forces finitely supported A-invariance of r, hence r=m s and z=delta s. Product Tor therefore vanishes. The bad two-term matrix is not this augmentation resolution. Adding finite preceding boundaries cannot repair its non-finitely-generated kernel (a quotient by a finitely generated boundary remaining finitely generated would force the kernel finitely generated), but realizing that differential within a genuine finite augmentation resolution requires further work. Noncoherence of this ring alone neither answers the original question nor converts H, which is not FP2, into an admissible group.
