## Original question

For a group Gamma virtually of type FP, is the total graded cohomology
H*(Gamma;ZGamma) finitely generated as a Gamma-module?

The primary is Michael Davis, joint with Jan Dymara, Tadeusz Januszkiewicz
and Boris Okun, “Cohomology of Coxeter groups with group ring coefficients,”
in Topologie, Oberwolfach Report43/2006, printed pp.2588–2590. The complete
contribution was read and p.2590 visually inspected. The question follows
a proved Coxeter special case. Primary PDF:
https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46073
Publisher page: https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/1381
The report volume is3(2006), pp.2579–2630; actual publication was30 September
2007. The import's 2007 label is not a contradictory report number.

The coefficient group ring has its left regular Gamma action for group
cohomology and its commuting right regular action. The latter gives the
natural **right ZGamma-module** on cohomology. Finite generation is over
this group ring, not over Z and not finite generation of an ordinary cup-
product algebra. These distinctions are explicit in the companion published
paper, Davis–Dymara–Januszkiewicz–Okun, Algebraic & Geometric Topology6(2006),
1289–1318, introduction and Theorem4.5/corollary,
https://msp.org/agt/2006/6-3/agt-v6-n3-p09-s.pdf .

## Exact FP convention

Use the source author's convention: type FP means that the trivial left
ZGamma-module Z has a **finite-length resolution by finitely generated
projective modules**. It is stronger than FP_infinity alone and does not
imply finite presentation of the group. Virtually FP means a finite-index
subgroup of this type; Gamma itself may have torsion. The same author's
*Poincare duality groups*, §2 p.4, states the definition explicitly:
https://people.math.osu.edu/davis.12/pdgroup.pdf . His2024 book *Infinite group
actions on polyhedra*, §7.2, printed p.223/PDF233, confirms it:
https://people.math.osu.edu/davis.12/IGAP.pdf . Relevant definition passages
were read; unrelated open claims in these sources are not adopted as current
facts. The finite-index reduction in the first proof turn will also make
explicit why total graded cohomology is supported in finitely many degrees.

## Credited known positive classes and literature scope

The2006 Coxeter paper proves finite generation for finite-rank Coxeter groups
through a finite filtration with finitely generated associated pieces.
This does not prove it for arbitrary virtually-FP groups.

Davis–Okun, *Cohomology computations for Artin groups, Bestvina–Brady groups,
and graph products*, Groups, Geometry, and Dynamics6(2012),485–531,
DOI10.4171/GGD/164, published author-hosted PDF:
https://people.math.osu.edu/davis.12/papers/do3final-okun.pdf . Its introduction,
Theorems4.1 and4.3 and their hypotheses give further known positive classes:
right-angled Artin groups, the indicated K(pi,1) Artin cases, and acyclic
Bestvina–Brady kernels. The finite equivariant filtration, rather than an
unjustified direct-sum splitting of modules, is the relevant mechanism.

For arbitrary graph-product factors the displayed formula retains the
cohomology of those factors. A single-vertex graph is just its factor, so
an introductory broad finite-generation sentence cannot on its own prove
the present general question. We do not promote that sentence into a proof
for arbitrary groups or assert a counterexample to its main calculations.
The older2010 preprint was also consulted, but the2012 published version
controls the stated formulas and hypotheses.

Primary author publication pages, exact-phrase searches and relevant sections
