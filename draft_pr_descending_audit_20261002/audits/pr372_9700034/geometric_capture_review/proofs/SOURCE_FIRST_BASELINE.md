## Independently derived universal results

1. For each finite n, E M_n <= n E len R(0,U_1) = (2n/3) Delta, Delta=E D1, by rotation/scaling and E|U|=2/3. M_n increases to M, so E M=lim_n E M_n in [0,infinity]. Finiteness for every n is not uniform integrability or a bounded limit.

2. In any jointly measurable realization of the route length field L(omega,u), conditional iid sampling yields M=ess sup_{u in disc(0,1)} L(omega,u) almost surely. For every rational t, conditional probability that all samples miss {L>t} is lim_n(1-q_t)^n, equal to 1 if q_t=0 and 0 otherwise. This is an essential supremum, not an all-point supremum; continuity/separability cannot be imported from endpoint density. If only FDD measurability is being used, formulate this via the corresponding measurable conditional sampling kernel rather than assuming an unprovided jointly measurable sample version.

3. General axioms imply a useful *almost-sure excursion capture*: all the countably sampled rooted routes are contained in one random bounded disc. Couple U_i as successive spatial arrivals of the independent space-time PPP in the unit disc. Equation (6.4) captures every rooted route after deletion of endpoint discs in the intrinsic E(infinity,r), simultaneously on this countable sample. Let C=E(infinity,1/2) intersect circle(0,2). The source Crofton/circle calculation yields E|C| <= 4*2*p(1/2)=16p(1), hence |C|<infinity almost surely. Every route that visits outside disc(0,2) has a first exit a and last entrance b on that circle. Both a,b belong to C because their distances to 0 and U_i exceed 1/2. The route is simple, so a != b for an actual exterior excursion. For each ordered pair (a,b) realized by some sampled route, select its least-index representative. Compatibility makes the entire a-to-b subroute identical for all routes using that pair, including every exterior piece. There are finitely many such pairs, each representative is finite-length and therefore bounded. Their union together with disc(0,2) captures all sampled routes. It also bounds the total exterior union length almost surely. No bound on the expectation of the capture radius or selected representative length has been proved. This does not prove M finite almost surely because arbitrarily long pieces could remain inside the captured compact set near varying endpoints.

4. For deterministic r,K, E len(E(infinity,r) intersect K)=(p(1)/r) area(K). For random capture radius A correlated with E, substituting K=disc(0,A) inside this expectation identity is invalid. A correct dyadic bound retains joint quantities E[len(E(infinity,r) intersect disc(0,2^{k+1})) 1_{A>2^k}]. A marginal tail of A and first moment of local length cannot generally be multiplied.

5. A random finite number of route cores plus each core's finite length yields almost-sure finite maximum of those cores. Finite mean count and finite mean deterministic-pair length do not bound the mean length of adaptively selected cores, absent appropriate joint control. The endpoint pieces still require uniform control.

## Adversarial controls (not SIRSN counterexamples)

A. Integrable deterministic length field with unbounded essential supremum: L(u)=1+|u-a|^{-1/2} for an interior a. In two dimensions its integral is finite, yet iid dense samples have infinite supremum almost surely. A version with arbitrarily large finite plateaus on tiny positive-area sets gives the same marginal/uniform gap without exceptional infinity. This only falsifies inference from integrable marginals.

B. Compatible compact planar tree with endpoint hairs: take a straight trunk in the unit disc and pairwise disjoint thin rectangles approaching one point of the trunk. In the nth rectangle place a finite simple polygonal serpentine of length at least n, attached to the trunk at one end, with terminal endpoint v_n. Rectangles can have diameters epsilon_n ->0 while their finite serpentine edge counts grow. Unique tree paths from the root to v_n are compatible, individually finite, and all contained in the unit disc, but their length supremum is infinite. For any fixed trimming radius r>0 only finitely many hairs have diameter >=r; the others are wholly deleted by the terminal ball. Thus compact containment, compatibility, and fixed-r trimmed local finiteness alone do not control endpoint decorations. This construction is not stationary, similarity invariant, a continuum route family, or a certified SIRSN; it does not answer Aldous's question.

C. Random-radius expectation control: an a.s. finite Pareto variable A with P(A>t)=1/t for t>=1 has infinite expectation. Even the deterministic local length function F(t)=t is finite for every fixed t but E F(A)=infinity. Almost-sure compact containment has no automatic expected-length consequence. To test correlation specifically, let A take value 2^k with mass proportional to 2^{-2k}, and define a random local measure with total length 2^{2k}/k on the event A=2^k, located outside radius 2^{k-1}. Every deterministic compact has finite expected measure, but the random captured total has mean proportional to sum_k 1/k=infinity despite E A<infinity.

D. Minimum-cost versus length: positive cost weights can make the selected path much longer in Euclidean length than a competing path. Triangle inequalities for the cost metric do not supply a triangle inequality for lengths of selected compatible routes. Any universal metric-chaining argument must name an extra metric/length comparison or explicit length-minimization hypothesis.

## Exact gap and planned falsification

The source axioms control deterministic-pair length and deterministic-window trimmed length. They imply finite crossing places and hence almost-sure capture of exterior excursions, but they have not supplied integrable selected exterior cores or a uniform bound on endpoint pieces across dense destinations. A proof of E M<infinity must close both gaps, or add an independently checkable sufficient hypothesis. A proposed SIRSN counterexample must satisfy all consistency, symmetry, scaling, compatibility, and p(1) requirements simultaneously. Quantitative tail statements must survive correlated random radii, variable lengths, null-set versions, and finite-to-infinite passage. Model-specific speed/time estimates are not automatically universal.
