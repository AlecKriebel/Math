## Exact target and boundaries over C

Let A be a finite set of distinct projective lines in P^2_C, Z the reduced finite set of points lying on at least two of them, n=|Z|, and k=max_{projective lines L}|L intersect Z| for n>0. Define epsilon as infimum d/M over all reduced geometrically irreducible complex plane curves C of degree d>=1 with M=sum_{p in Z} mult_p(C)>0. Target: M<=k*d for every such C. The line attaining k gives equality and hence the upper bound 1/k. Any line, including auxiliary noncomponents, has M equal to its number of marked points, so degree one is automatically handled by the definition of k. Replacing k by a component maximum requires a separate proof of equality or produces a different claim.

An arrangement of zero or one distinct line gives Z empty and k=0; the source's displayed reciprocal is undefined in ordinary real arithmetic. The usual infimum definition has no admissible curve and can be extended to +infinity, but no empty-set equality may be silently asserted. Repeated lines are not a reduced line arrangement and must be discarded or separately specified. Two or more distinct concurrent lines give Z={p}, k=1, epsilon=1: mult_p(C)<=degree(C), and any line through p attains it. No non-pencil hypothesis is needed to state the nonempty claim. Homogeneous defining forms, geometric reducedness, irreducibility over C, and actual point multiplicities are essential.

## Genus/Cauchy derivation and finite cutoff

For reduced irreducible C of degree d over C, blowing up the distinct points once gives arithmetic genus p_a(C')=((d-1)(d-2)-sum m_p(m_p-1))/2. C' remains integral, so p_a>=0. Thus sum m_p(m_p-1)<=d^2-3d+2. Cauchy gives M^2/n-M<=d^2-3d+2. For a strict target violation integer M>=k*d+1. Since f(M)=M^2/n-M need not be increasing for small M, substituting the minimum requires k*d+1>=n/2 (or a separate case). If k^2>n, d>=2, and k>=2, one has k*d+1>=n/2 only for d large enough; therefore a valid cutoff must either show that threshold holds throughout the range used or handle lower degrees independently. Above this threshold, the polynomial

    (k^2-n)d^2 + (2k+n(3-k))d + 1-3n <= 0

is necessary for a violation. If a=k^2-n>0, positive root R=(-b+sqrt(b^2-4ac))/(2a) gives d<=R once monotonicity applies. Taking max of a monotonicity threshold and floor(R) safely bounds all possible d. A cleaner universally valid alternative for large d is to first require d>=ceil(n/(2k)), which implies monotonicity, then compute R. Exact integer balancing strengthens Cauchy: write M=nq+r, 0<=r<n. Among nonnegative integer multiplicities, minimal sum m^2=(n-r)q^2+r(q+1)^2. This gives an exact finite necessary integer filter. Boundary k^2=n has a=0; b may be positive (k<=3), negative (k>=4), so there is no general finite-cutoff inference from equality alone. If k^2<n the quadratic opens downward and the genus relaxation allows arbitrarily large degrees. A finite sweep does not prove the unrestricted conjecture absent a justified cutoff. The volume upper bound epsilon<=1/sqrt(n) also means target equality would require k^2>=n; an unexplained assumption of strict > changes scope.

## Exact interpolation semantics

A homogeneous degree-d linear system imposing prescribed multiplicity lower bounds is linear over the field containing the geometric point coordinates. Exact full column rank over Q or an algebraic number field proves no such polynomial even after base change to C. A nonzero kernel gives existence of some form, not an irreducible/reduced curve. Factors, fixed components, extra multiplicities, and field extensions must be resolved before treating it as a counterexample. Irreducible over Q can split over C. For a number field, actual algebraic embeddings and distinct geometric points must be preserved; a quotient with zero divisors is not a field. Reduction modulo p can certify a nonzero characteristic-zero minor when denominators are p-integral and the matrix is the correct reduction. Mod-p deficiency cannot certify characteristic-zero deficiency. Floating tolerances are not exact rank/field semantics.

## Auxiliary line-cover mechanism and dual

For nonempty finite Z, the meaningful support sets of all projective lines are finite: lines through pairs of points produce every support of cardinality>=2, and singleton supports can be added. Define tau=min sum w_L, w>=0, with sum_{L through p}w_L>=1 for each p. Its finite-dimensional dual is max sum y_p, y>=0, with sum_{p on L}y_p<=1 for every included line. For an irreducible nonlinear C, C shares no line component with any L. Bezout gives sum_{p on L}m_p<=d, so M<=tau*d. Thus tau<=k suffices, after handling all lines separately, to prove epsilon=1/k. Arrangement-only covers can only have a larger or equal minimum; auxiliary lines may strengthen the certificate. A primal certificate proves an upper bound on tau and a dual certificate proves a lower bound, with equality only when objectives match. LP feasible multiplicities/dual weights are numerical relaxations and do not certify algebraic curve existence. For n>=2, singleton lines are dominated by any pair-line containing that singleton; for n=1 they cannot be omitted. An incomplete auxiliary list yields a sufficient certificate when primal weights are checked, but an incomplete-list dual is not a valid all-lines lower bound unless all missed constraints are independently proved redundant.

## Modular and Fermat independent checks

Modular arrangement: suppose p is a singular point such that every q in Z other than p lies on an arrangement component through p. Let t be the number of components through p. These t lines cover Z, giving M<=t*d for nonlinear C. If there is a component not through p, its t distinct intersections with the pencil components are singular, so k>=t. If none exists, this is a pencil and the earlier singleton argument applies. This proves the target under precisely that modular hypothesis; it does not prove every arrangement is modular.

Fermat: for s>=2, arrange the 3s linear factors of (x^s-y^s)(y^s-z^s)(z^s-x^s) over C. Z consists of s^2 nonzero grid points plus the three vertices; each component has s+1 marked points. A line not in the arrangement and avoiding vertices has at most s grid points by Bezout with x^s-y^s. One containing exactly one vertex has at most s grid points by using the polynomial of the opposite pencil, so at most s+1 total. One through two vertices is a coordinate line and has no grid points. Therefore all-lines k=s+1. For s>=3, every singular multiplicity of the arrangement is >=3, so summing Bezout against 3s components gives M<=s*d for nonlinear C; for s=2 each singular multiplicity>=2 gives M<=3d=k*d. Together with the component line upper bound, target equality follows. s=1 is three sides of a triangle with n3,k2 and can be checked separately. s=0 is not this reduced arrangement family.

An exact rational s=2 Fermat control shows auxiliary strengthening: component weights 1/3 plus coordinate-line weights 1/6 cover every marked point at total 5/2. Dual weights 1/2 on the three vertices and 1/4 on the four grid points obey every pair-line support constraint and sum to 5/2. Arrangement-only uniform weights 1/2 cost3. The script checks all supports exactly, not just components; this is an LP fact, not a novel/global theorem claim.
