# Turn 2: the framing obstruction and the complete forty-letter slice

Status: original classification unresolved2/5. This turn proves a literal Hurwitz-only obstruction, classifies all possible linking vectors at the three allowed lengths, and completely resolves the forty-letter slice modulo simultaneous conjugation. It does not use the convention issue to claim an unframed geometric classification.

## 1. Source convention must remain qualified

Wajnryb's own chapter does not define Hurwitz equivalence. Its question on printed126 says Hurwitz equivalent and points to Auroux's chapter. Auroux, printed131, explicitly defines adjacent Hurwitz moves and then separately introduces global conjugation. Nevertheless the geometric motivation and the following closed-surface comparison concern monodromy classifications that conventionally include simultaneous conjugation. We cannot infer from the abbreviated wording that Wajnryb intended to forbid it.

Accordingly the following is a theorem for the strict equivalence generated by adjacent Hurwitz moves alone. It is not promoted to a negative answer under the natural weaker equivalence including global conjugation. The first-turn statements about the literal wording should be read with this qualification; frozen earlier bytes remain unchanged.

## 2. A strict Hurwitz obstruction

Continue with c=(a1a2a3a4)^5, h=a5a4a3a2 a1² a2a3a4a5, z=(a1a2a3a4a5)^6 and G=B6/<<c h^−1>>. Turn1 gives c²=z in G. The full twist z is central already in B6.

Set d=a1a2a3a4a5. The standard braid identities give d a_i d^−1=a_(i+1) for i=1,...,4. Thus

    w=(a2a3a4a5)^10 = d c² d^−1 = z = c² in G.             (1)

The relation is pure, so the standard permutation map pi:G→S6 is well-defined. For the forty-term tuple underlying c², the subgroup generated by its images is the S5 fixing6. For the tuple underlying w it is the S5 fixing1. These are different subgroups of S6, although they are conjugate.

Every Hurwitz move (x,y)→(xyx^−1,x), or its inverse, preserves the subgroup generated by the complete tuple: each old pair can be recovered as words in the new pair and conversely. Therefore its image under pi is also invariant. The two forty-term tuples in (1) are not strictly Hurwitz equivalent. Neither can the second tuple be equivalent to the thirty-term or twenty-term standard tuple, because the number of factors is invariant.

This proves the negative assertion under the strict convention. Under simultaneous conjugation, the two tuples are explicitly equivalent by d, so it provides no obstruction at all to the modulo-conjugation question.

## 3. All possible pairwise linking vectors

The first-turn normal-closure argument gives, for any positive representative w of c² of length m,

    L_ij = beta_ij+T−2(t_i+t_j) ≥ 0,
    T=sum t_i=m/10−4,

where beta is twice the complete graph on vertices1,...,5 and zero on pairs incident to6. The integers t_i can have either sign. We now solve these inequalities at m20,30,40 completely.

Write s=sum_(i≤5)t_i and t6=T−s. The ten internal inequalities are t_i+t_j≤floor((T+2)/2); the five other inequalities are t_i+t6≤floor(T/2).

### Length20: twice a star

T=−2. Summing the internal inequalities gives s≤0, and summing the cross inequalities gives s≥−1. Thus s is−1 or0.

If s=−1, t6=−1 and every t_i≤0. Exactly one of the first five coordinates is−1 and the other four are0. If s=0, t6=−2 and each t_i≤1. The pair-sum bound is0. A positive coordinate M≥1 would force the other four to be at most−M and their total sum to be at most−3M<0. Hence all five are0.

Substituting, L is exactly twice a star, centered at one of the six vertices: L_ij=2 precisely when the chosen center is in {i,j}, and0 otherwise.

### Length30: the full complete graph

T=−1. The internal inequalities give s≤0. The cross inequalities give s≥0, so s=0 and t6=−1. Each first coordinate is≤0, hence all are0. Substituting gives L_ij=1 for every pair.

### Length40: twice a five-vertex clique

T=0. The internal pair bound is1. If the maximum first coordinate M is≤0 then s≤0. If M≥1, the other four are≤1−M, giving s≤M+4(1−M)≤1. Thus s≤1. The cross inequalities imply t6≤0, hence s=−t6≥0.

If s=0, t6=0 and all first coordinates are≤0, hence all0. If s=1, t6=−1 and all first coordinates are≤1. Pair sums≤1 allow at most one coordinate1, and the sum1 then forces precisely one1 and four0. Substituting, L is twice the complete graph on five vertices, with the remaining vertex isolated.

These are necessary conditions, not a claim that every positive braid with the prescribed linking vector has the required image in G. The distinctions matter in lengths20 and30.

## 4. Complete classification of the forty-letter slice

Let m=40 and let k be the isolated vertex in its linking vector. Since the word is positive, zero linking of strand k with every other strand means that strand k has no crossings at all. It therefore stays in its original position, and the word uses neither adjacent generator a_(k−1) nor a_k whenever defined.

If 2≤k≤5, the untouched strand separates strands to its left from strands to its right. No crossings between these two sets can occur in such a word. But the required linking vector is2 on every pair of vertices other than k, including a pair lying on opposite sides. This contradiction shows that k=1 or6.

If k=6, the word uses only a1,...,a4. The four-chain geometric homomorphism B5→Mod(Sigma_2^1) is injective: it is the A4 instance of the Perron–Vannier theorem stated as Theorem2.1 in Wajnryb's chapter, where the surface is a regular neighborhood of the chain. Under the map from G constructed in turn1, w and c² have the same image. Therefore they are equal already in this B5 subgroup.

The positive braid monoid embeds in B5 (classical Garside theory). Thus two positive words representing the same B5 element can be related by positive commutations and adjacent braid relations. Each such word relation induces strict Hurwitz equivalence of the factor tuples. A commutation xy=yx is one Hurwitz swap. For xyx=yxy, two inverse Hurwitz moves give

    (x,y,x) → (y,y^−1 x y,x) → (y,x,x^−1 y^−1 x y x)
            = (y,x,y).

Consequently every forty-letter representative with k=6 is strictly Hurwitz equivalent to c².

If k=1, the same argument on the other four-curve chain shows strict Hurwitz equivalence to w0=(a2a3a4a5)^10. That chain's neighborhood is again genus2 with one boundary, and its boundary is parallel to the surviving boundary of the capped five-chain surface. Alternatively conjugate the entire argument by d.

It follows that there are **exactly two strict Hurwitz classes** of positive forty-letter representatives of c², represented by c² and w0. They are distinct by §2. They form exactly one class after allowing simultaneous conjugation.

## 5. Credit, certificates and next gap

Classical inputs are the Artin action/full-twist identities, the Perron–Vannier geometric injection and the positive braid monoid embedding. The latter and the faithful free-group action are verified in González-Meneses, *Basic results on braid groups*, AMBP18(2011),15–59, §§1.6 and4 (especially printed43–44). The first-turn bound40 is still credited to Baykur–Monden–Van Horn-Morris. No historical novelty is certified.

The exact checker verifies the conjugation identities, centrality, permutation subgroups of order120, their different fixed vertices, and bounded stress cases of the linking classifications. The inequalities above prove the classifications without any bounded-integer assumption.

The remaining substantive question modulo simultaneous conjugation is now confined to lengths20 and30. Their linking patterns are completely determined as above, but linking data is only a necessary invariant. Further work must establish actual equality in G and equivalence of factorizations, not infer either from those vectors or from a closed-surface theorem.
