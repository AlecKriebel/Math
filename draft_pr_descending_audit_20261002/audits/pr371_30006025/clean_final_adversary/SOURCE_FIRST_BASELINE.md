The original problem is Louf's Question 4 on printed page 2405 of OWR 41/2024: a geometric adaptation of Chapuy's bijection to construct random surfaces. I read the surrounding printed pages 2404–2406 and visually inspected printed 2405. The context is closed genus-g hyperbolic surfaces under normalized Weil–Petersson volume, compared to Lebesgue-distributed one-face metric ribbon graphs at perimeter 12g. Both short-cycle/geodesic limits have intensity (cosh t − 1)/t. The wording is an open-ended question, not a precise pre-existing theorem to negate. A concrete new construction could advance it; obstructing specified constructions cannot settle all possible adaptations.

Credited facts: CFF gives 2^(n+1) copies of rooted unicellular maps in graph-preserving bijection with cycle-signed C-decorated trees; removing signs gives the stated 2^(2g) multiplicity in Janson–Louf's formulation. This does not canonically supply metric geometry. Uniform clean dessins already give torsion-free finite-index triangle-group surfaces and geodesic graph edges. The matching Poisson intensity, hyperbolic disk isoperimetry, triangle-group systole formula, and transcendence results are established inputs, not candidate discoveries.

Independent screening deductions and falsifiable criteria:

* A polygon paired isometrically must pair equal-length sides. At a glued vertex, smoothness requires the sum of incident corner angles to be 2pi. Euler characteristic alone does not ensure this. For an equiangular polygon, every quotient vertex must have the same corner multiplicity; boundary corners need not be convex in a general cut disk.
* Genus g>=2 is the smooth closed hyperbolic regime. For a one-face cubic map, Euler and degree counting give V=4g−2, E=6g−3, so 2E=12g−6 boundary sides. Angles 2pi/3 would sum to a smooth vertex; Gauss–Bonnet gives area 4pi(g−1). These formulas should be proved universally rather than checked only at small genus.
* For a finite side pairing, the quotient must be a genuine connected oriented surface. A cut along a filling geodesic graph produces an intrinsic disk even if a plane development overlaps. Any perimeter obstruction must apply intrinsically with appropriate interior curvature hypotheses.
* Normalized WP measure is continuous. A finite or countable atomic construction is singular at fixed genus, but that does not prevent weak large-genus approximation. A uniform systole lower bound does exclude the WP short-geodesic law at a fixed shorter interval.
* In a (2,3,q) orientation-preserving triangle group with q>=7, Philippe's minimum hyperbolic translation length is 2 arcosh(2 cos²(pi/q)−1/2). A torsion-free subgroup's geodesics are hyperbolic elements of that ambient group, so the ambient lower bound is inherited. One must distinguish the reflection group from the orientation-preserving group and not replace a genus-varying estimate by an unsupported uniform one.
* For algebraic nonzero a, exp(a) is transcendental; this can obstruct literal algebraic side lengths when algebraic cosh(l) is forced. LW linear independence concerns distinct algebraic exponents with algebraic coefficients, and may establish exact cancellations are absent after reducing identical exponents. Neither numerical closeness nor finite examples prove a universal irrationality/transcendence statement.
