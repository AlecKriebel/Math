## Precise prior mechanisms and current literature

1. The OWR reference [4] is Chapuy, *The structure of unicellular maps, and a connection between maps of positive genus and planar labelled trees*, PTRF147(2010),415–447, https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.0546 . Its abstract/introduction and scope were read. This is a bijection for a dominant subset in fixed genus, not the later exact all-map decorated-permutation theorem.
2. Chapuy–Féray–Fusy, *A simple model of trees for unicellular maps*, JCTA120(8)(2013),2064–2092, https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3252v5 , DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2013.08.003 . Definitions, trisection decomposition and Theorem 5 were read. With signed odd cycles, it gives 2^(n+1) copies of rooted genus-g n-edge maps in bijection with C-decorated trees, preserving underlying graphs. Signs, rootings and multiplicities must be retained correctly; arbitrary cyclic ordering of merged tree vertices is not the theorem.
3. Janson–Louf, *Unicellular maps vs hyperbolic surfaces in large genus: simple closed curves*, Ann. Probab.51(3)(2023),899–929, DOI https://doi.org/10.1214/22-aop1601 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11903v2 . Sections 1 and 2.4–2.5 were read. Its regime is n,g tending to infinity with g=o(n), scaling graph lengths by sqrt(12g/n). It uses the CFF correspondence and omits cycle signs for graph questions, giving a 2^(2g) multiplicity. Its Conjecture 1.5 explicitly says the core must be interpreted as a glued hyperbolic polygon, not just a metric graph; the intended geometric coupling remains conjectural.
4. Barazer–Giacchetto–Liu, *Length spectrum of large genus random metric maps*, Forum Math. Sigma13(2025),e70, DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2025.31 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10517 . The published paper was retrieved; its introduction, measure definitions, moduli-space correspondence and Section 6 sampling construction were read. It already describes the CFF/tree route to uniform trivalent one-face metric maps with Dirichlet edge lengths. This known sampler is not a new solution. For E=6g−3 edges and boundary length P, the edge lengths satisfy sum l_e=P/2, and 2l_e/P has Dirichlet(1,...,1) law. The source normalization is P=12g. The model also arises from independent Exp(1) edge lengths after conditioning on total perimeter.
5. The ribbon-graph/Riemann-surface homeomorphism through Jenkins–Strebel or Penner/Bowditch–Epstein is also established background, stated in the published paper's Section 2.3. A topological homeomorphism alone does not identify the Kontsevich/Lebesgue law with Weil–Petersson law or supply the source's large-genus geometric comparison. No new global proof of that correspondence is claimed.
