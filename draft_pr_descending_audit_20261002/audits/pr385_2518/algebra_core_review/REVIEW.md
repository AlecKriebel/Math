## Exact target, dependencies, and successful scope

The target is the finite-family existence question for every finite-rank nonabelian free pro-p group, with the whole group included, excluding every nontrivial subgroup contained and characteristic in each member. The current primary paper's Question 4 confirms the quantifiers and explicitly distinguishes the discrete-free analogue and the question involving finitely many individual automorphisms. [Barnea et al., Question 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04120v3#S12.SS2).

The credited external inputs actually used here are free pro-p bases, the explicit index-p Schreier basis, the Frattini generation criterion and neighborhood basis, and the natural surjection of automorphisms onto the p-adic general linear group. These appear in Sections 3.3–3.4, the proof of Proposition 7.14, and Notation 7.8 of the primary paper. They are classical dependencies, not new results established by the bounded scripts. [Barnea et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04120v3).

Strong completeness supplies openness of every finite-index subgroup of a topologically finitely generated profinite group; its lower-central closedness theorem supplies the optional agreement of algebraic and closed lower-central terms. The reviewed implications use the exact hypotheses of Theorems 1.1 and 1.4 and their consequences on printed pages 172 and 174. [Nikolov–Segal, published primary PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v165-n1-p05.pdf).

The strongest verified conclusion in this proof family is: for an index-p pair `{F,U}`, every common characteristic subgroup lies in `U'`, contains no nonidentity primitive power or complete finite lower-central term, and yields no finite-class nilpotent quotient. Its maximal common core has an exact descending construction and cofinality criterion. These necessary conditions do not exclude every possible deep subgroup.

## Turn 1: actual subgroup images, not their closures

Let `M <= Z_p^r` be an additive subgroup invariant under the full `GL_r(Z_p)`, with `r >= 2`. For any `v in M`, a transvection with arbitrary p-adic coefficient `c` gives `c v_j e_i in M` by subtraction. Permutation invariance moves that coordinate into every location. In particular every coordinate of every member can be extracted separately, and all p-adic multiples of each such coordinate are also extractable. If `I = {b : b e_1 in M}`, then `I` is an ideal and these two containments prove `M=I^r` as an equality of actual sets.

The minimum finite valuation among nonzero members of a nonzero ideal exists because it is a nonempty subset of the nonnegative integers. A member with that valuation is a unit times `p^a`, so multiplying by the inverse unit gives `p^a in I`. Conversely every member is divisible by `p^a`. Thus `I=p^a Z_p`; zero gives `M=0`. No limiting argument, density inference, or assumption that `M` is closed appears. Closedness is an output of the full invariance hypothesis.

For `K` characteristic in both `F` and `U`, surjectivity of the two automorphism actions makes its actual abelianization images invariant under the full general linear groups. If `M_U=p^b U_ab` is nonzero and `L=j(U_ab)`, then `M_F=j(M_U)=p^b L` as actual images. `L` is open: a finite p-group coset quotient gives an exponent bound, hence all sufficiently deep p-powers map into `U` and their abelianized coordinates contain `p^e F_ab`.

If `p^b L=p^c F_ab`, integrality forces `c>=b`; multiplication by `p^b` is injective in the torsion-free module, so division yields `L=p^(c-b) F_ab`. A nonscalar `L` therefore forces `M_U=0`. The index-p Schreier basis gives exactly `L=p Z_p e_1 + sum_(i>=2) Z_p e_i`, which is nonscalar for every rank `d>=2`. The basis contains `1+p(d-1)` elements; the target's rank formula is correct.

An open `K` cannot lie in `U'`: a subgroup containing an open subgroup has finite index, whereas `U_ab` is infinite. A primitive p-adic power has nonzero abelianized coordinate for any nonzero exponent, since that coordinate is in a torsion-free copy of `Z_p`. For primitive elements of `F`, a continuous basis automorphism sends the element to `x_2`, primitive in `U`. Characteristicity preserves that individual power; no p-adic power closure of nonclosed `K` is assumed.

Index-p subgroups correspond to hyperplanes of the Frattini quotient. Lifting the transitive general linear action and conjugating an arbitrary automorphism of the transported subgroup proves that characteristicity in `F` and one index-p subgroup implies characteristicity in every index-p subgroup. This proves redundancy of that entire orbit, not triviality of their common core.

Adversarial boundary checks: arbitrary additive subgroups invariant under only a finite integral action can be nonclosed, for example `Z^r <= Z_p^r` under coordinate permutations and sign changes. Even closed permutation-invariant subgroups need not be scalar: the coordinate-sum-zero-mod-p subgroup is a counterexample. A dense action restricted to integral matrices cannot replace the full p-adic action for arbitrary nonclosed subgroups. Rank at least two is sufficient for the proof as written; no rank-one counterexample is asserted. A scalar inclusion lattice does not meet the theorem's hypothesis.

## Turn 2: topology, finite core steps, and the limit

For any abstract automorphism of an ambient finite-rank free pro-p group, the preimage of an open subgroup has finite index. Strong completeness makes every such preimage open; the inverse has the same property. Hence it is a homeomorphism. Applying this to every open `U_i` is legitimate because Schreier's theorem makes it finitely generated. Taking the closure of any common characteristic `K` preserves both containment in the closed `U_i` and invariance under each automorphism. A nonzero subgroup and its closure are nonzero simultaneously. Thus the unrestricted and closed-subgroup versions of the original existence question agree. This is an equivalence of the question, not an assertion that every `K` is closed.

For a finitely generated profinite `G`, continuous coset actions of an index-m open subgroup take values in `Sym(m)` and are determined by finitely many generator images. There are finitely many such actions and hence finitely many subgroups of that index. The automorphism orbit of open `V` is finite, so `c_G(V)` is an open characteristic subgroup. Any characteristic subgroup contained in `V` lies in every image of `V`, even if it is nonclosed. Therefore this operator is genuinely maximal among all characteristic subgroups contained in `V`.

In the cyclic descent through a finite family, every new term remains in the original intersection, so all subsequent operators are defined. Each term is open in every ambient member. For a fixed member `U_i`, the terms immediately after its operator form a cofinal subsequence of the decreasing chain and are all characteristic in that member. Their intersection equals the full-chain intersection `C`, and intersections of invariant subgroups are invariant. Every possible common `K` is contained in the chain by induction using operator maximality. This gives the closed maximal common core without finite stabilization, uniform bounds, or retention of every earlier invariance at intermediate steps.

The finite-step computation is sound for the specific iterated-Frattini quotient. The coset action's finite image is a p-group `Q`; if `|Q|<=p^e`, successive nontrivial Frattini subgroups reduce its order by at least a factor p, so `Phi^e(Q)=1`. Epimorphic functoriality puts `Phi^e(G)` inside the coset kernel and hence inside `V`. This quotient is finite because open finite-rank subgroups have open Frattini subgroups.

For `e>=1`, `Phi(G/Phi^e(G))=Phi(G)/Phi^e(G)`. Any automorphism of the quotient therefore induces an invertible action on `G/Phi(G)`. Lift the images of a free basis; their Frattini images form a basis, so the lifts topologically generate and, being a minimum-cardinality generating set, are a free pro-p basis. The universal property then supplies an automorphism of `G` lifting the quotient automorphism. `e=0` concerns only `V=G` and needs no lifting. Replacing the induced action by the full automorphism group of an arbitrary quotient would be unjustified, but the packet makes no such replacement.

The dense abstract free-group construction is an in-principle computation of each step: applying `A -> A^p[A,A]` to a finite-index free subgroup produces another finite-index free subgroup, with index read from its mod-p abelianization. Schreier bases give finite coset equality tests. Passing to pro-p completion preserves the relevant finite p-power-index coset tables. It is not a terminating test for the original infinite intersection, and the packet claims neither an implementation nor an efficiency bound.

For an index-p pair, `c_F(U)=Phi(F)`. The inclusion of `U` into the Frattini quotient of `F` has rank `d-1`; its kernel is the proper subspace `Phi(F)/Phi(U)` of `U/Phi(U)`. The full general linear action on the latter vector space has zero intersection of images of any proper subspace. Hence `c_U(Phi(F))=Phi(U)` exactly, including `p=2` and `d=2`. If a complete cycle made no decrease, nesting would force both operators to fix the term, producing an open common characteristic subgroup and contradicting Turn 1. This proves unbounded cycle indices.

Triviality of `C` is equivalent to `forall k exists t: V_t <= Phi^k(F)`. If `C=1` but a fixed containment never occurred, the compact closed sets `V_t` outside the open-and-closed `Phi^k(F)` would be nonempty and decreasing, yielding an element of `C` outside that neighborhood. The converse is the trivial intersection of the Frattini neighborhood basis. The exact quantifier order matters. Unbounded indices alone do not prove this condition: preimages of `p^t F_ab` retain the nontrivial derived subgroup at their intersection. Its nontriviality is witnessed by a commutator mapped to a finite Heisenberg p-group, including the order-eight nonabelian example at `p=2`.

A finite family from a characteristic chain has a nontrivial smallest member characteristic in every larger member by transitivity, so that particular family cannot work. This excludes a family construction; it does not rule out the nonnested pair or settle the original question.

## Turn 3: words, saturation, and every prime

The index-p kernel `U` is normal. In `U_ab`, conjugation by `t=x_1` sends the Schreier basis element `y_j` to `y_(j+1)` for `j<p-1`. The last is sent to `t^p y t^-p`, with the same class as `y` because `t^p in U`. The remaining relevant coordinates are zero. Consequently the word recursion `w_(n+1)=t w_n t^-1 w_n^-1` gives exactly `(S-1)^(n-1)e_0`; structural induction puts each word in the corresponding algebraic lower-central term, irrespective of the commutator convention.

In the cyclic module, `T=S-1` has image the augmentation ideal `I`, which has rank `p-1`. This follows directly from the telescoping differences `e_j-e_0`, which generate all coefficient-sum-zero vectors. If a vector in `I` has all coefficients divisible by `p^a`, dividing them by `p^a` still leaves sum zero; hence `I` is saturated. The norm operator is zero on `I`, since it annihilates each difference.

Expanding the norm as `sum_(j=0)^(p-1) (1+T)^j` gives, on `I`, `T^(p-1)=pB` with `B=-(1+sum_(j=1)^(p-2) (binom(p,j+1)/p)T^j)`. Integrality uses primality, and modulo p the operator `T` is nilpotent because `(S-1)^p=S^p-1=0`. Thus `B mod p` is invertible. Its determinant is a p-adic unit, so `B` is invertible over `Z_p` and preserves primitive vectors in `I`. It commutes with `T`.

Write `k-1=a(p-1)+r`, `0<=r<p-1`. The equality `(T^(p-1))^a T^(r+1)e_0=p^a B^a T^(r+1)e_0` is valid on `I`, because `T^(r+1)e_0 in I`. The binomial polynomial for the latter vector has degree at most `p-1` and leading coefficient one, with no reduction across `X^p-1`; it is primitive in the ambient lattice and hence in the saturated `I`. Applying `B^a` preserves primitivity. Therefore the minimum coefficient valuation is exactly `a`, proving both nonvanishing at every finite depth and the valuation formula for all primes.

For `p=2`, `B=-1` and `T` acts as multiplication by `-2` on the rank-one augmentation ideal, so the exact value is `k-1`. The empty sum causes no exceptional gap. The first invisible word modulo `p^e` has `k=e(p-1)+1`, equivalently `n=e(p-1)+2`; the immediately preceding word is still visible. Divisibility at a fixed modulus does not imply integral zero.

Since `K <= U'`, it cannot contain any word with this nonzero image. Thus it cannot contain an entire finite lower-central term. If the abstract quotient by nonclosed normal `K` had class c, it would contain the algebraic `(c+1)`st term and hence the explicit forbidden word; the same exclusion therefore holds without assuming `K` closed. The closed-term convention has no effect on this argument. The other three standard series contain explicit primitive powers: `x^(p^a) in Phi^a(F)`, `x^(p^(n-1)) in P_n`, and `x^(p^j) in D_n` whenever `p^j>=n`. They are consequently excluded by Turn 1. No conclusion concerning every possible derived-series term or all nontrivial characteristic subgroups follows.
