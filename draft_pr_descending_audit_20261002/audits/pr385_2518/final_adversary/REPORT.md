### Turn 1: arbitrary actual images and nonscalar inclusion

For an additive subgroup invariant under the full `GL_r(Z_p)`, arbitrary-coefficient transvection differences place each coordinate's arbitrary scalar multiples in another coordinate; permutation moves them into every coordinate. Thus the coordinate set is a `Z_p` ideal and the actual subgroup equals its r-fold product. A nonzero ideal has a minimum finite valuation, contains the corresponding pure power by multiplying a unit inverse, and therefore equals `p^a Z_p`. No closure or density inference is used. The candidate retains the sufficient hypothesis r>=2.

Characteristicity of actual K makes its two abelianization images invariant under the full actions. Nonzero scalar image in U gives `j(M_U)=p^b L`; scalar image in F would make `p^b L=p^c Z_p^d`. Integrality gives c>=b, and torsion-free cancellation makes L scalar, contradicting the hypothesis. This establishes K inside the closed derived subgroup of U without assuming K closed. The explicit index-p basis gives rank `1+p(d-1)` and inclusion lattice `p Z_p e_1 + sum_{i>=2} Z_p e_i`, genuinely nonscalar for every d>=2.

The primitive-power exclusion uses each individual nonzero p-adic power and its nonzero torsion-free abelian coordinate. It never assumes that nonclosed K is closed under p-adic powering. Transport through an ambient basis automorphism handles primitives of F. Orbit transport proves that adding more index-p subgroups imposes no additional condition once K is characteristic in F; it does not prove the pair's core trivial. An action by only a finite or integral subgroup cannot replace the full p-adic action for arbitrary possibly nonclosed K. Those are material hypothesis boundaries.

Closure consequently preserves common characteristicity and containment in closed U_i, while nontrivial K and its closure are nontrivial together.
