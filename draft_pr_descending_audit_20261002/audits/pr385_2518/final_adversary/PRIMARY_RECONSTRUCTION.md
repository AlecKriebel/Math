# Independent primary-source reconstruction

Completed before reading package proofs or any prior review/root verdict.

The October 2026 Kourovka PDF, printed/PDF p.177, Problem 21.9, and Barnea et al. arXiv:2507.04120v3 (22 September 2026), printed/PDF p.53, Question 4, state the same target. For every prime p and every finite rank d>=2 free pro-p group F, seek a finite family of open subgroups containing F itself whose common contained characteristic subgroup is necessarily trivial. No closedness qualifier is printed for the subgroup. Under continuous Aut(U), abstract possibly nonclosed subgroup invariance is the operational scope. Strong completeness is required if one equates abstract and continuous automorphisms; the text on commensurators itself requires continuous virtual isomorphisms. The finite family, whole Aut(U), all primes, d>=2, and inclusion of F must not be weakened. Positive finite quotient controls cannot settle this claim.

Source deductions used as adversarial targets: an additive subgroup invariant under all GL_d(Z_p) should force a scalar ideal without taking its closure, using shears to isolate coordinates and units to obtain all scalars. Frattini-surjective possibly nonclosed characteristic N in free pro-p F need not be identified with F merely by dense generation; automorphisms replacing one free basis element by every element of its procyclic group supply the missing abstract containment. A cyclic-kernel characteristic core must use cofinal depths of the descending candidate kernels: characteristic subgroup at a cofinal selected depth is enough, not an equality of depth with arbitrary index. Valuations must include p=2. All-open separation may follow from Hall applied to the closed cyclic subgroup generated topologically by a chosen nontrivial element, rather than to the possibly nonclosed N itself. It cannot yield a fixed finite separating family without a new argument. A central subgroup of the Heisenberg group gives an all-open comparison, but the Heisenberg group is not free pro-p and hence is not a counterexample.

Source warning independently observed: v3 Lemma 9.7 printed p.39 says a basis Y of H contains y in [H,H], which is impossible because basis elements survive H/Φ(H). Its intended construction from Lemma 3.6(b) uses a primitive element of H lying in [F,F]. A package using the latter needs its own correct construction; the printed typo alone is not a refutation of that package.

Remaining central gap: no finitely many open subgroups have been proved to simultaneously exclude every nontrivial possibly nonclosed common characteristic subgroup. Status remains unsolved.
