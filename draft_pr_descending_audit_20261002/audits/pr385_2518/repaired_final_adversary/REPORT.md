## Exact original target and topology

The ambient F is a nonabelian free pro-p group of finite rank d at least two. One must choose finitely many open subgroups U_i including F so that every subgroup K contained in all U_i and characteristic in each is trivial. An affirmative solution needs the finite family plus exclusion of every possible K. A negative solution needs a nontrivial common characteristic subgroup for every finite family. The source does not restrict K to closed subgroups.

Strong completeness applies to F and all its open subgroups, since each is topologically finitely generated. Any abstract automorphism pulls an open subgroup back to one of finite index, which is open; the inverse does likewise. All these automorphisms are therefore homeomorphisms. For common characteristic K, closure(K) is still characteristic in every U_i and remains contained in them, since open subgroups are closed. K and its closure are trivial simultaneously. This proves equivalence of the arbitrary-K and closed-K versions without asserting that K is closed. It also reconciles abstract and continuous characteristicity. Including F makes K normal in F.

## Turn 1: full p-adic invariance and actual images

For an arbitrary additive subgroup M of Z_p^r, r at least two, invariance under every p-adic elementary transvection gives `c*v_j*e_i` in M by subtracting v from its transformed vector. Here c is any p-adic integer, not merely an integer or a residue at fixed precision. Permutations transport this coordinate multiple to every coordinate. Thus every coordinate of every member is individually extractable. The set I of first-coordinate values of isolated vectors is an ideal, and M=I^r as an equality of actual sets. A nonzero ideal has a minimum finite valuation a, includes p^a after division by a unit, and equals p^a Z_p. Closedness follows from invariance; it is not assumed.

Characteristicity of K in U and F, together with the two full general-linear surjections, makes both actual abelianization images scalar or zero. With inclusion map j and L=j(U_ab), a nonzero image in U gives `j(p^b U_ab)=p^b L`. L has full rank because U contains sufficiently deep powers of all basis elements. If the F-image is `p^c F_ab`, integrality forces c at least b, and injectivity of p-power multiplication gives `L=p^(c-b) F_ab`. Therefore a nonscalar L forces the U-image to vanish. This proves containment in the closed derived subgroup of U even for nonclosed K.

For index p, the displayed Schreier basis has one p-scaled coordinate and p repetitions of each remaining coordinate. Hence `L=p Z_p e_1 + Z_p e_2 + ... + Z_p e_d`, genuinely nonscalar for d at least two. No open K can lie in U', since U_ab is infinite. A nonidentity p-adic power of a primitive U-element has nonzero abelianized coordinate. For a primitive F-element, characteristicity transports its individual power to a power of x_2, primitive in U; no power-closure assumption on K is involved.

Automorphisms of F act transitively on index-p subgroups by their full action on Frattini hyperplanes. Transporting an arbitrary automorphism of one subgroup through an F-automorphism shows that K characteristic in F and one index-p U is characteristic in every index-p subgroup. This makes more members from that orbit redundant, without proving the common core trivial.

Adversarial boundaries: invariance under finitely many integral matrices cannot replace full p-adic invariance for nonclosed subgroups; a dense integral subgroup can preserve dense nonclosed additive subgroups. Scalar L does not meet the obstruction's hypothesis. The proof's rank-two coordinate-extraction hypothesis is retained. ## Turn 2: open descent, cofinal subsequences and exact lifting

For an open V in finitely generated G, the automorphism orbit is finite: fixed-index subgroups are determined by finitely many continuous coset actions, themselves determined by finitely many generator images. Therefore c_G(V), the intersection of all images, is open and characteristic. Every characteristic K inside V lies in this core, even if K is not closed.

In cyclic iteration through the finite family, every term stays inside the original intersection and is open. For fixed U_i, the terms immediately after its operator are characteristic in U_i and form a cofinal subsequence of the nested chain. Their intersection equals the full-chain intersection C. This supplies simultaneous invariance at the limit; invariance after a single cycle is not assumed. The largest-common-subgroup statement follows by induction using each core's maximality and includes arbitrary K.

The finite-step model uses a specific iterated-Frattini quotient of a free pro-p group. If Q is the finite p-group coset image and p^e is at least its order, every nontrivial Frattini step reduces order by at least p; hence Phi^e(Q)=1. Epimorphic functoriality places Phi^e(G) inside the coset kernel and V. Its quotient is finite. For e at least one, its Frattini quotient is G/Phi(G). An automorphism of G/Phi^e(G) gives invertible images of a free basis modulo Phi(G). Any lifts topologically generate G and form a minimum-cardinality generating set, hence a free pro-p basis. The free universal property gives the desired lifting automorphism. The e=0 case is only V=G. This argument never licenses all automorphisms of an arbitrary quotient.

## Turn 4: Hall hypotheses and normal containment

Hall's theorem is applied to the closed topologically finitely generated infinite-index container H, not to the hypothetical subgroup N. It gives an open V=H*B. B is nontrivial because otherwise H is open; it is finite-rank and has a quotient C_p. If nonidentity a in N is conjugated by b in B, normality puts bab^-1 in H. Retraction to H kills b and fixes every element of H, forcing bab^-1=a as actual elements.

A finite p-group image P of H separates a from the identity. The universal property of the free pro-p product maps H to coordinate-zero lamps of P wr C_p and B to the cyclic shift. The two supposedly equal elements then occupy different lamp coordinates and are unequal. This avoids an unsupported abstract normal form and works for every prime. Neither N's closedness nor its finite generation is required. Open H, nonnormal N, and nonclosed H are outside the stated obstruction and are not silently included.

For the source pair, closure(K) remains normal and lies in U', hence is nonopen. A nontrivial survivor must therefore have infinitely generated closure and cannot be confined to any closed finitely generated infinite-index container. Finite abstract generation would make its closure topologically finitely generated and is excluded. Finite normal generation is different: the finitely many basis commutators normally generate F' after closure, although F' is nontrivial and infinite-index. The finite Heisenberg witness proves its nontriviality. F' is explicitly a failed shortcut example, not a common survivor in U'. All hypotheses are retained in the proof and summaries.
