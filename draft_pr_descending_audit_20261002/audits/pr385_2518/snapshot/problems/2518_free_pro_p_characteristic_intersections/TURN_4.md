# Turn 4: no survivor can be confined to a finite-rank infinite-index subgroup

Completed 2026-10-02 UTC. Original unresolved, 4/5 substantive turns; subjective progress 20%. This direction tests whether a finite-rank description of a hypothetical surviving subgroup could be sufficient. It yields a strong obstruction, but does not replace infinite topological generation by finite normal generation.

## 1. Credited free-factor input

Use the pro-p Hall theorem as stated in Barnea et al., arXiv:2507.04120v3, Theorem3.11 on printed18: a topologically finitely generated closed subgroup H of a finite-rank free pro-p group F is a free pro-p factor of some open subgroup V of F. The source credits Lubotzky1982, Theorem3.2, and Ribes–Zalesskii, Theorem9.1.19. This deep free-factor input is credited, not reproved. The following consequence is a standard normal-subgroup phenomenon; no historical novelty is claimed.

## 2. Normal containment obstruction, with an explicit separating quotient

**Theorem.** Let H<=F be closed, topologically finitely generated, and of infinite index. No nontrivial subgroup N normal in F can be contained in H. N need not itself be closed or finitely generated.

By the credited theorem, choose an open V with a free pro-p decomposition

    V=H * B.

Here B is nontrivial, since otherwise H=V would be open. The free-factor retraction r:V->H kills B and fixes H. Suppose 1!=a belongs to N<=H. Choose b in B and a continuous epimorphism B->C_p sending b to a generator; such a choice exists since B is a nontrivial finite-rank free pro-p group.

Normality in F, hence in V, gives b a b^{-1} in N<=H. Applying r forces b a b^{-1}=a, because r is the identity on H and sends that conjugate to a. We now contradict this equality using only a finite p-group, rather than invoking an unproved abstract free-product normal form.

As H is pro-p and a!=1, it has a finite p-group quotient P in which the image a_bar of a is nonidentity. Form the finite regular wreath product

    W=P wr C_p = P^p semidirect C_p.

Map H to the lamp at coordinate0 using H->P, and map B onto the cyclic shift using the chosen B->C_p. The universal property of the free pro-p product gives a continuous homomorphism V->W. The image of a is a nonidentity lamp at0, whereas the image of b a b^{-1} is the same nonidentity lamp at a different coordinate. They are unequal. This contradiction proves the theorem. QED.

The same proof also establishes the familiar corollary: every nontrivial closed topologically finitely generated normal subgroup of F is open. Apply the theorem with H=N if N were of infinite index.

## 3. Consequences for the source family {F,U}

Take U of index p and K common characteristic in F and U. By Turn1, K<=U', with the closed pro-p derived subgroup understood. Turn2 shows that N=closure(K) is again common characteristic. Thus N is normal in F and cannot be open, because it is contained in the infinite-index group U'.

If K is nontrivial, Section2 implies all of the following:

- N is not topologically finitely generated.
- K is not even contained in a topologically finitely generated closed subgroup of infinite index in F.
- In particular K cannot lie inside a proper free factor of F, nor inside any other specified finite-rank closed infinite-index subgroup.
- K cannot be finitely generated as an abstract group: the closure of finitely many abstract generators would topologically generate N, contradicting the first conclusion.

These constraints apply to a possible survivor for the index-p pair; they do not assert that every common characteristic subgroup is trivial. Infinite-rank closed normal subgroups are abundant in free pro-p groups.

## 4. Finite normal generation is not the missing hypothesis

The closed derived subgroup F' is the closed normal closure of the finitely many commutators [x_i,x_j] of a free basis. Indeed the quotient by that normal closure is topologically generated by commuting basis images, so the universal property of free pro-p abelianization identifies it with Z_p^d. Conversely every displayed commutator lies in F', giving equality of the kernels.

Yet F' is nontrivial and of infinite index. Section2 therefore shows that it is not topologically finitely generated. Nontriviality can already be witnessed by mapping x_1,x_2 to the standard noncommuting generators of a finite Heisenberg p-group.

This example is **not** a common characteristic survivor for {F,U}; Turn3 explicitly shows that F' is not contained in U'. Its role is to disprove the proposed shortcut from finite normal generation to ordinary finite topological generation. In particular, even if a hypothetical quotient F/K had a finite pro-p presentation, that would only make K finitely normally generated; it would not activate Section2 with H=K.
