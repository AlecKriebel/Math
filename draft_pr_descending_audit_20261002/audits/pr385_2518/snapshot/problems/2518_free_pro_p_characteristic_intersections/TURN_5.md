## 1. Complete classification for free abelian pro-p groups

Let A=Z_p^d, d>=2, and let a finite family of open subgroups U_i include A. There is a nontrivial subgroup characteristic in and contained in every U_i **if and only if** every U_i is a scalar lattice p^{k_i}A.

Indeed, by the invariant-subgroup lemma of Turn1, a nonzero K characteristic in A is p^a A, even if K was not assumed closed. As each open U_i is a free Z_p-module of rank d, characteristicity there similarly gives K=p^{b_i}U_i. Comparing shows U_i=p^{a-b_i}A, with a-b_i>=0 because U_i<=A. Conversely if all U_i are scalar lattices, p^{max_i k_i}A is nontrivial, contained in each, and a power subgroup of each, hence characteristic there.

Consequently for d>=2 the pair

    A,  pZ_p direct-sum Z_p^{d-1}

has trivial common characteristic subgroup. For rank1 every open subgroup is scalar and no finite family has the property. These are exact abelian comparisons. They do not settle the nonabelian free-pro-p question: Turn1 applies the same lattice idea only to an abelianized image of K and leaves the actual commutator-layer subgroup.

## 2. A nonabelian p-adic comparison where no finite family works

For any prime p, let G be the p-adic Heisenberg group on Z_p^3 with multiplication

    (a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a', b+b', c+c'+ab').

Its topology is the product p-adic topology. It is a pro-p group, as the inverse limit of the corresponding finite groups modulo p^e. It is topologically generated by x=(1,0,0) and y=(0,1,0): their commutator z=(0,0,1) supplies the central coordinate, and p-adic powers of x,y,z supply every triple. Inverses and commutators are

    (a,b,c)^{-1}=(-a,-b,-c+ab),
    [(a,b,c),(a',b',c')]=(0,0,ab'-a'b).

It is nonabelian of nilpotency class2, hence is not a nonabelian free pro-p group.

**Theorem.** Every finite family of open subgroups U_i of G has a nontrivial subgroup contained in and characteristic in every U_i.

The center of G is Z={(0,0,c)} isomorphic to Z_p. For any open U<=G, choose e with the coordinate congruence subgroup p^e Z_p^3 contained in U. If (a,b,c) is central in U, it commutes with (p^e,0,0) and (0,p^e,0). The commutator formula gives p^e b=p^e a=0 in Z_p, forcing a=b=0. Conversely every element of U intersect Z is central in U. Thus

    Z(U)=U intersect Z=p^{k(U)} Z

for some finite integer k(U)>=0. The final equality holds since the intersection is an open subgroup of the procyclic group Z.

Given the finite family, let k=max_i k(U_i) and set N=p^k Z. For every i,

    N=(Z(U_i))^{p^{k-k(U_i)}}.

Centers are characteristic and power subgroups of abelian centers are characteristic, so N is characteristic in each U_i. It is contained in them all and is nontrivial. QED. This proof works for p=2 as well as odd p, and for arbitrary open subgroups, not just a chosen coordinate family.

## 3. A finite quotient of the same group has the opposite answer

Reduce G modulo p, obtaining the finite Heisenberg group G_bar on F_p^3. Let

    U_bar={(0,b,c):b,c in F_p}.

It is an elementary abelian group of order p², so its only characteristic subgroups are1 and U_bar. The map

    theta(a,b,c)=(b,a,ab-c)

is an involutory automorphism of G_bar. Direct substitution in the multiplication formula verifies that it is a homomorphism; applying it twice gives the identity. It moves U_bar to the distinct subgroup with b=0. Therefore U_bar is not characteristic in G_bar, and the pair {G_bar,U_bar} has trivial common characteristic subgroup.

This is valid for **every** prime, including p=2, where G_bar has order8. We do not claim that every maximal subgroup in that p=2 group is elementary abelian; only the displayed U_bar is used.

Upstairs, the full preimage U of U_bar is the open subgroup with a in pZ_p. Section2 gives Z(U)=Z(G)=Z and provides a nontrivial common characteristic subgroup for {G,U}. Thus even a finite quotient which exactly satisfies the analogous characteristic-family property does not supply that property in the original pro-p group.

## 4. The precise failed lifting mechanism

In U_bar the central line {(0,0,c)} is not characteristic: the automorphism of the elementary abelian U_bar swapping its b and c coordinates moves that line. This automorphism cannot be induced by any automorphism of the p-adic U. Such an automorphism would preserve Z(U)=Z and hence its image in U_bar whenever it descends to that quotient. The obstruction is the extra automorphisms of the finite quotient which forget the original center.

This is why one may not compute with all automorphisms of an arbitrary finite quotient and silently lift the answer. Turn2 used all automorphisms only for the specifically proved Frattini quotients of a **free** pro-p group, where the basis criterion guarantees a lift. The Heisenberg quotient illustrates the failure when that hypothesis is absent; it is not used as evidence for a yes or no answer for free pro-p groups.
