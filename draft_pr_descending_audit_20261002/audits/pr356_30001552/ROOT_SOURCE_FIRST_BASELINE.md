# Source-first reconstruction: PR356 / OWR-4425-007

The original source is Dirk Nowotka, joint work with Bastian Bischoff, “Word periods under involution,” in Mini-Workshop: Combinatorics on Words, Oberwolfach Report37/2010, printed2219–2222 / PDF25–28. Root read the whole section as extracted from the official PDF and inspected the actual conjecture page, printed2220 / PDF26, in pixels. The report discusses several distinct period notions.

Fix an alphabet Sigma and an antimorphic involution theta of its free monoid: theta(xy)=theta(y)theta(x) and theta^2=id. Such a map reverses order and applies an involution to letters; fixed letters are allowed. A positive p is an alternating theta-period of a finite word w when w is a prefix of (u theta(u))^omega for a word u of length p. General theta-periods permit an arbitrary sequence of u and theta(u); weak theta-periods use individual letter equalities. Neither is an acceptable replacement for the selected target.

The exact source conjecture is: if p and q are alternating theta-periods of w, d=gcd(p,q), and |w|>=p+q-d, then d is also an alternating theta-period of w. The source's Theorem23 gives p+q as an existing sufficient bound. The general antimorphic Theorem21 and morphic Theorem22 concern distinct hypotheses and conclusions. The separate later conjecture about unbordered factors is not part of this target.

Success requires a checkable proof for every alphabet, antimorphic involution, positive p,q and finite prefix length satisfying the exact bound. At this bound |w|>=max(p,q), so the seeds are the actual prefixes of w of lengths p and q. Longer words, not only the threshold prefix, must be covered. Check equal periods, divisibility, large gcd, p or q=1, identity-on-letters reversal, involutions without fixed letters, and mixed fixed and swapped letters. Empty w cannot meet the positive-period length premise. A claim of sharpness requires an explicit family and a precise uniform-versus-fixed-pair distinction; p=q and divisibility are degenerate for a blanket pairwise optimality assertion.

Independent challenge mechanisms: signed constraint graphs/reflection composition; word overlap and period subtraction with exact prefix orientation; and semantic/finite universal-constraint counterexamples with representative letter involutions.
