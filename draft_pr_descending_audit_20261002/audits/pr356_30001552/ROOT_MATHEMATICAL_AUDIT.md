## Universal proof check

An involutive antimorphism of a free monoid fixes the empty word and preserves atoms, hence is an involutive permutation sigma of letters followed by reversal. This allows any alphabet and both fixed letters and transposed pairs. Let w have length L and alternating p-period, witnessed by a prefix of the repeated block u theta(u), |u|=p. Extend that block periodically to all integer positions. Its letters s_i satisfy s_{−1−i}=sigma(s_i). On the interval[−L,L−1] this extension is precisely the finite word W=theta(w)w, independently of the ending phase of w. Thus W has ordinary period2p. The independent q-witness gives the same finite W and ordinary period2q; equality of unseen positive tails is never assumed.

Write d=gcd(p,q). The hypothesis gives2L≥2p+2q−2d, the classical finite Fine–Wilf threshold, so W has ordinary period2d. Since L≥max(p,q)≥d, all central positions[−d,d−1] are present. Put v=w[0:d]. For an observed right-half position i, reduce its residue modulo2d to[−d,d−1]. Residues in[0,d−1] give the appropriate letter of v; residues in[−d,−1] give its sigma-reflected letter. Period comparisons stay inside the finite interval. Therefore w is a prefix of(v theta(v)) repeated. This proves the full alternating conclusion, with no finiteness, fixed-point-free, primitive, minimal-period, nondivisibility or completed-block assumption.

The equality/divisibility cases are included. Empty words and witnesses longer than L cannot satisfy the positive threshold. The reflected concatenation is theta(w)w with the specified phase; using an arbitrary reflected concatenation would need a separate argument. Morphic involutions and freely chosen mixtures of u and theta(u) are distinct models and are not covered by this proof.
