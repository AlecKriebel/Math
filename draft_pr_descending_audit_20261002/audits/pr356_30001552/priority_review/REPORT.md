# Bounded priority audit: PR356 / problem30001552 / OWR-4425-007

Audit date: 3 October 2026 PDT / 4 October 2026 UTC. Status: reviewable dossier, awaiting root authorization for its single closure seal.

No earlier exact theorem was found in the primary texts inspected here. This supports a qualified presentation as a short deduction proving the bound stated in the original conjecture; it does not certify global novelty, first discovery, or the current absence of unpublished knowledge. Mathematical correctness is reviewed separately.

The source-first baseline was fixed before candidate/prior-review exposure. The complete [Nowotka contribution, joint with Bischoff](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46296), printed 2219–2222, was read. The target is the unnumbered conjecture after Theorem 23 on printed 2220: if an arbitrary antimorphic involution theta gives alternating periods p,q and |w| >= p+q−g, g=gcd(p,q), then g is an alternating period. Alternating means a prefix of (u theta(u))^omega, not arbitrary block choices. The source already proves the longer p+q bound. Fixed letters, reversal, and p=q are included; this is not numbered Conjecture 27.

Candidate 16 was released explicitly after gate validation. Its comparison mechanism extends the whole finite prefix to theta(w)w, obtains ordinary periods 2p and 2q on this same word, and applies classical Fine–Wilf at length 2|w|. The additional reflection recovers alternating period g. Neither ordinary Fine–Wilf nor reflection-induced periodicity is a new ingredient.

For equivalent-form searches, an independently checked audit lemma untwists theta=sigma∘reversal by b_i=sigma^i(w_i), preserving word length and **every** alternating period. Thus an earlier exact reversal-only theorem would already cover this target. A second aligned two-letter orbit encoding also works, doubling lengths and periods. Neither operation makes the inspected Simpson theorem meet the short threshold. These are comparison deductions of this audit, not prior theorems or claims of new priority.

The candidate example w=abb, reversal, p=2,q=3 at length3 shows that the displayed formula cannot be lowered by1 uniformly. It does **not** prove optimality for every pair(p,q), every alphabet, or every involution. Keep that qualification wherever sharpness is stated.
