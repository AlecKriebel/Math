## Definitions, precisely separated

* A is an alphabet; A* is the free monoid of all finite words. No alphabet-cardinality bound or finiteness assumption is stated for the period theorems or either conjecture.
* theta is an involution on A*: theta(theta(w))=w. It is morphic when theta(uv)=theta(u)theta(v), and antimorphic when theta(uv)=theta(v)theta(u). The antimorphic DNA example reverses order and swaps A/T and C/G. For a morphic or antimorphic involution of a free monoid, the action is induced by an involutive permutation of letters, possibly with fixed letters; the order of letters is additionally reversed in the antimorphic case. This latter structural fact is a deduction, rather than a separately stated source hypothesis.
* A word w is primitive if w=u^i forces i=1. Its primitive root is the shortest u with w=u^i for some i>=1. It is theta-primitive if w in {u,theta(u)}^i forces i=1, and the theta-primitive root is the shortest such u. The source gives no explicit empty-word qualification here.
* If w is a prefix of u^omega, |u| is an ordinary global period. Pi(w) is the set; pi(w) its minimum. If w is a prefix of {u,theta(u)}^omega, |u| is a theta-period (arbitrary choices of the two blocks); Pi_theta and pi_theta denote its set and minimum.
* If w is a prefix of (u theta(u))^omega, |u| is an **alternating theta-period**, with the initial phase u first. Pi_theta^alt and pi_theta^alt denote its set and minimum. Thus the blocks have length p=|u|; the infinite template has length 2p. A final block of w may be shorter than p, and its content is fixed as the appropriate prefix. The definition does not require |w| divisible by p or 2p. Changing phase is encoded by choosing theta(u) as initial block, rather than adding a separate initial-phase hypothesis.
* A natural p is a weak theta-period if w[i]=w[i+p] or w[i]=theta(w[i+p]) for 1<=i<=|w|-p. The suitable substitution chooses one representative per involutive letter orbit and maps each letter to its chosen representative. Theorem 19 identifies the weak-period set with ordinary periods after this substitution. The contribution thereafter explicitly considers only (alternating) theta-periods; weak periods must not be substituted into the conjecture.
* u~p v means either is a prefix of the other; u~s v means either is a suffix of the other. At factorization w=uv, x is a repetition word when u~s x and v~p x; its length is a local period, with minimum pi(u,v). It is a theta-repetition word when u~s x and v~p theta(x); minimum pi_theta(u,v). These local notions are different from the global alternating periods.
* A bordered word has a proper prefix that is also a suffix. tau(w) is the maximum length of unbordered factors. A theta-bordered word has a proper prefix u such that theta(u) is a suffix; otherwise it is theta-unbordered. The use of tau_theta(w) immediately thereafter makes its intended meaning the maximum length of theta-unbordered factors, although the source does not add an explicit standalone definition of that notation.

## Relevant source conjecture A: unnumbered alternating antimorphic overlap bound

On printed page 2220, Theorem 23 says: for an antimorphic involution theta on A* and w in A*, if p,q belong to Pi_theta^alt(w) and |w|>=p+q, then gcd(p,q) belongs to Pi_theta^alt(w). The immediately following paragraph conjectures the improved bound **|w|>=p+q-gcd(p,q)** for this same alternating antimorphic case. It says this conjecture is still open. Read with its explicit antecedent theorem, the exact target is:

For positive integers p,q, arbitrary alphabet A, antimorphic involution theta on A*, and finite word w with p,q in Pi_theta^alt(w), if n=|w|>=p+q-d where d=gcd(p,q), then d in Pi_theta^alt(w).

Positive periods and nonempty template u are the necessary conventional interpretation of the omega expression; the PDF never explicitly defines whether the natural numbers include zero. This must be declared, not treated as an explicit printed assumption. No p>q condition occurs in Theorem 23 or this conjecture; it occurs only in different Theorem 21. Neither fixed-point-free theta, binary alphabet, nor coprimality is required. The conclusion is alternating theta-period d, not merely weak period, arbitrary theta-period, or common primitive root. The conjecture is distinct from the morphic theorem and from Conjecture 27.

Boundary checks required for any proposed proof: p=q gives d=p and an existing period; if one period divides the other then d equals the smaller input period, again immediate. At equality n=p+q-d, both initial p/q blocks are available since n>=max(p,q). The word can end mid-block. The cases d=1, p/q odd/even after scaling by d, fixed letters, identity-on-letters with reversal, one-letter or empty alphabet, and theta-palindromic initial blocks must be tested. Under positive p,q the threshold is positive, so the empty word cannot satisfy the antecedent. An empty alphabet therefore supplies no nonempty witness. A bound that holds only for complete blocks or only beyond strict inequality would not establish the source claim.

Adjacent context is essential: Fine--Wilf Theorem 20 has the ordinary threshold p+q-d. Theorem 21 ([2], Czeizler/Kari/Seki) has arbitrary antimorphic theta-periods, p>q, n>=2p+q-d, and conclusion that length-p/q prefixes have the same theta-primitive root. Theorem 22 gives the morphic arbitrary theta-period threshold p+q-d and the morphic alternating threshold p+q. The contribution says its bounds to that point are tight, then distinguishes the unresolved tightness of Theorem 23. These other statements must not be interchanged with conjecture A.

## Relevant source conjecture B: numbered Conjecture 27

On printed page 2222: for a **morphic** involution theta on A* and w in A*, if |w|>=3 tau_theta(w), then tau_theta(w)=pi_theta^alt(w). No nonidentity or |A|>=3 hypothesis is attached to this conjecture; such restrictions appear only in earlier Propositions 25/26 about local periods. The conclusion concerns a minimum global alternating period and maximum theta-unbordered-factor length, not a gcd closure theorem.

The motivating family, for i>=2, is w_i=(ab)^i abb(ab)^i aab(ab)^i a, with theta(a)=b and theta(b)=a. The source reports |w_i|=6(i+1)+1, tau_theta(w_i)=2i+4, and pi_theta^alt(w_i)=4i+5. Its ratio tends to 3 while the quantities remain unequal; the source claims these show the ordinary 7/3 threshold cannot transfer and conjectures the example best possible. The finite family has |w_i|/tau_theta(w_i)=3-5/(2i+4)<3 and therefore does not itself contradict the conjectured >=3 implication. At i=2 the ordinary threshold is already met, as the source says.

Boundary ambiguity: the literal proper-prefix border definition would include the empty word; the conventional nonempty-border reading is necessary to avoid making every nonempty word bordered. The source leaves that convention implicit. It also does not resolve tau_theta(empty), minimum positive period of empty, and the equality antecedent for empty word. A careful statement should restrict to nonempty w or declare consistent conventions. Fixed letters and identity morphic theta are allowed by the printed quantifier. This conjecture must receive its own independent scope decision rather than being swept into conjecture A.

## Independent falsification priorities and scope of this baseline

Before seeing a candidate, the main falsifier families for conjecture A are phase incompatibility across p and q grids, reversal versus complement confusion, equality n=p+q-d, partial final blocks, orbit fixed points, and a gcd conclusion proved only after imposing a parity or alphabet restriction. For conjecture B, the theta-border convention and fixed-point/empty-word boundaries are distinct issues. Checkable universal reasoning must establish the original unrestricted quantified claim; a bounded search cannot do that.
