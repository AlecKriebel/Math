This verdict is deductive, not based on future bounded verifier outputs. Lemma 1 correctly derives a letter permutation from free-monoid antimorphism and involutivity: epsilon's image has twice its own length, hence is empty; bijectivity ensures no nonempty word has empty image; a length>=2 image of a letter would become a nontrivial factorization after applying theta. The involutive letter map therefore preserves lengths and gives reversal with sigma.

Here is my own coordinate check of the essential mechanism. For a p-template, put B=u theta(u), with coordinates 0,...,2p-1. At a residue r<p, reflected residue 2p-1-r lies in B's second half and has letter sigma(u_r). At r>=p, reflected residue lies in the first half and applying sigma twice restores u. Thus s[-1-i]=sigma(s[i]) on the entire two-sided periodic extension. Reflecting a finite prefix across the boundary -1/2 therefore produces precisely theta(w)w. The same construction works separately for q but produces the same finite doubled word W; the proof makes no assertion that the unseen infinite tails coincide. An arbitrary theta-block concatenation lacks this fixed boundary symmetry and is not covered by this deduction.

The length inequality L>=p+q-d implies L>=p,q,d, so both base words are the actual prefixes, the central restriction exists, and ordinary periods 2p and 2q are not being inferred from missing initial blocks. Fine--Wilf's threshold on W is exactly (2p)+(2q)-gcd(2p,2q)=2(p+q-d). Therefore 2d is an ordinary period, with no odd/even restriction on p/d or q/d. For each i>=0, the representative j in [0,d-1] or [-d,-1] has the same residue modulo 2d; the chain from j to i uses only positions in [-L,L-1], including equality boundary cases. The negative representative corresponds to sigma(v[2d-1-r]), giving the required *alternating* phase. Ordinary period 2d alone on w would not encode the phase; the known central theta(v)v block in W supplies it.

The uniform sharpness proposition is also valid: reversal and abb have alternating periods 2 and 3 but not 1, at one below threshold. The proposition correctly limits its claim to a single uniform obstruction, rather than pairwise sharpness for every p,q or fixed-point-free theta. p=q and one-period-divides-the-other are tautological source cases and remain included.
