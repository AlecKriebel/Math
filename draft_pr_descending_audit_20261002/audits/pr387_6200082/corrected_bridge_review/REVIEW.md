## 2. All metric-measure and convergence hypotheses

For any compact set `K` in a complete hyperbolic quotient, use the closed ambient thickening `M=N_10(K)`, ambient restricted distance and ambient restricted volume. It is compact and has finitely many path components whenever `K` does. It is not necessary to give `M` an intrinsic path metric or assert that its cut balls are convex.

If `x` is on `partial M`, then `dist(x,K)=10`. A nearest point of `K` is on `partial K`, since `x` is outside the closed set `K` and a minimizing geodesic first enters it at the boundary. Consequently

    N_R(partial M) subset N_(R+10)(partial K),
    M minus K subset N_10(partial K),
    vol(M)/vol(K) -> 1.

These include the external ambient portion of each boundary neighborhood. An intrinsic-neighborhood estimate would not suffice for the later packing argument; the frozen candidate correctly uses ambient neighborhoods. Metric covering radius on `M` is at least that on `K` minus 10, by enclosing every lifted ball centered on `M` in a larger lifted ball centered on `K`.

The volume diverges: fix `R`; once the radius-R boundary ratio is below one, there is a point of `K` at distance greater than `R` from its boundary. Its ambient R-ball is contained in `K`. Once the local covering radius exceeds `R`, this ball has volume `V_H4(R)`. Since `V_H4(R)` is unbounded as `R` increases, bounded volume would contradict the preceding statement for some fixed `R`.

The parameters `(r0,r1,r2,r3)=(1,2,4,5)` satisfy the exact theorem inequalities. For every `p in M`, let `q` be a nearest point of `K`. If `d(p,q)>=1/4`, move `1/4` toward `q` to a center `z`; `B_E(z,1/4)` lies in `M` and in `B_E(p,1/2)`. If `d(p,q)<1/4`, use `B_E(q,1/4)` instead. Since these are open balls, the displayed radius `1/4` already avoids endpoint problems. Eventual local thickness makes them embedded, so `v_min=V_H4(1/4)` works. Discarding a finite prefix is permissible and gives the lower bound for every remaining index.

At every radius `r>0`, a quotient r-ball is covered by an r-ball in the universal cover and has volume at most `V_H4(r)`; intersecting with `M` only decreases volume. Thus one common function `v_max(r)=V_H4(r)` supplies the stronger all-radius requirement. This estimate does not assume the quotient r-ball is embedded at large r.

## 3. Every weighted net and the ambient good cover

An allowed `(1,4)`-net is finite because its centers are separated in compact `M`. Arbitrary weights in `[4,5]` cover `M`, so their ambient open balls have union `U` containing a neighborhood of `M`. Eventual local covering radius makes every original ball globally strongly convex in `E`, and makes every nonempty finite intersection contractible. An injectivity lower bound merely greater than the radius would not by itself guarantee strong convexity; here divergence supplies as much margin as needed.

The complementary cover can be constructed explicitly. Put `F=E minus U`. It is closed, and `dist(F,M)>0` by compactness of `M`. Exhaust `F` by its intersections with compact distance annuli. For each annulus choose a finite family of balls centered on `F`, of radii at most `1/4`, at most half `dist(F,M)`, and less than the local strong convexity radius. These balls cover that annulus and lie in `E minus M`. Their union is locally finite in all of `E`: only boundedly many annuli can meet a fixed compact set. Add the finite original cover. Each ball has compact closure, so local finiteness also makes the full nerve locally finite. The full good cover models `E`, whose second homology is zero. No global lower injectivity radius is required on the complement.

Let `A` be the original nerve. Let `B` be the induced subcomplex on added vertices and on every original vertex adjacent to an added vertex. Every mixed simplex lies in `B`, because each original vertex of such a simplex meets an added ball. Hence the full nerve is `A union B`, and `A intersection B` is precisely the original induced subcomplex on these boundary-adjacent vertices. They have centers in `N_5(partial M)`: an added ball is disjoint from `M`, so an original radius-at-most-five ball meeting it leaves `M`, and a geodesic crosses the boundary.

The open ambient half-balls about these centers are pairwise disjoint and embedded. They lie in `N_6(partial M)`, giving

    #boundary vertices <= vol(N_6(partial M))/V_H4(1/2) = o(vol(M)).

Adjacent original centers are at distance less than ten. Packing their disjoint half-balls into `B_E(x,10.5)` gives a common degree bound, for example

    Delta = ceil(V_H4(10.5)/V_H4(1/2)).

Thus `#2-simplices(A intersection B)` is at most `#boundary vertices * binom(Delta,2)`. The relevant exact-sequence segment is

    H_2(A intersection B) -> H_2(A) directsum H_2(B) -> H_2(full nerve).

It gives `b_2(A) <= b_2(full nerve)+b_2(A intersection B)`, even if `B` has infinite-dimensional homology. In particular

    0 <= b_2(A) <= C vol(N_6(partial M)) = o(vol(M)).

The constants do not depend on the net or its weights. Therefore

    sup_allowed_nets |b_2(A)-1|/vol(M) <=
        C vol(N_6(partial M))/vol(M) + 1/vol(M) -> 0.

This proves precisely the quantified hypothesis of ABBG Theorem 2.9, including positivity of `B_i=1`. A convenient single chosen net would not have established it. The ambient construction avoids the false assertion that balls intersected with a reentrant boundary remain convex.

## 4. Replacement theorem proof audit

The finite-stage mechanism was checked directly in primary §§2.3–2.4. At stage `j`, the decision for a point compares with **all** earlier-marked Poisson points of that stage and with retained points of earlier stages. It has dependence radius bounded by `j*r1`; there is no unbounded recursion within the current stage through decisions to retain other current points. On fixed bounded neighborhoods this is a finite computation in the distances, marks and independent coins. Joint inclusion-event probabilities, not just their one-point marginals, are consequently continuous. This is the check that prevents reintroducing Bowen's problematic infinite-stage continuity assertion.

The completion error has a uniform decay estimate, and uniformly bounded degree controls the homology cost of the extra points. Several literal packing sentences in the v3 proof need routine constant bookkeeping, which I checked rather than adopting blindly:

Likewise, multiple successful centers `z` producing the same accepted point are controlled by the same packing bound.

These replacements still give a definite contraction whenever the uncovered proportion exceeds the fixed target. They require only the same hypotheses, so they do not transfer the central difficulty to a new claim. They are not a failure of the theorem or the candidate application.
