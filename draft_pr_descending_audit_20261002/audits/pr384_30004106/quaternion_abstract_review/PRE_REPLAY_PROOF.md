## Independent deduction: Q8

1. Hamilton-unit multiplication independently gives a group of eight elements with unique involution `z=-1=a^2`. The right-module differentials expanded by left multiplication have dimensions `8x16,16x16,16x8,8x8`, respectively. Independent pivot minors have determinants one over F2. The four compositions vanish and ranks are `7,9,7,1`. These zero identities and nonzero unit minors work over every characteristic-two field, including transcendental extensions: every such field contains F2 and extending scalars preserves exactness/rank. GF4 checks corroborate, but do not supply, that structural statement.

2. Rank plus zero-composition proves the infinite resolution exact at each of the four repeating positions. The augmentation kernel has dimension seven and is the first image. Entries lie in the augmentation ideal; the independent radical power dimensions `8,7,5,3,1,0` also confirm locality/minimality for this exact group algebra. No inference from finitely many degrees to an arbitrary resolution is required: the maps repeat literally.

3. Minimal syzygy dimensions are `1,7,9,7,1`. The last image is the norm line, with trivial group action. A projective kernel summand would be injective (self-injective algebra), would split from the covering projective, and would lie in its radical. Projection onto that summand would yield `Q=JQ`, impossible by Nakayama. Thus all chosen syzygies are projective-free.

4. Tensor `0->Omega(k)->kG->k->0` with any finite-dimensional module `M`. The middle module is projective, and the sequence remains exact, so Schanuel gives `Omega(k) tensor M = Omega(M)` stably. Tensoring a projective with `M` is projective via the regular-module tensor identity. Repeating and applying duality proves the stable syzygy tensor law and inverse. Therefore the stable element `u=[Omega(k)]` has order dividing four. Projective-free cancellation (Krull–Schmidt) and dimensions seven/nine exclude orders one/two; these are all proper divisors of four. Thus its exact order is four, including exclusion of period three. The four powers are distinct nonprojective coordinates over every characteristic-two coefficient field.

5. In the whole stable completion, four nonzero orthogonal Fourier idempotents `e_zeta=(1/4) sum_{j=0}^3 zeta^(-j)u^j` sum to its unit. They are nonzero by the independent coordinates. For `h=u+u^-1-2`, the values are `0,-2,-4,-2`. Each is spectrally persistent in any containing unital algebra: `(h-lambda)e_zeta=0` with a nonzero idempotent obstructs invertibility. For other lambda the finite Fourier formula constructs the inverse. This proves precisely `{0,-2,-4}` in the ambient stable completion without assuming extension of subalgebra characters.

6. For the full completion, `e=[kQ8]/8` has `e^2=e=e*`, and `ea=dim(a)e` by the regular tensor identity and continuity. The projective ideal is exactly Ce because this p-group algebra is local and its only indecomposable projective is free rank one. Consequently `A=Ce x (1-e)A`, and the stable quotient identifies with `(1-e)A`. The canonical representative of `h` has dimension twelve, so `J(h)=h-12e=[Omega k]+[Omega^3 k]-2[k]-(3/2)[kQ8]`. It has zero scalar factor and stable spectrum `{0,-2,-4}`. Its full spectrum is the union with `{0}`, hence exactly the same set. Its spectral radius is four. Duality exchanges syzygies one/three, so it is self-adjoint and has an entirely real spectrum.

7. A direct module control applies `z+1` to independent image bases. Its ranks on syzygies one/three are three, in dimension seven. Since `(z+1)^2=0`, Jordan theory over *every* characteristic-two field gives exactly `k + 3 kC2` in each case. The free regular module restricts as `4 kC2`. The signed element restricts to zero as a *full split-ring element*, and to zero at the trivial subgroup because its total dimension is zero. Those are all elementary abelian subgroups of Q8. Thus it falsifies every unrestricted signed-element inequality `rho_G(x) <= C max_E rho_E(Res x)` with finite C (also norm versions). It does not falsify positive-module detection, and is not a nonsymmetry example.

## Independent deduction: abstract family

Write `c=q-1`, `m=|H|`, `s=sum_H g`, `P=c+s`, and `d=c+m`. In the additive copy of ZH define `alpha o beta=P alpha beta` and unitize with a new separate basis unit. Associativity follows directly from ordinary associative multiplication (`P^2 alpha beta gamma`); commutativity uses abelian H. The multiplication coefficients are `c` at `gh` plus one everywhere, all nonnegative. Fixing all basis elements is an involution and its conjugate-linear extension is multiplicative since these constants are real.

- The new unit occurs only in its own square, with coefficient one. This checks (ii') fully.
- Since `P^2=c^2+(2c+m)s`, the multiplicity of `x_g` in `x_g^3` is `2c+m+c^2*[g^2=1]`. It is at least two (indeed at least five under H having an element of order >2 and q>=2). This is exactly (iii), not a trace or bilinear-form substitute.
- Setting all nonunit dimensions to d makes every product have total weighted dimension d squared. The weights are positive integers and star invariant. The norm is submultiplicative by positivity and multiplicativity of dimension; finite rank makes it complete and all linear functionals continuous.
- `rho=sum x_g` is positive nonzero and `x_g rho=d rho`. This checks the full axiom (v), which cannot be dropped.

For complex characters of H, the value of P is d for the trivial character and c for every other character. Both are nonzero when q>=2. Mapping the twisted ideal by `alpha -> P alpha` is an algebra isomorphism onto CH. The unitization consequently is `C x CH = C^(m+1)`; an explicit full transform is `(a,alpha) -> (a,(a+lambda_chi*chi(alpha))_chi)`. It is invertible by Fourier inversion and nonzero lambdas. Therefore exactly the claimed m+1 characters exist, the Jacobson radical is zero, and spectral values are their values. Their basis norms are bounded by dimension.

An element g of order >2 admits a character with nonreal value (restrict to its cyclic subgroup and extend that character in the finite abelian character group). At that character `x_g=x_g*` has nonreal value `c*chi(g)`. Its square has either negative or nonreal value at that character. Hence this completion is nonsymmetric. For C3,q=2 the exact multiplication operator characteristic polynomial is `t(t-4)(t^2+t+1)`; squaring gives the stated same pair of cube-root values. This persists without radical directions.

Every nonnegative real combination is self-adjoint, including its image in every representation-ideal quotient. Spectral mapping for squares gives the positive gamma duality identity for all those elements; it imposes no character conjugation constraint on arbitrary complex linear combinations. A useful extra falsifiable control is `y=(x_1-x_2)/sqrt(3)` in C3,q=2: y is self-adjoint, character values `0,0,i,-i`; for `a=1+i*y`, the values are `1,1,0,2`, so `rho(a)^2=4`, while `a*a=1+y^2` has values `1,1,0,0` and radius one. This directly violates the *all-element* spectral-radius identity while every positive-element identity holds.

Boundary controls: H=C2 and C2xC2 have only real character values and hence this fixed-basis-star algebra is symmetric. At q=1, c=0, the nontrivial Fourier directions collapse; `x_g-x_h` form square-zero radical directions and semisimplicity fails. These controls distinguish the order>2 and q>=2 hypotheses from mere finite rank. The generic proof above does not rely on the finite sample groups.

## Independent nonrealizability proof for every field

A realization here means a based split-Green-ring isomorphism preserving the whole indecomposable basis, its ordinary k-dimensions, and duality. An arbitrary abstract ring isomorphism is outside this assertion. The complete proposed indecomposable dimension list is one trivial module of dimension one plus m modules of dimension d>=4.

If k is projective, char(k) is zero or coprime to |G|, including G trivial. Maschke makes the category semisimple. Every nonzero simple M has a nonzero invariant identity in `M tensor M*`; the resulting inclusion of the trivial module splits by semisimplicity, over a nonsplitting field as well. This contradicts the zero unit coefficients. Algebraic closure is unnecessary.

Otherwise the projective cover P_k is indecomposable and differs from k, so its dimension must be d. Its kernel K has dimension d-1, nonzero. For a self-injective finite-dimensional algebra, projective presentations and injective copresentations define inverse functors on the stable category: lifts define the functors, maps through projectives vanish, and applying either inverse to `0->K->P_k->k->0` recovers k by Schanuel. This is a deduction from the read self-injectivity and Schanuel inputs. The stable endomorphism ring of k is k, because a nonzero scalar identity factoring through a projective would make k projective. Thus K is stably indecomposable. A decomposed nonprojective K would give a nontrivial stable idempotent of k under that equivalence. A summand with zero stable identity is projective, which was excluded for this projective-cover kernel by the radical/Nakayama argument above. Hence K is actually indecomposable. But d-1 is neither one nor d: contradiction. This excludes every coefficient field and both characteristic regimes under the explicitly based/dimension/duality hypothesis.
