# Completed Green-ring symmetry: five-turn scoped results

**Problem 30004106 / OWR-16776-002 remains unsolved after 5/5 substantive author turns.** No actual finite-group counterexample to the universal symmetry question is established. The packet proves reductions, proper-family positive theorems, and limitations of two proposed proof routes. No novelty certification is claimed.

## Exact target

For a finite group G over a field k of characteristic p, take the split Green ring on finite-dimensional indecomposable kG-modules, complexify, and complete in the norm sum |c_M| dim_k M. The involution conjugates coefficients and dualizes modules. The question is whether this commutative Banach *-algebra is always symmetric, equivalently whether every bounded species respects this involution. The genuinely unresolved modular regime is p>0 dividing |G|. The scalar field of the Banach algebra is C even when the module field k has characteristic p. There is no algebraic-closure assumption on k.

This is neither the ordinary character ring nor a p-adic or augmentation completion. A C*-completion is a different algebra and does not answer the question without the missing spectral control.

## Results by turn

1. **Field and countable reductions.** Algebraic field extension induces an isometric *-embedding, so symmetry over the extension descends. The question reduces to the countable tensor/dual closure of each single indecomposable. Non-Hermitian species are characterized by compatible finite systems of polynomial equations and disk constraints; finite feasibility alone is not a counterexample certificate. A weak abstract example distinguishes positive-element radius identities from full symmetry.

2. **Endotrivial directions.** The closed stable span of all endotrivial cores is exactly a weighted group algebra with subexponential weights and unitary character space, hence symmetric. The subexponential growth input is credited to Benson's stated theorem and its Carlson/Dade prerequisites. For p-groups, the full Green completion splits into a projective scalar factor and the stable completion with explicit norm bounds. The full span of projectives and endotrivial cores is symmetric.

3. **An actual obstruction to a restriction argument.** For Q8 in characteristic two, an explicit periodic resolution, with exact rank certificates and Langer's resolution credited, proves stable syzygy order four. A self-adjoint element has spectrum {0,-2,-4}, while its restriction to the unique nontrivial elementary abelian subgroup and to the trivial group is zero. Thus positive-module detection cannot be extended to a spectral-radius bound for arbitrary signed or complex Green elements. Its spectrum is real; it is not a counterexample to symmetry.

4. **Axioms alone are insufficient.** A finite-rank family satisfies all five of Benson's abstract representation-ring axioms and the strengthened closed condition, yet has non-Hermitian characters and zero radical. All positive-element gamma identities still hold. A separate projective-cover/syzygy argument proves that these based dimension rings cannot be finite-group Green rings. This excludes an axioms-only proof, while leaving the original group question open.

5. **A larger actual positive family.** For elementary abelian E, the closed span of the projective and all modules induced from endotrivial cores of nontrivial subgroups is symmetric. The proof establishes distinct actual indecomposable coordinates before using the normalized intersection algebra. A finite subgroup-lattice Möbius transform gives a product of weighted endotrivial group algebras, with explicit bounds for both the map and its inverse on the completions. A two-dimensional F8 representation of C2^3 is outside the span, so the theorem is genuinely a proper-family result.

## Remaining gap

The packet controls endotrivial and induced-endotrivial directions but not bounded species on arbitrary other indecomposables. The actual Q8 example prevents a naive passage from positive restriction estimates to signed spectral estimates. The finite abstract nonsymmetric examples are proved nonrealizable with the required based dimension data. Neither obstacle supplies an actual-group counterexample or a universal proof.
