# Turn 2: the endotrivial directions are symmetric

**Scoped positive results; original unresolved, 2/5 substantive author turns.** This turn tests whether the infinite syzygy/endotrivial directions can produce the missing non-Hermitian species. Their complete weighted group-algebra description excludes that route. For p-groups, the result lifts to a concrete closed subalgebra of the full completion, with explicit norm control.

The representation-theoretic input is credited: Benson arXiv:2008.13155v2, Lemma4.4.7 and Theorem4.4.8, p69, establish gamma_G(E)=1 for endotrivial modules. Their proof uses the stated Carlson/Dade results and polynomial growth of ordinary syzygies. This packet uses that published input, rather than claiming a new proof of those classification and cohomological theorems.

## 1. Stable endotrivial classes give an exact weighted group algebra

Let p divide |G|, let A be the full completion and let A_st be its quotient by the closed span of the projective indecomposables. Write T=T(G;k) for the group of stable endotrivial classes. Each class t has a unique indecomposable nonprojective representative E_t; its other representatives differ by projective summands.

Here is why the uniqueness/indecomposability used in the algebra is appropriate. The stable module category is Krull–Schmidt, and its unit k is indecomposable: a scalar identity on k cannot factor through a projective unless k is projective. Tensoring with an invertible object preserves indecomposability. Thus a stable endotrivial object has one indecomposable nonprojective summand. Krull–Schmidt uniqueness supplies E_t. Its inverse is represented by E_t*, and tensor product gives

    core(E_s tensor E_t) = E_(st).

The group T is abelian by the tensor flip. We do not require a finite generation theorem for T.

Put w(t)=dim_k E_t. Then

    w(1)=1,  w(t)>=1,  w(t^{-1})=w(t),
    w(st)<=w(s)w(t).                                      (1)

The last inequality follows by taking dimensions before removing projective summands. The closed coordinate span B_T of the E_t inside A_st is consequently the weighted convolution algebra

    l¹(T,w)={ sum_t a_t delta_t : sum_t |a_t|w(t)<infinity },

isometrically as a Banach *-algebra. Its involution is

    (sum_t a_t delta_t)*=sum_t conjugate(a_t)delta_(t^{-1}).

This uses the quotient norm on the actual stable Green ring: nonprojective indecomposables remain a free coordinate basis. It is not an abstract assignment of a different norm to the same group.

The credited endotrivial gamma theorem gives, for every fixed t,

    lim_{n->infinity} w(t^n)^(1/n)=1.                      (2)

Indeed the core of E_t^(tensor n) is E_(t^n), so this is exactly the defining gamma limit. The same applies to t^{-1}.

## 2. Complete character space and symmetry of the stable span

**Theorem.** The characters of B_T are exactly the unitary group characters chi:T->S¹, evaluated by absolutely convergent Fourier sums. In particular B_T is symmetric, and for x=sum a_t delta_t,

    spectrum_(B_T)(x)
       ={sum_t a_t chi(t) : chi in Hom(T,S¹)}.             (3)

To prove this, a Banach-algebra character s has s(delta_1)=1 and hence s(delta_t) nonzero. The numbers chi(t)=s(delta_t) form a group homomorphism into C*. Contractivity and (2) imply

    |chi(t)|^n=|s(delta_(t^n))|<=w(t^n),
    |chi(t)|<=1.

Applying the same argument to the inverse gives |chi(t)|>=1. Thus chi is unitary. Conversely every unitary group character defines a bounded multiplicative functional, since

    |sum_t a_t chi(t)|<=sum_t |a_t|<=sum_t |a_t|w(t).

It respects the involution. Absolute convergence justifies multiplication and the evaluation formula. Gelfand's character description of spectra proves (3), and the self-conjugate-character criterion proves symmetry. This proof does not assume every species of a subalgebra extends to the ambient stable algebra.

In particular, the ordinary syzygy family generated by Omega(k) cannot cause failure. If Omega(k) has infinite stable order, its closed span is a weighted l¹(Z) with subexponential weight, and its character space is the unit circle. If its stable order is d<infinity, the span is the group algebra of Z/d, with character space the d-th roots of unity. The latter is finite dimensional; the former does not require finite dimensionality.

For any character s of the entire stable completion, its restriction to B_T is therefore unitary. Twisting a module M by E_t changes its duality defect only by a unit complex factor:

    s([(E_t tensor M)*])−conjugate(s([E_t tensor M]))
      =conjugate(chi(t))
            (s([M*])−conjugate(s([M]))).                  (4)

Thus such a twist preserves the absolute defect and cannot create or remove it for that species.

## 3. An explicit full/stable splitting for p-groups

Now suppose G is a nontrivial finite p-group. Over every field of characteristic p, kG is local, so its only indecomposable projective is P=kG. For completeness, the augmentation ideal is nilpotent: induct on |G|, choose a central element z of order p, use the nilpotence of z−1 and of the augmentation ideal in k[G/<z>]. The quotient by the augmentation ideal is k. Finite projectives over this local finite-dimensional algebra are free.

For every module M,

    P tensor M = (dim M)P,   P*=P.

Set e=P/|G| in the complexified split ring. Then e=e*, e²=e, ||e||=1, and

    e a=dim(a)e                                              (5)

for all a in A, by continuity of the dimension character. The projective ideal is exactly Ce. Hence

    A ≅ Ce × (1−e)A                                         (6)

as a unital Banach *-algebra (with factor units e and 1−e), and (1−e)A is *-isomorphic to A_st through the quotient map. Therefore, for p-groups,

    A symmetric  iff  A_st symmetric.                     (7)

This is an equivalence for the entire stable quotient, not a claim that it is symmetric. The unresolved part has simply been isolated.

There is useful exact norm information. Write x in A_st using its canonical nonprojective representative, and let D(x) be the sum of its coefficients weighted by their dimensions. D is a bounded linear functional on that coordinate space; it is NOT asserted to be multiplicative for the stable product. The inverse of the quotient identification is

    J(x)=(1−e)x=x−D(x)e.

Because its nonprojective and projective supports are disjoint,

    ||J(x)||_A=||x||_st+|D(x)|,
    ||x||_st<=||J(x)||_A<=2||x||_st.                       (8)

Multiplicativity follows from the idempotent splitting, not from a false multiplicativity claim for D. This bound also gives equality of spectral radii by taking n-th roots of norms of powers, while preserving the correct units of the two factors.

## 4. A symmetric closed subalgebra of the full p-group completion

Let C_T be the closed coordinate span in A of P and all the E_t. Products of two endotrivial representatives have one endotrivial core plus a projective summand, and products with P stay projective. Thus it is a closed unital *-subalgebra. Under (6),

    C_T ≅ Ce × J(B_T).

The first factor is C with its usual involution, and the second is symmetric by Section2 and the *-isomorphism J. Consequently:

**Theorem.** For every finite p-group and every coefficient field of characteristic p, the full-completion subalgebra generated by its projectives and all stable endotrivial representatives is symmetric.

This covers the entire infinite family of ordinary syzygies and all its endotrivial twists, including their norm-convergent sums. It does not cover arbitrary non-endotrivial indecomposables. A non-Hermitian species in the original p-group algebra would have to involve those additional directions.

## Remaining gap after 2/5

The endotrivial subalgebra is positive, but its complement in the stable Green ring is generally enormous. Symmetry of one closed subalgebra does not imply symmetry of the ambient algebra. Likewise, the scalar/projective splitting for p-groups does not settle the remaining stable factor. The next route must control non-endotrivial directions or find a species there, without replacing signed/complex norm information by a theorem stated only for positive module classes.
