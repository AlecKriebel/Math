# Turn 4: finite abstract representation rings need not be symmetric

**Original finite-group question unresolved, 4/5 substantive author turns.** This turn tests a proof from the five abstract axioms emphasized in the source. Those axioms alone are insufficient, even with a finite basis, zero complex radical and the strengthened “closed” unit-coefficient condition.

The example is explicitly proved not to be the Green ring of any finite group with its dimension and duality data. It is a counterexample to the stronger abstract assertion, not to the original group problem. Benson's abstract framework and artificial examples in Definition1.1.1/Example1.1.2 are credited; no priority claim is made.

## 1. Construction of the family

Let H be a finite abelian group of order m with an element of order greater than two, and let q>=2 be an integer. Let ZH have its usual group-basis elements g. Put

    c=q−1,  s=sum_(g in H) g,
    P=c·1_H+s,  d=c+m=q+m−1.

Take an abelian group I freely generated by symbols x_g, g in H, identified additively with ZH. Define its multiplication by

    x_g x_h = c x_(gh)+sum_(u in H) x_u.                  (1)

Equivalently, the underlying group-ring elements multiply by alpha beta ->P alpha beta. This is associative and commutative: both triple products equal P² alpha beta gamma in ZH. I need not have an integral identity.

Now form its unitization

    R=Z·1 direct sum I,

with 1 a new basis element acting as the identity. All structure constants in the basis {1} union {x_g} are nonnegative integers. Define the involution on this integral basis to fix every element. On R_C it conjugates coefficients. Set

    dim(1)=1,  dim(x_g)=d,
    rho=sum_(g in H) x_g.                                 (2)

This is finite rank m+1.

## 2. Verification of every abstract axiom

We check Benson's Definition1.1.1, including condition(ii') for a closed representation ring.

(i) The identity permutation of the basis is involutive and preserves multiplication. Since the ring is commutative, its conjugate-linear extension is a *-operation.

(ii') A product of two nonunit basis elements lies in I and has zero coefficient at the new unit. The only product with positive unit coefficient is 1·1, where that coefficient is1. Thus the strengthened condition holds.

(iii) For x_g, its product with its dual has zero unit coefficient, so we need [x_g³:x_g]>=2. In the additive ZH description,

    x_g³ corresponds to P² g³,
    P²=c²·1_H+(2c+m)s.

Hence

    [x_g³:x_g] =
       c²+2c+m,  if g²=1_H,
       2c+m,     otherwise.

Both numbers are at least2. The condition does not apply to the unit, whose unit coefficient is nonzero.

(iv) Every nonunit basis dimension is the positive integer d. The sum of the coefficients in (1) is c+m=d, so dim(x_g x_h)=d²=dim(x_g)dim(x_h). The dimension map is therefore a ring homomorphism and is invariant under the involution.

(v) The nonzero positive element rho satisfies

    x_g rho=d rho,    1 rho=rho.

By linearity, a rho=dim(a)rho for every a. In particular rho²=md rho=dim(rho)rho. This checks the regular-element axiom, not merely the first four positivity axioms.

Thus R is a closed abstract representation ring in the source's precise sense. “Closed” here names the strengthened unit-coefficient condition; it is not a statement about a topological closure.

## 3. All complex characters and the nonsymmetric spectrum

Let chi range over the m ordinary one-dimensional characters of H. The group-ring value of P is

    lambda_chi =
       d,  if chi is trivial,
       c,  otherwise.

All these values are nonzero. The following are characters of the unital algebra R_C:

    epsilon(1)=1,  epsilon(x_g)=0,
    s_chi(1)=1,    s_chi(x_g)=lambda_chi chi(g).           (3)

For example, applying s_chi to (1) gives
c lambda_chi chi(gh)+lambda_chi sum_u chi(u), which equals
lambda_chi² chi(gh) in both the trivial and nontrivial cases.

The Fourier transform of the finite abelian group is invertible, and multiplication by P is invertible on C H because all lambda_chi are nonzero. Consequently the m+1 maps in (3) identify

    R_C ≅ C^(m+1).                                       (4)

In particular R_C is semisimple and has no nonzero nilpotent or Jacobson-radical element.

The dimension-weighted norm is submultiplicative by the checked nonnegative structure constants and dimension homomorphism. Since R_C is finite dimensional, it is already complete. Every character in (3) is dimension bounded on the basis: |s_chi(x_g)| is either d or c<=d. Thus these are characters of exactly the required normed completion, with no discontinuous character hidden in the example.

Choose g with order greater than two and a character chi with chi(g) nonreal. Such a character exists because characters of a finite abelian group separate its cyclic subgroups. The element x_g is self-adjoint, but

    s_chi(x_g)=c chi(g)

is nonreal. Its square x_g*x_g has a nonreal or negative value for some such character: equivalently, failure already follows from the self-adjoint-spectrum characterization of symmetric commutative Banach *-algebras. Therefore the completion is not symmetric.

For the smallest example, take H=C3 and q=2. The basis is 1,x_0,x_1,x_2, all three x_i have dimension4, and with zeta a primitive cube root,

    spectrum(x_1)={0,4,zeta,zeta²},
    spectrum(x_1*x_1)={0,16,zeta,zeta²}.

This is an explicit finite-dimensional obstruction to the axioms-only proof route.

## 4. Positive-element radius identities still all hold

Every nonnegative real basis combination a is self-adjoint for this involution. For every representation ideal X, its image in the quotient completion is still self-adjoint. Hence automatically

    gamma_X(a a*)=rho_X(a²)=rho_X(a)^2=gamma_X(a)^2.       (5)

The equality rho(a²)=rho(a)^2 is the general spectral-radius power rule, not symmetry. Thus all positive-element gamma duality identities hold in this entire abstract family even though symmetry fails.

This sharpens the logical warning from turn1: the issue persists after imposing all five abstract representation-ring axioms and finite dimensionality. It does not establish that the converse fails for actual finite-group Green rings; their additional structure might enforce it.

## 5. These based dimension rings cannot be actual finite-group Green rings

Suppose, for contradiction, that R is the split Green ring of some finite group G over a field k, by an isomorphism preserving the distinguished indecomposable basis, dimensions and duality. Then its complete list of indecomposable module dimensions would be

    1 (the trivial module), and d repeated m times,
    with d=q+m−1>=4.                                     (6)

Every nontrivial indecomposable in this list is self-dual and has no trivial summand in its tensor square.

If k were projective, then char(k) would not divide |G|, and Maschke's theorem would make kG semisimple. Every indecomposable M would be simple; the nonzero invariant identity in M tensor M* would supply a trivial direct summand by semisimplicity. This contradicts the zero unit coefficients. Thus k is not projective.

Let P_k be its indecomposable projective cover. It is not k, so (6) forces dim P_k=d. Its kernel Omega k has dimension d−1.

For a finite group algebra, Omega k is indecomposable and nonprojective. One direct explanation is that kG is self-injective and the syzygy functor is an autoequivalence of its stable category. The stable endomorphism ring of k is k, since a nonzero scalar identity cannot factor through a projective unless k is projective. Thus Omega k is stably indecomposable. Its minimal-projective-cover kernel has no projective summand: such a summand would be injective and split off inside the radical of P_k, which is impossible by Nakayama's lemma. Therefore Omega k is an actual indecomposable module.

But d−1 is neither1 nor d, contradicting (6). This excludes realization over every field, without an algebraic-closure assumption. The construction therefore cannot be advertised as a finite-group counterexample.

## Remaining gap after 4/5

A universal proof must use more than the abstract positivity, involution, dimension and regular-element axioms. The family also shows why nil-radical information and all positive gamma identities do not suffice abstractly. The original problem still asks whether the extra constraints of actual modular group representations force all bounded species to respect duality. That remains unproved here.
