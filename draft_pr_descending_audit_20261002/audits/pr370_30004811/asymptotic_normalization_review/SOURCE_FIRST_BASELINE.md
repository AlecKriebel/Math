## Exact question and normalization

OWR 40/2021, Jauregui abstract, pp.2255–2258, proposes the capacity-volume mass as supremum over compact exhaustions of the limsup of volume radius minus capacity. Coordinate balls are one admissible family, not the whole optimization. The announced conjecture asks for equality with ADM mass for general asymptotically flat metrics, in context of nonnegative scalar curvature and empty/minimal boundary. Jauregui 2002.08941, Definition 9 and the discussion after Theorem 7, states the intended general case under Theorem 6's global hypotheses: smooth connected one-ended 3-manifold, compact boundary if any, order tau>1/2 with decay through two derivatives, integrable scalar curvature, globally nonnegative scalar curvature, and empty/minimal boundary. The literal OWR sentence is broader; any result must identify its actual scope rather than silently importing or removing these assumptions.

Use normalized Newtonian capacity c(K)=(4pi)^(-1) inf integral |grad u|^2 dV, with u=0 on K and u→1 at infinity; equivalently use v=1-u and exterior energy. Then c(B_r in Euclidean space)=r. The OWR prose omits the normalization prefactor, but its inequality and zero Euclidean example require it, and Jauregui Definition 3 explicitly supplies it. Write v(K)=(3|K|/(4pi))^(1/3), and D(K)=v(K)-c(K). The alternate local expression Q(K)=(v(K)^3-c(K)^3)/(3c(K)^2)=D(K)(v(K)^2+v(K)c(K)+c(K)^2)/(3c(K)^2). Equality of the supremum limsups does not follow for arbitrary individual sequences without near equality v/c→1; Jauregui Lemma 10 supplies the reduction through better competitors.

For general p, BFM's normalized c_p scales as length^(3-p), and its local mass scales as length. At p=2 it becomes exactly Q(K). A comparison theorem in that language must bridge Q to D at the supremum-over-exhaustions level.

## Strongest primary-source results and scope

1. Jauregui Theorem 5 provides m_CV≥m_ADM when scalar curvature is nonnegative outside a compact set. Theorem 6 provides the reverse bound only on coordinate balls under global nonnegative scalar curvature and empty/minimal boundary. Theorem 7 provides the arbitrary-exhaustion reverse bound under harmonic flatness and nonnegative ADM mass. Thus neither coordinate-ball equality nor harmonic-flat equality alone solves the general problem.
2. BFM 2305.01453v2 Theorem 5.6 states m_iso^(p)≤m_iso for C0 asymptotically flat manifolds with compact boundary, 1<p≤2. It relies on Theorem 5.5's sharp isocapacitary asymptotics and Proposition 5.2's conversion of definitions. Its full ADM identification, Theorem 1.3, adds H_2(M,∂M;Z)=0; citing only that theorem as literally covering unrestricted original hypotheses would omit a real assumption. The comparison plus an independently applicable isoperimetric ADM theorem is a distinct route that may avoid it.
3. Benatti 2511.11155v2 Proposition 3.1 repairs a signed-coefficient flaw in the older BFM proof of the comparison and proves the comparison under strong p-nonparabolicity. His Lemma 3.3 uses sufficiently large sets in inclusion, avoiding a volume-only relaxation. The correction explains that BFM's use of an upper integral bound in a term multiplied by m_iso needs nonnegative m_iso; JLU supplies nonnegativity for AF metrics. This is a known, explicit concern to check rather than evidence that the theorem is false.

Jauregui–Lee 1602.00732 proves m_iso=m_ADM in smooth AF nonnegative-scalar-curvature manifolds with appropriate minimal-boundary assumptions; BFM Penrose 2212.10215 provides identification under C1 AF tau>1/2 with global nonnegative scalar curvature and smooth closed minimal boundary. ## Independent mechanism worth testing

For p=2, an inclusion-uniform asymptotic isoperimetric inequality A^(3/2)/(6sqrt(pi))+(mu/2)A≥V on all sufficiently outward sets yields A≥4pi R_mu(V)^2, where R_mu(V) solves V=(4pi/3)R^3+2pi mu R^2. For mu>0 this is strictly monotone for R>0. Integrating coarea/Cauchy–Schwarz along capacitary level sets should give c(K)≥[4pi integral_{V0}^infty (4pi R_mu(V)^2)^(-2)dV]^(-1). Exact substitution dV=(4pi R^2+4pi mu R)dR gives c(K)≥R0^2/(R0+mu/2). Meanwhile v(K)=(R0^3+(3mu/2)R0^2)^(1/3), so D(K)≤F_mu(R0):=v(K)-R0^2/(R0+mu/2). Its leading expansion is mu−mu^2/(2R0)+mu^3/(3R0^2)+O(mu^4/R0^3). Uniform estimates for level sets, properness, critical levels, boundary smoothing, and arbitrary exhaustions are necessary. A formal one-variable expansion is only an auxiliary calculation.

## Falsifiable success criteria and boundary tests

Accept a general-case closure only if: normalized capacity and exhaustions agree with the source; lower and upper bounds apply to exactly the intended class; no unproved isoperimetric inequality or equivalent conjecture carries the central difficulty; the proof controls all outward sets uniformly, with a justified extension to admissible rough compact exhaustions; primary prior results are distinguished from the contribution; and code is treated as checking calculations rather than global geometry.

Mandatory controls: Euclidean space D(B_r)=0 and arbitrary-shape D≤0; Schwarzschild m>0 with isotropic u=1+m/(2r), c(B_r)=r+m/2 and v(B_r)=r+3m/2+O(r^-1); m=0 limit; homothety g→lambda^2 g gives v,c,m→lambda times original; signs mu>0 versus zero/negative, particularly loss of monotonicity for signed mu; compact fill/cut changes volume by O(1), hence v by O(v^-2), with unchanged exterior capacity; disconnected or holes admitted by exhaustion definitions; and tau>1/2 threshold/errors such as r^(1-2tau)→0. Coordinate-ball asymptotics cannot be generalized by a finite computation or finite count.
