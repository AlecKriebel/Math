# Independent effectivity derivation

Written after TURN_1 and complete relevant primary-source reading, before any historical review or historical verifier read. This is an independent mathematical check, not a production implementation.

## 1. Effective search principle

Let X be the finite rational triangulated oriented surface produced by the credited PPP construction, with its finite rational dual dissection D and finite rational removed collars. For each H, form the finite set of triangle-labeled segments whose endpoints have rational barycentric coordinates with bounded numerator/denominator. Form all cyclic lists, with total segment count at most H, then all ordered 2g-tuples. Test endpoint identifications by shared simplex identity and a fixed canonical edge parameter. In a chart with the opposite edge order the local coordinate is 1-t; in a shared canonical order it is t. All values are rational. Therefore each height is finite and computable, and every finite rational PL system occurs at some finite height (take H at least every numerator, denominator and total number of segments). No bound on that height is required.

For rational triangle gluings there is no irrational seam obstruction. Match each edge using its ordered endpoint labels. The affine edge map is rational, and rational subdivisions and rational crossing points remain rational. General affine polygon charts can be replaced by standard rational triangle charts, so no unspecified numeric embedding is needed.

## 2. Decidable acceptance

Subdivide each candidate curve and D at their common intersections and triangle seams. Quotient coincident points only after preserving triangle/edge-sector occurrence labels. Exact segment determinants decide ordinary transverse intersections, collinearity and overlap. Rejected degeneracies (vertices/endpoints, triple points, overlaps and reversals) do not require a limit or a genericity oracle. Curve embeddedness is the finite injectivity test except for its prescribed cyclic closure; local branches at a common quotient point can be checked by their cyclic sector order. A crossing has alternating branches. A conservative predicate rejecting any additional PL-vertex degeneracy is sound and still admits generic witnesses.

The finite embedded graph has a ribbon rotation system. A regular neighborhood is the union of one vertex disk and one edge band, with attachments in that order. Its boundary components are cycles of the dart permutation sigma composed with the edge involution alpha; its Euler characteristic is V-E. Equivalently one can cut a finite subdivision along the graph, split side copies, and traverse components. Both give a finite exact algorithm. A transverse pair with one crossing has the alternating four-valent rotation, two loop edges after suppressing valence-two vertices, chi=-1, and one boundary component; hence genus1. The nonalternating rotation would have three boundaries and genus0, which demonstrates why occurrence/rotation data are indispensable. The submission explicitly retains these data.

To test the complement of disjoint regular neighborhoods, split finitely many incident sectors and rationally triangulate the residual planar cells. Traverse the resulting finite triangles/edges; find component boundary cycles and chi. The original surface is oriented, and a cut subsurface inherits orientation. Thus for each connected component, g=(2-h-chi)/2. Genus recognition here is an integer calculation on a known 2-manifold, not a general manifold/homeomorphism oracle. Positive rational widths can be found by enumerating rational choices and testing the finitely many incidence/intersection conditions; finitely many compact disjoint nonincident segments have positive separation. Coincident adjacent edges are handled inside vertex neighborhoods, not incorrectly assigned positive distance.

## 3. Witness existence and simultaneous constraints

The classification of compact connected oriented surfaces gives g disjoint once-holed torus subsurfaces, a connected planar complement, and one simple core pair per handle crossing once. Use handles inside the surface away from all original boundaries and the finite removed puncture/boundary collars. This theorem supplies a witness, not an algorithmic classifier: the enumerator searches independently for any such witness.

Choose finitely many local coordinate disks around the prescribed pair crossings. Outside them, branches that must stay disjoint have separated compact neighborhoods. Apply a small generic perturbation to the curve system, leaving prescribed crossings in their disks, so curves avoid vertices, all D/triangulation crossings are transverse, and no D crossing occurs at a pair crossing. There are only finitely many fixed edges/vertices and prescribed crossings. These conditions are open after transversality has been arranged. There is therefore no conflict between pair intersections, collars, dissection transversality and triangulation transversality.

Subdivide at triangle-edge crossings. In each triangle approximate the relevant curve pieces by sufficiently close PL paths. Choose seam points once per quotient edge and share them, then approximate interior vertices rationally. The open conditions and compact separation margins survive sufficiently close approximation. Rational PL vertex/endpoint choices are dense in the required open parameter sets. Exact equalities at seams are preserved by choosing their one rational parameter jointly, rather than independently approximating its two copies. The resulting curves are ambient-isotopic to the generic curves, so neighborhood types and complementary connectedness/genus are preserved.

It remains to check the extra disk-side condition; this must not be assumed. The finite-dimensional hypothesis implies no white punctures. By APS Proposition3.11 (with colors swapped) each component cut along the black dual dissection is an open disk with one white boundary mark. Each handle curve is nonseparating (it intersects its partner once), hence cannot lie in a single such disk. The crossings with D are finite and nonempty. A maximal portion between successive crossings, viewed in the cut-open disk with occurrence copies retained, is a proper embedded interval with distinct boundary occurrence endpoints. If both endpoints correspond to the same physical crossing (a one-crossing loop), they lie on the two different cut-side occurrences, so are still distinct. A proper embedded interval in a disk is a crosscut and separates the disk into two regions. Consequently every maximal portion meets the submitted disk-side test. No separate normal-position/minimal-intersection theorem is needed.

The word "segment" in the submitted final bullet must mean this maximal portion between D crossings, as it does in its winding paragraph and in APS Lemma3.18. An elementary triangle-chart PL edge with interior endpoints is not a disk crosscut. Reading the bullet as requiring each elementary PL edge to separate the whole D polygon would invalidate the intended argument. This is a concrete editorial ambiguity to correct, not a counterexample to the intended maximal-portion algorithm.

These paragraphs prove a witness passes all acceptance tests. The enumerator reaches it at a finite height; decidable finite stages halt one by one; hence the whole search halts on the promised nonzero finite-dimensional input. This is the ordinary computable-search theorem, not an appeal to an uncomputable bound or a negative semi-decision procedure.

## 4. Winding and exceptional cases

At every D crossing choose a small disk disjoint from every other crossing/vertex/curve and the prescribed pair-crossing disks. A smooth embedded isotopy supported there adjusts the crossing tangent to be orthogonal to D. Smooth remaining PL corners in similarly small disks. These changes introduce no curl, cusp, extra intersection or change of which white mark lies in which cut side. Since the original and final embeddings are related by an ambient isotopy, they are regularly homotopic. APS Proposition3.20(2) preserves winding. Thus Lemma3.18 applies, and the winding is the finite sum +1 or -1 according to the cut disk's unique white boundary occurrence. A rational arrangement/sector traversal finds this occurrence's component after the crosscut, so no tangent angle or analytic line-field construction needs to be computed.

Peripheral curves are constructed as polygonal boundaries of sufficiently small regular collars of the original boundary cycles or puncture stars. They are simple and can be put transverse to D by the same local method. For every black puncture, the boundary of a sufficiently small puncture star meets its incident dual edges. No black puncture can be unincident to D, since it would give a puncture in a dual-complement component, contrary to the disk description. Black punctures are not hidden white punctures in this argument.

For an original boundary component, its parallel is noncontractible in the puncture-removed surface unless the connected component is an unpunctured disk. Such a noncontractible parallel cannot lie in one dual-complement disk, so its crossing count is nonzero. In the unpunctured disk, all dual arcs have both endpoints on the original boundary. A nonzero algebra has nonempty Delta and nonempty D, since the primal/dual dissections have equally many arcs (also APS Proposition3.12 with |Mwhite|=|Mblack|). Therefore a sufficiently small whole boundary collar crosses at least one dual arc near its endpoints. D empty instead gives the disk with one mark of each color, Delta empty, no quiver vertices, and the zero algebra. This is the excluded empty case, not an unexplained nonzero contractible exception. A handle or noncontractible peripheral loop cannot be contained in a disk polygon.

The isolated-field case has disk, two white/two black boundary marks, and one dual arc. A clockwise square collar crosses that arc twice. Both white marks are on the left in their cut disks, so w=2, in agreement with APS Proposition3.20(5), 4-2b-4g=2. The dual-numbers annulus (one loop with square-zero relation) has one original white boundary mark and one black puncture; its dual arc joins the puncture to the boundary. Each peripheral curve crosses once. With surface to the right, outer w=+1 and puncture w=-1; the total0 matches the annulus formula. This example specifically needs distinct cut-side occurrences of one physical seam crossing.

For the empty input, an implementation may instead return the special zero-algebra key and no handle data, avoiding any winding formula with n=0. TURN_1 explicitly excludes/rejects empty input but attributes this to a nonzero source class without an explicit source quote; parent/source auditor should resolve that minor scope convention. The claimed algorithm's promised nonzero class has no zero-crossing exception left.
