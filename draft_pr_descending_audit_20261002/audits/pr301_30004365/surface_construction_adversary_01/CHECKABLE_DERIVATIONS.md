# Finite surface and topology derivations

UTC 2026-10-05. Independently derived from the submitted claim and primary definitions, before any historical-review read. This note accepts the published PPP/APS classification theorems within their stated hypotheses. It supplies additional elementary effectiveness and boundary-case arguments, rather than re-proving their representation theory.

## 1. Relation completion has no oracle

At an original vertex list the original incoming arrows first in two incoming slots, and similarly the outgoing arrows in two outgoing slots. Fill missing slots with fresh leaf arrows. A completed relation table is exactly a permutation p of {0,1}: the pair (incoming i, outgoing j) is related iff p(i)=j. Its complementary table is also a permutation.

Gentleness says that each row and column of the fixed rectangular original table has at most one entry of each truth value. Thus its restriction extends to one of the two permutations. If both incoming and outgoing original sets are nonempty, one fixed entry determines which of the two permutations is possible. If one original set is empty, the two choices are exchanged by permuting two fresh blossom arrows on the opposite side. Consequently any completion is isomorphic to any other by an isomorphism fixing all original arrows and vertices. This is a direct local proof, including isolated vertices and loop incidences; no recognition of homeomorphic surfaces is needed.

Completing relations is therefore finite and canonical up to fresh blossom labels. A loop has separate incoming and outgoing incidences even when the underlying arrow label is identical. The number of completed arrows is 4|Q0|-|Q1|, and each original vertex has precisely four local composition pairs.

## 2. Links and colors directly from the lozenges

Use counterclockwise lozenge corners G,S,R,T, with G=(0,1), S=(-1,0), R=(0,-1), T=(1,0). The four sides in that order are GS, SR, RT, TG. A nonrelation glues TG of the incoming arrow to GS of the outgoing arrow, identifying their G corners. A relation glues RT to SR, identifying R corners. These identifications reverse polygon boundary orientation.

The original quiver vertex becomes one interior vertex in the glued complex: its four lozenge corner sectors have link a four-edge circle. To see this without a drawing, at the original vertex each incoming sector is incident once to a relation and once to a nonrelation, and each outgoing sector is incident once to each. The two matching permutations differ, so their union is the alternating four-cycle on the two incoming and two outgoing occurrences. A blossom leaf has a single corner sector, hence interval link.

At a G corner, only nonrelation side identifications occur. The permitted-arrow successor relation has indegree and outdegree at most one; after completion its components are chains between blossoms or cycles. The link of a G corner is therefore an interval for a chain and a circle for a cycle. The same argument holds at R using related-arrow successors. All links are circles or intervals, so no pinched vertex is hidden by corner union-find. The surface is oriented because all identified sides reverse polygon orientation.

An oriented permitted cycle would give arbitrarily long nonzero paths in the monomial quotient. Finite-dimensionality therefore excludes G cycles. Conversely, on a finite arrow set, if the permitted-successor graph is acyclic, its path lengths are bounded, so the quotient is finite-dimensional. Thus finite-dimensional input has only boundary G marks and may have interior R punctures. Green is APS white, and red is APS black.

The original quiver vertex is the crossing of one concatenated green arc and one concatenated red arc. The two half-edges of either color through that crossing are one dissection arc, rather than two algebra vertices. Around a G mark, an original arrow's lozenge contributes the counterclockwise consecutive pair of green arcs with labels s(alpha),t(alpha); a blossom lozenge contributes a boundary edge and supplies no original arrow. A product alpha beta uses the same G endpoint of the middle arc precisely for a nonrelation. If it uses the other endpoint occurrence, it is related. These are precisely the incidence rules of APS Definition 4.1, including when endpoints have the same global mark label. This supplies a direct local consistency check of PPP's inverse theorem.

Every connected nonzero finite-dimensional component has a blossom: otherwise the finite permitted-arrow graph would have outdegree one everywhere and hence a cycle. The finite permitted chain through a blossom gives a boundary white mark. Consequently each component has nonempty boundary as required by APS, and no exceptional closed white-free finite-dimensional component is omitted.

## 3. Cutting the red dissection gives actual disks

Cutting along the red arcs means disabling red side gluings while retaining green side gluings and splitting original vertex sectors accordingly. The face adjacency graph of each resulting piece is one finite permitted chain. Gluing a new lozenge to the preceding union along its designated whole green side attaches a disk along one boundary interval; by induction the union is a disk. Distinct occurrences of the same original corner label are kept distinct during this cut. Its green corner sectors form an interval, so the one G mark occurs on the boundary exactly once.

Thus each cut-open dual polygon is a disk with one white boundary mark, including relation cycles and self-loops. Original black punctures become separate red corner occurrences on its boundary, not new punctures inside a cut-open dual polygon. This is also consistent with APS Proposition 3.11 after interchanging colors. Removing sufficiently small black puncture collars merely truncates such corner sectors. Curves avoiding those collars still have their sides decided in the original cut disk with its same unique white mark.

## 4. A finite rational triangulation is explicit

Put one center and one midpoint per lozenge side, matching midpoints on side pairs, and triangulate a lozenge into eight triangles (corner, adjacent side midpoint, center). Original corner equivalences, side classes, and face interiors are different types, so the three vertices of each triangle remain distinct after quotienting. Distinct edges or triangles can still have the same endpoint set: this is a finite triangle complex with distinguished incidences, rather than yet an abstract simplicial complex.

Now take its barycentric subdivision with one new midpoint per genuine edge class and one new center per triangle occurrence. Every small triangle is indexed by a flag vertex < edge < triangle. Its edge and triangle barycenters distinguish all formerly repeated incidences. Since each pre-subdivision triangle had distinct vertices and edges, no flag gives duplicate vertex sets except genuine shared faces. This gives a genuine finite simplicial triangulation. All new points can be chosen rational within their parent charts; matched edge parameters agree. The original colored sides remain subcomplexes. No floating-point embedding, surface recognition, or unspecified drawing is used.

Deleting a puncture collar can be effected in the star of the puncture vertex by equal sufficiently small rational edge parameters and a polygon connecting the parameters within consecutive triangles. Different stars can first be subdivided until these neighborhoods are disjoint. This construction is finite, and cutting it uses the recorded link order.

## 5. Curve search and complement predicates do not hide a central gap

All rational segment intersections within a triangle are determined by determinant signs and linear equations. Intersections on an edge are identified by the paired rational parameter. When cutting, vertices split according to sectors in their cyclic link; the glued endpoint's name is never used as a substitute for the sector occurrence. Connected components and boundary components then follow from finite graph traversal, and their Euler characteristics determine their genera.

For a once-crossing embedded pair of loops, the neighborhood spine has one vertex and two loop edges with alternating cyclic order. Its oriented thickening has Euler characteristic -1 and one boundary cycle, hence genus one. Conversely the acceptance test explicitly checks this genus and boundary information. Disjoint such neighborhoods with connected genus-zero complement leave a sphere with boundary after deleting the handles. Capping the original boundaries and puncture collars therefore gives the standard handle pairs as a symplectic basis. This certificate is stronger than the simple-loop homology condition stated before APS Theorem 7.3.

Existence of a standard handle decomposition for a compact orientable surface is an accepted elementary topological theorem, not a decision oracle in the algorithm. Perturbing its finitely many curves away from the finite triangulation vertices and dissection endpoints, then to transverse position, preserves the crossing pattern and complement type. Choose disjoint small neighborhoods of prescribed intersections and of the remaining embedded arcs. A sufficiently close PL approximation stays in those neighborhoods and is isotopic to the smooth system. Rational interior and edge parameters can be chosen within the resulting open constraints. It has finitely many segments and eventually occurs in the height enumeration. Therefore the only unbounded search has a guaranteed finite witness.

Each connected portion cut at dual-arc crossings is a proper embedded arc in a cut disk. It separates that disk; its unique white boundary occurrence lies in exactly one side. Local smoothing and orthogonalization at a crossing can be confined to disjoint small disks that avoid every other crossing and endpoint, preserving sides and regular homotopy. Thus APS Lemma 3.18 applies. Handle curves cannot be contained in one disk because their once-crossing handle system makes them essential. Required boundary collars cross dual arcs: each black boundary mark's completed relation chain passes through at least one original vertex, and each original vertex gives a dual-arc end. The only empty-dissection disk has zero algebra and is excluded.

## 6. Independent collar-sector formulas

Orient every peripheral collar so that the main surface lies to its right. For an original boundary component let m be its white-mark count and d the number of dual-arc endpoint occurrences on that component. Every white boundary interval contributes one positive cut piece. At a black boundary mark of dual valency r, there are r-1 additional pieces cutting off the black corner; their white mark lies on the right, and each contributes -1. Hence w(boundary)=m-(d-m)=2m-d. Endpoint occurrences are counted with multiplicity, including both ends of a self-loop.

For an interior black mark of dual valency r, its puncture collar has r sectors and all unique white marks lie to the right. Hence w(puncture)=-r. A full relation cycle of length ell has r=ell, including ell=1. These formulas follow by applying the published signed-side rule to these explicitly described collar sectors; they do not implement arbitrary handle winding.

Counting all dual-arc ends gives total d + sum(r)=2|Q0|. There are total m=2|Q0|-|Q1| white boundary marks. Summing gives sum w=2|Q0|-2|Q1|. PPP's genus formula with no white punctures rewrites this as 4-2(b+p)-4g, agreeing with APS Proposition 3.20(5) on the compact surface after puncture collars are removed. This is an independent consistency check of the orientation convention, not a substitute for checking each side.
