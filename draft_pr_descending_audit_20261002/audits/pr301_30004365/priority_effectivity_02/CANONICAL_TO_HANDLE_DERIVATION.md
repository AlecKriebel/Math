# Older explicit handle algorithms and finite end avoidance

Independent derivation, 2026-10-05. This is a composition argument for priority assessment. It is not attributed verbatim to LPVV and does not assert an earlier published solution of the gentle-algebra question.

## Stronger published input: separated handle pairs in 1990

Vegter and Yap, *Computational Complexity of Combinatorial Surfaces*, SoCG1990 pp.102–111 (Extended Abstract), [institutional primary PDF](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/3354078/1990ProcSCGVegter.pdf), explicitly give the desired geometric pairs before converting them to common-basepoint generators. Lemma4.3, printed p.107, computes a refinement and simple closed edge paths C_i,D_i with exactly one intersection within each pair and disjoint pair unions. Its proof construction occupies §§4, pp.107–111. Lemma4.8, p.110, supplies connectedness of the complement; Lemma4.9, p.111, proves that complement is a sphere with g holes. The local construction has an alternating crossing at each pair intersection, so a small regular neighborhood of each pair is a once-holed torus. Its O(gn) bound is for this closed triangulated-surface operation. The extended abstract includes proof sketches and refers to a full paper for some elementary maintenance details; it nevertheless explicitly publishes the geometric output and its constructive mechanism, rather than only an abstract existence statement.

Consequently, the strongest composition below can use these pairs directly and omit common-basepoint surgery. The following independent 2001 route remains as a second check. The 1990 final remark says boundary extensions are possible but does not provide their details. The all-end conversion below supplies those details here; it is not misattributed as a published 1990 boundary theorem.

## Independent published input: canonical loops in 2001

Lazarus, Pocchiola, Vegter and Verroust, *Computing a Canonical Polygonal Schema of an Orientable Triangulated Surface*, SoCG2001 pp.80–89, DOI10.1145/378583.378630, Theorem1 and the incremental algorithm §§3–4, give a finite algorithm on an oriented **closed triangulated** surface of genus g. It outputs 2g PL simple loops meeting only at a common basepoint, whose cut complement is a disk and whose side word is a product of g commutators. The end of §4 explicitly establishes noninterleaving of the distinct generator pairs near the basepoint and describes the computed connected sum of g tori. The full primary PDF is [HAL](https://hal.science/hal-00804691/document), deposited22May2013 but explicitly the June2001 proceedings paper. The theorem's O(gn) bound is the published bound for that closed-surface operation only; no complexity bound for the composed gentle algorithm is asserted here.

## Basepoint surgery — finite geometry and topology

Let W be this loop graph. Make a sufficiently small rational polygonal disk U around the basepoint, disjoint from every other bend/vertex, meeting each of the 4g incident strands once. The ribbon neighborhood N(W) consists of U and 2g rectangular bands, one for each loop. It has one boundary component because the canonical polygon complement is one disk.

Write the canonical face word as a1 b1 a1^-1 b1^-1 ... ag bg ag^-1 bg^-1. If f is cyclic successor in that word and alpha reverses each paired letter, the vertex rotation is f∘alpha. It groups the four ports of each pair consecutively, in cyclic order a_i+, b_i-, a_i-, b_i+ (up to simultaneous global convention reversal). In particular, the endpoints of the two strands of a_i alternate with the two of b_i, and ports belonging to different i do not interleave. This is exactly the geometric conclusion supplied by LPVV §4; it is not inferred from an abstract symplectic matrix.

Replace the four rays in each block by two rational PL chords connecting the same loop ends in U. Use a convex rational polygon for a disk chart, and connect opposite endpoints. The two chords within each block cross once transversely. Chords from different blocks have noninterleaving endpoints and are disjoint. Refine the PL disk chart if needed; this is a finite triangulation and affine operation. Orient one member of every pair to make the intersection sign positive. Outside U preserve the already disjoint loop strands. The resulting a_i,b_i are simple and have exactly the required crossings.

The g pair spines have disjoint small regular neighborhoods N_i. A pair has one vertex and two loop edges, so chi(N_i)=-1. Its alternating ribbon order gives one boundary cycle (the permutation of rotation with paired-band reversal has one cycle), hence it is a once-holed torus.

The complement is connected: the old outside disk meets every gap between consecutive ports on ∂U, since those gaps lie on ∂N(W). The finite chord arrangement within U has a central face plus three additional faces per crossing block; each face touches such a gap. Thus all U faces connect to that same outside disk. The two sides of each band attach to these faces and to the outside collar; they introduce no additional complementary component. This gives a combinatorial connectedness certificate, rather than assuming it from Euler characteristic. The complement then has g boundary cycles and

    chi(complement)=chi(closed_surface)-sum_i chi(N_i)
                   =(2-2g)-(-g)=2-g.

Since it is connected and oriented, its genus is zero. The outcome is a geometric handle system with connected planar complement. For g=1 the same rotation/chord calculation applies; for g=0 the handle system is empty.

## Keeping boundary and puncture caps out of the handles

Starting with the rational compact core S0, cap its h=b+p boundary circles by disjoint rational fan disks D1,...,Dh to get a finite closed triangulation. Apply Vegter–Yap's direct separated-pair algorithm, or LPVV followed by the basepoint surgery above. **Do not extend the line field over these caps.** They are used only for computing geometric curves.

The loops or their N_i may initially meet caps. Here is a finite end-avoidance conversion. Work on the capped surface with the geometric handle graph G and process D1,...,Dh in turn. Suppose G already avoids the first j-1 disks. Choose a small rational disk E in an open face of the planar complement of G, disjoint from all caps. Such a disk exists because a finite graph has empty interior and S0 has a nonempty open interior. Choose a rational PL path from an interior point of Dj to E that avoids D1,...,D(j-1) and the finite graph vertices. Existence follows from connectedness of the surface minus finitely many disjoint disks; a finite triangulation supplies the path by finite graph traversal after subdivision. The path is allowed to cross graph edges transversely.

Construct an ambient orientation-preserving rational PL homeomorphism F, supported near Dj and along this path, mapping Dj into E and fixing all earlier disks. Its construction requires only finite disk-chart operations: shrink the fan disk inside its collar using nested rational fan levels; cover the finite path by finitely many overlapping triangle-chart disks; move the small disk through those charts. A point move within a triangle is realized by its two stellar subdivisions at the old and new interior points and the affine map between corresponding triangles, fixing the triangle boundary. Choose the initial shrink and each subsequent chart neighborhood small enough for positive rational clearance. Finite rational inequalities and affine maps determine all these choices. There is no homeomorphism decision oracle, unknown mapping-class orbit problem, or new handle search in this construction.

Replace G by F^-1(G). It avoids Dj because F(Dj) lies in E, which was disjoint from G. It still avoids the earlier disks because F fixes them. Simplicity, all prescribed intersections and connected planar complement are preserved by a homeomorphism. Repeat finitely many times. Shrink the N_i inside the graph's positive clearance from the caps. Removing all the cap interiors from the connected planar complement leaves it connected and planar; its boundary now has g+h cycles and chi=2-g-h. The resulting handle system lies in the interior of S0, so boundaries, puncture ends and marks remain in the complement.

No winding invariance under the capped homeomorphism is assumed. Winding is computed **after** the geometric curves have been brought back into S0, with the original canonical line field on S0. A line field generally does not extend over the cap disks; using a cap extension would be invalid and is unnecessary.

## Composition into a numerical procedure

1. PPP Definitions4.6/4.8 and Theorem4.10 give a finite quiver-to-polygonal-surface construction. Rational triangulation and truncation turn it into S0.
2. Cap, run Vegter–Yap (alternatively LPVV plus surgery), do the finite end-avoidance construction, and put the resulting curves in generic rational PL position against the dual dissection. These are finite operations; generic position can be obtained by rational perturbations avoiding a finite list of equalities.
3. Every maximal cut portion of an embedded handle curve is a proper embedded crosscut of one of the dual-complement disks. Such a curve cannot remain entirely in a disk because it intersects its partner once. APS Lemma3.18 (2019 Lemma1.18) computes its winding as a finite signed count of sides containing the unique white occurrence. The peripheral curves are explicit collars; the nonzero disk case is treated separately as in the accepted proof.
4. All-end LP classification, plus APS Theorem6.1/Remark7.2 (2019 Theorem4.1/Remark5.2), yields the complete paired numerical key. Genus-one gcd, parity and Arf calculations are finite integer arithmetic. Empty/disconnected input is handled by finite component decomposition, outside the connected surface theorem.

This composition shows no substantive computability obstruction remains once those older theorems and standard rational PL operations are admitted. The latest essential source input existed by4Apr2019 (APSv1); Vegter–Yap is from1990, LPVV is from2001, PPP from2018/2019, and the all-boundary LP criterion was present19Jan2018. This is an **independent deduction from older results**, not evidence that the exact composition had already been published, nor a claimed polynomial bound, nor an implemented software package.

## Priority implication and exact residual gap

The present exhaustive enumeration does not introduce a new general surface-basis mechanism: the required separated geometric pairs with planar complement were explicitly published in1990. The end conversion above is finite local PL topology. Therefore, novelty based on the mere existence of a terminating numerical algorithm has a strong routine-corollary objection. A paper could still make an explicit bridge to the gentle question, carefully document finite predicates, correct the printed range, and provide verified counterexamples/certificates. The historical question whether someone already published that **exact** bridge remains distinct and unresolved in this family. APS's explicit2023 algorithm remark prevents attributing the quiver-basis-walk algorithm to APS without qualification.
