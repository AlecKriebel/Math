## Manuscript proofs: independent challenges and conclusions

### Definitions and support geometry

The finite-factor class is defined with the product already normalized. No undefined infinite factors or divisions by zero are admitted. Finite real signed factors producing nonnegative probabilities can be replaced by their absolute values without changing the probability table. The probability topology is the ordinary finite-dimensional simplex topology, so pointwise convergence and coordinatewise closure agree.

For a finite nonnegative product, every local cell occurring at a positive joint cell has a positive factor entry. Consequently an assignment satisfying all *actual* unary and edge projections of the support has a strictly positive product and lies in the support. This proves the local-cylinder equality used in the density proof. It is stronger than merely saying each supplied factor's positive cells are constraints; no cancellation or identification of unused factor cells is needed.

MTP2 makes the nonempty support a Boolean sublattice. Its meet and join supply 0 and 1 on every coordinate remaining after pins are removed. Thus active edge projections contain 00 and 11 and have only the four listed possibilities: full square, either implication, or equality. Pinned-edge constraints reduce to the pins and the active coordinate's actual unary projection. Original-edge implications and unary pins therefore describe the entire support. Strongly connected implication classes exactly encode deterministic equalities; contracting them gives a poset whose upsets enumerate the allowed joint states. This construction does not introduce a new edge or hidden variable.

The logarithms are taken only on positive local cells and hence are finite. Arbitrarily extending a missing local logarithm gives a finite two-bit polynomial. Substitution of pins and equalities, and reduction of comparable products to a unary variable, preserves the function on the support. A coefficient between incomparable equality classes aggregates every original cross-edge contribution; it is zero if no such original edge exists.

For incomparable classes K,L, the union of their strict-successor sets is an upset excluding K,L. Adding K, L, or both produces the required four support states. All other variables are fixed, so their logarithmic MTP2 inequality isolates the aggregate coefficient B(K,L) and proves B(K,L)≥0. This argument correctly establishes aggregate attraction; it does not claim that each originally supplied local factor is attractive. I specifically tested the possibility of a negative supplied interaction hidden by pins or cancelled across tied coordinates.

Each nonzero aggregate is lifted onto an existing original cross edge, and each quotient unary coefficient onto a representative original coordinate. The lift agrees with log p up to one finite constant on the support. The sum D of unary pin violations and original-edge implication violations is a nonnegative integer, vanishes exactly on the support and is at least 1 elsewhere. For every finite penalty parameter t≥0, the exponent L−tD has nonnegative pair coefficients: each implication penalty contributes a positive pair coefficient and a finite unary coefficient. The normalizing finite sum tends to the strictly positive support mass, so the normalized laws converge to p. This checks the essential locality, sign and normalization bridges simultaneously.

No-active-coordinate support uses only the pin penalties and is valid for a one-point support. Singleton equality classes, no implications, full support, multiple tied classes, negative coefficients between comparable classes, pinned neighbors, disconnected graphs and isolated coordinates are covered by the same reasoning. On the empty vertex set the unique mass is 1 and needs no scalar absorption. No empty-support probability law is used.

### Residual-flow compactness and normalization

For the reverse inclusion I independently expanded the oriented pair identity and terminal capacities. With a bit equal to 1 on the source side, the original cut cost is the quadratic energy plus a constant. Every capacity is nonnegative and finite. Only the original vertices and two terminals are present. An absent reverse arc is assigned capacity zero.

A maximum flow exists for arbitrary finite real capacities because the feasible flow polytope is compact. The argument does not depend on a naive augmenting-path algorithm terminating for irrational capacities. The stated max-flow/min-cut theorem is sufficient. For each directed arc, r(u,v)=c(u,v)−f(u,v)+f(v,u) is nonnegative; conservation makes the residual capacity crossing any cut equal its original capacity minus the flow value. This is E(x)−min E, not E(x) plus an untracked normalization constant.

Reverse nonterminal residual arcs remain on an original undirected edge. Terminal residual arcs supply unary entries; reverse terminal arcs which can never cross a source–sink cut add no unaccounted contribution. Exponentiating the negative residual costs yields local entries in (0,1] and a product W with maximum exactly 1. Therefore its finite normalizer lies in [1,2^|V|]. This lower bound is the central protection against a zero product/zero normalizer in the subsequential limit.

There are finitely many local entries. Their compact subsequence has a product limit W*, with sum at least 1, and the original probability limit is W* divided by that sum. Absorbing its finite positive reciprocal into one unary factor gives a normalized finite nonnegative product. Empty V is treated separately. MTP2 passes to the limit through polynomial inequalities. This establishes the reverse inclusion without importing a zero-cell positivity theorem or assuming the conclusion.

### Literal source convention and global Markov property

For E nonempty the literal edge-only product ignores every isolated coordinate. Normalization therefore forces their joint independent uniform law, a property retained in limits. The normalized law on nonisolates has finite unary/edge factors by the theorem. Every unary on that induced subgraph can be absorbed into an incident original edge, and the uniform-isolate scalar can be absorbed into any edge. Signed factors can first be made nonnegative. These steps prove exactly the source's edge-only conclusion, rather than silently redefining M_I.

With E empty and nonempty V, the literal empty product assigns 1 to every cell and is not a probability law; that literal family is empty and closed. If an implicit normalizing scalar is intended, its singleton uniform law is closed instead. V empty gives the singleton mass 1. The manuscript explicitly distinguishes these conventions.

Finite local products are globally Markov: for a separator event having positive probability, fixing its coordinates leaves a product over disconnected remaining components and each component has a finite positive normalizer. Conditional independence has a division-free rank-one polynomial formulation after marginalizing leftover coordinates. A null conditioning event imposes no additional condition. Thus the closedness arguments do not divide by a null marginal, restrict separations to partitions of V, or replace global Markov by only pairwise Markov at zeros.

## Supplement: the additional attributed derivation

I personally read and independently checked the entire facial-lattice support derivation. A nonempty facial support for the original-edge design is the zero set of a finite pairwise energy H≥0, with H≥1 elsewhere. The proof never assumes H is submodular.

For an arbitrary Boolean sublattice, the coordinatewise minimal state containing a given active 1 coordinate defines its implication relations. A state satisfying the pins and implications is the join of the appropriate minimal states with the lattice bottom. This supplies the all-pair description, including repeated coordinate classes.

If an equality class were disconnected in its induced original graph, use two allowed ideals differing exactly on that whole class. Splitting it into two pieces with no cross edge makes the mixed-energy sum equal the zero-energy endpoint sum. Nonnegativity forces both mixed states into the zero set, contradicting equality. If a quotient cover had no original cross edge, the three permitted patterns 00,10,11 with a common exterior would force the forbidden 01 pattern to have zero energy by the same pairwise identity. The chosen exterior really is an ideal: a retained element above the lower cover endpoint would create an intermediate class. Hence every equality class is connected and every cover has an original cross edge.

Original-edge projection cylinders then enforce ties and every cover implication, hence the whole order. Incident edge projections enforce each pinned nonisolate. Since H does not involve isolated coordinates, faciality cannot pin or relate an isolate. This establishes original-edge support feasibility, including zeros and isolates. With E nonempty, the original-edge design meets the GMS hypotheses; the manuscript's separate empty-edge cases avoid applying that design argument when it has no rows.

Continuity of toric binomials, faciality of toric support, MTP2 lattice support and GMS feasible-support factorization give the claimed closure consequence. This is an additional derivation obtained in the audit and expressly described as an attributed extension, not as a theorem literally printed in the earlier papers. The main attractive-approximation proof is independent of it. I found no transferred unsupported central claim or circular use of closure in this derivation.
