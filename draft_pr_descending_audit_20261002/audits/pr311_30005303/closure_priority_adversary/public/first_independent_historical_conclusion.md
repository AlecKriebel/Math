## Verified prior ingredients and exact gaps

- Lauritzen–Uhler–Zwiernik (2021), Theorem 3.2, printed 1438: relative closedness of MTP2 CANONICAL parameters in a regular exponential family. This does not give finite edge factors at zero-probability limits; parameters may diverge. Proposition 3.3, printed 1439, supplies the canonical convex cone; Proposition 3.6, printed 1440, characterizes strictly positive quadratic MTP2 laws by nonnegative off-diagonal interactions.
- Same paper, Section 4.4 printed 1448–1450, defines the compact extended graphical family P2 intersect M_E. Lemma 4.9/Corollary 4.10, printed 1449, show that binary MTP2 support is determined by all pair projections, and full pair support implies full support. Theorem 4.11 and Lemmas 4.12–4.13 prove a full-support MLE condition from edge samples. They do not state that arbitrary incomplete supports are determined by ORIGINAL-edge projections. That missing reduction is the precise boundary gap.
- Fallat et al. (2017), Theorem 7.5, printed 1173, states edgewise MTP2 equivalence under POSITIVE edge potentials. The proof cancels positive factors. It cannot simply be applied to arbitrary zero potentials; an approximation or reparametrization bridge is needed.
- Geiger–Meek–Sturmfels (2006), Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 (PDF pp. 8–9) give finite nonnegative monomial factorization iff toric membership AND A-feasible support, while toric membership alone gives closure membership. Appendix Lemma A.2 (PDF pp.29–30) characterizes facial support. With A equal to original-edge configuration indicators, this precisely reduces Conjecture 1 to ORIGINAL-edge support feasibility of the limit. It does not prove that feasibility merely from MTP2 or a lattice.
- Kolmogorov–Zabih (2004), Theorem 4.1 and Section 4.1, printed 150–151: a finite binary pairwise submodular energy has a graph-cut representation with just the original variable vertices and s,t; the constructed nonterminal edges coincide with its pair terms.
- Picard–Queyranne (1979 technical report), Theorem I, printed report p.4 (PDF p.9): all minimum s–t cuts are precisely closures under positive residual arcs of any maximum flow, with terminals fixed. Combining this with KZ gives a ROUTINE SUPPORT corollary: minimizers of a finite binary quadratic submodular energy on G are described by finitely many original-edge implications and unary terminal pins, hence their indicator factors over original edges plus unary pins. Unary pins can be assigned to incident edges for nonisolated vertices. This support theorem is an old ingredient, not new. But no inspected prior theorem establishes that EVERY Conjecture-1 limit support arises from such an energy, nor that all zero-cell source-literal starting laws admit positive ferromagnetic approximants.

## Hypothesis-preserving partial derivation

For original-edge indicator matrix A, each real factorization of a nonnegative probability p can first be replaced by absolute values, since abs(product psi)=p cellwise. Thus GMS's nonnegative setting loses nothing. A limit p of these products lies in X_A by continuity of its binomials. GMS Theorem 3.1 then yields finite original-edge factors exactly when supp(p) is A-feasible. For this A, A-feasibility says supp(p) equals the intersection of its original-edge projection cylinders. On graphs with isolates, the literal product is constant over the isolated coordinates; these coordinates impose no separate restrictions. The still-unproved bridge is precisely: toric/lattice limits in this family have that original-edge cylinder property.

For an energy H(x)=sum_i h_i(x_i)+sum_{ij in E} h_ij(x_i,x_j) with each h_ij submodular, KZ represents H by a cut cost plus constant on V union {s,t} without auxiliary variable vertices. PQ expresses argmin H by prohibiting residual arcs from a cut's source side to its sink side. A nonterminal residual arc is on an original edge, so its constraint is an edge indicator; a terminal arc is a unary pin. The conjunction of those indicators gives exact finite support factors. This is a routine specialization of prior theorems, but applying it to arbitrary MTP2 limits requires the additional bridge named above.
