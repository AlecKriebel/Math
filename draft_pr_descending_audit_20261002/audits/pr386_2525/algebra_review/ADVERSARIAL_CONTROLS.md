# Adversarial counterexamples and limiting cases

All infinite constructions here test the cost steps or scope of hypotheses. Where a control itself fails CB, it does not refute an unproved strengthened theorem retaining CB; it refutes only the stated shortcut or transfer of evidence. None is a claimed CB/non-MB separator.

1. **One bounded symmetric generating set is insufficient.** In Z, S=N_0∪{−1} is genuinely monoid generating, has symmetric diameter 1 and unbounded positive diameter. Its symmetric core already group generates at level 1. This disproves replacing all-generating-set CB by a statement about the original S.

2. **Inverse representatives are not bounded by index minus one.** In C_{2M}=<a>, H=<a²>, and S={a}, a shortest positive representative of the nontrivial right coset is a, but its inverse positive cost is 2M−1. The index is 2. This gives arbitrarily large inverse cost with a fixed finite index, so the separate c=max l(r_C^{-1}) in directed Schreier transport is essential. The checker uses M=6.

3. **Factor constants cannot be omitted in a nonsplit extension.** In additive G=Z, H=2Z, Q=C2, choose r_0=0,r_1=5 and S={2}∪{−2n:n≥0}. S positively generates H, and l_S(10)=5 because every S letter is≤2. W=S∪{5} positively generates Z (the normal form H+{0,5} covers it) and l_W(10)=2. The factor c(1,1)=10 therefore has to be included: without it, l_S(10)>l_W(10), violating the supposed lower comparison. Adding c gives l_T(10)=1 as required. Here all alpha_q are trivial; the defect still matters.

4. **A finite quotient is not an action.** In Q16=<a,b:a^8=1,b²=a^4,bab^{-1}=a^{-1}>, H=<a²,b>≅Q8 has index 2. With representatives 1,a, c(q,q)=a². The section conjugation alpha_q has alpha_q²=Inn(a²), which sends b to a^4 b≠b; hence it is not a C2-action. The correct twisted-action and cocycle identities are checked independently. The issue can be stronger in infinite groups: in F(a,b), the kernel H of parity of the exponent sum of a has index 2; section conjugation by a has infinite order on H, since a^k b a^{−k} are distinct reduced words. Thus a finite quotient need not even induce a finite automorphism group through the chosen representatives. Only the split semidirect-product part permits ordinary Q-invariant saturation and the iff criteria.

5. **Positive witness saturation can destroy its unboundedness.** In the infinite dihedral group, the genuine positive witness on rotations S={r^n:n≥0}∪{r^{-1}} is unbounded, while inversion saturation is all rotations. Adding one involution t gives exact positive diameter 3; r^{-3} requires three letters by exhaustive two-letter type analysis. The group itself is not CB, as its finite standard symmetric generating set has finite balls and the group is infinite.

6. **Relation length one does not replace bounded conjugator cost.** Let G be the restricted direct sum of S_n for n≥3. In each coordinate let a_n be the n-cycle and include a_n and all adjacent transpositions in S. The set S positively generates G because the involutions generate each finite coordinate and every element has finite coordinate support. Each S letter is conjugate to its inverse: involutions use the identity; a_n uses a reversing permutation h_n. Thus each inverse relation has one conjugate, but the conjugators vary.

   The positive cost of a_n^{-1} is at least ceil(n/2). Indeed a positive word of length k in the coordinate generators can be rewritten as a_n^j times a product of at most k transpositions, 0≤j≤k. If k<n/2, then 1≤j+1<n and a_n^{−(j+1)} moves all n points. A product of at most k transpositions moves at most 2k<n points, contradiction. Projection from the direct sum cannot increase word length, so the same bound holds in G. This rigorously supplies unbounded inverse costs despite one-conjugate inverse relations. A finite set of conjugators in the direct sum has finite total coordinate support and cannot invert all a_n. The group is not CB: elements supported in arbitrarily many coordinates require arbitrarily many S∪S^{-1} letters. Exact S_n controls for n=3,…,7 independently support the algebra, while the support proof establishes all n.

7. **Uniform order bounds need not be a common exponent.** In C6, chosen generators a² and a³ have orders 3 and 2, both≤3, but (a³)^3≠1. The inverse-cost bound uses order≤3, without assuming an exponent-3 identity. Unbounded finite orders are left outside the proof; each individual finite inverse expression gives no uniform R.

8. **Degenerate and cardinality cases.** H=G and quotient Q=1 reduce to the original lengths. H=1 and finite G are harmless. Empty or identity-only S is a genuine generating set only for the trivial group; maxima over empty sets should be set to zero in implementations. The derivations use only finite words, countable exhaustions and finite representatives, so they do not depend on countability of G, topology, measurable sets, or a faithful finite action. Subgroups in the finite-index Schreier proof may be nonnormal. Normality is required precisely for quotient group and extension factor-set formulas.
