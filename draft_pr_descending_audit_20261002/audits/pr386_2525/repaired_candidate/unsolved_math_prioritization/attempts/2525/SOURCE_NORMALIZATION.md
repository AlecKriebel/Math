# Source normalization: Kourovka 21.16 (ID 2525)

The October 2026 maintained 21st edition, printed/PDF page 179, asks whether a group can have finite width as a group and fail finite width as a monoid. Its definition quantifies over **every applicable generating set**, allowing the bound to depend on that set. Group words permit inverses; monoid words do not. We include the empty word for the identity, which has no effect on finiteness. No topology, measurability or cardinality assumption is imposed. The target has no solution marker and no entry in the October updates-only file.

Write CB for finite group width and MB for finite monoid width. Avoid the ambiguous unqualified phrase 'Bergman property': different authors use it for CB or for CB together with uncountable cofinality. MB implies CB by applying MB to the symmetric set S union S^{-1}. Bergman's 2006 paper, Section 3 before Question 9, proves CB plus uncountable cofinality implies MB. Here uncountable cofinality means there is no increasing countable exhaustion by proper subgroups (including the usual finitely generated convention). Thus a separating example must have countable cofinality. An arbitrary unbounded symmetric subadditive length refutes the stronger conjunction, and does not by itself refute CB.

Bergman Question 9 also asks whether any CB group of countable cofinality satisfies MB. The Kourovka target asks only for separation. Do not substitute this second question for the target. Nor does a proper positive submonoid which group-generates G answer the target: it fails the required positive generation of all G.
