# Turn 5: compatible products, algebraic generation and Baire obstructions

## Final direction and outcome

The remaining candidate route tries to assemble increasingly large finite directed diameters in a product or inverse limit. We prove exactly why the most direct constructions either fail genuine positive generation or also fail CB. A compact-category argument excludes all analytic positive witnesses in compact Polish realizations. It is credited to classical Baire/Pettis methods and Rosendal's stronger topological result, not claimed as new. The unrestricted abstract question remains unresolved after this fifth turn.

## 1. Cartesian generating sets: exact formula and a diagonal obstruction

Let G=product_i G_i and let S_i subset G_i contain1 and monoid-generate G_i. Put S=product_i S_i. For every g=(g_i),

    l_S(g)=sup_i l_{S_i}(g_i),                  (1)

allowing infinity. Projection proves the lower bound. If the supremum is a finite integer m, choose an S_i word for each g_i of length at most m, pad it to m with identities, and read its jth letters across coordinates. These m tuples belong to S and multiply to g. This uses the ordinary choice of coordinate words; no topology is needed.

Suppose each D_i=sup_{G_i} l_{S_i} is finite. If sup_i D_i is finite, S positively generates all G with that finite diameter. If the D_i are unbounded, choose a countable sequence of distinct coordinates i_n with D_{i_n}>n, and g_{i_n} with length>n, setting the remaining coordinates to1. Formula(1) gives l_S(g)=infinity. Thus S **does not monoid-generate the full product**. This is not an unbounded finite-valued metric on G.

Therefore a Cartesian product of finite-diameter witnesses cannot separate CB from MB on the full product. An unbounded positive word metric on a direct sum is easier: take the direct sum of nontrivial finite cyclic groups with one positive generator per coordinate. But elements with increasingly many nonzero coordinates have unbounded **symmetric** word length too. That group is not CB. Replacing the direct sum by the full product and replacing the generator set by its Cartesian closure changes the generation question, not just the diameter.

## 2. Closed inverse-limit witnesses

Let G=lim G_n be a profinite group represented by a sequence of finite groups with surjective bonding maps, and let S subset G be closed and contain1. Put S_n=pi_n(S). For every g,

    l_S(g)=sup_n l_{S_n}(pi_n(g)),              (2)

again with infinity allowed. Projection gives one inequality. If all finite-level lengths are at most m, the subsets of the compact space S^m consisting of tuples whose product has projection pi_n(g) are nonempty, closed and nested. Compactness gives a common tuple, whose product equals g because all finite projections agree. This proves(2).

For the same reason, a common finite bound m on the diameters of all (G_n,S_n) gives G=S^m. **Having some finite bound at each level is insufficient.** For example take the additive2-adic integers and S={0,1}. Every quotient Z/2^nZ is positively generated by S_n, with exact diameter2^n-1. The element-1 has these exact finite-level lengths and hence has no finite positive S expression in Z_2. The generated monoid is the nonnegative integers embedded in Z_2, which is dense but not all Z_2. Even group generation by1 is only dense, not algebraic generation of all Z_2.

The inverse-limit formula tracks positive lengths, but it does not turn a merely dense invariant semigroup into a genuine generating set. No group CB claim is inferred from finite quotient boundedness either.

## 3. Compact Baire lemma for genuine positive generation

**Theorem.** Let G be a compact Hausdorff topological group and let S monoid-generate G. Assume every positive ball B_n=(S union {1})^n has the Baire property. Then S has finite positive diameter.

Proof: Compact Hausdorff spaces are Baire. Since G=union_n B_n, some B_n is nonmeagre. Having the Baire property, it is comeagre in a nonempty open U. For every x in U U, the open set U intersect x U^{-1} is nonempty. The sets B_n and x B_n^{-1} are both comeagre there, so their intersection is nonempty. Therefore x=ab for some a,b in B_n, and

    U U subset B_n B_n subset B_{2n}.

Let V=U U, a nonempty open subset. Its left translates cover G. Compactness selects finitely many g_1 V,...,g_t V covering G. Genuine positive generation gives k=max_j l_S(g_j)<infinity. Thus

    G subset union_j g_j B_{2n} subset B_{k+2n}.

This proves the theorem without using CB. The argument uses the product B_n B_n, not an uncontrolled difference set B_n B_n^{-1}.

Applications and precise regularity scope:
- If S is sigma-compact in a compact Hausdorff group, every B_n is a countable union of compact, hence closed, sets and has the Baire property. In particular this covers closed S and completes the obstruction in Section2.
- In a compact Polish group, analytic S has analytic finite products and finite unions, hence all B_n have the Baire property. This uses the classical theorem that analytic sets have the Baire property. Thus every analytic, and in particular every Borel, genuine monoid-generating set has finite positive diameter.
- Merely assuming S itself has the Baire property has not been substituted for the stronger word-ball hypothesis: images under multiplication need not preserve that class by a general closure rule.
- Compactness and algebraic generation are essential. The infinite discrete Z example in turn1 is not compact; the2-adic singleton example above does not algebraically generate G as a monoid.

The analytic application is already covered by Rosendal's stronger topologically2-Bergman theorem. The downloaded author version has Proposition3.9 on page9 (earlier arXiv numbering differs); its full proof and the analytic/Baire definitions were read. We give the simpler sufficient proof above to expose the positive-word issue. No general abstract CB conclusion follows from topological property(OB), which restricts the regularity of the sets or actions considered.

## 4. What the last route actually excludes

A separating witness on an abstract group admitting a compact Polish topology must be nonanalytic in that topology; more generally at least one positive ball must fail the Baire property. A closed compatible family in finite quotients cannot supply it. This is a conditional restriction, not a proof that no abstract separating group exists. The source imposes no topology or definability assumption, and pathological generating sets cannot be discarded.

To build a witness through a countable union or an inverse system, one must separately prove: every element has a finite positive expression; some such finite lengths are arbitrarily large and survive later shortcuts; and **every** symmetric generating set of the resulting group has bounded width. The constructions tested here fail the first or third obligation. Finite-level directed diameters alone establish none of the missing global implications.

## 5. Final controls and stop

The checker verifies the Cartesian maximum-distance formula on exact finite products with all specified factor generating sets, the quotient length comparison and the2-adic residue formula, and growing-support lower bounds in finite direct-sum windows. It does not simulate Baire category, certify analyticity of an arbitrary set or decide CB for an infinite group. Those statements are covered by the written proofs and explicitly cited classical theorem.
