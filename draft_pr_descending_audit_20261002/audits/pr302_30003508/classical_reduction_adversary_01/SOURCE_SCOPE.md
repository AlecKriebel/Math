# Primary-source verification and historical limits

Only the three sources directly cited by the proposed reduction were needed. Their complete retrieved PDF bytes and response headers are retained in `sources`; subprocess download records retain actual child PID, argv, cwd, UTC start/end, full stdout/stderr bytes and exit status. Exact file hashes and versions are in `SOURCE_PINS.json` at freeze. This is a bounded source audit, not a search certifying universal firstness.

1. **Hansen–Scheinkman, 1993 NBER technical working paper 141.** [Primary NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/t0141/t0141.pdf), September 1993 working-paper version, 56 PDF pages. Printed p26 is PDF page28; `hs-page-28.png` was independently rendered, OCRed and visually inspected. Proposition 5.5 identifies self-adjoint generators sharing the same discrete-time conditional expectation operator by injectivity of exponentiation in the spectral representation. This is generator identification, without a noisy-density estimator or an inverse-regularization consistency theorem. The surrounding next page begins scalar diffusion examples. The reduction correctly uses the general operator proposition rather than treating those scalar examples as tensor estimation. Ordinary text extraction exited successfully but produced only 56 form-feed bytes; that limitation is preserved and not passed off as readable source text. Web-tool opening NBER failed, while direct HTTPS retrieval returned the genuine PDF successfully.

2. **Lenzen–Scherzer, ECCOMAS 2004.** [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://franklenzen.de/pdf/lenzen_scherzer_eccomas2004.pdf), 21 pages. Section 3 introduces continuous weakly closed nonlinear maps between Hilbert spaces. Theorem 3.2 on printed p6 (PDF page6) gives quadratic Tikhonov subsequential convergence under vanishing alpha and noise-squared/alpha, with uniqueness giving full convergence. It credits the earlier nonlinear-regularization literature; it does not state this diffusion application. Its data-index wording is compressed, and it treats exact minimizers. Our direct proof supplies the constrained-domain, positive approximate-minimizer-error and measurability adaptations rather than silently asserting the printed theorem covers them verbatim. The review's bibliography verifies the named 1989 works and 1996 book as references; those original sources were not downloaded or independently theorem-checked here. The 2004 statement alone establishes the pre-PR availability of the abstract mechanism.

3. **Bruce Hansen, Econometric Theory 2008, 24, 726–748.** [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~behansen/papers/et_08.pdf), DOI 10.1017/S0266466608080304, published-paper version, 23 pages. Theorem 3 is the centered almost-sure uniform kernel-average result, not the separate smooth-density bias theorem. Assumption 2 allows joint pair densities to fail at finitely many short lags; the paragraph on printed p729 explicitly discusses overlapping lag vectors. The stationary observed pair process fits this structure: marginal density q is bounded; for l>=2 the four-state Lebesgue density is mu(x0)p_Delta(x0,x1)p_{(l-1)Delta}(x1,xl)p_Delta(xl,xl+1), uniformly bounded using the fixed-time heat bound and the semigroup property. Spectral-gap conditioning bounds the strong mixing coefficient of the pair process by a constant times rho^{l-1}, so every finite polynomial mixing exponent is available. Bounded response one supplies all moments. A smooth compact kernel satisfies Assumptions 1 and 3. The log-slow bandwidth makes the positive-power-of-n bandwidth conditions hold; a fixed compact set satisfies the region condition. This checks the published stochastic ingredient. No global smoothness of the zero-extended q is needed by the centered theorem. Our elementary covariance/Borel-Cantelli proof is independently sufficient and gives its own L^2 rate, so the main theorem does not depend on borrowing a bias hypothesis from this source.

The priority inference must remain at the right level. A correct application now shows that the weak existence problem follows by combining established nonlinear inverse regularization with fixed-lag reversible identification and density estimation. That substantially weakens an unrestricted novelty assertion about the *existence of some measurable consistent estimator*, and warrants saying exactly what new estimator or theorem is contributed. It does not establish that an earlier publication actually wrote down and proved this full unknown-density tensor-diffusion application. Therefore it is not, alone, a decisive refutation of a literal chronological claim of first publication of the application. None of these inspected sources proves convergence of the submitted finite-rank empirical spectral-equation estimator. That theorem still requires its own mathematical audit.
