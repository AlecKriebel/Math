## Adversarial analytic reconstruction

The complete derivation is retained separately. The following are the principal possible failure points I reconstructed and attempted to falsify.

1. **Reflection and density conventions.** Integration by parts yields the stated conormal generator `L=mu^-1 div(S grad)`, covariance `2S/mu`, and drift `divS/mu`. Symmetric anisotropic tensors do not justify replacing this reflection law by ordinary normal reflection. The theorem does not make that replacement. The weighted Poincare calculation supplies a strictly positive population spectral gap without requiring the estimator to know its numerical value.

2. **Boundary correction and empirical derivative consistency.** The explicit finite reflection extension matches derivatives through order6; differentiation of the mollifier supplies all required kernel bounds without incorrectly claiming that this extension is infinitely differentiable across the seam. The signed extension kernel need not preserve positivity or mass. The smooth density clipping/normalization ensures a deterministic positive denominator and eventually becomes inactive, including truths touching their pointwise bounds. The new boundary-sample convention makes the construction Borel for every data tuple and has probability-zero effect under the stationary model.

3. **Overlapping pairs.** Conditioning gives covariance `\u27e8r,P^(a-1)s\u27e9` for pair observations at separation `a`, including `a=1`; it is not a calculation for independent pairs or a `rho^a` bound. With the dyadic sample schedule and explicit bandwidth, derivative variance, net cardinality, the summable Chebyshev bounds, and Lipschitz interpolation all close. The product-net spacing is `2^(-j/(4d))`. One probability-one event handles all finitely many derivative indices; the dyadic prefix covers all sample sizes. Compactness and the fixed collar avoid a hidden convex-domain assumption.

4. **Population regularity versus empirical eigenvectors.** Smooth conormal elliptic graph regularity, polynomial eigenvalue counting, and the exponential positive-time factor justify the population kernel and its differentiated spectral series. No convergence of individual empirical eigenvectors, simple spectrum, fixed spectral gap between empirical modes, or empirical positivity is assumed.

5. **Continuous weighted functional calculus.** The complete finite-rank positive empirical spectral sum is exactly represented by continuous functions `g(t)=t^2` and `h(t)=t^2 log(t)` on positive t, extended by zero on nonpositive t. Both are continuous at zero. The outside evaluation factors supply the other two powers, producing the required fourth-power weights. Continuous functional calculus gives the normal matrix and linear-term limits without eigenvector alignment, without commuting operators, and despite crossings, multiplicity, negative modes, zero modes, or finite-sample eigenvalues exceeding1. No logarithm is applied to a nonpositive empirical mode.

6. **Full jet excitation.** An annihilator of the positive weighted population normal matrix annihilates every population eigenfunction jet. Interior compactly supported smooth functions lie in every generator power domain; graph convergence plus sufficiently high Sobolev embedding transfers annihilation to their full jets. Cutoff affine and quadratic functions realize arbitrary gradient and symmetric Hessian. Consequently the normal matrix is positive definite pointwise in the interior, with a strictly positive truth-dependent bound on each interior compact. This neither assumes a predetermined finite exciting family nor claims a uniform bound at the reflecting boundary.

7. **Ridge and global conclusion.** Population excitation bounds the inverse eventually on each compact, so any deterministic positive vanishing ridge works; no unproved error-to-ridge rate is needed. Projection onto the prescribed tensor ellipticity interval is nonexpansive and bounded. Interior pointwise convergence and dominated convergence yield the global projected L2 result. The fitted divergence is separately estimated; the proof does not silently identify it with differentiation of the fitted tensor.
