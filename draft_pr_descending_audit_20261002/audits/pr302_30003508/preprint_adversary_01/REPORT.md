## Reconstructed original question and strongest conclusion

Reiss's complete contribution occupies printed pages 1507–1509 in OWR24/2017 and is joint work with Chorowski, Gobet and Hoffmann. The official report/event year is 2017; the report was published 28 April 2018. The final multidimensional passage assumes stationarity on a bounded domain with reflecting boundary and divergence form with respect to the invariant density. It gives Σ=2S/μ and β=(div S)/μ, then asks for convergence of reconstruction from empirical generator eigenpairs and μ with K to be determined. “Empirical (noisy)” does not define additive sensor noise. The next contribution by Dunker and Hohage concerns different observations; its maximum-likelihood regularization claims are not silently attributed to Reiss or substituted for the spectral question.

The candidate supplies a definite estimator in a smooth reversible model: known bounded connected C∞ domain in d≥2, unknown smooth symmetric tensor and smooth positive normalized invariant density, known pointwise ellipticity and density bounds, known fixed positive lag, stationary exact positions, and conormal reflection. Its all-positive-mode finite-rank fit, kappa^4 weights and vanishing ridge are concrete choices for the originally unspecified spectral retention. The proved result is almost-sure local uniform recovery of S and a separately fitted div S, C² recovery of μ up to the boundary, and global L² recovery of the clipped tensor. Dyadic prefix reuse covers all sample sizes. Covariance and interior drift then converge locally uniformly with the source's correct factor two and density normalization.

This is a scoped answer to the original final spectral question. It does not solve an unrestricted rough, nonreversible or sensor-noise problem; it supplies no convergence rate, minimax theorem, class-uniform conditioning bound, efficient algorithm, quadrature/eigensolver accuracy result, boundary-uniform tensor/divergence recovery, or convergence of derivatives of the fitted tensor. ## Separate classical alternative: independent validation and priority limit

I read both bundled classical notes in full and reconstructed their application independently. The H^s admissible parameter set is closed, convex and weakly closed; strict s>d/2+2 gives compact C² and C¹ embeddings, with the conventional Hölder range clarified below. Smooth truth has finite unknown norm and no known-radius oracle is used. Weak formulations define the forward semigroup throughout the H^s class. Conjugation to L²(dx) gives a common H¹ form domain, so moving conormal operator domains do not invalidate the explicit Lax–Milgram norm-resolvent comparison.

A continuous resolvent-to-heat function gives operator norm semigroup convergence. The independently checked uniform min-max heat singular-value tail supplies the indispensable upgrade to Hilbert–Schmidt convergence and joint-kernel L² continuity. Equal joint marginals recover μ; unbounded self-adjoint logarithmic calculus recovers the generator even with zero as a spectral accumulation point; cutoff quadratic tests then recover the full tensor. This is identification on the entire admissible class, not merely on a known compact radius.

The ordinary zero-extended KDE is not uniformly consistent at the boundary. The supplement uses the weaker L² statement correctly: finite perimeter of D×D and smooth truth give O(√h) bias, while the dependent-pair variance and dyadic schedule give a summable stochastic bound. At h_j proportional to (j+1)^{-4}, δ_j=O((j+1)^{-1}) almost surely. Countable dense feasible candidates and first epsilon-approximate minimization provide actual Borel estimators. With alpha_j=(j+1)^{-1} and epsilon_j=(j+1)^{-2}, both requisite ratios tend to zero; truth comparison bounds the unknown Sobolev norm of the estimates, compact subsequences and injectivity give C¹ convergence.

That application is valid. It is a new derivation during the audit using older identification and regularization ingredients, not an earlier published theorem for this exact diffusion reduction and not consistency of the spectral-equation objective. Lenzen–Scherzer Theorem 3.2 supports the older nonlinear Hilbert regularization mechanism, while the diffusion forward-map checks still had to be supplied. Hansen 2008 is a genuine earlier mixing kernel theorem; its large-lag joint-density condition permits overlapping lag vectors. The included elementary covariance proof makes the classical application self-contained even if that attribution were removed. The candidate correctly avoids claiming the first possible consistent tensor estimator.
