## What is actually claimed and proved by the candidate

TURN_1.md and TURN_2.md assert pointwise-in-model, almost-sure consistency for each fixed smooth symmetric uniformly elliptic tensor S and smooth positive normalized invariant density μ on a known connected smooth bounded domain D in dimension d≥2. The numerical bounds `ell, Lambda, c, C` and lag Δ>0 are known. The diffusion is stationary with generator `μ^(-1)div(S∇)` and natural conormal reflecting boundary `n·S∇u=0`. The observations are precisely `X_0,...,X_(NΔ)`, without additional measurement error. Both S and μ remain unknown functions.

The estimator converges uniformly on each compact interior set for S and separately fitted div S. Projection of the tensor onto the known closed ellipticity interval gives almost-sure L²(D) convergence. There is no globally uniform boundary statement, uniform minimax rate, finite-sample conditioning guarantee, known true eigenpair assumption, efficient numerical algorithm claim, or quadrature/eigensolver error theorem.

This is a genuine nonparametric model: the prior bounds do not fix coefficient values, smoothness norms, finite expansion length, density, or eigenfunctions. Smoothness constants and the true spectral gap may be arbitrarily large/small across the class. The convergence event and conditioning constants belong to each fixed parameter pair; no simultaneous probability-one event over an uncountable model class is claimed.

## Observability audit: no true spectral oracle

The empirical functions `k_i(x)=K_h(x,Y_i)` are determined by the observed positions, a deterministic known-domain boundary atlas, and the bandwidth. The invariant-density estimate uses the same `Y_i`, a fixed known clipping map ψ, and its normalization integral. Symmetrized consecutive-pair kernels retain time ordering of neighbors; they are not computed from the stationary marginal alone.

For a sample with `n+1` states, let `v_i=k_i/sqrt(μ_j)` and let V map coefficient vectors to the span of these functions in L²(dx). With B the symmetric weighted adjacency matrix, the empirical conjugated operator is exactly `T_j=V B V*`. Its Gram matrix `G=V*V` consists of integrals of fully observed functions over known D. Thus its nonzero spectral problem can be evaluated in a space of dimension at most n+1, using the symmetric matrix `G^(1/2) B G^(1/2)` on the range of G. If G is singular, restriction to its range removes the null directions; the theorem needs no feature-independence assumption. Nonzero eigenfunctions lie in the smooth empirical feature span. Their derivatives are derivatives of known smooth functions, not estimates of derivatives of a separately supplied unknown eigenfunction.

I independently checked a nontrivial rational Gram-matrix instance: the integral operator on three features agrees exactly with the finite coefficient action `BG`; the pair matrix is symmetric and can be indefinite. This is an algebraic observability check, not a statistical test. Positivity of the finite kernel is correctly unnecessary: the candidate discards negative eigenvalues through continuous positive-part operator functions. Empirical eigenvalues above one and positive logarithms can also occur; these are well-defined finite-sample quantities, and convergence does not require every intermediate operator to be a Markov operator.

In the infinite limit, every true transition eigenvalue is positive. For any fixed number r of true modes, operator-norm convergence implies that the empirical operator is eventually positive on their r-dimensional span: the norm error becomes smaller than half the least true eigenvalue on that span. Hence the number of positive empirical eigenvalues must tend to infinity. Its increase is a consequence, not an assumed spectral observability property.

The original unknown density is not bypassed by using a density-conditioned experiment. `μ_j` is constructed from the same sample, converges uniformly and in C², and is eventually an inactive smooth modification followed by normalization. The limiting density is the actual normalized μ. The pair density tends to the reversible symmetric q; its temporary lack of matching empirical marginals does not affect the stated deterministic hypotheses.

## Proof-mechanism assessment relevant to scope

The two turns close the conditionality explicitly acknowledged at the end of TURN_1. TURN_1 alone does not resolve the question; TURN_2 supplies its empirical assumptions from the actual fixed-lag trajectory.

1. **Identification.** The fitted jet lists all independent second derivatives, with twice each mixed derivative, and all first derivatives. Their coefficients are S and div S. Every true eigenfunction satisfies the fitted PDE. If a coefficient direction annihilates all eigenfunction jets at an interior point, graph-norm convergence of the spectral expansion of any compactly supported smooth test function, followed by interior Sobolev embedding, makes it annihilate every test-function jet. Cut-off affine and quadratic functions span those jets. No genericity, unverified finite-mode rank, or true eigenfunction design is assumed. Boundary conormal restrictions do prevent the same jet argument right at the boundary; the theorem appropriately restricts local uniform convergence to the interior.
2. **Stability.** Smoothing at fixed positive Δ turns the differentiated kernel evaluation functionals into bounded maps. The spectral weights are powers κ⁴; with the two outer smoothing factors, the middle continuous functions are `g(t)=t_+²` and `h(t)=t_+² log(t_+)`, defined as zero at and below zero. Both extend continuously through zero. Continuous functional calculus for self-adjoint operators gives basis-independent limiting normal equations. The positive limit jet Gram matrix has a positive minimum eigenvalue on every compact interior set. Ridge therefore only needs to tend to zero; no hidden noise/ridge rate or uniform global conditioning bound is required.
3. **Smoothing construction.** The reflected finite-order extension has the displayed exact derivative-matching coefficients. Smooth chart cutoffs and mollification yield known smooth kernel sections; higher derivatives of the mollified kernel need not require infinite-order regularity of the extension itself. True coefficients and fixed positive-time heat kernel are smooth under the stated conormal elliptic model. No unrestricted nonsmooth heat-kernel theorem is inferred.
4. **Dependence.** The stationary reversible chain has a genuine spectral gap. For overlapping pair statistics, conditioning on the intervening endpoint gives covariance decay with `P^(l−1)`, not an independence assertion. The spectral-gap constant is allowed to depend on the true model; the chosen sample schedule need not know it.
5. **Almost-sure derivative convergence.** The bandwidth decreases only polynomially in j while the dyadic sample size grows exponentially. Sup-norm derivative bounds grow polynomially. A deterministic net in dimension2d has cardinality of order `2^(j/2)`, leaving a summable exponential tail after Chebyshev and the union bound. One additional derivative controls interpolation even on nonconvex domains, since the kernels exist in a fixed neighborhood. The same argument treats μ. This proves the strong derivative assumptions without knowing the true smoothness norms.
6. **All sample sizes and global loss.** The estimator reuses the latest dyadic prefix and never reads beyond N. As N grows, j grows; convergence is along all sample sizes. The projected tensor error is uniformly bounded on the finite-volume domain, and local convergence on a countable compact exhaustion gives almost-everywhere convergence. Bounded dominated convergence proves L² consistency. This is not a derivative estimate for the projected field, or uniform convergence at the reflecting boundary.

I found no transfer of the central difficulty to an assumed eigenpair estimator, unproved generic excitation hypothesis, independence of pairs, or unknown ground-truth truncation. The infinite elliptic and probability steps remain analytical arguments; the finite controls are deliberately not presented as their proof.

## Adversarial boundary witnesses and their interpretation

### A single fixed eigenpair is insufficient

In dimension2 let u be any smooth nonconstant eigenfunction of a smooth conormal model, choose a nonnegative smooth interior cutoff ψ, and set `v=(-∂_y u,∂_x u)`. The symmetric tensor perturbation `A=ψ vvᵀ` satisfies `A∇u=0` identically. Therefore `S_ε=S+εA` has the same PDE for u and the same boundary flux, while changing the tensor wherever ψ and ∇u are nonzero. For ε≥0 it preserves positive definiteness. With μ fixed, it leaves the same eigenvalue/eigenfunction pair. If u is a first nonconstant eigenfunction, a positive form perturbation cannot lower the first positive eigenvalue, and the unchanged u attains its old Rayleigh quotient, so that eigenpair can remain first as well. This exact witness rules out treating one fixed empirical mode as enough to identify every tensor. It does not defeat the candidate's increasing full positive spectrum and complete-jet proof. The flux and quadratic-form identities were checked symbolically for an arbitrary differentiable u, not only a polynomial example.

### Ordinary normal reflection is different

On the unit disk take constant `S=[[2,1],[1,2]]` and `g(x,y)=xy(2-x²-y²)`. On the boundary, `∂_n g=0`, while the conormal flux `n·S∇g=cos²(2θ)`. Its integral is π, and direct integration of `div(S∇g)` over the disk gives the same π. Thus the uniform density is not invariant for this operator when its domain uses ordinary normal Neumann boundary, despite the vanishing ordinary normal derivative. The tensor is smooth positive definite, with eigenvalues1 and3. This witness makes the boundary qualifier substantive: the symmetric divergence model requires the compatible conormal condition. The submitted theorem states that condition correctly; the source's asserted self-adjoint divergence model must be interpreted compatibly.

### Disconnected stationary sampling need not observe the whole field

If a bounded open set has two disjoint components, a stationary reflected path stays in its initially selected component. Choose two coefficient models that agree on one component but differ on the other, with the same positive stationary density. The event of starting in the common component has positive probability and has identical conditional data laws under both models. One estimator cannot converge almost surely to both different global tensors on that identical experiment. Connectedness/ergodicity is therefore essential for a whole-domain result from one path. This is outside the candidate's class and outside the usual connected meaning of domain; it prevents casual extension to arbitrary bounded sets.

Additional boundaries are equally explicit: removing known Δ permits time-scale confounding; invariant density alone does not determine diffusivity; unknown sensor error requires another observation model; nonreversible dynamics do not supply the same real self-adjoint logarithmic calculus. None of those substitutions is made in TURN_1 or TURN_2.
