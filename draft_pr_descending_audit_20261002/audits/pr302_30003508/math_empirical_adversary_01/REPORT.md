## 1. Exact theorem and assumptions

The observations are `Y_i=X_(i Delta)` for a known fixed `Delta>0`, from the stationary diffusion generated by

`L f = mu^(-1) div(S grad f)`, with `n dot S grad f = 0`.

The domain is known, bounded, connected and C-infinity; `d>=2`. Both the symmetric tensor S and probability density mu are C-infinity on the closure. Known constants satisfy `ell I <= S <= Lambda I`, `0<c<=mu<=C`, and `integral_D mu=1`. These are model/class assumptions. The boundary condition is **conormal**. An anisotropic diffusion with ordinary normal reflection cannot be silently substituted. The observations contain sampling error but no added sensor noise. Stationarity is explicitly used in the expectation and covariance arguments.

The conclusion is pointwise-in-model, fixed-lag, almost-sure consistency: the spectral estimate of S and its separately fitted divergence converges uniformly on each compact interior set, and the tensor clipped into the known ellipticity interval converges in Lebesgue L2(D). There is no uniform rate over all smooth models, no minimax result, and no guarantee for an approximate quadrature or numerical eigensolver. The proof does not claim a finite prescribed K identifies every tensor. It uses all positive empirical modes, whose number is finite and can grow.

The source's multidimensional paragraph was independently checked in the [official original workshop report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46692?nt=1), printed p.1509. It asks for convergence of tensor reconstruction from empirical spectral data and an unknown invariant density at fixed sampling lag. The actual smooth-class construction is a meaningful version of that request; whether its explicitly added class assumptions justify an unrestricted "solved" label belongs to the separate source-scope adjudication.

## 2. Independent overlap and variance derivation

For a fixed bounded real F on consecutive states, put

`Z_i = F(Y_i,Y_(i+1)) - E F`.

Define `s(z)=E[Z_0 | Y_0=z]` and `r(z)=E[Z_0 | Y_1=z]`. Stationarity makes both centered under mu; conditional expectation gives `||r||_2,||s||_2 <= sqrt(Var(F))`.

For lag l>=1, condition successively on Y_l and Y_1 and use the Markov property:

`E[Z_l | Y_1] = P^(l-1)s(Y_1)`,

`Cov(Z_0,Z_l) = E[r(Y_1) P^(l-1)s(Y_1)]`.

This derivation includes l=1: P^0 is the identity and the common state Y_1 is retained. It does not suppose pair independence or that F is symmetric. Conditioning backwards merely defines r; reversibility is not needed for this identity itself.

For centered f, the reversible diffusion's spectral gap gives `||P f||_2 <= rho||f||_2`, `0<=rho<1`. Therefore

`|Cov(Z_0,Z_l)| <= rho^(l-1) Var(F)`.

Consequently

`Var(n^(-1) sum Z_i) <= (Var(F)/n) [1+2 sum_(l>=1) rho^(l-1)]`

`<= [1+2/(1-rho)] ||F||_infinity^2 / n`.

The final replacement is legitimate because `Var(F)<=E F^2<=||F||_infinity^2`. A bounded state function similarly has l-step covariance bounded by `rho^l Var(F)`. The constants depend on the true model but do not depend on the kernel bandwidth or evaluation location. At each j the test function is deterministic, since the bandwidth and domain kernel were fixed in advance; density normalization is performed after the raw averages, so it does not insert a random denominator into this variance calculation.

The gap follows from the Dirichlet form, not a statistical assumption. Let C_D be a Lebesgue Poincare constant. For mu-centered f, its mu variance is no greater than its squared mu distance to its Lebesgue mean. Hence `||f||_(L2(mu))^2 <= C C_D integral |grad f|^2 <= (C C_D/ell) E(f,f)`. Thus the gap is at least `ell/(C C_D)>0`, and one can take `rho=exp(-Delta*gap)`. Connectedness matters. Constants may deteriorate for a narrow neck or small ellipticity, but the schedule need not know them.

Independent exact controls cover eight reversible two-state chains, stationary laws (1/2,1/2) and (1/3,2/3), and gaps with rho=0,1/2,99/100,999/1000. For all 16 sign-valued pair functions, they check centering, conditional-expectation contraction, the literal three-state overlap at lag one, the direct four-endpoint formula for lags 1–8 and the complete finite-sample variance for sample sizes 1–9. These are finite controls; the displayed analytic derivation proves the general statement.

An adversarial negative control illustrates the importance of the shared endpoint. For independent Bernoulli(1/2) Y_i, F(a,b)=a+b has pair variance 1/2 but consecutive covariance 1/4. The exact variance of the n-pair mean is `1/n - 1/(2n^2)`, not the pair-independence value `1/(2n)`. The candidate uses the former mechanism correctly.

## 3. Boundary extension and signed kernels

## 4. Positivity, normalization, finite rank and expectation

`c*vol(D)/2 <= Z_j <= 2C*vol(D)`,

`Pr(max_net |derivative_error|>(j+1)^(-1)) <= C(j+1)^(4d+10)2^(-j/2)`.

`f_i(x)=K_h(x,Y_i)/sqrt(mu_j(x))`, `V c=sum_i c_i f_i`, `G=V*V`.

Let C_j be the symmetric adjacency matrix described above and `H_j=G^(1/2) C_j G^(1/2)`. Then `T_j=V C_j V*`. Write `v_i(x)=K_h(x,Y_i)/mu_j(x)` and let D_j(x) be the m-by-r matrix with columns `J v_i(x)`. Thus

The polar decomposition of V yields, for either continuous function g or h from TURN_1 (both vanish at zero),

`V* g(T_j) V = G^(1/2) g(H_j) G^(1/2)`.

The same identity holds with h. Therefore

`A_j=D_j C_j G^(1/2) g(H_j) G^(1/2) C_j D_j^T`,

## 9. Boundary and limiting cases challenged

- Arbitrarily slow mixing: rho<1 can approach one. The model-dependent constant grows, but exponential-in-j sample size dominates every fixed constant; no known rho is needed.
- Disconnected domains: the Poincare gap for global centered functions can fail. This is excluded by connectedness; no extension to disconnected domains is claimed.
- Delta=0 or Delta tending to zero: fixed positive Delta is essential for smoothing and the current variance constants. These regimes are outside the theorem.
- Density approaching zero: c>0 is assumed. A density with zeros cannot satisfy the multiplier/normalization estimates; it is excluded rather than covertly covered.
- Negative kernels or empirical eigenvalues: both occur and are handled explicitly. The Gram representation remains valid at exact zero eigenvalues and dependent features.
- Reflecting boundary: no spatial derivative uniformity of the final inverse is claimed at the boundary; kernel/density estimates themselves are boundary corrected. Conormal rather than ordinary normal reflection is retained.
- Nonconvex geometry: the ambient-neighborhood interpolation is sufficient; no convexity is assumed.
- Coincident observations, rank loss, finite samples: normalization is positive and ridge inversion is unique. No statistical assumption makes these exceptional cases undefined.
- Empirical eigenspaces need not converge individually. The continuous functional-calculus formulation handles multiplicities and spectrum crossing.
