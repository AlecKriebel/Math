## Mechanism audit and manuscript requirements

The newness cannot be the idea of estimating a semigroup spectrum, taking logarithms, or fitting drift/tensor coefficients to elliptic eigen-equations. Nor can it be nonparametric spectral residual fitting as a general algorithm: 2006 already proposes that. Eigenvalue-power weights are also prior in 2011. These sources should be cited prominently, including 2006 rather than only the later finite-expansion paper.

The full-jet span argument uses an established spectral approximation mechanism: approximate compactly supported test functions in sufficiently strong graph/Sobolev norms and transfer their derivatives to finite eigenfunction spans. ABJN 2025 Lemma 3.8 is a close explicit predecessor for gradients; its own text credits earlier work of Alberti. Adding affine/quadratic test jets is a generalization of that mechanism, not a basis for claiming that finite spectral excitation was first discovered here. The scalar proof subsequently uses a sign/absolute-value coercivity identity; that identity does not, by itself, yield coercivity for all independent entries of an arbitrary signed matrix perturbation.

The defensible proposed contribution is the **completed almost-sure empirical spectral reconstruction theorem for the precise smooth full-tensor/unknown-density reflected fixed-lag model**: full-jet identification, stable multiplicity-independent weighted operator expressions, and a specified empirical derivative-kernel construction combined to close the statistical limit. These are a description of the candidate's accepted theorem, not a claim that each ingredient is historically new. ROOT must reconcile any other priority family evidence before choosing a novelty statement. The paper should say that it supplies convergence analysis for the stated smooth setting of the OWR question, disclose what assumptions are strengthened, and separate existence/consistency from computational efficiency and numerical implementation.
