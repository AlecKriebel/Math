## Nonzero I(G,3): explicit ideal membership

Let T=(T_ij) be the 16 edge statistics. det(B) is exactly the 4-by-4 minor of S with row set L and column set R. It is therefore an explicit generator of the all-4-minor ideal. For columns T_j,B_j,

 det(T)−det(B)=sum_(j=1)^4 det(T_1,...,T_(j−1),T_j−B_j,B_(j+1),...,B_4).

Expanding each summand in its j-th column writes the difference as a polynomial combination of the 16 relations T_ij−B_ij. Thus det(T) is in J(G,3) and lies in R[t], so it belongs to the actual elimination ideal. It is nonzero as a polynomial because det(I_4)=1. This argument is identical after the diagonal color tie. It does not claim that observed minor is principal: its disjoint row/column sets are essential and allowed.

## All-output derivative right inverse

Let U=I_4 and V_j=e_j+e_(j+1), cyclic indices modulo 4 (V_4=e_4+e_1). For X=[U;V], Phi(X) consists of left row squared norms, right row squared norms, and UV^T. X has rank 4 because of U. Prescribe arbitrary output differentials (a,c,H), with a,c in R^4 and H a 4-by-4 matrix. Define D by

 D_ii=a_i/2,
 D_(j,j+1)=(VH)_jj−(a_j+a_(j+1)+c_j)/2,
 D_ij=0 for all remaining off-diagonal positions,

and define E=H^T−VD^T. The four ordered cyclic positions (1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,1) have four different unordered supports, so their reverse entries vanish and none interfere. These are a linear function R(a,c,H)=[D;E].

The cross derivative is DV^T+E^T=H. Left squared-norm derivatives are 2D_ii=a_i. For right row j,

 2<v_j,E_j>=2(VH)_jj−2 v_j D v_j^T
 =2(VH)_jj−a_j−a_(j+1)−2D_(j,j+1)=c_j.

The scalar v_j D^T v_j^T equals v_j D v_j^T, which is why either transpose placement in this scalar gives the same result; E itself must use D^T. Every one of the 24 prescribed outputs is attained, so dPhi_X R is the 24-by-24 identity. The kernel dimension in the 32-dimensional data space is therefore 8. Orthogonal right rotations give a 6-dimensional kernel, leaving two more directions; there is no dimension contradiction with the rank-4 symmetric stratum dimension 26.

## Exact elimination ideal zero, without radicality or topology

Define theta:R[s,t]→R[x] by S↦XX^T and t↦pi_G(XX^T). Each statistic relation is killed. Each 5-minor is killed, since XX^T factors through four columns (or by Cauchy–Binet with an impossible 5-subset). Hence theta(J(G,4))=0, and every f in I(G,4) obeys f(Phi(X))=0 as a polynomial, a direction requiring no Nullstellensatz, prime ideal, radicality, or closure-equals-image claim.

For a direct injectivity proof, set t0=Phi(X0) and X(Y)=X0+R(Y). Since dPhi R=identity and Phi is quadratic,

 Phi(X(Y))=t0+Y+Q(Y),

where Q is homogeneous quadratic. If f is a nonzero polynomial, translate it to f(t0+Z) and take its lowest nonzero homogeneous component f_d(Z). Substitution Z=Y+Q(Y) gives lowest homogeneous component f_d(Y), which remains nonzero. Thus f(Phi(X(Y))) cannot be the zero polynomial. This contradicts f in I(G,4), and proves I(G,4)=(0) as an exact ideal.

This proof works over R and C with the same rational formulas. Alternatively, the real inverse function theorem gives a nonempty Euclidean open image and a real polynomial vanishing on that open set is zero. Complex coefficients likewise vanish coefficientwise on that real open set. Neither alternative silently assumes the elimination ideal is radical.

For the tied model let P:R^24→R^23 sum the first two coordinates and retain the others. It has an explicit section L that assigns tied target z to a_1=z,a_2=0. Then P dPhi R L=identity_23. Apply the identical lowest-degree argument to P Phi; its exact rank-4 elimination ideal is zero.

## Boundary and falsification controls

V=I+cyclic_shift is singular (rank 3), but U=I ensures rank(X)=4 and every formula above remains valid. The proof divides only by 2, never by det(V), det(B), an output coordinate, or a row norm. At X=0 the differential is zero; at U=V=I the map's differential is not onto. These do not invalidate the existence of the exhibited full-rank point. They show why dimension counting or an arbitrary point cannot replace the explicit right inverse.

The determinant obstruction for rank 3 is not a statement that it vanishes for every rank-4 Gram matrix: choosing U=V=I makes B=I and det(B)=1. Thus the relevant nonzero polynomial cannot accidentally be zero on the entire rank-4 image. More generally all ranks <=3 obey it, including zero rows and negative or complex symmetric inputs. The all-minor ideal remains the one under review; replacing it by principal minors is neither assumed nor needed.
