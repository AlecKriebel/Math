## Exact claim subjected to the priority test

The numerator is the ordered signed shoelace area of intersections of consecutive lines through the *original* billiard vertices perpendicular to their rays from an original focus. The denominator is the ordered signed area of the perpendicular feet from that same focus onto the original orbit's side lines. The target is constancy of their quotient, at either focus, throughout each family with primitive least period N congruent to 2 modulo 4, including all admitted coprime star windings and a strict noncircular, nondegenerate, nested confocal elliptical caustic. Positive pedal denominator and finite antipedal intersections are part of the accepted candidate's theorem. A zero numerator is allowed.

The following are insufficient precedents by themselves: equality of the two focal areas, constancy of an area *product* at N divisible by four, a statement about the outer or caustic polygon, a focus-inversive perimeter, a centroid statement, a triangle formula, or a single low-period observation. I also looked for the two components A_F^*/A and A_F/A separately, because an earlier pair of applicable proved components would genuinely cover the quotient.

### Polarity challenge checked at the equations

For a focus F and u=P-F, the actual antipedal line is

    u · (X-F) = |u|².

A fixed-circle polar of P instead has right-hand side rho². These are different across the vertices of a noncircular focal ellipse: for semiaxes 5,3, F=(4,0), the vertices (5,0) and (0,3) have squared focal distances 1 and 25. No single rho² makes both lines agree. The actual antipedal is the fixed-circle polar of the *inverted* original vertices, rather than the bicentric polar of the original vertices. Thus the classical bicentric theorem cannot be applied through a mistaken identification. This elementary distinction is reproducible in construction_checks.py. It is not a counterexample to k404.

Its Sturm/Steiner fixed-polygon area isocircles do not supply constancy under a varying billiard polygon with a fixed focus.
