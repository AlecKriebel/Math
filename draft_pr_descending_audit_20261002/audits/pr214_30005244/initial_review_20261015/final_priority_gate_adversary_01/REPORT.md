## Claim and source scope checked directly

The positive theorem concerns fixed complex frequency, bounded measurable particles, translated cubic center-membership grids, and condition (G), `|Omega_h symmetric_difference Omega| -> 0`. The matrix is precisely the omitted-diagonal point-sampled matrix `h^3 K_kappa(x_m-x_n)`. The continuum comparison is `S_kappa=A_kappa-I/3`. The excluded interval is the **exact** numerical range of the static infinite lattice operator. The theorem supplies uniform resolvent bounds on compact subsets of the continuum resolvent set off that interval, absence of pollution there, isolated eigenvalue approximation with algebraic multiplicity, and norm convergence of embedded Riesz projections. It includes every bounded Lipschitz particle and every bounded particle with boundary of volume zero. It claims no spectral correctness inside the lattice interval, no rate, no high-frequency limit, and no necessary-and-sufficient geometry classification.

The complete relevant [OWR contribution](https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2022/43), pp. 2580–2582, was read in the original full report, and p. 2582 was checked visually. Section 4 asks for a description and proof of the observed discrete nonreal eigenvalues away from the lattice accumulation interval. It does not require a necessary-and-sufficient characterization of admissible particles. Section 2 uses a compact-support description without spelling out a geometric consistency condition. It would therefore be inaccurate both to assert that (G) is printed in the source and to treat the source as explicitly demanding an unrestricted measurable-set theorem.

The entire full [CDN v3 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13159v3), not just its abstract or cited proposition, was read. Its §1.1 expressly distinguishes the proved static overshoot/stability results from the observed approximation of discrete eigenvalues still needing a precise description/proof. Sections 1.2 and 1.6 fix the exact scheme and shift; §§2.1–2.4 prove the static lattice bound; §3.5 proves the Maxwell specialization. There is no dynamic sampled-kernel or changing-mask Riesz-projection theorem in that source. Its endpoint decimals use numerically indicated extremizer locations; the candidate correctly uses the exact interval rather than promoting those decimals to certified endpoints.

Thus the positive theorem can be described as a rigorous qualified formulation and proof of the off-interval behavior described in OWR §4. It cannot be called a theorem for every compact support, or an unqualified settlement of every possible reading of the brief question. The separate TURN_2 construction closes the unrestricted reading in the opposite direction; it does not invalidate the usual regular-particle setting.

## Is this merely an already sufficient known combination?

The abstract proof architecture is classical. Direct reading of [Anselone–Palmer (1968)](https://msp.org/pjm/1968/25-3/pjm-v25-n3-p02-p.pdf), pp. 423–431, verifies the compact-multiplication lemma, resolvent/spectral approximation and eventual dimensions of spectral subspaces. [Anselone–Treuden (1985)](https://msp.org/pjm/1985/120-2/pjm-v120-n2-p01-p.pdf), §§7–9, verifies stable/regular convergence and the compact-operator setting. The candidate cannot claim a new compact-perturbation theorem, analytic Fredholm theorem, Riesz calculus, or abstract multiplicity principle.

Those sources do not, by themselves, prove the candidate's exact matrix hypotheses. Direct collectively compact approximation of the full matrix family is unavailable: the continuum volume operator is noncompact, each embedded finite matrix is finite rank, and their differences are noncompact. Factoring through the stable static resolvents removes this obstruction, but uses genuine DDA consistency inputs. Similarly [Descloux–Nassif–Rappaz (1978)](https://www.numdam.org/article/M2AN_1978__12_2_97_0.pdf), pp. 97–99, assumes norm consistency on the entire changing trial space (P1). Strong consistency on each fixed vector is not P1, and the point-sampled matrix is not the continuous Galerkin compression. Reading the conclusion of that theorem while omitting its premise would transfer the main difficulty rather than close it.

## Counterexample credit and limits

The separate construction has classical ingredients. The compact set avoiding a countable collection of grid points comes from a standard summable-ball deletion argument. The one null anchor makes the diagonal-omitting DDA matrix nonempty and zero. The static ball modes, small-frequency radiation and nonreal continuum poles are not discoveries here. Direct [ALZ 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07116), Theorem 3.2 and its proof, already supplies strict pole sign on smooth domains, and its finite-type/Fredholm analysis supplies the qualitative setting. [Zhou v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4540v5), §2.1, explicitly separates a bounded static longitudinal operator and a Hilbert–Schmidt dynamic correction. The radiation coefficient and sphere literature identified by the counterexample family must also receive credit. No new continuum pole theory should be claimed.

The potentially distinctive assembled statement is one **fixed compact positive-volume** support, at the explicit real frequency `1/10000`, whose continuum comparison operator has an isolated nonreal pole while every `h=1/n` matrix is `3x3` zero. Its critical bridge is persistence under strong static mask convergence plus Hilbert–Schmidt correction convergence; small removed volume does not itself give norm closeness of the strongly singular static operator. This is a real mathematical bridge, proved using established spectral machinery. No exact earlier assembly was located. That bounded finding permits presenting the example with modest credit-aware language; it does not certify a first counterexample.

The example proves why a universal claim over arbitrary pointwise compact masks fails. It does not show that (G) is necessary, refute convergence for physical regular particles, identify a practically important DDA pathology, or classify all sampling geometries.
