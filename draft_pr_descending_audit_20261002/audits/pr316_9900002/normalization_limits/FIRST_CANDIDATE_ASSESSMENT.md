## Independent reconstruction of the all-scale theorem

Let real-valued W_n and deterministic real c_n obey P(W_n=c_n)>=1-e_n with e_n->0. If W_n converges weakly to a proper probability law nu on R, its distributions are tight. Choose M so that P(|W_n|<=M)>3/4 eventually. Also P(W_n=c_n)>3/4 eventually. The two events then have positive intersection probability, which is impossible if |c_n|>M. Thus c_n is eventually bounded.

Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence with c_(n_j)->c in R. For bounded continuous f,

|E f(W_(n_j))-f(c_(n_j))| <= 2 ||f||_infinity e_(n_j) -> 0.

Consequently W_(n_j) converges weakly to delta_c. Since its weak limit must also be nu, nu=delta_c. Furthermore all cluster points c must be equal; otherwise two further subsequences would force different point-mass limits. Hence c_n itself converges whenever a proper weak limit exists, and that limit is degenerate.

For this candidate W_n=D_(t_n)/phi(t_n), c_n=a_n/phi(t_n)>0 and e_n=epsilon_n. This proves the advertised all-normalizer result for every real finite positive law-dependent phi in the eventual domain. ## Explicit exhaustive scale cases

- c_n->0: W_n->0 in probability, because outside the concentration event has probability at most epsilon_n. The limit delta_0 is proper but degenerate.
- c_n->c in (0,infinity): W_n->c in probability. The limit delta_c is proper but degenerate.
- c_n->infinity: for each finite L, P(W_n>L)>=1-epsilon_n eventually. This is improper escape, so tightness fails.
- c_n bounded but nonconvergent: distinct finite subsequential constants force different weak limits. Full convergence cannot hold.
- c_n unbounded with any oscillation: a subsequence tends to infinity; along it the preceding escaping-mass argument violates tightness. Other subsequences cannot repair proper full convergence.

These cases exhaust positive sequences c_n. They exclude a proper mixed limit with an atom at zero and mass elsewhere: any proper subsequential full limit is delta_c, and every normalized sequence in question has its mass concentrated at one deterministic c_n.
