## Exact target and success test

The source considers S_n=S_0+X_1+...+X_n, with nonnegative delay S_0 independent of i.i.d. strictly positive recurrence times. Their distribution is non-lattice: it is not supported on dZ for any d>0. Null recurrence means E[X_1]=infinity. With N_t=inf{n>=0:S_n>t}, total life is D_t=X_{N_t}=A_t+B_t. Problem 1.2 asks whether a nondecreasing deterministic scale phi makes D_t/phi(t) converge in distribution to a nondegenerate random variable, specifically whether phi(t)=E[min(X_1,t)] works.

To refute existence in this generality, exhibit ONE fully specified admissible recurrence law (and an admissible delay, such as S_0=0) for which EVERY finite positive eventually defined nondecreasing deterministic scale fails to give a proper nondegenerate finite weak limit. The scale may depend on this law. Failure of one proposed scale, or failure of time scaling alone, is insufficient. Failure of every deterministic scale would be a stronger sufficient result. Degenerate zero or positive constants and improper escape to infinity are not nondegenerate proper solutions. Possible mixed limiting laws with an atom at zero must also be excluded.
