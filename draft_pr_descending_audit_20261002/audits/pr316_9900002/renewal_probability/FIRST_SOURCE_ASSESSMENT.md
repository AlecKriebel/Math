## Independent probability attack mechanisms

1. **Admissibility:** reconstruct the probability measure from its literal masses/densities. Check total mass one, nonnegative probabilities, no atom at zero or infinity, and measurable finite support points. Establish infinite mean by an explicit divergent positive series/integral. Prove non-lattice using an absolutely continuous positive component or explicit incompatible supports, rather than merely observing infinitely many atoms.
2. **First-large-jump renewal argument:** for any proposed threshold class, set `J=inf{i>=1:X_i in H}` with `q=P(X in H)>0`. The failures are conditioned on `H^c`, so their mean is not automatically the unconditional mean. Let `W=sum_{i<J}X_i` and `m=E[X 1_{H^c}]`. If `m<infinity`, independence and Tonelli yield `E[W]=m/q`: the ith term has expectation `(1-q)^(i-1)m`. This directly proves the identity without invoking Wald at the crossing index. Markov then gives `P(W>t)<=m/(q t)`.
3. If every successful length exceeds `t`, then on `W<=t` the first success crosses `t`, and `D_t=X_J`. If a chosen successful class `C subset H` has probability `p`, then `P(X_J in C)=p/q`, whence `P(D_t in C)>=p/q-m/(q t)`. This is a sufficient route, not an assertion that the unseen candidate satisfies it. It also isolates the three quantitative requirements: the desired class dominates the success tail, the preceding sum is small compared with the observation time, and successful lengths actually cross that time.
4. **Concentration check:** if the candidate aims at `D_{t_k}/b_k -> 1` in probability, check `t_k -> infinity`, class widths relative to `b_k`, `p_k/q_k -> 1`, and `m_k/(q_k t_k) -> 0` with exact indices. A single diverging concentration subsequence is enough to feed a separate universal-normalization argument; simulation cannot prove it. If the probability derivation uses `N_t` as a stopping time and applies Wald, reconstruct it from the independent first-hit argument above and check integrability instead of accepting the named theorem.
5. **Falsification controls:** stress the mechanism when desired-class mass is not tail dominant, when observation time is below the preceding-sum scale, or when the alleged giant jump is shorter than the observation time. The crossing identification must fail or the bounds must become weak in these regimes. Check strict/weak inequalities, renewal epochs, base-component contribution, and whether conditioning changes distributions. Check all asymptotic comparisons rather than relying on rounded decimal computations.
