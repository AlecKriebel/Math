## Exact source claim and boundary

OWR 26/2018, printed 1625, Question 111 (Röver, Sapir): Does every finitely presented subgroup of F have torsion-free abelianisation? F is the usual dyadic orientation-preserving PL interval group, with finitely many breakpoints. There is no solvability, closedness, fixed-point, orbit, or finite-index restriction. The preceding printed 1624 example refutes the finite-generation variant, and does not refute the finite-presentation question. The workshop dates are 3-9 June 2018; the publisher records publication on 12 April 2019.

For the source example, identify the lamp module of Z wr Z with R=Z[x,x^-1]. The subgroup generated by t, 2, and x-1 is M semidirect Z with M=(2,x-1). Then H_ab=Z direct_sum M/(x-1)M. Independently, M consists exactly of Laurent polynomials of even evaluation at 1. The map f -> (f(1)/2,f'(1) mod 2) descends to M/(x-1)M and is onto Z direct_sum Z/2. If both components vanish, f=(x-1)g with g(1)=f'(1) even, so g belongs to M. Its kernel is exactly (x-1)M. Thus the example has H_ab=Z^2 direct_sum Z/2. This reproduces and strengthens the source's displayed nonzero 2-torsion witness, without assuming finite presentation.

## Credited source scope

- Guba-Sapir, arXiv:math/0301225v2, Theorem 9.9, physical PDF p35: every integral homology group of every diagram group is free abelian. The proof first gives zero boundary maps after a collapsing scheme for a complete directed 2-complex (Theorem 9.7), then uses the completion retraction and functoriality of homology. A retract of a free abelian group is free abelian. There is no finiteness assumption. This gives torsion-free H_1 for diagram groups, not arbitrary subgroups of diagram groups.
- Golan, arXiv:2609.14702v1 (13 September 2026), Theorem 2.13, PDF pp11-12: the diagram group of the core is isomorphic to Cl(H), hence to H when H is closed. Theorem 8.3, PDF p86, requires the core finite and full in addition to closedness. Corollary 8.7, pp89-90, retains those hypotheses and FP2 for effective presentations and finite-rank homology. Neither statement applies automatically to arbitrary finitely presented H <= F. The theorem uses prior core/closure results, and the homology consequence remains credited to Guba-Sapir.
- Farley, arXiv:2606.27753v2 (11 July 2026 submission, 14 July PDF date), Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3, PDF p2: the groups G_(n,l) are the full prescribed n-adic PL groups fixing the specified finite sequence. Rational entries yield F_infinity; an irrational entry makes the full group not finitely generated. All their integral homology groups are free abelian via diagram-group theory. This is not a statement about every subgroup fixing that sequence. Section 9.2 explicitly credits the diagram-group identification to Golan-Polak-Sapir. Sections 7 and 8 were read through the end of the proofs.
- Bieri-Geoghegan-Kochloukova (BGK), arXiv:0807.5138v1, Theorem 2.7, p6: for a group of type FP2(R) with no nonabelian free subgroup, a nonzero discrete real character yields an ascending HNN description with finitely generated base inside its kernel. The coefficient convention on p2 is a commutative unital ring with 1 != 0. BGK explicitly attributes the splitting to Bieri-Strebel, CMH 53 (1978), Theorem A, with the no-free-subgroup hypothesis forcing ascendancy. A fresh e-periodica attempt returned verification HTML; BGK is the accessible primary statement actually relied on. No challenge was solved.
- Bleak, arXiv:math/0602038v2 (13 April 2006), Lemma 1.3/Corollary 4.8: a solvable PL_o(I) subgroup of derived length n embeds in G_n, with G_0=1 and G_n=direct_sum_(Z)(G_(n-1) wr Z). The proof uses split groups, unchanged derived length, controllers and restricted wreath decompositions. It applies to all solvable subgroups; finite generation is not required there. The distinction between restricted wreath products/direct sums and unrestricted products is essential below.

## Independent solvable-image deduction

Let Gamma be of type FP2(R), for a nonzero commutative unital ring R, with no nonabelian free subgroup. Any homomorphism from Gamma into a solvable subgroup of PL_o(I) has abelian image. In particular, a solvable FP2(R) subgroup of PL_o(I) is finitely generated free abelian.

Proof. Use Bleak to place the image in G_n. First prove a support lemma for any homomorphism rho:Gamma -> A wr Z. If the top projection chi is nonzero, it is a discrete character, so BGK gives Gamma=<B,t>, B finitely generated inside ker chi, with either tBt^-1 <= B or t^-1Bt <= B. The image rho(B) lies in the restricted base direct_sum_(Z)A. If b_1,...,b_r generate B, the union S of coordinate supports of rho(b_j) is finite and is also the union of supports of every element in rho(B). Products and inverses cannot introduce a new coordinate.

Conjugation by rho(t), whose top exponent k is nonzero, shifts this union to S+k, or S-k for its inverse. The lamp part merely conjugates coordinate values and cannot change whether a value is the identity. Ascendancy therefore forces S+k <= S or S-k <= S. A nonempty finite integer set cannot contain a nonzero translate: choose its maximal or minimal element according to the sign of k. Hence S is empty, rho(B)=1, and rho(Gamma) is generated by rho(t), so is infinite cyclic (or cyclic in the harmless zero-image case).

Now induct on n. FP2(R) implies finite generation. A finitely generated image in G_n uses only finitely many outer summands. In each A wr Z summand with A=G_(n-1), if its top projection is nonzero, the preceding lemma makes that image cyclic. If it is zero, finite generation puts its image in finitely many coordinates of direct_sum A. Apply the inductive claim to each of the resulting homomorphisms from the original Gamma into G_(n-1). Each image is abelian. Thus every outer summand image is abelian, hence the original image is abelian. Crucially, FP2 is never assumed for an image or kernel; BGK always applies to the original Gamma. Finally PL_o(I) is torsion-free, so a finitely generated abelian subgroup is finite-rank free abelian. QED.

The strongest result here concerns solvable images, and does not cover arbitrary nonsolvable subgroups H <= F in Question 111. F itself is nonsolvable. Diagram-group homology and closed-subgroup closure do not supply missing information about an arbitrary subgroup's own abelianization.
