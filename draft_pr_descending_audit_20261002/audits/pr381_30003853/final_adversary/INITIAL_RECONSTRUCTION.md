# Independent reconstruction before candidate access

Created before opening PR381 candidate proofs/code, root and family reports, or
historical verdicts. The parent supplied approach labels, source identifiers and
expected gaps; those exposures are disclosed in RESEARCH_LOG.md. They guided which
claims to challenge but are not premises of the deductions below.

## Literal target and falsifiers

EMS OWR26/2018, printed page 1625, Question 111 (Röver, Sapir):
"Does every finitely presented subgroup of F have torsion-free abelianisation?"
Here F has finitely many linear pieces, dyadic breakpoints and slopes in 2^Z;
BGK page 1 independently confirms that definition. The finite-presentation
hypothesis is essential: the preceding printed pages 1624--1625 give Röver's
finitely generated subgroup of Z wreath Z with 2-torsion in its abelianisation.

A positive answer requires proving [H,H] is isolated in every finitely presented
H<=F: h^m in [H,H], m>=1 implies h in [H,H]. A negative answer requires a
specified embedding into this F, a verified finite presentation for that subgroup,
and a nonzero finite-order class in its abelianisation. Group torsion-freeness,
bi-orderability, unique roots, a finite-window computation, or a finitely
generated example alone meets neither success criterion.

## 1. Laurent lamps: torsion and a one-sided obstruction

Let R0=Z[t,t^-1], B=direct_sum_Z Z, and W=B semidirect Z, where t translates
lamp coordinates. For m>=2 put I_m=(m,t-1)<=R0 and H_m=I_m semidirect <t>.
The lamp generator a corresponds to 1; the generators a^m and [a,t] correspond
to m and t-1. Consequently H_m is generated by those two words and t. The base
is abelian, so H_m'=(t-1)I_m and

    (H_m)_ab = Z + I_m/(t-1)I_m.

Define Phi:I_m->Z + Z/m by

    Phi(f)=(f(1)/m, f'(1) mod m).

The expressions are integral even for Laurent polynomials. Phi sends m and t-1
to the standard two generators. If Phi(f)=0, then f(1)=0, so f=(t-1)g for a
Laurent polynomial g. Differentiating gives g(1)=f'(1)=0 mod m; hence g lies in
I_m (reduce g modulo m and divide by t-1). Thus ker Phi=(t-1)I_m. This proves

    (H_m)_ab = Z^2 + Z/m

with exact torsion order m, reproducing the source's m=2 example. No support
cutoff is used in this proof.

More generally let H<=A wreath Z, where A is any torsion-free abelian group, H
is finitely generated, and its projection is kZ with k>0. Its base kernel M is
abelian and invariant under shifts by k. A lift u of k splits the cyclic quotient,
so H=M semidirect <u>. Conjugation on M is the k-shift; the lamp component of u
does not alter that action. If M=0, H is cyclic. If the projection vanishes, H is
abelian. In the remaining case M is nonzero.

Credited input: BGK arXiv:0807.5138v1 Theorem 2.7, page 6, with its footnote 7,
applied to a nonzero discrete character on an FP2 group with no nonabelian free
subgroup, supplies an ascending HNN decomposition with an **ordinary finitely
generated** base in the character kernel. The paper defines its coefficient rings
to be nonzero commutative rings with identity. This is the imported theorem; the
original Bieri--Strebel 1978 proof has not been audited here.

Suppose H has type FP2 over such a ring. It is metabelian, hence has no
nonabelian free subgroup. Apply the credited input to H->kZ. The ascending base
K<=M is generated by finitely many finite-support lamp vectors. All K is therefore
supported in one finite coordinate window [a,b]. In one HNN orientation,

    M = union_{n>=0} u^n K u^-n.

Each term is supported in [a+nk,b+nk]; the union has a uniform left bound.
The opposite orientation instead gives a uniform right bound. But any nonzero
v in M has shifts u^j v u^-j for every j in Z, contradicting either bound.
Therefore M!=0 excludes FP2 over every such coefficient ring. This excludes
the lamp torsion examples from the target's finitely presented domain. It does
not establish the target for arbitrary H<=F.

## 2. Simple-derived subdirects and why perfectness matters

Let L' = S be nonabelian simple and L/S torsion-free abelian. Let H<=L^r project
onto L in every coordinate, with r finite. Set D=H'. Every coordinate image of D
is S, hence D is a subdirect subgroup of S^r. The finite-factor Goursat argument
shows D is a product of twisted full diagonals over a partition of coordinates:
each block is one copy of S linked by coordinate isomorphisms. To see this
inductively, project onto the first r-1 coordinates, whose image is a product of
simple factors by induction, and use Goursat with the last S. Any quotient of
that product is S only when it selects one factor; otherwise the last S splits
as a new independent factor. A homomorphism to S cannot combine two selected
factors: their images commute and are normal, while S is nonabelian simple.

Each diagonal is perfect, so D is perfect. It is also its own normalizer inside
S^r: on a block with maps alpha_i, if (x_i) normalizes the diagonal, then
alpha_i^-1 inn(x_i) alpha_i is the same automorphism in every coordinate. This
implies alpha_i^-1(x_i) differ only by central elements of S. Its center is
trivial, so (x_i) lies in the diagonal. Product blocks give N_{S^r}(D)=D.

The kernel K=H intersection S^r normalizes D=H'. Hence K<=D by the normalizer
calculation, while D<=K is automatic. Consequently K=D and H/H' embeds into
(L/S)^r. In particular its abelianisation is torsion-free. For L=F, the standard
inputs F' simple and F/F'=Z^2 apply. This whole restricted result does not need
finite presentation or finite generation. The surjective-coordinate hypothesis
cannot be silently replaced by an arbitrary subgroup of F^r.

Negative mutant with stronger controls than an abelian finite-group toy: let L
be the ordinary integer Heisenberg group, law

    (a,b,c)(d,e,f)=(a+d,b+e,c+f+ae).

Then L' is central infinite cyclic and L_ab=Z^2. For m>=2 let

    H={(g,h) in L^2 : a(g)=a(h) mod m, b(g)=b(h) mod m}.

This is a subgroup, is torsion-free, and projects onto both L factors. It is
generated by the diagonal x,y, the two separate central generators z, and x^m,
y^m in the first factor. Its central intersection K is Z^2. Its commutator
lattice is generated by (1,1),(m,0),(0,m): basic commutators obey the congruence
c=d mod m, and the displayed generators attain that whole lattice. Thus
K/H'=Z/m, giving torsion in H_ab. The factor abelianisations are free, but the
factor derived group lacks perfectness and self-normalization. This disproves
the extension of the simple-derived argument to arbitrary torsion-free factors.
It is not an embedding of this nilpotent group into F.

## 3. Solvable finite-break PL groups: keep FP2 on the original domain

Credited input: Bleak arXiv:math/0602038v2 Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 1.3 identify
each solvable subgroup of PL_o(I), of derived length n, with a subgroup of G_n,
where G_0=1 and G_n is the countable direct sum of copies of G_{n-1} wreath Z.
The finite-break condition is explicit in the paper's introduction.

Let H be finitely generated and FP2 over a nonzero commutative ring, and suppose
it embeds in G_n. I reconstruct the following proof that H is abelian. A finite
generating set occupies only finitely many outer direct-sum components. Project
onto any one component Q<=G_{n-1} wreath Z. Compose the original embedding and
this projection with the top Z-coordinate to obtain a character chi:H->Z.
If chi is nonzero and ker(chi) has nonzero image in the base of this component,
apply BGK Theorem 2.7 to **H**, not to Q or ker(chi). Its ascending base is
ordinary finitely generated. Its image in the restricted-wreath base has finite
support in a uniform window. Conjugation by a lift of the top shift translates
that support, possibly also conjugating the nonzero lamp entries; conjugation
never changes whether a coordinate is trivial. The same one-sided contradiction
from section 1 applies. Therefore, whenever the top character is nonzero, Q's
base kernel is zero and Q is cyclic.

If the top character is zero, the component image lies in a restricted direct
sum of G_{n-1}; its finite generating set uses finitely many coordinates. Project
the original H onto those lower components and repeat. The depth decreases at
every step; all tested characters still have domain the original H. At depth
zero the leaf is trivial, and every branch terminated earlier has cyclic image.
The collection of finite projections remains jointly injective because only
actual direct-sum coordinate projections were made. Thus H embeds into a finite
product of cyclic groups and is abelian. No quotient, kernel, projection image,
or subgroup has been assumed to inherit FP2. Such inheritance would be invalid
in general. This is a restricted solvable-class theorem, not a theorem about
all finitely presented subgroups of F.

For the stronger wording without a separate finite-generation assumption, the
standard equivalence FP1(R)<=>ordinary finite generation for R!=0 is an
additional homological input. A direct support argument establishes its relevant
augmentation-ideal step: if I_R(H) is generated as an R[H]-module by finitely
many vectors, let K be generated by their finitely many support elements. Each
vector lies in R[K]I_R(K), so I_R(H)=R[H]I_R(K). Quotienting gives R[H/K]=R,
and nonzero R implies H/K has one coset. FP2 implies that finite augmentation-
ideal generation by the usual finite partial resolution/Schanuel argument.

## 4. Germ maps, ambient normality and generation notions

For finite-break orientation-preserving PL maps fixing endpoints of an interval,
the two endpoint slopes are multiplicative homomorphisms. In F their base-2
logarithms give F->Z^2 with kernel F'. They detect ambient commutators, but for
an arbitrary subgroup H their kernel can be strictly larger than H'. Thus one
cannot prove the target merely by mapping H into endpoint germs.

There are two independent valid normality controls. First, if an ordinary
finitely generated N<=F' is normal in F, its finite generators all vanish on
fixed neighborhoods of 0 and 1. All N has support in one compact subinterval.
Any nontrivial element of N has a nonempty support interval; conjugating by
suitable powers of the standard x0 transports it outside that fixed compact
subinterval. Normality would put those conjugates back in N. Hence N is trivial.
Finite **normal** generation supplies no such common support bound: F' is simple
and a nontrivial element normally generates it, while its transported supports
show it is not ordinary finitely generated.

Second, every nontrivial normal N<=F contains F'. Indeed N intersection F' is
normal in the simple F'. If it were trivial, [N,F']<=N intersection F' would
vanish, making N centralize F'. But the centralizer of F' in F is trivial:
a nonidentity g moves some small dyadic interval J disjointly from itself;
choose a nontrivial local element of F' supported in J. Conjugation by g moves
its support, so they cannot commute. Therefore F'<=N. Since F' is perfect,
N'=F' and N_ab is the subgroup image in Z^2. This proves the normal-subgroup
case independently of finite presentation. It does not make a nonnormal H
normal, and does not imply H' is ordinary finitely generated.

These are precise germ/normality deductions. Any different ambient normality
claim in the candidate must later be checked against its literal hypotheses;
the reconstruction does not substitute a convenient ambient group.

## 5. Nilpotence, unique roots and false abelianisation proxies

Credited local input: Kassabov--Matucci arXiv:math/0607167v3 Lemma 4.6 says
the initial slope is an injective homomorphism on the centralizer of a
fixed-point-free PL bump. Its image is abelian. Their Theorem 5.1 credits the
stronger cyclic-centralizer statement to Brin--Squier; only injectivity is needed
below. A quick direct verification is that a commuting map with initial slope 1
is identity on an initial neighborhood, and commuting transports that identity
over the whole bump interval.

Suppose a finite-class nilpotent subgroup N of PL_o(I) is nonabelian. Choose a
nontrivial commutator z=[x,y] in the last nontrivial lower-central group, so z is
central in N. Each support component J of z is preserved by x and y: commuting
with z preserves its fixed set, and increasing maps cannot permute its finitely
many components. On J both restrictions lie in the abelian centralizer of the
fixed-point-free restriction z|J. Hence [x,y]|J is identity, contradicting that
z has nonempty support there. Thus every such nilpotent subgroup is abelian;
ordinary finite generation is not required for this argument.

The same bump argument proves unique roots for finite-break PL maps. If f^k=g^k
=h, their fixed sets equal that of h, and both commute with h. On every support
component they lie in its abelian centralizer; equal kth powers in that
torsion-free slope group imply f=g. On fixed intervals both are identity.

Neither unique roots nor bi-orderability entails torsion-free abelianisation.
The integer group P_m with law

    (a,b,c)(d,e,f)=(a+d,b+e,c+f+m ae),  m>=2,

is torsion-free class-two nilpotent and satisfies [x,y]=z^m. Its abelianisation
is Z^2 + Z/m. Its power formula

    (a,b,c)^k=(ka,kb,kc+m binom(k,2)ab)

gives unique roots by comparing coordinates. The lexicographic order on
(a,b,c) is invariant under conjugation: the first two coordinates never change,
and when both are zero the element is central. It is therefore a bi-order.
The nilpotent PL lemma forbids an embedding of this nonabelian P_m into F.
This is a falsifier of the proxy inference, not a counterexample to Question 111.

## Sources delimiting the still-open gap

Guba--Sapir arXiv:math/0301225v2 Theorem 9.9 proves free integral homology for
diagram groups. Golan arXiv:2609.14702v1 identifies closed subgroups of F with
the relevant diagram groups; its FP2/finite-presentation equivalences in Theorem
A assume finite generation, closedness, and finitely many dyadic orbits. Farley
arXiv:2606.27753v2 treats the specified full fixed-point stabilizer groups and
states their diagram-group/free-homology consequences. Those inputs verify
restricted positive classes; neither says every finitely presented subgroup of
F is closed, a diagram group, a normal subgroup, solvable, nilpotent, or a
surjective-coordinate simple-derived subdirect.

The exact remaining target gap is a proof of isolated commutator subgroup for an
arbitrary finitely presented subgroup H<=F (in particular outside the classes
above), or a verified finitely presented embedded torsion example. A route that
merely asserts one of those structural reductions transfers the central problem
to an unsupported claim and is blocked until a new mechanism proves it.

All finite controls and exact outputs are in controls/independent_controls.py and
its complete stdout. They verify concrete lamp actions, coinvariant relations,
A5 simple diagonal normalizers, the Heisenberg negative mutant, and nilpotent
unique-root/biorder formulas. They are not raw-primary-proof checks or a finite
exhaustion of arbitrary subgroups of F.
