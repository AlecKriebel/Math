## Exact primary target and success criteria

EMS PDF 46748, Oberwolfach Report 26/2018, printed p.1625 (PDF page 47), Question 111 (Röver, Sapir): “Does every finitely presented subgroup of F have torsion-free abelianisation?” The PDF was independently downloaded, text extracted, and this literal page rendered and visually read first. Its preceding construction distinguishes finitely generated from finitely presented.

Use the finite-piece definition F = orientation-preserving piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of [0,1] with finitely many dyadic breakpoints and slopes powers of two. Positive resolution requires a proof for every finitely presented abstract subgroup H ≤ F, including H=1, cyclic and abelian H, and arbitrary nonclosed H. Negative resolution requires a concrete H, a verified injective homomorphism into this F, verified finite presentation, and a nontrivial finite-order class in H/[H,H]. Any substitution of finite generation, homological FP2, a finite quotient, arbitrary interval homeomorphisms, unrestricted locally PL maps, or subgroup of a diagram group requires separately proved implications. Finite tests can falsify claimed equalities but cannot establish the universal claim, an infinite presentation, or injectivity.

## Control A: genuine finite-piece wreath embedding

Let t have breakpoints (0,0),(1/2,1/4),(3/4,1/2),(1,1). Let a be identity outside [1/2,3/4] and have breakpoints (1/2,1/2),(5/8,9/16),(11/16,5/8),(3/4,3/4) inside. Both are in F. Write f^g=g^{-1}fg. Each a_i=a^{t^i} is nontrivial, with support t^{-i}(1/2,3/4). The closures have disjoint interiors for all i∈Z: for i≤−1 these intervals are (2^{i-1},2^i), and for i≥0 they are (1-2^{-i-1},1-2^{-i-2}). Every a_i has infinite order, since an increasing nonidentity homeomorphism moves a point strictly along a monotone sequence under positive powers. Disjoint supports show that any nonzero finite integer combination of the a_i is nontrivial. A word with nonzero total exponent of t has nonunit derivative at 0, whereas every finite product of a_i is identity near 0. Thus ⟨a,t⟩ is the restricted wreath product W=(⊕_{i∈Z}Z)⋊Z, injectively realized in finite-piece F. Infinite-piece individual maps are unnecessary: each group element is a finite word and has finitely many pieces. The executable tests a finite window; the argument just given is global.

## Control B: exact torsion abelianization with finite-generation boundary

Identify the base of W with R=Z[x,x^{-1}], a_i↔x^i, conjugation by t↔multiplication by x. For m≥2 set I_m=(m,x−1)R and H_m=I_m⋊⟨t⟩=⟨a^m,a^{-1}a^t,t⟩. Normal generation under shifts supplies all of I_m; conversely every base contribution from a word in these generators lies in I_m. Write u=x−1. Since the base is abelian, H_m' = u I_m: every commutator is in uI_m, while [f,t] realizes uf, and uI_m is invariant under the base and t. In particular [r,s]=1 and [s,t]=r^m where r↔u,s↔m. Pairwise generator commutators normally generate the derived subgroup for every finitely generated group: quotienting by their normal closure makes the generating images commute, so makes the quotient abelian. This says nothing about finite generation of the defining-relator kernel.

The exact isomorphism I_m/uI_m → Z⊕Z/m is [f]↦(f(1)/m,f'(1) mod m). Augmentation is divisible by m on I_m. It annihilates uI_m, whose derivative at 1 is divisible by m. It is onto, with s↦(1,0), r↦(0,1). If f is in its kernel, augmentation zero gives f=ug; then g(1)=f'(1) is divisible by m, so g∈I_m, establishing the kernel exactly. Thus H_m^ab≅Z²⊕Z/m, with the additional Z from t. H_m is torsion-free as an actual subgroup of F and has index m in W: R/I_m≅Z/m and the quotient kills t. This is a strong check against any claimed implication “torsion-free subgroup implies torsion-free abelianization”. For m=1, I_1=R and H_1=W with abelianization Z².

## Control C: finite presentation and FP2 genuinely fail here

A direct finite-presentation obstruction for W is independent of its abelianization calculation. W has presentation ⟨a,t | [a,a^{t^k}]=1, k≥1⟩. If its relator kernel were normally finitely generated, each of those finite normal generators would use finitely many displayed relations, so some bound K would suffice. Instead let A_K be the graph group on generators a_i (i∈Z), with [a_i,a_j]=1 only when 0<|i−j|≤K. Shift extends to an automorphism, so A_K⋊Z satisfies every bounded relation. Its commutator [a_0,a_{K+1}] survives: kill all other generators to retract onto the free group on these two nonadjacent vertices. Therefore no finite bound, and no finite presentation, exists. Finite-index invariance of finite presentability rules out H_m.

There is a separate stronger obstruction to FP2 over Z. A K(B,1) for B=⊕_Z Z is the weak product of circles, with homology H_1(B)=B and H_2(B)=∧²B. The shift is a cellular homeomorphism; its mapping torus is a K(W,1), since the lifted mapping telescope has contractible fibers and base. The Wang exact sequence gives 0→(∧²B)_Z→H_2(W;Z)→B^Z→0. The fixed module B^Z vanishes, since a finitely supported sequence invariant under shift is zero. The coinvariant wedge module is free on positive distances d=j−i: H_2(W;Z)≅⊕_{d≥1} Z. These distances are independent because shift preserves d, with no sign reversal. H_m has finite index m; inclusion after transfer multiplies H_2(W;Q) by m. Hence H_2(H_m;Q) is infinite dimensional as well. FP2 implies finitely generated H_2(G;Z): tensor a resolution finite through degree 2 with the trivial module, and take homology of its finitely generated degree-2 group. Thus W and H_m are not FP2, without any inference that FP2 and finite presentation are equivalent for arbitrary groups.

## Control D: finitely presented torsion-free nonembedding proxy

For n≥2, BS(1,n)=⟨a,t | a^t=a^n⟩ has abelianization Z⊕Z/(n−1) and is torsion-free. Its faithful model is Z[1/n]⋊Z acting on the real line by translations and positive dilations (choose dilation 1/n for the conjugation convention). The standard ascending normal form verifies injectivity. Conjugating this action into (0,1) and extending to endpoints gives increasing homeomorphisms, so an interval-homeomorphism embedding is insufficient.

It does not embed in finite-piece PL_o([0,1]). Suppose A≠1 and T^{-1}AT=A^n. A and A^n have the same finite collection of support intervals. Increasing T permutes their endpoints and therefore fixes each endpoint and interval. At the left endpoint c of a support interval, the right germ of A has positive slope s≠1 (a slope-1 affine germ fixing c is identity). Conjugation by the fixed-point affine germ of T preserves s; taking n-th power changes it to s^n. Thus s=s^n, forcing s=1 and a contradiction. No numerical test or torsion argument is needed. Boundary n=1 is Z², which embeds by disjoint bumps; n=2 has torsion-free abelianization despite the same nonembedding obstruction. Klein-bottle inverse conjugation also fails because an increasing conjugator cannot reverse the displacement sign on an invariant support interval.

## Control E: genuine positive mechanisms and their hypotheses

1. If P=F^k and P' is perfect, then every H with P'≤H≤P has H'=P': inclusion H'≤P' follows from abelian P/P', while P'=[P',P']≤H'. Consequently H^ab embeds in P^ab=Z^{2k} and is free abelian. This is a containment argument, not an endpoint-image identification for arbitrary H. An internal bump a is nontrivial but has both endpoint slope characters zero. Thus endpoint characters alone cannot determine H^ab.
2. Bieri–Geoghegan–Kochloukova v1, Theorem 1.1/Corollary 1.2 and Theorem A: the Sigma criterion applies to normal subgroups with abelian quotient, and in its discrete-character specialization tests both [χ] and [−χ]. For χ=aχ_0+bχ_1, χ_i=log_2 slope at endpoint i, a,b both nonzero gives finite generation; ab<0 gives F∞; ab>0 gives not FP2; a=0 or b=0 gives a non-finitely-generated kernel. A zero character has kernel F and is a separate case. The sign and two-ray hypotheses matter.
3. Kassabov–Matucci v3, Lemma 4.6: for a one-bump z on J with no interior fixed point, the initial slope homomorphism on its centralizer is injective. A centralizer element with slope 1 is identity near the left endpoint and commuting propagates identity along iterates of z or z^{-1} throughout J. In a restriction of F, the logarithmic slope image is a subgroup of Z, so the centralizer restriction is cyclic. Interior fixed-point components require separate treatment; the statement cannot be applied globally to a multibump element.
4. Bleak v2, Theorems 1.1–1.2 classify solvable PL_o(I) subgroup isomorphism types using subgroups, restricted wreath products with Z, and bounded countable direct sums. They do not say those subgroups are finitely presented or have torsion-free abelianization. W and H_m are explicit boundary controls for those additional claims.
5. Guba–Sapir v2, Theorem 9.7 requires a complete directed 2-complex; its Remark 6.7 presentation abelianizes freely. Theorem 9.9 covers arbitrary diagram groups by a retraction into a complete case and functoriality of integral homology: all integral H_n are free abelian. A subgroup of a diagram group need not be a diagram group. H_m≤F, with its abelianization torsion, already disproves that inheritance. Any application must establish the candidate group itself is a diagram group.
6. Golan v1, Theorem A/8.3: H must be closed, finitely generated, and have finitely many orbits on dyadic rationals, equivalently finite full core. In this class FP2, finite presentability, F∞, finite path classes, and finite orbit counts on dyadic pairs are equivalent. Closedness additionally identifies H with the diagram group of its core. Finite generation alone produces a finite core and closure, not equality H=Cl(H). Endpoint rank criteria require the paper's action/minimality and closed/full-core hypotheses. The published theorem cannot be extended to arbitrary H≤F by finite core evidence.
7. Farley v2, Definition 1.1/Theorem 1.2: G_{n,ℓ} is the full specified group on [r_1,r_k] fixing an increasing finite list ℓ; all entries rational gives F∞; any irrational entry gives not finitely generated; the full group is a diagram group and has free integral homology. An arbitrary subgroup of a stabilizer is outside this conclusion. For a usual point stabilizer of F include 0 and 1 in ℓ and n=2.
