## Proof audit

1. The restricted wreath obstruction is valid. A nonzero top character is normalized to an epimorphism before using Bieri–Strebel in the precise BGK formulation. The nontrivial HNN kernel is generated by conjugates of the base, so a nontrivial kernel forces a nontrivial base. The finitely generated base has finite total lamp support. Nonzero translation contradicts forward invariance of that finite support. The zero-projection case is finitely generated torsion-free abelian. BGK explicitly permits every nonzero commutative coefficient ring R; neither rational coefficients nor finite presentation alone is silently substituted for FP_2(R).

The Laurent derivative quotient has both a surjectivity argument and the exact kernel argument: f(1)=0 implies divisibility by t-1 even for negative exponents, and the quotient's value at 1 is f'(1). This proves the displayed torsion calculation. The m=1 case correctly remains non-FP_2 without abelianization torsion. The disjoint fundamental-interval construction really gives a restricted wreath embedding in F: each individual element has only finitely many pieces. No FP repair inside this ambient wreath product is possible.

2. The subdirect theorem includes the necessary self-normalizer step. The induction through Goursat's graph case is valid because the other projection is a finite product of simple diagonal blocks. Distinct normal factor images cannot both be the same nonabelian simple quotient while commuting. A twisted diagonal tuple normalizes that diagonal only when all its conjugations agree; centerlessness then puts it in the diagonal. Consequently H intersect product(S_i) is contained in H', not merely a subgroup with the same projections. The endpoint-slope corollary follows for intermediate factors containing F'. Finite-index factors contain F' by the finite coset action and infinite simplicity. A nonsaturated lattice is still free abelian; quotient torsion is correctly kept distinct.

3. The solvable-image argument retains the original FP_2 domain at every step. No coordinate image or quotient is incorrectly asserted FP_2. In a nonabelian lamp group the conjugation changes entries but preserves whether they are identity, so support translation remains exact. Finite generation reduces both restricted sums to finite products. Bleak's finite-derived-length classification then applies; the statement does not extend to arbitrary elementary amenable groups. A solvable subgroup itself has no nonabelian free subgroup, as required by the splitting input.

4. The normal-subgroup germ argument is sound. A finite generating set has a finite union of support intervals. Normality and order preservation fix each component and its left endpoint for every ambient element. Finite piecewise linearity yields actual affine germs, not just derivatives. If all generator slopes there were 1, a common initial interval would be fixed, contradicting the component definition. In F the slope exponent is integral even at a non-dyadic common fixed point. Real characters annihilate the isolator of H', so no nontrivial ordinarily finitely generated normal subgroup can lie there. The distinction between ordinary finite generation and finite normal generation is essential and is preserved throughout.

5. The one-bump germ centralizer injection proves root uniqueness and balanced signed powers under the stated finite-piece hypothesis. Every conjugator fixes the finite ordered list of support components; its endpoint slope cancels. The initial slope of a one-bump finite-piece map cannot be 1. The integer nilpotent model has precisely the finite presentation: collection provides every normal form and the model separates them. Its commutator, power and order formulas establish all advertised proxy properties and the exact Z/m summand. It is not an F counterexample. Indeed, centrality forces the images of x and y into an abelian one-bump centralizer on any support component of z, contradicting their commutator z^m there. Every such PL image kills z.
