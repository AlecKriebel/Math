# Turn 2: subdirect products with simple derived factors

Completed 2026-10-02 UTC. Original unresolved, 2/5 substantive turns; subjective progress 10%. This direction addresses large non-solvable interval projections, rather than the wreath products of Turn 1. No historical novelty is asserted for the standard subdirect-product methods.

## 1. Algebraic theorem

Let G_1,...,G_r be groups, with S_i=G_i' nonabelian simple and G_i/S_i torsion-free abelian. If H <= product_i G_i projects onto every G_i, then

    H' = H intersect product_i S_i.

In particular H_ab injects into product_i (G_i/S_i), so it is torsion-free. If the latter group is finite-rank free abelian, so is H_ab. Neither finite generation nor finite presentation of H is required.

Here and below a prime denotes the subgroup generated by all commutators, not just the set of commutators.

### Subdirect lemma, including its normalizer

A subgroup D of a finite product of nonabelian simple groups which projects onto every factor is a product of twisted diagonal groups on a partition of the coordinates. Isomorphic factors can share a block; distinct blocks vary independently. Further,

    N_{product_i S_i}(D)=D,

and D is perfect.

We give the elementary proof to make all uses explicit. First every normal subgroup of a finite product of nonabelian simple groups is a product of some of its factors. Indeed, if its projection onto a factor is nontrivial, commute a suitable element with a single-coordinate element. The resulting nontrivial single-coordinate commutator belongs to the normal subgroup. Such a commutator exists because a nonabelian simple group has trivial center. Simplicity then puts that entire factor in the normal subgroup. Repeat over the nontrivial projections.

Now induct on the number of coordinates of D. The projection P onto the first r-1 coordinates is, by induction, a product of simple diagonal blocks. The kernel of D -> P, viewed inside the final simple factor S, is normal in S, since D projects onto S. If it is S, then D=P times S. If it is trivial, D is the graph of a surjective homomorphism P -> S. The images of the simple direct factors of P are normal in S, thus trivial or S. Exactly one can be S: images of distinct factors commute, whereas S is nonabelian. The nontrivial map is an isomorphism by simplicity. This appends the last coordinate to exactly one existing diagonal block and completes the induction.

For a twisted diagonal block, identify its coordinates with a common simple group S. A normalizing tuple (x_1,...,x_k) induces the same inner automorphism of S in every coordinate. Thus x_i x_1^{-1} is central after the identifications, and equals 1. The tuple belongs to the diagonal. Applied independently to all blocks this proves the normalizer claim. A nonabelian simple group is perfect (its nontrivial derived subgroup is normal), so the product of these blocks is perfect as well.

### Proof of the theorem

Put D=H'. Surjectivity of each projection H -> G_i gives projection D -> G_i'=S_i onto. Thus D is a subdirect subgroup of product S_i, and the lemma applies. Every element of K=H intersect product S_i normalizes D, because H' is characteristic in H. The normalizer assertion gives K <= D. The reverse inclusion follows from the definition of the derived factors. The kernel of the natural map H -> product(G_i/S_i) is exactly K=D, proving the theorem.

The normalizer step is essential: projecting H' onto each S_i alone would not justify equality with H intersect product S_i.

## 2. Thompson-F consequence

Use the classical facts that F' is nonabelian simple and that the endpoint-slope map

    pi:F -> Z^2, f |-> (log_2 f'(0+), log_2 f'(1-))

has kernel F' and is onto. These are credited standard inputs, stated in Bieri–Geoghegan–Kochloukova, arXiv:0807.5138v1, introduction and p6. Let G_i be any intermediate group F' <= G_i <= F. Then G_i'=F': containment in F' is automatic, and containment of F' in G_i' follows from its perfectness. Moreover G_i/F' is a subgroup of Z^2.

**Corollary.** If H <= F^r has every coordinate projection containing F', then its abelianization is a free abelian group of rank at most 2r, detected exactly by the combined endpoint slopes. Its commutator subgroup is a finite direct product of twisted diagonal copies of F'.

In particular the corollary applies when all coordinate projections have finite index in F. To see that a finite-index subgroup contains F', let F act on its finite coset set. The restriction to the infinite simple group F' has trivial kernel or full kernel; an injection into a finite symmetric group is impossible, so the kernel is full. The subgroup therefore contains F'.

The group F^r embeds in F by placing independent copies on r disjoint dyadic intervals. Restrictions of a subgroup supported on those intervals can accordingly meet this criterion. Arbitrary subgroups of F need not have such large restrictions. No statement here assumes that being finitely presented forces them.

## 3. Congruence fibre products do not manufacture torsion

For any lattice L <= Z^{2r}, form H=pi^{-1}(L) in F^r. It contains (F')^r, which is perfect, and H/(F')^r=L is abelian. Consequently H'=(F')^r and H_ab=L. A nonsaturated lattice is still free abelian: torsion in the finite quotient Z^{2r}/L is not torsion in H_ab.

For example,

    H_m={(f,g) in F^2 : chi_0(f) = chi_0(g) mod m}

has index m but abelianization Z^4, not Z^4 plus Z/m. A basis for its endpoint lattice, ordered as (left_f,right_f,left_g,right_g), is

    (m,0,0,0), (0,1,0,0), (1,0,1,0), (0,0,0,1).

Every admissible integer vector (a,b,c,d) uniquely has coefficients ((a-c)/m,b,c,d) in this basis. Thus attempting to transplant Röver's finite-index congruence mechanism into these full F interval factors loses the torsion, for a precise perfect-kernel reason.
