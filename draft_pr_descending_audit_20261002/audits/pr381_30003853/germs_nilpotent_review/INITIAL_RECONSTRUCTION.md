# Independent initial reconstruction — sealed before historical verdicts

The original target is: for every finitely presented subgroup H of Thompson's F,
is H/H' torsion-free? Success would be either a general proof or a finitely
presented group with a proved faithful embedding in F and a nonzero torsion
abelianization class. Group torsion-freeness alone is irrelevant to that last
condition. This audit does not certify present-day open status or novelty.

I independently retrieved and read the original EMS 46748 PDF, including rendered
printed pp.1624–1625. The source first gives a finitely generated subgroup of the
embedded Z wr Z with an order-two class and then asks the finitely presented
question. Its distinction between the two finiteness conditions is explicit.
The exact PDF SHA256 agrees with the author source manifest. Candidate Turn4 and
Turn5 were read to identify the claims; historical REVIEW.md, FINAL_RESULT.md,
REVIEWED_STATUS.md, and sibling/root conclusions remain unopened at this seal.

## Reconstructed germ implication

If N≠1 is finitely generated and normal in any finite-piece PL interval group H,
the union U of supports of a finite generating set is the support of N. Each
generator has finitely many support components, so U has finitely many components.
Normality makes U invariant. An increasing homeomorphism preserving U fixes each
component and each of its endpoints. At the left endpoint a of any component,
log h'(a+) is a character of H. If it were zero on every N generator, finite
piecewise linearity and a minimum of finitely many positive initial affine
neighborhoods would produce an initial interval missing U, a contradiction.
Thus a character is nonzero on N. This proof uses ordinary finite generation,
not finite normal generation. At a common fixed point in F, all one-sided slopes
are powers of two even when a is nondyadic, so log base two is integral.

For T=preimage of the torsion subgroup of H/H', every real character vanishes
on T. Therefore T contains no nontrivial finitely generated H-normal subgroup.
An actual torsion representative h≠1 has an infinitely generated normal closure.
This is compatible with finite presentation of H and supplies no general solution.
In particular, h's own support endpoints need not be fixed by H; extracting a
character there without proving a common fixed point would be invalid.

## New genuine F control

Use t with vertices (0,0),(1/2,1/4),(3/4,1/2),(1,1). Put a equal to a rescaled
copy of t supported on (1/2,3/4), and a_i=t^i a t^-i. Supports of a_i have
disjoint interiors and all maps are in F. The subgroup generated by t and a is
faithfully Z wr Z: finite lamp words are detected in their disjoint supports;
t^k for k≠0 has nontrivial global endpoint slope and cannot be such a lamp word.
For m≥2 take H_m=<t,a_0^m,r>, r=a_1 a_0^-1. In the additive Laurent model,
its base is I_m=(m,t-1) and derived base is (t-1)I_m. The map
f -> (f(1)/m, f'(1) mod m) on I_m has kernel (t-1)I_m: when f(1)=0 write
f=(t-1)g; then f'(1)=g(1), whose divisibility by m is exactly g∈I_m.
Thus r has class of exact order m, while r itself has infinite order.
The independent segment implementation verifies r^m=[t,a_0^m]. Its global
endpoint slopes are 1, while its right germ at 1/4 is 1/2; t moves 1/4.
This directly falsifies the inference that all own-support germs of a torsion
witness must vanish. The normal closure of r is (t-1)Z[t,t^-1], freely generated
as an abelian group by the shifts of r, so is infinitely generated.

Additional controls include disjoint components meeting at a fixed point and
a genuine F map with nondyadic isolated fixed point 7/24 and right slope 4:
vertices (0,0),(1/4,1/8),(3/8,5/8),(1/2,3/4),(1,1). Its adjacent support
components remain distinct even though they share that fixed endpoint.

## Reconstructed nilpotent countermodel and nonembedding

For P_m=<x,y,z | [x,z]=[y,z]=1, [x,y]=z^m>, with commutator xyx^-1y^-1,
the faithful matrix normal form is

    M(a,b,c) = [[1,ma,c],[0,1,b],[0,0,1]], a,b,c∈Z.

Collection to x^a y^b z^k gives c=mab+k; distinct collected forms give distinct
matrices and all matrices are generated, so this realizes the finite presentation
exactly. Commutators have central coordinate m(ab'-a'b), whence P_m'=mZ in
the center and P_m,ab=Z²⊕Z/m. The central z is infinite order and its class is
exactly order m, detected by c mod m. Signed powers have coordinates
(na,nb,nc+m n(n-1)ab/2). They prove torsion-freeness, unique positive roots and
balanced signed powers by comparing (a,b), then the central coordinate. The
lexicographic cone on (a,b,c) is multiplicatively closed and conjugation-invariant.
Thus bi-orderability, finite presentation, unique roots, balanced powers and no
nonabelian free subgroup together fail to ensure torsion-free abelianization.
These matrices are not an F embedding.

For any homomorphism P_m→PL_+(I), suppose image(z)=h≠1 and restrict to a support
component J of h. Images of x and y centralize h, hence fix J setwise. On J,
the initial-slope map on the one-bump centralizer is injective: a commuting map
with slope one fixes an initial neighborhood, and an iterate of h or h^-1 moves
any point there. The centralizer is therefore abelian. But [x,y]|J=h^m|J≠1,
a contradiction. Every such homomorphism kills z and has abelian image; no P_m
with m≥1 embeds in F. The identity image is a covered boundary case.

This proof independently matches the precise one-bump hypothesis of
Kassabov–Matucci Corollary4.5/Lemma4.6 (printed pp.9–10); no cyclic-centralizer
claim is necessary. Bleak's geometric Theorem1.1 and algebraic Theorem1.2 classify
solvable subgroups via towers/iterated wreath groups; those primary statements do
not themselves convert finite normal generation into finite generation and do
not imply that all subgroups are solvable. No such conversion is used here.

## Preliminary result and exact gap

Turn4's theorem and Turn5's countermodel/nonembedding argument survive this
reconstruction. The new genuine F example closes a finite-test coverage gap at
actual torsion witnesses, and the independent matrix implementation checks an
alternative normal form. Neither addresses arbitrary finitely presented H≤F.
The exact missing step is an admissible mechanism using finite presentation to
exclude a noncentral torsion witness with infinitely generated normal closure,
or a proved embedded finitely presented example possessing one.
