## 4. Independent derivation of the main theorem

Let sigma be Frobenius on k. F is sigma-semilinear and V sigma-inverse-semilinear. In the ordered prime-field basis e0,...,e5 the nonzero assignments are

    F: e0 -> e1, e1 -> e2, e3 -> e4;
    V: e0 -> e5, e3 -> e2, e5 -> e4.

All other images vanish. Directly FV=VF=0, imF=kerV=span(e1,e2,e4), imV=kerF=span(e2,e4,e5), and F^3=V^3=0. No rank-balance inference is needed.

Set u=e1+e3+e5, v=e2+e4, w=2e1+e3, z=e2, a=e0, b=e1. The inverse coordinate identities are e0=a, e1=b, e2=z, e3=w-2b, e4=v-z, e5=u-w+b. Thus this is a basis over every characteristic without division. Its actions are

    Fu=Vu=v, Fv=Vv=0,
    Fw=v+z, Vw=z, Fz=Vz=0,
    Fa=b, Va=u-w+b, Fb=z, Vb=0.

The subspaces M1=span(u,v), M2=span(u,v,w,z), M3=M are F,V-stable. Their three consecutive factors each have the standard supersingular elliptic p-kernel module I with Fx=Vx=y and Fy=Vy=0. Each factor is I, so reversal preserves the required qss property. For duality, evaluation gives FD(phi)(m)=sigma(phi(Vm)) and VD(phi)(m)=sigma^-1(phi(Fm)). With general coefficient matrices A,B this means matrices sigma(B)^t and sigma^-1(A)^t, respectively. Only because the displayed coefficients lie in Fp do these simplify to plain transposes. The actual dual actions are FD:e2*->e3*, e4*->e5*, e5*->e0* and VD:e1*->e0*, e2*->e1*, e4*->e3*.
