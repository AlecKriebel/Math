Exact target: Takao, contribution in OWR 42/2023, printed 2476-2480, PDF pages 100-104 one-based. Immediately after Proposition 2 on printed 2479, the unnumbered question asks whether the assertion of Proposition 2 remains true for n >= 3. Proposition 2 assumes n <= 2 and that G, the special fibre of a p-torsion finite flat commutative W-group scheme calG of rank p^(2n), is quasi-supersingular. Its conclusion is that G and calG are automatically self-dual. The antecedent is special-fibre qss, not calG qss. The global report hypotheses are p > 3 and k perfect, W = W(k); Proposition 1 additionally assumes k algebraically closed. Proposition 2 itself does not repeat that extra hypothesis. A counterexample over an algebraically closed k in characteristic p >= 5 is within the global perfect-field setting and is sufficient to refute the universal higher-n assertion.

Definition 1(3), printed 2477: qss means a length-n filtration by p-torsion finite flat commutative group schemes over the stated base with each successive quotient isomorphic over that base to the actual p-kernel of a supersingular elliptic curve. A module filtration with merely rank-two factors, formal F,V notation, or nonsaturated integral lattices is insufficient. Over W the curves must be elliptic curves over W whose special fibres are supersingular. The zero case n=0 is allowed; n=1,2 are attributed small-rank cases and n=3 is first possible refutation rank p^6.

Success criteria for a negative answer: give a checkable p-killed finite flat commutative calG/W of rank p^6 (or some higher p^(2n)), establish that its special fibre G has the exact qss filtration, and prove G is not isomorphic to G^D as a group scheme. This also rules out calG ~= calG^D by Cartier-dual base-change compatibility. A stronger calG-qss construction is welcome but not logically needed for this target. Field descent, polarizations, Jacobians, Raynaud types, and the Coleman conjecture are distinct claims and must not be inferred.
