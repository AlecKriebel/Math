## Exact proposition under review

Let

\[
f=x^4+xy^3+y^4-3x^2yz-4xy^2z+2x^2z^2+xz^3+yz^3+z^4.
\]

The principal algebraic claim is that `f` is a sum of squares over the real
numbers, but is not a finite sum of squares of polynomials in `Q[x,y,z]`,
without any imposed degree bound. The extension needed by the submission is
that `f` does not belong to the rational quadratic module generated by
`g=1-x^2-y^2-z^2`. Here “rational SOS” means rational coefficients in each
polynomial being squared, not merely rational coefficients in their sum.

Success criteria: verify the displayed coefficients, prove the necessary
Galois action and incidence facts, reduce arbitrary inhomogeneous squares
to quadratic forms, and show no unjustified degree truncation. A finite SOS
search would not meet these criteria.

## The quartic is the indicated norm

Set `h(t)=t^4-t+1`. For the four distinct roots `a_i` in a normal closure,
put `l_i=x+a_i y+a_i^2 z`. The scalar resultant is

\[
\operatorname{Res}_t(h(t),x+yt+zt^2)=\prod_{i=1}^4 l_i.
\]

Since `h` is monic, there is no omitted leading-coefficient factor. Its
Sylvester matrix, in the coefficient convention used by the fresh checker, is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&-1&1&0\\
0&1&0&0&-1&1\\
z&y&x&0&0&0\\
0&z&y&x&0&0\\
0&0&z&y&x&0\\
0&0&0&z&y&x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Each determinant term has exactly four factors linear in `x,y,z`, so it is a
homogeneous quartic even when the specialized quadratic drops degree. The
fresh computation evaluates this scalar determinant by fraction-free Bareiss
elimination at the 15 nodes `(a,b,1)` with nonnegative integers `a+b<=4`.
It solves for all 15 homogeneous quartic coefficients over `Q`. The evaluation
matrix has exact determinant **47,775,744**, so these values determine the
polynomial identically. All 15 coefficients agree, including six zero
coefficients. This is an exact identity check with a proven finite-dimensional
degree bound, not a numerical sampling argument for positivity.

The complete coefficient vector is in `EXACT_CONTROLS.json`. Separate exact
specializations with `z=0`, negative coordinates, and the origin check that no
division by a specialized leading coefficient has entered the implementation.

## Root and Galois facts

For `t<=0`, `h(t)>=1`; for `0<=t<=1`, `h(t)=t^4+(1-t)>0` because its two
nonnegative summands cannot both vanish; for `t>=1`, `h(t)>=1`. Thus there are
no real roots. Independently, the exact Sturm sequence

\[
h,quad 4t^3-1,quad \tfrac34 t-1,quad -\tfrac{229}{27}
\]

has two variations at both infinities, giving zero real roots. The exact
discriminant is 229, so the quartic is separable and primes 2 and 3 are good.

Modulo 2, `h=t^4+t+1` has no roots in `F_2`. The only irreducible monic
quadratic is `t^2+t+1`; modulo this quadratic, `t^3=1` and `t^4=t`, so `h`
has remainder 1. A reducible quartic with no linear factor would have two
quadratic factors, so this proves irreducibility. The fresh checker instead
uses the exact Frobenius criterion: `t^16=t mod h` and
`gcd(h,t^4-t)=1`. The derivative modulo 2 is 1.

Modulo 3,

\[
h=(t+1)(t^3-t^2+t+1).
\]

The cubic has values 1, 2, 1 at 0, 1, 2 modulo 3, hence is irreducible. It
does not vanish at -1, so the two factors are distinct. The standard
factorization-cycle theorem at unramified primes therefore gives a 4-cycle
and a 3-cycle in `Gal(h/Q)`.

The group is a subgroup of `S_4`, and its order is divisible by 4 and 3, hence
by 12. If its order were 12, it would be an index-two subgroup of `S_4` and
therefore `A_4`, which contains no 4-cycle. To justify the uniqueness used
here: any nontrivial homomorphism from `S_4` to a group of order two maps all
conjugate transpositions to its nonidentity element (transpositions generate
`S_4`), and so agrees with the sign homomorphism. Thus the Galois group is
`S_4`. As a separate finite group control, the fresh checker verifies that
each of the eight possible 3-cycles together with a fixed 4-cycle generates
all 24 permutations. This calculation supplements the group proof.

## Real sum of squares and real zeros

Conjugation acts as two disjoint transpositions on the four roots. Label the
pairs `(a_1,a_2)` and `(a_3,a_4)`, with `a_2=bar(a_1)` and `a_4=bar(a_3)`.
For real `x,y,z`,

\[
f=l_1\bar l_1 l_3\bar l_3=|l_1l_3|^2
 = (\operatorname{Re}(l_1l_3))^2+
   (\operatorname{Im}(l_1l_3))^2.
\]

These are real homogeneous quadratic forms, so this is a global real SOS
identity, not merely nonnegativity on the ball.

For any distinct roots `a,b`, the two lines meet at

\[
P_{ab}=[ab:-(a+b):1].
\]

Substitution verifies both equations directly. If `b=bar(a)`, this coordinate
vector is real: `(abs(a)^2,-2 Re(a),1)`. It is nonzero because its last
coordinate is 1. The point is a real zero of `f`. Any three line equations
are independent, since their coefficient determinant is a nonzero
Vandermonde determinant. Consequently the two real pair points are distinct,
and each of the four lines meets the other three in three distinct projective
points. No coincident-root or triple-incidence case has been silently assumed
away; separability and the Vandermonde determinant exclude them.

## Rational quadratic forms cannot occur

Suppose `f=sum_j q_j^2` for homogeneous rational quadratic forms. At the real
vector for a conjugate pair, `f=0`, so every `q_j` vanishes there. Its rational
coefficients are fixed by every Galois automorphism. Applying `S_4` to the
explicit coordinates `P_ab`, whose last coordinate stays 1, forces `q_j`
to vanish at all six pair points. One conjugate pair suffices for this orbit
argument; using both real pair points adds no assumption.

Fix a line `l_a=0`. Substituting `x=-ay-a^2z` into `q_j` gives a binary
homogeneous form of degree at most two. The other three roots `b` give its
zeros at `(y,z)=(-(a+b),1)`. These are three distinct finite values of `y/z`.
A polynomial of degree at most two with three distinct roots is zero, so the
restriction is identically zero. Equivalently, `l_a` divides `q_j` in the
normal-closure polynomial ring. This holds for all four distinct, pairwise
nonassociate linear forms. Unique factorization implies their product divides
`q_j`; its degree is four, whereas `q_j` has degree two. Therefore every
`q_j` is zero, contradicting `f(1,0,0)=1`.

No stronger rational-SOS conjecture has replaced the central problem. The
obstruction is established by the explicit real zero, the verified `S_4`
action, and elementary polynomial interpolation.

## Inhomogeneous and high-degree rational squares

For a finite SOS identity `f=sum_j r_j^2` with rational polynomials, let `d`
be the largest total degree among nonzero summands. The degree-`2d` component
is the sum of squares of the degree-`d` leading forms. It cannot vanish
identically: a sum of real squares that is zero at every real vector has each
summand zero at every vector, and a real polynomial zero everywhere is the
zero polynomial. Thus `2d<=4`. Evaluating at the origin forces every constant
coefficient to vanish. The degree-two component is now the sum of squares of
the linear parts, and must vanish; hence all linear parts vanish. Every `r_j`
is a homogeneous quadratic form, which the preceding argument excludes.

This is an argument for arbitrary finite degrees. Bounded computations do not
justify the conclusion and are not used to do so.

For completeness, the displayed ball module also has no high-degree escape.
If `f=sum a_j^2+g sum b_k^2`, evaluation at zero annihilates all constant
parts because both weights there are 1. Its degree-two component then
annihilates all linear parts. Once every summand vanishes to order at least
two, degree four is exactly `sum (a_j^[2])^2+sum (b_k^[2])^2`: the term
`-(x^2+y^2+z^2) sum b_k^2` begins at degree six. This yields the excluded
rational SOS quartic, irrespective of the higher degrees. The separate
module/quantifier audit family owns the broader claim assessment.

## Optional beta certificate, checked independently

Let `beta` be a negative root of `beta^3-4 beta-1=0`. There are exactly two
negative roots, one in `(-2,-1)` and one in `(-1,0)`, and one positive root;
the fresh Sturm check records those counts. None is zero. In the quotient
field, `beta^(-1)=beta^2-4`. Expanding `U^2-beta V^2-4f` in the basis
`1,beta,beta^2` yields zero in every one of the 15 quartic monomials for the
displayed `U,V` in the submission. Therefore the identity holds at either
negative real root, and `f=(U/2)^2+(sqrt(-beta)V/2)^2` is valid.

This gives a second global real SOS certificate. It uses exact quotient
arithmetic, no numerical root selection, no denominator clearing copied from
the submitted checker, and no possibility of accidentally selecting a
positive root with a negative square weight.
