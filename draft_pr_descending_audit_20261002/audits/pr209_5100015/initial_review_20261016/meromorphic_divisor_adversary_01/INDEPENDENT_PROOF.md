# Direct area-divisor proof

This is an independent reconstruction of the genuine elliptic-caustic target. The mechanism and formulas were frozen before reading the submitted proof. It uses neither the submitted edge-vector transformation nor reduction of the areas to a scalar dn trace. Classical Jacobi identities and the elliptic billiard parametrization remain shared standard inputs.

Let E have axes a>b>0 and a strictly interior confocal elliptic caustic. Use Jacobi modulus 0<k<1, complementary modulus k', quarter periods K,K', and parameter v in (0,K), so

    alpha=a cn(v)/dn(v), beta=b cn(v), k'=beta/alpha.

These imply a²−alpha²=b²−beta²>0. The canonical vertices are

    p(t+js), p(t)=(a sn t,b cn t), s=2v.

This differs from Stachel's x-coordinate by a fixed reflection; both signed areas reverse and their product is preserved. For primitive period n and winding w,

    v=2Kw/n, gcd(n,w)=1, 0<w<n/2.

The contact/chord equation follows directly from addition:

    (sn t dn v)x/a+(cn t)y/b=cn v

for endpoints p(t−v),p(t+v). The consecutive-tangent intersection is

    q(t)=(a sn t dn v/cn v,b cn t/cn v),

as substitution in both endpoint tangent equations verifies. The center foot to the tangent at p(t) is

    f(t)=(a b² sn t,a²b cn t)/(a²−(a²−b²)sn²t).

Define the meromorphic area functions, in the original-vertex phase,

    A(t)=1/2 sum[j=0..n−1] q(t+v+js) wedge q(t+v+(j+1)s),
    B(t)=1/2 sum[j=0..n−1] f(t+js) wedge f(t+(j+1)s).

They equal the requested two signed areas on real t. There is no division by either area.

## Periods and symmetry

Each area summand has real period 2K: p,q,f change overall sign under 2K. Let m=n/gcd(n,2). The real subgroup generated by s modulo 2K has order m, so the cyclic sums have real period L=2K/m. Both area functions change sign under 2iK': sn is unchanged, cn changes sign, and the foot denominator depends only on sn². Thus a reflection of the two coordinates reverses each wedge product. Both have period 4iK'.

Both area functions are even in t. For B, f(−z)=R f(z), R=diag(−1,1), and reflection contributes det R=−1 while reversal of the cyclic indices contributes the second minus sign. Explicitly the edge at indices j,j+1 after t→−t becomes the edge at indices −j−1,−j with its order reversed. For A, the same computation on midpoint arguments replaces j by −j−2; the extra v is essential. Hence A(−t)=A(t), B(−t)=B(t).

## Pole locations and orders

The only poles of sn and cn occur at iK' modulo 2K and 2iK', and they are simple. Neighboring q values cannot be singular simultaneously, since s is nonzero modulo 2K for a genuine primitive period n≥3. Therefore all possible poles of A are at most simple, at

    t=−v+iK' modulo L and 2iK'.                 (A)

At the sn/cn poles, f has a removable zero: its numerator has order one and denominator order two, with nonzero leading coefficient since a≠b. Its other possible poles are the zeros of a²−(a²−b²)sn²t. The Jacobi half/quarter shifts give

    sn(K+iK'±v)=dn v/(k cn v),
    cn(K+iK'±v)=−i k'/(k cn v).

The first squared value equals a²/(a²−b²). These are all denominator zeros: sn² has degree two on the torus with periods 2K,2iK', and the two displayed points are distinct because 0<v<K. They are simple zeros; neither point is a branch value of sn. Thus f has simple poles precisely at K+iK'±v and their half-period translates.

The two pole points differ by s, so a wedge product of neighboring feet can have both factors singular. However their normal vectors are equal:

    (sn/a,cn/b) at either displayed point = (1,−i)/(k alpha).

Each foot's residue vector is a scalar multiple of this isotropic direction. Their wedge is zero, so the coefficient of a potential double pole vanishes. All summands, and therefore B, have poles of order at most one. Translation by the cyclic subgroup puts every possible pole in the class

    t=K+v+iK' modulo L and 2iK'.              (B)

No residue value or nonvanishing claim is needed beyond this order bound.

## Complementary zeros and the parity condition

If n is odd, m=n, v=wL and K=mL/2. Thus the real pole classes in (A),(B) are 0,L/2. If n=2m with m odd, w must be odd, v=wL/2 and K=mL/2. The real pole classes are then L/2,0. In both cases m odd is exactly n not divisible by four.

For an even, L-periodic function F with F(t+2iK')=−F(t), and beta in {0,L/2}, regularity at beta+iK' forces

    F(beta+iK')=F(−beta−iK')
                =F(beta−iK')=−F(beta+iK'),

so F(beta+iK')=0. Each area is regular at the other area's possible poles because the real classes are distinct. Therefore A vanishes at every possible pole of B, and B vanishes at every possible pole of A. Since those poles are at most simple, every pole of AB is removable.

The product AB is consequently holomorphic on the compact torus with periods L,4iK', and is constant. This establishes the product for every real phase and every admissible primitive star winding. Reversal of traversal reverses both areas. Repeating a primitive orbit r times multiplies each signed area by r and their product by r². If the listed period N is not divisible by four, its primitive period divisor cannot be divisible by four; the repetition extension therefore follows.

## Boundaries and exact remaining scope

All real feet are finite because a²−(a²−b²)sn²t≥b²>0. Real outer intersections are finite: parallel endpoint tangents would require antipodal endpoints, giving a center-crossing chord, which cannot touch a strictly interior nondegenerate elliptic caustic. N=2 cannot occur in this domain. Stars never require reordering points.

The proof uses 0<k<1, positive caustic axes, and 0<v<K. It does not extend the meromorphic torus argument to the circle or focal/hyperbolic caustics. The circular case follows separately: the family consists of rigid rotations of fixed-step regular stars, and the two areas are individually fixed. Hyperbolic and focal caustics remain outside this theorem. The source paper's explicit definition of EB uses two confocal ellipses.
