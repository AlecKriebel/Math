The candidate's unbounded-degree mismatch is almost sure, not just a comparison of distributional supports. Because every vertex has a child, follow the first child forever. Along this ray the offspring numbers are independent with the original law. For each finite `M`, the probability all those numbers are at most `M` is zero, since `P(D<=M)<1`. Taking a countable union over `M` proves the ray, and hence the tree, has unbounded offspring almost surely. Also `E D=sum_{k>=1} P(D>=k)=infinity`, whereas every individual offspring count is finite. This is precisely the distinction between local finiteness and bounded degree that the paper's introduction makes.

## Full-body mechanism checks and boundary cautions

PDF pages 1–7 give the complete setup and all main statements. Pages 7–10 construct indexed BRW infection trails and establish the walk-counting local-survival criterion. In Lemma 3.1's proof, PDF 9 / p1571, the expected number of trails following a fixed length-`n` walk and finishing by a fixed time `T` is

\[
\frac{\lambda^n}{(n-1)!}\int_0^T e^{-s}s^{n-1}\,ds
\leq \frac{(\lambda T)^n}{n!}.
\]

This is used to ensure arbitrarily long return trails signify local survival at unbounded times. A uniform degree bound `Delta` gives at most `Delta^n` possible walks, so summing the finite-time bounds is controlled by `exp(lambda Delta T)`. Equivalently, a finite-start constant-rate CP on such a graph is dominated by a pure-birth population with per-particle birth rate `lambda Delta`. This supplies a direct bounded-degree nonexplosion check. It is an elementary deduction from the model and the displayed estimate, not a new theorem attributed to the paper about all locally finite graphs. It does not apply to the candidate's unbounded degrees or arbitrary unbounded fitness values.

Pages 10–16 prove the BRW global-survival bound by finite-length generation/walk counting and second moments. Pages 16–20 use a summable spatial weight and a BRW supermartingale to prove nonsurvival below a threshold; the spatial summability is not a summable infection-time construction. Pages 21–22 complete the other BRW phase-transition bound by fixed finite side-trip counting. None of these arguments leaves independent unrevealed residual CP lifetimes along a growing-degree infection ray in a way that yields the candidate event.

Pages 22–23 prove the first CP counterexample by comparison with homogeneous trees of finite offspring `c` and `d`. Pages 23–26 develop the second CP counterexample: finite-tree persistence gives many attempts to transmit over a fixed finite distance; induction gives a uniform positive probability of later root infection for arbitrarily large times. Theorem 4.1's long survival time for a finite tree is quoted from Stacey's finite-tree work, not proved here. Large but finite persistence times and finite sets of simultaneously infected transmission vertices cannot be relabeled as infinite infection in finite time. Pages 26–28 give conclusions, phase-transition conjectures, and references; they add no such explosion result. Conjecture 5.3 is stated for locally finite graphs, but is a conjecture about invariance of CP critical values after adding an edge, not a proved explosion construction or nonexplosion theorem.

Two specific boundary issues deserve care:

1. PDF 4 / p1566 remarks that the no-extinction assumption can be relaxed by conditioning on nonextinction and that the case `d=infinity` will be clear from the results. This is not a displayed theorem for infinite-mean CP explosion. Finite embedded subtrees may indeed yield BRW critical-value consequences when `d=infinity`; I do not deny those consequences. They do not automatically transfer to a process that suppresses multiple particles, and a vanishing survival threshold alone does not give summable passage times or simultaneous infinite infection.
2. The rendered PDF 17 / p1579 literally prints expectation tending to infinity in a sentence whose differential inequality and Lemma 3.4 statement require tending to zero. This local typo is recorded rather than silently corrected into a quote. It is immaterial to the candidate exclusion, which uses explicit standing hypotheses and the full mechanism review; it is not a reason to allege a universal flaw in the paper.
