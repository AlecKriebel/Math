# Independent target baseline, sealed before candidate exposure

Seal time: 2026-10-03T06:13:18Z. Audit completeness: 10%; discovery status: undetermined. No candidate file, prior audit, sibling report, checker, or derivation has been read.

The literal 1998 question concerns a periodic large square nearest-neighbor lattice, unit-modulus hopping entries, zero diagonal, Hermitian reverse edges, and E(T)=sum of the lowest N/4 eigenvalues with 4 dividing N. All edge phases are admissible; it conjectures existence of an optimizer with oriented elementary flux +pi/2 everywhere. The page's abstract also raises the density rule, but the exact technical statement fixes the quarter sum. Its 'large' qualification is not an explicit universal finite-size quantifier. Finite refutations and thermodynamic refutations must be distinguished.

Use sites (x,y) in Z/Lx x Z/Ly, forward phase a(x,y) for T[(x,y),(x+1,y)] and b(x,y) for T[(x,y),(x,y+1)]. The counterclockwise plaquette phase is a(x,y)+b(x+1,y)-a(x,y+1)-b(x,y). Its sign reverses under conjugation, which preserves the spectrum, so either uniform handedness is spectrally equivalent. Gauge is T -> D* T D. Gauge invariants include all circuit phases; on a torus the elementary phases have one product constraint and leave TWO noncontractible loop phases. Thus 'same plaquette flux' alone does not imply gauge equivalence or identical finite spectra. Global uniform flux phi requires N phi=0 mod 2pi, automatically for phi=pi/2 and 4|N. The literal size convention leaves width 2 ambiguous (simple graph versus two parallel forward bonds); use Lx,Ly>=3, and even Lx,Ly when invoking bipartite symmetry. A square Lx=Ly with 4|N has even side.

A genuine solution must establish global E(T)>=min_h E(T_uniform(pi/2,h)) for every admissible phase field on the intended sizes, or a precisely stated thermodynamic analogue. A Hessian or uniform-flux comparison proves much less. The half-filled result from Lieb 9410025 minimizes all negative levels via reflection positivity, particle-hole symmetry and chemical potential zero; its proof does not automatically optimize a selected lowest quarter. Lieb-Loss 9209031 proves a planar canonical-flux determinant maximum, and selected classes' half-filled spectral sums; determinant comparison neither fixes partial sums nor extends unqualified to toroidal cycles. Their gauge lemma uses ALL circuits. Their section VII gives counterexamples to broader amplitude claims.

Independent falsifiable mechanisms:

1. Variational: E_k(T)=min_{P=P*=P^2,rank P=k} Tr(TP). For bipartite even tori, T=[0 B;B* 0], E_{N/4}=-sum of the largest N/4 singular values of B. Any candidate universal estimate must actually dominate this top-half singular-value sum, not Tr|T| or det T.
2. Gauge/holonomy controls: conjugate phases, permute sites, conjugate by random diagonal unitaries; compare energy. At fixed plaquette phases vary both seam twists continuously; a finite uniform comparator must optimize both.
3. Analytic Hessian at a gapped quarter Fermi level: for edge direction u with H'=V and H''=W, E''=Tr(PW)+2 sum_{o occupied,r empty}|<r|V|o>|^2/(lambda_o-lambda_r). The interlevel term is nonpositive. Gauge vectors should lie in the kernel. A full matrix needs all cross terms and a certified inertia after quotienting gauge. Gap closure invalidates the smooth formula.
4. All-size uniform pi/2 spectrum: independently derive magnetic Bloch reduction, keep quantized momenta and both holonomies. A periodic q=4 cell's characteristic equation should emerge from its matrix, not assumed. Moment-grid minimum proofs must cover frustrated sizes and phase endpoints.
5. Global bounds: degree gives ||T||<=4; Frobenius gives sum s_j^2=2N and E>=-N/sqrt(2) at quarter filling, a coarse lower bound with no uniform-flux conclusion. Stronger estimates must retain the exact selected-level functional.
6. Thermodynamics: cutting or restoring each edge perturbs E_k by at most the trace norm of its rank-two matrix (2). Tiling permits boundary O(perimeter) errors, while a phase-separated density mixture obeys global particle count; occupied ranks need not be one-quarter in EACH tile. Convexification at total density 1/4 can defeat a naive fixed-density tiling inference. A finite energy advantage requires controlled persistence per area to refute the density limit.
