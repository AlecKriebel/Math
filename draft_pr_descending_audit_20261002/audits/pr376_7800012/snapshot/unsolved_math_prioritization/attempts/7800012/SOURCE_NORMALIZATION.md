# Source normalization and eligibility

Target7800012 / AMR-077-0012 is Elliott H. Lieb's1998 quarter-filled-band question in Aizenman's mathematical-physics problem collection. The entire original HTML, including its embedded technical TeX, was read. The linked legacy PDF returns404; it is not needed to recover the complete statement.

The finite matrix has one unit-modulus hopping entry on each nearest-neighbor edge and its complex conjugate on the reverse edge, with zero diagonal. The objective is the sum of the lowest N/4 eigenvalues. The optimization allows arbitrary edge phases, including spatially nonuniform plaquette fluxes. There is no interaction term. The cited1994 reflection-positivity theorem concerns half filling and cannot be substituted for the quarter-filled objective.

For explicit finite results this packet uses the simple L by L torus with even L>=4, so the graph is bipartite, degree4 and N=L² is divisible by4. Statements for rectangular or infinite lattices require their own hypotheses. The source says 'large' and gives a finite-volume objective; it does not explicitly quantify a thermodynamic-limit formulation. A small-size theorem therefore does not settle its intended large-lattice question.

On a torus, local plaquette fluxes alone do not determine the gauge class or spectrum: two noncontractible loop holonomies must also be retained. Lieb–Loss1992 Lemma2.1 uses the flux through every circuit, which is the correct general statement. We normalize the source by keeping all edge phases, equivalently all compatible plaquette fluxes and both loop holonomies. No incomplete comparison at fixed arbitrary twists is promoted to a counterexample.
