# Claims and adversarial review scope

The target question is unresolved after five attempts. This is a partial-results
package, not a proposed proof of the unrestricted Kourovka 21.136 statement.

Review the following specific claims independently:

1. Attempt 1: the factorial-power quotient construction retains an infinite-order
   element and makes the quotient countably based; inheritance is proved only
   for continuous images and open subgroups. Check all cardinal inequalities.
2. Attempt 2: fusion among topological generators of A is exactly the action of
   N_G(A), giving [Z_p^times:U] classes. Compactness makes U closed. A quotient
   smaller than continuum is finite. Ensure the p=2 unit-group case is included.
3. Attempt 3: the torsion quotient makes every induced unit torsion; this does
   not require a uniform exponent. Verify the abelian-kernel argument and its
   induction on the closed derived series of a normal soluble subgroup.
4. Attempt 4: verify the affine conjugation formula, the valuation-class count,
   and the multiplier obstruction in the inverse limit. Replay the exact finite
   controls independently. They must not be interpreted as a proof about all
   infinite profinite groups.
5. Attempt 5: verify the finite-quotient embedding of the class space, the
   closedness of bounded-order loci, the Cantor-tree cardinal argument, the
   complete metric on the non-torsion locus, and the Baire isolated-point step.
   Relative isolation must not be confused with an open conjugacy class.
6. Attempt 5: verify the finite-centralizer bound excluding an open
   infinite-order conjugacy class, and the precise remaining local assertion.
7. Source scope: Wilson's displayed Corollary 3.1 cannot be used in its
   unrestricted printed form. Its claimed unrestricted finiteness has the
   explicit counterexample product C_p. The package uses only the finite-
   generation theorem. Problem 21.137 is excluded throughout.

The finite calculations cover ten explicitly specified groups, the largest of
order 512. They support only the affine formulas. All unrestricted statements
about topological groups require the written arguments, not those calculations.
