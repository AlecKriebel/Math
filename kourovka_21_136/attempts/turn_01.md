# Attempt 1: quotient reduction and the precise finite-generation boundary

Date: 2026-10-03. Problem: KOU-21.136 / catalogue ID 2645.
This is the first substantive proof attempt. Source retrieval and the prior-attempt
gate are not counted as proof turns. No resolution is claimed.

Write P(G) for the assertion that the number of G-conjugacy classes represented
by infinite-order elements is strictly smaller than c = 2^aleph_0.

## 1. Inheritance that actually holds

If pi: G -> Q is a continuous surjective homomorphism and P(G) holds, then P(Q)
holds. Choose a lift in G of one representative of every infinite-order class of
Q. Each lift has infinite order. Lifts of nonconjugate representatives cannot be
conjugate, because pi preserves conjugacy. This proves the cardinal inequality.

If H is open in G, then P(H) holds. A G-class meeting H splits into at most
[G:H] H-classes: write G as the union of left cosets r_i H and use
x^(r_i h) = (x^r_i)^h. Multiplication of a cardinal smaller than c by a fixed
finite integer still gives a cardinal smaller than c. These arguments do not
establish inheritance by arbitrary closed subgroups.

## 2. A counterexample can be chosen countably based

Suppose P(G) holds and g has infinite order. For each n >= 1 choose an open normal
subgroup V_n not containing g^(n!). Set N_n = V_1 intersect ... intersect V_n and
K = intersection_n N_n. Then Q = G/K is a closed subgroup of the countable
product of finite groups G/N_n, hence is a metrizable, countably based profinite
group. The image of g has infinite order: if its order were m, then for n >= m
we would have g^(n!) in K, contradicting K subset V_n. By section 1, P(Q) holds.
Thus proving the conjecture for countably based profinite groups would prove it
in general. No finite-generation assertion follows from countable baseness.

Wilson's 2023 Lemma 4.2 already proves that P(G) forces pi(G), the set of primes
occurring in finite continuous quotients, to be finite. Consequently an infinite
procyclic subgroup of such a G has a Z_p factor for at least one prime p. One can
choose g above to be a p-element of infinite order, and the same construction
preserves that property in Q. This finite-prime reduction is credited to Wilson,
not claimed as new.

## 3. Why reducing to a finitely generated subgroup is invalid

Wilson's Theorem A proves that a topologically finitely generated profinite
group with P is finite. In particular every topologically finitely generated
continuous quotient of a hypothetical counterexample is finite. It does not
follow that the closed procyclic subgroup generated by g satisfies P internally:
many internally different classes in a closed subgroup may fuse in G. Neither
the quotient argument nor the finite-index argument controls infinite-index
subgroup fusion.

The published Corollary 3.1, on printed p. 561, omits finite generation in its
displayed statement, although the immediately preceding sentence specifies the
finitely generated case and its proof invokes Theorem C, which explicitly
requires finite generation. The unrestricted finiteness statement is false:
the infinite group product_{n>=1} C_p has exponent p and hence zero infinite-order
classes. We therefore use only the finitely generated version; this is a
source-scope correction, not a solution of the torsion question.

## Outcome

The general problem reduces to a countably based, infinitely generated profinite
group with finite prime support and an infinite-order p-element. Every finitely
generated continuous image must be finite. The missing step remains control of
conjugacy fusion on infinite-index closed subgroups. Attempt 2 investigates that
fusion directly inside a procyclic subgroup.

Primary source: John S. Wilson, Profinite groups with few conjugacy classes of
elements of infinite order, Archiv der Mathematik 120 (2023), 557-563,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-023-01851-4.
