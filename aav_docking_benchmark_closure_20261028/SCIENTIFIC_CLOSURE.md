## The conclusion that can be earned

The defensible finish is a scoped performance statement: **for specified algorithms, input information, receptor states, chemistry classes, compute budgets, and held-out targets, estimate site recovery, pose recovery, and discrimination of experimentally tested binders from tested nonbinders, with uncertainty.** A finite benchmark cannot establish universal reliability of molecular docking. It can establish conditional usefulness, unreliability in a defined regime, or insufficient precision.

The repository's protocol already makes many of the right distinctions (lines 19–38, 46–66). The remaining scientific task is to instantiate them in frozen cohorts and execute the comparisons. A numerical pass threshold is needed only for an affirmative reliability claim; an interpretable negative or mixed result is publishable. The threshold must be chosen for the intended use before inspecting confirmatory outcomes. Merely exceeding random performance is weaker than being reliable enough to use.

## Three different questions and their ground truth

| Question | Ground truth needed | Evidence that would answer it | Evidence that does not answer it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where does a known binder bind? | Valid experimental binding site, including biologically relevant assembly and uncertainty | Blind or predicted-site Top-1/Top-5 site recovery, compared with credible surface-patch baselines | A ligand-derived search box; pocket score alone |
| What pose does a known binder adopt? | Complete, chemically correct ligand coordinates supported by local density | Receptor-aligned ligand RMSD, contacts, geometry and clashes; assess ranked output and common-pool ranking | Scoring a native pose, independently aligning the ligand, low total energy |
| Which glycans bind under an assay context? | Tested positives and tested low/no-binding controls, with chemical identities, measurement limits and assay conditions | Within-assay discrimination/ranking on held-out identities and targets, including size/charge-matched contrasts | Incorrect poses as substitute nonbinders; untested glycans as negatives; docking energy converted to experimental affinity |

Two decoy collections are therefore needed. **Structural decoys** are wrong poses/sites of a known ligand and test pose ranking. **Assay controls** are independently tested alternative ligand identities and test specificity. The first cannot replace the second. A geometrically wrong pose can be energetically favorable even for a true binder. Conversely, failure of one pose prediction does not establish absence of binding.

## Minimal decisive sequence

1. **Freeze a reference and assay ledger first.** For every eligible case record deposited version, biological assembly, chemical graph/stereochemistry, atom map, missing atoms, local density evidence, apo–holo mapping, and admissible endpoint. Grade cases separately as atomic-pose references, site-only references, or assay-only specificity references. Record exclusions and unsupported chemistry before docking outcomes are known.
2. **Close a small development set.** Reproduce native-coordinate scoring controls and one released benchmark example; execute the AAV9 diagnostic plus unrelated nonviral examples spanning the intended chemistry. This establishes that preparation and metrics work. It does not establish general performance. AAV9 monosaccharide redocking cannot test glycosidic-torsion handling.
3. **Freeze a manageable confirmatory comparison.** Use the proposed 24–36 complexes across at least 12 families as a starting inventory, then choose cohort size/claim breadth from the desired uncertainty. Prioritize equally informed full-surface/predicted-site versus true-site comparisons, ligand-stripped holo versus independent apo structures, and complete assembly neighborhoods versus deliberately incomplete controls. Different programs must receive comparable information and budgets. Keep experimental restraint and oracle inputs visibly separate from blind inputs.
4. **Preserve the evidence needed to diagnose failures.** Record site coverage, best returned pose, Top-1/5 poses, rank of the closest pose, and ranking on the same candidate pool. If generated candidates before ranking are unavailable, call the failure “no near-native pose returned”; do not assert that search never generated one. A common-pool rescue is evidence about ranking conditional on that pool, not proof that the original sampler was adequate.
5. **Run specificity only on assay-qualified cases.** Evaluate structural recovery and experimental discrimination separately; require both for the broadest claim. Use the same assay/protein context, preserve censoring and uncertain labels, and show raw as well as size/charge-matched results. A single favorable glycan score has no discriminative content. A small, well-defined panel is more informative than a large set with guessed labels.
6. **Publish conditional outcomes and a failure taxonomy.** Report each method/regime with family-aware uncertainty and preparation sensitivity. A result may be “poses recoverable given the site, but blind site localization and assay discrimination remain unreliable.” This is a complete scientific outcome if the negative conclusions have adequate precision. Archive the cohort, inputs, commands, raw outputs and analysis so another researcher can independently recompute every table.

## Existing evidence that can shorten the route

**HADDOCK benchmark:** Ranaudo et al. provide 89 bound complexes and 55 unbound cases, with downloadable prepared inputs and configurations. Both restraint scenarios supply the protein interface. Their acceptable-pose cutoff is interface-aligned ligand RMSD ≤3 Å; the repository's primary endpoint is ≤2 Å. The approximately 70%/40% Top-5 headline cannot be treated as blind-docking performance or directly compared to an endpoint with a different cutoff. Reuse cases for development or explicit external baseline reproduction; audit overlap before treating them as independent confirmation. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11480977/), [authors' data](https://github.com/haddocking/protein-glycans).

**AAV9 reference:** RCSB identifies 7MTZ as terminal-galactose-bound AAV9 at pH 7.4 and 7MT0 as the corresponding unbound pH 7.4 structure. Both biological assemblies are 60-mers. Current nominal resolutions are 2.43 and 2.82 Å, respectively. These entries justify an apo/holo diagnostic after local ligand and assembly checks; they do not establish specificity for full cell-surface glycans. [7MTZ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7MTZ), [7MT0](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7MT0).

**Existing AAV specificity evidence missing from the bibliography:** Mietzsch et al. 2014 studied broad glycan arrays and a natural/synthetic heparin array. This is a candidate source for assay-specific controls, including AAV2, subject to raw-data recovery and chemistry/provenance checks. The paper explicitly excluded AAV9 and AAV11. Some signals were removed for batch-specific nonspecific behavior; original datasets and array versions matter. Array nonbinding is conditional on presentation and detection, not proof of absolute nonbinding. Do not transfer labels between serotypes or assay platforms. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3958061/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/JVI.03371-13).

**AAV2 heparin data:** Zhang et al. combine SPR competition with heterogeneous GAGs and chain-length fractions and discuss avidity and aggregation. Their low-resolution heparin modeling represents heterogeneous bound material. These data are useful as contextual validation but are not automatically a chemically exact monovalent-affinity benchmark or an atomic RMSD reference. [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3859860/).

**2026 overlap check:** Lannot et al.'s published abstract describes apo-water-derived guidance and a 30-complex oligosaccharide benchmark. Any novelty claim about water-guided improvement needs comparison to its exact information regime, overlap and implementation. Abstract-level verification is sufficient to flag overlap, not to conclude novelty clearance. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41543133/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/glycob/cwag005).

**Major October 2026 overlap, independently checked after root's discovery:** ProtGlycanDock (posted 19 October) curates 1,781 noncovalent complexes, tests ten methods and uses separate protein/glycan holdouts. It excludes structures with more than four protein chains; Vina-family methods use predicted receptor structures and predicted pockets. Its reported endpoints concern structure recovery and validity; its main evaluation does not establish discrimination of experimentally tested binders/nonbinders. A generic docking leaderboard or failure-case classification therefore has substantial overlap. The more defensible remaining contribution is a carefully evidenced test of assembly dependence, information dependence, and assay-specific discrimination. Those differences are plausible scope distinctions, not proof of novelty. Audit exact datasets and pipelines before committing to the large run. [Primary preprint, sections 3.2, 5.1 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26009v1), [dated record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26009).

## Falsification and stopping rules

- If blind recovery vanishes when reference-derived neighborhoods or waters are removed, withdraw the blind-prediction claim.
- If performance is driven by a few homologous families, report family-specific findings or expand the independent cohort.
- If specificity disappears on size/charge-matched alternatives, limit the score claim to the tested unmatched distribution; do not call it demonstrated chemical specificity.
- If the native ligand is unsupported by local density or atom mapping, remove that case from atomic-pose inference regardless of its docking outcome.
- If positives and qualified tested negatives cannot be assembled, close and publish the site/pose study and explicitly leave experimental specificity unresolved.
- If confidence intervals overlap the predeclared useful-performance boundary, conclude that the available study is inconclusive at that boundary. Do not repeatedly add cases after each look without a prespecified sequential rule or new confirmatory cohort.

## Boundary of computational closure

Existing deposited structures and public assay data can be enough to conclude an **evaluation benchmark**. New wet-lab work is not a prerequisite merely because the project is biological. If relevant assay controls or reference quality are absent, additional computation cannot manufacture that evidence. At that point narrow the claim or record the missing independent evidence as a future need; do not substitute more docking, native energy minimization, or unvalidated simulations for specificity measurements. No engineering or optimization of viral behavior is required by this assessment.
