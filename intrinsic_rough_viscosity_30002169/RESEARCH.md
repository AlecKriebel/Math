# Intrinsic viscosity for transport-noise control HJB: completed restricted-scope research

Row 1838, ID 30002169. Original low-regularity problem is NOT claimed solved.

## Source-confirmed boundary
Diehl–Friz–Gassiat, arXiv:1303.7160, Definition 1 defines rough viscosity by universal smooth approximation. Remark 2 explicitly distinguishes the low regularity sigma in Lip^gamma (gamma>p) of Theorem 3 from the Lip^{gamma+2} coordinate-transform regime. Theorem 3 handles a control Hamiltonian, normally unbounded in the gradient.
Gubinelli–Tindel–Torrecilla, Controlled viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear rough PDEs (March 11 2014 manuscript), Definitions 3.4/3.6 and 4.1 provide controlled test functions. Propositions 4.3 and 4.5 identify them with classical tests after smooth rough-flow transport. Theorem 4.7 states smooth bounded vector fields and bounded Lipschitz F. Remark 4.8 allows broader examples in principle but is not itself a theorem for the original low-regularity HJB. Exact original-source text is kept outside this authored report.

## Avenue 1: precise theorem bridge
For smooth bounded transport vector fields, GTT Proposition 4.5 is an algebraic equivalence independent of boundedness of F: it uses the bijection of test classes and local maxima under a diffeomorphism. The bounded-F assumption enters the comparison/existence invocation of Theorem 4.7. Thus one can replace that last invocation with a separately justified deterministic control-HJB comparison theorem. This does NOT remove the regularity needed by the test-class bijection.

## Avenue 2: control Hamiltonian extension under smooth flows (authored partial proposition)
Fix a geometric alpha-Hölder step-2 rough path, 1/3<alpha<=1/2, and C_b^infinity vector fields sigma (the fields themselves and all derivatives are bounded). Assume b(x,a) is uniformly bounded and globally Lipschitz in x; f(t,x,a) is uniformly bounded, globally Lipschitz in x and uniformly continuous in t, uniformly over controls; g is bounded uniformly continuous. For a concrete sufficient control setup take a compact metric control set U, jointly continuous b(x,a) and f(t,x,a), and measurable U-valued controls. Define H(t,x,p)=sup_a[b(x,a) dot p+f(t,x,a)]. Consider the backward equation -du=H(t,x,Du)dt+sigma_i(x) dot Du dZ^i, u(T)=g.
Let Phi_t be the forward flow dPhi=sigma(Phi)dZ with Phi_0=id, and J_t=D Phi_t. Set w(t,y)=u(t,Phi_t(y)). Then the noise cancels and
-w_t=Hhat(t,y,Dw), Hhat(t,y,q)=sup_a[betahat(t,y,a) dot q+fhat(t,y,a)],
where betahat=J_t(y)^{-1} b(Phi_t(y),a), fhat=f(t,Phi_t(y),a), and terminal datum is g(Phi_T(y)).
For each fixed path and compact time interval, global bounds for J, J^{-1}, D(J^{-1}) imply betahat is bounded and globally Lipschitz in y uniformly in t,a; fhat is bounded and globally Lipschitz in y. Explicitly with M0=sup|b|, Lb=Lip(b), K1=sup|J|, Kinv=sup|J^{-1}| and K2=Lip(J^{-1}),
|betahat(y)-betahat(z)| <= (K2 M0+Kinv Lb K1)|y-z|.
Hence Hhat is globally Lipschitz in q with constant Kinv M0, and
|Hhat(t,y,q)-Hhat(t,z,q)| <= [(K2 M0+Kinv Lb K1)|q|+Lf K1]|y-z|.
These are the standard first-order comparison structure bounds; boundedness of Hhat in q is neither needed nor true. The standard doubling-variable argument yields comparison for bounded uniformly continuous sub/supersolutions: at q=(y-z)/epsilon, the spatial error is bounded by C|y-z|^2/epsilon + L|y-z|, which tends to zero. A coercive localization with bounded derivative controls maxima at spatial infinity; bounded drift makes its error tend to zero. Time doubling is not needed beyond the standard parabolic theorem of sums at a common time. This avoids claiming a uniform-in-q time modulus.
Existence follows from the deterministic controlled ODE dY=betahat(t,Y,a)dt with bounded costs fhat and terminal g o Phi_T. Its value is bounded by T||f||infty+||g||infty, is uniformly continuous by Gronwall and the modulus of g, and dynamic programming gives viscosity inequalities. GTT's test bijection gives an intrinsic controlled-test solution u; comparison transfers back. This is an authored proof using imported rough-flow calculus, deterministic DPP and viscosity comparison, not a new full low-regularity theorem.

### Stability and approximation equivalence in the partial proposition
If smooth lifts Z^n converge geometrically to Z in a fixed rough-path topology, smooth rough-flow stability gives locally uniform convergence Phi^n, D Phi^n and their inverses, with uniform derivative bounds on a rough-path bounded set. Consequently the transformed drifts/costs converge locally uniformly, uniformly in controls; all associated trajectories from a fixed compact initial set stay in a common compact set because the transformed drift has a common bound. Gronwall gives uniform-in-control trajectory convergence. Uniform continuity of f and g on those sets gives convergence of payoffs uniformly in controls. The inequality |sup A-sup B|<=sup|A-B| proves local uniform value convergence. Composing back proves that this intrinsic controlled-test solution equals the DFG approximation-defined value in this stronger smooth regime. No gradient bound for u and no truncation of H is required.

## Avenue 3: low-regularity tests and jet obstruction
For transport noise the natural formal controlled-test coefficients are psi^{Z;i}=-sigma_i dot Dpsi and psi^{ZZ;ij}=sigma_i dot D(sigma_j dot Dpsi), with index convention as in GTT. The second coefficient already uses Dsigma and D²psi. Writing these coefficients down is not an existence theorem for sufficiently many C²-spatial controlled tests. The proof of richness in GTT uses composition with a smooth rough flow and its inverse. The low-regularity DFG theorem does not supply the stronger uniform spatial derivative estimates used by that argument; DFG explicitly says this coordinate transformation is not applicable there. This is a gap in this proof strategy, NOT a proof that an intrinsic characterization is impossible and NOT an assertion that every Lip^gamma flow fails to be differentiable.

A purely formal Taylor jet is insufficient as a replacement: compatibility coefficients alone do not guarantee a genuine local touching test with a continuous drift coefficient and the required remainder; little-o bounds do not themselves produce an exact local maximum against the polynomial part. GTT Remark 3.9 explicitly describes the general jet/test identification as heuristic and difficult to formalize, and Remark 4.11 privileges the test-function route. Thus we do not infer low-regularity comparison from an unproved rough Ishii lemma or a formal jet expression.

Naively truncate H in p to invoke bounded-F results: this changes the PDE unless one proves all relevant touching gradients are bounded. BUC terminal data do not supply a global spatial Lipschitz bound. Even for Lipschitz data, such a bound and its uniformity in noise smoothing must be proved before truncation can establish the original equation. The smooth-flow control comparison above avoids this unnecessary gap.

## Avenue 4: explicit transport checks
1. Additive noise: sigma_i are constant vectors and S Z=sum sigma_i Z^i. Phi_t(y)=y+S(Z_t-Z_0), J=id. The transformed drift is b(y+S(Z_t-Z_0),a), with no derivative loss. For H=0 and terminal g, u(t,x)=g(x+S(Z_T-Z_t)). Its composition with Phi_t is independent of t. Tests psi(t,x)=h(t,x-S(Z_t-Z_0)) for smooth h give a rich controlled class. This special case proves genuine intrinsic well-posedness even with merely BUC g; no classical derivatives of g are claimed.
2. Smooth commuting transport fields: locally or globally where complete flows exist, Phi is the composition of their commuting ordinary flows with parameters Z_t^i-Z_0^i. Antisymmetric second-level area contributes through Lie brackets, which vanish. This yields a useful consistency check, not a proof of the noncommuting low-regularity case.
3. Noncommuting area check: in R² choose sigma_1=(1,0), sigma_2=(0,x_1). In the convention Z^{ij}=integral Z^i dZ^j, a pure-area geometric step-2 rough path has first level zero, Z^{12}_{st}=a(t-s), Z^{21}_{st}=-a(t-s). The RDE gives Phi_t(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,x_2+a t) because Dsigma_2 sigma_1=(0,1) and Dsigma_1 sigma_2=0. Thus H=0 gives u(t,x)=g(x_1,x_2+a(T-t)). Different rough lifts over the same zero first level can give different u. The unbounded affine fields in this simple calculation are explicit and do not purport to meet the bounded-field partial proposition; smooth cutoffs yield the same local calculation on a region containing all trajectories under consideration. Pure area is geometric for p>2 as a rough limit of small loops; it is not the lift of the identically zero smooth path.
4. A generic p-variation problem is not silently the same as an alpha-Hölder one. A control-based time change can often recast finite p-variation rough paths as Hölder paths, but it also transforms dt and the control cost. The original statement starts with geometric p-variation; the adjacent duality theorem's p in (2,3) is not automatically a restriction of the question. This report proves only the stated alpha>1/3 smooth-flow subcase and does not claim an unchecked time-change theorem.

## Avenue 5: precise remaining task
At the original admissible low spatial regularity, construct an intrinsic local test/jet definition that (a) is rich enough without assuming extra flow regularity, (b) is stable under smooth geometric approximation, (c) characterizes the DFG control value, and (d) has comparison in the appropriate bounded uniformly continuous class for the full control Hamiltonian and original time/variation formulation. It is insufficient to define the solution as the already-known approximation limit, to cite GTT's bounded-F theorem without the HJB bridge, or to enlarge sigma's regularity without labelling a restricted result. No proof or counterexample closing (a)-(d) at the original threshold is obtained here.
