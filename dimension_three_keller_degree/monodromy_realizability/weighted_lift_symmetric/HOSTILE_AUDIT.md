## Verdict

**The theorem and its all-degree proof survive the audit.**  I found no
counterexample and no theorem-strength gap.  In particular, the proof does
not make the false composite-degree inference that a \(d\)-cycle and an
unspecified transposition generate \(S_d\).  It proves instead that the
whole geometric group is generated by the finite simple-branch
transpositions and is transitive; that is sufficient in every degree.

A stronger statement also survives: **every admissible Gallagher
weighted lift of generic degree \(d\) has geometric monodromy \(S_d\)**,
not only the compact seed in `NOTE.md`.  This follows directly from
Brink's 2004 Theorem 13 after checking the Gallagher target fields and
recovery.  Consequently the compact Morse calculation is correct but is

## 1. Independent reconstruction of the function fields

Fix \(d\ge3\), and retain the notation of the note.  Direct multiplication
of the displayed coordinates gives
\[
 P=B_dC_d=\gamma+p_d(w),\qquad
 p_d(w)=\frac{2w-dw^{d-1}}{d-2},
\]
and
\[
 Q=A_dC_d^2=w\gamma+q_d(w),\qquad
 q_d(w)=\frac{w^2-(d-1)w^d}{d-2}.
\]
Since
\[
 wp_d(w)-q_d(w)=\frac{w^2-w^d}{d-2},
\]
eliminating \(\gamma=P-p_d(w)\) gives exactly
\[
 w^d-w^2+(d-2)Pw-(d-2)Q=0.
\]
Thus, after \(U=(d-2)P\) and \(V=-(d-2)Q\), the eliminant is
\[
 f_d(T)=T^d-T^2+UT+V.
\]

There are three field points that need to be kept separate.

1. The nonzero constant Jacobian makes \(A_d,B_d,C_d\) algebraically
   independent.  Moreover
   \[
   (A_d,B_d,C_d)\longleftrightarrow(P,Q,C_d)
   \]
   is birational, since \(B_d=P/C_d\) and \(A_d=Q/C_d^2\).  Hence
   \[
   \mathbb C(A_d,B_d,C_d)=\mathbb C(U,V,C_d),
   \]
   with \(C_d\) transcendental over \(\mathbb C(U,V)\).
2. The recovery formulas give
   \[
   \mathbb C(x,y,z)=\mathbb C(U,V,C_d)(w).
   \]
   They are not merely a dominant rational parametrization: every source
   coordinate is displayed as a rational function of \(w\) and the target.
3. The useful identity
   \[
   f_d'(w)=d w^{d-1}-2w+U=(d-2)\gamma
   \tag{A}
   \]
   makes the denominator issue completely transparent.  On the separable
   generic fibre no root has \(\gamma=0\).  If also \(C_d\ne0\), then
   \(x=C_d/\gamma\ne0\), and all the remaining recovery denominators are
   valid.  Thus no conjugate root is a branch introduced by denominator
   clearing.

Identity (A) should be added to the main note because it is the shortest
certificate against extraneous generic roots.  Its absence is not a gap:
the existing equality of function fields already proves the needed claim.

The irreducibility argument is also valid.  In
\(\mathbb C[U,T][V]\), the polynomial \(V+T^d-T^2+UT\) is monic linear in
\(V\), and its quotient ring is \(\mathbb C[U,T]\), a domain.  It is
therefore prime and irreducible.  It is monic, hence primitive, as a
polynomial in \(T\) over \(\mathbb C[U,V]\), so Gauss's lemma gives
irreducibility over \(\mathbb C(U,V)\).  Irreducibility is preserved after
adjoining the independent transcendental \(C_d\).

## 2. Audit of the Morse calculation

Put
\[
 g_U(T)=T^d-T^2+UT,\quad
 \psi(t)=2t-dt^{d-1},\quad
 \phi(t)=t^2-(d-1)t^d.
\]
At a critical point, \(U=\psi(t)\) and \(g_U(t)=\phi(t)\).

### Simple critical points

A repeated root of \(g_U'\) would satisfy
\[
 dT^{d-1}-2T+U=0,\qquad d(d-1)T^{d-2}-2=0.
\]
The second equation makes \(T\) algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), and the first
then makes \(U\) algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), impossible for the
indeterminate \(U\).  This proves that all \(d-1\) critical points are
simple.

### Distinct critical values

Suppose \(x\ne y\) are critical and \(\phi(x)=\phi(y)\).  If \(y=0\), then
the critical equation gives \(U=0\), which is impossible in
\(\mathbb C(U)\).  With \(r=x/y\ne1\), subtraction and division by
\(x-y\) give exactly
\[
 d y^{d-2}S_{d-2}(r)=2,
 \qquad
 (d-1)y^{d-2}S_{d-1}(r)=r+1.
\]
The first equation also proves that \(S_{d-2}(r)\ne0\), so no hidden
division occurs.  Eliminating \(y^{d-2}\) yields
\[
 N_d(r)=(d-2)r^d-dr^{d-1}+dr-(d-2)=0.
\]
For every \(d\ge3\), \(N_d\) is nonzero because its leading coefficient is
\(d-2\).  Thus \(r\) is algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), the first displayed
equation makes \(y\) algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), and
\(U=\psi(y)\) is then algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), a contradiction.

This checks all exceptional divisions in the note: \(y=0\), \(r=1\), and
\(S_{d-2}(r)=0\).  Hence \(g_U\) is Morse over
\(\overline{\mathbb C(U)}\) for every \(d\ge3\).

## 3. Audit of the branch-cycle and group steps

At a simple critical point \(t_i\), the local expansion is
\[
 g_U(T)-g_U(t_i)
 =\frac{g_U''(t_i)}2(T-t_i)^2+\cdots,
\]
so inertia at the corresponding finite branch value is exactly one
transposition.  The critical values are distinct, so no finite inertia
element is a product of two or more disjoint transpositions.  At infinity
the polynomial has one point of ramification index \(d\), hence inertia is
a \(d\)-cycle.  Characteristic zero makes all of this tame.

The degree-\(d\) cover has connected source \(\mathbb P^1\), so its
monodromy is transitive.  The finite inertia subgroups and their conjugates
generate the Galois group; equivalently, in branch-cycle language, the
infinite cycle is the inverse of the product of the finite cycles.  It
follows that the group is generated by transpositions.

For completeness, form a graph on the \(d\) sheets, placing an edge for
each generating transposition.  The subgroup generated by the edges is
the product of the symmetric groups on the graph's connected components.
Its action is transitive, so the graph has one component and the subgroup
is \(S_d\).

This is the classical Morse-polynomial theorem.  A precise source is
Jean-Pierre Serre, *Topics in Galois Theory*, Theorem 4.4.5 and
Proposition 4.4.6: a Morse polynomial in characteristic zero has Galois
group \(S_d\), with the finite inertia groups generating.  The main note
should cite this result rather than leave the branch-cycle generation
statement uncited.

### Composite-degree trap actively checked

The frequently used shortcut
\[
\text{“a \(d\)-cycle and a transposition imply \(S_d\)”}
\]
is false without information about the transposition.  For example,
\[
\langle(1234),(13)\rangle
\]
has order \(8\), not \(24\).  Similarly, a \(6\)-cycle together with the
step-two transposition \((13)\) preserves an imprimitive two-block
structure and generates a group of order \(72\), not \(S_6\).

The audited proof does not use this shortcut.  It uses the full connected
graph of finite inertia transpositions, so quartic and composite degrees
are covered.

## 4. Geometric versus arithmetic monodromy

Let \(\Omega=\overline{\mathbb C(U)}\).  The Morse argument computes
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(f_d/\Omega(V))=S_d.
\]
This geometric subgroup injects into
\(\operatorname{Gal}(f_d/\mathbb C(U,V))\), which already acts faithfully
on \(d\) roots and is therefore a subgroup of \(S_d\).  The latter group
must also be \(S_d\).

Because \(\mathbb C\) is already algebraically closed, this is the
geometric Galois group relevant to the Keller map.  Adjoining \(C_d\) is a
purely transcendental extension: the splitting field becomes \(E(C_d)\)
if \(E/\mathbb C(U,V)\) is the original splitting field, and its
automorphism group is unchanged.  The recovery formulas identify the
degree-\(d\) root action with the action on the generic fibre of \(F_d\).
There is no arithmetic/geometric conflation in the theorem proof.

## 5. Counterexample search and exact stress tests

### The exceptional even slice

For even \(d\), the specialization \(U=0\) is not Morse:
\[
 g_0(T)=T^d-T^2
\]
is even, so opposite critical points have the same critical value.  This
is a real degeneration and would break a proof based on the specialization
\(U=0\).  It does not affect the generic-\(U\) theorem.

For \(d=4\), an exact independent PARI calculation gives
\[
\operatorname{disc}_T(T^4-T^2+UT+V)
=256V^3-128V^2+(16-144U^2)V+4U^2-27U^4.
\]
At \(U=1\), this is
\[
256V^3-128V^2-128V-23,
\]
whose discriminant in \(V\) is \(-449511424\ne0\).  Thus the quartic
specialization has three distinct simple finite branch values and
geometric group \(S_4\).

Using `verify_geometric_branches_pari.gp`, independently of the supplied
SymPy script, I checked that \(U=1\) has a squarefree degree-\((d-1)\)
branch polynomial for every \(3\le d\le20\), including
\(d=4,6,8,9,10,12,14,15,16,18,20\).  The script computes the exact
resultant and certifies both its degree and its gcd with its derivative.
This is finite regression evidence only; the proof above is uniform.

### Collision certificate

At \(A_d=B_d=s_d=(4-2^d)/(d-2)\) and \(C_d=1\), the inverse polynomial has
the distinct roots \(1\) and \(2\).  Their recovery denominators are
\[
\gamma(1)=\frac{d+2-2^d}{d-2}\ne0,\qquad
\gamma(2)=2^{d-1}\ne0.
\]
Since \(2^d>d+2\) for \(d\ge3\), both formulas are valid.  Distinct \(w\)
values give distinct source points, and the recovered \(P,Q,C_d\) give
the stated \(A_d,B_d,C_d\).  Thus the maps are genuinely noninjective
Keller maps, not automorphisms.

## 6. Required correction to the verification claim

`VERIFICATION.md` currently says that the number-field Galois groups of
\[
X^d-X^2-3X-5
\]
give independent evidence for the generic group, and the PARI script says
that a specialized group embeds in “the generic group.”  The exact field
must be stated.

A good number-field specialization embeds in the **arithmetic** generic
group over \(\mathbb Q(U,V)\).  It does not by itself determine the
geometric generic group over \(\mathbb C(U,V)\).  In principle an
arithmetic \(S_d\) can have geometric subgroup \(A_d\), with the missing
sign supplied by a constant quadratic extension.  Therefore the PARI
rows are useful exact arithmetic sanity checks, but they are not a second
methodologically independent verification of geometric monodromy.

The independent script `verify_geometric_branches_pari.gp` now computes
\[
\operatorname{Res}_T(T^d-T^2+T+V,\ dT^{d-1}-2T+1)
\]
and certifies that it has degree \(d-1\) and is squarefree in \(V\), for
every \(3\le d\le20\).  Together with the classical Morse theorem, this
directly checks geometric \(S_d\) row by row and is independent of the
SymPy implementation.  The prose describing the number-field script
now includes the arithmetic/geometric qualification above.

## 7. Stronger follow-up audit: every Gallagher weighted lift

### General setup and hypotheses

Take a fixed Gallagher-admissible seed \(p\in\mathbb C[T]\) of degree
\(d-1\), with \(d\ge3\).  In the notation of Gallagher's general
construction, the hypotheses include
\[
p(0)=0,\qquad p(1)=-c,\qquad \int_0^1p(t)\,dt=0,
\]
\[
b,c\ne0,\qquad \kappa=p'(1)/c\ne-2.
\]
The last condition makes the lift parameter
\(a=-(1+\kappa)/(2+\kappa)\) defined.  The endpoint and integral
conditions are precisely the divisibility conditions that make the
apparently rational coordinates polynomial.  Gallagher's determinant
identity then gives
\[
\det JF_p=bc\ne0.
\]
The family-wide monodromy assertion applies to these actual polynomial
Keller maps.  It does not assert anything about a formal seed that fails
Gallagher's polynomiality hypotheses.

Put
\[
\Phi(T)=\int_0^T p(t)\,dt.
\]
In Gallagher's quotient coordinates,
\[
P=BC=c\gamma+p(w),\qquad Q=AC^2=w\gamma+q(w),
\]
where \(q'(w)=wp'(w)/c\).  Hence
\[
wP-cQ=wp(w)-cq(w)=\Phi(w),
\]
the last equality following by differentiation and evaluation at \(w=0\).
The general inverse equation is therefore
\[
H(T)=\Phi(T)-PT+cQ=0.
\tag{B}
\]

### Fields and recovery

The constant nonzero Jacobian makes \(A,B,C\) algebraically independent.
The change
\[
(A,B,C)\longleftrightarrow(P,Q,C)=(BC,AC^2,C)
\]
is birational, so
\[
\mathbb C(A,B,C)=\mathbb C(P,Q,C)
\]
and \(P,Q,C\) are algebraically independent.

A root \(w\) of (B) recovers
\[
\gamma=\frac{P-p(w)}c,\qquad
x=\frac C\gamma,\qquad
u=\frac w\gamma,\qquad
y=\frac{u-1}{x},
\]
and, from \(\gamma=1+a(u-1)+b x^2z\),
\[
z=\frac{\gamma-1-a(u-1)}{b x^2}.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb C(x,y,z)=\mathbb C(P,Q,C)(w).
\]
Moreover,
\[
H'(w)=p(w)-P=-c\gamma.
\tag{C}
\]
Equation (C) proves that every simple generic root has
\(\gamma\ne0\); after also restricting to \(C\ne0\), every root
reconstructs a unique generic source point.  This rules out denominator
branches uniformly for all admissible seeds.

### Brink's theorem applies exactly

Because \(\deg p=d-1\) in characteristic zero, \(\Phi\) has degree \(d\).
Write its leading coefficient as \(\lambda\ne0\).  Dividing (B) by
\(\lambda\) gives
\[
T^d+a_{d-1}T^{d-1}+\cdots+a_2T^2+ST+R,
\]
where
\[
S=-P/\lambda,\qquad R=cQ/\lambda
\]
(fixed lower coefficients or fixed affine shifts would make no
difference).  Algebraic independence of \(P,Q\), together with
\(\lambda,c\ne0\), gives
\[
\mathbb C(P,Q)=\mathbb C(S,R).
\]

David Brink, *On alternating and symmetric groups as Galois groups*,
Israel J. Math. **142** (2004), 47--60,
[DOI 10.1007/BF02771527](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02771527),
Theorem 13, proves that
\[
X^d+a_{d-1}X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_2X^2+SX+R
\]
has Galois group \(S_d\) over \(K(S,R)\) whenever
\(\operatorname{char}K\nmid d(d-1)\).  Taking \(K=\mathbb C\) gives
directly
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb C(P,Q))=S_d.
\]
Adjoining the independent \(C\) does not change the group, and the recovery
above identifies its root action with the generic-fibre action of \(F_p\).
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Mon}(F_p)=S_d}
\]
for every Gallagher-admissible seed of degree \(d-1\).

Irreducibility and generic degree are included in Brink's \(S_d\)
conclusion; alternatively, (B), the recovery formulas, and the fact that
it is linear in the independent constant parameter \(Q\) give the same
degree-\(d\) conclusion directly.  Since \(d\ge3\), each such lift is a
Keller counterexample rather than an automorphism.

### Consequence for the compact note

The explicit \(N_d(r)\) calculation in `NOTE.md` remains a correct,
self-contained proof for its chosen seed.  Brink's theorem proves more
with less: the special coefficients of that seed play no role once the
linear and constant coefficients are independent target parameters.
The strongest bankable statement is therefore family-wide, and the
priority language must acknowledge that its Galois-theoretic content is a
direct application of a 2004 theorem.
