# Full symmetric monodromy in a weighted-lift family

**First draft (UTC):** 2026-07-25T19:47:04Z
**Status:** candidate theorem under hostile audit; not peer reviewed.

## Statement

For every integer \(d\geq 3\), there is a Keller counterexample
\[
F_d:\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}
\]
of generic degree \(d\) whose geometric monodromy group, in its natural
action on the generic fibre, is the full symmetric group \(S_d\).

The Keller maps and their generic degrees come from Gallagher's
weighted-lift construction.  The addition here is the monodromy
calculation for the following particularly simple choice of seed.

## 1. The weighted-lift map and its inverse polynomial

Fix \(d\geq3\), and put
\[
u=1+xy,\qquad
\gamma=1-\frac d{d-1}xy+x^2z.
\]
Define \(F_d=(A_d,B_d,C_d)\) by
\[
A_d=
\frac{(d-2)u+u^2-(d-1)u^d\gamma^{d-2}}{(d-2)x^2},
\]
\[
B_d=
\frac{(d-2)+2u-du^{d-1}\gamma^{d-2}}{(d-2)x},
\qquad
C_d=x\gamma.
\tag{1}
\]
The apparent denominators cancel, so these are polynomials.  Gallagher's
weighted-lift identity gives
\[
\det JF_d=1.
\tag{2}
\]
The same construction supplies an explicit collision, so \(F_d\) is not
injective.  These facts, including the cancellation and collision, are
recalled in Section 4 below only to keep the monodromy statement
self-contained.

Set
\[
w=u\gamma,\qquad P=B_dC_d,\qquad Q=A_dC_d^2.
\]
Then \(w\) satisfies
\[
w^d-w^2+(d-2)Pw-(d-2)Q=0.
\tag{3}
\]
After the invertible target rescaling
\[
U=(d-2)P,\qquad V=-(d-2)Q,
\]
this becomes
\[
f_d(T)=T^d-T^2+UT+V.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, put
\[
p_d(T)=\frac{2T-dT^{d-1}}{d-2}.
\]
At the generic point one recovers
\[
\gamma=P-p_d(w),\qquad
x=\frac{C_d}{\gamma},\qquad
u=\frac w\gamma,\qquad
y=\frac{u-1}{x},
\]
\[
z=\frac{\gamma-1+\frac d{d-1}(u-1)}{x^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
K=\mathbb C(A_d,B_d,C_d)
  =\mathbb C(U,V,C_d),\qquad
L=\mathbb C(x,y,z),
\]
one has \(L=K(w)\).  There is therefore no extra component created by
the elimination.

The polynomial (4) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C(U,V)\).  Indeed,
\(V+(T^d-T^2+UT)\) generates a prime ideal in
\(\mathbb C[U,T,V]\), and Gauss's lemma applies.  It follows again that
\([L:K]=d\).

Thus the geometric monodromy of \(F_d\) is the Galois group of (4) over
\(\mathbb C(U,V,C_d)\).  Since \(C_d\) is an independent transcendental,
it remains to compute the group over \(\mathbb C(U,V)\).

## 2. A Morse-polynomial lemma

### Lemma

For every \(d\geq3\),
\[
\operatorname{Gal}\bigl(T^d-T^2+UT+V\,/\,\mathbb C(U,V)\bigr)=S_d.
\tag{6}
\]

### Proof

Regard (4) as the fibre equation of the polynomial cover
\[
g_U:\mathbb P^1_T\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1_s,\qquad
g_U(T)=T^d-T^2+UT,
\]
where \(s=-V\).  Its critical points satisfy
\[
g_U'(T)=dT^{d-1}-2T+U=0.
\tag{7}
\]
At a critical point \(t\), equations (7) and \(g_U(t)\) become
\[
U=\psi(t):=2t-dt^{d-1},\qquad
g_U(t)=\phi(t):=t^2-(d-1)t^d.
\tag{8}
\]

First, the critical points are simple over
\(\overline{\mathbb C(U)}\).  A multiple critical point would also obey
\[
d(d-1)t^{d-2}-2=0.
\]
This makes \(t\), and then \(U=\psi(t)\), algebraic over \(\mathbb C\),
contradicting the transcendence of \(U\).

Second, distinct critical points have distinct critical values.  Suppose
\(x\ne y\) satisfy (7) and \(\phi(x)=\phi(y)\).  The case \(y=0\)
would give \(U=0\), again impossible.  Put \(r=x/y\ne1\), and write
\[
S_m(r)=1+r+\cdots+r^m.
\]
Subtracting the two critical equations and the two critical-value
equations gives
\[
d\,y^{d-2}S_{d-2}(r)=2,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
(d-1)y^{d-2}S_{d-1}(r)=r+1.
\tag{10}
\]
Eliminating \(y^{d-2}\) yields
\[
N_d(r):=(d-2)r^d-dr^{d-1}+dr-(d-2)=0.
\tag{11}
\]
This is a nonzero polynomial.  Hence \(r\) is algebraic over
\(\mathbb C\); equation (9) then makes \(y\), and finally
\(U=\psi(y)\), algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), a contradiction.

Thus \(g_U\) is a Morse polynomial: its \(d-1\) finite critical points
are simple and have pairwise distinct values.  Local inertia about each
finite branch value is consequently a transposition.  Infinity is
totally ramified of index \(d\), so its inertia is a \(d\)-cycle.

The cover has connected source and hence transitive monodromy.  Its
branch cycles generate the monodromy group, and the inertia at infinity
is the inverse of the product of the finite branch cycles.  The group is
therefore generated by its finite transpositions.  A transitive subgroup
of \(S_d\) generated by transpositions is \(S_d\): form the graph whose
edges are those transpositions; the generated group is the product of
the symmetric groups on the connected components, while transitivity
makes the graph connected.  This proves (6). \(\square\)

## 3. Monodromy of the Keller maps

Let \(\Omega=\overline{\mathbb C(U)}\).  The preceding branch-cycle
argument computes the group after base change to \(\Omega(V)\) as
\(S_d\).  This group embeds in the Galois group over
\(\mathbb C(U,V)\), which is already a subgroup of \(S_d\); hence the
latter group is also \(S_d\).  Adjoining the independent target
coordinate \(C_d\) does not change the splitting field group.

The rational recovery (5) identifies this root extension with the
function-field extension induced by \(F_d\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Mon}(F_d)=S_d
\]
in the natural degree-\(d\) action, for every \(d\geq3\).

## 4. Counterexample certificate

For completeness, the cancellation in (1) follows by writing
\(v=xy\) and \(\tau=x^2z\).  The numerator of \(B_d\) has zero constant
term in \(v,\tau\); the numerator of \(A_d\) has zero constant and
linear-in-\(v\) terms.  The remaining monomials are divisible by \(x\)
and \(x^2\), respectively.

For the collision, set
\[
s_d=\frac{4-2^d}{d-2}.
\]
At the target \((A_d,B_d,C_d)=(s_d,s_d,1)\), equation (3) has the
distinct roots \(w=1,2\).  Formula (5) reconstructs two distinct source
points because
\[
\gamma(1)=\frac{d+2-2^d}{d-2}\ne0,\qquad
\gamma(2)=2^{d-1}\ne0.
\]
Thus each \(F_d\) is a Keller counterexample, rather than merely a
Keller map.

## 5. Scope and attribution

Gallagher proved the weighted-lift construction uniformly for every
generic degree at least three.  The seed used here,
\[
\frac{2w-dw^{d-1}}{d-2},
\]
is an immediate specialization of that construction.  No novelty is
claimed for the classical fact that a Morse polynomial cover has full
symmetric monodromy.  The candidate new statement is their combination:
the realization of every \(S_d\), \(d\ge3\), as the geometric
monodromy group of a dimension-three Keller counterexample.
