# Working theorem: the binary fixed-divisor conic stratum

**Status:** proved and independently adversarially confirmed from the raw
determinant identities.  This is not peer reviewed.  The source-specific
priority search found no exact prior statement and is not a guarantee of
worldwide priority.

**First recorded:** 2026-07-25T02:47:00Z.

**Promoted after audit:** 2026-07-25T03:20:00Z.

## 1. Statement and precise scope

Let
\[
F=L_0X+H_2+H_3+H_4:\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}\longrightarrow
\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}
\]
be a polynomial map of total degree four, where \(H_i\) is homogeneous of
degree \(i\).  Suppose that, after independent linear source and target
changes,
\[
H_4=h(p,q)A(p,q),\qquad
A=(p^2,pq,q^2)^T,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(p,q,r\) are source coordinates and \(h\) is a nonzero binary
quadratic.

### Theorem

If \(F\) is Keller, then \(F\) is a polynomial automorphism.  In particular,
no degree-four Keller counterexample has leading part (1).

This is the **binary**, or scalar-aligned, subrow of the fixed-divisor conic
row
\[
(e,a,b,\delta,\nu)=(2,1,2,2,1).
\]
It is not the entire row.  Intrinsically, let \(W=\langle p,q\rangle\) be
the minimal source pencil, let \(k\) span the quotient source direction,
and let \(B_h\) be the symmetric bilinear form associated with the quadratic
form \(h\).  The hypothesis in (1) is
\[
\boxed{\iota_kB_h=0.}
\tag{2}
\]
Section 9 records the five nonbinary normal forms that remain open.

## 2. Top two determinant identities

Put
\[
A_p=\partial_pA,\qquad A_q=\partial_qA,\qquad
\Delta=A_p\times A_q=2(q^2,-2pq,p^2)^T.
\]
Euler's identities for \(A\) and \(h\) give
\[
(H_4)_p\times(H_4)_q=2h^2\Delta.
\tag{3}
\]
The degree-eight Keller identity is therefore
\[
\Delta\mathbin{\cdot}\partial_rH_3=0.
\]
The Hilbert--Burch syzygy module of the three components of \(\Delta\) is
generated by \(A_p,A_q\).  Since \(\partial_rH_3\) is quadratic,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
H_3={}&V(p,q)
+r\bigl((ap+bq)A_p+(cp+dq)A_q\bigr)\\
&+\frac{r^2}{2}(eA_p+fA_q),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4}
\]
where \(V\) is an arbitrary binary cubic vector.

Over \(\mathbb C\), the two \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\)-orbits of nonzero
binary quadratics are
\[
h=pq\qquad\text{and}\qquad h=p^2.
\tag{5}
\]
A raw degree-seven solve in the full twelve coefficients of \(V\) and
eighteen coefficients of \(H_2\) forces \(e=f=0\).  It also forces
\[
b=c=0\quad(h=pq),\qquad b=0\quad(h=p^2).
\tag{6}
\]
After solving the remaining degree-seven equations, the coefficient of
\(r^2\) in degree six is, respectively,
\[
\boxed{12p^2q^2(a-d)^2(a+d)}
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{24dp^2\bigl(cp+(d-a)q\bigr)^2.}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the residual tangent fields \(\partial_rH_3\), modulo the stabilizer of
the leading form and scaling of \(r\), are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
h=pq&2A,\quad pA_p-qA_q,\quad0,\\[2mm]
h=p^2&2A,\quad pA_p,\quad pA_q,\quad0.
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) are a finite and exhaustive branch reduction, including
the zero specializations.

## 3. Split roots: opposite weights

Let \(h=pq\) and normalize
\[
\partial_rH_3=pA_p-qA_q.
\tag{10}
\]
Order the coefficients of a general binary cubic vector componentwise by
\((p^3,p^2q,pq^2,q^3)\), writing them \(v_0,\ldots,v_{11}\), and order the
coefficients of a general quadratic vector componentwise by
\((p^2,pq,q^2,pr,qr,r^2)\), writing them
\(w_0,\ldots,w_{17}\).  All indices in this section are zero-based.

The degree-six solve includes
\[
w_{15}=v_9=w_4=v_7=w_2=w_{12}=0,
\]
\[
4v_{10}+3w_{16}-2w_9=0,\qquad
-12v_{11}+8v_6+6w_{10}-w_3=0.
\tag{11}
\]
With the complete degree-six solution imposed, the degree-five coefficient
is linear in the six remaining entries of \(L_0\).  An exact left-null
certificate pairs with its inhomogeneous side to give
\[
\boxed{64=0.}
\tag{12}
\]
Hence no Keller map occurs in this branch.

## 4. Split roots: scalar tangent

Normalize \(\partial_rH_3=2A\).  Degrees seven through five give
\[
H_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
(U-X)p^3+(V-Y)p^2q\\
Up^2q+Vpq^2\\
(U+X)pq^2+(V+Y)q^3
\end{pmatrix}
+2rA
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
(H_2)_1={}&(18XY+8XT-8YS-4ST-C+2B_1)p^2\\
&+(9Y^2+8YT+2T^2+2B_2)pq+2Tpr,\\
(H_2)_2={}&B_0p^2+B_1pq+B_2q^2+Spr+Tqr,\\
(H_2)_3={}&(9X^2-8XS+2B_0+2S^2)pq+Cq^2+2Sqr.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Four division-free degree-four square certificates force
\[
S=2X,\quad T=-2Y,\quad
B_0=UX-X^2,\quad B_2=-VY-Y^2,
\tag{15}
\]
and the residual degree-four equation gives
\[
C=B_1+UY+VX+XY.
\tag{16}
\]

After the degree-six-through-four solve, only two displayed entries
\(\ell_4,\ell_7\) of \(L_0\) remain free.  The complete degree-two
coefficient is
\[
\boxed{E_2=(Rp-2Qq)^2,}
\tag{17}
\]
where
\[
Q=B_1Y+UY^2+\ell_4,\qquad
R=-2B_1X-2UXY+VX^2+X^2Y+\ell_7.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(Q=R=0\).  Direct expansion of the same solved linear part gives
\[
\det L_0=
\bigl(VX^2Y+X^2Y^2+2X\ell_4+Y\ell_7\bigr)^2.
\tag{19}
\]
Substitution of \(Q=R=0\) makes the factor in (19) identically zero, with
no division by \(X\) or \(Y\).  This contradicts the Keller condition.

## 5. Split roots: zero tangent

If \(\partial_rH_3=0\), the degree-seven equation has the exact form
\[
H_3=V(p,q),\qquad
H_2=B(p,q)+r(\alpha A_p+\beta A_q).
\tag{20}
\]
The stabilizer of \(pq\), together with scaling of \(r\), has three support
orbits on \((\alpha,\beta)\):
\[
(0,0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(1,1).
\tag{21}
\]
For \((1,0)\), the raw degree-six solve followed by a degree-five
left-null certificate gives the nonzero constant \(8\).  For \((1,1)\),
three independent degree-five certificates give
\[
8,\qquad-16,\qquad8.
\tag{22}
\]
Hence neither nonzero orbit contains a Keller map.

For \((\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)\), all nonlinear homogeneous parts depend only
on \(p,q\).  The plane-plus-shear argument of Section 8 then makes every
Keller map an automorphism.

## 6. Double root: scalar tangent

Let \(h=p^2\) and normalize \(\partial_rH_3=2A\).  The complete high-degree
solve has
\[
H_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
(U-X)p^3+Vp^2q\\
\frac Z2p^3+Up^2q+Vpq^2\\
Zp^2q+(U+X)pq^2+Vq^3
\end{pmatrix}
+2rA.
\tag{23}
\]
Before the final degree-four compatibilities, the quadratic part is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(H_2)_1={}&(9X^2+8XT+2T^2-C+2B_1)p^2
            +2B_2pq+2Tpr,\\
(H_2)_2={}&B_0p^2+B_1pq+B_2q^2+Spr+Tqr,\\
(H_2)_3={}&(9Z^2/4-4ZS+2S^2)p^2\\
&+(2B_0+9XZ-8XS+4ZT-4ST)pq+Cq^2+2Sqr.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Division-free square certificates force
\[
S=Z,\quad T=-2X,\quad B_0=\frac{UZ}{2},\quad B_2=-VX,
\quad C=B_1+UX+\frac{VZ}{2}+X^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The complete degree-two coefficient is
\[
\boxed{
E_2=\frac1{16}\left[
\begin{aligned}
&(-4B_1Z-4UXZ+VZ^2-4X^2Z+4\ell_7)p\\
&\quad+(-8B_1X-8UX^2-8X^3-8\ell_4)q
\end{aligned}\right]^2.}
\tag{26}
\]
Meanwhile the solved linear part satisfies
\[
\det L_0=
\frac1{16}\bigl(VXZ^2+4X\ell_7+4Z\ell_4\bigr)^2.
\tag{27}
\]
The two coefficients of the linear form in (26) make the factor in (27)
zero.  This remains valid when \(X=0\) or \(Z=0\), so there is no hidden
rank-jump branch.

## 7. Double root: semisimple and nilpotent tangents

### 7.1 Semisimple one-zero-eigenvalue field

For \(\partial_rH_3=pA_p\), exact solution of degrees seven through four
gives
\[
H_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
2(U-T)p^3+2Vp^2q\\
Sp^3+Up^2q+Vpq^2\\
2Sp^2q+2Tpq^2
\end{pmatrix}
+rpA_p
\tag{28}
\]
and
\[
H_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_0p^2+A_1pq+V^2q^2+2(U-T)pr+2Vqr+r^2\\
B_0p^2+B_1pq+VTq^2+Spr+Tqr\\
C_0p^2+C_1pq+T^2q^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{29}
\]
The stacked degree-six, degree-five, and degree-four system in the nine
row-major entries \(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_8\) of \(L_0\) has a constant
\(9\times9\) minor \(32768\).  Its unique solution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell_0={}&-A_0T+A_0U+A_1S+2STV-2SUV+T^3-3T^2U+3TU^2-U^3,\\
\ell_1={}&A_0V+A_1T+T^2V-U^2V,&
\ell_2={}&A_0-T^2+2TU-U^2,\\
\ell_3={}&-B_0T+B_0U+B_1S-S^2V+STU-SU^2,\\
\ell_4={}&B_0V+B_1T-SUV+T^3-T^2U,&
\ell_5={}&B_0+ST-SU,\\
\ell_6={}&-C_0T+C_0U+C_1S-S^2T-S^2U,\\
\ell_7={}&C_0V+C_1T-S^2V-2ST^2,&
\ell_8={}&C_0-S^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Direct expansion gives \(\det L_0=0\) identically.  The constant minor
rules out exceptional parameter specializations.

### 7.2 Nilpotent field

For \(\partial_rH_3=pA_q\), retain the zero-based \(v_i,w_i\) convention of
Section 3.  After the degree-seven and degree-six eliminations, degree five
forces
\[
w_{10}=K=v_6,\qquad
w_8=\frac{v_{10}K}{2},\qquad
w_{14}=\frac{v_{10}^2}{4},\qquad
Kw_3=0.
\tag{31}
\]
If \(K\ne0\), then \(w_3=0\) and the remaining equations give
\[
v_0=2w_9,\qquad v_8=0,\qquad w_{15}=2v_4.
\tag{32}
\]
The lower linear solve then makes \(\det L_0=0\).

If \(K=0\), degree five instead gives
\[
\ell_1=\frac{v_{10}}2\ell_2,\qquad
\ell_4=\frac{v_{10}}2\ell_5,\qquad
\ell_7=\frac{v_{10}}2\ell_8.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus the second column of \(L_0\) is exactly \(v_{10}/2\) times its third
column.  Again \(\det L_0=0\).  The split \(K=0\) in (31) is essential and
prevents an illicit division by the branch parameter.

## 8. Double root: zero tangent and the automorphism exit

With \(\partial_rH_3=0\), degree seven again gives (20).  The Borel
stabilizer of \(p^2\) has the three orbits
\[
(0,0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(0,1).
\tag{34}
\]
For \((1,0)\), degree six forces
\[
v_1=3v_{11}-2v_6,\qquad v_2=6v_7,\qquad v_3=0,
\tag{35}
\]
and degree five has a constant compatibility obstruction \(8\).  For
\((0,1)\), degree six forces
\[
v_2=3v_7,\qquad v_3=0,
\tag{36}
\]
and degree five again has the constant obstruction \(8\).

It remains only \((0,0)\).  In that case every nonlinear homogeneous part
of \(F\) is binary.  Since the constant determinant coefficient is
\(\det L_0\ne0\), let \(v=L_0(\partial_r)\) and make a target-linear change
sending \(v\) to the third coordinate vector.  The first two components
become a plane Keller map
\[
G:\mathbb A^2_{p,q}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^2
\]
of degree at most four.  The unconditional plane degree bound implies that
\(G\) is an automorphism.  The third component is a nonzero scalar multiple
of \(r\) plus a polynomial in \(p,q\); hence \(F\) is an automorphism.
This closes every branch in (9) and proves the theorem.

## 9. The exact remaining fixed-conic frontier

For a general quadratic fixed divisor write
\[
h=\gamma r^2+r\ell(p,q)+q_2(p,q).
\tag{37}
\]
The parabolic source group preserving \(W=\langle p,q\rangle\) gives the
following complete list outside the binary condition (2).

- If \(\gamma\ne0\), complete the square in \(r\) and classify the residual
  binary quadratic by rank:
  \[
  r^2,\qquad r^2+p^2,\qquad r^2+pq.
  \]
- If \(\gamma=0\) but \(\ell\ne0\), normalize \(\ell=p\), absorb the
  \(p^2,pq\) terms into \(r\), and classify the remaining \(q^2\) term:
  \[
  pr,\qquad pr+q^2.
  \]

These five nonbinary strata remain open.  Therefore the theorem excludes a
complete invariant subrow, but not yet the full
\((e,a,b,\delta,\nu)=(2,1,2,2,1)\) taxonomy row.
