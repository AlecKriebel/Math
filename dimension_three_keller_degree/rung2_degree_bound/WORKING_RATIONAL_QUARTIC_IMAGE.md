# Working theorem: the unramified rational-quartic leading curve

**Status:** proved and independently adversarially audited.  This is not
peer reviewed.  No priority claim is made.

**Recorded:** 2026-07-25T00:30:00Z.

## 1. Statement

Let
\[
F=L_0X+H_2+H_3+H_4:\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}\longrightarrow
\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}
\]
be a Keller map of total degree four, with
\(L_0\in\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb C)\).  Suppose there are independent
linear source coordinates \(p,q,r\) and coprime binary quartics
\[
A_1,A_2,A_3\in\mathbb C[p,q]_4
\]
such that
\[
H_4=A=(A_1,A_2,A_3).
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=A_p\times A_q.
\tag{2}
\]

### Theorem

If
\[
\gcd(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3)=1,
\tag{3}
\]
then \(F\) is a polynomial automorphism.

Thus a quartic Keller counterexample whose projective leading image is a
birationally parametrized plane quartic must have a nonconstant common
ramification factor in the three normal minors of that basepoint-free
parametrization.

The theorem uses the established plane low-degree bound only as a black box.
It neither assumes the plane Jacobian Conjecture nor undertakes new work in
dimension two.

## 2. The normal-minor syzygies

Let
\[
S=\mathbb C[p,q].
\]
The entries of \(\Delta\) are the signed maximal minors of
\[
\mathcal A=
\begin{pmatrix}
(A_1)_p&(A_1)_q\\
(A_2)_p&(A_2)_q\\
(A_3)_p&(A_3)_q
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]
Under (3), the ideal generated by those minors has height two.  The
Hilbert--Burch theorem therefore gives the exact resolution
\[
0\longrightarrow S(-9)^2
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal A\ }
S(-6)^3
\longrightarrow(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, every nonzero homogeneous syzygy of \(\Delta\) has
coefficient degree at least three.  The two degree-three generators are
the columns \(A_p,A_q\).

## 3. Degrees eight and seven

In the coordinates \((p,q,r)\),
\[
JH_4=\begin{pmatrix}A_p&A_q&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)=e_r\Delta^T.
\tag{6}
\]
The degree-eight coefficient of the Keller determinant is consequently
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)JH_3\bigr)
=\Delta\cdot\partial_rH_3=0.
\tag{7}
\]

The vector \(\partial_rH_3\) is homogeneous of degree two.  Expanding it in
powers of \(r\) makes every coefficient a syzygy of \(\Delta\) of degree
zero, one, or two.  Resolution (5) has none, so
\[
\partial_rH_3=0.
\tag{8}
\]

With (8), both \(JH_4\) and \(JH_3\) have zero \(r\)-column.  Every
degree-seven term using one \(JH_4\) and two \(JH_3\) factors vanishes.
The remaining degree-seven term is
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)JH_2\bigr)
=\Delta\cdot\partial_rH_2=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Now \(\partial_rH_2\) has degree one, so the same syzygy argument gives
\[
\partial_rH_2=0.
\tag{10}
\]

## 4. Plane reduction

Equations (1), (8), and (10) show that every nonlinear term of \(F\)
depends only on \(p,q\).  The nonzero vector \(L_0(\partial_r)\) can be sent
to the third target direction by a target-linear change.  The resulting map
has the form
\[
F(p,q,r)=
\bigl(G_1(p,q),G_2(p,q),\alpha r+h(p,q)\bigr),
\qquad \alpha\ne0,
\tag{11}
\]
where \(\deg G\le4\).  Its Jacobian is
\[
\alpha\det JG\in\mathbb C^\times.
\]
The established plane bound makes \(G\) a polynomial automorphism, and the
third component of (11) is a shear.  Hence \(F\) is a polynomial
automorphism.

## 5. Scope

Condition (3) is a genuine ramification restriction on the parametrized
leading curve.  The theorem does not yet classify the common-factor locus
\[
\gcd(A_p\times A_q)\ne1,
\]
and it does not assert that every point of that locus is compatible with the
remaining Keller identities.

The restriction is already sharp for the degree-eight identity.  Take
\[
A=(p^4,pq^3,q^4).
\]
Then
\[
A_p\times A_q
=4q^2(q^4,-4p^3q,3p^4)
\]
and the reduced normal has the linear syzygy
\[
(q^4,-4p^3q,3p^4)\cdot(0,3p,4q)=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
H_3=\left(0,\frac32pr^2,2qr^2\right)
\]
has
\(\partial_rH_3=r(0,3p,4q)\ne0\) while still satisfying (7).  This example
certifies only that the ramified leading identity admits extra branches; it
is not asserted to extend to a Keller map.
