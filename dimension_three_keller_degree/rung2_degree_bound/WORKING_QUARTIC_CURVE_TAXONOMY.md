# Working taxonomy: quartic rank-two leading maps with curve image

**Status:** classification and generic cubic-stratum calculation proved and
independently adversarially audited.  The audit corrected several coverage
descriptions but found no missing row or algebraic error.  This is not peer
reviewed, and no priority claim is made.

**Recorded:** 2026-07-25T01:40:21Z.

## 1. Scope and canonical data

Let
\[
H_4:\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}
\]
be homogeneous of degree four, with
\[
\operatorname{rank}JH_4=2,
\]
and suppose the projective image of
\[
[H_{4,1}:H_{4,2}:H_{4,3}]
\]
is a curve.  Put
\[
h=\gcd(H_{4,1},H_{4,2},H_{4,3}),\qquad e=\deg h.
\]

The homogeneous rank-two factorization theorem, in its minimal-pair
normalization, gives
\[
\boxed{H_4=h\,A(p,q),\qquad e+ab=4.}
\tag{1}
\]
Here:

- \(p,q\) are coprime homogeneous source forms of the same degree \(a\);
- \(\mathbb C(p/q)\) is the relative algebraic closure of the field
  generated by the projective ratios of \(H_4\);
- \(A=(A_0,A_1,A_2)\) is a triple of binary forms of degree \(b\);
- \(\gcd(A_0,A_1,A_2)=1\), so
  \(A:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^2\) has no base point.

This is the specialization of Michiel de Bondt,
*Rational maps \(H\) for which \(K(tH)\) has transcendence degree \(2\)
over \(K\)*, arXiv:1501.06046, Theorem 2.7.

More explicitly, that theorem first writes the reduced projective map as a
superprimitive binary triple evaluated at a minimal pair.  Superprimitivity
makes its rational scalar equal, up to a unit, to the polynomial gcd \(h\).
Homogeneity of \(H_4\) and \(h\) makes the reduced triple homogeneous, and
Theorem 2.7(viii) lets the minimal pair \(p,q\) be chosen homogeneous of the
same degree.  This gives \(e+ab=4\).  Minimality together with
Theorem 2.7(ix) identifies \(\mathbb C(p/q)\) with the relative algebraic
closure of the field generated by the projective component ratios.

Let \(C\) be the projective image of \(A\), let
\(\delta=\deg C\), and let
\[
\nu=[\,\mathbb C(\mathbb P^1):\mathbb C(C)\,]
\]
be the generic degree of the parametrization.  Pulling back a generic
target line gives a divisor of degree \(b\) on \(\mathbb P^1\), while that
line meets \(C\) in \(\delta\) points and every point has \(\nu\)
preimages.  Hence
\[
\boxed{b=\delta\nu.}
\tag{2}
\]

The image of the irreducible surface \(\mathbb P^2\) is irreducible, and it
is dominated by \(\mathbb P^1\); thus \(C\) is a rational irreducible plane
curve.  Equations (1)--(2) make the degree list finite.

## 2. Complete degree taxonomy

The following table lists every possibility.  The pair \((a,b)\) records
the degree of the minimal pencil and the degree of its outer binary map.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|l}
e&a&b&(\delta,\nu)&\text{normal form}\\ \hline
0&4&1&(1,1)&(p,q,0)\\
0&2&2&(1,2)&(A_0(p,q),A_1(p,q),0)\\
0&2&2&(2,1)&\operatorname{Ver}(p,q)\\
0&1&4&(1,4)&(A_0(p,q),A_1(p,q),0)\\
0&1&4&(2,2)&\operatorname{Ver}(B_0(p,q),B_1(p,q))\\
0&1&4&(4,1)&A_4(p,q)\\ \hline
1&3&1&(1,1)&h(p,q,0)\\
1&1&3&(1,3)&h(A_0(p,q),A_1(p,q),0)\\
1&1&3&(3,1)&hA_3(p,q)\\ \hline
2&2&1&(1,1)&h(p,q,0)\\
2&1&2&(1,2)&h(A_0(p,q),A_1(p,q),0)\\
2&1&2&(2,1)&h\operatorname{Ver}(p,q)\\ \hline
3&1&1&(1,1)&h(p,q,0).
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]

In the conic rows,
\[
\operatorname{Ver}(s,t)=(s^2,st,t^2),
\]
and in the conic double-cover row \(B_0,B_1\) are coprime binary
quadratics.  The map \(A_3\) is the normalization parametrization of an
irreducible rational plane cubic, hence is nodal or cuspidal.  The map
\(A_4\) is birational onto an irreducible rational plane quartic.

For completeness, the elementary enumeration behind (3) is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
e& (a,b)\\ \hline
0&(4,1),(2,2),(1,4)\\
1&(3,1),(1,3)\\
2&(2,1),(1,2)\\
3&(1,1),
\end{array}
\]
followed by the factorizations \(b=\delta\nu\).  Thus there is no omitted
projective cubic or quadratic image: cubics occur only for
\((e,a,b)=(1,1,3)\), while conics occur in exactly the three rows displayed
in (3).

Projective-point images would have \(\delta=0\).  They are not curve-image
strata, have \(\operatorname{rank}JH_4\leq1\), and belong to the separate
rank-one and projected-cubic-quotient analysis.

## 3. Fixed divisors and base points

The divisor
\[
(h=0)
\]
is the fixed divisorial component of the original three-component linear
system.  It is cancelled when the associated projective rational map is
formed.

After removing that fixed component, the base ideal is
\[
I_A=(A_0(p,q),A_1(p,q),A_2(p,q)).
\tag{4}
\]
Because the binary triple \(A\) has no common zero,
\[
\sqrt{I_A}=(p,q).
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, outside \(V(p,q)\), the value \([p:q]\) is a point of
\(\mathbb P^1\), at which at least one \(A_i\) is nonzero.  Conversely, all
the degree-\(b\) forms \(A_i(p,q)\) vanish when \(p=q=0\).

Thus the base support is the complete intersection
\[
Z=V(p,q)\subset\mathbb P^2,
\]
of scheme length \(a^2\).  The actual thickening in (4) depends on the
outer syzygy type.  For example,
\[
(p^2,pq,q^2)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(p^2,q^2)
\]
have the same support but different base schemes.  Consequently
“basepoint-free parametrization” refers to the outer map
\(A:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^2\), not to the composed rational map from
\(\mathbb P^2\).

The remaining common-factor-free conic row deserves separate emphasis.
Writing
\[
H_4=\operatorname{Ver}(B_0(p,q),B_1(p,q)),
\tag{6}
\]
the pencil \(\langle B_0,B_1\rangle\) is a pencil of binary quadratics.  Its
projective line meets the Veronese conic of double lines in two points
counted with multiplicity.  Tangency would make both generators divisible
by the tangent double line, contrary to coprimality.  Hence (6) has two
distinct double-line fibres.  The current conic theorem excludes both the
no-double-line and unique-double-line genuine \((2,2)\) loci.  Its
two-double-line presentations are nonminimal and reclassify exactly into
the present \((a,b,\delta,\nu)=(1,4,2,2)\) row, with canonical leading form
\[
\operatorname{Ver}(p^2,q^2)=(p^4,p^2q^2,q^4).
\]
That conic-double-cover stratum was the remaining branch at the raw
taxonomy checkpoint.  The audited theorem
`WORKING_CONIC_DOUBLE_COVER_EXIT.md` now excludes it:
the degree-eight and degree-seven kernels split into three stabilizer
zero-patterns, and every lower branch either reduces to a plane
degree-at-most-four automorphism or forces \(\det L_0=0\).

## 5. The transverse cubic stratum

Consider the cubic-image row
\[
(e,a,b,\delta,\nu)=(1,1,3,3,1).
\]
On the open transverse-scalar locus, \(h,p,q\) are independent linear
forms.  Use source coordinates \((p,q,r)\) with \(h=r\), and write
\[
H_4=rA(p,q),
\tag{7}
\]
where \(A=(A_0,A_1,A_2)^T\) is a basepoint-free birational binary cubic.
Put
\[
\Delta=A_p\times A_q,\qquad
k=(-p,-q,3r)^T.
\tag{8}
\]
Euler's identity
\[
pA_p+qA_q=3A
\tag{9}
\]
gives
\[
JH_4=[\,rA_p\;\;rA_q\;\;A\,],\qquad
JH_4\,k=0.
\]
The third row of the adjugate is
\((rA_p\times rA_q)^T=r^2\Delta^T\), so the exact rank-one adjugate is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)=\frac r3\,k\Delta^T.}
\tag{10}
\]

Assume now that
\[
\gcd(\Delta_0,\Delta_1,\Delta_2)=1.
\tag{11}
\]
This is the unramified normal-minor branch.  Over
\(S=\mathbb C[p,q]\), Hilbert--Burch applied to the two derivative columns
gives
\[
0\longrightarrow S(-6)^2
\xrightarrow{\ [A_p\ A_q]\ }
S(-4)^3
\longrightarrow(\Delta_0,\Delta_1,\Delta_2)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, there are no nonzero syzygies of coefficient degree below
two, and all degree-two syzygies are constant linear combinations of
\(A_p,A_q\).

## 6. Degree eight

Let \(B=JH_3\).  The homogeneous degree-eight Keller identity is
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)B\bigr)=0.
\]
By (10), after cancelling the nonzero polynomial \(r/3\), this is
\[
\boxed{\Delta\cdot T(H_3)=0,\qquad
T=-p\partial_p-q\partial_q+3r\partial_r.}
\tag{13}
\]

Expand
\[
H_3=\sum_{j=0}^3 r^jU_{3-j}(p,q).
\]
On the \(j\)-th summand, \(T\) has eigenvalue
\[
3j-(3-j)=4j-3,
\]
which is nonzero for \(j=0,1,2,3\).  Equation (12), coefficient by
coefficient in \(r\), therefore yields
\[
U_1=U_0=0,
\]
\[
U_2=\alpha A_p+\beta A_q,
\qquad
U_3=\ell A_p+mA_q,
\]
where \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C\) and \(\ell,m\) are binary linear forms.
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
H_3=A_p(\ell+\alpha r)+A_q(m+\beta r).
}
\tag{14}
\]

## 7. Degree seven

The degree-seven determinant coefficient has exactly the two polarization
types \(JH_4,JH_4,JH_2\) and \(JH_4,JH_3,JH_3\).  Consequently its exact
form is
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_4)JH_2\bigr)
+\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_3)JH_4\bigr)=0.
\]
Using (10),
\[
\boxed{
\frac r3\,\Delta\cdot T(H_2)
+\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(JH_3)JH_4\bigr)=0.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Set
\[
V=\ell A_p+mA_q.
\tag{16}
\]
At \(r=0\), the first term of (15) vanishes,
\[
JH_4\big|_{r=0}=[\,0\;\;0\;\;A\,],
\]
and the first two columns of \(JH_3\) become \(V_p,V_q\).  Thus every
Keller map in this generic cubic stratum satisfies the
\(H_2\)-independent normal-minor obstruction
\[
\boxed{\det(V_p,V_q,A)=0.}
\tag{17}
\]

This is the first necessary condition.  For the nodal normal form below,
`WORKING_NODAL_CUBIC_CURVE_EXIT.md` carries the lower identities through
degrees six and five and excludes the transverse nodal branch.
`WORKING_SCALAR_ALIGNED_NODAL_CUBIC_EXIT.md` independently handles
\(h\in\langle p,q\rangle\).  Thus the nodal row is now empty; the cuspidal
ramified normal-minor locus \(\gcd\Delta\ne1\) remains separate.
`WORKING_CUSPIDAL_CUBIC_CURVE_EXIT.md` now excludes its transverse branch.

## 8. Nodal gcd-one representative

A convenient generic nodal cubic is
\[
A=(p^2q,\;pq^2,\;p^3+q^3)^T.
\tag{18}
\]
It is basepoint-free, birational because the ratio of its first two
coordinates is \(p/q\), and identifies \([1:0]\) and \([0:1]\) at its
node.  Its normal minors are
\[
\Delta=
\left(
3q^4-6p^3q,\;
3p^4-6pq^3,\;
3p^2q^2
\right)^T,
\tag{19}
\]
whose gcd is a nonzero constant.  Thus (12)--(17) apply.

By contrast, the cuspidal parametrization
\[
(p^2q,p^3,q^3)
\]
has a nonconstant common factor in its derivative minors and lies on the
ramified branch.  The audited cuspidal note carries that branch through the
lower identities when the fixed linear divisor is transverse to
\(\langle p,q\rangle\).  The scalar-aligned companion note independently
handles the cusp, flex, and general marked-point positions, so no cuspidal
placement remains.

Every irreducible nodal plane cubic over \(\mathbb C\) is projectively
equivalent to (18).  The two audited lower-identity calculations in
`WORKING_NODAL_CUBIC_CURVE_EXIT.md` and
`WORKING_SCALAR_ALIGNED_NODAL_CUBIC_EXIT.md` cover respectively
\(h\notin\langle p,q\rangle\) and \(h\in\langle p,q\rangle\).  They
therefore exclude the complete nodal row, not merely the displayed
representative.
