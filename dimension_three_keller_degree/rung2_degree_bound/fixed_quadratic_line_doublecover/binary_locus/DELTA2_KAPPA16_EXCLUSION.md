## 1. Theorem

Consider the binary fixed-quadratic line-double-cover row
\[
H_4=h(p,q)(p^2,q^2,0)
\]
on the two-ramification-contact, exact-\(\delta=2\),
\(\{k_1,k_2\}=\{2,0\}\) stratum at orbit modulus \(\kappa=16\).
Then no member can be a Keller counterexample.

More precisely, every putative Keller map on this stratum either:

- has singular linear part;
- is a polynomial automorphism by the banked degree-four plane-field
  exit; or
- has a nonzero \(E_4\) coefficient.

## 2. Normal form and exact open set

The sign choices \(\eta=\pm4\) are conjugate by the diagonal squaring-cover
stabilizer \(p\mapsto-p\).  Fix
\[
h=p^2+4pq+q^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The two contact equations \(3a\eta=4b\) and
\(4c=3d\eta\) give
\[
R=ap^3+3ap^2q+3dpq^2+dq^3.                         \tag{2}
\]
Exact calculation gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}(h,R)=-2(a+d)^2.                 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the exact-\(\delta=2\) open set in this family is
\[
a+d\ne0.                                           \tag{4}
\]
No normalization of \(a,d\), or of the complementary source coordinate,
is used in the solve.

The \(r^1\) Hilbert--Burch kernel is generated by
\[
N=(N_u,N_v,N_t)
  =(5p+q,\,-p-5q,\,3(a-d)).                         \tag{5}
\]
The two \(r^0\) tangents are \(pN,qN\).  Therefore the complete integrated
\(E_7\) family is
\[
\begin{aligned}
S&=\frac{k}{2}r^2+r(mp+nq),\\
H_3&=(U_0+N_uS,\ V_0+N_vS,\ R),\\
H_2&=(A_0+rA_1+x_5r^2,\ B_0+rB_1+y_5r^2,\ T_0+N_tS),
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{6}
\]
where \(U_0,V_0\) are arbitrary binary cubics,
\(A_0,B_0,T_0\) arbitrary binary quadratics, \(A_1,B_1\) arbitrary
binary linears, and the linear part \(L=(\ell_{ij})\) is arbitrary.
Thus every lower coefficient is present before the next identities are
solved.  The factor \(1/2\) in (6) comes from integrating \(krN\).

## 3. Complete \(E_6\) solve

### The genuine \(r^1\) tangent dies

The coefficient of \(r^3\) in \(E_6\) is
\[
12k^2\left\{
(a+2d)(p^3+3p^2q)+(2a+d)(3pq^2+q^3)
\right\}.                                          \tag{7}
\]
If the cubic in braces vanished, its endpoint coefficients would give
\[
a+2d=2a+d=0,
\]
hence \(a=d=0\), contrary to (4).  Therefore
\[
k=0.                                               \tag{8}
\]

After (8), the coefficient of \(r\) in \(E_6\) involves only
\[
m,n,x_5,y_5.
\]
Its \(p^5,q^5\) coefficients, divided by \(12\), are
\[
2m^2(a+2d),\qquad2n^2(2a+d).                       \tag{9}
\]
If neither endpoint factor vanishes, (9) gives \(m=n=0\).  The remaining
four equations in \(x_5,y_5\) have rank two for every
\((a,d)\ne(0,0)\): two of their \(2\times2\) minors are
\[
-8a(a+2d),\qquad-8d(2a+d).                         \tag{10}
\]

The endpoint specializations require separate checks and no division:

- if \(a=-2d\), then (4) gives \(d\ne0\); the equations contain
  \[
  x_5-y_5,\qquad7x_5-y_5,\qquad
  -9m^2+7x_5-7y_5,
  \]
  while (9) gives \(n=0\);
- if \(d=-2a\), the swapped equations give the same conclusion.

Hence in every case
\[
m=n=x_5=y_5=0.                                     \tag{11}
\]

Now all of \(H_3\) and the third component of \(H_2\) are binary.  The
constant part of \(E_6\) is the homogeneous \(\mathcal M_1\) system.
Its exact solution, including the divisor \(a=d\), is
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1&=\lambda(5p+q),\\
B_1&=\lambda(-p-5q),\\
\ell_{33}&=3\lambda(a-d).
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{12}
\]
No division by \(a-d\) occurs.  Rank four across (4) is guarded by the
two literal minors
\[
34560(a-d)(a+d)^2,\qquad
34560(13a-d)(a+d)^2.                               \tag{13}
\]

## 4. Three exhaustive lower branches

### 4.1. \(\lambda=0\): singular linear part

With \(\lambda=0\), equation \(E_5=0\) is the constant syzygy
\[
\alpha\ell_{13}+\beta\ell_{23}=0.                  \tag{14}
\]
The pair \(\alpha,\beta\) is constant-linearly independent on the exact
stratum.  Equivalently, its coefficient matrix has rank two; two covering
minors are
\[
-288a(a+2d),\qquad-288d(2a+d).                     \tag{15}
\]
Thus
\[
\ell_{13}=\ell_{23}=0.
\]
Together with \(\ell_{33}=0\) from (12), this makes the entire third
column of \(L\) zero, contradicting
\(\det L=\det JF(0)\ne0\).

### 4.2. \(\lambda\ne0,\ a\ne d\): plane-field automorphism exit

Here
\[
c=3\lambda(a-d)\ne0,
\]
and the third component has the form
\[
F_3=cr+G_3(p,q),\qquad \deg G_3\le3.                \tag{16}
\]
The triangular polynomial source change
\[
w=r+\frac{G_3(p,q)}c                                \tag{17}
\]
is an automorphism.  After (17) and scaling the third target coordinate,
the map has the form
\[
(P(p,q,w),Q(p,q,w),w).                             \tag{18}
\]

This step preserves the crucial degree bound.  Before (17), the
\(r\)-coefficient of each of \(F_1,F_2\) is a binary affine-linear form
\(\ell_{i3}+\lambda N_i\).  Substituting
\(r=w-G_3/c\) therefore adds a product of degrees at most \(1\) and
\(3\).  Consequently
\[
\deg_{p,q}P,\deg_{p,q}Q\le4.                       \tag{19}
\]

Over \(K=\mathbb C(w)\), the block determinant of (18) gives
\[
\frac{\partial(P,Q)}{\partial(p,q)}\in K^\times.   \tag{20}
\]
The established plane low-degree theorem applies after base change to
\(\overline K\): a plane Keller counterexample has degree far larger
than \(4\).  Thus the plane map in (20) has generic degree one, and
\[
K(p,q)=K(P,Q).                                     \tag{21}
\]
Generic degree descends under algebraic base change, so (18), and hence
the original three-variable map, is birational.  The classical
birational Keller theorem then makes it a polynomial automorphism.

This is exactly the banked argument in
`../../WORKING_FIXED_CUBIC_LINE_ROW.md`, Section 4 ("Birational plane
exit"), with the additional degree check (19).  It uses only the known
degree-\(\le4\) plane theorem, not the full plane Jacobian Conjecture.
It proves a three-variable polynomial automorphism, not merely
fibrewise invertibility.

### 4.3. \(\lambda\ne0,\ a=d\): direct \(E_4\) contradiction

Condition (4) now gives \(a=d\ne0\), and
\[
R=a(p+q)^3.                                        \tag{22}
\]
Write
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_0&=u_0p^3+u_1p^2q+u_2pq^2+u_3q^3,\\
V_0&=v_0p^3+v_1p^2q+v_2pq^2+v_3q^3,\\
T_0&=t_0p^2+t_1pq+t_2q^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The complete rank-six solution of \(E_5=0\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
t_0&=t_2,&t_1&=2t_2,\\
v_0&=-\frac15u_0+\frac1{15}u_1+\frac13v_1,&
v_2&=-5u_2+15u_3+3v_3,\\
\ell_{13}
 &=\lambda\left(-\frac3{10}u_0+\frac{11}{10}u_1
                 -\frac72u_2+\frac{15}{2}u_3\right),\\
\ell_{23}
 &=\lambda\left(\frac32u_0-\frac12u_1
                 +\frac{11}{2}u_2-\frac{33}{2}u_3
                 +v_1-3v_3\right).
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{23}
\]
The pivot determinant is
\[
-59719680\,a^4\lambda^4,                            \tag{24}
\]
so (23) is complete on the current open set.  All coefficients of
\(A_0,B_0\) and all unused entries of \(L\) remain free.

After (23), the coefficient of \(r\) in \(E_4\) collapses to
\[
[r]E_4=72a\lambda^2(p+q)^3.                        \tag{25}
\]
This is nonzero because \(a\lambda\ne0\), a contradiction.

The three cases exhaust every value of \(\lambda\) and \(a-d\), proving
the theorem.
