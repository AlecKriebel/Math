## 1. Intrinsic form of the row

The frozen tuple is
\[
(\operatorname{rank}JH_4,e,a,b,\delta,\nu)=(2,0,2,2,1,2).
\]
Thus \(H_4\) is primitive, its reduced projective image is a line, the
canonical pencil on the source is generated by coprime quadratic forms
\(p,q\), and the map from the canonical pencil line to the image line has
degree two.

Choose two independent target vectors \(u,v\).  Before making any
normalizing divisions, every point of the row has the form
\[
H_4=uR(p,q)+vS(p,q),
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
R(s,t)&=r_0s^2+r_1st+r_2t^2,\\
S(s,t)&=s_0s^2+s_1st+s_2t^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The binary quadratics \(R,S\) have no common zero on \(\mathbb P^1\);
equivalently, their homogeneous resultant is nonzero.  The conic pencil
\(\langle p,q\rangle\) is primitive: its general member is geometrically
integral.  For quadratic pencils this is equivalent to the determinant
cubic of the associated symmetric matrix pencil not being identically
zero.  Indeed, an imprimitive quadratic pencil is a degree-two map composed
with a linear pencil, hence, after linear changes, is
\(\langle x^2,y^2\rangle\); conversely that pencil is visibly composite.

Over \(\mathbb C\), a basepoint-free degree-two map
\([R:S]:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1\) has two distinct ramification points.
Independent changes of coordinates on its source and target give
\([s:t]\mapsto[s^2:t^2]\).  Therefore a useful divided normal form is
\[
H_4=u\,p^2+v\,q^2,
\tag{NF}
\]
with \(u,v\) independent and \(p,q\) a primitive coprime quadratic pencil.
All coverage and coefficient calculations below use the undivided form
\(uR(p,q)+vS(p,q)\), so no point is lost when a chosen normalizing minor
vanishes.

## 2. Exhaustive intrinsic conic-pencil charts

Associate symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices \(P,Q\) to \(p,q\), and write
\(\Delta(\lambda,\mu)=\det(\lambda P+\mu Q)\).

If \(\Delta\not\equiv0\), choose a nonsingular member as the bilinear form.
The other member gives a self-adjoint endomorphism.  Generalized
eigenspaces are orthogonal, and on each Jordan block the bilinear form is
congruent to the reverse-identity form.  Partitions of dimension three,
modulo a projective change of the pencil parameter, give exactly the
following five charts:

| chart | invariant type | representative \(\langle p,q\rangle\) | determinant-root type |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P111` | three simple generalized eigenvalues | \(\langle x^2+z^2,\ y^2+z^2\rangle\) | \(1+1+1\) |
| `P11_1` | semisimple double eigenvalue plus a simple one | \(\langle x^2+y^2,\ z^2\rangle\) | \(2+1\), two size-one blocks over the double root |
| `P2_1` | size-two Jordan block plus a simple eigenvalue | \(\langle y^2+z^2,\ 2xy+z^2\rangle\) | \(2+1\), one size-two block |
| `P21` | one eigenvalue, blocks of sizes two and one | \(\langle y^2,\ 2xy+z^2\rangle\) | \(3\), partition \(2+1\) |
| `P3` | one size-three Jordan block | \(\langle 2yz,\ 2xz+y^2\rangle\) | \(3\), partition \(3\) |

Each displayed pair is coprime, and each has
\(\Delta\not\equiv0\), so every chart remains in the canonical
degree-two-pencil row.

If \(\Delta\equiv0\), symmetric Kronecker decomposition has generic rank
two.  A positive minimal-index block of size three gives
\(\langle xy,xz\rangle\), which has a common linear factor and therefore
does not have \(e=0\).  A constant kernel leaves a regular binary
\(2\times2\) pencil.  Its nonsemisimple form again has a common linear
factor; the only coprime form is
\[
\langle x^2,y^2\rangle.
\tag{B0}
\]
This form is composite:
\(\mathbb C(x^2/y^2)\subsetneq\mathbb C(x/y)\).  Its canonical pencil has
degree one, so it exits to `Q2-E0-A1-B4-D1-N4`; it is not a sixth internal
chart.

These five charts also include every collision of discriminant roots that
preserves the frozen tuple: `P111` specializes to `P11_1` or `P2_1`, and
the latter specialize to `P21` or `P3`.  The remaining collision
\(\Delta\equiv0\) is exactly the canonical-degree drop (B0), or a
