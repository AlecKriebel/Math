## PASS_CONDITIONAL: direct finiteness → Hopfian wreath products (#197 → PR #389)

This is potentially a full negative answer to our original problem 2531 / Kourovka 21.22, conditional on OpenAI's direct-finiteness counterexample being valid. Our frozen `FINAL_RESULT.md` explicitly describes the target as the standard restricted regular wreath product of two finitely generated Hopfian groups, with no residual-finiteness or finite-presentation requirement.

The published Bradford–Fournier-Facio [paper](https://d-nb.info/1355447615/34), Theorem 4.11 (printed p. 22), states that universal Hopficity of `A wr H` for finitely generated abelian `A` and finitely generated Hopfian `H` is equivalent to Kaplansky direct finiteness. Its proof explicitly uses the embedding of every finitely generated group into a finitely generated Hopfian group (Miller–Schupp, 1971). Theorem 1.5 (printed p. 3) gives the prime-field matrix criterion. Thus it is unnecessary to prove that OpenAI's original group is Hopfian. The embedding removes that apparent hypothesis gap.

For the prime-field `F_2` scalar counterexample, the deduction can be checked directly. Embed the source group in finitely generated Hopfian `H`; the group-ring inclusion preserves `ab=1`, `ba≠1`. Identify the restricted lamp group of `C_2 wr H` with the additive group of `R=F_2[H]`, on which `H` acts by left multiplication. Define

`Phi(r,h)=(rb,h)`.

Right multiplication by `b` commutes with the left action, so `Phi` is a group homomorphism. A preimage of `(r,h)` is `(ra,h)`, because `(ra)b=r`. The nonzero lamp `c=1-ba` is in its kernel, since `cb=b-bab=0`. Therefore `C_2 wr H` is non-Hopfian although both factors are finitely generated Hopfian. All lamps have finite support; this is the restricted regular wreath product. A simple two-generator overgroup is another valid embedding route, but the already credited published equivalence suffices.

**Required wording:** “If the claimed OpenAI counterexample is correct, the published equivalence gives a negative answer to our original Hopfian wreath-product question.” Do not mark our problem solved merely from this comparison, or claim the reduction as new.

## PASS_CONDITIONAL: planar Fourier certificate → dimension-two three-point equality (#090 → PR #487)

Theorem 1.1 of [A sharp Fourier certificate for planar circle packing](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-sharp-Fourier-certificate-for-planar-circle-packing-September-23-2026/paper.pdf) states exactly the required one-point normalization: real radial Schwartz `f`, Fourier kernel `exp(-2πix·ξ)`, `fhat(0)=1`, `f(0)=2/√3`, global `fhat≥0`, and `f≤0` for `|x|≥1`, including the boundary. This matches the definition of `L_2` in our retained PR #487 proof. No regularity, Fourier phase, or sign-domain mismatch was found.

The lattice generated by `(1,0)` and `(1/2,√3/2)` has squared lengths `m²+mn+n²`, minimum nonzero length 1, covolume `√3/2`, and number density `2/√3`. Our proof §1, applied to its squared lattice, gives `P(C_triangle)≥4/3`; §3 gives `P(C_triangle)≤L_2²`. The OpenAI certificate and ordinary one-point Poisson bound give `L_2=2/√3`. Consequently,

`4/3 ≤ P(C_triangle) ≤ L_2² = 4/3`.

This conditionally establishes `P(C_triangle)=4/3` and adds dimension 2 to the sharp cases discussed in our proof. Packing density is `π/(2√3)`; it must not be substituted for the raw number-density value `L_2`. The conclusion uses our cited Cohn–de Laat–Salmon product theorem; the naive primal tensor still fails.

**Boundary:** this supplies a special dimension, not the all-dimension equality, universal optimizer conversion, or a new symmetry theorem.
