\begin{abstract}
We prove a parameter-independent recurrence theorem for stochastic
mass-action reaction networks.  If every complex has molecularity at most
two, there are at most three species, and there are at most two weakly
reversible linkage classes, then the minimal continuous-time Markov chain is
nonexplosive and every state in every closed irreducible component is positive
recurrent, for every positive rate vector.  More generally, the same result
holds when these bounds are satisfied after exact reduction on the chosen
closed class.  The proof combines an exact fixed-class projection, a symbolic
tier and affine-class reduction, all-clock stopped Foster estimates on the
remaining boundary faces, and a finite disjoint support identity.  Finite
computation is used only for finite support, tier, affine, and set identities;
no stochastic estimate is obtained by searching orientations, reaction
histories, or population boxes.  A separately compiled technical supplement
contains the complete byte-frozen local proofs and their reproducibility
manifest.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Weak reversibility is a central structural condition in reaction network
theory.  Deterministically, it is closely tied to persistence and complex
balance.  Stochastically, however, recurrence is sensitive to boundary faces,
disabled sources, and physical holding times.  A descending deterministic
tier need not be enabled, and recurrence of an embedded jump chain need not
control the continuous-time process.  The chemical recurrence conjecture asks
whether weak reversibility alone forces positive recurrence on every closed
irreducible class of the stochastic mass-action chain.

Several important cases are known.  Complex-balanced systems have
class-restricted product-form stationary laws
\cite{AndersonCraciunKurtz2010}, and are nonexplosive
\cite{AndersonCappellettiKoyamaKurtz2018}.  Anderson and Kim gave a tier
criterion that forces positive recurrence \cite[Theorem~9]{AndersonKim2018}.
For one linkage class, Anderson, Cappelletti, and Kim proved the binary case
when every species occurs as a pure unary or binary complex, together with a
broader proper-tier criterion \cite{AndersonCappellettiKim2020}.  These
results do not cover disabled mixed top complexes on boundary faces.

This paper establishes the conjectured conclusion in the following finite but
genuinely three-dimensional regime.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $(\cS,\cC,\cR,\kappa)$ be a finite weakly reversible stochastic
mass-action reaction network.  Suppose every complex has total molecularity
at most two, $|\cS|\le3$, and the reaction graph has at most two linkage
classes.  For every positive rate vector $\kappa$, the minimal continuous-time
Markov chain is nonexplosive, and every state in every closed irreducible
population class is positive recurrent.
\end{theorem}

The intrinsic version, Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic}, allows more ambient
species or linkages when all but three coordinates are constant and all but
two projected linkages are inactive or merge on the fixed closed class.

The proof is deliberately separated into analytic and finite components.
The analytic component treats arbitrary strongly connected orientations and
arbitrary positive rates.  It includes every physical clock, the reaction
that causes a cutoff, the actual terminal population, and physical duration.
The finite component verifies only an exact disjoint partition of a finite
support universe.  This distinction is essential: a catalog of reaction
words cannot prove the uniform stochastic estimates used here.

The local estimates are substantial, so their complete proofs are supplied
in the accompanying Technical Proof Supplement.  The main article proves the
global reduction, records the exact hypotheses and conclusions of each local
theorem, proves the symbolic tier and affine interfaces, and shows how the
standalone one- and two-linkage results imply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Stochastic reaction networks}

Let $\cS=\{S_1,\ldots,S_d\}$ and identify a complex
$y=\sum_i y_iS_i$ with $y\in\N^d$.  A labelled reaction is $y\to z$ with
rate constant $\kappa_{yz}>0$.  At population $x\in\N^d$ its stochastic
mass-action propensity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:propensity}
 \lambda_{y\to z}(x)=\kappa_{yz}(x)_y,
 \qquad
 (x)_y=\prod_{i=1}^d\frac{x_i!}{(x_i-y_i)!},
\end{equation}
with the convention that the propensity is zero unless $x\ge y$.
Parallel labelled channels are retained.  The generator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator}
 \cA f(x)=\sum_{y\to z\in\cR}
       \lambda_{y\to z}(x)\{f(x+z-y)-f(x)\}.
\end{equation}

The network is \emph{binary} when $|y|_1\le2$ for every complex.  Its
linkage classes are the connected components of the undirected complex graph.
It is weakly reversible when every linkage is strongly connected as a
directed graph.  A closed irreducible population class $\Gamma$ is a closed
communicating class of the chain.

Positive recurrence is always a classwise assertion here.  Finite or
absorbing classes are included.  For an infinite irreducible class it is
equivalent to finite mean positive return to one, and hence every, state.

\section{Exact reduction on a closed class}

Fix a closed irreducible class $\Gamma$.  Call coordinate $i$ constant if
$x_i=m_i$ for all $x\in\Gamma$.  Delete constant coordinates, delete a
linkage having no enabled source anywhere on $\Gamma$, project all remaining
complexes, and merge projected linkages that share a projected vertex.
Retain parallel labels and discard projected zero-displacement labels.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-class conjugacy]\label{lem:projection}
The reduced stochastic mass-action chain is exactly conjugate to the original
chain restricted to $\Gamma$.  It is weakly reversible, and projection cannot
increase the number of dynamic species or active linkage classes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ contain a complex $y_0$ enabled at $x\in\Gamma$, and put
$r=x-y_0\ge0$.  Along a directed path
$y_0\to y_1\to\cdots\to y_k$ in $L$, the state before the $j$th reaction is
$r+y_j$.  Thus the whole path is physically executable, and closure keeps all
its states in $\Gamma$.  If coordinate $i$ is constant, then
\[
             m_i=r_i+(y_j)_i
\]
along the path.  Strong connectivity therefore makes $y_i$ constant over all
complexes in $L$.  The deleted-coordinate factor
\[
          \prod_{i\ \mathrm{constant}}(m_i)_{y_i}
\]
is a positive constant and is absorbed into the labelled rate.  The remaining
factor is exactly the propensity of the projected source.

An inactive linkage contributes no transition on $\Gamma$.  Two projected
strong linkages sharing a vertex have strongly connected union; retaining
their labels and summing parallel propensities preserves the generator.
The coordinate-deletion map is one-to-one on $\Gamma$, and the preceding
identity intertwines the generators.  Deletion and merging can only reduce
the two stated counts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Binary nonexplosion]\label{lem:nonexplosion}
A finite binary stochastic mass-action network is nonexplosive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $N(x)=1+|x|_1$.  A degree-two source cannot increase total population,
because its binary target has degree at most two.  Every population-increasing
reaction therefore has source degree zero or one.  Since jumps are bounded,
for some $C<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-growth}
 \sum_{y\to z}\lambda_{y\to z}(x)(|z|-|y|)^+\le CN(x).
\end{equation}
Stop on first reaching $N=R$.  Dynkin's formula and Gronwall give
$\mathbb E N(X_{t\wedge\tau_R})\le N(x)e^{Ct}$, hence
$\mathbb P(\tau_R\le t)\le N(x)e^{Ct}/R$.  This tends to zero.  Within a
fixed population sublevel the state space is finite and the total rate is
bounded, so neutral quadratic reactions cannot accumulate infinitely many
jumps in finite time.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic form]\label{thm:intrinsic}
Fix a closed irreducible class of a finite weakly reversible binary network.
If its exact reduction in Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} has at most three dynamic
species and at most two active linkage classes, then the original chain is
positive recurrent on that class.
\end{theorem}

The remainder of the paper proves this theorem for one and two active
linkages.

\section{Tier and stopped-Foster interfaces}

For a population sequence $x_n$, deterministic tiers compare monomials
$(x_n\vee1)^y$, while stochastic tiers compare falling factorials $(x_n)_y$
and record whether $y$ is enabled.  Passing to a subsequence fixes both weak
orders and the eventual coordinate caps $0,1,\ge2$.

Let $E$ be the global top stochastic source tier and let $a_E$ be the
deterministic-tier level occupied by $E$.  For a linkage support $L$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:superlevel}
 U_L=\{y\in L: \text{the deterministic tier of }y
                    \text{ is at least }a_E\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact strong-orientation cut]\label{prop:cut}
Every strongly connected orientation of the linkage supports has a
deterministic-tier descending reaction sourced in $E$ if and only if, for
some linkage $L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut}
          \varnothing\ne U_L\subsetneq L,
          \qquad U_L\subseteq E.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \eqref{eq:cut} holds, take a directed path from $U_L$ to its complement.
The first exit is sourced in $E$ and descends.  Conversely, if
\eqref{eq:cut} fails, choose in each linkage a directed Hamiltonian cycle
whose unique exit from a nontrivial $U_L$ is sourced in
$U_L\setminus E$.  When $U_L$ is empty or whole there is no descending edge.
The resulting orientation is strong and has no descending edge sourced in
$E$.  This is a graph construction, not an orientation enumeration.
\end{proof}

The use of the $E$-level superlevel is essential.  The older test using the
absolute top deterministic tier is sufficient but not necessary when that
tier is wholly disabled.

The class-local form of Anderson--Kim's theorem is also needed.

\begin{proposition}[Class-local tier Foster]\label{prop:class-tier}
Let $\Gamma$ be closed.  If every proper tier sequence contained in $\Gamma$
has a top stochastic source of a deterministic-tier descending reaction, then
the chain is positive recurrent on $\Gamma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Repeat the contradiction proof of \cite[Theorem~9]{AndersonKim2018} with
the generator restricted to $\Gamma$.  Failure of a uniform entropy drift
outside a finite subset gives $x_n\in\Gamma$, $|x_n|\to\infty$, and
$\cA V(x_n)>-1$.  A tier subsequence remains in $\Gamma$, and the stated
hypothesis forces $\cA V(x_n)\to-\infty$, a contradiction.  The published
theorem is global; this paragraph is the required class-local restriction.
\end{proof}

For stopped regions we use the following standard state-selected form.

\begin{proposition}[Random-time Foster lemma]\label{prop:random-foster}
Let a nonexplosive countable-state chain have a proper $W\ge0$.  Suppose that
outside a finite set $K$, each state $x$ is assigned an almost surely finite
physical stopping time $\tau_x>0$ such that, for fixed $\eta,\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:random-foster}
 \mathbb E_x[W(X_{\tau_x})-W(x)+\eta\tau_x]\le-\delta,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_x[W(X_{\tau_x})+\tau_x]<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then $\mathbb E_xT_K<\infty$.  On a closed irreducible class, the chain is
positive recurrent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Iterate the selected rules at their actual physical endpoints and stop upon
the first hit of $K$.  Conditional expectation and localization give
\[
 \delta\,\mathbb E(m\wedge N_K)
 +\eta\,\mathbb E S_{m\wedge N_K}\le W(x)+\delta.
\]
Monotone convergence yields finite mean hitting time.  From the finite target,
take one ordinary jump and apply the hitting bound to its finitely many
successors.  A finite trace state has finite mean positive return; irreducibility
then gives positive recurrence.
\end{proof}

\section{One active linkage}

\begin{theorem}[One-linkage theorem]\label{thm:one-link}
Let the exact reduced network on a closed irreducible class be binary, weakly
reversible, have at most three dynamic species, and have exactly one active
linkage.  Then its chain is nonexplosive and positive recurrent for every
positive rate vector.
\end{theorem}

We summarize the proof, whose local theorems appear in Part I of the Technical
Proof Supplement.  On a proper escaping tier sequence, the top deterministic
block is proper.  If it contains an enabled source, a simple directed path to
the first exit yields an all-clock access episode.  For
\[
 G_\ell(x)=K_\ell+\sum_i\log(x_i!)+\ell\cdot x,
 \qquad W_\ell=G_\ell^4,
\]
the successful exit supplies a diverging logarithmic gap, while every
competitor is paid by the exact bound
\[
                  r\{1+\log(1/r)\}^q\le C_q.
\]

If every top source is disabled, binarity gives a symbolic classification.
With two dynamic species, the only non-deficiency-zero exception is, up to
relabeling,
\[
                   \{0,B,2B,A+B\}.
\]
With three dynamic species, the top is a mixed complex containing one zero
and one divergent coordinate.  There are only three nonstandard geometries:
a separated singleton top, a balanced tied top, and a bounded two-disabled-top
face.  Their full all-clock theorems prove actual-endpoint, physical-duration,
and fourth-power drift estimates.  No orientation list is used in this
classification or in those estimates.

A finite menu of literal statewise rules is fixed.  If no menu member had
uniform negative drift outside a finite set, an escaping sequence and a fixed
proper-tier subsequence would contradict one of the preceding coercive local
estimates.  Selecting the first rule that satisfies
\eqref{eq:random-foster} and applying Proposition~\ref{prop:random-foster}
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:one-link}.  The complete composition and all local
calculations are Supplement Sections 1--6.

\section{Two active linkages}

After exact reduction and merging, the two supports are disjoint subsets of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-complexes}
 \cC_2=\{0,A,B,C,2A,2B,2C,A+B,A+C,B+C\}.
\end{equation}

Assigning each of the ten binary complexes to linkage one, linkage two, or
neither, while requiring two disjoint supports of size at least two, gives
the exact ordered universe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:46872}
 3^{10}-2\{2^{10}+10\,2^9\}+\{1+10+10+90\}=46{,}872.
\end{equation}
The complete support certificate proves the pairwise-disjoint identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final-union}
 46{,}872=27{,}462+432+146+336+18{,}496.
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:branches}.  Every row is a standalone fixed-pair theorem for
arbitrary strongly connected labelled orientations and arbitrary positive
rates.

one actual-target marked factorial handles every dimension: the all-active

countable Flat0 phase is closed by a cap-free killed factorial resolvent.

chart exits or SCC quotient.

The executable certificate establishes only the finite support, tier,
affine, and disjoint-union identities.  Every stochastic implication in the
preceding paragraphs is a theorem in the byte-pinned Technical Proof
Supplement.

\begin{theorem}[Two-linkage theorem]\label{thm:two-link}
Let the exact reduced network on a closed irreducible class be binary, weakly
reversible, have at most three dynamic species, and have exactly two active
linkages.  Then its chain is nonexplosive and positive recurrent for every
positive rate vector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identity~\eqref{eq:final-union} assigns the fixed support pair to exactly one
row of Table~\ref{tab:branches}.  Apply that row's standalone theorem, which
gives finite mean return on the fixed physical class.  A support pair never
moves to another row, so no potential comparison across rows is required.
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonexplosion} supplies nonexplosion.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:intrinsic}]
Fix a closed irreducible class $\Gamma$ and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}.  Let $\ell_\Gamma$ be the number of active
projected linkages.  If $\ell_\Gamma=0$, the irreducible class is a singleton.
If $\ell_\Gamma=1$, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:one-link}.  If
$\ell_\Gamma=2$, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:two-link}.  These cases exhaust the
hypothesis.  Lemma~\ref{lem:nonexplosion} applies throughout, and the exact
conjugacy transports positive recurrence to the original physical class.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and audit boundary}

The source package contains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the main article and the complete Technical Proof Supplement;
\item a SHA-256 manifest for every local proof rendered into the supplement;
\item independent hostile proof audits, each pinned to exact theorem bytes;
\item a corrected finite certificate for the support, tier, affine, and
      disjoint-union identities; and
\item focused tests that reproduce the finite counts and fingerprints.
\end{enumerate}

The executable layer has a deliberately narrow claim boundary.  It neither
samples nor enumerates arbitrary reaction orientations, stochastic histories,
or population states.  All rate, orientation, endpoint, and physical-time
quantifiers are discharged in the mathematical proofs.

The older 2,511-based global composition is retained only as provenance.  It
correctly certifies its literal seed-level residual but does not exhaust the
full support universe after species and linkage symmetry.  The publication
dependency is now the exact 46,872-pair identity~\eqref{eq:final-union} and
the five standalone pair theorems in Table~\ref{tab:branches}.
