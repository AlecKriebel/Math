# Anisotropic 336: quotient entropy and physical-time Foster

**Proof-first standalone theorem, 2026-08-12 PDT.**  This note closes the
global boundary problem for the 24 anisotropic incidences in the residual
336 family.  After a coordinate permutation their supports are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h&=(1,1,2), & R&=\{0,A,B\},\\
 T&=\{C\}\cup Q, &
 Q&\subseteq\{2A,A+B,2B\},\qquad |Q|\ge2 .
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{1.1}
\]

Each support carries an arbitrary strongly connected directed graph and
arbitrary fixed positive labelled rates.  No orientation, rate vector,
reaction history, or population box is enumerated below.  The proof uses one
global quotient of the marked factorial entropy and a physical-time Foster
lemma which permits the expected decrement per jump to vanish at rate
\(H^{-1}\).

## 1. Statement

Fix a closed irreducible population class of the stochastic mass-action
chain generated by (1.1), and suppose the class is infinite.  Carry the
actual target \(t\) of the last physical reaction.  Put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H&=A+B+2C,
 &F(x,t)&=\sum_{i=A,B,C}\log((x_i-t_i)!),\\
 V(x,t)&=H(x)+\epsilon {F(x,t)\over H(x)+1}.&&
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{1.2}
\]

The first ordinary reaction supplies the initial mark; an absorbing state is
already recurrent.  The result proved here is the following.

> **Theorem 1.1 (global anisotropic level-set theorem).**  For sufficiently
> small fixed \(\epsilon>0\), there are a finite marked set \(K\), a constant
> \(\eta>0\), and a finite menu of all-clock, state-selected physical
> episodes which tile the trajectory until \(K\), contain at least one actual
> jump each, retain every competing clock, and satisfy
> \[
>  \mathbb E\!\left[
>   V(Y_{S_{n+1}})-V(Y_{S_n})+\eta(S_{n+1}-S_n)
>      \mid\mathcal F_{S_n}\right]\le0 .               \tag{1.3}
> \]
> Every endpoint is the actual population and target mark of the last
> included reaction.  Consequently \(\mathbb E_y\tau_K\le V(y)/\eta\), and
> the physical chain is nonexplosive and positive recurrent on its fixed
> irreducible class.

The proof is global across all coordinate faces.  It does not assume that a
compact \(A,B=\Theta(\sqrt H)\) shell is reached on a prescribed time scale,
and it uses no stationary averaging of the neutral linkage.

## 2. Exact marked identities and global hazard

For a complex \(y\), let

\[
 K_y=\sum_{e:s(e)=y}\kappa_e,
 \qquad \lambda_y(x)=K_y(x)_y,
 \qquad \Lambda(x)=\sum_y\lambda_y(x),
 \qquad p_y={\lambda_y\over\Lambda}.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

If the next labelled reaction is \(e:y\to u\), with population increment
\(\zeta_e=u-y\), factorial cancellation gives

\[
 F(x+\zeta_e,u)-F(x,t)=\log{(x)_t\over(x)_y}=:r_e(x,t).
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

Thus one ordinary all-clock jump has mean reward

\[
 D(x,t)=\sum_e {\lambda_e(x)\over\Lambda(x)}r_e(x,t)
       =\log p_t-\sum_y p_y\log p_y-\log K_t+\sum_y p_y\log K_y,
                                                               \tag{2.3}
\]
and hence

\[
 D(x,t)\le\log p_t+C,
 \qquad
 \sup_{x,t}\sum_e {\lambda_e(x)\over\Lambda(x)}[r_e(x,t)]_+^q<\infty
                                                               \tag{2.4}
\]
for every fixed \(q<\infty\).

Every \(T\)-reaction preserves \(H\).  The lower linkage changes \(H\) only
through zero-source births and unary-to-zero deaths.  If

\[
 \beta=\sum_{0\to u}\kappa_{0u},\qquad
 \delta_A=\sum_{A\to0}\kappa_{A0},\qquad
 \delta_B=\sum_{B\to0}\kappa_{B0},                    \tag{2.5}
\]

then

\[
                       \mathcal LH=\beta-\delta_AA-\delta_BB. \tag{2.6}
\]

At least one of \(\delta_A,\delta_B\) is positive.  More importantly, the
total hazard has the global lower bound

\[
                            \Lambda(x)\ge c_0(H(x)+1). \tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed the strong graph on \(R\) gives a positive total outgoing rate at
each of the sources \(A,B\), while \(C\in T\) gives one at \(C\).  Their
propensities bound \(A+B+C\ge H/2\).  The constant source covers \(H=0\).

The quotient in (1.2) is proper because \(V\ge H\).  Since \(t\) is binary,

\[
                  0\le F(x,t)\le C(H+1)\log(H+2).       \tag{2.8}
\]

## 3. Exact quotient increment

Write \(d_e=H(x+\zeta_e)-H(x)\).  Here \(d_e=0\) on \(T\) and on lower
unary transfers, \(d_e=1\) on lower births, and \(d_e=-1\) on lower
deaths.  Combining (2.2) with (1.2) gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_eV
 &=d_e+\epsilon\left\{{F+r_e\over H+d_e+1}-{F\over H+1}\right\}\\
 &=d_e+{\epsilon r_e\over H+1}
   -{\epsilon d_e(F+r_e)\over(H+1)(H+d_e+1)} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

There is a useful sign which prevents a logarithmic denominator toll.  Write
\(F'=F+r_e\ge0\) for the endpoint factorial value.  On a birth,
\[
 1+\epsilon\left\{{F'\over H+2}-{F\over H+1}\right\}
 \le 1+{\epsilon r_e\over H+1},                       \tag{3.2}
\]
because the omitted term is \(-\epsilon F'/((H+1)(H+2))\).
On a death,
\[
 -1+\epsilon\left\{{F'\over H}-{F\over H+1}\right\}
 =-1+{\epsilon r_e\over H+1}
             +{\epsilon F'\over H(H+1)}.              \tag{3.3}
\]
By (2.8), the last term in (3.3) is \(o(1)\), uniformly over one
bounded-displacement jump.  It can therefore be absorbed into half of the
favorable workload death outside a finite set.

A birth has probability at most
\(\beta/\Lambda=O(H^{-1})\).  Its workload cost has expected size
\[
                              O(H^{-1}).                \tag{3.4}
\]

Every other reaction has \(d_e=0\), so its quotient reward is exactly
\(\epsilon r_e/(H+1)\).  These estimates remain valid, with changed fixed
constants, along every bounded-depth prescribed path: populations and \(H\)
move by \(O(1)\), and (2.7) continues to hold.

## 4. Rare-source Bellman episodes

Fix one linkage \(L\in\{T,R\}\), an actual target \(t\in L\), and a
terminal complex \(c\in L\).  Choose a simple directed path

\[
                    t=y_0\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow y_m=c. \tag{4.1}
\]

Retain all clocks.  Continue only on the designated label, stop at the
actual endpoint of the first competitor, and after designated arrival at
\(c\) take one final ordinary jump.  Let \(J(x,t;c)\) denote its expected
total \(F\)-reward.  If the source probability of \(c\), at the deterministic
success endpoint, tends to zero along an escaping sequence, the exact
Bellman recursion

\[
                         J_i=D_i+a_iJ_{i+1}             \tag{4.2}
\]

and the first-vanishing-source argument give

\[
                            J(x,t;c)\longrightarrow-\infty. \tag{4.3}
\]

All positive \(F\)-reward moments and all jump counts are uniformly bounded.
Equation (2.7) gives

\[
                     \mathbb E\tau\le {C\over H+1}.    \tag{4.4}
\]

At every stage, use the sign in (3.2)--(3.3), discard the remaining
favorable death contribution, and charge the expected births by (3.4).
The stage workload differs from its initial value by \(O(1)\).  Moreover,
whenever the current target and next source are enabled binary complexes,
\[
                         |r_e(x,t)|\le C\log(H+2).      \tag{4.5}
\]
Replacing each denominator \(H_i+1\) by the initial \(H+1\) therefore costs
only \(O(\log(H+2)/H^2)\) over the bounded episode.  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E\Delta V
 \le {\epsilon J(x,t;c)+C\over H+1}
        +{C\log(H+2)\over(H+1)^2}.                     \tag{4.6}
\]

Hence, once \(-J\) is larger than a fixed constant, (4.4) and (4.6) imply
(1.3), with one common sufficiently small \(\eta>0\).  Sequential
compactness over the finite menu makes this uniform outside a finite set.
No fixed embedded-jump decrement is asserted or needed.

For the lower linkage use \(c=0\).  Its propensity is constant, while
\(\Lambda\ge c_0H\), so

\[
                              p_0\le {C\over H+1}\longrightarrow0. \tag{4.7}
\]

Thus every mark in \(R\) starts a rare-source episode.

## 5. Exhaustive top-source dichotomy

It remains to treat marks in \(T\).  For the actual target \(t\) and each
\(c\in T\), fix a simple path from \(t\) to \(c\).  Its deterministic
success population is
\[
                              z_c=x-t+c.               \tag{5.1}
\]
Because all members of \(T\) have the same \(H\)-level, \(H(z_c)=H(x)\).

The quantitative Bellman recursion behind (4.3) has the following finite-
menu consequence.  There is a decreasing function \(A(\delta)\uparrow
\infty\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 p_c(z_c)\le\delta
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad J(x,t;c)\le-A(\delta). \tag{5.2}
\]
Otherwise a sequence with \(\delta\downarrow0\) would contradict the
first-vanishing-source recursion (4.2).  Here is a direct quantitative
version.  Let \(\alpha_*>0\) be the smallest conditional branching
probability of a labelled edge used by the finite path menu, and let \(C_0\)
be the constant in \(D_i\le\log p_{y_i}+C_0\).  Set
\[
 B_0(\delta)=\log\delta+C_0,\qquad
 B_{k+1}(\delta)=C_0+\sup_{0<p\le1}
       \{\log p+\alpha_*pB_k(\delta)\}.                \tag{5.3}
\]
Once \(B_k<0\), replacing an actual edge branching probability
\(\alpha\ge\alpha_*\) by \(\alpha_*\) only increases the upper bound.
Induction in (4.2) therefore gives \(J\le B_m(\delta)\) for a path of
length \(m\).  If \(B_k=-L\), the supremum in (5.3) is at most
\(-\log(\alpha_*L)-1\) for large \(L\).  Thus every \(B_k(\delta)\to-\infty\).
Taking the maximum over the finitely many path lengths proves (5.2).

Choose \(\delta>0\) so small that (4.4),(4.6) imply (1.3) whenever (5.2)
applies.

Suppose instead that
\[
                              p_c(z_c)>\delta\qquad(c\in T). \tag{5.4}
\]
Apply this first to \(c=C\).  The global lower bound (2.7) at \(z_C\)
and \(\lambda_C(z_C)\ge\delta\Lambda(z_C)\) give
\[
                              C(x)\ge cH,\qquad
                              \Lambda(z_C)\le CH.       \tag{5.5}
\]
The states \(z_c\), \(z_C\), and \(x\) differ by bounded vectors.  For any
quadratic \(q\in Q\), (5.4) and (2.7) at \(z_q\) give
\[
                              (z_q)_q\ge cH,            \tag{5.6}
\]
while the upper bound in (5.5), transported across bounded displacement,
gives
\[
                              (z_q)_q\le CH.            \tag{5.7}
\]
Hazard transport across the bounded displacement must retain a linear
remainder, because the displacement can activate a mixed source on a face.
Sourcewise, for every fixed bounded integer vector \(b\) for which both
populations are nonnegative,
\[
 (w+b)_i\le (w)_i+C_b,
 \qquad
 (w+b)_{2i}\le C_b\{(w)_{2i}+w_i+1\},
\]
and
\[
 (w+b)_{i+j}\le
 C_b\{(w)_{i+j}+w_i+w_j+1\}.                          \tag{5.8}
\]
After multiplication by the fixed source rates and summation over the
finite source menu, these inequalities give the correct global comparison
\[
              \Lambda(w+b)\le
                  C_b\{\Lambda(w)+H(w)+1\}.            \tag{5.9}
\]
Since \(\Lambda(z_C)=O(H)\), equation (5.9) gives
\(\Lambda(z_q)=O(H)\), including when the bounded displacement activates a
previously disabled mixed source.

Since \(Q\) contains at least two of \(2A,A+B,2B\), (5.6)--(5.7) imply
\[
                c\sqrt H\le A\le C\sqrt H,\qquad
                c\sqrt H\le B\le C\sqrt H.             \tag{5.10}
\]
Indeed, the pair \(\{2A,2B\}\) is immediate.  For
\(\{2A,A+B\}\), the \(2A\) bounds give \(A=\Theta(\sqrt H)\), and the
\(A+B\) bounds then give \(B=\Theta(\sqrt H)\); the remaining pair is
symmetric.  Extra vertices do not alter the conclusion.  Equations
(5.5),(5.10) also give at the current state
\[
                              cH\le\Lambda(x)\le CH.    \tag{5.11}
\]

Let \(d\in\{A,B\}\) be a species with \(\delta_d>0\).  Equations
(2.6) and (5.10) give
\[
                           \mathcal LH\le-C_1\sqrt H.   \tag{5.12}
\]

Take one ordinary all-clock jump.  Its expected duration is
\(1/\Lambda\le C/H\) by (2.7).  Combine a death's denominator correction
with half of its favorable workload decrement as in (3.3).  Birth
denominator corrections are favorable.  The remaining expected quotient
reward is at most \(\epsilon D/(H+1)\le C\epsilon/H\) by (2.3)--(2.4).
After this absorption, the workload part is bounded above by
\[
              {\beta-\tfrac12\delta_AA-\tfrac12\delta_BB\over\Lambda}.
\]
Thus (5.11)--(5.12) give
\[
                           \mathbb E\Delta V
                              \le-{c\over\sqrt H}+{C\over H}. \tag{5.13}
\]

The duration term is at most \(\eta C/H\), so the one-jump rule satisfies
(1.3) for all sufficiently large \(H\).  The alternatives (5.2) and (5.4)
are pointwise and exhaustive.  There are only finitely many targets and
paths, so they define a finite state-selected menu without a compactness
gap.

## 6. The episode-time Foster lemma

For completeness, here is the exact random-time interface used above.

> **Lemma 6.1 (vanishing-margin physical-time Foster lemma).**  Let \(Y\) be
> a nonexplosive CTMC, \(K\) a finite set, and \(V\ge0\) one proper
> function.  Until \(K\), suppose state-selected physical episodes tile the
> trajectory at strict stopping times
> \[
>                  0=S_0<S_1<S_2<\cdots,              \tag{6.1}
> \]
> every episode contains at least one actual jump, and (1.3) holds for one
> fixed \(\eta>0\).  Suppose the stopped conditional sums are justified by
> localization; it is sufficient that, for every finite \(V\)-sublevel,
> the positive endpoint increment of an episode started in that sublevel is
> integrable.  Then
> \[
>                              \mathbb E_y\tau_K\le {V(y)\over\eta}. \tag{6.2}
> \]

Stop the conditional sum of (1.3) at the first **episode endpoint** in
\(K\), at the first endpoint outside a finite \(V\)-sublevel, and after
\(n\) episodes.
Localization and conditional summation give

\[
                        \eta\,\mathbb ES_{n\wedge N_K\wedge\sigma_R}
                                  \le V(y).             \tag{6.3}
\]

Let \(R\to\infty\) and then \(n\to\infty\).  Nonnegativity of \(V\) and
Fatou handle the endpoint term.  On non-hitting, the endpoints contain an
infinite subsequence of genuine jump times, so nonexplosion forces
\(S_n\to\infty\).  Equation (6.3) rules out non-hitting with positive
probability, and monotone convergence gives (6.2).

In the present network, every rule in Sections 4--5 contains at least one
ordinary jump.  The actual endpoint mark chooses the next rule immediately;
there is no zero-duration classifier episode and hence no artificial
zero-time cycle.  A localized \(V\)-sublevel contains only finitely many
marked starts by properness.  From any such start an episode has bounded
jump count and bounded population displacement, so its positive endpoint
increment is bounded on that localized start set.  This verifies the exact
integrability hypothesis.  Globally, (2.4) also supplies all fixed
source-weighted logarithmic positive moments.

## 7. Recurrence and scope

Binaryity gives nonexplosion: population-increasing reactions have degree at
most one, so their aggregate rate is at most \(C(1+|x|_1)\), while neutral
quadratic clocks cannot accumulate inside a finite population sublevel.

Lemma 6.1 gives finite mean hitting of \(K\).  One physical jump from this
finite set has finitely many successors, each with the same bound, so \(K\)
has finite mean return.  Irreducibility normalizes its finite occupation
measure to the unique stationary probability; forgetting the target mark
proves positive recurrence of the physical class.

Thus the theorem covers exactly the 24 residual anisotropic permutations of
(1.1); the homogeneous \(h=(1,1,1)\) family has different dormant faces and
lies outside its scope.
