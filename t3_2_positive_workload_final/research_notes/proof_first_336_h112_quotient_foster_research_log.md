# Research log: anisotropic 336 quotient Foster theorem

## 2026-08-12 13:39 PDT

The initially proposed compact-shell entrance lemma was rejected as too
strong: the carrier example \(T=\{C,A+B,2A\}\), started from a large pure
\(B\)-reservoir, need not enter the \(A,B=\Theta(\sqrt H)\) shell on the
\(H^{-1/2}\) time scale.

The replacement is proof-first and global.  The exact structural identity
\(R=\{0,A,B\}\), \(C\in T\) gives \(\Lambda\ge c(H+1)\) on every face.  This
allows the quotient potential
\(V=H+\epsilon F/(H+1)\) and a physical-time Foster lemma whose decrement per
jump may vanish as \(H^{-1}\).  Marks in \(R\) always have a path to the rare
constant source zero.  Marks in \(T\) split analytically: either a top source
probability vanishes and the marked Bellman recursion is coercive, or every
top source has positive limiting probability.  The simultaneous sources
\(C\) and at least two of \(2A,A+B,2B\) then force
\(\Lambda=\Theta(H)\) and \(A,B=\Omega(\sqrt H)\), so direct lower death gives
one-jump workload descent of order \(H^{-1/2}\), larger than quotient errors.

This removes the proposed two-reservoir occupation theorem from the
anisotropic branch.  The homogeneous \(h=(1,1,1)\) dormant faces remain a
separate problem.
