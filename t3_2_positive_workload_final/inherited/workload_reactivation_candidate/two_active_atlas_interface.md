# Two-active atlas interface

Assume `A,B` are active and `C` lies in a fixed finite box. Up to exchanging
`A,B`, the four workload representatives

\[
(1,1,0),\quad(2,3,0),\quad(1,2,0),\quad(1,3,0)
\]

cover every chamber and wall of the ten-complex bimolecular arrangement.

Three independent implementations certify:

- 187,488 workload assignments;
- 446 shielded assignments;
- 382 common-invariant assignments;
- 60 deficiency-zero assignments;
- four ordered service assignments;
- zero unclassified assignments.

The reduced noninvariant atlas contains 27 deficiency-zero classes and two
service architectures.

Flat linkages are workload-neutral and are retained in the faster zero trace.
Every available linkage has an enabled top/unary witness or a finite
active-plus-bounded-cofactor carrier phase; actual-target service gives strict
reward or a recorded box/support exit. Hence a closed edgewise-zero phase can
contain only flat or shielded linkages. If both represented linkages are
shielded, the certified pair atlas applies.

The three atlas conclusions are:

1. a rational affine invariant with positive coefficients on `A,B`;
2. a weakly reversible deficiency-zero network, with a summable
   product-Poisson stationary measure on every closed lattice class;
3. one of the two service architectures
   \[
   \{C,2C\}\ \&\ \{0,A,2A,B+C\},
   \]
   \[
   \{0,C,2C\}\ \&\ \{A,2A,B+C\}.
   \]

For the service systems set `W=B-C`. The mixed linkage preserves `W`; the
`C` linkage supplies a finite-shell trial satisfying

\[
W_\tau\le W_0-1
\]

or produces active-set promotion or lower-rank exit. Every strongly
connected directed support of the exceptional complex sets is included in
the service certificate.
