# Smallest remaining theorem

## Rate-weighted current-target trace theorem

Fix a terminal chart for a three-species, two-linkage bimolecular network,
including its bounded-coordinate box, complete source-rate flag, compact tied
ratio cell and exact current-target phase.

Partition every physical path into the certified current-target episodes.
At a slower reaction-count scale, contract faster recurrent zero classes by
exact Poisson correctors, but retain every activation jump and the target
episode it creates at their natural, unconditioned probabilities.

The remaining theorem is to prove that every recurrent effective trace class
has exactly one of:

1. strictly negative stationary corrected reward;
2. positive structural-exit flux;
3. a finite zero coboundary extending to an affine workload invariant
   positive on the chart's escape cone;
4. one of the certified deficiency-zero or service architectures;
5. an exact positive/critical Markov-additive class liftable to a physical
   counterexample.

The theorem must allow an available linkage's reaction-count fraction to be
`1/log R`, `1/R`, or an iterated-log scale.  Vanishing at one normalization
must transfer the linkage to the next slower physical trace.  It must not
condition on the activation reaction.

## Why the existing finite tools are not yet sufficient

For a supplied finite kernel, Bellman, Poisson, Schur and Farkas alternatives
are exact.  What remains unproved is that the original unbounded physical
process induces, after every source-layer contraction, a finite kernel whose
complete rewards satisfy the required nonpositive-cycle property.  The
activation-pair counterexample shows that this property cannot be assigned
edge-by-edge or to a conditioned activation block; it must be proved for the
whole unconditioned current-target trace.

Closing this theorem would complete the available-linkage branch of the
certified two-active atlas.  The common-invariant, deficiency-zero and two
service branches are already finite and certified.
