# One-active-coordinate closure

Assume `A` is the only unbounded coordinate; `B,C` and all marks lie in one
finite padded phase.

Every nonexit transition is

\[
(n,\phi)\to(n+k,\phi'),\qquad |k|\le2,
\]

with exact rate

\[
c_2(\phi,e)n(n-1)+c_1(\phi,e)n+c_0(\phi,e).
\]

## A `2A` source

Every genuine channel from `2A` lowers `A`. All upward channels have source
degree at most one. Thus, uniformly over the finite phase,

\[
\mathcal L A\le-c_2A(A-1)+c_1A+c_0,\qquad c_2>0,
\]

unless a declared phase or chart exit occurs.

## No `2A`

Every degree-one channel has `A`-increment zero or minus one. The finite
degree-one phase classes are treated exactly:

- a negative edge gives strict negative stationary mean and a bounded Poisson
  corrector;
- otherwise every recurrent degree-one edge preserves `A` and the class is
  contracted.

A bounded-source channel creates at most one `A` and its actual target is
`A` or `A+D`. The creator linkage's return path contains a one-`A` service
source. Bounded-coordinate theft is included in the finite phase, and
aggregate debt handles repeated births before service.

If all trigger-relaxation macroedges are zero, let `K` be the bounded service
species occurring in one-`A` complexes. Either a unary one-`A` complex or a
zero-`A` complex containing `K` gives unpaired service, or

\[
A-\sum_{D\in K}D
\]

is an exact invariant. Since the bounded coordinates and phase correction are
bounded, this invariant excludes one-active escape.

A zero mean with nonzero corrected variance is explicitly rejected. The zero
branch used here is edgewise zero after complete fast relaxation.
