## Exact classical statement and provenance

For a field k, Costa's question is whether every k-algebra retract of k[x_1,...,x_N] is isomorphic, as a k-algebra, to a polynomial ring. An algebra retract means a k-subalgebra B with a k-homomorphism r:k[x_1,...,x_N]→B whose restriction to B is the identity. Equivalently, it is the image of an idempotent k-algebra endomorphism. There is no requirement that the retraction preserve the standard grading.

Original bibliographic record: Douglas L. Costa, *Retracts of polynomial rings*, Journal of Algebra **44**(2) (February 1977), 492–502, [DOI 10.1016/0021-8693(77)90197-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(77)90197-1). Crossref's primary publisher metadata was downloaded and inspected. The original full text could not be accessed through the available publisher and text-mining endpoints, so this audit does not claim to have read it. The question's attribution and its location in original Section 4 are checked through the exact reproduction in Chakraborty–Dasgupta–Dutta–Gupta, [arXiv:1910.11023v1, Section 5, Question 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11023v1#S5), and Nagamine's Question 1.3. Cite Costa, and explain that the formulation used is reproduced in those primary papers; do not invent an original question number beyond the verified Section 4 attribution.

Nagamine's [arXiv:1811.04153v2, Proposition 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.04153v2#S1) says that an affirmative answer to the retract question in N=n+1 variables gives affirmative affine-space cancellation in dimension n. Its entire proof was read in the PDF and HTML: for X×A¹≅Aⁿ⁺¹ with coordinate ring A, one has A[t]≅k^[n+1], transcendence degree n, and A is a retract of that polynomial ring. The statement is over an arbitrary field and has no characteristic-zero restriction. This is exactly the contrapositive implication needed here.

The arXiv paper was first submitted 9 November 2018 at 22:39:15 UTC and revised to v2 on 24 November 2018 at 22:08:06 UTC. **Version 1 does not contain Proposition 1.4.** Thus the explicit numbered proposition is documented publicly by 24 November 2018, not by the first submission. Earlier literature already records the reduction: Epstein–Nguyen, [arXiv:1301.3967v1, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3967v1#S1), submitted 17 January 2013 at 02:16:27 UTC, gives the polynomial-extension retract and states Costa's question as stronger than cancellation. This audit does not claim to identify the first historical observation.

## Verified prior status and exact boundaries

- Nagamine proves polynomiality for all retracts of a three-variable polynomial ring over any characteristic-zero field (Theorem 2.5). Journal version: J. Algebra **534** (September 2019), 339–343, [DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.05.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.05.040).
- Chakraborty–Dasgupta–Dutta–Gupta prove a stronger low-image-dimension result: every characteristic-zero retract of k^[N] of transcendence degree two is k^[2], for arbitrary N (Theorem 5.8). Their Question 3.1 asks about characteristic zero, N≥4, image transcendence degree ≥3. Journal version: J. Algebra **567** (February 2021), 243–268, [DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2020.08.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2020.08.030); arXiv:1910.11023v1 submitted 24 October 2019 at 10:47:57 UTC.
- Chakraborty–Pal's [arXiv:2504.14382v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14382v2), revised 20 August 2026 at 18:51:33 UTC, states in the introduction and the end of Section 5 that the question remains open in characteristic zero for every N≥4. Its correct current title is *Monomial and binomial retracts of polynomial rings*; version 1 (19 April 2025 at 19:07:02 UTC) was titled *Monomial retracts of polynomial rings are polynomial rings*. Journal version: J. Pure Appl. Algebra **230**(9) (September 2026), article 108387, [DOI 10.1016/j.jpaa.2026.108387](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2026.108387). The full relevant statements, not just the abstract, were checked. Its monomial and binomial affirmative results concern restricted maps and do not duplicate a general characteristic-zero counterexample.

Therefore, conditional on a sound family 047, a complex nonpolynomial retract of C^[5] with image transcendence degree four gives a negative answer at ambient dimension five to the characteristic-zero assertion. It leaves the ambient-four-dimensional question untouched. It does not contradict polynomiality for two-dimensional images. Extending a five-variable idempotent by sending additional variables to zero gives a nonpolynomial four-dimensional image in every ambient N≥5; this is another elementary consequence and does not supply evidence at N=4.
