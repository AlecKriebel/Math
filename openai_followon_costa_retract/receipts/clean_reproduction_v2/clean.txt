An explicit complex polynomial retract in ﬁve variables:
a consequence of aﬃne-space cancellation failure
Alec Kriebel

Abstract
OpenAI’s explicit complex cancellation fourfold has a polynomial one-variable cylinder and
is not aﬃne four-space. We transport coeﬃcient inclusion and evaluation at zero through its
concrete cylinder coordinates. This gives a complex algebra retract of a ﬁve-variable polynomial
ring, of transcendence degree four, that is not a polynomial algebra. We provide split maps and a
ﬁve-component idempotent polynomial endomorphism, in factored form with no denominators,
and exact symbolic certiﬁcates for the coordinate identities, including the ﬁber at the parameter
zero. The implication from cancellation failure to a negative answer to Costa’s retract question
is classical; the nonpolynomiality input and cancellation breakthrough belong to OpenAI. This
note makes that consequence explicit in ambient dimension ﬁve and makes no assertion about
ambient dimension four.

The question and the inherited input

Costa asked whether a retract of k [n] containing the ﬁeld k must itself be a polynomial k-algebra
[1]. Here a retract is speciﬁed by unital k-algebra maps i : A → B and r : B → A with ri = 1A ;
we identify A with i(A) ⊂ B. Nagamine explicitly records the classical implication: a positive
answer in n + 1 variables implies cancellation for An [2, Proposition 1.4]. His proof is coeﬃcient
inclusion followed by evaluation of the cylinder variable at zero. Nagamine settles the ambient
three-variable case in characteristic zero. Earlier literature also states the polynomial-extension
retract relationship [5, Introduction]. More generally, every characteristic-zero polynomial-ring
retract of transcendence degree two is polynomial [3, Theorem 5.8]. The August 2026 version of
Chakraborty–Pal still records the general characteristic-zero question as open for ambient n ≥ 4
[4].

Veriﬁcation, attribution, and limits

The supplement identiﬁes all source hashes, the exact dependency statements, audit scopes, and
reproducible checks. The symbolic script uses integer polynomial arithmetic through SymPy. It
checks the derivation, exponential inverse, determinant-one change, root and ﬁfth-coordinate certiﬁcates, and the polynomial inverse identities. The other inverse composition on w uses the proved
domain and p-cancellation, rather than a sample-point test. The structural proof of (12) then certiﬁes idempotence and the image algebra. Additional exact rational examples are regression checks
only; they do not prove nonpolynomiality or replace polynomial identities.
The actual upstream Lean declaration is OAI.ComplexCancellation.main in OAI/Algebra/
AffineCancellation/Main.lean; its statement matches (1) and the cylinder/nonpolynomiality
claim. The comparator’s sorry is a separate challenge statement. We inspected the actual solution sources and their semantic scope, but did not reproduce the full Lean kernel build: the
local exact-version dependency build was stopped by disk limits. Accordingly this note relies on
the independently audited written theorem, and claims neither a reproduced formal proof nor a
formalization of the follow-on retract theorem.
Our primary-source and companion-corpus audit found no earlier explicit statement or ﬁvecomponent transport for this particular complex example. A failed search is not proof of priority.
The general cylinder-to-retract mechanism is already explicit in Nagamine and is not claimed as
new. We attribute the deﬁning example, stabilization, and nonpolynomiality to OpenAI using its
manuscript-speciﬁc citation. This is a consequence note, not an independent cancellation breakthrough.
