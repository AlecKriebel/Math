# Proof attempt 2: companion congruences and nontriangular candidates

## Outcome

The growing-dimensional first-vector/SVP exponent-1/2 target of attempt 1 remains unresolved. This attempt genuinely tests a second construction: reversing the coefficient coordinates of the near-critical basis, so that multiplication by X wraps every basis vector through the freely chosen quotient polynomial. The entire reversed family is refuted by an integral-action recurrence independent of q. ## 1. Scope carried forward

## 2. Reversing the critical candidate: exact failure for every q

Fix n>=2 and rational 1/sqrt(2)<t<1. In fixed physical coefficient coordinates e_0,...,e_{n-1}, define

    b_1=e_{n-1},
    b_i=(1/2)b_{i-1}+t^{i-1}e_{n-i},  2<=i<=n.

Clear denominators by a common positive integer D. Coordinate reversal is orthogonal, so this basis inherits the reducedness and short-vector ratio proved in attempt 1. Unlike the original increasing-degree triangular basis, every b_i has a highest-degree component. Thus changing q can affect every Xb_i, and the earlier no-wrap lemma does not directly apply.

Suppose nevertheless the lattice were an ideal for some monic q. Let A_i in Z^n be the coefficient column of X(D b_i) in the integral basis (D b_1,...,D b_n). For i>=2, the highest-coordinate component cancels in

    2b_i-b_{i-1}=2t^{i-1}e_{n-i}.

Its multiplication by X does not wrap, because n-i<=n-2. For i=2 this gives

    2A_2-A_1=2t E_1,

where E_j means the j-th standard vector in basis-coefficient space, not physical coefficient space. If n=2, integrality already forces 2t to be an integer, impossible for sqrt(2)<2t<2.

For i>=3, use

    e_{n-i+1}=(b_{i-1}-(1/2)b_{i-2})/t^{i-2}

to obtain

    2A_i-A_{i-1}=2t E_{i-1}-t E_{i-2}.

The left side is integral, so t must be an integer, again impossible. In fact the i=2 equation alone excludes all n>=2 in the stated interval. The stronger i>=3 equation makes the rank-one limitation especially explicit.

Thus **no choice of monic q can make any denominator-cleared reversed candidate an ideal**. The argument uses exact integral action, not numerical evidence, and remains valid for the dimension-dependent t_n approaching 1/sqrt(2) chosen in attempt 1.

## 3. Exact companion-integrality feasibility test

Let C be an integral nonsingular matrix whose columns are a proposed coefficient basis. Let S e_i=e_{i+1} for i<n-1 and S e_{n-1}=0. Every monic q of degree n gives multiplication matrix

    T=S+c e_{n-1}^T,

where c in Z^n is minus the vector of lower coefficients of q. Conversely every integer c gives a monic q. Ideal closure is therefore exactly

    C^{-1} T C is integral.

This suffices because closure under addition and integer scalars is built into the lattice, and closure under X then gives closure under every element of Z[X]/(q).

Put d=|det C| and J=d C^{-1}, an integral matrix. The criterion is the finite system of linear congruences

    J S C + (J c)(e_{n-1}^T C) = 0 mod d.

Vectorize the n-by-n matrices using one fixed entry order. Let M be the n^2-by-n integer matrix whose k-th column is vec(J e_k e_{n-1}^T C), and let r=-vec(J S C). Then an integer c exists exactly when

    r lies in the column lattice generated by [ M | d I_(n^2) ].

The accompanying exact program obtains a square column-HNF basis H of this lattice and tests whether H^{-1}r is integral. This is an exact yes/no criterion for some monic q, not merely a necessary test for one selected q. Uniformly scaling C does not change C^{-1}TC, so clearing rational denominators is harmless.

## 4. Cyclic additive quotient criterion

**Proposition.** A full-rank lattice L in Z^n with Z^n/L cyclic of order m can be a coefficient ideal for some monic polynomial q of degree n if and only if, for some integer a,

    L={ (v_0,...,v_{n-1}) : sum_j v_j a^j = 0 mod m }.

For a fixed q this additionally requires q(a)=0 mod m.

**Proof.** If L is an ideal, the quotient R/I is a unital ring whose additive group is cyclic of order m. The additive order of 1 equals m: if that order were r, then r times every ring element would vanish, whereas the exponent of a cyclic group of order m is m. Thus the integer multiples of 1 identify the ring with Z/m. The image of X is some a, proving the evaluation-kernel statement. Conversely evaluation at a modulo m is a surjective unital ring homomorphism from Z[X]/(q) exactly when q(a)=0 mod m, and its kernel is an ideal. For the “some q” version one may choose q=X^n-a^n.

This is not a statement about cyclic rotation of coordinates. In particular, the mere Smith-normal-form condition Z^n/L cyclic leaves the power-congruence constraints unproved.

## 5. Positive nontriangular example, completely checked

Let

    q=X^2-18X+300,
    R=Z[X]/(q),
    I=(300, X-18) in R.

Since q(18)=300, reduction/evaluation at 18 defines R -> Z/300, and I is its kernel. Its coefficient lattice has index 300. Consider the basis vectors

    b_1=(12,16),  b_2=(-6,17).

Their evaluations at 18 are both 300. Their determinant is 12*17-16*(-6)=300, so they generate exactly the coefficient lattice of I, not merely a sublattice. The polynomial q is irreducible over Q because its discriminant is -876<0. No canonical-embedding metric is used.

The Gram-Schmidt data are

    ||b_1||=20,
    mu_21=(b_1 dot b_2)/400=1/2,
    ||b_2^*||=15.

Lovasz is strict: 15^2+(1/4)20^2=325 > (3/4)20^2=300. Thus the basis is classically reduced. To determine lambda_1 exactly, write a lattice vector as z_1 b_1+z_2 b_2. Its squared norm is

    400(z_1+z_2/2)^2 +225 z_2^2.

If |z_2|>=2 this is at least 900. If z_2=0 and the vector is nonzero it is at least 400. If |z_2|=1 the minimum is 100+225=325. Therefore

    lambda_1=5 sqrt(13),
    ||b_1||/lambda_1=4/sqrt(13)>1.

This basis is obtained from the rational near-critical control basis with t=3/4 by an orthogonal rational coordinate transformation and uniform scaling. It demonstrates that the ideal-integrality test can succeed after a genuine nontriangular rotation. It does **not** give an unbounded-dimensional family, the target exponent, a cyclic-coordinate ideal, or an HNF-start lower bound.

## 6. Exact finite searches and their limitations

For each n=2,3,4, the tested set applies every permutation of the n physical coefficient coordinates to the recursive control basis, then one rational Givens rotation in every coordinate plane. The rotation triples are

    (a,b,c)=(1,0,1), (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17),

with two-by-two block [[a/c,b/c],[-b/c,a/c]]. Every resulting matrix is denominator-cleared and checked by the exact HNF congruence criterion of section 3. The counts include repeated matrices from the identity rotation.

At t=3/4, the tested counts were 8,72,576, respectively. There were 2 feasible presentations in n=2, zero in n=3, and zero in n=4. The quadratic example above comes from one of these hits.

At t=5/7, the same tested counts were 8,72,576. There was 1 feasible presentation in n=2 and zero in n=3 or n=4. The accompanying computation/check_attempt_2.py reproduces the t=5/7 test with exact rational and integer arithmetic.

These tests are not exhaustive over rational orthogonal transformations, ideal lattices, or reduced bases. A fixed t=3/4 or 5/7 would itself give a smaller exponent than 1/2; the finite checks probe algebraic membership only, not the asymptotic target. No probabilistic absence claim is inferred from the zero-hit sets.

## 7. Remaining mathematical gap

The reversed family is ruled out in all dimensions, while the rational-rotation route remains unresolved outside the finite tested subset. A successful general-q construction must produce integer companion action after a suitable nontriangular metric-preserving transformation or construct a reduced ideal basis directly. Evaluation kernels are an exact candidate class, but their powers-of-a congruences have not been reconciled here with a growing near-critical Gram-Schmidt profile.

Attempt 2 outcome: **additional infinite candidate family refuted; exact general membership test established; finite positive quadratic example established; growing-dimensional target still unresolved.**
