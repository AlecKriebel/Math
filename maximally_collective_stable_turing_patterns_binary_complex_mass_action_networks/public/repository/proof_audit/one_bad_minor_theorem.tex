\section*{A one-bad-minor diffusion-ray theorem}
Let $J\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ have one semisimple zero eigenvalue and be
Hurwitz on a complementary invariant subspace.  Assume every principal block
of order at most $n-2$ is Hurwitz, and among the signed principal minors of
order $n-1$ all are nonnegative except possibly one.  For $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)
\succ0$ put
\[
p_D(s)=\det(sD-J).
\]
The principal-minor expansion is
\[
p_D(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_k(D)s^k,
\quad
\beta_k(D)=\sum_{|I|=n-k}(-1)^{|I|}\det J_{I,I}
                 \prod_{j\notin I}d_j.
\]
(The $k=0$ term vanishes because $\det J=0$.)  Since a real Hurwitz matrix $M$
of order $r$ satisfies $(-1)^r\det M>0$, every summand defining $\beta_k$ is
strictly positive for $k\ge2$.  Hence
\[
p_D(s)=s q_D(s),\qquad
q_D(s)=\beta_1+\beta_2s+\cdots+\beta_ns^{n-1},
\]
and $q_D$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$.

Consequently a nonzero positive stationary threshold exists if and only if
$\beta_1(D)<0$.  In that case there is one and only one $s_*>0$ with
$p_D(s_*)=0$; it is a simple root, $p_D(s)<0$ for $0<s<s_*$, and
$p_D(s)>0$ for $s>s_*$.  Simplicity as a scalar root is also algebraic
simplicity of the zero eigenvalue of $J-s_*D$, because
$p_D(s)=\det(D)\det(sI-D^{-1}J)$.

The stationary real-eigenvalue band is exact.  Write
$a_I=(-1)^{|I|}\det J_{I,I}$.  For fixed $s>0$,
\[
\chi_s(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I+sD-J)
=\sum_I a_I\prod_{j\notin I}(\lambda+sd_j).
\]
Every $a_I$ with $|I|\le n-2$ is positive.  The only possibly negative terms
have $|I|=n-1$, and their total derivative contribution is
\[
\sum_{|I|=n-1}a_I=\beta_1(\mathbf 1)>0,
\]
because equal diagonal damping shifts the stable nonzero spectrum and the
conservation zero strictly left.  All remaining derivative contributions are
nonnegative, with positive constant contribution, so
$\chi_s'(\lambda)>0$ for every $\lambda\ge0$.  Therefore
\[
J-sD\text{ has a positive real eigenvalue}
\iff p_D(s)<0
\iff 0<s<s_*.
\]
The positive root is unique and simple.  At $s_*$ the zero crosses
transversely, and for $s>s_*$ no positive real eigenvalue exists.

This exact statement still does not exclude a nonreal unstable pair.  Wave
instability requires a separate spectral argument.  For the selected profile
below, an exact modulus certificate proves that $J-tD$ is fully Hurwitz for
every $t>1$.
