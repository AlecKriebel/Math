\section*{Patterned branch and exchange of stability}
On $(0,\pi)$ with Neumann boundary conditions and diffusion
$(1-\mu)D_m$, work in the fixed integrated-mass spaces
\[
X_c=\left\{u\in H_N^2:\int_0^\pi c^Tu\,d\xi=0\right\},
\qquad
Y_c=\left\{g\in L^2:\int_0^\pi c^Tg\,d\xi=0\right\}.
\]
The stationary operator is analytic, Fredholm of index zero, and at
$\mu=0$ has kernel $\operatorname{span}\{r\cos\xi\}$ and cokernel
$\operatorname{span}\{\ell\cos\xi\}$.  The transversality pairing is
$(\pi/2)\ell^TD_mr\ne0$.  The reflection
$(\mathcal Ru)(\xi)=u(\pi-\xi)$ preserves these fixed-mass Neumann spaces,
commutes with the semiflow, and sends $r\cos\xi$ to $-r\cos\xi$.
Consequently an equivariant one-dimensional center manifold has an odd
reduced vector field in the adjoint-projection amplitude.  The stationary
simple-eigenvalue theorem gives the local curve, and reflection exchanges its
two positive nonconstant halves,
\[
A_\pm(\mu)=\pm\sqrt{-\eta_m\mu/c_m}+O(\mu^{3/2})
\qquad(\mu>0).
\]
Since the base equilibrium is $\mathbf1$ and the branch perturbation tends to
zero in $H_N^2\hookrightarrow C^0$ for each fixed $m$, both
reflection-related equilibria remain componentwise strictly positive for
sufficiently small $\mu>0$.
The center eigenvalue on either branch is
\[
\eta_m\mu+3c_mA_\pm(\mu)^2+O(\mu^2)
=-2\eta_m\mu+O(\mu^2)<0.
\]
At onset, the homogeneous stoichiometric spectrum, the noncritical first-mode
spectrum, and every higher Neumann mode lie strictly in the left half-plane.
For each fixed $m$, positive diffusion sends the high-mode spectral bound to
$-\infty$, so the complementary spectrum has a positive gap.  Analytic
perturbation preserves this gap along the small branch.

The quadratic mass-action nonlinearity is smooth from the real fixed-mass
$H^1$ phase space to $L^2$ in one dimension, and the positive diagonal
Neumann diffusion operator is sectorial.  The fixed-mass restriction removes
the conservation zero and Neumann boundaries leave no continuous translation
mode.  The principle of linearized stability therefore gives local exponential
asymptotic stability of each branch in fixed-mass $H^1$.  No global basin or
dimension-uniform neighborhood is claimed.
