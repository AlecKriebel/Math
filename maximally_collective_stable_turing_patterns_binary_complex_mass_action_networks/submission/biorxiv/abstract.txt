Multicomponent Turing theories often seek a lower-dimensional unstable subsystem responsible for patterning. We construct, for every n>=4, an n-species classical mass-action topology whose source and product complexes contain at most two molecules and whose instability is maximally collective. Uniformly over every positive-equilibrium realization of the topology, every principal subsystem below order n-1 is Hurwitz, whereas one order-(n-1) subsystem has a positive real eigenvalue. We then derive an exact diffusion-design law: for any homogeneously stable realization, stationary destabilization along a positive diagonal diffusion ray occurs if and only if d_Z>8h_Z sum_{j=2}^{n-2} d_j/h_j. This yields sharp fixed-equilibrium contrast infima and the universal trade-off chi_D chi_H>8(n-3). At unit equilibrium we give a rational primary supercritical Turing realization with chi_D=23(91n-274)/63 and locally exponentially attracting positive patterns. A second exact family redistributes heterogeneity between diffusion and equilibrium concentrations; both contrasts are Theta(sqrt(n)), which matches the universal lower bound in exponent. The proof combines a complete positive-realization parametrization, an exhaustive strongly connected block classification, a one-bad-minor matrix theorem, conservation-compatible Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, and exact symbolic sign certificates. The networks are synthetic, and the bifurcation and stability conclusions are local for each fixed dimension.
