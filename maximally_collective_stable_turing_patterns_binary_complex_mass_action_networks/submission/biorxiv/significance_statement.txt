Structural accounts of Turing patterning often look for a small activator-inhibitor subsystem. This work shows that such localization can fail maximally in sparse classical mass-action networks: every subsystem below the largest proper order is stable for every positive realization of one reaction topology. The same topology nevertheless supports rigorously stable nonlinear concentration patterns. An exact diffusion law identifies which species-specific transport profiles can trigger the stationary instability and yields sharp lower bounds on required heterogeneity. An explicit family then distributes that cost between diffusion and equilibrium concentrations so that both grow only as the square root of system dimension, and this exponent is optimal. The construction is synthetic and does not assert that the reactions occur naturally, but it provides an exact benchmark for multicomponent reaction-network design and for the limitations of low-dimensional motif explanations.
