\section*{Conservation-compatible primary bifurcation}
Define
\[
F(u,\mu)=f_m(\mathbf1+u)+(1-\mu)D_mu''
\]
as an analytic map $X_c\times\mathbb R\to Y_c$.  Its derivative at the
origin is
\[
L_0=A_m+D_m\partial_{\xi\xi}.
\]
Neumann Fourier decomposition gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item on the homogeneous coefficient, $A_m:c^\perp\to c^\perp$ is invertible;
\item on mode one, $A_m-D_m$ has kernel $\operatorname{span}\{r_m\}$ and
      cokernel $\operatorname{span}\{\ell_m\}$;
\item on every mode $k\ge2$, $A_m-k^2D_m$ is invertible.
\end{itemize}
Therefore $L_0$ is Fredholm of index zero and
\[
\ker L_0=\operatorname{span}\{r_m\cos\xi\}.
\]
The transversality pairing is
\[
\int_0^\pi\ell_m^TD_mr_m\cos^2\xi\,d\xi
=\frac\pi2\ell_m^TD_mr_m\neq0.
\]
Crandall--Rabinowitz therefore gives a local curve of nonconstant steady
states in the fixed integrated mass class.  Because its leading term is
$s r_m\cos\xi$ and the base equilibrium is strictly positive, all
concentrations remain positive for sufficiently small $|s|$.
