# Literature and attribution review

Audit date: 2026-09-06 UTC. Target: commit `6f68ad3e795c239e452206c84ce4ce331386a094`.

The focused primary-source search did not reveal a prior result containing the manuscript's combined all-dimensional, classical mass-action, maximal-localization and nonlinear-stability construction. This is a reasoned novelty assessment within the sources examined, not a proof of universal priority. The paper's contribution should remain framed as that combination and its exact topology-specific trade-off, rather than the first connection between unstable subsystems and diffusion-driven instability.

| Prior work and primary source | Relevant overlap and distinction |
|---|---|
| [Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini (2000), author-hosted paper](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/maini/PKM%20publications/122.pdf) | General subsystem/activator criteria already allow an activator subsystem through order n−1. The endpoint bound itself is inherited background. The present paper supplies an explicit classical mass-action realization with binary complexes, all smaller principal subsystems Hurwitz across its complete positive-equilibrium realization family, and nonlinear stable branches. |
| [Anma, Sakamoto and Yoneda (2012)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kodaimath/35/2/35_215/_article/-char/en) | Connects unstable subsystems to stationary and wave instabilities in three-component systems. This does not establish the present arbitrary-dimensional topology and fixed-mass branch theorem. |
| [Villar-Sepúlveda and Champneys (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-023-01870-3) | Gives general conditions and constructive diffusion choices for linear Turing/wave instability. Its setup assumes a Hurwitz full kinetic Jacobian; here the full Jacobian has a conservation zero and stability is on the compatibility space. The present principal-minor ray result also specifies a unique stationary crossing and real-eigenvalue band. The [published correction](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-023-01884-x) fixes the article title, rather than correcting a mathematical theorem. |
| [Vassena and Stadler (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2023.0694) | Unstable cores are established organizing objects. The article's parameter-rich kinetic framework is not a blanket theorem for classical mass-action realizability. The present construction's complete mass-action family and all-spectrum localization are therefore material, provided their scope is retained. |
| [Haas and Goldstein (2021), authors' preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.04614v2) | Studies random reaction–diffusion Jacobians and diffusive thresholds, including finite-dimensional sampling. Its statistical behavior is not contradicted by an exact lower bound for this particular topology. Neither result should be generalized to the other's ensemble. |
| [Piskovsky, published 2025, authors' preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14682) | Necessary and sufficient three-species instability criteria provide relevant linear context, without duplicating the arbitrary-dimensional stable-branch construction. |
| [Villar-Sepúlveda, Champneys and Krause (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06860) | Designs reaction–cross-diffusion systems with Turing and wave instabilities. Cross-diffusion and general reaction design are different assumptions from the fixed classical mass-action topology with diagonal physical diffusion here. |
| [Paul et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-52591-0) | Mass-action binding/unbinding networks can already produce Turing patterns without imposed feedback. Their finite network enumeration and numerical pattern evidence make an unqualified “first mass-action Turing patterns” claim inappropriate. The manuscript instead proves an all-dimensional localization and local nonlinear stability theorem. |
| [Conradi, Mincheva and Uecker (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16049v1) | The cited preprint exists. It offers sufficient structural conditions in a monomially parameterized class and distinguishes finite-wavelength “genuine Turing” instability from long-wave or domain-mode instability. The present construction is a stationary diffusion-driven bifurcation on a fixed Neumann interval. Its unstable continuous-wavenumber band extends to zero, so it should not imply domain-independent wavelength selection. A one-sentence convention clarification would improve the comparison. |

Conradi–Mincheva–Uecker also numerically continues nonlinear patterned branches and reports stable segments in a binary-complex classical mass-action example (Section 4.3). The project's `literature/theorem_comparison.csv` therefore needs more accurate entries than the unqualified “no” values in its nonlinear/stable-branch columns. This correction does not erase the all-dimensional, all-realization and exact contrast-scaling distinctions. The separate [novelty crosscheck](../pde/NOVELTY_CROSSCHECK.md) records the evidence and also examines Waters' weakly nonlinear results and Vassena–Stadler's exact core notion.

The finite-domain terminology point is a scope clarification, not a counterexample to the actual fixed-interval theorem. No title change is demanded solely to enforce one literature convention. The manuscript already makes the conservation constraint and fixed interval visible; retain these prominently in the abstract and theorem statements.

For the nonlinear functional-analysis citations, see [the PDE referee report](../pde/PDE_REPORT.md): the application hypotheses were checked against the original Crandall–Rabinowitz theorem and directly against the operator, smoothness and spectral setting. Henry and Kato publisher metadata were checked; their entire books were not reread. Successful bibliographic metadata resolution is not evidence that every theorem in a cited work was audited.
