## Conclusion

The checked sources did not supply the manuscript's combined result: a binary-complex classical mass-action topology for every species dimension, maximal order-(n-1) principal-block localization throughout **all** positive realizations, an exact stationary diffusion law, rigorously stable local nonlinear branches, and simultaneous square-root growth of diffusion and equilibrium contrasts with a matching topology-specific exponent lower bound.

The defensible novelty claim remains this precise conjunction and its quantified construction. “Stable Turing patterns in mass-action networks,” “large unstable cores,” and “weakly nonlinear supercriticality” individually are established topics and should not be presented as new. Finding no collision in this bounded search is evidence of a plausible novelty distinction, not proof that no overlooked prior theorem exists.

## Closest requested sources

**Conradi--Mincheva--Uecker (2026).** Their [primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16049v1), Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 3.6, gives sufficient determinant-sign/domain-size conditions for spatial instability under monomial steady-state parameterization. Its nine-species phosphorylation example has binary complexes and classical mass action. Section 4 numerically continues nonlinear branches with fixed integrated-mass constraints; a primary subcritical branch stabilizes after a fold. This overlaps the network class and finite-domain setting, but supplies neither the all-dimensional realization-wide extremal principal-block theorem nor the contrast-scaling law. Remark 4.2 calls long-wave onset “Turing-like,” reserving “genuine Turing” for domain-independent finite-wavelength onset. Clarify the present manuscript's finite-interval convention.

**Vassena--Stadler (2024).** The [primary v3 text](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.11486v3) defines unstable cores through minimal unstable *Child-Selection matrices* extracted from stoichiometry (Definition 14); these are not simply principal species blocks of every realized Jacobian. Its principal sufficient-instability mechanism uses parameter-rich kinetic models. Section 3 explicitly explains why classical mass action lacks the independent derivative freedoms required, and why parameter-rich instability does not automatically transfer to classical mass action on the same network. The paper also contains classical mass-action examples, so it should not be described as excluding that kinetic class altogether. The displayed mass-action example (61) includes cubic reaction rates. In the inspected text I found no diffusion-driven nonlinear branch theorem or contrast-trade-off theorem. The connection is structural unstable-core theory; the new claim must be distinguished by the kinetic restrictions, realization quantifier, specific principal-block notion, and PDE conclusions.

**Waters--Yates--Dawes (2025).** The [publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2024.134427) treats two-species mass-action models with quadratic reaction terms in eleven minimal scheme classes. It explicitly gives weakly nonlinear stable supercritical patterns, subcritical regions, and two degenerate classes requiring quintic normal forms, with numerical validation. Therefore neither a quadratic mass-action pattern construction nor a cubic-sign calculation is novel alone. Its stated scope is two species, so it does not contain the claimed increasing-dimension localization/scaling conjunction. The abstract restricts interacting reactant count; this alone does not certify the manuscript's stronger restriction on both source and product complex sizes. The distinction concerning product molecularity should cite a checked reaction table if emphasized; this bounded run did not freshly inspect the complete 2025 table.
