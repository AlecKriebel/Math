\section*{Stable Pareto theorem}
For every integer $m\ge3$, write $\nu=m-2$ and let
\[
 \kappa_\nu=\begin{cases}
 1/\sqrt3,&\nu=1,\\
 \sqrt5/2,&\nu\ge2,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 L_0=\frac{\kappa_\nu}{\sqrt\nu},
 \qquad
 L_1=\frac{90\nu}{90\nu+1}.
\]
For every $L\in[L_0,L_1]$, the frozen binary-complex topology admits positive
rates, a positive equilibrium, and positive diagonal diffusion coefficients
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the homogeneous equilibrium is stable on its stoichiometric class;
\item the first Neumann mode has one simple transverse stationary zero and all
other eigenvalues stable;
\item all higher modes are stable;
\item the conservation-compatible cubic normal form has $\eta_m(L)>0$ and
$c_m(L)<0$;
\item the two nearby positive patterned equilibria are locally exponentially
asymptotically stable in the fixed-mass $H^1$ phase space;
\item the physical contrasts are
\[
 \chi_D(L)=\frac{23}{63}(91\nu L),
 \qquad
 \chi_H(L)=\frac{91\nu-1}{91\nu L}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently
\[
 \chi_D(L)\chi_H(L)=\frac{23(91\nu-1)}{63}
 =\frac{23(91m-183)}{63}=\chi_D^{\rm unit}(m),
\]
and at $L=L_0$ both contrasts are $\Theta(\sqrt{\nu})$.  Thus $L$ reallocates
a fixed contrast product between physical diffusion and equilibrium
concentration scales; it reduces $\max(\chi_D,\chi_H)$, not their product.
Since every stationary crossing in this topology satisfies
$\chi_D\chi_H>8\nu$, the simultaneous square-root exponents are optimal among
stationary-crossing realizations of $\widehat N_m$.


\section*{An exact equilibrium-scaling family}
Let $\nu=m-2$ and $K_i=91m-181-i$.  Retain the unit-flux effective reaction
operator $A_m$, its critical vector $r_m$, and the effective diffusion matrix
\[
 \Delta_m=\operatorname{diag}\left(
 \frac{23}{63},\frac1{K_2},\ldots,\frac1{K_{m-1}},
 \frac17,\frac{16}{45}\right).
\]
For a scalar $L>0$ define
\[
 \mathsf H_m(L)=\operatorname{diag}(h_1,\ldots,h_m,h_Z),
\]
with
\[
 h_1=h_m=h_Z=1,
 \qquad
 h_i=\frac{K_i}{L K_{i-1}}
 \quad(2\le i\le m-1),
\]
and take the physical diffusion matrix
\[
 D_m^{\rm phys}(L)=\mathsf H_m(L)\Delta_m.
\]
The positive equilibrium is $x^*=\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}\mathbf1$, and choosing each
rate constant as the reciprocal of its source monomial at $x^*$ realizes
unit reaction flux.  Hence every parameter is positive and the physical
Jacobian is $\mathsf H_m(L)A_m$ in normalized concentration coordinates.

Writing $\widehat x=\mathsf H_m(L)x$, the parameterized reaction--diffusion
equation becomes
\[
 \partial_t\widehat x=\mathsf H_m(L)
 \{f_m(\widehat x)+(1-\mu)D_m^{\rm eff}
 \partial_{\xi\xi}\widehat x\},
 \qquad D_m^{\rm eff}=\Delta_m.
\]
Thus, at $\mu=0$, the stationary bracket and its critical right vector are
unchanged by $\mathsf H_m(L)$.  The dynamic left critical vector is
\[
 \widetilde\ell_m(L)=\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}\ell_m.
\]
Its transversality numerator is independent of the equilibrium scaling:
\[
 \widetilde\ell_m(L)^T\mathsf H_m(L)\Delta_m r_m
 =\ell_m^T\Delta_m r_m<0.
\]
Since $\widetilde\ell_m(L)^Tr_m<0$, the quotient defining
$\eta_m(L)$ is positive.
The unit derivative identity gives
\[
 \ker[\mathsf H_m(L)(A_m-\Delta_m)]
 =\ker(A_m-\Delta_m)=\operatorname{span}\{r_m\}.
\]
If $\mathsf H_m(L)(A_m-\Delta_m)v=r_m$, multiplication by
$\widetilde\ell_m(L)^T$ would give
$0=\widetilde\ell_m(L)^Tr_m$, which is impossible.  Thus the row-scaled
critical zero is algebraically simple.
The fixed physical conservation class becomes the normalized constraint with
left vector $\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}c$.
Here $\chi_H$ measures equilibrium concentration-scale separation, not a
universal measure of biological expense or implausibility.


\section*{All-mode stability on the certified interval}
For a mode with scalar damping $t$, row scaling gives
\[
 \det(\lambda I-\mathsf H_m(L)(A_m-t\Delta_m))
 =\det \mathsf H_m(L)\,\{Q_m(\lambda,t)F(\lambda,t)-G(\lambda,t)\}.
\]
The fixed boundary factors are
\[
 g_1=\lambda+2+\frac{23}{63}t,
 \quad g_m=\lambda+5+\frac17t,
 \quad g_Z=\lambda+4+\frac{16}{45}t,
\]
\[
 F=g_1g_mg_Z-4g_1-4g_m+g_Z,
 \qquad G=g_Z(4g_1+g_m)-36.
\]
For $2\le i\le m-1$,
\[
 g_i=\frac{\lambda}{h_i}+1+\frac{t}{K_i}
 =\frac{K_{i-1}}{K_i}(1+L\lambda)+\frac{t-1}{K_i}.
\]
If $t\ge1$ and $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge0$, then
\[
 |Q_m|^2\ge\left(\frac{91}{90}\right)^2|1+L\lambda|^{2\nu}
 \ge\left(\frac{91}{90}\right)^2
 \left(1+2\nu Lx+\nu L^2y^2\right),
\]
where $x=\operatorname{Re}\lambda$ and $y=\operatorname{Im}\lambda$.
Throughout the certified interval $\nu L^2\ge1/3$, so this dominates the
lower bound with coefficient $1/3$ in $y^2$.

With $z=y^2$ and $s=t-1$, the exact 84-term source polynomial is
\[
 E_{84}(x,z,s;A)=\left(\frac{91}{90}\right)^2
 \left(1+A x+\frac13z\right)
 |F(x+i\sqrt z,1+s)|^2-|G(x+i\sqrt z,1+s)|^2,
 \qquad A>0,
\]
has 84 nonzero monomials in $(x,y^2,t-1)$.  Every coefficient is a
polynomial in $A$ with nonnegative rational coefficients.  Its coefficients
of $x$, $y^2$, and $t-1$ are strictly positive, so equality occurs only at
$(\lambda,t)=(0,1)$.  This proves a simple isolated first-mode zero and
Hurwitz stability for every $t>1$.

The homogeneous mode uses a different boundary polynomial.  At $t=0$,
\[
 \frac{\det(\lambda I-\mathsf H_m(L)A_m)}{
 \det\mathsf H_m(L)}=Q(\lambda)F_0(\lambda)-R(\lambda),
\]
where
\[
 Q=\prod_{i=2}^{m-1}
 \left(1+L\frac{K_{i-1}}{K_i}\lambda\right),
 \quad F_0=\lambda^3+11\lambda^2+31\lambda+16,
 \qquad R=5\lambda^2+33\lambda+16.
\]
For $x\ge0$, each factor in $Q$ has modulus at least $|1+L\lambda|$.
If $\nu\ge2$, Bernoulli's inequality and the definition of $L_0$ give
\[
 |Q|^2\ge |1+L\lambda|^{2\nu}
 \ge1+2\nu Lx+\nu L^2y^2
 \ge1+Ax+\frac54y^2,
 \qquad A=2\nu L.
\]
Here $A\ge\sqrt{5\nu}\ge\sqrt{10}>1/4$.
With $U=A-1/4$, the exact 22-term source polynomial is
\[
 E_{22}(x,z;U)=
 \left[1+\left(U+\frac14\right)x+\frac54z\right]
 |F_0(x+i\sqrt z)|^2-|R(x+i\sqrt z)|^2.
\]
It has 22 nonzero monomials.  Every coefficient is a
polynomial in $U$ with nonnegative rational coefficients, and equality occurs
only at $\lambda=0$.  Hence no nonzero closed-right-half-plane homogeneous
root exists.  The coefficient $5/4$ is sharp for this coefficientwise
certificate only: replacing it by $B$ makes the coefficient of $z=y^2$ at
$x=0$ equal to $256B-320$, which is zero at $B=5/4$ and negative below it.
This identifies the boundary of the present modulus certificate, not an
intrinsic dynamical boundary for $L_0$.  Also
\[
 (QF_0-R)'(0)
 =16L\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}\frac{K_{i-1}}{K_i}-2>0,
\]
because every ratio is greater than one and
$L\ge\sqrt5/(2\sqrt\nu)$.  Thus the conservation zero is simple.

For $\nu=1$, put $\gamma=91L/90$.  Then
\[
 \frac{QF_0-R}{\lambda}
 =\gamma\lambda^3+(1+11\gamma)\lambda^2
 +(6+31\gamma)\lambda+(16\gamma-2).
\]
Here $\gamma>1/8$, all coefficients are positive, and the cubic Routh--Hurwitz
difference is
\[
 (1+11\gamma)(6+31\gamma)-\gamma(16\gamma-2)
 =6+99\gamma+325\gamma^2>0.
\]
Thus the complementary homogeneous spectrum is Hurwitz in the exceptional
dimension as well.

The 22- and 84-term coefficient tables are in
\texttt{pareto\_all\_m\_certificate.json} and are regenerated independently.


\section*{Uniform supercritical sign along the scaling family}
Let
\[
 \mathfrak h_m=\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\frac1{K_j}.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac1{91}\le\mathfrak h_m<\frac1{90}.
\]
Let $w_0^{\rm ref}$ denote the reference zero-mode correction satisfying
$c^Tw_0^{\rm ref}=0$, and let $\rho_m$ generate $\ker A_m$.  The corrected
zero mode for the physical fixed-mass class is
\[
 w_0(L)=w_0^{\rm ref}+\tau_m(L)\rho_m,
\]
\[
 \tau_m(L)=-\frac{(\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}c)^Tw_0^{\rm ref}}
 {(\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}c)^T\rho_m}.
\]
The second harmonic is unchanged, because the stationary equation inside the
invertible row factor $\mathsf H_m(L)$ is unchanged.

With the inherited normalization of $r_m$ and $\ell_m$,
\[
 \ell_m^T\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}r_m
 =-\frac{485873}{924210}-\frac{11180}{1467}L(m-2)<0.
\]
The cubic numerator has the exact decomposition
\[
 N_m(L)=N_m^{\rm ref}+\tau_m(L)S_m,
\]
Put $\nu=m-2$.  Since $C_m<0$ and
$\mathfrak h_m\le\nu/(90\nu+1)$, exact clearing gives
\[
 N_m^{\rm ref}-\frac1{100}\ge\frac{P_{\rm ref}(\nu)}{D_{\rm ref}(\nu)},
\]
where
\begin{align*}
P_{\rm ref}={}&3790502986637265684840\nu^5
-974216530468600286489\nu^4-53103567440921218871\nu^3\\
&-576386186827093561\nu^2+3649732858601219\nu+55281268032918,
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
D_{\rm ref}={}&715296950550(8\nu-1)(90\nu+1)\\
&\times(589180301\nu^3+35065866\nu^2+629431\nu+3306).
\end{align*}
The denominator is positive.  After $\nu=v+1$, the descending coefficients
of $P_{\rm ref}$ are
\[
\begin{gathered}
3790502986637265684840,17978298402717728137711,
33955060177057334483573,\\
31899843595051464379292,14895188986348368035728,
2762610207555043720056,
\end{gathered}
\]
so $N_m^{\rm ref}>1/100$.

Separately,
\[
 S_m=-\frac{4(1760850\mathfrak h_m-10253)}{462105}.
\]
The bounds $1/91\le\mathfrak h_m<1/90$ and the exact comparisons
\[
 \frac{1760850}{91}-10253>0,
 \qquad
 \frac4{462105}\left(\frac{1760850}{90}-10253\right)<\frac1{10}
\]
prove $-1/10<S_m<0$.  For the gauge, define
\begin{align*}
A_\tau={}&1494249120\mathfrak h_mL\nu^2-69786990\mathfrak h_mL\nu
+108738630L\nu^2\\
&+1214388L\nu-8521L-125249670\nu^2+1031940\nu,
\end{align*}
\[
B_\tau=32760\mathfrak h_mL\nu+32760L\nu^2+4L-4095\nu,
\qquad
\tau_m(L)=-\frac{A_\tau}{15876(8\nu-1)B_\tau}.
\]
On $L\ge1/\sqrt{3\nu}$,
$B_\tau=4095\nu[8L(\nu+\mathfrak h_m)-1]+4L>0$ because
$L\nu\ge1/\sqrt3$, and
\[
\partial_{\mathfrak h}\tau_m
=-\frac{4225L\nu^2(182448L\nu+1008L-7513)}{2B_\tau^2}<0,
\]
\[
\partial_L\tau_m
=-\frac{65\nu(-61531470\mathfrak h_m\nu+125249670\nu^2
+1031940\nu-7513)}{252B_\tau^2}<0.
\]
Thus its maximum occurs at $\mathfrak h_m=1/91$ and
$L=1/\sqrt{3\nu}$.  With $y=\sqrt{3\nu}$, define
\[
P_\tau=-1040195520\nu^3+756272790\nu^2y-507201030\nu^2
-21412755\nu y-935658\nu+58481.
\]
Then
\[
\tau_m(1/91,1/y)-\frac1{20}
=-\frac{P_\tau}{79380(8\nu-1)
(-32760\nu^2+4095\nu y-360\nu-4)}.
\]
The denominator is negative because $y<2\nu$.
For $\nu=1$, $\sqrt3<7/4$ gives
$P_\tau<-1049074663/4<0$.  For $\nu\ge2$, $y\le5\nu/4$ gives
$P_\tau<P_{\rm up}$, where
\[
P_{\rm up}=-\frac{189709065}{2}\nu^3-507201030\nu^2-935658\nu+58481.

Since
\[
P_{\rm up}'=-\frac{569127195}{2}\nu^2-1014402060\nu-935658<0,
\qquad P_{\rm up}(2)=-2789453215<0,
\]
the endpoint, and hence the full range, satisfies $\tau_m(L)<1/20$.

If $\tau\le0$, then $\tau S_m\ge0$.  If $0<\tau<1/20$, then
$\tau S_m>-1/200$.  Therefore
\[
 N_m(L)>\frac1{200}>0.
\]
The normal-form coefficients are
\[
 \eta_m(L)=\frac{\ell_m^T\Delta_m r_m}
 {\ell_m^T\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}r_m}>0,
\]
\[
 c_m(L)=\frac{N_m(L)}{\ell_m^T\mathsf H_m(L)^{-1}r_m}<0.

Thus every member with $L\ge1/\sqrt{3\nu}$ is supercritical.  The certified
spectral interval below is a subset of this range: its lower endpoint agrees
with $1/\sqrt{3\nu}$ for $\nu=1$ and is larger for $\nu\ge2$.
The diagonal scaling commutes with spatial reflection
$\xi\mapsto\pi-\xi$; consequently the scaled reduced vector field remains
odd and reflection exchanges its two nonzero branches.


\section*{Certified stable Pareto curve}
Set
\[
 \kappa_\nu=\begin{cases}
 1/\sqrt3,&\nu=1,\\
 \sqrt5/2,&\nu\ge2,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 L_0(\nu)=\frac{\kappa_\nu}{\sqrt\nu},
 \qquad L_1(\nu)=\frac{90\nu}{90\nu+1}.
\]
The interval is nonempty for every $\nu\ge1$.  When $\nu=1$, the squared
inequality $L_0<L_1$ is $8281<24300$.  When $\nu\ge2$, it follows from
\[
 (90\nu+1)^2\le(91\nu)^2<6480\nu^3,
\]
which is the squared endpoint inequality.  For
$L\in[L_0,L_1]$, every interior equilibrium scaling satisfies $h_i\ge1$.
The largest is
\[
 h_2=\frac{91\nu-1}{91\nu L},
\]
while the boundary scalings equal one.  Thus
\[
 \chi_H(L)=\frac{91\nu-1}{91\nu L}.
\]
The physical diffusivities are
\[
 d_1=\frac{23}{63},
 \qquad d_i=\frac1{LK_{i-1}}
 \ (2\le i\le m-1),
 \qquad d_m=\frac17,
 \qquad d_Z=\frac{16}{45}.
\]
Their minimum is $d_2=1/(91\nu L)$ and their maximum is $d_1$, so
\[
 \chi_D(L)=\frac{23}{63}(91\nu L).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\chi_D(L)\chi_H(L)=\frac{23(91\nu-1)}{63}
 =\chi_D^{\rm unit}(m)}
\]
throughout the certified stable curve.  Increasing $L$ increases diffusion
contrast and decreases equilibrium contrast, reallocating a fixed product.
Thus no two points of this curve dominate one another within the curve.

At the certified square-root-scaling endpoint $L=L_0$,
\[
 \chi_D=\frac{2093}{63}\kappa_\nu\sqrt{\nu},
 \qquad
 \chi_H=\frac{\sqrt\nu}{\kappa_\nu}\frac{91\nu-1}{91\nu}.
\]
Hence both contrasts are $\Theta(\sqrt m)$.

For every stationary crossing of $\widehat N_m$, the exact diffusion law gives
the strict topology-wide trade-off
\[
 \chi_D\chi_H>8\nu.
\]
In particular
\[
 \max(\chi_D,\chi_H)>\sqrt{8\nu}.
\]
The construction therefore attains the optimal square-root exponent in both
coordinates among stationary-crossing realizations of this topology.  The
complete constant-optimal diffusion--equilibrium Pareto frontier is not
determined: the constructed product has asymptotic coefficient $2093/63$,
while the topology-specific stationary lower coefficient is $8$.
