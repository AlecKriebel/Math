 8                                                Abstract
 9            Multicomponent Turing theories often seek a lower-dimensional unstable subsystem
10        responsible for patterning. For every n ≥ 4, we construct an n-species binary-complex
11        classical mass-action topology for which, throughout every positive-equilibrium realiza-
12        tion, every principal subsystem below order n − 1 is Hurwitz while one order-(n − 1)
13        subsystem is unstable. We prove a general principal-minor diffusion-ray theorem and
14        obtain an exact network law: for any homogeneously stable realization, a stationary
                                                                                         Pn−2 cross-
15        ing occurs along a positive diagonal diffusion ray exactly when dZ > 8hZ j=2 dj /hj .
16        This gives the nonattained fixed-equilibrium contrast infimum, the unit-equilibrium
17        value 8(n − 3), and the topology-wide stationary trade-off χD χH > 8(n − 3). At unit
18        equilibrium, an exact rational design undergoes a primary supercritical bifurcation with
19        locally exponentially stable positive patterns in the fixed-integrated-mass class. A second
20        exact family redistributes heterogeneity
                                         √          between diffusion and equilibrium concentrations
21        so that both contrasts are Θ( n), matching the topology-specific stationary minimax
22        lower bound in exponent. The proof uses explicit flux-cone design, exhaustive strongly
23        connected block classification, conservation-compatible center-manifold reduction, and
24        inspectable symbolic certificates. The networks are synthetic; the bifurcation, stability,
25        and robustness conclusions are local for each fixed dimension, and the stationary diffusion
26        law does not classify arbitrary wave instability.

27   Keywords: reaction–diffusion systems; Turing instability; mass-action kinetics; reaction
28   networks; bifurcation; diffusion design.

31   1    Introduction
32   Turing’s diffusion-driven instability mechanism shows how a spatially homogeneous equilib-
33   rium can be stable in a well-mixed system and unstable to a nonconstant spatial mode after
34   species-dependent diffusion is introduced [16]. For systems with many components, a central
35   structural question is whether the instability can always be attributed to a smaller unstable
36   subsystem. General matrix theories allow responsible principal subsystems of orders up to
37   n−1 and distinguish stationary from wave instabilities [14, 1, 19, 18]. Random-matrix studies
38   also find that diffusion thresholds often decrease with component count and that many sam-
39   pled instabilities resist reduction to fewer diffusing species [5]. Reaction-network approaches
40   instead constrain the Jacobian by stoichiometry, positive steady fluxes, and mass-action
41   derivatives [11, 4, 15, 17]. Here “Turing bifurcation” means a stationary diffusion-driven
42   crossing of a nonconstant Neumann mode on a fixed interval. We do not claim intrinsic
43   finite-wavelength selection under enlargement of the spatial domain.
44       A large unstable block in one numerical Jacobian does not settle the reaction-topology
45   question. Reparameterizing the same network can change its Jacobian and can expose a
46   smaller unstable subsystem. Our first result closes this quantifier exactly: for one explicit
47   indexed topology, every positive rate/equilibrium realization has all principal blocks below
48   order n − 1 Hurwitz. Thus the upper endpoint n − 1 in principal-subsystem localization
49   cannot be lowered even within a highly restricted classical mass-action class.
50       The second question is quantitative. Differential diffusion is often presented as an
51   empirical tuning parameter, whereas the present topology admits a complete exact design
52   law. A single signed order-(n − 1) minor is negative, and the remaining omission minors are
53   positive or zero. This network-specific one-bad-minor structure reduces stationary diffusion
54   design to one strict linear inequality and yields sharp heterogeneity lower bounds. We
55   further show that the same topology supports primary supercritical bifurcations and locally
56   exponentially stable patterns with substantially reduced diffusion contrast, as well as a
57   stable diffusion–equilibrium trade-off whose two contrasts grow only as the square root of
58   dimension.
59       Linear Turing instability alone does not guarantee a persistent nonlinear pattern [9]. We
60   therefore prove the primary crossing, the conservation-compatible branch, the exact cubic
61   sign, exchange of stability, and local semilinear stability separately. Related weakly nonlinear
62   analyses for minimal two-species schemes demonstrate the importance of this additional
63   layer [21, 22].

75   The topology was deliberately designed so that its positive-flux image and induced strongly
76   connected blocks are explicit; no black-box real-algebraic elimination is used.

77   Scope. The network has one semipositive conservation law: the conserved functional
78   does not contain X1 . Accordingly we call the system stoichiometric-codimension one, not
79   conservative or mass conserving. The construction is synthetic and not proposed as a natural
80   biochemical mechanism. All species diffuse with positive diagonal coefficients. The pattern,
81   stability, and robustness statements are local for each fixed m; no arbitrary-data global
82   existence, global attraction, dimension-uniform basin, cross-diffusion, or immobile-species
83   theorem is claimed.

101   For a real square matrix M , write α(M ) = max{ℜλ : λ ∈ σ(M )}.

102   Lemma 3.1 (Exhaustive strongly connected blocks). Let I be a principal species set with
103   0 < |I| < m. Every strongly connected component of the induced Jacobian graph on I is
104   either a singleton, whose corresponding diagonal entry is negative, or one of the following
105   non-singleton blocks:

106     1. the complete long cycle X1 , . . . , Xm−1 ;

107     2. the complete long cycle X2 , . . . , Xm ;

108     3. a non-singleton principal subgraph of the boundary triad {X1 , Xm , Z}.

109   After a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns, every principal matrix with 0 <
110   |I| < m is block upper triangular with diagonal blocks of these forms. On the hypersurface
111   b = 2a, the edge X1 → Xm is absent; this can only split components and hence refines the
112   same classification.

                                                         5
113   Proof. Use the directed convention Xj → Xi when the (i, j) Jacobian entry is nonzero. For
114   m = 3, inspect the principal sets of order at most two directly. The sets {X1 , X2 } and
115   {X2 , X3 } are the two complete long cycles; every other non-singleton strongly connected
116   component is a principal subgraph of {X1 , X3 , Z}. Hence assume m ≥ 4 below. Along the
117   interior chain the only off-diagonal arrows are Xi → Xi+1 for 2 ≤ i ≤ m − 2. Thus a retained
118   proper chain segment cannot close by itself.
119       A component containing an interior chain arrow can close in only two ways. If it
120   reaches Xm−1 and returns through Xm−1 → X1 , then strong connectivity forces every
121   vertex X1 , . . . , Xm−1 : the only path from X1 back through the chain successively uses
122   X2 , . . . , Xm−1 . This is the first long cycle. If it reaches Xm and returns through Xm → X2 ,
123   then strong connectivity forces every vertex X2 , . . . , Xm ; this is the second long cycle. The
124   other potential chain closure X1 → X2 → · · · → Xm → X1 already uses m vertices and is
125   excluded by |I| < m.
126       Each complete long cycle has m − 1 vertices. Adding any further boundary vertex
127   therefore gives at least m retained vertices, again excluded. If no complete long cycle occurs,
128   every feedback path involving no interior chain lies entirely inside {X1 , Xm , Z}: the arrows
129   among those vertices are the only remaining off-diagonal boundary arrows. Every retained
130   chain vertex outside such a feedback component has no return path and hence forms a
131   singleton diagonal block with negative diagonal entry. Frobenius permutation gives the
132   asserted block-triangular form. When b = 2a, the sole parameter-dependent arrow X1 → Xm
133   disappears. This arrow belongs to neither complete long cycle, so both long-cycle blocks are
134   unchanged. Within the boundary graph, deleting an arrow can only split strongly connected
135   components and cannot merge them, so no new case arises.

289   10     Relation to prior work and limitations
290   The possibility of order-(n − 1) unstable (activator) subsystems for general fixed matrices
291   is established in multicomponent Turing theory [14]; that fixed-matrix possibility is not
292   claimed as new. Anma, Sakamoto, and Yoneda give complementary three-component
293   unstable-subsystem analysis [1]. The present distinction is the conjunction of one indexed
294   binary-complex classical mass-action topology, every positive rate and equilibrium realization,
295   all-spectrum stability of every smaller principal block, exact diffusion design, and stable
296   nonlinear patterned branches in every dimension.
297       Haas and Goldstein quantified diffusion thresholds statistically for random Jacobians
298   through six components and found that many sampled instabilities cannot be reduced
299   to fewer diffusing species [5]. Here the scope is deterministic and complementary: one
300   constrained mass-action topology is treated exactly in every dimension, with all positive
301   realizations quantified and nonlinear branch stability proved.
302       Mincheva and Craciun’s principal projected-network injectivity condition is a different
303   reaction-network notion and is not identified with principal-block Hurwitz localization
304   [11]. Interaction-topology and atlas studies identify broad structural features and finite
305   collections of Turing-capable networks [10, 4, 15]; the present result instead gives one exact
306   infinite topology family and a theorem uniform over all positive realizations. Marcon and
307   collaborators showed that some architectures can pattern with equally diffusing signals
308   under their modeling assumptions [10]. For the present topology, equal diagonal diffusion
309   cannot destabilize: it only shifts the homogeneous spectrum left. Instead, the required
310   stationary heterogeneity is quantified exactly by (31); at unit equilibrium its contrast floor
311   is 8(m − 2), and with equilibrium scaling the topology-specific stationary heterogeneity
312   requirement is (35). This is a complementary, topology-specific quantitative result about
313   differential-diffusion tuning, not a statement about all binary-complex networks. Parameter-
314   rich unstable cores allow derivative freedoms absent from classical mass action [17]. Exact
315   necessary-and-sufficient linear criteria for Turing and Turing–Hopf instabilities in general
316   three-species systems address a different fixed-dimension scope [13]. Complementarily,
317   Villar-Sepúlveda, Champneys, and Krause design Turing and wave instabilities of prescribed
318   wavelength by choosing a generally non-diagonal diffusion tensor at fixed linearized kinetics,
319   or conversely choosing linearized kinetics at fixed transport [20]; here diffusion is positive
320   diagonal self-diffusion, and the Jacobians arise from—and are quantified over—all positive-
321   equilibrium realizations of one indexed classical mass-action topology. Biochemical screens
322   study interpretable reaction architectures rather than the synthetic extremal design here
323   [12]. Conradi, Mincheva, and Uecker give sufficient spatial instability conditions for a
324   restricted mass-action class and numerically continue nonlinear patterned branches, including
325   numerically stable segments in a finite example [2]. Their work does not supply the present
326   all-dimensional realization-wide localization theorem or exact contrast law.
327       The construction has one semipositive conservation law and an inflow; X1 is not bounded
328   by the conserved functional. The local stability theorem does not require an arbitrary-
329   data global boundedness theorem. All diffusivities are positive and diagonal. We do not

333   11      Discussion
334   The family is designed around two coupled positive-flux circuits. Their explicit two-parameter
335   positive-flux factor Am (a, b), together with arbitrary positive right-diagonal equilibrium
336   scaling H, makes the topology-wide quantifier tractable. The long chain forces every unstable
337   principal subsystem to contain almost all species, while the boundary triad remains Hurwitz
338   under arbitrary positive equilibrium scaling. The unique negative order-(n − 1) omission
339   minor is the one omitting Z, which collapses stationary diffusion design to one coefficient.
340   Finally, equilibrium scaling redistributes the necessary heterogeneity between concentrations
341   and physical diffusion without changing the effective critical bracket or losing the negative
342   cubic sign. Along the certified scaled family, this is a contrast allocation rather than a product
343   reduction: χD (L)χH (L) = χunitD (m) is fixed. Within the certified interval, χD (L) > χH√     (L),
344   and their maximum is therefore minimized at the lower endpoint L0 , where it is Θ( m)
345   rather than the unit-profile Θ(m).
346       The resulting conclusion is both structural and quantitative. The largest proper unstable-
347   subsystem order is unavoidable within this mass-action class; the exact diffusion contrast is
348   computable; stable positive patterns occur; and diffusion and equilibrium contrasts can be
349   controlled simultaneously at the stationary-optimal square-root exponent for this topology.
350   The remaining constant gap is explicit rather than hidden and provides a focused nonlinear
351   design problem.
