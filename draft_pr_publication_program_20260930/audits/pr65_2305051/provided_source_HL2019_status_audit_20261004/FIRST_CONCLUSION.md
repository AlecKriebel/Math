Saved 2026-10-04, after direct inspection of the frozen 2019 source and the literal candidate, before reading any ROOT, prior-review, or sibling-review opinion body.

The strongest presently supported historical conclusion is adverse to a claim of a newly solved open explicit-construction problem. In the Springer 2019 book, Problem 5.51 is printed on p.121 (PDF p.127) and explicitly assumes existence before asking for a construction. Its update spans printed pp.121-122 (PDF pp.127-128), attributes a stronger small-hyperbolic-derivative inner-function construction to Aleksandrov, Anderson, and Nicolau [21], and explicitly says that this inner function is constructed explicitly. This wording was verified against rendered page images, not solely extracted text.

The update does not say "no progress" or "open" for 5.51. The adjacent 5.52 update does say that no progress was reported; transferring that status to 5.51 would be a source error. The statement of an old problem under "Progress on Previous Problems" is not itself an open-status assertion.

The book does not define "explicit" as a closed-form zero list, as a computable compact-uniform approximation algorithm, or as an algorithm with a specified modulus. The candidate transparently adopts the last form of explicitness and admits that it does not give a short closed-form zero list. Such an algorithm may be a valuable particular construction if analytically correct; its claimed novelty or fulfillment of a stronger intended explicitness requirement does not follow from this source.
