# PR65 supplied-source audit: explicit real-variable mechanism

## Finding and scope

The deterministic absorbed four-adic unit-step mechanism is already printed in Piranian (1966), pp. 260--261, with explicit exclusion of finite nonzero derivatives. The companion Duren--Shapiro--Shields paper (1966), pp. 248--250, prints the periodic Zygmund/Herglotz derivative equivalence used to obtain Bloch functions. The submitted neighbor-directed ordering is a different arrangement of this old mechanism; its arrangement is not needed to obtain the target or the effective compact approximation property. These supplied sources therefore materially strengthen the earlier classical-method attribution and defeat a claim that PR65 introduces this measure mechanism.

Neither complete 1966 paper prints a normalized pure Blaschke product B whose Cayley transform is Bloch as an answer to Holland. The distinction is essential: the old premises imply that target after a further classical analytic argument, but finding premises that suffice does not prove that the complete application had already been printed. Priority of the final articulation remains unestablished. No conclusion here is based on a failure to find a source in a search.

## What Piranian actually prescribes

Piranian's introduction, p. 255, states the purpose of providing explicit descriptions of singular monotonic Zygmund functions for the companion Duren--Shapiro--Shields application. Theorem 1 states strict increase and derivative, wherever it exists, equal to zero or infinity. Two extensions are given: f(x+1)=f(x)+1, and a constant extension outside [0,1]. Both preserve a global Zygmund bound.

The first mechanism, pp. 256--260, is piecewise cubic. Start with f_0(x)=x and g(x)=-x(2x-1)(x-1). On a cell of a subdivision into n_i equal intervals, put f_i(x)=f_(i-1)(x)+a_(i,k)g(n_i x-k), where a_(i,k) is the greatest coefficient at most 1/n_i preserving increase on the cell. n_i is a proper multiple of n_(i-1). The required growth is specified qualitatively as sufficiently rapid, with additional oscillation and curvature requirements in the proof; the printed construction does not choose a canonical integer sequence n_i. In particular, p. 259 requires the oscillation of f'_(i-1) on each fine cell to be less than 2^(-i-1). The second-difference proof treats subdivision crossings explicitly on p. 258 and gives the global bound less than 23h on p. 259. The no-finite-positive-derivative proof spans pp. 259--260. This is a separate earlier explicit construction in a conventional mathematical sense, but it is not the fully fixed four-adic algorithm of PR65.

The decisive second mechanism is identified as Kahane's construction on p. 260. It starts with integer step height phi_0=1 on [0,1]. At stage i, divide into 4^i equal cells. A zero parent stays zero. A positive parent of height a has two outer quarters at a-1 and the central half at a+1. In list notation this is exactly

    w^(0)=(1);  a>0 -> (a-1,a+1,a+1,a-1);  0 -> (0,0,0,0).

No free frequency, small parameter, unspecified search, or existence-selected measure appears in this rule. Piranian defines the limiting distribution by f(x)=lim_i integral_0^x phi_i(t)dt (p. 260). On p. 261 he argues that a surviving positive path prevents a finite ordinary derivative, while zero intervals and their endpoints give derivative zero. His stated consequence is that the derivative has no finite value other than zero. He states the Zygmund property and omits its proof as essentially the same as the earlier second-difference proof. Theorem 2 says the derivative vanishes on an open set of full Lebesgue measure. The independent proof below supplies the omitted global circular estimate and checks all density boundary cases.

The modifications on pp. 261--262 are distinct. A low-height multiplicative adjustment gives a strictly increasing example; replacing the cubic amplitude cap by eta_i/n_i with eta_i tending sufficiently slowly to zero and rounding corners gives smooth examples. Piranian explicitly warns on p. 261 that these smooth modifications can acquire finite positive derivatives, so the earlier derivative exclusion does not survive automatically. This audit uses the fixed absorbed unit-step example, not those modifications.

## What Duren--Shapiro--Shields actually proves

The paper addresses rectifiable Jordan domains not of Smirnov type, not the requested Blaschke-product problem. Its p. 247 application is a derivative represented by exp(-aF), a singular inner expression when the underlying measure is positive singular. That exponential must not be confused with the Cayley transform (F-1)/(F+1).

On p. 248, for a bounded-variation function U on [0,2pi], the authors define V(t)=U(t)+Kt, with K=(U(0)-U(2pi))/(2pi), then extend V periodically. Membership U in their Zygmund class means membership of this periodic V. Thus the affine correction and periodic seam normalization are printed explicitly. Their Theorem 1 defines F(z)=integral (exp(it)+z)/(exp(it)-z) dU(t). On p. 249, equation (7) is F'(z)=O((1-r)^(-1)), uniformly as r approaches one. On pp. 249--250 they prove it equivalent to the periodic Zygmund condition, using Zygmund's analytic theorem and the integration-by-parts identities (10)--(11). Multiplying this estimate by 1-r^2 gives the modern Bloch seminorm bound. The same equivalence is repeated on p. 252 before the Fourier-coefficient tests.

Normalization is harmless but should be explicit. For the old mass-one construction let U(t)=f(t/(2pi)) on [0,2pi]. Then K=-1/(2pi), V(t)=f(t/(2pi))-t/(2pi), and the Herglotz integral is exactly the mass-one transform in normalized coordinates used in PR65. There is no change of total mass or hidden selection of a good Cayley shift.

## Independent all-stage verification of the old fixed rule

Write I_(n,j)=[j4^(-n),(j+1)4^(-n)), with a periodic lift. Let q_n equal the height w^(n)_j on I_(n,j). A positive parent has two upward and two downward children, hence the child sum is four times the parent height; zero is absorbing. Induction gives 0<=q_n<=n+1, integral q_n dx=1, and conditional mean q_(n+1)=q_n on each parent. Put mu(I_(n,j))=4^(-n)w^(n)_j.

The consistent masses determine an atomless probability measure. One may take a weak limit of q_n dx. A small open neighborhood of any point, including a grid endpoint, fits in at most two generation-n cells; its mass in every later stage is at most 2(n+1)4^(-n). The weak-limit open-set inequality gives the same bound, which tends to zero with n. Thus the limit has no atoms. All cell boundaries are continuity sets, so consistency fixes their masses for every weak limit, and these generating intervals determine a unique measure. This argument avoids assuming cell-mass identities before verifying atomlessness.

Under dx, the nested-cell heights form the simple symmetric walk started at one and absorbed at zero. For any integer M>1 the walk exits {1,...,M-1} almost surely: each successive block of M independent child digits gives probability at least 2^(-M) of sufficiently many downward choices. The stopped height is bounded by M and has initial mean one, so the probability of first reaching M is 1/M. Never reaching zero therefore has probability at most 1/M for every M, hence zero. The union of all zero cells has full dx measure and zero mu measure. This proves singularity; it is not an inference from unverified uniform integrability.

The same-level circular neighbor bound is two. Inside a positive parent its children differ by zero or two. Across positive neighboring parents a,b, the facing children are a-1 and b-1, so their difference equals a-b. If a=0 and 0<b<=2, the facing values are 0 and b-1, differing by at most one; the opposite case is identical. Two zero parents remain zero. This proves induction from the one-cell list. At generation one the first and last quarters have height zero, and all their descendants stay zero. Consequently f is constant near both ends of [0,1]; H=f-x has slope -1 in these two neighborhoods and its period-one extension has no seam defect. This supplies more than an informal instruction to repeat an arbitrary interval example periodically.

For a quantitative global proof define H_n(x)=integral_0^x(q_n(t)-1)dt and extend periodically. Each increment has zero integral in an old cell and absolute slope at most one, so ||H_(n+1)-H_n||_infinity<=4^(-n). Consequently H_n converges uniformly to H, with ||H-H_n||<= (4/3)4^(-n), ||H||<=4/3, and dH=dmu-dx. For 0<h<=1/4 choose ell=4^(-n)<=h<4ell. The interval (x-h,x+h) meets at most nine consecutive cells. The neighbor bound gives slope range at most 16, and hence the looser bound 18h for the second difference of H_n. The tail contributes at most (16/3)ell<=(16/3)h. Thus |H(x+h)+H(x-h)-2H(x)|<=24h. For h>1/4, the bound 4||H||<=16/3<24h completes the real-line estimate. This proof works across every grid boundary and the seam. DSS's printed criterion now gives a Bloch mass-one Herglotz transform.

At every point x, the prescribed nested half-open cell averages are the exact heights q_n(x). They either become zero forever or change by magnitude one at every later step. They cannot converge to a finite positive number L. For a non-grid point, ordinary density L, meaning the limit over all intervals (s,t) with s<x<t shrinking to x, would force these cell averages to converge to L. At a grid endpoint the half-open cell is one-sided, which requires a separate check: compare (x,x+h) with (x-h^2,x+h). Ordinary density L gives mass L(h+h^2)+o(h) for the latter. The extra portion is bounded by the centered interval (x-h^2,x+h^2), whose mass is O(h^2), by positivity. Thus the right-hand average tends to L; the same argument gives the left-hand average. Atomlessness removes inclusion conventions. Using a periodic lift proves the same statement at x=0. Therefore mu has no finite positive ordinary density anywhere.

This all-point statement is stronger than a.e. singularity and is the exact real-variable premise of the usual purity proof: a nonzero singular factor of B forces B to tend nontangentially to zero on a nonempty set of positive factor-measure; then F tends to one. The positive-measure converse-Fatou theorem would give ordinary density one there, contradicting the proved premise. This implication is conditional here on the exact analytic theorem, not on a presumed radial-to-ordinary converse. The separate analytic-family audit must verify that theorem and the disk normalization. The real-variable mechanism does not leave an endpoint or seam exception for that analytic argument.

## Effectivity and comparison with PR65

The old fixed rule computes all cell masses as nonnegative rational numbers with denominator 4^n. Move each mass to the specified cell midpoint exp(2pi i(j+1/2)4^(-n)). Its Herglotz transform F_n is a finite algebraic rational formula. Since the angular derivative of its kernel has absolute value at most 4pi r/(1-r)^2 on |z|<=r, moving mass by at most 4^(-n)/2 gives

    sup_|z|<=r |F-F_n| <= 2pi r (1-r)^(-2) 4^(-n).

Positive real parts give |F+1|,|F_n+1|>=1; the exact Cayley identity gives

    sup_|z|<=r |B-B_n| <= 4pi r (1-r)^(-2) 4^(-n).

Thus an effective recursive construction with the same compact modulus follows from the printed old rule. The paper does not itself print these midpoint Cayley stages or this numerical error bound. They are elementary deductions from its specified masses. Each atomic Cayley stage is rational inner and hence finite Blaschke; purity of its compact limit still requires the separate density/factorization argument. These stages do not give a short closed-form list of the limit's zeros, and compact convergence of finite Blaschke products alone never certifies purity.

The fixed first-stage list is (0,2,2,0). PR65's is (2,0,2,0), which is not a circular rotation or reflection of it. In particular the first-quarter limiting masses are zero and 1/2 respectively, so the measures are not identical. The old first midpoint Cayley stage is -z(z+1/sqrt(2))/(1+z/sqrt(2)); the submitted first stage is -i z^2. At higher generations PR65 consults neighboring heights and fixes asymmetric ties and a middle-pair ordering. It therefore differs from the literal fixed rule. Nevertheless both have the same absorbed-walk transition law under dx, the same mass consistency and height bounds, all-point obstruction, and a circular neighbor bound of two. Those are all the real-variable properties used by the target proof. The evidence supports 'modified deterministic ordering of an old mechanism', not 'new measure mechanism', and it does not identify a new mathematical benefit of the ordering.

The independent diagnostic in check_recursions.py checked both families through generation nine, 225 bounded local child cases and 1,586 bounded facing-edge cases. CHECKS.json preserves bounded summaries and stage-array hashes. It confirms mass, nonnegative heights, absorption, child increments, circular neighbor jumps, the old seam zero quarters, equality of height distributions, and the first-stage distinction. These computations could have falsified coding or transcription mistakes; they do not establish the universal conclusions, which have the proofs above, or historical priority.
