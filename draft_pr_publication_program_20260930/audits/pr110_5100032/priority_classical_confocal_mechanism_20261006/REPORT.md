## Scope and novelty hypothesis

The claim concerns two outer foci, two separate antipedal polygons, and equality of **sums of ordinary nonnegative Euclidean distances** from each focus to its own antipedal vertices. Vertices are intersections of supporting lines through consecutive outer-ellipse vertices perpendicular to their focal radii. The outer ellipse has a>b>0 and a fixed strictly nested confocal elliptic caustic has 0<lambda<b². The claim covers every regular closed billiard/Poncelet orbit, including admitted star windings, reversals and repetitions. It does not claim an individual sum is phase independent, nor a hyperbolic-caustic extension.

The mathematical gate already verifies the edge identity qplus-qminus=Gamma*(yB-yA), with Gamma fixed by the two ellipses. My question was whether an earlier **conclusion**, rather than familiar ingredients or proof technique, already entails the complete focal antipedal sum equality.

The centrally symmetric case already follows by pairing opposite edges: central inversion exchanges the two foci and their antipedal vertices and preserves ordinary distances. Such a proof does not require a new antipedal theorem. For a primitive even elliptic orbit the usual opposite-vertex symmetry gives this pairing; its repetitions inherit equality. An even-length list made by repeating a primitive odd orbit need not be centrally symmetric. The substantive unresolved component is therefore the general odd primitive orbit (and consequent repetitions), rather than the centrally symmetric special case. Stachel's primary2021 discussion on p100 distinguishes the even-period/odd-winding symmetry. [Primary article](https://www.geometrie.tuwien.ac.at/stachel/202_billiards_III_JGG.pdf). I inspected that exact full discussion in the primary-source web rendering; I do not claim a complete read of its remaining22-page paper.

## Primary corpus and transfers examined

**Chasles1828.** I read the entire original pp269–276 of “Géométrie pure. Théorèmes sur les sections coniques confocales” and its contemporaneous erratum, pp386–387. Sections4–5 concern the reciprocal polar of a circle and its focal conic; sections13–16 treat angle-bisectors, directrices and the usual two-focus sum/difference of radii of a point on a conic. These concern one conic point or the conic's tangent lines. They do not concern radial distances to the two *different* antipedal intersections associated with a billiard chord, or their cyclic sums. The erratum corrects the one-focus orthogonality wording on p275 to **both** foci; I visually checked it. I do not propagate the misprint. [Original](https://www.numdam.org/item/AMPA_1827-1828__18__269_1/), [erratum](https://www.numdam.org/item/AMPA_1827-1828__18__386_0/).

**Graves/string construction and Ivory.** I read the complete planar arguments in Izmestiev–Tabachnikov's primary author preprint arXiv1610.01384v2, §§2.1–2.2, and the complete relevant Stäckel/Poncelet/string discussion §§3.3.4–3.3.5. Graves produces confocal billiard boundaries from a string on a caustic. Theorem1 establishes four-reflection porism/equal side perimeter and hence equal diagonals in a curvilinear confocal quadrilateral. Theorem2 establishes a circle inscribed in a tangent quadrilateral. None identifies its diagonal or perimeter with a focal antipedal radial norm, and none asserts the difference of the two sums in k603. Constancy of a usual side perimeter along a family is also different from equality of two radial sums on each orbit. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01384v2); the shorter published version is DOI10.1093/integr/xyx006, not the actual 51-page preprint read here.

**Darboux1879.** I read the entire pp64–72 of “Sur les polygones circonscriptibles à un cercle.” It corrects the naive opposite-side Pitot statement when contacts occur on extensions, and studies flexible circumscribed even polygons and their incircle centers. Its alternating side sums, sign choices and circle tangencies do not supply two focal antipedal radial sums for arbitrary odd and even periods. I visually checked the correction on pp64–65. This source reinforces the need to verify signs before transferring circle/perimeter results; it is not a k603 resolution. [Primary article](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMA_1879_2_3_1_64_1.pdf).

Its objects are polygons tangent to two spatial confocal quadrics, with vertices constrained to appropriate confocal quadrics and reflection/regularity conditions. Even the planar billiard specialization retains only closure of the original polygon, which the PR already assumes before summing the edge identity. [Primary original proof](https://www.numdam.org/item/NAM_1908_4_8__317_1.pdf). I did not claim a complete read of Darboux's separate multivolume monograph.

This gives the classical relation negative-pedal = reciprocal polar of inverse and the explicit focal-ellipse negative-pedal envelope, a quartic. Its envelope calculation uses precisely lines X·(P-f)=|P-f|². It does not introduce a second confocal ellipse, discrete tangent chords, closed orbits or equality of two radial sums. The construction and inversion/polar relation therefore deserve classical credit; the source does not already prove the PR's cyclic consequence. ## Exact quantity distinction and checkable transfer obstruction

Translate the selected focus to the origin and invert in a unit circle. The ordinary polar line of a vertex P has equation X·P=1. The antipedal line is X·P=|P|², which equals the polar line of the **inverse** P/|P|². Thus the correct object identity is

    antipedal(P)=polar(inversion(P)),
    antipedal(inversion(P))=polar(P).

The inverse of a noncircular focal ellipse is generally not a circle or conic; the classical focal-conic polar theorem applies to polar(P), and hence to the second identity, not to the first. Salmon's quartic envelope supplies explicit historical confirmation of this distinction. Both identities are background, not claimed new contributions.

For an edge AB and focus f, denote r=|A-f|, s=|B-f|, h=dist(f,lineAB) and ell=|AB|. The verified antipedal radial norm is q=rs/h, whereas its unit-focus-inverted side length is ell_inv=ell/(rs). Consequently q=ell/(h*ell_inv): an unweighted invariant perimeter sum of ell_inv cannot simply be substituted for the sum of q. A theorem addressing a reciprocal, focal-height-weighted sum would be needed for such a transfer. None is in the inspected statements.

The independent program `quantity_transfer_controls.py` checks this distinction exactly without importing any submitted verifier. For the genuine four-cycle (5,0),(0,3),(-5,0),(0,-3), outer a=5,b=3 and caustic lambda225/34, all four antipedal radial norms at f=(4,0) have q²=850/9. Yet the inverted side squares take values34/25 and34/2025; q²/ell_inv² takes625/9 and5625. Thus even on one valid closed orbit they cannot be identified by a fixed edge-independent scale.

A second, fixed-caustic chord control a=5,b=3,lambda5 compares horizontal and vertical supporting chords. The squared ratio (qplus+qminus)²/ell² is841/180 horizontally and841/36 vertically. Thus the combined radial sum is not a fixed multiple of the ordinary side length either. All field calculations and focal heights are exact and positive; these are quantity-transfer controls, not an all-N proof or an impossibility theorem for every conceivable deduction.
