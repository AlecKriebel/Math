## Hypothesis under test and falsifiers

The required lemma is: all mean-zero data in B={f in C1([0,L]): f(0)=f(L)}, with no condition on endpoint derivatives, admit a unique local characteristic solution; uniformly bounded C1 data starting from X=id share a positive lifespan; before characteristic collision, the reconstructed Eulerian function solves u_t+(u^2/2)_x=P u distributionally and is C1 away from the seam; a finite lifespan cannot end with bounded spatial slope. The initial perturbations are smooth on the closed interval, so their interior smoothness must persist. A wrong primitive constant, unpreserved physical mean, forced matching slopes, dependence of lifespan on second derivatives, or continuation that assumes the desired lifespan would falsify this lemma.

## Independent derivation

Let L=2pi. B is a closed subspace of C1 and is complete. Write X=id+Y, Y,V in B, a=X'=1+Y'. Since Y(L)=Y(0), integral a=L. On the open subset min a>0, X extends by X(xi+L)=X(xi)+L to an increasing bi-Lipschitz degree-one circle lift. Endpoint values need agree but endpoint derivatives need not: this is a piecewise C1 circle homeomorphism, precisely adequate for a single transported corner.

Define m=L^(-1) integral Va, K(xi)=integral_0^xi (V-m)a, and d=L^(-1) integral Ka. Then K(0)=K(L)=0, H=K-d belongs to B, H'=(V-m)a, and integral Ha=0. The last normalization is physical (weighted by a), not the unweighted label average. For bounded C1 inputs, m is a continuous bilinear expression, K a polynomial combination of continuous bilinear products and integration C0->C1, and d a further continuous product/integral. Therefore F(Y,V)=(V,H) is a polynomial map BxB->BxB, bounded and Lipschitz on every norm-bounded ball. No second derivative occurs. This also defines F outside the positivity set; the positivity restriction is for Eulerian inversion only.

For an explicit bound on a ball ||Y||C1,||V||C1<=R, set S=1+R. Then |m|<=RS, ||K'||infinity<=R(1+S)S, ||K||infinity<=LR(1+S)S, |d|<=S||K||infinity. Thus ||H||C1<=(1+S)LR(1+S)S+R(1+S)S for the sum norm. Expanding differences of each finite product yields a finite L_R. Picard iteration in a fixed radius r<1/2 around each (0,V0), ||V0||C1<=R0, uses the same bound and L_R on the union of these balls. Choose T with TB_R<=r and TL_R<1. Then a>=1-r>1/2 on all these solutions. This proves a common positive lifespan regardless of arbitrarily large initial C2 norms.

Along the ODE, Y_t=V and V_t=H. Differentiation in xi is a bounded map C1->C0, so a_t=V'. The physical mean M=integral Va satisfies M'=integral Ha+integral VV'=0. Here the final term is [V^2/2]_0^L=0; no derivative matching was used. Since M(0)=0, m=0 identically. Put u(t,X(t,xi))=V(t,xi). Then H' =Va and integral Ha=0 prove H=(P u) composed with X, where P is the unique zero-mean periodic primitive. Away from the seam, inverse differentiation gives u_x=(V'/a) composed with X^(-1) and u_t=H-u u_x. The endpoint values match, so u and u^2/2 are continuous at q(t)=X(t,0). Their distributional first derivatives have no delta mass at the moving seam.

A direct weak check avoids informal corner differentiation. For a smooth periodic spacetime test psi, write I(t)=integral Va psi(t,X). Differentiate: I'=integral Ha psi+integral V a psi_t+integral V^2 a psi_x+integral VV' psi. Integration by parts in xi makes the last term -1/2 integral V^2 a psi_x; its boundary term vanishes by endpoint values and periodic psi. Thus I'=integral [(P u)psi+u psi_t+(u^2/2)psi_x] dx, which is exactly the weak conservative PDE. This verifies every corner term.

The slope w=V'/a belongs to C([0,L]) and varies continuously in time there. Its exact ODE is w_t=(V-m)-w^2=V-w^2. At the right/left endpoints w_+,w_-, their forcings are equal because V(0)=V(L). Hence J=w_--w_+ obeys J'=-(w_-+w_+)J and J(t)=J(0) exp(-integral(w_-+w_+)). A nonzero initial corner persists as long as the finite solution exists; no fixed crest speed is assumed. Also q'=V(0). Equality of the spatial essential supremum with max_[0,L]|w| follows from continuity of w on the interval and its one-sided endpoint limits. Thus the slope norm is continuous in time.

For continuity in the appropriate class, set A_t(xi)=X(t,xi)-q(t), an increasing C1 map [0,L]->[0,L], with A_t(0)=0 and A_t(L)=L. On any compact time interval min a is positive. Uniform C1 continuity of A_t and the identity (A_t^(-1))'=1/(a composed with A_t^(-1)) prove C1 continuity of inverses. Uniform continuity of V' on the fixed compact label interval then proves that U(t,z)=V(t,A_t^(-1)(z)) is continuous into B. The same argument combined with Banach ODE dependence gives continuous dependence on data in this aligned-corner topology. It does not claim strong continuity of arbitrary translations in periodic W1,infinity.

Naturality under an endpoint-preserving increasing C1 relabeling r is checkable: transformed X=X composed with r, V=V composed with r, so change of variables leaves m,d unchanged and gives K_new=K composed with r and H_new=H composed with r. Thus the characteristic vector field respects label choice. A global spatial translation adds a constant to X and leaves a,H unchanged. For Eulerian single-corner Lipschitz solutions of this regularity, the usual unique Lipschitz-velocity particle paths recover this system; uniqueness asserted in the candidate is already narrower (local uniqueness of the Banach ODE) and follows directly.

For continuation, suppose |u_x|<=M for 0<=t<T<infinity. Then a=exp(integral_0^t w), so exp(-MT)<=a<=exp(MT). The periodic primitive kernel k(x)=1/2-x/L on (0,L) has integral |k|=L/4=pi/2. Consequently ||V(t)||infinity<=||V0||infinity exp(pi t/2). Further ||V'||infinity<=M exp(MT), ||Y'||infinity<=exp(MT)+1, and ||Y||infinity<=integral_0^T||V||infinity. These are finite uniform bounds. F is bounded in C1 on that bounded set, hence (Y,V) is uniformly Lipschitz in time in BxB and has a norm limit at T; boundedness alone would not suffice in an arbitrary infinite-dimensional ODE. The limiting a is still >=exp(-MT)>0. Picard at the limit extends the solution beyond T. The argument assumes only the slope bound on the already existing interval, never existence through T.

For the stated data, Ck regularity on the closed label interval is preserved. In each Bk={f in Ck([0,L]): matching values}, F is locally Lipschitz with no loss. For n>=2, differentiate V_t'=Va: the highest derivative equations are (D^n X)_t=D^n V and (D^n V)_t=V D^n X+lower-order products. They are linear in the highest derivatives, with coefficients controlled by the C1 solution; induction and Gronwall bound each Ck norm on every finite interval with bounded slope. Therefore no higher-derivative lifespan can end first, even if the initial C2 norm is large. Interior smoothness follows. The core departure argument requires only C1 and endpoint slopes.
