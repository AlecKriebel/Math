## Original claim and priority test

The OWR4/2005 report, OWR-785-003, printed pp.203–205, asks whether symmetric four-qubit W belongs to the LOCC dense-codeable class. Its source context is Bruß, D’Ariano, Lewenstein, Macchiavello, Sen(De), and Sen, *Distributed Quantum Dense Coding*, PRL93 210501 (2004), quant-ph/0407037v3 (8 December 2004), and the expanded quant-ph/0507146v1 (15 July 2005).

The frozen allocation is two independent local-unitary message encoders, A1→B1 and A2→B2 over noiseless physical qubit channels. The receivers may perform LOCC across A1B1:A2B2, including collective quantum measurements across copies **within one receiver's laboratory**. Free message transmission from senders to receivers, quantum receiver-to-receiver transmission, a joint sender encoder, extra preshared entanglement, and global measurement across receivers are different resources. A sum rate strictly greater than two classical bits per W4 copy/two sent qubits answers the membership question positively; exact capacity need not be determined.

The candidate proposes independent uniform real-Pauli inputs, Bell measurement at B1 on each received pair, forwarding the whole outcome sequence, and a quantum collective cq-MAC decoder at B2 conditioned on that sequence. It claims `C_LOCC(W4) ≥ 3/2+h2(1/4) ≈ 2.311278124`, with the strictly interior achievable rate pair (9/8,9/8). All outcomes are retained. This report uses that submitted mechanism as the priority target and does not independently re-prove its entropy calculations.

A complete historical overlap would be a previously published explicit W4 LOCC strict-above-two-bit construction, or a complete published mathematically equivalent application with the same resources. A generic old theorem from which a new application can be deduced today establishes antecedents; it does not by itself establish that the original application was historically published. Conversely, failure to find that application in a finite search proves neither novelty nor that the original question remains open globally.

### W4 Bell decomposition and preprocessing are prior art

Cao Hai-Jing and Song He-Shan, *Quantum Secure Direct Communication with W State*, Chin. Phys. Alice Bell measures her retained pair, sends a classical bit based on the secret message and her outcome, and Bob Bell measures his pair. Printed p.291/Table1 transmits **one secret bit using one sender classical bit**. It supplies neither two independent unitary message encoders nor the original transmitted-qubit dense-coding rate. The successful publisher PDF route was supplied by ROOT after FIRST; this family independently captured all170,073 bytes and visually read all three pages, including Eq.(1), the complete protocol, conclusion, and references. Biswajit Pradhan, Pankaj Agrawal, and Arun Kumar Pati, *Teleportation and Superdense coding with Genuine quadripartite entangled states*, arXiv:0705.1917v1, submitted 14 May 2007, §§4.1–4.2, specifically §4.2.3 p.36, give four W4 codewords and Bell decompositions, Eq.(108)–(109). ### Quantum MAC coding and dense-coding applications predate the submission

Andreas Winter, *The Capacity of the Quantum Multiple Access Channel*, quant-ph/9807019, first submitted 6 July 1998; audited v3 submitted 1 February 2001; IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory47,3059–3065 (2001), Theorem9, proves achievability for independent classical inputs and a memoryless quantum output, with conditional mutual-information inequalities. The complete seven-page text, including proof and appendix, was read. No W4 Bell-preprocessing application is given there. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9807019v3)

Minxin Huang, Yongde Zhang, and Guang Hou, *Classical Capacity of A Quantum Multiple Access Channel*, quant-ph/9911120v1 (30 November 1999), v5 (6 November 2000), PRA62 052106 (2000), explicitly give a pure-output two-input MAC achievability proof and generalized superdense-coding application, §6. Both versions were retained; all v5 main sections were read. Two independent senders share entanglement and both send to **one common quantum receiver**. For the bipartite source discussed, the sum bound is 2logN; their concluding tripartite extension is also a common receiver. This establishes prior method/application structure, not the original W4 receiver-LOCC result. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9911120v5)

A.E. Allahverdyan and D.B. Saakian, *Multi-access channels in quantum information theory*, quant-ph/9712034v1 (17 December 1997), formulate independent sources and one quantum output, entropy constraints, and bosonic noisy-channel examples. The complete text was read. This is an earlier MAC antecedent, without the W4 dense-coding application. Winter cites the related conference publication under *Accessible Information in Multi-Access Quantum Channels*; the arXiv title is different. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9712034v1)

These are substantial antecedents. The submission should not claim a new quantum MAC theorem or a new general idea of using MAC coding for dense coding.
