## Target authentication

The PRL author PDF is the published 2004 article (PRL 93, 210501), while arXiv quant-ph/0407037v3 explicitly identifies 8 December 2004 and retains the four-party W question near its end. OWR 4/2005 pp. 203–205 is a short conference contribution summarizing that published article and explicitly asking whether the four-qubit W state belongs to the LOCC dense-codeable class. quant-ph/0507146v1 identifies 15 July 2005 and is an expanded treatment, especially useful for the operational/asymptotic model and the shell definitions; it is not an interchangeable version of the OWR text.

The model uses independent sender probabilities/product local unitary encodings. The receivers' LOCC cut is their respective received sender systems plus their original subsystems. The capacity is asymptotic (and expanded sections consider collective encodings across copies). For two one-qubit senders the benchmark is 2 bits/copy. Membership in the LOCC-DC shell requires a strict advantage and exclusion of LO-DC. This supports the pinned target.

## Independent findings

1. Winter, quant-ph/9807019v3, Theorem 9 (2001 version; work originating in 1998), already supplies the independent-message cq multiple-access coding theorem and its subset conditional-mutual-information constraints. It has a single quantum output receiver in the achievability theorem. It is a known engine, not itself a W-state LOCC decoder or an explicit application to the target. A candidate using it must credit it and establish an admissible LOCC reduction and information quantities.
2. Cao–Song CPL 23, 290–292 (2006), DOI 10.1088/0256-307X/23/2/005, was independently downloaded from its publisher and all three pages rendered and inspected. Its message is one secret bit; Alice measures her retained pair and sends a message-dependent classical bit. This is not the pinned sender-unitary/two-quantum-transmission task. Bell-pair decompositions and classical assistance were known; the protocol is not a strict >2-bit solution.
3. Pradhan et al., arXiv:0705.1917v1, Sec. 4.2.3 p.36 gives four W codewords distinguished by local Bell measurements plus receiver communication and reports two cbits. That is a boundary-achieving one-shot code under a single sender holding two qubits, not a proof of a strict asymptotic independent-sender advantage. It is close prior art and any proposed decoder must be compared with it.
4. Hayashi–Wang's *published* PRX Quantum 3, 030346 (2022), Theorem 2 and Secs. II, III.C, IV.A–C were read through the publisher PDF in the web tool; the curl PDF request failed with child exit 56. An independently downloaded arXiv URL yielded v1 (26 September 2021), whose theorem numbering differs; I do not treat it as the published version. The final theorem assumes a multiplicity-free group representation on the transmitted/encoded system and has one sender with a fixed helper. Grouping sender qubits together changes the receiver partition; retaining a receiver spectator while encoding its companion qubit introduces multiplicity, while encoding the second independent message on the helper changes the channel model. No justified direct reduction to the target has emerged. The theorem must be cited as related, not claimed to settle or exclude the target automatically.
5. Das et al. quant-ph/1412.6247v1 (PRA 92, 052330, 2015) and Muhuri et al. arXiv:2211.13057v2 (PRA 109, 032616, 2024) explicitly distinguish LOCC capacity from an upper bound; Muhuri's Eq.(6)/(7), two-receiver discussion, and plots use upper bounds. Their W/noisy-state discussions do not independently establish the requisite noiseless achievable advantage. A >2 entropy upper bound cannot resolve this existence question.
6. Yuan's 2011 IJTP publisher abstract (10.1007/s10773-011-0729-7) and publisher-deposited IJQI Crossref abstract (10.1142/S0219749911006879) advertise two secret bits per W state. Bodies were not accessed. Shukla–Banerjee–Pathak arXiv:1204.4573v1, Sec.2 pp.2–3, explicitly describes Yuan and Zhao/Guo (10.1016/j.proeng.2012.01.005) as two-bit protocols and improves a W protocol to three bits with one Alice encoding both home qubits and one Bob holding all four qubits for a W-basis measurement. Sec.3's higher-rate DSQC sends all resource qubits. Neither is the receiver-LOCC independent-message task. Publisher/abstract evidence suffices to exclude the *advertised two-bit result* as an already-stated >2-bit solution; missing bodies prevent an assertion that no other material appears in those bodies, not every bounded scientific novelty judgment.
7. Search found Liu et al., arXiv:2310.02923v1 (a paper also published in QIP in 2020). Its ostensibly maximal four-qubit “W1” example, Table4 p.16, is (|1100>+|0110>+|0011>+|1001>)/2, a different state. The appendix p.22 explicitly identifies symmetric single-excitation W2 and says its minimal-two-qubit scheme fails. It supplies no LOCC decoder. It is not a direct target solution.
