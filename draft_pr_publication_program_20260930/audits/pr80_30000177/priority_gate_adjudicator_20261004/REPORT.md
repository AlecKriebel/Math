## Corrected local-isotropic instrument comparison

MWW's Eq24 gives `Iacc_LOCC >= I(X:U_A⊗U_B) >= [SL(rhobar)−sum_x p_x SL(rho_x)]/(306 ln2)`. Its displayed lower bound is at most **.00471469 bits** for a pure-state ensemble. This small lower bound supplies no converse for the sharper middle quantity.

An elementary additional check bounds that particular middle quantity. With normalized Haar measure, the dimension-d isotropic POVM has outcome density `q_rho(psi)=d<psi|rho|psi>`. For any ensemble,

`I(X:Y)=sum_x p_x D(q_rho_x ||1)−D(q_rhobar ||1) <= max_rho D(q_rho ||1)`.

Convexity puts the maximum at a pure state. Haar invariance and the Beta(1,d−1) distribution of `t=|<psi|phi>|²` give `c_d=[ln d+1−H_d]/ln2` for this maximum. The chain rule then gives `I(X:Y_A,Y_B)<=c_dA+c_dB`: bound the A marginal, then bound the B ensemble conditional on each A outcome. For the target laboratories 4×4 this is **.874160745 bits** for any ensemble. A single independent Haar measurement in each n-copy laboratory instead gives `2c_(4^n)<2(1−gamma)/ln2<1.22` bits per block. [check_isotropic_instrument.py](check_isotropic_instrument.py) cross-checks the exact pure-state integral and numeric constants; it is not an interval certificate and the analytic derivation carries the inequality.

Thus the corrected product-Haar instrument does not itself yield a strict rate above two. These are bounds on that fixed instrument, including block Haar measurements, **not upper bounds on arbitrary LOCC accessible information or dense-coding capacity**. Different structured measurements and further theorem reductions remain possible. Neither MWW's inspected primary statement nor an established old application supplies the target strict-rate code. The prior correction must be cited if discussing the older local-subentropy restriction. This is a priority-instrument applicability check, not a new search for the central W4 proof.
