## Exact target and historical evidence

The threshold is **strictly more than two bits per W4 copy / pair of transmitted qubits**. The senders have independent messages, choose local unitary encodings, and transmit `A1 -> B1`, `A2 -> B2`. Receiver operations are LOCC across `A1B1:A2B2`; sender-to-receiver classical information is forbidden. Arbitrary block operations within one receiver's lab are allowed. The source's LOCC-DC shell excludes LO-DC. Neither a global receiver nor a one-copy orthogonal-alphabet requirement can be substituted.

The full primary [PRL 93, 210501 (published 19 November 2004)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.210501), printed p.210501-4, says W4 “is yet unknown whether it is LOCC-DC”. The authenticated [OWR 4/2005 contribution, printed pp.203–205](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2005/04), defines the asymptotic task and repeats the question on p.205. The complete [2005 exposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0507146v1), §§3.1, 6–7, equations (16)–(26), confirms this interpretation. Its 2018 running-header timestamp is not the preprint date.

From the published formulas and W marginals: the LO expression is `2h2(1/4)<2` (or two with the classical fallback); the global-receiver capacity is three; the receiver-LOCC upper bound is `1+2h2(1/4)=2.622556249…`. The last quantity is a bound, not an attained rate. These are deductions from the authenticated model, not new priority claims.
