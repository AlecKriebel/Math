# Disconnected lifts of planar subsurfaces: partial results and the remaining gap

**Target:** 30003955 / OWR-16415-018, Dmitri Gekhtman's Problem 12 in OWR 40/2018, p. 2528.
**Status:** unresolved in general; the sufficient conditions below are partial results, with no novelty claim. Separate adversarial review is pending.
**Prepared:** 30 September 2026, gpt-6-astra at xhigh reasoning.

Let f: S_h -> S_g be a connected, unbranched finite cover of closed oriented surfaces, where h>g>=2. Let d>1 be its degree. Choose a base point and label its fiber by a set Omega of size d, giving the transitive monodromy homomorphism

\[
\rho:\pi_1(S_g)\longrightarrow G\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega).
\]

We establish the existence of an embedded four-holed sphere H with essential boundary and disconnected f^(-1)(int H) under any of the following sufficient conditions:

1. The cover factors through a nontrivial connected cyclic cover of S_g. In particular this holds for every regular cover whose deck group is nonperfect, including every nontrivial solvable deck group.
2. Every subgroup of G generated by at most three elements is intransitive on Omega. For a regular cover, it suffices that G needs more than three generators.
3. The base genus satisfies g >= 2|G|. In particular g >= 2d! suffices for any degree-d cover, and g >= 2d suffices for a regular degree-d cover.

Each condition also answers the three-holed-sphere question affirmatively. None of these conditions is proved for every cover, and the general source question remains open in this analysis.

## 1. Exact monodromy criterion

For any connected subsurface Y and a base point in its interior, components of f^(-1)(int Y) correspond to the orbits of rho(pi_1(Y)) on Omega. Indeed, two points in the fiber lie in the same lifted component precisely when a path between them projects to a loop in Y; lifting that loop gives the monodromy action. Thus

\[
f^{-1}(\operatorname{int}Y)\text{ is disconnected}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho(\pi_1(Y))\text{ is intransitive on }\Omega.
\]

Changing the path used to identify the base point only conjugates the subgroup and does not change its orbit count.

For a regular cover, the action is the regular action of its deck group. A subgroup A then has [G:A] orbits. Consequently disconnectedness is equivalent to A being proper. This last equivalence is generally false for irregular covers.

If f factors as S_h -> Z -> S_g and the inverse image of Y in Z is disconnected, its inverse image in S_h is also disconnected: the inverse images of the nonempty open-and-closed pieces remain nonempty because S_h -> Z is surjective.

A four-holed sphere H contains a pair of pants P all of whose boundary curves remain essential in S_g. One can cut H along a curve separating two boundary components from the other two and take either side. Essential boundary makes H incompressible, so the new curve is also essential in S_g. Moreover rho(pi_1(P)) is contained in a conjugate of rho(pi_1(H)). An intransitive action stays intransitive upon restriction to a subgroup. Therefore a positive four-holed-sphere result implies the corresponding pair-of-pants result for the same cover.

## 2. Covers with a cyclic intermediate cover

It suffices to treat a connected cyclic cover of prime degree p: any nontrivial cyclic cover has such an intermediate quotient. It is classified by a nonzero character

\[
\chi:H_1(S_g;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p.
\]

There is an oriented nonseparating simple closed curve c such that, after multiplying chi by a nonzero scalar, chi is algebraic intersection with [c] modulo p. To see the integral step explicitly, first scale a nonzero coordinate of chi to 1, then lift its coordinates to integers with that coordinate exactly 1. This produces a primitive integral cohomology class. Its Poincare dual is primitive in H_1(S_g;Z), and a primitive homology class on a closed oriented surface is represented by a nonseparating simple closed curve. The latter standard surface fact follows by extending the class to a symplectic basis and realizing the basis change by handle slides.

Every loop disjoint from c has zero intersection with c. Hence chi vanishes on the fundamental group of the complement of a collar of c. Extend c to a cut system of g disjoint nonseparating curves with connected complement. Removing collars of the complete cut system produces a sphere F with 2g boundary circles, and chi vanishes on pi_1(F).

As 2g>=4, F contains a four-holed sphere H whose boundary curves are all nontrivial in F: partition its boundary circles into four nonempty groups and take a planar central subsurface separating the four groups. For g=2 simply take H=F. All boundaries of F are essential in S_g, so F is incompressible and every boundary of H is essential in S_g as well. The cyclic cover restricts trivially over H, so its inverse image consists of p disjoint copies of H. The factor-cover observation proves condition 1.

If a regular cover has nonperfect deck group G, then its nontrivial finite abelianization has a quotient of prime order. The original cover therefore factors through the corresponding cyclic cover. A nontrivial solvable group cannot be perfect, since its derived series would otherwise stay equal to G forever. This proves the stated regular solvable case.

This argument does not assert that every irregular cover with nonperfect monodromy image has a cyclic intermediate cover. Such a factor requires its point stabilizer to be contained in the kernel of a nonzero cyclic character.

## 3. A generator-count condition

The fundamental group of a four-holed sphere is free of rank three. Its monodromy image is therefore generated by at most three elements. Condition 2 immediately makes this image intransitive. Essential four-holed spheres exist in every S_g with g>=2, for example inside the cut-system complement used above, so this criterion is not vacuous.

For a regular action, every proper subgroup is intransitive. Thus the minimal number of generators d(G)>3 suffices for the four-holed sphere. Similarly d(G)>2 suffices for the pair-of-pants question, since its fundamental group is free of rank two. These are sufficient conditions, not converses.

## 4. An elementary large-genus construction

Write m=|G|. Since d>1, m>=2. Assume g>=2m. Choose disjoint subsurfaces U_1,U_2, each a genus-m surface with one boundary component, in S_g.

Within either U_i choose the usual m disjoint handle curves a_1,...,a_m, with based representatives obtained by paths in a tree. If any rho(a_j)=1, that curve is a nonseparating simple curve with trivial monodromy. Otherwise the m images take values in G minus {1}, which has only m-1 elements. Two images coincide, say rho(a_j)=rho(a_k). An oriented band sum of a_j with the oppositely oriented a_k, using the connecting tree, is freely homotopic to a_j a_k^(-1). It is a simple curve with trivial monodromy. It is nonseparating because its homology class [a_j]-[a_k] is nonzero in the standard independent handle classes.

This gives disjoint nonseparating simple curves alpha in U_1 and beta in U_2, both killed by rho. Choose a simple dual curve intersecting alpha once inside U_1; a regular neighborhood is a one-holed torus T_1. Do the same for beta in U_2, obtaining a disjoint one-holed torus T_2. Join T_1 and T_2 by an embedded band in their complement. A regular neighborhood T of this union is a genus-two surface with one boundary circle. Its complement in S_g is connected of genus g-2 with one boundary. In particular the boundary of T is essential, since g>=4 here.

Cut T open along collars of alpha and beta. The result F is a five-holed sphere. Four of its boundary circles are copies of alpha and beta. A planar surface has fundamental group generated by based loops around all but one boundary circle; choose these four. Their images under rho are conjugates of rho(alpha), rho(alpha)^(-1), rho(beta), and rho(beta)^(-1), all equal to 1. Thus rho(pi_1(F))=1.

Choose a four-holed sphere H inside F by cutting off a pair of pants containing the two alpha boundary circles. The remaining boundary components of H are the two beta circles, the original boundary of T, and the new separating circle. The new circle bounds the one-holed torus reconstructed by gluing the two alpha circles, so it is essential in S_g. The other three are also essential. Since rho(pi_1(H))=1, the original degree-d cover restricts to d disjoint copies of H. This proves condition 3.

Only the elementary pigeonhole principle is used to obtain the numerical genus bound. The bound is not asserted to be optimal.

## 5. Why a regular refinement does not settle an irregular cover

Here is an exact negative control for an invalid reduction, not a counterexample to Gekhtman's question. Use the presentation

\[
\pi_1(S_2)=\langle a,b,c,e\mid[a,b][c,e]=1\rangle
\]

and map a to r=(1 2 3), b to t=(1 2), c to r^(-1), and e to t in S_3. The relation holds because [r,t]=r^2 and [r^(-1),t]=r. The image is all S_3.

Inside the first one-holed torus, remove a collar of a. The remaining pair of pants P has fundamental group generated by a and b a^(-1) b^(-1), up to common conjugation. Its image is A_3=<r>. Consequently P has disconnected inverse image in the regular S_3-cover, with two components. But A_3 acts transitively on the natural three-point fiber, so the inverse image of the same P is connected in the associated irregular degree-three cover. Its genus is 4, while the regular degree-six cover has genus 7.

The exact checker `check_monodromy.py` verifies the surface relation, subgroup orders, and both orbit decompositions. It also checks simple cyclic examples. It does not enumerate all subsurfaces and does not settle either original question.

## 6. Exact remaining gap and literature boundary

For a general transitive finite monodromy action, the missing step is to find an essential four-holed sphere whose rank-three image subgroup is intransitive. Properness alone is insufficient in an irregular action. For regular covers, the remaining cases include perfect deck groups needing at most three generators, with base genus below the sufficient bound above. The pair-of-pants variant may require a separate argument in cases not covered by the four-holed result.

There is a genuine limitation on the stronger strategy of finding a planar subsurface with completely trivial monodromy. [Funar–Pagotto, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09174) constructs finite simple characteristic quotients of every closed surface group of genus at least two whose kernels contain no essential simple-loop class. Such quotients cannot kill the fundamental group of any essential planar subsurface, since its boundary classes would be killed. This does not refute the desired disconnectedness, which only requires a proper image for a regular cover. It shows that the trivial-monodromy construction cannot cover all cases.

A limited current primary-source search did not locate a resolution of the exact OWR problem. Related results on compatible pants decompositions and holomorphic maps of Teichmuller spaces do not, by themselves, assert the required intransitivity. No comprehensive priority claim is made for the elementary partial results above. Both selected proof routes stop at the stated general-case obstruction.
