## Covering, quotient and surface lemmas

A loop whose monodromy sends one fiber point to another lifts to a connecting path, and a connecting path projects to such a loop. Thus components are A-orbits, equivalently double cosets A\G/L. Changing the connecting base path conjugates A and relabels the fiber by a bijection. Restricting to B<=A can split orbits but cannot merge them. For a regular cover L=1, a subgroup A has [G:A] orbits; properness then exactly means disconnectedness. For irregular covers this substitution is invalid. ## Cyclic criterion and generator criterion

Any nontrivial finite cyclic cover has an intermediate prime-degree cyclic quotient. Its nonzero mod-p homology character can be multiplied by the inverse of a nonzero coordinate. Lift the resulting coordinates to integers, choosing that coordinate exactly1; the integral cohomology vector is primitive. The unimodular intersection form identifies it with a primitive integral homology class. A nonzero primitive homology class has a simple nonseparating representative c. The basic representation fact is explicitly stated in [Hatcher–Margalit, Generating the Torelli Group, §3 printed10](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/Torelli.pdf). Its use here is not the separate genus-three connectivity theorem on that page.

The algebraic basis step is independently checkable: Bezout and unimodularity give w with intersection(v,w)=1; subtract the two pairing coordinates to split a rank-two symplectic summand, then induct on its orthogonal complement. The usual elementary handle moves realize the integral symplectic changes, sending the first standard simple meridian to c. Equivalently this is the standard primitive-class representation lemma credited above. A separating simple curve on a closed oriented surface is zero in homology; c is therefore nonseparating.

After scaling, the character is algebraic intersection with c modulo p. Every loop disjoint from c has zero intersection, so the character vanishes on the whole collar complement. Cutting a nonseparating curve leaves a connected genus-(g-1) surface with two boundary circles. Repeatedly choose a nonseparating cut in the remaining positive genus; classification keeps the complement connected. After g cuts it is a sphere F with 2g boundaries, all ambient-essential collar copies. F is still disjoint from c, hence has trivial cyclic image.

For g=2 take H=F. For larger genus group the 2g peripheral circles into four nonempty groups and take a planar central H4. This can be built by successively thickening trees connecting each group's holes in the planar complement; the remaining surface has the four central boundaries. Each boundary encloses a nonempty proper set of holes, so it is nontrivial in F, including singleton groups. Essential-boundary injection transfers this to S_g. The cyclic restriction has p distinct copies, and the valid factor implication gives disconnectedness in the original cover.

A finite nonperfect regular deck group has a nontrivial finite abelianization and thus a prime cyclic quotient; its stabilizer is trivial, so it supplies the intermediate cover. A nontrivial solvable group is nonperfect because a perfect group's derived series would never decrease to1. These corollaries are valid. Nonperfect irregular monodromy need not kill its stabilizer, so no such corollary is inferred there.

The fundamental group of H4 is free of rank3, and pants has rank2. Therefore the exact all-at-most-three-generated-subgroups-intransitive assumption suffices for any essential H4. The preceding cut-system construction supplies one in every g>=2. In a regular action d(G)>3 forces an H4 image to be proper; d(G)>2 does the same for pants.
