## Independent source interpretation

The source asks for an embedded sphere minus four disks with boundary components essential in S_g, with h>g>=2, then separately asks the pants version and permits restricting to regular covers. It does NOT require pairwise nonisotopic boundary components. In genus 2, a cut-system complement has four boundary circles in two isotopy pairs. It meets the literal source premise. A strengthened pairwise-nonisotopic premise is a different problem: at most 3g-3=3 disjoint pairwise nonisotopic essential curves exist in S_2, so four such boundaries are impossible.

## Proof obligations and potential falsifiers

1. Cyclic character: scalar normalization of a nonzero coordinate to 1 permits an integral lift with that coordinate exactly 1, hence a primitive cohomology vector. Integral Poincare dual must be represented by a simple nonseparating curve; a reference or independent standard-proof expansion is required. A vector merely nonzero modulo p, without this primitive lift, is insufficient.
2. Complete cut system: first curve must extend to g disjoint nonseparating curves whose joint complement is connected planar with 2g boundary components. The genus-2 construction uses F itself. Central four-boundary extraction must give every boundary a nontrivial class in F, including singleton peripheral groups; incompressibility must transfer this to S_g.
3. Incompressibility: each boundary essential in the closed surface suffices for a compact subsurface with nonempty boundary to inject pi_1. This is a surface fact, not an inference from nontrivial boundary words in an arbitrary group. Interior 2+2 separating curves of a four-holed sphere are essential there and remain essential in S_g. Subgroup restriction preserves intransitivity.
4. Genus-m blocks: their existence at g=2m includes an annular outside region; verify no nonempty-extra-genus assumption. Both a_j loops must use one coherent tree of whiskers. Equality of freely represented/conjugate images is insufficient in a nonabelian group.
5. Band-sum lemma: for standard disjoint a_j,a_k with coherent tree whiskers, the oriented opposite band sum must represent a_j*a_k^-1 up to common conjugation, without an additional rho(arc) inserted. Its integral homology e_j-e_k is nonzero, proving nonseparation. Opposite orientation is essential: same orientation gives product, not quotient.
6. Dual and torus: an embedded curve crossing a nonseparating alpha once exists using an arc between the two boundary copies after cutting; its union with alpha has a regular neighborhood of type (genus1,boundary1). Two choices remain disjoint in the two blocks.
7. Connecting band: exterior of these tori must be connected. A proper embedded arc connecting two DISTINCT exterior boundary circles keeps the cut exterior connected; an arbitrary band/same-boundary arc need not. This yields a genus2 one-boundary T with complement genus g-2,boundary1. delta_T is essential only if both sides have positive genus; g>=2m and m>=2 ensures g>=4.
8. Cut alpha,beta in T: independent torus placement gives connected genus0,boundary5 F. Its four alpha/beta boundary loops generate pi_1(F), so killed curves imply rho(pi_1(F))=1. Killing all boundary loops does not imply trivial pi_1 for positive-genus surfaces; planarity is essential.
9. Final H: cut off pants around the two alpha copies. New boundary bounds a reconstructed one-holed torus and is essential. The remaining beta pair and delta_T are essential. Check annular exteriors and minimum m=2,g=4 expressly.
