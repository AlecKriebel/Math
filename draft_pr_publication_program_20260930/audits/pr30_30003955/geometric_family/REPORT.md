## Literal source premise and boundary cases

Gekhtman's Problem 12, printed p.2528 of [OWR 40/2018](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/3662/OWR_2018_40.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), asks for an embedded four-holed sphere with ambient essential boundary and disconnected lifted interior for h>g>=2. It separately asks the pants version and allows regular covers as a restricted case. It does not demand pairwise nonisotopic boundaries. Cutting a genus-two surface along a two-curve cut system gives four essential circles in two ambient isotopy pairs, and meets the literal premise. A strengthened demand for four disjoint pairwise nonisotopic essential curves on genus two is impossible: the maximum is 3g-3=3, as follows by extending an essential nonparallel system to a pants decomposition and counting Euler characteristic. That strengthened statement is not used.

All arguments assume closed connected oriented base and total surfaces and an unbranched finite covering. Since h-1=d(g-1), h>g implies d>1; transitivity then makes |G|>=2. Connected cyclic quotients of prime order are genuinely nontrivial. Neither degree-one covers nor genus-one bases are silently included.

## Universal background lemmas: proof and use

**Essential-boundary injection.** Let Y be a compact connected proper subsurface of a closed oriented surface and suppose every component of its boundary is noncontractible in the ambient surface. Cut the ambient surface along these circles. Every component has boundary and no component is a disk, since such a disk would make its boundary ambient contractible. Every boundary circle of a nondisk compact oriented surface injects its Z fundamental group: for an annulus it generates Z; for a surface with at least two boundary components, choose all but one boundary among its free generators and note that the remaining boundary word is nontrivial; for a positive-genus one-boundary surface the boundary word is a freely reduced product of commutators. Free groups are torsion-free. Reattach the cut surfaces along boundary annuli. The graph of groups has injective edge maps, so its vertex groups embed. This uses the standard graph-of-groups theorem, independently checked in [Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Theorem 1B.11, printed p.92](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch1.pdf). The application gives pi_1(Y)->pi_1(S_g) injective even when several boundary circles are ambient parallel and complementary annuli occur. Merely checking some boundary images in an arbitrary target group would not establish this lemma.

**Pants extraction.** In a four-holed sphere the curve dividing its boundary circles into two pairs is embedded, nonperipheral and noncontractible. Essential-boundary injection transfers essentiality to the ambient surface. Either side is pants with three essential boundaries. For compatible base paths, its monodromy subgroup is contained in a conjugate of the four-holed image. A subgroup orbit is a subset of an original subgroup orbit, so restriction cannot turn an intransitive action transitive. This proves the four-holed-to-pants implication without assuming regularity.

**Primitive classes and simple curves.** A primitive integral cohomology vector is sent to a primitive homology vector by the unimodular intersection pairing. A primitive homology vector is represented by a nonseparating simple closed curve on a closed oriented surface. This standard fact is independently supported by [Hatcher–Margalit, Generating the Torelli Group, §3, printed p.10](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/Torelli.pdf), which points to Farb–Margalit Proposition 6.2. An algebraic proof of the basis step chooses a vector pairing 1 with the primitive vector by Bezout, splits off the resulting rank-two unimodular symplectic summand, and repeats on its orthogonal complement; standard integral symplectic basis changes are realized by surface handle moves. The simple representative is nonseparating because a separating simple curve on a closed oriented surface has zero homology. This lemma is used only on closed surfaces, not asserted for arbitrary multiply bounded surfaces.

**Cut-system extension.** Cut a collar of a nonseparating simple curve c. The complement is connected of genus g-1 with two boundary circles. Choose a nonseparating curve there until no genus remains, keeping the cut complement connected at each step. Classification of compact oriented surfaces supplies each choice. After g cuts the result is a sphere with 2g boundary circles. In the original closed surface all those circles are parallel copies of nonseparating curves, hence essential. This is a topological construction, not an enumeration of permutation words.

## Cyclic-factor route

For a nonzero mod-p character chi, scale a nonzero coordinate to 1, then choose integral coordinate lifts with that coordinate exactly 1. Their gcd is 1, so the lift is primitive. Its Poincare dual has a nonseparating simple representative c. The scaled character is intersection with [c] modulo p; scaling does not change the kernel. Every loop in the collar complement of c has zero intersection, so chi vanishes on its whole fundamental group, rather than just selected generators.

Extend c to the cut system just proved. Its planar complement F has 2g>=4 boundary circles and trivial cyclic monodromy. To construct H, partition those holes into four nonempty sets. In the disk-with-holes model join each group by disjoint embedded trees and thicken them; their boundaries leave a central four-holed region. Equivalently the planar surface is a central S_(0,4) glued to four planar peripheral surfaces S_(0,n_i+1), with each n_i>=1. Each central boundary separates at least one original hole from at least one other hole, hence is noncontractible in F; singleton groups use annular peripheral regions. Essential-boundary injection transfers this to S_g. For g=2 take F itself (or move all four boundaries inward through collars). The cyclic restriction is trivial and has p copies of H.

Disconnectedness in an intermediate cover pulls back to the total cover: the nonempty open-and-closed pieces pull back to nonempty open-and-closed pieces under the surjective intervening covering. A nonperfect finite regular deck group has a nontrivial finite abelianization and thus a prime cyclic quotient. A nontrivial solvable group cannot be perfect, by its derived series. These group deductions fit the geometric construction. No assertion is made that a nonperfect irregular monodromy image automatically gives an intermediate cyclic cover; the point stabilizer condition remains necessary.

## Generator-count route

An essential four-holed sphere exists on each g>=2 base by the cut-system construction independent of the character. Its fundamental group is free of rank three, so its image has at most three generators. The assumed intransitivity of every such image subgroup therefore gives the desired lifted disconnectedness. In a regular action, a group needing more than three generators cannot equal this image; a proper subgroup of a regular action is intransitive. Pants have rank two, giving the stated independent d(G)>2 sufficient condition. No converse or guarantee for every finite group is inferred.

## The original same-tree band-sum step fails

The false sentence is the §4 assertion that, for based standard handle curves chosen with paths in a tree, an opposite-oriented band sum along that same tree is always simple and freely homotopic to a_j a_k^(-1). A fixed tree does not by itself give that orientation formula.

Here is a concrete local counterexample. Take disjoint simple independent nonseparating curves x and y and a connecting embedded tree arc in a genus-m one-boundary block. Their regular neighbourhood N is a pair of pants. Orient and base x,y coherently as the two peripheral generators of this pants neighbourhood. These are permissible orientations and common tree paths for the stated standard curves. The third pants boundary represents xy, up to conjugacy and inversion. Every simple closed curve in pants is either contractible or parallel to one of its three boundary circles. The nonempty word xy^(-1) has homology (1,-1) in H_1(N)=Z^2, whereas the three peripheral possibilities have vectors (1,0),(0,1),(1,1), up to sign. Therefore it cannot be a simple class in this tree neighbourhood. In particular no simple band sum confined to its small tree/collar neighbourhood can have the claimed free homotopy class.

For an exact finite-image witness, use m=3 and a genus-three block with all three coherent peripheral handle loops sent to r in C3, and send the dual b-generators to identity; the surface relation holds since the target is abelian. Place two such blocks in S_6 with annular exterior and make the same assignments on all six handles. The resulting surjective C3 character defines a connected regular degree-three covering S_16->S_6, satisfying g=2|G| and h>g. None of the handle images is identity and every pair has equal image. A quotient word is killed, but the compatible two-hole pants boundary has image r^2, which survives. Reversing generator orientations within this fixed planar neighbourhood does not rescue the pigeonhole argument: equal images in {r,r^-1} occur in the same orientation class, and their quotient has the same nonsimple local pattern. Choosing a different tree or leaving this neighbourhood might produce a killed simple curve, but the original paragraph neither proves nor controls the required changed base paths. This is a counterexample to its same-tree lemma, not to the existence of some killed simple curve in the whole block and not to Gekhtman's question.

For three or more coherently peripheral holes, signs cannot force the quotient to match a two-hole boundary for every possible equal-image pair: each such pair would require opposite signs, which cannot hold pairwise for three indices. The safe repair therefore controls products in their actual boundary order instead of presuming a universally compatible quotient surgery.

`PROPOSED_REPAIR.md` supplies the replacement. The planar m-hole neighbourhood gives coherent loops s_1,...,s_m with independent ambient homology. Among the m+1 images of prefixes 1,s_1,s_1s_2,...,s_1...s_m two agree. Their difference is the image of a nonempty consecutive interval product s_(i+1)...s_j, with no extra relative conjugator. A standard comb of whiskers makes that interval product the boundary of an embedded planar region enclosing exactly its consecutive holes. Its homology is the nonempty sum of independent handle classes, hence nonzero and nonseparating. Singleton and full intervals are allowed. This gives the required killed curve in every genus-m block and preserves m=|G| and g>=2m.

## Every subsequent large-genus geometric step

**Two blocks and the equality case.** For g>=2m choose U_1,U_2 by the standard connected-sum model; their exterior is connected of genus g-2m with two boundary circles. At equality it is an annulus, which still gives a connecting region. The valid repaired argument above works in each block separately. The resulting alpha,beta lie in disjoint blocks. Nonzero homology in a one-boundary block proves nonseparation there: every separating curve bounds a side whose boundary is either just that curve or that curve plus the original boundary, and the original boundary is zero in H_1. An essential-boundary injection or a dual curve also verifies ambient essentiality.

**Dual curves and one-holed tori.** Cutting alpha leaves its block connected. A simple embedded arc in that cut surface between the two boundary copies, closed across the removed collar, gives a simple curve meeting alpha exactly once. A compact regular neighbourhood of these two curves is a one-holed torus: the crossing gives alternating four darts, Euler characteristic -1 and one boundary cycle. The same construction lies entirely within the other block for beta, so the tori T_1,T_2 are disjoint. Arbitrary two-loop bouquets without an alternating crossing would instead thicken to pants, as the negative ribbon control demonstrates.

**Exterior connectivity and the band.** Removing the interior of a connected one-boundary subsurface from a connected surface leaves a connected exterior: every exterior component must attach along that one connected boundary circle, which can adjoin only one component; a component not attaching there would disconnect the original surface. Apply this successively to T_1 and T_2. The exterior has genus g-2 and two boundary circles. Choose a properly embedded arc joining these DISTINCT circles. Such an arc is nonseparating: if its cut separated the surface, walking around either endpoint boundary would have to cross the separating arc an even number of times, but that boundary contains just one endpoint. An arc with both endpoints on the same boundary can split off a disk and would not justify this step.

Thicken the chosen arc to an untwisted band and join the tori. The resulting connected T has chi=-1-1-1=-3 and one boundary, hence genus two. The exterior is the connected surface obtained by cutting the two-boundary exterior along that arc, so has genus g-2 and one boundary. The boundary delta_T separates genus two from genus g-2 and is essential since m>=2 implies g>=4. At g=2 that very construction would have a disk exterior and delta_T would be inessential; this out-of-hypothesis counterexample is retained in the controls. At the valid minimum m=2,g=4, both sides have genus two.

**Five-holed F and its whole image.** In each torus, cutting the original killed nonseparating curve leaves pants. The joining band joins these two cut tori into a connected planar surface F, with four collar boundaries alpha+,alpha-,beta+,beta- and delta_T. Its chi=-3 and five boundaries confirm genus zero. Its rank-four fundamental group has a basis consisting of coherent based loops around the first four circles. Each is a conjugate of a killed curve or its inverse, so each image is 1, and the entire pi_1(F) is killed. No vanishing of a single torus boundary is used; a positive-genus surface can have every boundary image killed while interior generators survive, as the independent C2 character on a one-holed torus shows.

**Final H and all four boundaries.** Cut from F a pants region P containing exactly alpha+ and alpha-. The other side H has boundary beta+,beta-,delta_T,delta_new and is a four-holed sphere. Each beta copy is essential because beta is nonseparating. The old delta_T was checked above. Gluing the alpha pair back to P turns it into a genus-one one-boundary subsurface bounded by delta_new; the complementary side has genus g-1 and one boundary. Neither side is a disk, so delta_new is essential. beta+ and beta- are ambient isotopic, which is allowed by the primary premise. The image of pi_1(H) is trivial because H lies in F, so the degree-d covering restricts to d copies. This proves the sufficient conclusion after the local repair, including every boundary claim.
