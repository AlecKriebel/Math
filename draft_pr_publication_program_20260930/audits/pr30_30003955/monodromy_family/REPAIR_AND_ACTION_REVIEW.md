## Path lifting and regular versus irregular actions

Let Gamma=pi_1(S_g), K the covering subgroup, C its core, G=Gamma/C, and H=K/C. Up to the ordinary opposite-group convention, the fiber is G/H with left action. If A is the restricted monodromy image of a connected subsurface Y, components of f^-1(int Y) correspond to A-orbits. Every lifted component meets the chosen fiber, by lifting a path to the base point in Y. A lifted path between two points projects to a based loop and realizes the orbit relation; conversely an orbit loop lifts to such a path. Thus the component count is |A\G/H|, and disconnectedness is exactly intransitivity. The argument does not assume regularity or faithful restriction.

Changing the base-point path conjugates A and relabels fiber points by a bijection. Hence orbit counts are conjugacy invariant. Restricting from A to a subgroup cannot merge distinct A-orbits. For a regular cover H=1, each orbit is a left coset of A and the count is [G:A]. A deck-group/opposite-group identification changes neither the index nor intransitivity. For an irregular cover, A<G can be transitive; replacing orbit count by index is invalid.

An essential-boundary four-holed sphere is incompressible in S_g. A curve separating its boundary circles into two pairs is nontrivial in its own fundamental group, hence essential in S_g. Either pants side has essential boundary and image contained in the four-holed image after a common base-point conjugacy. Therefore disconnectedness for this four-holed sphere implies disconnectedness for its pants side. The implication is one-directional and does not say every pants solution extends to four boundaries.

## Exact intermediate cyclic quotient criterion

For a connected regular intermediate Q-cover, let N be its normal subgroup in G. The fiber map gH -> gN is well-defined precisely when H<=N. In particular a prime cyclic intermediate cover exists precisely when there is a nonzero character G -> C_p killing H. At the surface-group level this is K<=ker(chi). Such a character factors through G because C<=K<=ker(chi). This proves necessity as well as sufficiency, with no unspoken descent assumption.

If a quotient action is intransitive for A, the action upstairs is intransitive: every upstairs A-orbit maps into one quotient orbit, and the fiber map is surjective. Topologically, the preimages of distinct nonempty open-and-closed intermediate components are nonempty and disjoint. Disconnectedness upstairs has no converse descent guarantee.

A nonperfect finite regular deck group has a nontrivial abelianization and hence a prime cyclic quotient. Since its stabilizer is trivial, the quotient is a genuine intermediate cover. A nontrivial solvable group cannot be perfect, because its derived series would otherwise remain the whole group. For irregular covers, nonperfectness alone omits the stabilizer-containment condition.

## Universal sufficient hypotheses 1 and 2

For the cyclic-factor hypothesis, take a prime cyclic quotient. Its nonzero finite-field cohomology character can be rescaled and integrally lifted with one coordinate exactly 1, making the integral class primitive. Poincare duality and a symplectic basis give a nonseparating simple representative c of its dual. Every loop disjoint from c has zero algebraic intersection with c. Extend c to a cut system; its collar complement F is planar with 2g essential boundary circles and trivial cyclic monodromy. For g=2 use F itself. For larger g, a central four-holed sphere separating four nonempty groups of boundary circles has boundaries nontrivial in F, hence essential in S_g by incompressibility. The cyclic restriction has p components and the valid factor implication lifts this result to the original cover. Pants follows as above. This proves hypothesis 1 for every cover that actually factors as stated, and the advertised regular nonperfect/solvable corollaries.

For hypothesis 2, pi_1 of a four-holed sphere is free of rank 3, so its monodromy image has at most three generators. The action hypothesis makes this image intransitive for any essential four-holed sphere. Such spheres exist for all g>=2, including parallel essential boundary realizations. For a regular action, d(G)>3 ensures the image is proper and therefore intransitive. For pants, rank 2 and d(G)>2 are the corresponding regular sufficient condition. These are universal deductions from the explicit hypotheses; checking a few or even all subgroups in one finite group is not a certificate for every cover.

The regular restriction is essential. The independently computed W4 example below has a faithful transitive degree-sixteen action and d(W4)=4, but a one-generated proper cyclic subgroup is transitive. Thus d(G)>3 cannot replace the all-three-generated-subgroups-intransitive hypothesis for arbitrary actions.

## Original condition-3 flaw and exact replacement

The original argument asserted that a fixed-tree band sum of two equally imaged meridians, oppositely oriented, is freely homotopic to a_j a_k^-1 and simple. Equality kills this algebraic quotient, but does not guarantee that its word is the simple boundary provided by that tree.

Take the planar essential pants neighborhood of two independent handle meridians and their connecting tree arc. Let x,y be coherent inner peripheral generators. Its third boundary has class (xy)^±1. Every essential simple closed curve in pants is boundary-parallel, so its possible classes are x^±1, y^±1, (xy)^±1. The cyclically reduced word xy^-1 is none of these: its abelianization is (1,-1), rather than (±1,0), (0,±1), or ±(1,1). The same class has no simple representative elsewhere in S_g: choose geodesic essential pants boundaries; the subgroup's convex lift contains the geodesic representative of every carried class, and ambient geodesicity cannot turn this nonsimple pants class into a simple curve. This exhibits the hidden orientation/geometric-word assumption.

In additive C3, assign both coherent loops image 1. The quotient word is killed (1-1=0) but the actual simple third boundary is not (1+1=2). Assigning image 1 to each of six coherent meridians and 0 to their six duals gives a genus-six surface character with image C3. The surface relation holds because all commutators vanish. Its connected degree-three cover has genus sixteen. In either genus-three block, every pair of nonzero images agrees while the asserted quotient-band step fails. This refutes the literal proof step, not the bound or the existence claim.

Here is the checked replacement, valid for arbitrary nonabelian G, with m=|G|:

1. In a genus-m one-boundary block U, choose the standard m independent disjoint nonseparating meridians. Join their annular neighborhoods by an embedded tree of m-1 narrow bands. The resulting regular neighborhood F is connected and planar with m+1 boundary circles: it has Euler characteristic -(m-1), and each band joins distinct components. Its m inner boundaries are parallel to the meridians. Choose coherent orientations and a standard ordered peripheral free basis x1,...,xm from a base point on the remaining outer boundary, using noncrossing arcs in F. Each [x_j] is a signed distinct handle class.
2. Let p_0=1 and p_i=rho(x1...x_i), using one common path from the global base point to F. There are m+1 prefixes in a group of order m, so p_i=p_j for some 0<=i<j<=m. The identity p_j=p_i rho(x_(i+1)...x_j) uses literal concatenation order; cancellation gives rho(x_(i+1)...x_j)=1. No commutation, reordering, independently chosen conjugation, or quotient-band assertion appears.
3. In this standard ordered planar basis, the consecutive product x_(i+1)...x_j represents, up to free homotopy and orientation, the simple boundary of a neighborhood enclosing exactly those consecutive inner holes. The arcs and boundary traversal give the product in that order. For a single hole the curve is parallel to that hole; for all m holes it is parallel to the outer boundary. All cases give a simple representative.
4. Its homology is the sum of the distinct signed classes [x_(i+1)]+...+[x_j]. The block is nonempty and these handle classes are independent, so the sum is nonzero. A simple curve separating an orientable one-boundary surface has zero homology, so this curve is nonseparating in U. Its global image is killed; a different global connecting path merely conjugates the identity.

This proves one killed nonseparating curve in each genus-m block. For g>=2m, choose disjoint blocks and obtain disjoint curves alpha,beta. The remaining geometric construction can be checked separately: take disjoint dual one-holed tori, join their distinct boundary components by an embedded band in their connected exterior, and get a genus-two one-boundary subsurface T with connected exterior of genus g-2. Cutting both killed nonseparating curves leaves a five-holed sphere with four peripheral generators conjugate to alpha^±1,beta^±1, so its entire monodromy is trivial. Cut off pants containing the two alpha copies. The remaining four-holed sphere has the two essential beta copies, the old T boundary separating genera 2 and g-2, and a new boundary separating genera 1 and g-1. Since m>=2 and g>=2m>=4, every listed boundary is essential. Its inverse image consists of d copies. This preserves condition 3 and the coarse |G|<=d!, |G|=d regular corollaries after the proof is revised.

- **Separate D8 action:** the rotation C4 is a proper subgroup of the square dihedral group, transitive on four square vertices but with two orbits in the regular eight-point action.
- **Generator-threshold controls:** all 4,096 ordered triples in regular F2^4 span at most eight elements; exact span-size distribution is {1:1,2:105,4:1470,8:2520}. All 64 ordered pairs in F2^3 span at most four, while the three standard basis vectors generate its full group. This checks the rank-three/rank-two distinction without equating tests to a universal theorem.
- **Irregular four-generator control W4:** the full binary-tree automorphism group on sixteen leaves has order 2^15. Four nested flips generate it and their level-swap parity vectors form the basis of a quotient F2^4, so d(W4)=4. A binary odometer generates a sixteen-cycle in this group and is a proper transitive cyclic subgroup. The parity homomorphism is exact: composition permutes nodes of each level, so their swap parities add. Code checks it on every element times each of the four generators; the argument supplies the universal group statement. The result refutes extending the regular generator corollary to arbitrary faithful actions.
