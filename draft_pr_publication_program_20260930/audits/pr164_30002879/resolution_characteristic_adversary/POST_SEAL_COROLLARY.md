# Post-seal corollary: ordinary Gerstenhaber quotient of this algebra

This consequence was suggested by the root after this family's independent first assessment was sealed. It is supplemental work, not part of the original PR164 candidate, not independent evidence of the initial candidate, and not a claim about priority or the full general Question 9.8. Its hypothesis is the verified identification HH*(B)=L from `DERIVATION.md`.

Let N be the homogeneous elements of L that are nilpotent for the cup product. Let G(N) be the ideal for both the cup product and the Gerstenhaber bracket generated by N: a bracket with any element of L is allowed. This uses the ordinary Gerstenhaber-ideal convention, rather than just requiring Lie-subalgebra closure among elements already in the ideal.

Define epsilon:L->K by the constant coefficient of degree zero. It is a unital ring homomorphism. It also preserves the bracket with zero bracket on K. Indeed, a bracket can have cohomological degree zero only if its input degrees are zero and one. In this case write

    F=lambda+y b(x),
    G=a(x,y)u+y c(x)v,

where a is arbitrary in A and b,c are in K[x]/(x²). Then the formal bracket is

    [F,G] = y b'(x) a(x,y) + b(x)y c(x),

which belongs to yA and has constant coefficient zero. Scalars contribute nothing. Two positive-degree inputs have bracket degree at least one; two degree-zero inputs have bracket zero. Thus ker epsilon is a cup and bracket ideal. Since nilpotence maps to nilpotence in a field, every element of N belongs to this kernel, and G(N) is contained in ker epsilon. The unit is excluded.

Conversely, every basis monomial in L with an x or y coefficient squares to zero, and hence belongs to N. The remaining basis monomials of ker epsilon are the pure positive-degree u^i v^j with i>=1. For every such monomial,

    [u, x u^i v^j] = u^i v^j.

The second input belongs to L and is homogeneous and cup-nilpotent, since x²=0. It follows that every such pure monomial belongs to G(N). These monomials together with the nilpotent coefficient monomials span ker epsilon. Therefore

    G(N)=ker epsilon,        HH*(B)/G(N) is isomorphic to K.

There is no central-characteristic assumption here beyond the candidate's arbitrary characteristic-two field. This answers the finite-generation subquestion for this specific algebra under the ordinary Gerstenhaber-ideal convention: the quotient is generated by its unit. It makes no claim about the general class of algebras in Hermann Question 9.8.

If “Gerstenhaber ideal” is additionally required to be closed under a standard graded divided-square operation of degree n -> 2n-1 on even cohomological degrees, ker epsilon remains closed by degree alone: positive even degrees map to positive degrees, and degree zero maps to HH^(-1)=0. This is only a compatibility observation; it neither computes that operation nor substitutes it for the ordinary bracket calculation. Other conventions should be stated before promoting a stronger structural conclusion.
