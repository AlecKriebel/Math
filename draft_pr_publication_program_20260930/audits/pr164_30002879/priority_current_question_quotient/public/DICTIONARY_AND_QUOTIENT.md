# Exact dictionary and nilpotent-quotient verification

This is an audit derivation over every field K of characteristic two. It assumes the stated candidate model L=K+(y,u)R, R=K[x,y,u,v]/(x²,y²), with degree-zero x,y and degree-one u,v and ordinary bracket
[F,G]=F_x G_u+F_u G_x+F_y G_v+F_v G_y.
It verifies the presentation's overlap with prior results and a consequence. It does not independently reprove the entire Hochschild comparison in the candidate.

## Algebra identity

Write Λ for the quiver algebra with two vertices e1,e2, loops a,b at e1, arrow c from e1 to e2, and relations a²=b²=0, ab=ba, ac=0. Under the path concatenation convention used in the primary works, e1 c=c=c e2. Its corner e1Λe1 is K[a,b]/(a²,b²). Its off-diagonal corner has basis c,bc, with a annihilating both and b sending c to bc and bc to zero. The other diagonal corner is K e2, and the reverse off-diagonal corner is zero.

The algebra map to the candidate B sends e1 to the upper-left idempotent, e2 to the lower-right idempotent, a to y, b to x, c to the class of 1 in M=A/(y), and bc to the class of x. It preserves each displayed multiplication and maps the seven-element basis bijectively. Thus it is an isomorphism, directly in this path convention. For the EI-category convention f·g=f∘g the category algebra is the opposite, as already explained in the candidate; this does not alter the char2 ordinary Gerstenhaber comparison.

## Coordinates behind the prior presentation

Use distinct notation X_{n,q} for the generators of Long–Shi–Zhou (LSZ), Theorem7.1, to avoid confusing their algebra generators with the candidate's x. The dictionary is

| LSZ generator | Model monomial | range |
|---|---|---|
| X_{n,1} | y v^n | n>=0 |
| X_{n,2} | xy v^n | n>=0 |
| X_{n,3} | u v^(n-1) | n>=1 |
| X_{n,4} | xu v^(n-1) | n>=1 |

This is fixed at the cochain level, not inferred from dimensions. LSZ §4 defines z1=e1,z3=a,z4=b,z6=ba and f^n_{l,j} as the coefficient z_j at the Anick chain s^n_l with n-l copies of a and l copies of b. Section5 constructs Φ_n(s^n_l) as the signed sum of all such shuffles. In characteristic two its signs are one. The candidate shuffle comparison gives the same coordinate class z_j u^l v^(n-l), after a=y,b=x. In degree zero the unit is the diagonal e1+e2; the other basis classes are y and xy. LSZ's mixed coefficient cocycles f^n_{n+1,5},f^n_{n+1,7} are boundaries and disappear.

All LSZ Theorem7.1 relations hold under the dictionary by direct multiplication. Conversely, the unit and the following images recover the model basis:
X_{n,1},X_{n,2} supply yv^n,xyv^n; and for i>=1,j>=0,
C_1^(i-1) C_{j+1}=u^i v^j,
C_1^(i-1) D_{j+1}=xu^i v^j,
with y times each supplying the remaining two coefficients.
Here C_n=X_{n,3}, D_n=X_{n,4}. The LSZ normal forms map bijectively to the model monomials. The degrees have 3 basis elements at degree0 and 4n+2 at each n>=1, so the graded algebra map is an isomorphism.

## All-index bracket comparison

Put A_n=yv^n, B_n=xyv^n, C_n=uv^(n-1), D_n=xuv^(n-1). For m,n>=1 and h=m+n-1, differentiation gives

[A_m,A_n]=(m+n)A_h, [A_m,B_n]=(m+n)B_h, [B_m,B_n]=0;
[A_m,C_n]=(n-1)C_h, [A_m,D_n]=(n-1)D_h;
[B_m,C_n]=A_h+(n-1)D_h, [B_m,D_n]=B_h;
[C_m,C_n]=0, [D_m,C_n]=C_h, [D_m,D_n]=0.

Coefficients are reduced modulo two. These are precisely all ten unordered family pairs of LSZ Theorem8.3(2); the reverse pairs follow by characteristic-two symmetry.

For degree-zero A_0=y and B_0=xy, and m>=1, the same calculation gives

[A_m,A_0]=m A_(m-1), [B_m,A_0]=m B_(m-1);
[C_m,A_0]=(m-1)C_(m-1), [D_m,A_0]=(m-1)D_(m-1);
[A_m,B_0]=m B_(m-1), [B_m,B_0]=0;
[C_m,B_0]=A_(m-1)+(m-1)D_(m-1), [D_m,B_0]=B_(m-1).

For m=1 the undefined C_0,D_0 terms have zero coefficient and are omitted. The unit has zero bracket. This is all of Theorem8.3(1), including its m=1 boundary cases. Both brackets obey the cup biderivation identity, so agreement on these algebra generators yields agreement on the entire Gerstenhaber algebra in every degree.

## Weak versus ordinary full Gerstenhaber ideals

Let N be the cup ideal generated by homogeneous nilpotent elements. Projecting R to the reduced polynomial ring K[u,v] shows that an element of L is nilpotent exactly when its monomials all have an x or y factor. Thus
N=(x,y)R∩L.
Every element of this ideal has square zero in characteristic two, since each monomial squares to zero and cross terms cancel.

A bracket of two x/y-bearing monomials differentiates exactly one x/y factor, so at least one x/y factor remains in each nonzero term. Hence [N,N]⊆N. Therefore the weak cup ideal plus Lie-subalgebra closure is N itself. Its quotient is
L/N = K+uK[u,v].
This algebra is not finitely generated: its positive-degree ideal P has infinitely many independent images u v^j (j>=0) in P/P², while every product of two positive-degree elements is divisible by u². A finite set of algebra generators would give a finite-dimensional P/P².

Now let J be the ordinary Gerstenhaber ideal generated by homogeneous cup nilpotents, meaning a cup ideal satisfying [L,J]⊆J. Let epsilon:L→K be constant augmentation and H=ker epsilon.

First H is a cup ideal and epsilon kills every bracket. A possible constant term in a bracket would require input degrees0 and1. In the x,u component it would require the pair x,u, but x is absent from L_0. In the y,v component it would require y,v, but v is absent from L_1. Constants bracket to zero. Thus [L,L]⊆H and H is an ordinary Gerstenhaber ideal containing N, so J⊆H.

For i>=1,j>=0, xu^i v^j is a homogeneous cup nilpotent belonging to L, and
[u,xu^i v^j]=u^i v^j.
Thus J contains all nonconstant monomials without an x/y factor; it already contains all x/y-bearing monomials. These are every basis monomial of H, so H⊆J. We have
J=ker epsilon and L/J≅K.

This is a complete conditional deduction over any char2 field. It is exactly the char2 consequence disclosed in LSZ Theorem8.6(2), under the dictionary: their [X_{m,4},X_{1,3}]=X_{m,3} is [xu v^(m-1),u]=u v^(m-1). The consequence was suggested during the audit; it is not asserted to have been in the original submission.

Hermann §9.2 calls the first closure weak G and the second full closure calligraphic G. Visual inspection verifies that Question9.8 asks about plain weak G. Therefore the quotient K does not answer his weak-quotient question positively, and no statement here establishes finite generation for arbitrary finite-dimensional algebras.
