## Algebra and full ring dictionary

[Long–Shi–Zhou v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19560v1), printed pp2,11, defines the seven-dimensional quiver algebra with relations a²=b²=0, ab=ba, ac=0, and basis e1,e2,a,b,c,ba,bc. In characteristic two the algebra is exactly our upper triangular B under
e1=f, e2=e, a=y, b=x, c=m, ba=xy, bc=xm.
There is no trivial-module substitution. The displayed multiplication identifies M=A/(y); it covers every characteristic-two field, as does their theorem.

Their generator dictionary, from the coefficient basis in §4, the shuffle comparison in §5 and Theorem7.1, is

| Prior generator | Our coordinate |
|---|---|
| X_(n,1), n≥0 | y v^n |
| X_(n,2), n≥0 | xy v^n |
| X_(n,3), n≥1 | u v^(n−1) |
| X_(n,4), n≥1 | xu v^(n−1) |
| 1 | 1 |

The shuffle words contain n−l occurrences of a=y and l occurrences of b=x. Their coefficients and comparison signs reduce to1 in characteristic two, so coefficient class f^n_(l,j) has coordinate z_j u^l v^(n−l), with z1=1,z3=y,z4=x,z6=xy. This fixes normalization rather than infer it from dimensions.

Every relation in Theorem7.1 is respected: products of two y-bearing generators vanish; X_(m,2)X_(n,4)=0 by x²; X_(m,2)X_(n,3)=X_(m,1)X_(n,4); products of two X4 vanish; multiplication by X_(0,1)=y and X_(1,3)=u reproduces the remaining indexed relations. Their normal basis maps bijectively onto the complete basis of L=K+(y,u)R. In positive degree n, use u^(i−1)X_(j+1,3) for pure u^iv^j (i≥1), the corresponding X4 for x-bearing monomials, and multiply by y when needed; the no-u classes are X_(n,1/2). In degree zero the basis is1,y,xy. Thus this is an exact graded algebra isomorphism, with dimensions3 and4n+2.

## Full bracket, not just dimensions or degree one

Theorem8.3, printed p41, provides all generator brackets. Substitution of the above dictionary into
[F,G]=F_xG_u+F_uG_x+F_yG_v+F_vG_y
reproduces every table entry in all degrees. Illustrative identities include

- [X_(m,1),X_(0,1)]=m X_(m−1,1).
- [X_(m,3),X_(0,1)]=(m−1)X_(m−1,3), with the m=1 exception equal to0.
- [X_(m,3),X_(0,2)]=X_(m−1,1)+(m−1)X_(m−1,4), with m=1 giving y.
- [X_(m,1),X_(n,1/2)]=(m+n)X_(m+n−1,1/2).
- [X_(m,1),X_(n,3/4)]=(n−1)X_(m+n−1,3/4).
- [X_(m,2),X_(n,3)]=X_(m+n−1,1)+(n−1)X_(m+n−1,4).
- [X_(m,4),X_(n,3)]=X_(m+n−1,3).

The omitted pairs in these examples are also explicitly listed in the source and agree by the same substitution; these examples are not a substitute for the complete table comparison by the independent families. Parity descriptions in their table are exactly these integer coefficients reduced modulo2. Since the generators generate the ring and the bracket is a biderivation, equality of the full generator table proves equality of the whole Gerstenhaber bracket. This explicit source subsumes the original primary Lie-structure target.

## Weak versus full nilpotent ideal

Hermann §9.2/Question9.8 and Oke distinguish the cup ideal closed under brackets of its own elements ([G,G]⊂G) from a Lie ideal ([HH*,G]⊂G). Question9.8 uses the former, weak convention. Consequently ROOT's separate conditional quotient-K corollary is for the full Gerstenhaber ideal; it must not be described as an affirmative answer to Hermann's weak-ideal question.

In our L, homogeneous cup nilpotents form N=L∩(x,y)R. N is a cup ideal and [N,N]⊂N because each bracket term retains a nilpotent coefficient from one input. Its quotient is K+uK[u,v], which is not finitely generated: its monomials uv^j have u-order1, so finitely many algebra generators could supply only finitely many v-exponents at u-order1. This weak quotient is prior work, including Oke2021/2022.

For the full Lie ideal generated by N, [u,xu^iv^j]=u^iv^j kills the remaining positive pure monomials, while constant augmentation is a Gerstenhaber map and excludes1. Its quotient isK. Long–Shi–Zhou Theorem8.6(2), printed p42, explicitly gives exactly this result, including the same nilpotent-stripping identity in its generator coordinates. It supplies no residual new theorem here.
