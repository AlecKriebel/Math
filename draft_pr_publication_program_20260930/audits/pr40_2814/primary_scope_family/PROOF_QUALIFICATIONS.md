## Xia2110.14376v1: axial roots and distinctness

Simplify the displayed boundary transformation to f_t(x)=b_t+Q_t x/|x|², where Q_t is orthogonal and |b_t|→infinity. The inverse is f_t^{-1}(y)=Q_t^{-1}(y−b_t)/|y−b_t|². Set B=|b_t| and rho=2/B. For sufficiently large B, f_t sends the closed Euclidean ball B(b_t,rho) into itself, while f_t^{-1} sends B(0,rho) into itself. Their derivative operator norms on the respective balls are at most(B−rho)^{-2}<1. Banach contraction gives a fixed point x_large in B(b_t,rho) and a distinct fixed point x_small in B(0,rho), also fixed by f_t. Since t*g is a nonidentity element of the discrete free manifold group, it is not elliptic. The two boundary fixed points make it axial, including for orientation-reversing ambient isometries. This supplies the root/existence step compressed in Lemma2.1, which norm inequalities alone would not prove.

The axis is the Euclidean semicircle with endpoint separation |x_large−x_small|=B+O(1/B), hence radius R=B/2+O(1/B). Its intersections with the height1 horosphere differ from the ideal endpoints by R−sqrt(R²−1)<=1/R. Thus C_t→A0 and D_t−B_t→0 in the Euclidean horosphere metric. Its maximum cusp height R→infinity. Every one fixed geometric closed geodesic has bounded cusp height because its image is compact, so these simple axes give infinitely many distinct images; arbitrary powers of a fixed image cannot explain escaping height.

The horoball tangent to height1 with ideal center0 has Euclidean radius1/2 and center(0,...,0,1/2); the text's radius1 at that center is a normalization typo. For h^{-1}g0 the immediate preserved horoball is H0. Fixing its tangency point A0 and its ideal center forces preservation of the same vertical normal axis, and therefore of H_infinity and all horizontal horospheres, yielding the orthogonal block used in the formula.

Lemma2.2's exclusion of identified points means distinct points/nontrivial identification; allowing the identity at the same point would make its hypothesis impossible. The short geodesic arc converges to A0 inside a fixed injective local ball. For sufficiently large t it lies outside the interior of the chosen maximal cusp, while the long arc lies inside; other lifted horoballs are locally finite and none can intrude into the shrinking neighborhood of this unique two-horoball tangency. This prevents a missed long/short self-intersection.

For a toral cusp use the fixed lattice basis, not the ambient Euclidean coordinate norm without a comparison constant. The long displacement has at least one transverse coordinate in a strict interval(0,1), and every other transverse displacement has modulus<1. A lattice translate identifying two points must therefore have zero transverse integers; it would be parallel to the winding direction, contradicting the nonzero transverse displacement.

For a Klein-bottle cusp write s(x,y)=(x+alpha,−y), t(x,y)=(x,y+beta). Translations have x displacement2k alpha and y displacementm beta. Glide reflections have odd-alpha x displacement and reflection-axis y coordinatem beta/2 (the sign of m is immaterial). Two identified points of the semicircle must have equal height; distinct equal-height points have projected parameters u,1−u and projected midpoint P equal to the endpoint midpoint. A glide reflection can identify them only when P_y belongs to the half-beta axis lattice.

- Unequal x coordinates in a fundamental strip: the long family winds in y, while endpoint x width is bounded below alpha. Every nonzero horizontal translation or glide has x displacement at least alpha and is excluded; a vertical translation would force the chord to be vertical, contrary to its nonzero x displacement.
- Equal x and y-symmetric pair(0,−a),(0,a),0<a<beta/2: use t^n s. The x width is close to alpha and below2alpha, excluding horizontal translations. The midpoint y is n beta/2−a+O(epsilon), separated from the half-beta lattice when epsilon<min(a,beta/2−a)/2. This excludes glides. A pure vertical translation cannot match distinct points of the nonvertical chord.
- Equal x and nonsymmetric y pair: use s^{2n}. Their midpoint y lies strictly between−beta/2 and beta/2 and is nonzero, so stays a positive distance from the half-beta lattice after a sufficiently small endpoint perturbation. The y chord width is nonzero and less than beta, eliminating nonzero transverse translations; horizontal translations cannot match a chord with nonzero y component.

These cases cover all distinct tangency lifts. Freeness rules out coincident A0,B0 because the chosen isometry would fix an interior point.
