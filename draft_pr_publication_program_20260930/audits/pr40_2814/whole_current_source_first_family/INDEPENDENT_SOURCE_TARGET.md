The target is Kirby K3 Problem3.16 (printed/PDF page142), identical in scope to
Burns-Matveev/AIM Question5.6.1 (PDF page12): every finite-volume hyperbolic
3-manifold should contain infinitely many simple closed geodesic images.
K3 Chapter3.1 (PDF pages131-132) defines hyperbolic using a complete Riemannian
metric of constant sectional curvature-1 and the quotient of H^3 by a discrete
isometry group. No orientability restriction appears in Problem3.16 or this
convention; Chapter3.1 also explicitly discusses nonorientable manifolds.
AIM's opening conventions assume a connected smooth manifold, and its Section2
separates geometrically different closed geodesics from repeated traversals.

Thus the operative object is a connected, complete, boundaryless, finite-volume
Riemannian 3-manifold of constant sectional curvature-1, equivalently H^3/Gamma
with Gamma a discrete torsion-free group acting freely. Allow orientation-
reversing elements. The curvature/quotient definition excludes incomplete
metrics, geodesic-boundary variants, cone orbifolds and arbitrary finite-volume
variable-negative-curvature metrics. These are separate questions.

Simple means the primitive arclength map from R/LZ is an embedding. Count
unoriented geometric images, disregarding basepoint/time shifts and orientation
reversal. Iterates of one simple image do not provide infinitely many images;
the repeated parametrization is not injective. Closed refers to the geodesic,
not an assumption that the ambient manifold is compact. Finite-volume manifolds
split into compact (closed) and noncompact cusped cases. Both orientable and
nonorientable cases must be covered. A theorem only about an orientable cover
cannot be pushed down without checking that a lifted curve is disjoint from its
nontrivial deck translates; projection alone does not preserve embeddedness.
