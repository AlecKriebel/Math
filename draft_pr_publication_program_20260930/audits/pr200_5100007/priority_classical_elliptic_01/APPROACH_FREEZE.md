Frozen 2026-10-16 04:36:33 UTC, before reading the candidate proof or any priority conclusions.

## Claim under audit
For a primitive periodic billiard orbit of least period N in a proper nested confocal ellipse pair, let Q_i be the intersections of successive tangents to the outer ellipse at successive billiard vertices. If 4 divides N, the product over i of the Euclidean distances from Q_i to either outer focus is independent of the phase of the orbit; the two focal products agree. All primitive star rotation numbers coprime to N are included.

The optional stronger claim is: in the strict noncircular model, the primitive product is phase-constant if and only if 4 divides N. This audit does not assume that this classification is new.

## Frozen distinct approach families
1. **Classical Poncelet and duality.** Search Poncelet, Jacobi, Darboux, Cayley and their later primary expositions for products of distances from vertices of a tangential Poncelet polygon to a focus. Explicitly distinguish the original inscribed billiard polygon from the polygon formed by intersections of its outer-ellipse tangents.
2. **Focal / isotropic factors.** A distance to a focus can be factored into intersections with the two complex isotropic lines through the focus. Search classical polygon/conic theorems for invariant products of linear forms, cross-ratios, or powers with respect to foci and isotropic points. Seek a theorem that includes this exact dual construction and the mod-4 condition.
3. **Elliptic-function norm / multiplication.** A finite product over a torsion orbit on an elliptic curve is a norm. Determine whether classical Jacobi theta or elliptic multiplication identities alone, together with an explicitly established divisor/pole cancellation for this observable, give the exact result; separate the standard method from a prior exact theorem or complete stated corollary.
4. **Confocal tangential polygons.** Search named theorems about tangent polygons, polar reciprocals, confocal dual conics, focal distance ratios/products and alternating products. Beware invariants applying only to N even, special convex orbits, or vertices on the original ellipse.
