## Exact hypothesis and success criterion
For a>b>0, c=sqrt(a^2-b^2), and a proper inner confocal caustic 0<lambda<b^2, let P_i be a primitive real Poncelet billiard orbit of least period N divisible by 4, with fixed caustic. Let Q_i be the intersection of the tangents to the outer ellipse at P_i and P_{i+1}. Prove, or give a counterexample to, phase independence of product_i |Q_i-(c,0)|. The reflected focus must follow too. Every primitive admissible winding must be covered; finite real Q_i and nonzero distances must be justified. No novelty claim is being assessed.

## Independent mechanism obtained so far
Write P(theta)=(a cos theta,b sin theta), m=(theta+phi)/2, d=(phi-theta)/2. Consecutive tangent intersection is Q=(a cos m/cos d,b sin m/cos d). Confocal caustic tangency is sin^2 d=lambda(cos^2 m/a^2+sin^2 m/b^2). Therefore Q traces the ellipse

  ((a^2-lambda)/a^4) Q_x^2+((b^2-lambda)/b^4) Q_y^2=1.

A convenient real elliptic parametrization, derived from the Jacobi addition law and to be independently justified below, is

  P(u)=(a sn(u,k), b cn(u,k)), k^2=c^2/(a^2-lambda),
  sn(v,k)=sqrt(lambda)/b, 0<v<K,
  P_i=P(u_0+2iv),
  Q_i=(A sn(u_0+(2i+1)v), B cn(u_0+(2i+1)v)),
  A=a^2/sqrt(a^2-lambda), B=b^2/sqrt(b^2-lambda).

Thus the squared focal distance is the order-four elliptic function

  f(u)=(A sn u-c)^2+B^2 cn^2 u
      =B^2+c^2-2Ac sn u+(A^2-B^2)sn^2 u.

A straightforward group-by-quarter-period argument is insufficient for a generic caustic: the product over u,u+K,u+2K,u+3K is generally not constant. Any proof using it must use the particular torsion/closure relation. The N=4 caustic is special (A=B).

## Distinct direct conic/divisor strategy
Factor the squared Euclidean distance as the two isotropic line intersections (Q_x-c+iQ_y)(Q_x-c-iQ_y). Locate all complex zeros and poles of f on its elliptic curve. The orbit product can be constant only if its zero and pole divisors cancel under translation by the real step 2v. Determine explicitly whether the focal position forces zero/pole differences into the cyclic subgroup generated by 2v (possibly using K and 2v). This would explain the N divisible by 4 condition without simply checking an author's asserted product identity.

## Adversarial challenges and boundary cases
1. Fixed caustic is essential; N alone does not choose a unique caustic when star windings are included.
2. Primitive closure requires 2v=4rK/N, gcd(r,N)=1, 0<r<N/2. If N divisible by 4 then r is odd and K belongs to the cyclic orbit subgroup. A repeated orbit with a period label divisible by 4 is a separate issue.
3. Complex zeros/poles and real period conventions must be explicit; a real product proof may not silently use an incorrect imaginary period of cn.
4. Tangent intersections are undefined for antipodal endpoints. Proper caustic 0<lambda<b^2 should exclude this for a genuine billiard step, but it must be proved.
5. Denominators must remain nonzero for every real phase, including vertices on axes and rotated/reflected star orbits.
6. Euclidean distances use the positive real square root. A squared product identity must not introduce a phase-dependent sign.
7. The circular limit a=b, degenerate caustics lambda=0,b^2, and a tangential-polygon circle A=B must be handled as limits or expressly excluded; division by A^2-B^2 is unsafe.
8. An identity in one complex torus is not by itself proof that all real primitive billiard windings have been parametrized.
