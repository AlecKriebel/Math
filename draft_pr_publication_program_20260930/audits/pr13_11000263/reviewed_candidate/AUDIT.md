## 1. Source fidelity

The source question is “Does X3 equal 0 in Zn?” It occurs in Section 8 of
Stephen Bigelow, *Braid groups and Iwahori-Hecke algebras*,
[arXiv:math/0505064](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505064), preprint p. 14;
[published scan](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~bigelow/publications/10.pdf),
p. 298, DOI [10.1090/pspum/074/2264547](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/074/2264547).
The queue points to the [edited-volume draft](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf),
pp. 315–316. The definitions agree across those versions, including the defect.
Both the preprint and published page were visually inspected.

Here are the mathematical definitions, retaining the source's parameter powers
and inverse convention. The bar reverses the **whole word**:

\[
\sigma_{i_1\ldots i_s}=\sigma_{i_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_s},\qquad
\bar\sigma_{i_1\ldots i_s}=(\sigma_{i_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_s})^{-1}.
\]

Thus \(\bar\sigma_{21}=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2^{-1}\). The elements are

\[
X_2=q\sigma_1^{-1}+(1-q)-\sigma_1,\qquad
X_k=(q^{k-1}\bar\sigma_{(k-1)\cdots1}-\sigma_{1\cdots(k-1)})X_{k-1}.
\]

Write the relation elements as

\[
R_2=\{q\sigma_2^{-1}+(1-q)-\sigma_2
       -q\bar\sigma_{21}+\sigma_{12}\}X_2,
\]

\[
R_k=\{q^{k-1}\bar\sigma_{k\cdots2}-\sigma_{2\cdots k}
       -q^{k-1}\bar\sigma_{k\cdots1}+\sigma_{1\cdots k}\}X_k
\quad(k\ge3).
\]

Bigelow's text says to quotient \(RB_n\) by \(R_2,\ldots,R_n\), but
\(R_n\) uses \(\sigma_n\), absent from \(B_n\). It does not literally define
an ideal in that group algebra. This is not merely an OCR error. The author's
[errata list](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~bigelow/publications.html), checked
2026-09-30, contains no correction for this paper; it is labeled current to 2020,
so absence there is not evidence that no correction exists anywhere.

We distinguish the following **conditional** formulations over \(F=\mathbb Q(q)\):

- \(A_n=FB_n/(R_2,\ldots,R_{n-1})\), for \(n\ge3\)
- \(C_n=FB_{n+1}/(R_2,\ldots,R_n)\), for \(n\ge3\)

These are quotients by the two-sided ideals generated by the listed rows.
The inherited source convention has both q and q-1 units: Section 6 assumes
q1+q2 is a unit from then on, and Section 7 sets (q1,q2)=(-1,q). The field F
meets that convention. Numerical q=1 controls are outside the inherited source
regime; they cannot establish source-valid specialization coverage.

The second includes the printed terminal row on an extra strand. Neither
identification with an unstated author-intended convention, nor equality of
\(A_n\) and \(C_n\), is assumed.

## 3. Independent algebraic check of the witness

Take \(K=\mathbb Q(q,u)\), with \(q,u\) algebraically independent, and use
column vectors in \(K^m\). For \(1\le i<m\), let \(B_i\) be the identity off
coordinates \(i,i+1\), with block

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}1-u&u\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\u^{-1}&1-u^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both products of these two blocks equal the identity. Disjoint blocks commute.
On three consecutive coordinates the two braid products coincide:

\[
B_1B_2B_1=B_2B_1B_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-u&u(1-u)&u^2\\
1-u&u&0\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Consequently \(\sigma_i\mapsto B_i\) defines a representation of \(B_m\).
For \(1\le r<m\), define

\[
v_r=u^{1-r}e_r-u^{-r}e_{r+1},\qquad
\lambda=-e_1^T+e_2^T,\qquad E_r=v_r\lambda.
\]

Direct substitution gives

\[
qB_1^{-1}+(1-q)I-B_1=(q-u)E_1.\tag{1}
\]

The local propagation calculation can be verified without a general matrix
formula. For \(1\le r\le m-2\), acting first by \(B_{r+1}\), then by \(B_r\), gives

\[
B_rB_{r+1}v_r=u^{1-r}e_{r+1}-u^{-r}e_{r+2}=u v_{r+1}.\tag{2}
\]

Acting by the corresponding inverses gives

\[
B_r^{-1}B_{r+1}^{-1}v_r
 =u^{-r}e_{r+1}-u^{-r-1}e_{r+2}=v_{r+1}.\tag{3}
\]

All preceding factors with indices at most \(r-1\) fix the resulting vector.
Therefore, for \(1\le r\le m-2\),

\[
(B_1\cdots B_{r+1})v_r=uv_{r+1},\qquad
(B_{r+1}\cdots B_1)^{-1}v_r=v_{r+1}.\tag{4}
\]

For \(2\le r\le m-2\), the same equalities hold with the initial index 1 replaced by
2. In particular no order of inverses has been silently changed.

Let \(c_k=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q^j-u)\). Equation (1) starts an induction, and
(4) gives its step:

\[
\rho(X_k)=c_k E_{k-1}\quad(2\le k\le m).\tag{5}
\]

Every defining relation must still be checked. For the exceptional first row,
straight substitution into the three-coordinate block gives

\[
\{qB_2^{-1}+(1-q)I-B_2\}v_1=(q-u)v_2
 =\{qB_1^{-1}B_2^{-1}-B_1B_2\}v_1.\tag{6}
\]

Multiplication by \(c_2\lambda\) proves \(\rho(R_2)=0\). For \(k\ge3\),
(4) and its index-2 version show that both bracketed sides of the relation,
applied to \(v_{k-1}\), give

\[
(q^{k-1}-u)v_k.\tag{7}
\]

Thus \(\rho(R_k)=0\) for every legal \(k\le m-1\). With \(m=n\), this
checks all of \(A_n\)'s relations. With \(m=n+1\), it separately checks
\(C_n\)'s terminal relation too. The argument does not assume that passing to
a quotient preserves a previously detected nonzero element.

Finally, \(E_2\ne0\), and neither \(q-u\) nor \(q^2-u\) vanishes in \(K\).
Hence

\[
\rho(X_3)=(q-u)(q^2-u)E_2\ne0.\tag{8}
\]

Since these are \(F\)-algebra representations, (8) detects nonzero \(X_3\)
in the \(F\)-algebras \(A_n\) and \(C_n\) themselves. No claim is made that
it is nonzero for every specialization of the parameters.

### Specialization and boundary checks

The same explicit matrices give \(B_1v_1=-uv_1\) and
\((B_1B_2)^3v_2=u^3v_2\). Therefore the two scalar twist identities hold.
Every matrix and identity above is defined over the Laurent-polynomial ring
\(F[u,u^{-1}]\). Evaluation \(u\mapsto q^3\) is a map from that ring,
not a homomorphism from the whole field \(\mathbb Q(q,u)\), in which
\(u-q^3\) is invertible. Setting \(u=q^3\) through this model in (5)
annihilates \(X_4\) whenever at least four matrix strands are available, while
\((q-q^3)(q^2-q^3)E_2\ne0\) over \(\mathbb Q(q)\). At \(u=q\), \(X_2\)
vanishes; at \(u=q^2\), \(X_3\) vanishes. These controls matter: a single
singular specialization cannot establish generic nonvanishing. The proof above
uses independent indeterminates before specialization. The boundary \(A_3\)
has no \(X_4\); \(C_3\) has four matrix strands and does. The twist
values are linked, \(t_2=-u\) and \(t_3=u^3=-t_2^3\); arbitrary
independently prescribed scalar twists are outside this witness. A finite
matrix module over \(\mathbb Q(q,u)\) does not by itself make its image
finite-dimensional over \(F\); after \(u=q^3\), the image lies in
\(M_m(F)\). Neither observation proves finite dimension of a universal
quotient with extra relations.

This audit does not prove finite-dimensionality of a universal quotient by
additional relations, identify the author-intended corrected presentation, or
certify the archive's claimed novelty. Those are separate questions.
