The generator convention is `B_n=<sigma_1,...,sigma_(n-1)>` (arXiv p. 3; scan p. 287). The shorthand is `sigma_i1...ik=sigma_i1...sigma_ik` and its bar is the inverse of that **complete word**. Therefore `bar(sigma_21)=sigma_1^-1 sigma_2^-1`; it is not `sigma_2^-1 sigma_1^-1`. Visual anchors: arXiv p. 14, scan p. 298, book printed p. 315/physical PDF p. 322. The relation powers are `q^(k-1)`, including q in the exceptional first row and q^2 in the X3 row.

The X_n recursion uses generators through sigma_(n-1), so it is a legal element of RB_n. The final displayed defining relation is different: its factors have words `sigma_2...n`, `sigma_1...n` and corresponding whole-word inverses. Thus it uses sigma_n in RB_n. All three original versions visibly retain this mismatch. No such generator is supplied by the earlier B_n convention. The literal display consequently does not specify an ideal of RB_n. A strand shift, terminal-row omission or a direct-limit interpretation is a **repair**, not a deduction about author intent.

The coefficient restrictions are fuller than merely “generic q.” Section 3 starts with q1,q2 units in a domain R (arXiv p. 4, scan p. 288, book PDF p. 312).
