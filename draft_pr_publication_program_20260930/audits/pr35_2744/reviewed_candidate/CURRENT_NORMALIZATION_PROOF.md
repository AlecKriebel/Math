# Universal normalization certificate and exact remaining gap

This verifies elementary consequences and conventional normalization in original PR35, not a solution of KP-1.85. Fix a hyperbolic knot in S3 and one oriented complete holonomy. All character varieties below are reduced affine GIT varieties. The scientific object is the irreducible component through that fixed holonomy, not a component chosen after finding compact representations.

## Liftability and the finite map

Let M be the compact knot exterior and Gamma its fundamental group. Its interior has universal cover H3, hence M is a K(Gamma,1). Alexander duality gives H1(M;Z)=Z and H2(M;Z)=0. The universal-coefficient theorem, including the Ext term from the torsion-free first homology, therefore gives H2(Gamma;F2)=H2(M;F2)=0. Every homomorphism Gamma→PSL2(C) lifts across the central extension 1→{±I}→SL2(C)→PSL2(C)→1: its obstruction class is in that vanishing second cohomology group. Two lifts of the same projective homomorphism differ by a homomorphism Gamma→{±I}, and H1(Gamma;F2)=F2. Denote its nontrivial action on characters by sigma, the meridian-sign twist.

Heusener–Porti, v2, Remark4.1 and Proposition4.2 prove that X_SL//H1(Gamma;F2) is the character variety of the *liftable* projective representations. Their full Proposition4.2 proof identifies the invariant coordinate ring, rather than identifying a naive orbit space. Example4.6 makes this the whole X_PSL for knot exteriors. Thus q:X_SL→X_PSL is finite and surjective, its closed-point fibres are the finite sigma orbits, and its image of a closed irreducible subvariety is closed and irreducible. Fibres need not always have size two; Remark4.3 gives the exceptional Ad-reducible branching. Nothing here asserts liftability for arbitrary groups.

At nonsemisimple reducible representations a GIT character records a semisimplification, not its entire raw conjugacy orbit. Compact representations are completely reducible (average a Hermitian form), so their conjugacy orbits are closed. This makes the compact characters used below unambiguous.

## The selected components

The two lifts of the fixed complete projective holonomy have meridian traces +2 and −2. They are distinct characters x+ and x−=sigma(x+). Porti2017 Theorem3.1 gives that each is a smooth point of X_SL and lies on a unique irreducible complex curve C+ or C−. His §3 explains the dimension-one tangent calculation, the lower dimension bound, and smoothness, including the character-scheme issue. This uses the usual finite-volume hyperbolic deformation/Calabi–Weil theorem; the present audit does not reprove its analytic L2 vanishing theorem.

The action sigma is free at these two points because the nonzero meridian trace changes sign. The finite quotient has an étale germ there, so y0=q(x+)=q(x−) is also smooth of dimension one. Consequently it has a unique irreducible component C. The finite image q(C+) is a closed irreducible curve through y0, hence is C; the same holds for C−. Also sigma(C+)=C− by uniqueness of the smooth local component. They may coincide; if they differ their projective images still coincide. Complex conjugation is a separate antiholomorphic operation and sends the chosen oriented holonomy to the opposite oriented holonomy; it is not the sign twist.

## Compact-real loci

Under the standard embedding PSU2≅SO3 in PSL2(C), the inverse image of PSU2 is exactly SU2. Indeed a determinant-one representative of a projectively unitary matrix is ± a determinant-one unitary representative. Conjugating a projective representation into PSU2 can be lifted to an SL2(C) conjugation; every resulting lift is in SU2. Conversely every SU2 representation projects to SO3. Since each q-fibre is a sign orbit and sign twists preserve SU2, this proves the character-set identity

q^−1(X_SO3)=X_SU2.

It proves compactness of all characters in a fibre, including possible reducible compact characters; it does not replace compactness with real trace values. A knot group is finitely presented. Its SU2 representation space is a compact real algebraic set in finitely many matrix coordinates. Finite generating trace coordinates give a polynomial map to the affine SL character variety. Its image X_SU2 is therefore compact and semialgebraic by Tarski–Seidenberg. The analogous projective locus is its finite polynomial image. Intersection with a fixed complex algebraic component remains semialgebraic by writing each complex equation as two real equations. An infinite semialgebraic set has positive real dimension and contains an injective real analytic subarc; a zero-dimensional semialgebraic set is finite. Countably infinite arbitrary sets would not suffice.

## Both directions, including any initially fixed lift

If C+ contains an SU2 character arc, finite fibres ensure its q-image is infinite. The image is semialgebraic after choosing a semialgebraic subarc of the compact locus, and lies in C∩X_SO3; hence it contains an arc. Equivalently one may choose a smaller subarc outside the finite singular and branching loci of the finite map on these curves. A family of conjugations of one representation has constant character and cannot satisfy this hypothesis.

Conversely suppose C∩X_SO3 contains an arc. Its q-preimage contains infinitely many characters in X_SU2. The finite algebraic preimage q^−1(C) has finitely many irreducible components. At least one component D contains infinitely many of these compact characters. As q is finite and C is a curve, every component has complex dimension at most one. D has dimension one, and q(D), closed and irreducible, is therefore all C. In particular D contains some point over y0. That fibre is exactly {x+,x−}. At that point the entire X_SL has one smooth one-dimensional local component, so D equals the corresponding canonical curve C+ or C−: its containment in a full irreducible component and equality of dimensions force equality, not just a local path connection. The semialgebraic set D∩X_SU2 is infinite, so it contains an arc. Applying sigma if necessary transports that arc to the other lifted canonical curve. Thus the PSL/SO arc assertion is equivalent to the SL/SU arc assertion on **any** initially selected lift of the fixed oriented holonomy.

The converse does not assume that every component of q^−1(C) dominates C, nor that its fibres are everywhere free, nor that a merely connected path cannot cross component intersections. Those shortcuts are unnecessary.

## The printed faithful-SO3 consequence is also conditional

For each nontrivial g in the countable knot group, the condition rho(g)=1 defines a closed conjugation-invariant algebraic subset R_g of the projective representation variety. A reductive good quotient sends such an invariant closed subset to a closed character subset Y_g. The fixed faithful irreducible holonomy character is not in Y_g: every representation with its irreducible character is conjugate to that holonomy. Hence C∩Y_g is a proper closed subset of the irreducible curve C, and is finite. If the requested SO3 arc exists, its uncountably many characters cannot all lie in the countable union of these finite subsets. A compact representative of a character outside that union is faithful. This justifies K3's stated implication, without proving that the arc exists. One must use the full matrix-identity locus and its invariant quotient; the condition trace²(rho(g))=4 alone is insufficient because a nonidentity parabolic also has that trace square.

## Real-locus and finite-control obstructions

For f=y²+x²(1+x), the monic quadratic in y has discriminant −4x²(1+x). Its simple zero at x=−1 prevents it from being a square in C(x), hence f is irreducible over C[x,y] by Gauss's lemma. In |x|<1/2, f≥y²+x²/2, so its only nearby real zero is the origin. The parametrization x=−1−t², y=t(1+t²) is an actual nonconstant real arc elsewhere. This example is not a knot-character curve; it only falsifies the inference from a singular isolated real point to an arc through that point.

The submitted real elliptic matrices A=[[0,−1],[1,0]] and B=[[0,−4],[1/4,0]] are separately conjugate and determinant one, but AB=diag(−1/4,−4) has trace −17/4. No simultaneous conjugation puts this pair in SU2 because every SU2 product has real trace in [−2,2]. The pair is irreducible over C: its eigenlines are distinct, and it has no common invariant line. No knot relation is asserted for it.

In finite trace coordinates compact characters are fixed by the standard complex-conjugation real structure. If an irreducible curve C differs from its conjugate, C∩conjugate(C) is finite: a proper closed subvariety of an irreducible algebraic curve has dimension zero and finitely many points. Then C cannot contain a compact arc. This is a conditional obstruction, requiring a genuine hyperbolic S3 knot realizing that nonreal component. Long–Reid's §4 general one-cusped manifold examples are not such knot examples; §5 explicitly distinguishes the knot case. Its field of definition is not the invariant trace field. Real characters also need not be compact; split SL2(R) characters must be excluded separately.

## Boyle–Rouse source qualification

Fresh v1 Theorem1.1 and Remarks1.2–1.3 verify that a hyperbolic freely two-periodic knot has two SL canonical components for the same oriented projective holonomy; Figure1 names 10_157. The sign-component reasoning above proves their images are the same selected PSL curve. No distinct-projective-component or SO3-arc conclusion follows.

Their general Lemma2.6 proof asserts gamma^n belongs to an index-n subgroup. For nonnormal subgroups that assertion is false: a torsion-free rank-two free group can act transitively on three points, with a generator acting by (12); its cube does not stabilize point1. Replace n with n!, since the coset action places gamma^(n!) in the subgroup. Torsion-freeness then makes a nontrivial gamma^(n!) nontrivial, and faithfulness on the subgroup proves faithfulness on the whole group. Discreteness follows from the finite union of cosets of a discrete subgroup; each coset is closed and discrete in the Lie group. This elementary correction repairs the general lemma. Their application has index two, so the originally printed exponent is valid there. It does not invalidate the theorem or any original PR35 inference. The source and its historical review remain unchanged.

## Exact central gap and disposition

The normalization equivalence, conditional real-component obstruction, and algebraic diagnostics are valid under the hypotheses just stated. They do not provide a compact-real arc on the selected component of every hyperbolic S3 knot, and they do not provide a qualifying knot with no such arc. The geometric/arithmetic criteria in prior work have additional hypotheses; removing those hypotheses would transfer the entire open difficulty to an unsupported claim. This family therefore supports an accepted **unsolved** partial package, with the original budget1/5 and no new theorem, paper, DOI, or worldwide novelty claim. This certificate audits the frozen original package only and must not be transferred to any substantively edited package without fresh review.
