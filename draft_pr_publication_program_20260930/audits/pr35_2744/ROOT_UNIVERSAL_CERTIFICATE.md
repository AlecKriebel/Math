## Finite normalization and the chosen component

The hyperbolic exterior is aspherical. Alexander duality gives H₁(M;ℤ)=ℤ and H₂(M;ℤ)=0. Universal coefficients, including Ext from the torsion-free first homology, give H²(Γ;ℤ/2)=0 and H¹(Γ;ℤ/2)=ℤ/2. Thus every projective representation lifts to SL₂(C); its lifts differ by the meridian sign homomorphism σ. Heusener–Porti §4.1 proves the affine character quotient by this finite sign group identifies with the liftable projective character locus. Here liftability has first been proved for the entire knot group, so the map q:X_SL→X_PSL is finite and onto. Exceptional fibres may have smaller cardinality; the quotient is not globally a free cover.

The two lifts x₊,x₋ of χ₀ have meridian traces +2,−2 and are distinct. The complete-cusp smoothness theorem (Porti2017 Theorem3.1, or Heusener–Porti AppendixB TheoremB.1.2) gives a unique smooth complex curve C₊ or C₋ through each. The sign action is free at these points, so the projective quotient is locally unbranched and χ₀ has its unique smooth one-dimensional component C. A finite image of a closed irreducible curve is closed and irreducible, with the same dimension; therefore q(C₊)=q(C₋)=C. The sign action takes C₊ to C₋. They need not be distinct. Complex conjugation changes orientation and is a separate operation.

The inverse image of PSU(2) in SL₂(C) is exactly SU(2): if A and a determinant-one unitary U have the same projective image, A=λU and λ²=1. A projective conjugation can be lifted, so simultaneous compactness lifts too. Finite quotient fibres are sign orbits and compact representations are completely reducible. Consequently q⁻¹(X_SO3)=X_SU2 at the character level. This statement uses the reductive character quotient; it does not identify nonclosed raw conjugacy orbits with compact representations.

For a finitely presented group the SU(2) representation locus is compact and real algebraic. Polynomial trace-coordinate maps and Tarski–Seidenberg make its character image semialgebraic. Intersection with a complex algebraic component stays semialgebraic. An infinite semialgebraic set has positive dimension and contains an embedded real analytic subarc. Thus an SU(2) arc on C₊ projects to infinitely many characters in C, which contain an SO(3) arc. No assumption of global freeness is needed.

Conversely an SO(3) arc on C has infinitely many compact lifts. The finite preimage q⁻¹(C) has finitely many irreducible components, each of dimension at most one. One component D contains infinitely many compact characters. Its closed irreducible image is all C, so D meets the fibre {x₊,x₋}. Smooth unique-component identification at that point makes D the corresponding canonical SL curve. Its infinite semialgebraic compact locus contains an arc. Sign transport gives an arc on either initially chosen complete lift. This proves the normalization equivalence universally; it creates no such arc for an arbitrary knot.

K3's faithful-SO(3) implication is also conditional. For each nontrivial g∈Γ, the representation condition ρ(g)=1 is closed and invariant. Its image under the good character quotient is closed and misses faithful irreducible χ₀. Its intersection with C is therefore finite. A proposed compact arc is uncountable, whereas Γ is countable, so it contains a character outside the union of these finite identity loci; a compact representative is faithful. Trace-square4 alone cannot implement this argument because a nonidentity parabolic has that value.

## Conditional obstructions and exact diagnostics

If C differs from its complex-conjugate curve, their intersection is finite. Compact characters have real trace coordinates and hence lie in that intersection; no compact arc can then occur. This is a conditional obstruction requiring an actual hyperbolic S³ knot with that component property. Long–Reid's general one-cusped examples do not supply it, and an invariant trace field is not the curve's field of definition. Boyle–Rouse's two canonical SL components are sign-exchanged lifts of the same orientation and have the same projective image. They neither prove nor refute the projective arc assertion.

The toy curve f=y²+x²(1+x) is irreducible over ℂ: its discriminant −4x²(1+x) is nonsquare in ℂ(x), since its zero at −1 has odd order. In |x|<1/2, f≥y²+x²/2, so the origin is isolated in the real locus. The different locus x=−1−t², y=t(1+t²) is a genuine real arc. This is not a knot realization. Similarly A=[[0,−1],[1,0]] and B=[[0,−4],[1/4,0]] are individually elliptic and conjugate, but AB has trace −17/4, outside the SU(2) product range [−2,2]. They cannot be simultaneously compact. No knot relation or counterexample is inferred.

The prerequisite non-almost-product assumption is checked. Porti–Weiss Lemma2.1 identifies almost product with rotational holonomy inside O(2)⊂SO(3), whose disconnected component consists of half turns. At α<π a meridian cannot be in that component. The meridian normally generates the knot group, so the rotational image lies in SO(2). The invariant-axis flow and the full Lemma2.1/Corollary2.3 argument give a Seifert-fibered smooth part, contradicting a hyperbolic knot exterior. This reasoning alone must not be reused at α=π.

An independent standard homology check excludes the π case altogether. The double branched cover of an angle-π metric would be a closed connected orientable flat3-manifold, since its transverse angle doubles to2π. For any knot Seifert matrix V, H₁ of the double branched cover is coker(V+Vᵀ). Mod2 that matrix equals the unimodular intersection form V−Vᵀ; its determinant is odd and nonzero, so the homology is finite of odd order. Of the six orientable flat types, the torus has positive first Betti number and every k-twist (k=2,3,4,6) maps onto ℤ by sending the screw generator to1, the axial translation to k and transverse translations to0.
