# PR35 universal normalization and obstruction certificate

This verifies the original partial deductions, without constructing a new knot or proving the universal target. For a hyperbolic knot K in S3 let Γ be the exterior group, fix an oriented complete projective holonomy character χ0, and let C0 be its complex irreducible PSL2(C) character curve. Characters mean the affine reductive quotient. Compact representations have closed orbits, so that quotient introduces no additional compact-character ambiguity.

**Liftability and finite quotient.** The hyperbolic exterior is a K(Γ,1), and its homology is H1=Z, H2=0. Universal coefficients therefore give H²(Γ;Z/2)=0 and H¹(Γ;Z/2)=Z/2. The central extension obstruction to lifting every projective representation vanishes. If two lifts have the same projective representation, their pointwise ratio is a central ±I scalar and multiplicativity makes it a sign homomorphism. Thus the projective character variety is the finite sign quotient of the SL variety. This is an application of the liftable-locus quotient proved by [Heusener–Porti §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0302075v2), not a claim that arbitrary groups have no lifting obstruction. Finite group quotients are finite algebraic maps: each coordinate satisfies its monic orbit polynomial.

A lifted canonical curve D containing a lift of χ0 has finite, closed, irreducible image. Finite fibers exclude an image point and give dimension one. The complete holonomy is a smooth point on its unique one-dimensional projective component, so the image is C0. Smoothness here is the usual hyperbolic one-cusped holonomy local theorem; it is an imported dependency, not established by the finite tests.

**The compact locus.** The exact group equality is p⁻¹(PSU(2))=SU(2). Indeed an SL matrix A with p(A)=p(U), U∈SU(2), satisfies A=λU, det(A)=λ²=1, hence λ=±1. If projective compactness is achieved after conjugating, lift that conjugation to SL and apply the equality to every element. Therefore every lift of a projectively compact representation is unitary after simultaneous conjugation. This is stronger than saying its individual generator traces are real or elliptic.

For finitely many generators and relations the SU(2) representation locus is a real algebraic set. Polynomial character maps and real quantifier elimination show its character image is semialgebraic. An infinite semialgebraic subset of a complex algebraic curve has real dimension at least one and contains an embedded interval. Finite algebraic fibers imply an SL unitary character arc has infinite projective image, hence a projective SO(3) arc after restriction. This argument does not require the action to be everywhere free, nor an arc disjoint from all possible reducible loci: a finite real algebraic map has a smaller injective branch on any nonconstant real curve, even if the restriction is ramified as an ambient map.

Conversely, lift a projective compact arc. The finite algebraic preimage of C0 has finitely many irreducible components. Infinitely many compact lifts occur on one component D; it must be a curve, since a finite fiber precludes a positive-dimensional component mapping to a point. Its closed image is C0. Surjectivity supplies a point above χ0. Such a point is a complete holonomy lift, and its smooth unique local component identifies D as a lifted canonical component. Its infinite semialgebraic compact locus contains an arc. If the initial choice was the other complete holonomy lift, the sign twist sends D to that canonical component and preserves SU(2). Thus arc existence is equivalent on the chosen projective component and on its lifted canonical components.

**Central sign and orientation.** The sign twist sends every character of one complete lift to the other. Its action permutes irreducible components, and smoothness at complete holonomy identifies the image component. Since p∘ε=p, the two sign-exchanged canonical curves have the same projective image. The 2024 result producing two such SL curves consequently is not a counterexample to the fixed projective question. Complex conjugation, which changes orientation, is a different involution; it preserves the compact real form but need not fix the chosen complex component.

**An admissible conditional obstruction.** If C0 and its complex-conjugate curve are distinct, their intersection is zero dimensional and finite, since distinct irreducible complex curves cannot share a positive-dimensional closed subset. Every compact character is fixed by complex conjugation in real character coordinates. Thus the compact locus is finite and contains no arc. To use this against KP1.85 one must verify this property for a hyperbolic S3 knot. A construction for another one-cusped manifold or a pair exchanged only by the central sign does not supply that example. No such qualifying knot is supplied by the original packet or this review.

**Finite diagnostic examples.** For f=y²+x²(1+x), on |x|<1/2 one has f≥y²+x²/2, so (0,0) is an isolated real zero. The discriminant −4x²(1+x) has an odd-order zero at −1 and is not a square in C(x); Gauss's lemma proves f irreducible over C[x,y]. The parametrization x=−1−t², y=t(1+t²) is another nonconstant real arc. This is a complex-curve diagnostic and has no asserted knot realization. For A=[[0,−1],[1,0]], D=diag(2,1/2), B=DAD⁻¹, one obtains AB=diag(−1/4,−4), tr(AB)=−17/4. If A and B were simultaneously conjugate into SU(2), their product would be unitary with real trace in [−2,2], a contradiction. Separate ellipticity and real trace are therefore insufficient; no knot relation is asserted.

**Exact gap.** These deductions preserve the literal target and justify the normalization. They do not produce an arc on C0 for every hyperbolic S3 knot. No new universal continuation theorem, component-changing bridge, or qualifying counterexample is obtained. The result remains unsolved in this attempt, one substantive turn of five. Verification adds zero proof-attempt turns.
