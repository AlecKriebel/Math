## Cohomological lifting and the correct quotient

The interior of M is H³/Γ and its universal cover is contractible; its collar compactification has the same homotopy type. Thus M is K(Γ,1). Alexander duality (or the exterior homology calculation) gives H₁(M;Z)=Z and H₂(M;Z)=0. The cohomological UCT sequence has terms Hom(H₂,F₂)=0 and Ext(H₁,F₂)=0, because H₁ is free. Consequently H²(Γ;F₂)=0. This is cohomology, not merely an assertion that integral H₂ is zero for an arbitrary group. Pulling back the central extension SL₂→PSL₂ along any representation gives a central F₂ extension classified by H²; it splits. All projective representations lift. Differences of two lifts are homomorphisms Γ→{±I}, so the twisting group is Hom(Z,F₂)=C₂.

Heusener–Porti v2 §4.1 supplies an invariant-ring proof: average a trace monomial over the sign group; it survives precisely when the product of its words is in Γ². The corresponding projective invariant trace product pulls back to that monomial. Thus the ring of invariants is the projective liftable-character ring. Here lifting has already been established for every representation, so this is the whole projective variety. A finite group quotient is finite: every function satisfies the monic polynomial formed over its group orbit. Its closed-point fibres are group orbits; its images of closed irreducible sets are closed irreducible sets of the same dimension. Neither free action everywhere nor exactly two points in every fibre is assumed. Ad-reducible exceptional fibres really occur.

Let x₁,x₂ be the two lift characters over y. Meridian traces are +2 and −2. Complete-cusp regularity is a published import, explicitly stated/proved locally in Porti2017 §3 and Heusener–Porti Appendix B.1.1: each xᵢ is smooth of complex dimension one, on one irreducible component Dᵢ. Its analytic deformation foundation is Calabi–Weil/Thurston and is not reconstructed by finite diagnostics. The sign twist s exchanges x₁,x₂ and therefore D₁,D₂, but D₁ and D₂ can coincide. Since neither xᵢ is fixed, the finite quotient is locally unbranched there and y is a smooth complex-curve point on one component C. The finite image q(Dᵢ) is a closed irreducible curve through y; uniqueness and equal dimension force q(Dᵢ)=C.

## Compactness and arcs in both directions

The exact group identity p⁻¹(PSU(2))=SU(2) follows by writing A=λU for a determinant-one unitary U with p(A)=p(U): λ²=1. Simultaneous projective conjugation lifts to an SL conjugation.
