# Independent universal transform and cancellation proof

Target: unit ambient Lebesgue measure on a compact finite union P of convex polytopes in R^d, d>=1. All cone-indicator statements are modulo ambient null sets. V(P) is the literal intersection of all triangulation vertex sets, not a convex-hull or chosen-triangulation vertex set. Let A(P) be the points whose translated tangent indicator is not a finite real linear combination of line-cone indicators, where a line-cone is invariant under some nonzero translation direction. Different summands may have different directions.

The independently verified statement is

    Omega_P(u)=product_{v in A(P), v!=0}(1-<v,u>),

with the reduced denominator normalized by Omega_P(0)=1. It follows that the source's equality with the product over V(P) holds iff V(P)\{0} is contained in A(P). This verifies the candidate's known-result exposition; no novelty is asserted. The argument below was formed from the literal candidate/question before historical/author checkers or other fresh-family mathematics.

## 1. Simplex integration and squarefree divisibility

For a full-dimensional simplex with vertices v_0,...,v_d let D be the absolute determinant of its edge matrix, so D=d!Vol(Delta), and set lambda_i=<v_i,u>. Barycentric coordinates t_i>=0, sum t_i=1, have monomial integral

    int_standard_simplex product t_i^{beta_i} dt
       = product beta_i!/(|beta|+d)!.

This follows by successive one-variable beta integrals, or induction on dimension integrating the last coordinate and rescaling the remaining simplex. Expand the integrand for small u by the binomial and multinomial series. Its coefficient for product lambda_i^{beta_i} is (|beta|+d)!/(d! product beta_i!). The barycentric integral and the d! normalization cancel these coefficients exactly, leaving

    F_Delta(u)=D product_i (1-lambda_i)^{-1}.

The series is absolutely convergent near zero, so termwise integration is justified. This supplies the simplex identity independently of the prior-source citation.

A finite union can be partitioned into full-dimensional simplices with disjoint interiors by the finite supporting-hyperplane arrangement and a compatible refinement. Triangulate lower-dimensional residual pieces as well if the literal set requires them. They have zero ambient mass. For any such triangulation with finite vertex set W, F_P is the sum of the full-dimensional simplex transforms. Therefore its reduced denominator divides

    Q_W=product_{w in W, w!=0}(1-<w,u>).

Every nonzero w supplies a nonconstant irreducible linear polynomial; different points give distinct normalized factors because their constant terms are all 1. Thus Q_W is squarefree, even for dependent/collinear/coplanar vertex sets. No multiple pole or additional irreducible factor can survive reduction. This also proves analyticity and the normalization at zero. If F_P=0 its denominator is defined to be 1.

## 2. Simplex interpolation gives the tangent coefficient

On the open set where the lambda_i are distinct, the elementary partial-fraction identity in a new variable t is

    1/product_i(t-lambda_i)
       =sum_i [product_{j!=i}(lambda_i-lambda_j)]^{-1}/(t-lambda_i).

Subtract the two sides: their simple residues agree at every lambda_i and the difference has no poles and tends to zero at infinity, so it is zero. Set t=1. This gives

    F_Delta(u)=sum_i c_{Delta,v_i}(u)/(1-<v_i,u>),
    c_{Delta,v_i}=D/product_{j!=i}<v_i-v_j,u>.

The excluded sets are homogeneous hyperplanes. The identity extends as a rational identity without a general-position assumption on the geometric vertices. Summing over the triangulation yields

    F_P(u)=sum_{w in W} c_w(u)/(1-<w,u>).

Each c_w is homogeneous of degree -d, possibly identically zero. With the kernel exp(<u,x>), a simplicial cone generated by independent q_1,...,q_d has convergent integral, where Re<u,q_j><0,

    T_C(u)=(-1)^d |det(q_1,...,q_d)|/product_j <q_j,u>.

This follows by the change of variables x=sum t_j q_j and product integration over t_j>=0; each integral contributes -1/<q_j,u>. Thus c_{Delta,w} is precisely the valuation of the translated tangent cone of Delta at w. The sign in this formula is fixed independently by genuine convergent integration.

A simplex containing w on a face of positive dimension has a tangent cone invariant along that face and hence is a line-cone. A simplex not containing w has empty tangent cone. Local tangent indicators add almost everywhere because only finitely many simplices are involved. Consequently c_w=T_{C_w(P)}, independently of the triangulation, once the following cone kernel fact is proved.

## 3. Independent cone-kernel proof

Define T on finite signed simplicial-cone decompositions by the rational formula above. It is independent of decomposition: a common finite homogeneous fan refinement compares any two decompositions, and each refinement identity inside a pointed simplicial cone is a genuine convergent integral identity on the interior of its negative dual cone, hence a rational identity. Null boundaries do not contribute.

Every line-cone has valuation zero. For a fixed invariant direction q, cut it by a complementary linear hyperplane and partition the resulting base cone into simplicial cones. Each full-dimensional base cone plus Rq is the union, modulo a null boundary, of two cones obtained by adjoining q and -q. Their determinants have equal absolute value and exactly one denominator factor changes sign, so their valuations cancel. Degenerate pieces have zero ambient mass. Additivity proves the claim for any finite union defining the line-cone.

Conversely, let g be any finite signed sum of full-dimensional simplicial cone indicators with T_g=0. Choose eta nonorthogonal to every generator in this finite list. For each cone, change the sign of each generator with negative eta-product. Flipping one generator gives the exact indicator relation modulo boundaries

    [cone(q,others)]+[cone(-q,others)]
       =[R q + cone(others)].

The right side is a line-cone. Iterating therefore gives g equivalent, modulo finite linear combinations of line-cones, to a signed function g_plus whose generators all have positive eta-product. Its valuation is still zero.

Let K be the cone generated by all those finitely many flipped generators. There is a delta>0 with <eta,x>>=delta|x| for x in K: take the minimum of <eta,q>/|q| on the finite list, then use the triangle inequality on nonnegative combinations. Therefore g_plus(x)exp(-<eta,x>) is in L^1(R^d). At every complex u=-eta+i xi its Fourier transform equals the genuine convergent cone integral T_{g_plus}(u), which is zero because the rational valuation is identically zero. No incompatible convergence domains are assumed for the original unflipped cones.

For completeness, Fourier uniqueness here needs no unproved polyhedral assertion. Convolving this L^1 function with a Gaussian gives zero, by the Gaussian Fourier formula and Fubini, since its Fourier transform is zero. The normalized Gaussian kernels form an approximate identity in L^1, so the function itself is zero almost everywhere. The strictly positive exponential factor then implies g_plus=0 almost everywhere. Hence g is a finite signed sum of line-cones.

We have proved the full kernel statement

    T_g=0 iff g is a finite real linear combination of line-cone indicators.

In particular c_w!=0 iff w is algebraic. The signed combination is essential; demanding that the tangent cone itself be one line-cone would not give this kernel. The proof also supplies the finite geometric version: after flipping and overlaying the finite facet hyperplanes, some full-dimensional chamber has nonzero signed coverage iff the point is algebraic.

## 4. No cancellation of a nonzero affine vertex pole

Fix v!=0 in W and L_v=1-<v,u>. Its zero hyperplane differs from the zero hyperplane of every other L_w and from every homogeneous edge denominator in c_w. In the rational identity in Section 2, multiply by L_v and restrict to a generic point of L_v=0. Only the v term remains, giving c_v restricted there.

If c_v is identically zero there is no L_v pole. If a nonzero homogeneous rational c_v vanished identically on <v,u>=1, then for every generic u with <v,u>!=0, scaling by 1/<v,u> would imply c_v(u)=0. The domain so obtained is nonempty and open, so its rational numerator would vanish identically, a contradiction. Thus c_v!=0 guarantees that L_v survives reduction. Distinct affine factors cannot cancel this residue, and homogeneous edge hyperplanes intersect the affine pole only on proper exceptional subsets. Section 1 already bounds the multiplicity by one.

Accordingly the entire reduced denominator is exactly the product over nonzero algebraic vertices. At the origin the nominal factor is 1 and supplies no affine pole, regardless of whether that tangent cone is algebraic. This is a necessary exception, not a missing pole.

## 5. Comparison with the literal source V(P)

For any triangulation, a point absent from its vertex set has, in each containing simplex, a tangent cone at a positive-dimensional face, hence a line-cone; all other tangent contributions are empty or ambient null. The total tangent indicator would then be a line-cone sum. Therefore every algebraic vertex belongs to every triangulation's vertex set: A(P) is a subset of V(P). Choosing one finite triangulation shows both sets are finite. This argument avoids identifying the source's face-to-face triangulations with any different dissection convention in the prior paper.

Comparing the distinct normalized nonzero linear factors gives the exact equivalence

    Omega_P=product_{v in V(P)}(1-<v,u>)
       iff V(P)\{0} is a subset of A(P).

## 6. Boundary and measure conventions

The proof applies to holes, overlaps, disconnected unions, and dependent point configurations by a genuine union triangulation, rather than counting overlapping pieces twice. For a purely lower-dimensional set, F=0 and all tangent indicators have zero ambient mass, so A is empty. Literal nonzero triangulation vertices can remain in V, and the requested equality then fails exactly as the criterion predicts. Lower-dimensional appendages to a full-dimensional set leave the transform unchanged but can add such nonalgebraic essential geometric vertices. This is why geometry of V and ambient measure must be kept distinct.

For opposite d-simplices meeting at their apex, the apex cone coefficient cancels when d is odd and survives when d is even. In odd d>=3 the apex is still needed by triangulations; its cone is not itself a line-cone, but its indicator is a signed line-cone sum. The origin case can satisfy the literal denominator equality despite this nonalgebraic apex, because its factor is already 1. Translating it to a nonzero point exposes the canceled nonconstant factor and makes equality fail. In d=1 the common interior apex is not an essential triangulation vertex, so this should not be presented as the three-dimensional geometric counterexample.

To check essentiality explicitly for d>=2, P minus its apex has two connected components. A full-dimensional simplex with that apex removed is connected, so a simplex contained in P lies on one side. On that side the apex is an extreme point, and any contained simplex containing it must have it as a vertex. A finite closed simplex cover must have a full-dimensional simplex containing the apex, since otherwise its positive distance from the finitely many such simplices would leave uncovered full-dimensional points arbitrarily close to the apex. Thus every triangulation contains the apex vertex. The one-dimensional argument fails exactly because a punctured interval can be disconnected.

Atomic or surface measure and general polynomial density are outside this theorem. For example an ambient atomic transform can have L_v^{d+1} in its reduced denominator, contradicting squarefreeness; this does not contradict the unit-Lebesgue proof. The zero-dimensional ambient case has only constant denominator and is immediate.
