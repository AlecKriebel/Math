## Independent falsification of the universal mechanism

I read the manuscript before ROOT_PRIORITY_SYNTHESIS.json and all three priority-family reports. My initial approach was to attack the label dictionary, cone lifts and the direction-level cusp reduction separately. I then inspected the relevant original source pages rather than treating audit conclusions as evidence.

**Claim and source conventions.** Fuchs's printed p.512 gives exactly the labelled sine ratio. Section 1.1 defines the sum-or-difference dihedral parallelism, and §2.2 explicitly selects sum for odd N and difference for even N before Definition 2.1. I checked the literal even and odd inequality branches, including the source's odd first-branch bound, against formula (2). Boundary sides give the coincident residues 0 and n-2. The weights use canonical 0,...,n-3 representatives; arbitrary-residue periodic sine evaluation would be wrong. Every canonical weight is positive, so the denominator cannot vanish. “Short” means first subsequent vertex, not a metric cutoff. The argument does not assume Fuchs's other conjectures or identify a known unlabelled spectrum with a prescribed label.

**Physical unfolding and gluing.** Reflecting across a side produces a translate of -P by the midpoint-half-turn/symmetry factorization just described. Copy parity is therefore P for odd segment count and Q for even count. The side pairing identifies P_j with Q_{j+1}; the corner cycle increments j while changing sheet. It has length 2n for odd n and length n in each of two classes for even n. This gives one angle 2pi(n-2), genus (n-1)/2, or two angles pi(n-2), genus (n-2)/2. Keeping the double even polygon is essential: replacing it with the smaller quotient would change these cone germs.

**Hyperelliptic commutation.** Interior points cannot be fixed by the sheet-exchanging half-turn. Each glued side midpoint is fixed. The unique odd vertex is fixed, while the two even vertices are exchanged. The fixed count is 2g+2 in the genus>=2 range used by the cusp proof, so Riemann–Hurwitz gives a sphere quotient. An affine conjugate of iota has derivative -I even when conjugated by a real affine map, hence is holomorphic away from zeros and extends across them in the local coordinate z=w^r. Its quotient is topologically a sphere. Uniqueness of the hyperelliptic involution in genus>=2 proves commutation. The proof's use of this uniqueness is confined to the nonsmall cases; n=3,4,6 are proved separately.

**Angular lifts and reversal.** For a positive real-linear derivative, its continuous angle lift has degree one and antipodality gives F(theta+pi)=F(theta)+pi. The lifted cone map differs by a single integer sheet offset at a given point, cancelling in a difference. Same-endpoint backward germs consequently preserve m from pi+2pi m. For different even endpoints, commutation first brings both germs to the same point via iota, so the offset still cancels. I checked that endpoint exchange by an affine map does not break this construction. Orientation reversal sends m to -m-1 in the equal-endpoint case and to -m in the different-endpoint case; K=2m+1-e therefore changes to -K.

**Corner dictionary and Q-chart sign.** At a P terminal corner, the backward germ has offset pi-beta; at a Q terminal corner it has the reversed offset beta-2pi/n. For even n, write terminal-P t=2a+e and terminal-Q t=2a+1-e after identifying the other cone by iota. Formula (9) then gives respectively mn=a(n-2)+e(n-1)-s and mn=a(n-2)+s+e-2. Reducing modulo n-2 yields K=k in both parity cases. For odd n the analogous formulas with e=0 give 2m=k-1. Changing a full-cone lift contributes an integer multiple of nr, divisible by n-2 in both ranges. This removes a potentially hidden sheet-choice dependence.

A physical reflection changes alpha to delta-alpha and beta to pi+2pi/n-beta. Its sum parameter becomes n-s and difference parameter 2-s, so its source label is -k, independently of segment parity. Any orientation-reversing Q-to-P folding chart is a polygon reflection composed with iota, up to polygon symmetry normalization. Thus the physical label is k=sigma K. Physical path reversal has alpha'=pi-beta and beta'=pi-alpha: for odd N it negates k; for even N it preserves k. The pentagon palindrome therefore disproves the original unconditional physical-label-negation assertion. Canonical negation gives -k=n-2-k for k>0, so the sine weight becomes sin((n-1-k)pi/n)=sin((k+1)pi/n), with k=0 immediate. The repaired proof transports this weight, not an unqualified physical label.

**Actual Veech imports and whole-direction exhaustion.** I visually read Veech 1989 Proposition 2.10 (printed p.561), the double-polygon/hyperelliptic model pp.564–565, the group and parabolic cylinder construction pp.566–568, and the cusp fundamental-domain statements pp.572–573. Its k=1 model is the manuscript's actual double regular n-gon. These establish lattice saddle directions as parabolic, with one cusp for odd n and two for even n, represented by adjacent symmetry directions. Finster §§3.1–3.2, Remarks 3.2/3.5 and p.20 corroborate them, with the quotient/cover distinction stated above. A cusp-orbit representative is reached by one affine map acting on the entire direction; choosing different maps for different connections would not establish the cancellation and is unnecessary here.

For either model direction, reflection in the perpendicular polygon symmetry axis pairs every off-axis vertex with another vertex on the same parallel line. Convexity makes the inward ray hit that paired vertex before a side interior. An on-axis vertex is extremal in the perpendicular-axis direction, so the parallel line through it supports the polygon and provides no interior ray. Boundary rays follow sides to the next vertex. This applies to every corner of P and Q, so there are no additional crossing-polygon model connections hidden behind the asserted chord spectrum. The direct chord j has length 2R sin(jpi/n), alpha=(n-1-j)pi/n, beta=(n+1-j)pi/n, and k=j-1 modulo n-2. Its second boundary side j=n-1 wraps to k=0 with the same weight. Consequently every model connection satisfies L=2R w_n(K). A common derivative multiplies all vectors in a common unoriented direction by the same positive factor, so that factor cancels between any two connections.

**Source parallelism and small cases.** Polygon rotation/reflection aligns the sum/difference source directions modulo pi, while reversing an oriented connection causes no weight change. For odd n the double-polygon pi/n rotation exchanging polygons is well-defined and accounts for the possible half-step; for even n only 2pi/n steps are needed. I checked that such maps preserve the side gluings, not merely the planar vertex sets. For n=3 the primitive triangular-lattice endpoint gives one length in a source family and there is one type. For n=4 the primitive square endpoint and both-odd parity type rule are preserved by square symmetry. For n=6 the full triangular lattice is not entirely marked: vertices satisfy p+q !=2 mod3. A primitive residue-2 vector hits 2v first, while residues 0 and 1 hit v. Fuchs §2.3 gives weights sqrt3/2, 1/2, 1 respectively; the norm p^2-pq+q^2 and primitive condition survive the dihedral generators. Zero residue stays zero, and the other two residues interchange. Thus L/w is constant in either family. I found no omitted primitive, negative-coordinate, reflection, boundary, or small-n case.
