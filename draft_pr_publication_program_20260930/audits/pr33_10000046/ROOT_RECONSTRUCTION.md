## Universal finite and infinite theorem

There are L=(2d)^N labeled length-N increment words on each side, each with probability 1/L. Vertex paths uniquely recover their words; equal ranges must retain multiplicity. Put an edge between words exactly when their full ranges through N, including time zero, are disjoint. A finite coupling scaled by L is doubly stochastic. Its positive support satisfies Hall: mass |A| from left A enters columns of total capacity |N(A)|. An alternating-path maximum-matching argument proves Hall yields a perfect support matching. Subtract the smallest positive entry on that permutation, renormalize the residual, and repeat; the number of positive entries strictly decreases. This proves the required finite permutation decomposition rather than relying on its name as evidence.

Each permutation's allowed edges form a matching, so the allowed mass is at most M_N/L, with M_N the maximum allowed matching size. Complete a maximum allowed matching by any bijection between remaining labels. It cannot add another allowed edge without contradicting maximal size. Thus it attains exactly a_N=M_N/L with both complete prefix marginal laws. Starting alternating paths at unmatched left vertices gives a Hall set whose deficiency equals L−M_N; every subset has deficiency at most that number. Therefore a_N=1−max_A(|A|−|N(A)|)/L. The neighborhood is the union of right words avoiding at least one member of A, not the intersection of those neighborhoods.

Identify complete paths with the compact metrizable finite-alphabet increment product. Fixed full-law couplings form a closed, weakly compact set: joint cylinder probabilities admit a consistent diagonal subsequence, while marginal cylinder probabilities stay fixed. Every finite optimizer extends to a full-law coupling by adding two independent iid tails, independent of the coupled prefixes. This makes any prescribed longer marginal word have probability (2d)^−(N+k).

Let R_k be full-range avoidance through k. These events are clopen and decrease to R, the all-time event. Restriction gives a_(N+1)≤a_N. Select a weak subsequence of full extensions of finite optimizers, indexed by N_j→∞. For fixed k and eventually N_j≥k, their R_k mass is at least a_(N_j)≥inf_N a_N. The fixed clopen indicator passes to the weak limit; continuity from above then gives limit-coupling R mass at least inf_N a_N. Every coupling's R mass is at most a_N for all N. Hence the maximum infinite avoidance probability equals lim_N a_N=inf_N a_N and is attained. Moving-event continuity, projective consistency of chosen finite optimizers, or only one-time marginal laws are not used.

This proof applies to all d≥1, all starts and N≥0. Shared starts have optimum zero, including N=0; distinct starts have a_0=1. Unequal masses instead require the weighted Hall deficiency p(A)−q(N(A)); the independent family's rational transport controls verify that distinction. It is not necessary to promote that extension as a new result here.
