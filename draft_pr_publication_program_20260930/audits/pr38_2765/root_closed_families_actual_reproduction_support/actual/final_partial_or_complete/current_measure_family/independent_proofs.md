## A. Boundary necessity; no converse

A measured lamination λ≠0 is represented by a ray in the current realization of Thurston's boundary. Hence there are X_n and t_n>0 for which t_n L_{X_n}→λ. This follows from the current compactification, not just an asymptotic comparison for finitely many curves. In more detail, normalize projective representatives by a fixed positive filling intersection functional; compactness supplies convergent representatives. The properness of the Liouville embedding and its fixed self-intersection force a divergent sequence's scaling to zero, whence its limiting self-intersection is zero. Such a positive current is a measured lamination, by the disjoint-support construction. Identifying the topology with Thurston's compactification makes every measured-lamination ray approachable. Continuity on the closed surface then yields

    i(μ,λ)=lim t_n i(μ,L_{X_n})=lim t_n ell_{X_n}(a)=i(a,λ).

The zero lamination is immediate. This argument never divides by i(a,λ), so it includes disjoint/zero intersection cases that can invalidate careless ratio proofs.

A genuine counterfamily for the converse is Leininger's genus-two finite-cover construction. An immersed filling base curve γ₀ cuts the surface into two right-angled octagons. In a double cover it has an embedded lift a; a lift b of a nonseparating curve and a connecting arc define an embedded pair of pants. The curves represented there by a^(-2) b a b^(-1) and a^(-2) b a b descend as filling curves γ,γ'. Their six essential pants-arc intersection counts are (3,2,3,4,4,6) for both. A simple curve decomposes into these arc types, and lifting simple curves to the finite cover proves equal intersection against every simple curve downstairs. Density extends this to every measured lamination. Their downstairs integral homology classes are -[γ₀] and -[γ₀]+2[b]; these differ even up to sign. If they had equal all-hyperbolic-metric lengths, their squared trace functions would agree on the Fuchsian open real locus, then by analyticity and irreducibility on all complex representations. Abelian diagonal representations would force equal homology up to sign, a contradiction. Thus (2.1) is strictly weaker even for filling counting currents. This is an actual geometric construction with checked hypotheses, not a toy linear-algebra analogy.

## B. Simple reference rigidity

Let a=mα with m a positive integer and α essential and simple. Cut along α. Each component has positive genus: a separating α cannot bound a disk on a closed surface, and a nonseparating cut has genus g-1≥1. In a compact surface of positive genus with one or two boundary components, choose an interior pants decomposition and, for every internal cuff, a simple dual curve. Each dual is contained in the union of its neighboring pants (or the once-holed torus obtained when the same pants occurs twice); use one transverse passage or a pair as topology requires. Add further duals if necessary so that every non-boundary component is met. The pants seams selected by these duals cut each pants into disks and annular collars of the actual boundary. Thus a finite relative filling collection exists, all disjoint from α. Equivalently this is the usual pants-and-duals construction of relative filling; it does not require a false single globally simple filling curve.

By A, μ has zero intersection with α and every chosen simple curve. If a support geodesic h crossed a lift of one chosen curve δ, choose small crossing boxes U,V in geodesic space whose product injects into the transverse-pair quotient. Positivity and support give μ(U)>0 and δ(V)>0, hence i(μ,δ)≥μ(U)δ(V)>0, contradiction. So support geodesics cannot cross any selected lift, including at vertices of the filling graph.

The lifts of disk regions have compact closures; no complete hyperbolic geodesic lies in one. A lift of a compact boundary-parallel annulus stays in a uniformly bounded neighborhood of its boundary axis. A complete geodesic in that neighborhood has that axis's two ideal endpoints, so is the same axis. A geodesic along an interior selected curve is crossed by a dual and is excluded. At an intersection vertex, a line cannot be tangent to two transverse selected lines simultaneously, so it crosses at least one. The only remaining supported geodesics are the lifts of α. Radon local finiteness and deck invariance give a single weight b on this discrete orbit, μ=bα. Testing one metric gives b=m. This proves the original Proposition 2, including separating/nonseparating genus-two edge cases.

The proof breaks for a self-intersecting filling a because there need be no essential simple curve disjoint from a. Replacing this gap by an unsupported support classification would be circular.

## C. Compact convex fiber and atomicity gap

F_a is nonempty because it contains a, convex by bilinearity, and closed because each all-metric equality defines a closed affine level set. Fix X₀ and q=ell_{X₀}(a)>0. To directly verify the compactness needed here, for every geodesic h choose a geodesic k in supp(L_{X₀}) crossing h, and small boxes U_h,V_k whose product injects into the transverse-pair quotient. For μ in the nonnegative sublevel i(μ,L_{X₀})≤q,

    μ(U_h) ≤ q/L_{X₀}(V_k).

The denominator is positive. A compact K⊂G(H²) has a finite cover by U_h, giving a uniform bound on μ(K). Weak compactness of positive Radon measures on a locally compact second-countable space then supplies subsequential limits, and invariance is closed. On the closed surface intersection continuity makes the sublevel closed. Thus the sublevel, and its closed subset F_a, is compact. This is the mechanism of the operative theorem, with all surface and positivity hypotheses retained. BIPP Theorem 2.4 restricts one argument to a compact carrier; here K=S, so it applies to all currents. Proposition 2.6(1) likewise takes compact K=S and proves compactness of the normalized slice. The statement is not used with K=S noncompact.

Actual falsification of the inference “compact length slice implies atomicity”: the same compact slice contains q L_{X₀}/i(L_{X₀},L_{X₀}), a nonzero non-atomic current. It need not be in F_a; that is exactly why additional all-metric information is essential. Another actual current counterfamily to finite-orbit support from finite length is Σ 2^(-n) δ_{a_n}/ell_{X₀}(a_n), for infinitely many distinct primitive closed curves a_n. Its flow measure has total mass one, hence defines a current, yet has infinitely many distinct periodic-orbit components. Neither example solves the target fiber.

Even an extreme-point theorem would not by itself imply finite convex representability. In P([0,1]), the extreme points are Dirac measures, but Lebesgue probability is only in their closed convex hull. For any alleged finite atom set {x₁,...,x_r}, the nonnegative polynomial Π_j(t-x_j)² has zero integral against that atomic measure and strictly positive integral against Lebesgue measure. This checkable counterexample eliminates a finite Carathéodory inference in an infinite-dimensional current space. A singleton consisting of any non-atomic measure is also a compact convex set with a non-atomic extreme point, refuting the claim that compactness alone identifies extremes.

No theorem proving finite closed-orbit support or finite convex normalization for the nonsimple target has been supplied. Compactness and Krein–Milman routes are blocked at that specific unsupported step, not because the space is merely difficult.

## D. Complete finite-area punctured convention

Let S be the sphere minus three marked points, and metrics be complete finite-area curvature-minus-one metrics without geodesic boundary. Uniformization and three-point Möbius normalization give a single marked Teichmuller point X. This is uniqueness, not merely the fact that the dimension formula is zero. Orientation-preserving pure mapping classes add no further parameter; cusp permutations are realized by symmetries of the ideal pants. Varying three positive geodesic-boundary lengths instead gives a different three-parameter metric space.

The Liouville measure L on unordered distinct endpoint pairs is invariant under all H² isometries. In finite endpoint charts its density is proportional to da db/(a-b)². Every compact subset of geodesic space stays away from the diagonal in finitely many circle charts; the density is smooth and bounded there. Thus L is a nonzero non-atomic Radon current invariant under Γ, even though its total mass on the entire geodesic space is not required to be finite.

Define i on arbitrary noncompact Radon currents by the product-measure mass of the Γ-quotient of transverse geodesic pairs; its values may be +∞ and it need not be jointly continuous. For the particular pair (L,L), use crossing coordinates s along a fixed geodesic and θ∈(0,π). Standard normalization gives dL=(1/2) sinθ ds dθ, whose θ integral is one, so crossing mass of a segment is its hyperbolic length. For a current μ, integrating the second L variable therefore gives its transverse current measure times arclength on PT(S). For μ=L this local measure is (1/2) dA dθ, θ modulo π. Hence, by monotone integration over an exhaustion of S,

    i(L,L) = (π/2) area(X) = π² |χ(S)| = π².

This establishes both finiteness and positivity directly. It uses the finite area assumption, not a false general finite-intersection property for all noncompact Radon currents. It also avoids confusion between oriented T¹ volume 2π area=4π² and the current's intersection normalization. BIPP Examples 2.1(1), 2.2(2) supply the relevant Radon and Crofton normalization; Bonahon's A1–A3 and Proposition 15 give the full local computation, whose local formula extends without compactness. Trin's Section 3.1 verifies the finite oriented Liouville volume under its separate compact-core convention.

Take A=[[1,2],[0,1]], B=[[1,0],[2,1]] in Γ(2), viewed in PSL₂. Γ(2)'s modular index is six, it is torsion-free in PSL₂, and has three width-two cusps, so H²/Γ(2) is this surface. AB=[[5,2],[2,1]], trace six, eigenvalues 3±2√2; it is hyperbolic and represents a nonperipheral closed geodesic a of length 2 arcosh 3. Set c=(2 arcosh 3/π²)L. Then i(c,L)=ell_X(a), and uniqueness of X makes this equality of the entire stated metric functions. A finite positive, hence also finite convex, sum of closed counting currents is purely atomic on a countable union of lift orbits. The nonzero c is non-atomic and has zero mass on that set, so no such representation exists.

This counterexample only applies when the current space permits this cusped Liouville current. Trin Proposition 2.2 explicitly changes to currents on a compact hyperbolic core with geodesic boundary. There every peripheral winding sequence has uniformly bounded intersection with a fixed current, but its cusped hyperbolic lengths diverge, excluding a Liouville representative. The proposition is not a nonexistence theorem for Radon Liouville currents on the cusped interior. Conversely, if one requires a current to have finite intersection with every Radon current (including an ideal cusp-to-cusp current), L is excluded: its intersection with that infinite-length geodesic is infinite. Thus the admissible current class must be specified. The original Proposition 4 specifies the needed class and does not borrow closed-surface continuity or compactness for it.
