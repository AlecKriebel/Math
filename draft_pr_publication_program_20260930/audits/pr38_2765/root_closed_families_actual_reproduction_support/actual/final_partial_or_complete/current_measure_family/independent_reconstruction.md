# Independent reconstruction before original results or reviews

The literal problem asks: if a positive geodesic current on a hyperbolic surface has the same length function on the full space of hyperbolic metrics as a closed geodesic, must it be a convex combination of closed curves? The surface class and the meaning of convex combination are not specified in the literal statement. A finite convex combination and a closed convex hull are distinct claims. Closed surfaces and finite-area punctured surfaces require distinct current/intersection hypotheses.

Let C(S) denote positive, invariant Radon measures on unoriented geodesics in the universal cover. On a closed hyperbolic surface, set F_a={c : i(c,L_X)=ell_X(a) for every X in Teich(S)}. The intended exact claim is F_a contained in finite convex combinations of counting currents of closed curves. Deductions to test: F_a is nonempty and convex; continuity of intersection makes it closed; one fixed filling Liouville current bounds it in a compact current-length slice. None of these topological facts prove finite atomic support. A singleton fiber for a simple a would be a valid boundary case but cannot settle arbitrary nonsimple a.

Thurston degeneration X_n with positive scalars t_n and t_n L_{X_n} converging to a measured lamination lambda suggests i(c,lambda)=i(a,lambda), assuming the relevant convergence and intersection continuity. This is only a necessary condition until independently proved otherwise. Distinct closed curves may agree against all simple curves yet have different full hyperbolic length functions: seek a primary theorem and actual examples rather than infer a converse.

For a simple a, equality against measured laminations implies i(c,a)=0. Then c has no support geodesics crossing a. One must still eliminate currents entirely contained in complementary components or limiting leaves; this requires a filling test in each complementary component and an invariant-Radon argument for recurrent support. Any proof using only i(c,a)=0 is incomplete.

Noncompact surfaces: local finiteness of a measure does not imply finite global intersection, and compactness of length slices may fail by mass escaping into cusps. A rigid surface with Teichmuller space a point is a falsification family for any unrestricted all-surfaces assertion: matching one scalar length imposes no atomicity. The thrice-punctured sphere is the natural candidate, subject to checking Liouville current Radon-ness and finite positive self-intersection. No nonsimple target solution search will be attempted beyond this audit of original scope.

A convex compact fiber can have non-atomic extreme points; Krein-Milman does not identify its extreme points or turn closed convex combinations into finite sums. A finite-support assertion would need an additional theorem specific to F_a. Any assumption that all extreme points are counting currents merely transfers the central problem.
