# Initial independent universal reconstruction

The residue multiplication is bitwise XOR: writing c=u+2v and d=s+2t, the ordinary low-bit carry 2us is canceled by 2cd modulo 4. Thus the high bit is a circle-group character. The sign action on the trivial brace Z gives an associative circle group on all integer inputs, with inverse (-epsilon(c)n,c). The independent coefficient checker uses three formal integer basis vectors; equality of those coefficient tuples certifies equality for every n,m,l in Z, while exhausting every residue triple. This is not a finite-quotient argument.

Subtracting a in the abelian additive group gives lambda_(n,c)(m,d)=(epsilon(c)m,(-1)^c d). Subtracting b gives a*b=((epsilon(c)-1)m,2cd mod4). These are diagonal additive automorphisms, so the left brace law holds universally.

For z=(n,c), H imposes (epsilon(c)-1)m=0 and 2cd=0 mod4. The condition g*z=0 imposes (epsilon(d)-1)n=0 and 2cd=0; multiplicative commutation additionally imposes (epsilon(c)-1)m=(epsilon(d)-1)n. Therefore K imposes all three zero equations. For c=0,1, m is arbitrary and both subgroups contain Z x {0}; residue cosets give the exact table (0,0):1/1; (n!=0,0):1/2; (0,1):2/2; (n!=0,1):2/4. For c=2,3, m=0 for H and K. Their residue subsets are diamond subgroups; H is also an additive subgroup. Arbitrary integer representatives (j,0) yield pairwise distinct additive and multiplicative cosets, since differences and circle quotients equal (j-i,0). Hence both indices are infinite.

Consequently S=Z x {0,1}. x=(0,1) is in S; 2x=(0,2) is not. Also -x=lambda_x(x)=(0,3) is not. S is the multiplicative sign-kernel and is normal of index 2. The right brace identity fails at u=v=(0,1), w=(1,0), with respective values (-1,2) and (1,2). The example is not globally (S). Every finite quotient is globally (S), so truncation cannot establish infinite index.
