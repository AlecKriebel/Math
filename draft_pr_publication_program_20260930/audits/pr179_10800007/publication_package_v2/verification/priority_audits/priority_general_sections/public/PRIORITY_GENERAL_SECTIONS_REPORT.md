## Result supported by the actual evidence

The literal all-R6 exact/open problem has no admissible cover of any cardinality. Established classical topology gives a short, fully checked route to that conclusion. The strongest general-theorem evidence found is invariance of domain, which applies directly to the full incidence map after the elementary target-specific chart/duplicate-pair checks in GENERAL_THEOREM_APPLICATION.md. Later published primary bodies explicitly warn that a singular point lacking a local section makes an open section-cover formulation fail.

## Literal target and authenticated formulation

For p=(a1,a2,a0,b1,b2,b0)∈R6, require continuous exact real-root selections for

x²−y²+a1x+a2y+a0=0,  xy+b1x+b2y+b0=0.

An admissible U is open in all R6. The incidence space E⊂R6×R2 carries projection P:E→R6. A selection is a strict section p↦(p,selection(p)).

Vassiliev's final published Section 2, printed p. 205/PDF page 5, expressly specifies the full coefficient space, ordinary open sets, and exact solutions; the next problem 2B expressly switches to approximation. The following finite square-root hint supplies no punctured-domain qualification. Both the entire relevant page and its rendered image were read. The hint's assertion of two is consistent with punctured or filtered square-root domains but inconsistent with ordinary open sections over all C. This is a diagnosis of the written domains, not an assertion about intended repair. [Final primary PDF, DOI 10.1007/s40598-015-0011-9](https://armj.math.stonybrook.edu/pdf-Springer-final/015-0011-9.pdf).

## Checkable application and boundary controls

The global chart E≅R6 uses q=(a1,a2,b1,b2,x,y), with a0=−x²+y²−a1x−a2y and b0=−xy−b1x−b2y. In this chart P becomes Q:R6→R6. Q^-1(0) is singleton. For everyt>0, q±=(0,0,0,0,±t,0) map to the same coefficients and converge tozero. A section σ on openU is injective; invariance of domain makes σ(U) open around zero, so it contains both q± for small t, although Q must be injective onσ(U). Contradiction. Full target checks are in GENERAL_THEOREM_APPLICATION.md. This route is wholly on the full coefficient base.

The slice (a1,a2,a0,b1,b2,b0)=(0,0,−u,0,0,−v/2) is exactly z²=u+iv. A disk square root would restrict to h:S1→S1 with h²=id, giving2deg(h)=1. Covering-space lifting similarly fails on a surrounding circle; the square map is a genuine covering only after zero is removed. Any admissible open cover, finite or infinite, must have a member containingzero and therefore is impossible. The precise statement is that the class of admissible covers is empty. Assigning+∞ by inf∅ is a convention, not evidence that an infinite cover exists.

The target cannot be a Hurewicz fibration: pulling back to the square slice would give a fibration with one-point fiber at zero and a two-point fiber elsewhere, incompatible with the fiber-homotopy equivalence property over a path-connected base (Pavešić 2020p1340). Fibration-only finite-genus and homotopy-to-strict results cannot be invoked here. Mere criticality does not suffice: x↦x³ onR has a continuous inverse despite its vanishing derivative. The target's actual square pullback or duplicate-pair certificate is needed.

Homotopy sectional category over contractibleR6 is0normalized/1unnormalized: a constant map to a point ofE has composite homotopic to the identity ofR6. This is our calculation, not a source-quoted value. Allowing arbitrary or filtered domains also changes the slice problem: the complement of a closed ray is an open branch domain, and the remaining ray includingzero admits a continuous strict square root, giving a two-piece filtration. PuncturedC has two open slit branches and no global branch. For anyε>0 the constant valuezero is withinε of square roots on |w|<ε², so the exact local obstruction does not settle2B. None of these examples computes a repaired invariant on the full target.
