## Claim calibration

The six coefficients range independently over R6 in f=x^2-y^2+a1*x+a2*y+a0 and g=x*y+b1*x+b2*y+b0. A member of an admissible cover must be open in the coefficient space and support a continuous selection of an exact common real root at every point. An exact historical overlap must state this no-cover/local-no-section result, explicitly correct the printed formulation, or give an equivalent theorem for the full family. A theorem about global branches on a punctured parameter domain does not itself make that exact-target historical statement.

For mathematical checking, restrict to (a1,a2,a0,b1,b2,b0)=(0,0,-u,0,0,-v/2). Writing z=x+i*y, the equations become z^2=u+i*v. Every open neighborhood of zero in R6 pulls back to a disk about zero. On any sufficiently small nonzero circle, a continuous exact selection would give a circle map of integer degree k whose square has degree 2k=1. This is impossible. The slice has exactly the square roots and no escape roots; openness at zero is decisive. Thus no cover member can contain zero. This deduction neither requires a numerical computation nor depends on the total number or algebraic multiplicities of off-slice roots.

To discover differently named versions of the whole family, the audit also used the harmonic-polynomial form. Direct substitution gives f+2i*g=z^2+alpha*z+beta*conjugate(z)+gamma, with alpha=(a1+2b2+i*(2b1-a2))/2, beta=(a1-2b2+i*(2b1+a2))/2, gamma=a0+2i*b0. These are three independent complex coefficients. Root-count/caustic literature for harmonic polynomials is therefore relevant discovery, but counting or locating roots is a different success criterion from selecting a root on ordinary open neighborhoods.
