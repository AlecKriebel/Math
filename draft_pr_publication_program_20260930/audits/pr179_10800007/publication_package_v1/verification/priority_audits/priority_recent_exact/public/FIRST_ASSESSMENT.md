## Exact claim and success criteria

The target is the six-real-coefficient family f=x^2-y^2+a1*x+a2*y+a0, g=x*y+b1*x+b2*y+b0 on all R^6. An ordinary open-set cover of that entire base must support continuous exact real-root selections. Exact-target overlap means a publicly stated theorem, correction, counterexample, or explicit explanation ruling out a local section at zero and hence every such cover. General square-root monodromy, a genus computation over a discriminant complement, approximate roots, and homotopy sections are narrower or different evidence and must not be conflated.

## Mathematical assessment independent of priority

On the constant slice (a1,a2,a0,b1,b2,b0)=(0,0,-u,0,0,-v/2), the root equations become (x+i*y)^2=u+i*v. Every ordinary open neighborhood of the zero coefficient vector pulls back to a complex disk about zero. Its restriction to a small nonzero circle would give a map whose square is the identity circle map. The resulting degree equality 2*deg(s)=1 is impossible. Equivalently a lifted closed loop changes sign. The zero slice has no extra roots that could evade the obstruction. Thus no member of an admissible cover can contain zero, and no admissible cover exists even with infinite cardinality. This is an elementary deduction using a classical method, not a novelty conclusion.

The final journal p.205 and arXiv v1 p.5 both explicitly retain all R^6. Problem 2B separately changes exactness. The source itself invokes the complex square equation and claims covering number two, which is correct over the punctured plane but incompatible with a local section near zero under the printed ordinary-open definition. Source formulation consistency is therefore a central issue, not evidence of a difficult new theorem.
