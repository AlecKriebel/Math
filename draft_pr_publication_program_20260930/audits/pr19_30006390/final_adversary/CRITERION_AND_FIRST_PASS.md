## Every mathematical baseline claim

**Incidence and exceptional events.** A finite plane of integer order q≥2 has n points and n lines, each line and point incident with q+1. Distinct lines share one point and distinct points determine one line. For a fixed line, empty and full events each have probability 2^(-(q+1)). Union bounds each give n times this number; their simultaneous complement fails with probability at most 2n·2^(-(q+1)). No mutual independence between line events is needed. On no-empty, hitting line sections with B⊂R is precisely ordinary blocking; on no-full, no B⊂R contains a line. These reductions are valid for every existing finite plane, with errors depending on q only and hence uniform. For small q the combined good event may be absent; the claim is asymptotic, not a finite universal lower bound for all R.

**Bruen lower bound.** For any ordinary blocker B with no line, let k=|B| and a=k-q-1. Incidence counting gives k≥ceil(n/(q+1))=q+1, so a≥0. For any L choose x∈L\B. The q other lines through x require q distinct points of B\L, giving 1≤r_L≤k-q=a+1. Therefore r_L(r_L-1)≤(a+1)(r_L-1). The sums of r_L, 1, and r_L(r_L-1) are k(q+1), n, and k(k-1), respectively. Right minus left after substitution is exactly q(a²-q), so a≥√q. The integer conclusion is k≥q+1+ceil(√q). The no-full hypothesis is essential; a complete line has size q+1 and blocks every line. The proof uses no Desarguesian coordinates, prime-power construction, minimality, or equality classification. Positive primary attribution is available in Bruen's own 1980 paper, Theorem 1, explicitly citing his 1970 and 1971 originals; the SIAM publisher record identifies the 1971 original. This is classical, not novel. The ratio of this lower bound to q tends to 1.

**Concentration and alteration.** For D=√(3(q+1)log q), Hoeffding gives P(|X_L-(q+1)/2|>D)≤2q^-6 for each X_L∼Bin(q+1,1/2). A union bound gives simultaneous line concentration with failure ≤2nq^-6=O(q^-4), uniformly over all planes. Hence m≥(q+1)/2-D and m=(1+o(1))q/2. Independently of any line independence, |R|∼Bin(n,1/2); using d=√(3n log q) gives size failure ≤2q^-6 and |R|=(1+o(1))n/2. Relative errors are O(√(log q/q)) and O(√(log q/n)). On this event m>log(q+1)>0 for all sufficiently large q, so ρ=log(q+1)/m∈(0,1). Given R, the temporary point samples are independent. A line miss has probability (1-ρ)^|L∩R|≤exp(-ρm)=1/(q+1); line miss events need not be mutually independent. Adding a point from each missed nonempty section gives size no greater than |T| plus the number of missed lines; repeated added points only help. Thus some outcome has integer size ≤floor(ρ|R|+n/(q+1)). The ratio |R|/m=(1+o(1))n/q=(1+o(1))q, n/(q+1)=q+1/(q+1), and log(q+1)~log q. The universal upper bound (1+o(1))q log q follows, with natural logs. This is a standard alteration proof, not a growing lower bound. It is not asserted with the chosen ρ at all finite q.

**Minimal blocker equivalence and probability bound.** On no-empty, τ≤k iff some ordinary inclusion-minimal blocker of size at most k lies in R. One direction takes a minimal subset of a finite feasible B; the other uses the contained blocker directly. No exclusion of line blockers is made in M_q(k). For every fixed B, P(B⊂R)=2^-|B|. Off no-empty, pay at most n·2^(-(q+1)); union bound then proves the candidate's weighted sum. Correlations and multiplicities are irrelevant to this upper bound. All k can be interpreted by floor k, including k<0 (empty family and τ≤k false). For every fixed C>0, a uniform vanishing weighted sum at floor(Cq) is sufficient for τ/q→∞ in probability. It is not necessary: a union bound can vastly overcount intersecting containment events. For comparison, unrestricted subsets give sum_{j≤k} binom(n,j)2^-j, whose top term already grows like exp(Cq log q+O_C(q)) at k~Cq. The displayed binom(n,k)2^-k is a diagnosis of the failure of unrestricted counting, not an asserted enumeration of all blockers of size≤k.

To make the deterministic f explicit, suppose e_j(q):=sup_planes P(τ/q≤j)→0 for every integer j≥1. Choose increasing thresholds Q_j with e_j(q)≤1/j for q≥Q_j; set f(q)=max{j:Q_j≤q}. Then f→∞ and failure probability ≤1/f(q). This diagonal construction does not prove e_j(q)→0. The missing weighted-enumeration estimate is equivalent in difficulty to an unsupported route here and remains blocked; it is not promoted as partial evidence for divergence. A fixed C alone proves only a constant-ratio exclusion, not the conjecture.

**Correlated model.** Two distinct empty-line events jointly require exclusion of 2q+1 distinct points, so joint probability is 2^(-(2q+1)), twice the product 2^(-(2q+2)). More strongly, conditioning on B⊂R for a fixed ordinary blocker B makes every section hit B with probability 1. Alon's partial-design proof fixes an arbitrary B and multiplies independent per-line incidence failures; in this point model, failure events share points and the feasibility conditioning kills them. This is a falsification of transfer of that proof, not proof that no probabilistic argument can work.
