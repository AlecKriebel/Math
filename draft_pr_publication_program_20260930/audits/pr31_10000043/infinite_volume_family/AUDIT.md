## 1. Conditional propagation: universal argument

Fix an oriented base edge (u\to w), and write (F_z=\{z\}\times\mathbb Z). Let \(\mathcal A_w\) be the sigma-field generated by precisely the edges whose endpoints both lie outside (F_w). The complement includes every horizontal edge 
\[
h_n=\{(u,n),(w,n)\},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The product measure conditional on \(\mathcal A_w\) retains the mutually independent Bernoulli((p)) states of all these edges. This is a statement about the independent coordinates and the whole deleted fiber, not about conditioning on a completed cluster.

Enumerate the product vertices once and index each cluster of the revealed graph by its least vertex. This is a measurable countable indexing. Conditional on the revealed graph, a particular revealed cluster (D) with infinitely many vertices in (F_u) supplies a fixed infinite set (H_D\subseteq\mathbb Z). For (0<p<1), its bridge states have infinitely many successes almost surely. For example, for any integer (r), enumerate the first (m) coordinates of (H_D\setminus[-r,r]); the probability that all are closed is ((1-p)^m\to0). The event of only finitely many successes is contained in the countable union, over (r), of events with no success outside ([-r,r]), and hence has conditional probability zero. At (p=1) every bridge is open. Taking the countable union over the revealed-cluster indices gives one conditional full-probability event for every such (D).

Suppose a full open cluster (C) has (|C\cap F_u|=\infty) and (S=C\cap F_w) finite. If (S=\varnothing), its complement is connected. If (S\ne\varnothing), every component of (C\setminus S) has an edge into (S), by connectedness of (C). The number of those edges is at most
\[
\sum_{s\in S}\deg_{G\times\mathbb Z}(s)<\infty.
\]
There are therefore finitely many components, and at least one, (D), meets (F_u) infinitely. It is a full cluster of the revealed graph: any additional open path avoiding (F_w) would lie in (C) and belong to the same remainder component. The conditional conclusion supplies infinitely many open bridges from this (D) to distinct vertices of (F_w), all in (C), contradicting finite (S).

There are countably many oriented base edges. Intersecting the corresponding full-probability events proves the adjacency implication simultaneously for every full cluster. Connectedness propagates it along each finite base path, so simultaneously every cluster either has infinite intersection with every fiber or finite intersection with every fiber, with zero allowed. This uses local finiteness at the finite-deletion step, countability at both random-cluster indexing and the final intersection, and neither (p_c(G)=1) nor a uniform degree bound.

Independent finite controls enumerate 144 exposed-cluster/probability combinations on a five-height graph. Every conditional bridge success-count law is exactly binomial at (p=1/3,1/2,2/3). A separate exact conditioning control has a full cluster known to avoid the neighboring fiber: each candidate bridge then has conditional open probability zero, rather than (p). A deterministic non-locally-finite product construction has base (u-a_i-w\) for countably many (i\), with (C\cap F_u=\{(u,i):i\ge1\}\) and (C\cap F_w=\{(w,0)\}\). Deleting ((w,0)) leaves countably infinitely many finite branches. This refutes replacing local finiteness by countability. It is outside the target's assumptions and is not an iid Bernoulli counterexample.

## 2. Capped exploration: separately checked universal argument

Fix a root (x), cap (M\ge1), and the written deterministic breadth-first rule. Every fresh query is incident to a vertex already accepted. For base edge (e=\{u,v\}), a queried horizontal product edge has a height in the union of the accepted-height sets in (F_u,F_v). Each has at most (M) elements and each height identifies exactly one product edge over (e). The no-repeat-query rule therefore bounds the total number of queries above (e) by (2M), independently of all interleaving and of vertex degrees away from these two fibers.

Assign (2M) independent Bernoulli((p)) inputs to each base edge, with independent vertical coordinates. At each fresh query the next unused input is selected from past outcomes only. Its conditional law is Bernoulli((p)) independent of the past. Induction proves equality of every finite transcript law with the original product model. This induction applies even when the selected *height* depends on outcomes from other reservoirs; the unused input label has not been selected by looking at its value.

For each (e), let (Y_e) be the maximum of **all** (2M) inputs, including unused ones. The (Y_e) are mutually independent, as functions of disjoint independent reservoirs, and have the same parameter
\[
q_M=1-(1-p)^{2M}<1\quad(p<1).
\]
Each accepted vertex has an accepted ancestry path from (x). Every horizontal step on it has a (Y)-open projected edge, and vertical steps preserve the base vertex. Thus every accepted base vertex lies in the (Y)-cluster of the base root. The hypothesis (p_c(G)=1) implies that this component is finite almost surely. With at most (M) accepted vertices per fiber, the entire capped exploration terminates almost surely.

The finite transcript law equality also gives almost sure termination in an ordinary product-edge implementation. Conditional on any such finite transcript, the unqueried original edge variables retain their independent original law. Independent completion after this finite adaptive exploration therefore realizes the full original product law. This justifies using the coupled full cluster; no conditioning on the global bounded-fiber event is used.

On the event (|C(x)\cap F_v|\le M) for every (v), an unaccepted distinct open neighbor in a full fiber would be an ((M+1))-st member of (C(x)), which is impossible. Thus the cap cannot reject a genuine new cluster vertex on this event. Local finiteness ensures each queued vertex has finitely many incident queries, each fixed-radius graph ball is finite, and every vertex on a finite open path is eventually processed. The capped exploration recovers all of (C(x)), hence (C(x)) is finite almost surely on this event. Countably many roots and integer caps cover even a random finite global bound selected after seeing the configuration.

This proves exactly the written capped proposition. It does not prove that separately finite fiber counts have a global cap; it does not union over uncountably many capacity functions or claim that adaptively selected queried output edges are an independent family.

## 3. Vertical mixing, existence, cluster counts, and uniqueness

For arbitrary countable locally finite (G), the product-edge process is mixing under vertical translations. For two events depending on finite edge-coordinate sets (A,B), there is a finite set of shifts at which their supports can overlap: each relevant product edge has a base label and a finite integer height label. For all sufficiently large shifts they are disjoint, so the corresponding events are independent. Approximation of general measurable events in probability by finite-coordinate events extends this mixing assertion to the complete sigma-field. In particular the shift action is ergodic. No transitivity in the base is needed.

Existence of an infinite open cluster is invariant under vertical shifts, so it has probability zero or one. The number (N\) of infinite clusters is also invariant and hence deterministic. For (0<p<1), a deterministic finite (N=k\ge2) is impossible: countability gives fixed vertices (a,b) in distinct infinite clusters on an event of positive probability. Opening a fixed finite connecting path merges at least two infinite clusters and cannot create a new infinite cluster from finitely many finite clusters. The modified configuration has at most (k-1) infinite clusters. Its law is absolutely continuous with respect to the original Bernoulli law by finite-energy opening, a contradiction. Thus (N\in\{0,1,\infty\}\). The endpoints give (N=0) at (p=0) and (N=1) at (p=1). Nothing in this argument excludes (N=\infty\).

If existence has probability one, countability of product vertices gives some ((u,k)\) with positive infinite-membership probability. Vertical stationarity gives the same positive probability at ((u,0)\). For any (v\), choose a finite base path of length (d\) from (u\) to (v\). Harris-FKG for the increasing infinite-membership event and the increasing event that this path at height zero is open gives
\[
\theta(v):=\mathbb P((v,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)
\ \ge p^d\theta(u)>0\quad(p>0).
\]
This is a positive value for each vertex separately; no uniform lower bound over all (v\) is asserted.

For a fixed (v\), Birkhoff applied to (I_v(\omega)=\mathbf 1\{(v,0)\leftrightarrow\infty\}\) gives
\[
\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbf1\{(v,k)\leftrightarrow\infty\}
\longrightarrow\theta(v)>0
\]
almost surely. Countably many fibers give simultaneous convergence and infinitely many infinite-membership vertices in every fiber. When the infinite cluster is unique, all those vertices lie in it, proving the desired property. Without uniqueness, this is the density of the **union** of infinite clusters, not a density conclusion for each cluster. A vertically invariant ergodic Dirac configuration with all horizontal ray edges open and all vertical edges closed has union density one in every fiber but each infinite horizontal row meets a fiber once. This isolates the inference gap; that configuration does not have iid homogeneous bond law and is not a target counterexample.

## 4. Stretched tree and capacities: exact separation of hypotheses

In the written stretched binary tree, an original level-(n\) edge has length (2^n\). There are (2^n\) original root-to-level-(n\) paths, each of length
\[
S_n=\sum_{j=1}^n2^j=2^{n+1}-2.
\]
By the union bound the root-to-level-(n\) probability is at most (2^np^{S_n}\). For each fixed (0<p<1\), its logarithm is (n\log2+S_n\log p\to-\infty\). At (p=0\) it is zero. The subdivided region before any original level is finite, so any infinite root cluster reaches every level and hence has probability zero. If any other vertex (z\) had positive infinite-membership probability, FKG with the event that a fixed finite root-to-(z\) path is open would give positive infinite-membership probability at the root. Thus every vertex has probability zero below one. Countability then excludes any infinite base cluster below one; at one the base is connected and infinite. Consequently (p_c(T_*)=1\), not merely zero survival at a selected root.

Let (A_n\) be the full finite subdivided tree through original level (n\). Its (2^n\) original boundary vertices have two outgoing original subtrees each. Choose one infinite ray in each of these (2^{n+1}\) subtrees, starting at its boundary vertex. The rays are edge-disjoint, even though a pair may share an initial vertex. A cut leaving **all** vertices of (A_n\) in finite components must intersect each ray, so it has at least (2^{n+1}\) edges. Cuts internal to (A_n\) cannot evade the requirement, since the initial boundary vertices themselves belong to (A_n\). In contrast, a root-only cut can have just two edges. Thus the cutset hypothesis is strictly stronger than (p_c(G)=1\). This is not a conclusion about percolation on (T_*\times\mathbb Z\).

For completeness the uniform-cutset condition does imply (p_c(G)=1\) under this model. A cut of at most (K\) edges separating a finite set yields a finite union (Q\) of the components containing its vertices, with \(|\partial_E Q|\le K\). Starting at a root, repeat with the previous (Q\) and all its outside neighbors included in the next input set. The resulting finite (Q_i\) are nested and their edge boundaries are disjoint. At a homogeneous base parameter (q<1\), their independent all-closed events each have probability at least ((1-q)^K>0\). Almost surely one such closed boundary traps the root in a finite region. Connectedness transfers root nonpercolation to every vertex. Together with the stretched-tree example, this proves the claimed strict separation of hypotheses, rather than only failure of one implication.

The new checker independently computes finite-level probabilities using the branching recursion
\[
a_n=1,\qquad a_{i-1}=1-(1-p^{2^i}a_i)^2,
\]
and compares the exact result with the union bound at (p=1/3,1/2,4/5\), levels 1–6. A separate maximum-flow computation compares cuts for (A_n\) and the root on finite prefixes, obtaining (2^{n+1}\) versus (2\) for (n=1,2,3,4\). These checks validate formulas and detect a mistaken cutset definition. The infinite proofs above supply the universal conclusions.

On the author's inhomogeneous ray, (q_n=1-2^{-(n+1)}\), (n\ge1\), the failure probabilities sum to (1/2\). For every finite prefix the union bound gives probability at least (1/2\) that all its edges are open. Continuity from above gives the infinite product bound
\[
\prod_{n\ge1}q_n\ge\tfrac12>0.
\]
The underlying ray still has homogeneous (p_c=1\). Thus pointwise (q_e<1\) is insufficient for a homogeneous subcriticality conclusion. An additional control uses capacities (M_v=v+1\) on a ray at (p=1/2\). Reservoir sizes (M_v+M_{v+1}=2v+3\) give (q_e=1-2^{-(2v+3)}\), whose failure sum is (1/6\) and infinite product at least (5/6\). This embeds the same obstruction directly in the variable-cap reservoir formula. It is an inhomogeneous base process, not a claim that the actual Bernoulli product cluster realizes these capacities.

There is no purely geometric implication from finite intersections to a uniform cap: the connected deterministic region
\[
C=\{(v,h):v\in\mathbb N_0,\ 0\le h\le v\}\subseteq\mathbb N_0\times\mathbb Z
\]
has (|C\cap F_v|=v+1\). It can be isolated by opening its internal edges and closing its boundary. The base ray is admissible and has (p_c=1\), but for homogeneous (p<1\) the prescribed infinitely many internal open edges have probability zero. This proves a deterministic quantifier separation only, not a probabilistic counterexample.
