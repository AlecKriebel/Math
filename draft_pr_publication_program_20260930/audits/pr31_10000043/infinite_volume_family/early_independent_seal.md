## Independent universal deductions to audit

1. Fiber propagation requires no p_c hypothesis. For adjacent v,w and 0<p<1, expose all edges not incident to F_w. The residual graph is countable and has countably many clusters, each determined by exposed coordinates. For any residual cluster D with infinitely many (v,n), the untouched distinct edges (v,n)-(w,n) are conditionally independent Bernoulli(p). Their infinite success count follows from conditional Borel-Cantelli or the zero probability of finitely many successes. If a full cluster C met F_w only finitely but F_v infinitely, delete C intersect F_w. Local finiteness ensures finitely many pieces; one piece is infinite on F_v and is a residual cluster D, a contradiction. Countable intersection over residual clusters and base edges gives an event valid simultaneously for all full clusters. Consequently every infinite cluster either meets every fiber infinitely often or meets every fiber finitely often. This does not rule out the second class.

2. The whole-fiber exposure is essential: exposing or conditioning on the original C can reveal candidate edge states and invalidate independence. Countable clusters alone does not bound the number of pieces after a finite deletion; local finiteness does. A useful negative control will be a dependent candidate selection consisting only of closed edges, and a deterministic infinite star in which deletion of one vertex leaves infinitely many finite pieces.

3. Vertical shift mixing for the i.i.d. edge law follows by finite-coordinate approximation: any two finite edge-coordinate sets become disjoint after a sufficiently large vertical translation, even with infinitely many base vertices. Thus every vertical-shift invariant event has probability 0 or 1. The global infinite-existence event and infinite cluster number N are invariant. N is constant; for 0<p<1 it cannot be a finite number >=2, by positive-probability finite opening joining two infinite clusters and thereby reducing N. This does not exclude N=infinity.

4. If infinite clusters exist a.s., countability gives a vertex with positive infinite-membership probability. Vertical stationarity and a finite open base path imply theta(v)>0 at every base vertex, using Harris-FKG on the increasing path event and increasing infinite-membership event. Birkhoff then gives positive density theta(v) of vertices (v,n) in the union of infinite clusters along each fiber. Under uniqueness this is the density of the unique infinite cluster and proves all-fiber infinite visits. Without uniqueness this density is only for the union, so it does not establish infinitely many visits by every individual cluster.

5. A stretched rooted binary tree with level-n edge length L_n=n (or any sequence whose cumulative stretch grows superlinearly) has p_c=1: root-to-level-n open probability is at most 2^n p^(sum L_i), tending to zero at fixed p<1. Root failure alone must be converted to all-vertex failure by connectedness/root-independence or a finite-path FKG argument. The graph still has arbitrarily many edge-disjoint descendant rays outside suitable finite sets and therefore cannot admit a uniform bound on edge cutsets separating every finite set from infinity. p_c=1 therefore must not be silently substituted for a bounded-cutset hypothesis.

6. Inhomogeneous ray percolation can survive if sum(1-q_n)<infinity and every q_n>0, because product q_n>0. This does not contradict the homogeneous Bernoulli p_c=1 statement. Any capped-fiber argument using a fixed number M of independent horizontal opportunities has crossing probability <=1-(1-p)^M<1; finite opportunities separately for each fiber give no global M and may yield q_n increasing to 1. This quantifier gap is the central candidate obstruction and must not be hidden by examples or numerics.
