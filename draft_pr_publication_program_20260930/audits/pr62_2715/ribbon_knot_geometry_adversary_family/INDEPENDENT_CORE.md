# Independent core, saved before reading PR62

This note is the initial independent target/mechanism analysis. I have not yet read the original PR62 proof, any fresh PR62 analysis, or other reviewers' conclusions. Primary-source reading occurred through the web tool; URLs and exact read scope are recorded here. This is not a reproduction of every cited proof.

## Literal target and conventions

The authorized preliminary K3 list, printed page 55, Problem 1.56 (Hom), asks: “If K0 and K1 are ribbon concordant and ĤFK(K0) ≅ ĤFK(K1), are K0 and K1 isotopic?” The next page rules out the Wang band-twist family as pairs of ribbon-concordant knots. Source: https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/websites.umass.edu/dist/b/22144/files/2026/04/K3-problem-list-watermarked.pdf (printed pages 55–56, Problem 1.56 and its remarks).

I use J ≤ K for a smooth annulus from J at height 0 to K at height 1 with only births and saddles. Gordon/Agol's verbal “from” convention is reversed relative to Zemke's height convention. Equality means bigraded hat knot Floer homology, over F2 in Zemke's theorem, not merely equal total rank or a common concordance invariant. The conclusion is ambient isotopy of knots. The task is not to show the annulus itself is a product.

## Mechanisms and independent deductions

1. **Floer splitting.** Zemke's published Theorem 1.1 and proof summary give F_reverse F_C = identity; Theorem 1.2 gives grade-wise injections. Equal finite grade-wise dimensions make F_C an isomorphism and F_reverse its linear inverse. This cannot turn the reversed annulus into a ribbon annulus: time reversal converts index-0 births into index-2 deaths. A nonfaithful invariant functor also cannot infer isotopy from an invertible linear map. Source: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v190-n3-p05-s.pdf, introduction/Theorems 1.1–1.7 (pp. 931–935).

2. **Boundary cases.** Equal HFK implies equal genus and Alexander polynomial; the unknot is detected. If one knot is fibered, equal HFK detects fiberedness of the other and their genera agree. Gordon's equal-genus fibered ribbon rigidity then implies isotopy. Zemke's published p. 932 explicitly invokes Gordon Lemma 3.4 for two fibered equal-genus knots; Hom–Park 2026 repeats the genus-drop alternative for homotopy-ribbon-concordant fibered knots. These are prior mechanisms, not new PR62 results.

3. **Group hypothesis.** Residual finiteness of the full knot group is insufficient for the older equal-Alexander-degree rigidity argument. Transfinite nilpotence is a hypothesis about the commutator subgroup's transfinite lower central series. Fibered knot commutators are free. A nontrivial Alexander-polynomial-one knot has perfect nontrivial commutator, so its lower central series never removes that subgroup. Thus one cannot silently extend a conditional Gordon result to all knot groups. I could access Gordon's publisher metadata but not the full 1981 paper; I do not claim to have checked its original general lemma or the weakest one-sided group hypothesis. Conservative fibered rigidity is directly supported by the primary later papers read.

4. **Band-twist obstruction.** Wang's arXiv v1 Theorems 1.3/1.8 (published HFK theorem renumbered 1.2) give constant bigraded HFK and Kh(K_n) = B ⊕ h^(2n)q^(4n)H for nonzero finite-dimensional H. All total Kh dimensions are equal, while graded profiles differ. Levine–Zemke's corrected author version Theorem 1 supplies a grade-preserving injection for any ribbon annulus. Equal total dimensions would make that injection a graded isomorphism, contradicting distinct profiles. Hence no two different full twists are ribbon concordant in either direction. Both may be above the same connected sum; sharing a ribbon predecessor does not compare them. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01070, introduction/Theorems 1.3,1.7,1.8 and proof outline; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.01546 (v2, 2021), full five-page theorem/proof; publisher DOI https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12303 notes an erratum.

5. **Partial order.** Agol's result requires actual ribbon concordances in both directions. Residual finiteness/representation-variety proof addresses the composite self-concordance. A reverse Floer map supplies no missing reverse ribbon annulus. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03626, introduction and proof of Theorems 1.1–1.2.

6. **2026 scope.** Baldwin–Hanselman–Sivek Theorem 1.2 proves finitely many *fibered* predecessors of every knot; Corollary 1.3 applies all predecessors when the upper knot is fibered. Agol–Ren Theorem 1.6 independently proves that fibered-upper finiteness. These statements do not imply a unique predecessor with equal HFK. Their compression theorem concerns strongly homotopy-ribbon concordance in a homotopy I×S3, whose equivalence to ribbon concordance remains unproved. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21109v1, introduction; https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10884v1, introduction, Theorems 1.4–1.9.

7. **Newer special cases.** Dunkerley's 2026 preprint proves minimality for a few specified nonfibered knots using existing HFK detection, Alexander divisibility, and a separate concordance obstruction for a two-knot detection set. It does not prove that every equal-HFK comparable pair is isotopic. Its Theorems 1.5–1.6, introduction, and §§6.1–6.2 were read; its displayed internal date is August 24, 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20802v1. Hom–Park's published 2026 theorem assumes gamma_0-sharp fibered knots and uses Gordon/Miyazaki; it is not universal nonfibered rigidity. Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-026-04050-3, abstract/introduction and proof around Gordon Lemma 3.4.

## Independent status and falsifiable review plan

The retrieved April 2026 literal list treats Problem 1.56 as open. The current primary papers retrieved through October 3 add finite/special cases and do not state a general solution. My bounded search did not locate a proof or counterexample; this is not an exhaustive absence claim. Before reading PR62, my hypothesis is that the general nonfibered case remains genuinely unproved.

Falsification criteria for PR62: (a) any promotion from isomorphic Floer maps to geometric invertibility; (b) any use of residual finiteness where commutator transfinite nilpotence is required; (c) a band-twist “counterexample” without an actual pairwise ribbon annulus; (d) finiteness treated as uniqueness; (e) a fibered or detected-knot consequence credited as new. A valid general proof must supply a new geometric rigidity step, or a valid counterexample must exhibit the actual ribbon annulus and independently distinguish the endpoints.

Audit completion estimate: 35%. Discovery completion toward solving Problem 1.56: no new solution established.
