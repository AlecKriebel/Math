## Exact local formulas: all singularity classes and multiplicities

It is insufficient merely to count residues unless pole orders are established. Here the following exact quarter-shift formulas give local coordinates at every possible singularity:

`f(iK'+w)=i(1+sn w)/cn w`,

`f(p+w)=−i(cn w+dn w)/(k' sn w)`.

The first follows from `nd(w+iK')=i sc w` and `sd(w+iK')=i nc w`. The second follows from `sn(w+p)=dn w/(k cn w)` and `dn(w+p)=i k' sn w/cn w`. These formulas hold meromorphically, including at the removable values obtained by limits.

The derivative identities at zero imply

`sn w=w−(1+k²)w³/6+O(w⁵)`,

`cn w=1−w²/2+O(w⁴)`, `dn w=1−k²w²/2+O(w⁴)`.

Consequently

`f(iK'+w)=i[1+w+w²/2+O(w³)]`,

`f(p+w)=−2i/(k'w)+i(1+k²)w/(6k')+O(w³)`,

`f(p+2K+w)=ik'w/2+O(w³)`,

`f(z0+w)=−ik'w/2+O(w³)`.

The last two follow from the reciprocal/imaginary shifts. Their coefficients never vanish in the strict parameter range. Translations by `2K` and `2iK'` cover every point in the tabulated Jacobi pole/zero sets. Thus:

- At **every** common pole `2mK+(2n+1)iK'` of `sn,dn`, `f` extends analytically with value `±i`; there is neither a pole nor a zero. The cancellation does not depend on a genericity assumption about residues.
- At **every** zero of `dn`, namely `(2m+1)K+(2n+1)iK'`, `sn=±1/k`. If `sn=+1/k`, the numerator is 2 and the pole is exactly simple. If `sn=−1/k`, the alternative identity `f=dn/(1−k sn)` has denominator 2, so the zero is exactly simple.
- Elsewhere all inputs are finite with `dn≠0`; a zero of `1+k sn` would force `dn=0`, so there are no omitted zeros or poles.

Modulo Λ, the full divisor is therefore poles `{p,p+2iK'}`, zeros `{p+2K,p+2K+2iK'}`, all simple. Every period T must send p to a pole, hence `T∈4K Z+2iK' Z`. ## Grid collisions, cancellation, and the compactness step

Let N be odd and positive, and `h=4K τ/N`, `gcd(τ,N)=1`. For the abstract N=1 case one can simply take h=0. The subgroup H generated by h modulo 4K equals the group generated by `Δ=4K/N`; winding therefore permutes the same finite set. Define

`A(z)=Σ_{j=0}^{N−1} f(z+jh)`, `B(z)=Σ_{j=0}^{N−1} f(−z+jh)`.

The full pole set of A is `p−jh+2niK'` modulo real period 4K. Every pole belongs to exactly one summand: equality between two would give `(j−l)τ=0 mod N`, and coprimality forces j=l. The residue is `r_n=(−1)^n(−2i/k')≠0`. These are actual simple poles, rather than possible poles whose orders are being presumed. B has their negatives as its poles.

An intersection between A's and B's pole sets would require, in real coordinates,

`(j+l)h=2K mod 4K`, hence `2(j+l)τ=N mod 2N`.

This is impossible because N is odd. The imaginary coordinates cannot repair this parity obstruction. It holds for every real `0<k<1`; it does not depend on an irrationality condition involving K or K'.

The three shift/reflection identities compose to give `f(2z0−w)=−f(w)`, hence `f(z0+v)=−f(z0−v)`. The point z0 is an exactly simple zero by the local expansion. Every `z0+jh` is analytic by the same parity obstruction. Pairing j with N−j therefore proves the exact finite identity

`Σ_j f(z0+jh)=0`.

There is no unpaired fixed index other than j=0: `2j=0 mod N` implies j=0 when N is odd. At an arbitrary A-pole `z*=p−lh+2niK'`, the function B is analytic, and

`B(z*)=(−1)^n Σ_j f(z0+(l+j)h)=0`.

In the local variable w=z−z*, write

`A(z)=r_n/w+a0+a1w+…`, `B(z)=b1w+b2w²+…`.

Their product is `r_n b1+O(w)`. Its complete possible principal part consists of `r_n B(z*)/w`, which is zero. Thus **all** negative Laurent coefficients disappear; this is not a residue-only cancellation in the presence of an unchecked higher pole. Replacing z by −z handles all B-poles, since `B(z)=A(−z)` and AB is even. All other points are already regular.

A and B each have periods 4K,4iK'. Thus AB is entire and Λ-periodic. On a closed fundamental parallelogram, including its boundary after the removable extensions, it is continuous and bounded. Every complex point translates into that compact set. Liouville therefore applies to an entire bounded function on the full plane. Equivalently, it is holomorphic on the compact torus. No puncture, branch point, limiting parameter, or point at infinity is being omitted.

For real x, `dn x>0`, `|sn x|≤1`, and `1+k sn x≥1−k>0`. Hence A(x),B(x)>0. At z=0 the sd terms pair under j↔N−j and cancel, while nd is even, giving

`A(0)=B(0)=C_N(k):=Σ_{j=0}^{N−1} 1/dn(4Kj/N,k)>0`.

`A(z)B(z)=C_N(k)²`.
