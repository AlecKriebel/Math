### 1. Model normalization and the zero self term

Their first-order terms vanish after multiplication by the exponential. Subtracting the static kernel leaves a quadratic term -(k^2/(8*pi*r))(I+ee^T), then the isotropic term -i*k^3*I/(6*pi), and a locally O(r) remainder. Thus the dynamic difference has a 1/r bound on every fixed bounded difference set, even at a fixed complex frequency with negative imaginary part. There is no use of a bounded infinite nonzero-frequency lattice convolution.

The Fourier transform of the distributional operator -D^2(1/(4*pi*r)) is the longitudinal projection. Rotational invariance forces its difference from the spherical principal value to be a scalar matrix delta distribution. Taking its trace gives I*delta/3. The nonsingular difference g_k-g_0 has locally integrable Hessian and supplies no additional delta term. Therefore the actual matrix with omitted diagonal compares with A_k-I/3, not A_k. The manuscript's convention matches the primary DDA report and CDN equations (1.7), (3.39), (3.42).

### 3. Strong static consistency, including arbitrary translations

For a smooth compactly supported field phi, its sampled cell field phi_h converges in L2 uniformly with respect to grid translations. A finite radial lattice cutoff has exact zero tensor sum: reflections cancel all off-diagonal entries, permutations make diagonal entries equal, and each summand has zero trace. This is a finite symmetry identity, with no rearrangement of an infinite conditionally convergent series.

Subtracting phi(x) in the near field leaves a term bounded by C*||grad phi||_infinity times h^3 times the lattice sum of |h*j|^-2 up to radius 2*delta. Lattice shells of integer thickness have at most C*(q+1)^2 points. Their sum is at most C*delta; if delta<h/2 the sum is empty. The corresponding spherical principal-value integral has the same near-field estimate. For fixed delta, the remaining smooth, truncated integrands form a uniformly controlled compact family for bounded evaluation points, so ordinary Riemann sums converge uniformly under translations. The continuum output is smooth and moving from cell center to cell point contributes a vanishing error.

The whole-space tail deserves a separate check. Far outside the fixed support of phi, every physical separation is comparable with |x|, while the sampled L1 norm of phi is uniformly bounded. Both outputs are bounded by C*(1+|x|)^-3. The squared tail integrates to O(R^-3), uniformly in small h and all translations. This closes the passage from local to global L2 convergence. The common operator bound then replaces phi_h by phi and extends the convergence by density to every L2 vector.

Under (G), integration of |u|^2 over the symmetric difference tends to zero by absolute continuity, so M_h converges strongly to M. Products of uniformly bounded strongly convergent operators give B_h=M_h D_h M_h -> M D M strongly. Every B_h and B is selfadjoint. No pointwise convergence of the masks, boundary smoothness, eigenfunction regularity, or operator-norm convergence of B_h is required.

### 4. Sampled dynamic kernels converge in Hilbert--Schmidt norm

The boundedness of Omega places all occupied cubes in one common box for small h, independent of (G). The 1/r dynamic bound is square integrable on a bounded three-dimensional product domain. ### 5. Compact factorization and quantified stability

For z outside J_lat, the static resolvents have norm at most dist(z,J_lat)^-1. The resolvent identity and strong B_h convergence imply strong R_h(z) convergence; finite parameter nets and the resolvent identity make this locally uniform on each fixed vector. Selfadjointness also gives R_h(z)*=R_h(conjugate(z)), hence the required adjoint-strong convergence.

A uniformly bounded strongly convergent family converges in norm after right multiplication by a compact operator, as is proved by finite-rank approximation. Consequently F_h(z)=R_h(z) C_h converges in norm to F(z)=R(z) C, uniformly on compact parameter sets. The exact algebraic factorization is z-S=(z-B)(I-F), in that order. F is analytic and compact on the connected complement of the real interval. There are invertible points at large |z| because S is bounded. The analytic Fredholm alternative therefore yields only isolated finite-algebraic-multiplicity spectral points there. This application uses the spectral variable z; physical k remains fixed.

For a compact subset of the continuum resolvent outside J_lat, the inverse of I-F is uniformly bounded. Norm convergence of F_h gives a uniform Neumann inverse estimate for I-F_h. ### 6. Riesz projections, Jordan cases, and generalized subspaces

On an isolating contour whose entire closed disk misses J_lat, the static resolvents are analytic inside and integrate to zero. The full resolvents decompose as R_h+G_h R_h, where G_h=(I-F_h)^-1 F_h converges in norm to compact G. The first difference term (G_h-G)R_h tends to zero by the uniform bounds. The second term G(R_h-R) tends to zero in norm because the ADJOINT resolvents converge strongly. Taking adjoints reduces this to the previous compact-multiplication lemma. A compact parameter net makes the result uniform on the contour. Integrating gives operator-norm convergence of the Riesz projections, although the full resolvents generally need not converge in norm.

For idempotents P_h,P with distance below one, restriction of P_h to ran(P) and restriction of P to ran(P_h) are injective. Since the limiting off-interval projection has finite rank, both dimensions agree. These are total algebraic multiplicities, without a semisimplicity assumption. Applying the same result on smaller circles, together with compact-annulus stability, gives convergence of the full eigenvalue cluster. Distances from a unit vector in either range to its image under the other projection are bounded by ||P_h-P||, proving both subspace gaps. The matrix norms remain uniformly bounded because the static operators are uniformly bounded and C_h converges in norm.

Two conceptual stress tests clarify why the hypotheses and wording matter. On l2, B_n=|e_n><e_n| converges strongly to zero, yet at z=2 the static resolvent difference has norm 1/2 for every n. Thus full norm-resolvent convergence is unavailable; the manuscript properly avoids it and integrates out the analytic static term. Also X_n=|e_1><e_n| converges strongly to zero, while K X_n has norm one for K=|e_1><e_1|. Ordinary strong convergence alone would not justify the crucial compact factor on the LEFT; the adjoint argument supplied in this manuscript fixes that genuine danger. Finally a 2-by-2 block [[i,1],[d,i]] changes from defective at d=0 to split eigenvalues for d>0 while retaining the total off-real contour cluster. ### 8. Fixed compact mask construction

- Interval endpoints and spectral points on J_lat: excluded. No threshold or embedded-eigenvalue result is claimed.
- Defective eigenvalues: total contour projection and algebraic multiplicity work; individual Jordan blocks may change.
- Moving contours or moving frequency: not covered by this fixed-frequency compact-set theorem.
- Whole-space zeros: explicitly excluded from all relevant off-real/off-interval contours; the intrinsic ball calculation is separately justified.
