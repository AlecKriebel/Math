# Independent operator/spectral falsification plan

Frozen before reading any mathematical body or prior mathematical verdict.

Scope: the claimed spectral convergence for actual zero-diagonal cubic discrete-dipole matrices at fixed complex frequency, outside a specified static lattice interval, under voxel-volume condition (G). No correctness, novelty, or publication acceptance is presumed.

1. Reconstruct the exact Hilbert spaces, voxel embeddings and projections, continuum operator convention, singular-kernel distribution, zero self-term, and fixed-frequency dependence. Check constants and diagonal renormalization rather than silently identifying two different static operators.
2. Derive the lattice symbol and its spectral enclosure independently. Test the endpoints, zero frequency, complex frequency, longitudinal/transverse modes, Fourier normalization, finite section compression, and arbitrary finite voxel sets. Determine whether the enclosure holds for the actual finite matrices rather than a formal infinite convolution.
3. Reconstruct strong convergence of static compressions and of their adjoints. Specify the common ambient space, approximation of the identity, convergence on a dense domain, uniform bounds, and the geometric role of (G). Test holes, thin features, disconnected sets, boundary cells, moving voxels, and bounded versus unbounded domains.
4. Verify dynamic remainder compactness, collective compactness, adjoint compactness if needed, kernel-integrability estimates, quadrature and self-cell errors, and whether geometry controls all error terms. Look for a missing rate, a hidden smoothness or shape regularity assumption, or a switch from zero-diagonal matrices to a cell-averaged model.
5. Establish uniformly stable static inverses off the lattice interval with the correct spectral parameter convention. Check locally uniform convergence, strong adjoint convergence of inverses, behavior on the orthogonal complement of the voxel spaces, and whether an inadmissible inverse of a singular compression was used.
6. Reconstruct the compact factorization and the exact analytic Fredholm argument. Distinguish a spectral parameter from the fixed physical frequency; test whether the operator pencil is analytic on the claimed domain and whether at least one invertible point exists in every connected component. Check nonempty essential spectrum and behavior at infinity.
7. Independently prove the no-pollution and resolvent conclusions, stating quantifiers for compact subsets of the limiting resolvent set. Check that proofs do not infer norm convergence of inverses from strong convergence alone or assume normality of the full dynamic operator.
8. Challenge isolated eigenvalue and contour claims with nonnormal/Jordan examples. Verify algebraic multiplicity, winding/orientation, Riesz projection identities, uniform contour bounds, and norm convergence of embedded projections. Test right and left generalized eigenspaces, spurious zero modes from ambient extensions, and whether rank equality is justified.
9. Check all conclusions against boundary cases: an empty or null-volume domain if permitted, frequency zero, lossy/complex frequency, static interval endpoints, embedded eigenvalues on the excluded interval, threshold/resonance cases, multiple or defective eigenvalues, and parameter-dependent contours. Distinguish excluded cases from proved cases.
10. Map each essential step to an independently checkable derivation or the exact hypotheses of a classical theorem. Identify the actual new input and any theorem invoked outside its scope. Read primary sources where required after custody clearance. Do not read prior verdicts before the independent derivation.

Success criterion: either a checkable counterexample/gap in a stated claim, or a complete independently reconstructed proof with an explicit hypothesis/quantifier audit and an exact source-coverage inventory. Numerical experiments can falsify or illustrate but cannot substitute for singular-limit or spectral proofs.
