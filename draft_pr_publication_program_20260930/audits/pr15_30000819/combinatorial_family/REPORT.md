## Exact claim, assumptions, and checks

Let `A` be distinct integral points of affine dimension `d`, `a_i=(v_i,1)`,
`S=N A`, `G=Z A`, and `H=(cone(A) intersect G) minus S`. The intrinsic affine
lattice is `L=Z{v_i-v_1}`. Its normalized volume is `V`; its homogenization has
rank `r=d+1`; set `c=n-r`.

The checked deductions are:

1. `0 < |H| < infinity` implies every coordinate occurs in a rational relation.
2. A primitive circuit has binomial degree at most intrinsic `V`.
3. A nonpyramidal configuration satisfies `n <= 2 V c`.
4. An all-selected-point lattice triangulation gives `c <= V-1`.
5. Thus finite nonempty holes imply `V>=2`, `c>=1`, and `n<=2V(V-1)`.

The exact remaining algebraic dependency is that the stated standard-graded
regularity estimate and the quotient/local-cohomology argument imply
`h<=nVc-2`. Combining that dependency with the five checked deductions gives the
displayed quartic bound. No part of this argument gives the sharper `h<=V`.

The tests are falsifiable controls: a false claimed lattice group, cone
membership, height, offset, finite-hole hypothesis, or all-point triangulation
produces an explicit integer/rational witness. Tests retain chosen generators;
they never replace `A` with all lattice points unless doing so as a negative
control.

## Derivation 1: finite nonempty holes exclude every pyramidal column

Suppose `a_i` is independent of the real span of the remaining columns. Every
vector in the real span has a unique coefficient of `a_i` in the direct sum
`span(A without a_i) direct-sum R a_i`. On `G`, that coefficient is integral,
since `G` is generated integrally by the columns. On the cone it is nonnegative.
Consequently, with primed objects generated by the other columns,

```
S = S' direct-sum N a_i,
G = G' direct-sum Z a_i,
cone(S) intersect G = (cone(S') intersect G') direct-sum N a_i.
```

Therefore the holes are exactly `H' x N`. A single hole supplies distinct holes
of heights increasing by every nonnegative integer; if `H'` is empty there are
no holes. This proves the exclusion under **both** finiteness and nonemptiness.
It is a property of the chosen columns, rather than only the polytope shape.

Adversarial control: the script pyramids the two-dimensional finite-hole product
`{0,1,3,4} x {0,1}`. The base hole `(2,0)` becomes `(2,0,k-1)` at height `k`.
Its apex coefficient is forced to be `k-1`, leaving exactly one base generator;
it cannot be represented. The cone inequality is `2<=4(k-(k-1))`, so the witness
lies in the saturated cone at every height `k>=1`. The computation checks heights
1 through 8; the displayed forced-coefficient argument proves all heights.

## Derivation 2: circuit degree is bounded by intrinsic volume

This supplies an elementary independent proof of the classical circuit estimate
used in the source note. Choose a basis of `G`; normalized simplex volumes are
absolute determinants in that basis. This is the correct intrinsic volume:
choose the basis of `L x {0}` together with `(v_1,1)`, so the determinants equal
the determinants of affine differences in `L`.

Let `u` be a primitive integral circuit, supported on `B`. The columns in `B`
have rank `|B|-1`. Add columns from `A` until the rank becomes `r`, always adding
an independent column. This adds `r-|B|+1` columns, giving an `r x (r+1)` matrix
`D`. Its one-dimensional kernel is the original `u`, extended by zero on the
added columns. Such complements exist since `A` spans `G` over the rationals.

Let `Delta` be the positive gcd of the maximal minors of `D`, evaluated in a
basis of `G`. It is the index of the subgroup generated by `D` in `G`, hence
a positive integer. The signed cofactor vector is `Delta u`, up to an overall
sign: it spans the kernel, and dividing its entries by their gcd produces the
primitive kernel vector. Thus deleting column `i` gives simplex volume
`Delta |u_i|` for each supported coordinate, and gives a zero determinant for
an added coordinate.

The two sign classes of the circuit give triangulations of `conv(D)`: for one
class delete each positive coordinate, and for the other delete each negative
coordinate. To check this fact rather than assume it, take a point in `conv(D)`
and its convex-coordinate fiber. The fiber is a closed interval
`lambda+t u`; the sum of its coordinates remains one because all columns have
height one. At each endpoint at least one coordinate from the corresponding
sign class is zero. This proves coverage by the indicated simplices. Distinct
simplex interiors in a single sign class cannot overlap: moving the fiber from
one deleted coordinate toward another would make the first coordinate negative.
Boundary intersections have zero full-dimensional volume. Minimality of the
circuit ensures each indicated deletion is a full-rank simplex.

Since `sum u_i = 0`, the positive and negative coefficient sums are equal to
the circuit degree `g`. Either triangulation therefore has volume

```
Vol_G(conv(D)) = Delta sum_{u_i>0} u_i = Delta g.
```

As `conv(D)` is a full-dimensional subpolytope of `P`, monotonicity of volume
gives `g <= Delta g <= V`. This covers circuits whose affine span has dimension
less than `d`, nonspanning configurations in a larger ambient lattice, and
circuits with an interior selected point. No assumption that `A` contains all
lattice points is needed.

Exact controls enumerate 156 primitive circuits in eight configurations, using
rational elimination to certify minimal support. For each circuit the script
extends its support, checks the intrinsic determinant gcd, and checks both sign
volume sums against `Delta g`. The three-dimensional finite-hole prism has 120
circuits, including degree 7, while its intrinsic volume is 12. The nonspanning
diamond has ambient volume 4, index 2, intrinsic volume 2, and circuit degree 2;
the code uses the intrinsic value. These are examples validating the formulas,
not a replacement for the general argument.

## Derivation 3: circuit bases cover all coordinates only under nonpyramidality

Every rational relation is a linear combination of circuits. Indeed, if a
relation is not support-minimal, take a nonzero relation of strictly smaller
support contained in its support and subtract a rational multiple that cancels
one nonzero coordinate. This creates no new coordinate and reduces support;
induct. Choose `c` independent circuit relations as a rational basis and scale
them to primitive integral vectors.

Each circuit has disjoint positive and negative supports, degree `g<=V`, and
therefore support cardinality at most `2g<=2V`. A coordinate missing from the
union of the basis supports is zero in every rational relation, so that column
is independent of all the others. Conversely, a pyramidal column is zero in
every relation. Nonpyramidality consequently forces the basis supports to cover
all `n` coordinates, yielding `n <= sum support_sizes <= 2Vc`.

The square has `V=2,c=1,n=4`, realizing the support-cover equality. The pyramid
control has an uncovered apex coordinate; the script explicitly reports the
configuration as pyramidal and does not apply the nonpyramidal inequality to it.

## Derivation 4: triangulating every selected point gives c <= V-1

For `d>=1`, start with a triangulation of the polytope vertices and insert each
remaining selected point by stellar subdivision of the smallest simplex face
whose relative interior contains it. Subdivide all simplices incident to that
face, so points on common facets or on boundary faces are handled consistently.
The final triangulation uses exactly every selected point as a vertex. It does
not require the unselected lattice points to be vertices.

The full-dimensional simplex dual graph is connected: a path inside the convex
polytope between generic simplex interior points can avoid its finite skeleton
of codimension at least two, crossing successive common facets. Order the `t`
full simplices along a spanning tree. The first has `d+1` vertices; every later
one shares `d` vertices with its earlier parent and contributes at most one new
vertex. Hence `n <= d+1+(t-1)=d+t`. Every simplex has positive integer intrinsic
normalized volume, so `V>=t`, and `c=n-d-1<=t-1<=V-1`.

The planar control starts with a rectangle triangulation and inserts three
boundary points, a point on the original common diagonal, and an interior point.
It contains nine chosen points, omits six rectangle lattice points, and has
intrinsic volume 16. Exact controls check volume sums, all-point usage, connected
facet adjacency, no unsplit selected point on an edge, no crossing edges, and
opposite-side orientations across interior facets. Thus the test is not merely
a count of candidate simplices.

The prism examples below supply all-selected triangulations through dimension
five. Their staircase prism triangulation has `q+1` simplices; each contains one
vertical edge. Subdivide that edge at 1 and `m-1`. This gives `3(q+1)` simplices
with volumes `1,m-2,1` in each block, for total volume `m(q+1)`, and all
`4(q+1)` selected vertices. They attain `t=n-d`; this independently exercises
the vertex-count step in dimensions above two.

## All-degree certificates for new higher-dimensional finite-hole examples

Let `m>=4`, `q>=1`, and

```
A_(m,q) = {0,1,m-1,m} x {0,e_1,...,e_q}.
P_(m,q) = [0,m] x Delta_q.
```

The affine group is the full integer lattice: selected-point differences include
`(1,0)` and each `(0,e_i)`. The normalized volume is `V=(q+1)m`, dimension
`d=q+1`, and `n=4(q+1)`. Coordinate choices in a degree-`k` sum can be paired
arbitrarily, so

```
kA_(m,q) = (k{0,1,m-1,m}) x (k Delta_q intersect Z^q).
```

The first factor is the union of intervals
`[j(m-1),j(m-1)+k]`, for `j=0,...,k`. At `k=0` it is `{0}`. For positive `k`,
adjacent intervals have no missing integer precisely when `k>=m-2`. For
`1<=k<=m-3`, the `k` gaps each have `m-k-2` integers. Thus the exact hole count
at degree `k` is

```
#H_k = k(m-k-2) binomial(k+q,q),  for 1<=k<=m-3,
#H_k = 0,                       otherwise.
```

This is a genuine all-degree finiteness certificate with highest-hole height
`m-3`, not an inference from finding no holes up to a cutoff. It also explains
why a Cartesian product supplies higher-dimensional very ample examples whereas
a pyramid fails. The script independently constructs degree sumsets for
`m=4,...,7`, `q=1,...,4`, checking 16 families and 88 degree levels against the
count and set formulas. It verifies the explicit all-selected triangulations
for each family. These examples continue to satisfy `h<=V` and do not discriminate
between the sharp conjecture and the quartic bound.

## Ambient lattice, translation, and boundary failures

An invertible integral transformation `T` and translation `b` send a degree-`k`
point `x` to `Tx+kb`. The generated degree-`k` affine lattice is `kb+T Z^d`, not
all `Z^d`, and the ambient volume multiplies by `|det T|` while intrinsic volume
does not change. The code uses a triangular matrix with diagonal `2,...,d+1`
and lower shear 1, and dimensions 2 through 5. An explicit lattice-basis minor
certifies the index. The last column is `(d+1)e_last`, so `kb+e_last` is an
integer ambient point on the transformed simplex edge for every `k>=1`; its
intrinsic coordinate is `e_last/(d+1)`, which is outside the generated group.
It witnesses infinitely many ambient holes. The observed tested intrinsic
hole heights remain 2 for `m=5`.

More generally, if the full-rank generated group `G` is a proper subgroup of an
ambient homogenized lattice `M`, choose `b in M minus G` and let `w` be the sum
of all columns. The vector `w` lies in the relative interior of the cone: every
nonzero supporting functional is positive on at least one column, hence on
their sum. For sufficiently large `N`, `b+Nw` is in the cone, remains outside
`G`, and is an ambient hole. Adding any column repeatedly preserves its coset
and supplies distinct ambient holes at increasing heights. Thus finite ambient
holes require agreement with `G`. This is an all-degree lattice argument, not
a bounded coset enumeration.

The nonspanning simplex `[(0,0),(2,0),(1,3)]` has ambient volume and index 6,
intrinsic `V=1`, and `c=0`. Independent columns give unique integer coefficients
for points of `G`; cone membership makes those coefficients nonnegative. Hence
it is intrinsically normal. The ambient point `(1,0)` lies on its dilated bottom
edge and is missing at every positive height. An ambient `V=1` normality
assertion would therefore use the wrong lattice.

For any `c=0` configuration the same unique-coefficient argument proves intrinsic
normality. For `d=0`, distinctness of `A` gives exactly one point and one height-one
generator, also intrinsically normal. The all-point triangulation inequality
implies `V=1 => c=0`, covering that boundary. A highest-hole height does not exist
when the hole set is empty; the optional `-1` convention is consistent.

The suite rejects the following candidate mutations by explicit witnesses:

- Finite-hole preservation under pyramiding: the forced apex coefficient gives
  holes at every degree.
- Deleting a near-endpoint generator: `{0,3,4}` has the hole 1 at every positive
  degree, although its interval cone and generated lattice include that point.
- Defining holes using group membership alone: the integer 13 at height 3 is
  outside the degree-three cone of `[0,4]` and is not a hole.
- Dropping height from membership: 2 is `1+1` in total, but it is missing at
  height one for `{0,1,3,4}`.
- Dropping `kb` from the translated degree-`k` slice: exact transformed sumsets
  disagree with the unshifted slice.
- Silently filling unselected lattice points: adjoining 2 removes the genuine
  degree-one hole of `{0,1,3,4}`.
- Mixing the intrinsic and ambient lattice: the index-six simplex and transformed
  prisms have intrinsically finite or empty holes and infinitely many ambient
  coset holes.

These are mathematically specified interpretation mutations; this family did
not edit the canonical verifier or claim to run an automated code-mutant engine.
