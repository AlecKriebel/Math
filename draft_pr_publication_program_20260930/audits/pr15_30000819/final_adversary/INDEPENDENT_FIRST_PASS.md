## Exact independently reconstructed claim

For a finite set A of distinct lattice points, homogenize its columns as
`a_i=(v_i,1)`, let S be their nonnegative integer span, G their integer span,
and let H be `(cone(S) intersect G) minus S`. Volume V is normalized in the
affine difference lattice generated by A. If H is finite and nonempty, its
maximum height h obeys `h <= 2 V^2 (V-1)^2 - 2`.

The homogeneous grading, selected generators, generated group saturation,
finite *nonempty* holes and intrinsic volume are substantive hypotheses.
The claim does not assert a linear bound, priority, or a new resolution of the
source question.

## Reconstruction and attempted falsification

1. Suppose one column is outside the real span of the others. Writing every
   element of the group uniquely as `g' + z a_i`, and every cone element as
   `x' + t a_i`, forces `t=z` when they coincide. Thus saturation is
   `overline(S') direct-sum N a_i` and holes are the holes of S' times N.
   Every existing hole therefore produces infinitely many holes. Finite
   nonempty holes force every coordinate to occur in some rational relation.
   This defeats the dimension-increasing pyramid counterexample mechanism.

2. The rational kernel has dimension `c=n-d-1`. Support-minimal rational
   dependencies (circuits) span it: cancel a coordinate by a smaller-support
   dependency and induct on support size. A basis of c circuits covers every
   coordinate occurring in any kernel vector, hence all n coordinates after
   step 1. For primitive integer circuits, the two disjoint positive and
   negative parts have equal sum at most V. Their total support has size at
   most 2V. Therefore `n <= 2Vc`. No assumption that a circuit basis is an
   integer-lattice basis was used or needed.

3. A triangulation using all distinct selected points exists, including
   points in the interior of P or its faces. For d>0 its full-dimensional
   simplex adjacency graph through facets is connected. Order a rooted
   spanning tree with parents first: the first simplex uses d+1 vertices,
   every later simplex shares d vertices with its earlier parent and adds
   at most one vertex. If t is the simplex count, `n <= d+t`. Each simplex
   has positive integer volume in the generated affine lattice, and its
   volume contributes to V, so `c+1=n-d <= t <= V`. Thus `c <= V-1`.
   This argument counts actual selected generators, not all lattice points.

4. I independently checked Sturmfels, *Equations Defining Toric Varieties*,
   Theorem 1.4, (4.4), Lemma 4.6 and Theorem 4.5 from its primary PDF.
   These use the intrinsic affine lattice and ideal regularity: projective
   degree is V, primitive circuit degree is at most V, Graver degree is at
   most Vc, and `reg(I_A) <= n V c`. The circuit index discussion on the
   following page is a stronger notion of true degree, and does not remove
   or contradict the ordinary circuit-degree bound. The proof uses arbitrary
   homogeneous toric ideals, not only normal configurations.

   Independently, a reduced toric Groebner basis lies in the Graver basis.
   Every minimal initial generator therefore has each exponent at most Vc.
   Any Taylor-resolution least common multiple has total degree at most
   nVc, hence the initial *ideal* has regularity at most nVc. Flat
   degeneration gives the same bound for I_A. This is a cited classical
   inequality, not a proof of the stronger conjectural circuit/Graver
   equality discussed later in that paper.

5. The semigroup normalization B is a finite positively graded R-module.
   It need not be generated in degree one. Integrality gives
   `rad(R_+ B)=B_+`: mod R_+B every positive-degree homogeneous integral
   generator is nilpotent. B is Cohen--Macaulay by Hochster. In the eligible
   case its dimension r=d+1 is at least 2. Therefore its local cohomology
   in degrees 0 and 1 for this radical vanishes. R has no zeroth local
   cohomology because it is a positive-dimensional domain. The finite-hole
   quotient C=B/R is finite-length with last nonzero degree h. The long
   exact sequence gives `C ~= H^1_{R_+}(R)`. Thus `reg(R) >= h+1`, and for
   the nonzero toric ideal `reg(I_A)=reg(R)+1`, giving
   `h <= reg(I_A)-2`. Combining steps 2--4 gives the displayed quartic.
   No assertion about standard generation of B enters the deduction.

6. At c=0 every column is independent, S is free and normal in G. At d=0
   there is one distinct generator and no holes. V=1 forces c=0 by step 3,
   so there are no holes; applying the quartic value -2 to an empty-hole
   convention -1 would be invalid. The proof explicitly restricts its
   theorem accordingly. Ambient volume only increases by a positive
   integer index, and the quartic is increasing on integers >=2. If one
   instead uses ambient saturation and the generated group is proper,
   choose an ambient lattice point in a missing coset inside the cone;
   adding a sufficiently interior semigroup element supplies infinitely
   many missing-coset points. Hence finite ambient holes force lattice
   agreement. This qualification is necessary.

7. For `{0,1,m-1,m}`, fixing the number j of high summands gives the full
   interval `[j(m-1),j(m-1)+k]` at degree k. Adjacent intervals meet or are
   consecutive precisely when k>=m-2. At k=m-3 an internal gap remains,
   so h=m-3, for m>=4. This consistency example satisfies the stronger
   proposed h<=V; it proves no general sharp bound.

## Independent source and published-prior conclusion

The OWR source is Report 39/2007, page 2316. It uses the phrase
“by the normalized volume” and immediately mentions Eisenbud--Goto and
Herzog's multiplicity question. It does not print `h<=V`. A sharp reading
is plausible contextual inference; neither a specified formula nor the
author's precise intended strength is established by these sentences.

The original dataset record uses the weaker phrase “in terms of” and lists
an open-status triage dated 2026-08-21. That historical assertion is not a
current absence certificate.

I independently read the full primary CCMPV manuscript's Corollary 5.3
and its proof. It bounds regularity of a homogeneous nondegenerate prime
ideal over an algebraically closed field by multiplicity alone. Its
Theorem 5.2 retains height; the corollary removes it using height<degree.
The map from Q to the Laurent monomial ring is homogeneous, its kernel
is prime, distinct columns forbid linear forms, the field C qualifies,
and Sturmfels gives multiplicity V. Therefore this published corollary,
together with step 5, already implies an existential bound `F(V)=B(V)-2`
for the exact finite-hole dataset formulation. This subsumption does not
establish a particular quartic's priority or a sharp linear conclusion.
