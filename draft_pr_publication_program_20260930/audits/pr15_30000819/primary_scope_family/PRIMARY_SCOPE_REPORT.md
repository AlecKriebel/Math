## 3. Very ampleness, chosen lattice, and normalization

Beck--Delgado--Gubeladze--Michalek, arXiv:1307.7422v4 (5 December 2014),
Proposition 2.1 on PDF pp. 3--4 applies to an arbitrary finite point
configuration. It equates normality of `Proj K[A]`, saturation of the vertex
charts in `L(A)`, and finiteness of
`(cone(P,1) intersect gp(M_A)) minus M_A`. Section 2.3 then defines very ample
configurations by those equivalent properties, with height the final coordinate.

Merely saying that `O(1)` is a very ample line bundle on an arbitrary, possibly
nonnormal `Proj K[A]` would be insufficient: the paper explicitly constructs
that bundle before imposing normality. The finite-hole assumption in the audited
proof correctly avoids that ambiguity. The relevant saturation is in the
generated group, not automatically in a larger ambient lattice.

Source: [BDGM primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.7422v4).

Hochster's original 1972 paper, printed p. 320 (PDF p. 3), Proposition 1 and
Theorem 1, identifies normal monomial semigroups and makes their algebras
Cohen--Macaulay over a Cohen--Macaulay coefficient ring. This applies to the
pointed normal affine semigroup `overline S`; no standard-degree-one generation
of the normalization is required. Choosing coefficients in `C` is allowed.

Source: [Hochster original scan](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Hochster.pdf),

## 4. Checkable CCMPV subsumption certificate

### Imported theorem and its historical locator

CCMPV Theorem 5.2 bounds regularity using multiplicity `e` and height `c` for
homogeneous unmixed radical ideals. Corollary 5.3 removes height for nondegenerate
prime ideals over an algebraically closed field, using `c<e`. Both statements and
the proof were inspected on PDF pp. 10--11 of the NSF deposited manuscript;
the author-hosted version places them on pp. 11--12. The corollary asserts
existence of a constant `B(e)` independent of the number of variables. It does
not supply a sharp or quartic formula.

Sources: [original author manuscript](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~gcavigli/Articles-PDF/%5BM21%5D.pdf),
[NSF deposited manuscript](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10303748),
[publisher dates and DOI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-018-2089-y).

### Hypothesis-by-hypothesis application

Let `Q=C[x_1,...,x_n]`, `R=C[S]`, and `I=ker(Q -> R)`, sending `x_i` to
the monomial with exponent `(v_i,1)`.

1. **Standard grading and homogeneity.** All generators of `S` have height one;
   the map preserves degree, so `I` is homogeneous in the standard graded `Q`.
2. **Prime and radical.** `R` is a subalgebra of a Laurent polynomial domain.
   Therefore `I` is prime, in particular unmixed and radical.
3. **Nondegeneracy.** The points of `A` are distinct. Their degree-one Laurent
   monomials are linearly independent over `C`. Thus `I intersect Q_1=0` and
   `I` contains no linear form, exactly the theorem's nondegeneracy condition.
4. **Multiplicity.** Sturmfels Theorem 1.4 gives `e(Q/I)=V` in the generated
   affine lattice, including when points of `P` have been omitted from `A`.
5. **Coefficient field.** `C` is algebraically closed, as required. Hole
   membership and height are combinatorial and do not depend on this choice.
6. **Boundary cases.** Nonempty holes force affine dimension at least one and
   `I!=0`; a one-point or free configuration has no holes. Thus the use of
   positive height/codimension and normalization dimension at least two is sound.

CCMPV Corollary 5.3 now yields `reg(I)<=B(V)`.

### Hole height follows from regularity

Write `T=C[overline S]` and `C_holes=T/R`. Normalization is finite, so `T` is a
finite graded `R`-module. Finite holes make `C_holes` a finite-length module whose
last nonzero degree is `h`. The radical of `R_+ T` is `T_+`: every normalized
positive-height monomial has a positive power in `R_+`. Hochster and
`dim(T)>=2` therefore give `H^0_{R_+}(T)=H^1_{R_+}(T)=0`.
The domain `R` has `H^0_{R_+}(R)=0`, while finite length gives
`H^0_{R_+}(C_holes)=C_holes`. The long exact sequence yields

`C_holes = H^1_{R_+}(R)`.

Consequently `h+1<=reg(R)=reg(I)-1`, and hence

`h <= reg(I)-2 <= B(V)-2`.

This proves the exact existential `F(V)` assertion for every selected
configuration satisfying the finite-hole hypothesis. For an ambient normalized
volume `W` with intrinsic `V` dividing `W`, the finite maximum
`F_ambient(W)=max_{1<=v<=W} B(v)-2` supplies a bound even if `B` is not
monotone. No conjectural linear bound is used.
