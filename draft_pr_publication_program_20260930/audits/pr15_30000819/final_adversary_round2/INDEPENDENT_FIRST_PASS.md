## Reconstruction and adversarial checks

1. The generated group splits as G' plus Z a_i at a free column: uniqueness of the real a_i coefficient makes the saturation split as Sbar' plus N a_i. Every existing hole then propagates along N a_i. Thus finite nonempty holes exclude every configuration pyramid, including repeated geometric pyramids. This lemma does not assert that a normal pyramid has holes.
2. Primitive circuit relations span the rational kernel by support reduction. Homogeneity makes each signed side sum to its binomial degree; nonzero integer entries therefore give support at most twice that degree. A circuit basis covers every coordinate exactly when there is no free column. Hence n <= 2Vc. The primitive circuit degree <= V is verified directly in Sturmfels (4.4), with volume measured in the lattice affinely generated by A (Theorem 1.4).
3. A triangulation using all distinct selected points exists by successive stellar insertion. Its full-dimensional dual adjacency graph is connected for a convex polytope. A spanning-tree ordering bounds its total vertices by d+t, while lattice simplex volumes are positive integers summing to intrinsic V. Hence c = n-d-1 <= V-1. No replacement of A by all polytope lattice points is made.
4. Sturmfels Theorem 4.5 uses ideal regularity, confirmed by the syzygy definition immediately preceding it, and bounds it by n times degree times codimension. Lemma 4.6 and (4.4)-(4.5) apply to homogeneous toric ideals without normality. Graver degree <= Vc, a reduced Groebner basis lies in the Graver basis, and the Taylor least-common-multiple bound gives <= nVc. No projective-normality hypothesis enters.
5. Saturation is the normalization of C[S] in its fraction field and is finite over R. Its generators may have degrees above one; the proof never assumes standard grading of the normalization. Since every positive saturation monomial has a positive power in S, the extension of R_+ has radical Rbar_+. Hochster Cohen-Macaulayness thus kills H^0 and H^1 of Rbar when rank >= 2. C = Rbar/R is finite length precisely under the finite-hole assumption, and the exact sequence identifies C with H^1(R). Therefore reg(R) >= h+1 and reg(I_A) = reg(R)+1 give the offset h <= reg(I_A)-2. Combining n <= 2Vc and c <= V-1 gives h <= 2 V^2 (V-1)^2 - 2.
6. A zero-dimensional distinct configuration has one generator and no holes. Rank-one, c=0, and free configurations likewise have no holes in their generated group. V=1 forces t=1 and n=d+1, hence no holes; the displayed formula is expressly restricted to nonempty holes (V>=2). The zero semigroup element is always present, so nonempty holes have positive height. Empty holes have no maximum; an optional h=-1 convention is separated from the theorem. Ambient volume is an integer multiple of intrinsic volume; the polynomial bound is increasing for V>=2. If an ambient group is larger, a missing lattice coset contains infinitely many positive-cone points by translating a point by a sufficiently interior generated-group lattice element, giving infinitely many ambient holes. Finite ambient holes therefore require equality of lattices.
7. For A_m={0,1,m-1,m}, choosing j upper-pair summands produces exactly the interval [j(m-1),j(m-1)+k]. Adjacent interval starts differ by m-1; degree k>=1 has no gap exactly when k>=m-2. Degree zero has only zero. Thus h=m-3 for m>=4. The pyramid negative control is mathematically invalid as a finite-hole counterexample for the reason in item 1.
