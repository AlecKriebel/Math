# Checkable implication from the classical results

This is the reviewer's derivation, not an assertion that an earlier source printed this correction. All thresholds use known zero mean, n Gaussian columns, and scatter proportional to XX^T.

Let G=K4,4 with parts L={1,2,3,4}, R={5,6,7,8}. Let pi record the eight diagonal entries and all sixteen cross-block entries of an8-by8 symmetric matrix S. The OWR model permits singleton vertex and edge partitions, which yields exactly this ordinary graphical model.

## Exact ideal bridge

Write R[s,t] for the polynomial ring and let J_r be generated by every (r+1)-minor of S and the equations t=pi(S). Define I_r=J_r intersect R[t], as in the source.

For any g(t) in I4 and any complex symmetric S of rank at most4, every defining generator of J4 vanishes at (S,pi(S)), hence g(pi(S))=0. The Blekherman–Sinn rank4 dominance result says the image of that rank variety is Zariski dense in all24 statistic coordinates. Therefore g is identically zero. Thus I4=(0). This direction needs neither equality of a vanishing ideal with J4 nor radicality.

Let B=S[L,R]. The determinant det(B) is a4-by4 minor of S using rows L and columns R. It is a generator of the rank-at-most3 minor ideal. Replacing its entries by the corresponding distinct edge-statistic variables changes it by a polynomial in the linear equations t=pi(S). Its statistic form therefore belongs to I3. It is not the zero polynomial, because its value at cross-statistic matrix I4 is1. Thus I3 is nonzero. This last test is a polynomial nonzeroness test; it does not assert that those statistics come from a rank3 or PSD matrix.

## Interior point and thresholds

The earlier bipartite splitting proof has the following specialization. Put S*=diag(J4,J4). This is PSD of rank2. Its observed diagonals equal1 and every observed cross entry equals0, so pi(S*)=pi(I8). As I8 is PD, this tuple belongs to the source's open sufficient-statistic cone.

Every g in I3 vanishes on any actual PSD rank2 matrix, since rank2<=3. Consequently pi(I8) belongs to V_R(I3). This verifies the second numerical scenario's antecedent using a real PSD preimage, rather than only a Zariski-closure point.

A positive-definite completion for S* persists for an open set of data around a Gram factor of S*: the inverse image of the open cone pi(PD) under X to pi(XX^T) is open and contains that factor. A nondegenerate Gaussian density is positive there. This is the correct positive-probability bridge. Gross–Sullivant already supplies it.

With one data column x, any matching PD completion would have the observed2-by2 edge principal submatrix [[xi^2,xi*xj],[xi*xj,xj^2]], whose determinant is zero. No PD completion can have that singular principal submatrix. Therefore WMLT>=2; the previous construction gives WMLT2.

At n=3, the exact ideal hypotheses I4=0 and I3 nonzero now hold. The report's second bullet would force WMLT3, contrary to the older WMLT2. Separately, the Blekherman–Sinn MLT formula gives MLT4: binomial(4,2)=6<8<=10=binomial(5,2), hence M=4 and min(M,5)=4. The gcr theorem uses4<=binomial(4,2)=6, hence gcr4. These are distinct calculations. Neither generic rank nor the zero-ideal criterion was silently used as an MLT lower bound.

Because the tuple pi(I8) is a zero of all I3 polynomials, no generator pullback evaluated on its positive-diagonal Cholesky factor can be everywhere nonvanishing. The strict signed-SOS mechanism cannot supply a missing numerical case for this example. The positive-definite-intersection mechanism itself is present, so this does not disprove the Boolean alternative.

## Color tie as a consequence, not an earlier printed claim

Let T:R24 to R23 replace the first two diagonal coordinates by their sum and leave all other outputs unchanged. The new model imposes K11=K22 in concentration coordinates; its projection is pi'=T pi.

Because T is surjective and pi is dominant at rank4, pi' is dominant there as well. The same previous vanishing argument gives I'4=0. All sixteen cross-statistic variables remain independent outputs, so det(B) still gives a nonzero element of I'3. The PSD/PD equality pi(S*)=pi(I8) survives composition with T, so WMLT'<=2. For edge3–5 neither diagonal is involved in the tie; its singular one-sample principal submatrix gives WMLT'>=2. Thus the same numerical contradiction survives a genuine vertex-color tie.

This transfer is a short deduction from the older witness and rank-dominance result. It is not evidence that the exact tied example, or the tied model's exact MLT, was explicitly published before. The universal original literal numerical assertion is already refuted by the singleton example, so that historical distinction does not create a new resolution of that universal assertion.
