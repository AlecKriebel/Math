2. Compactification gives an interval suspension with a complete flat horizontal connection, since fibers are compact and endpoint collars invariant. Its underlying oriented interval bundle trivializes relative to both collars: the local coordinate average has positive fiber derivative and maps endpoints to endpoints, hence a smooth fiberwise inverse. This does not trivialize holonomy. Normalize a defining form on the positive vertical vector; Frobenius contraction yields d alpha = alpha wedge i_V d alpha. On collars this gives alpha = ds and eta = 0.
3. Capping the interval with a product arc gives the circle suspension of the same compactified action. The defining forms match smoothly and the added region has zero eta wedge d eta. The block integral therefore equals the nonzero closed suspension value, rather than an arbitrary boundary-sensitive number.
4. An orientation preserving pseudo-Anosov mapping class exists on the selected closed surface of genus at least two. Smooth Dehn twists supported in thin neighborhoods of a filling pair of multicurves leave some complementary disk fixed. Fathi printed pp.467–468 provides the relevant Penner theorem; actual pseudo-Anosov normal form is not required of this smooth representative. Thurston Theorem 0.1, Proposition 2.6 and the proof in section 5 apply to its mapping torus and give a closed finite-volume hyperbolic manifold.
5. Rescaling each positive-width slab by h, alpha_j = length h*alpha, eta_j = h*eta preserves d alpha_j = alpha_j wedge eta_j and gives alpha_j(partial_t) = 1. In exact open collars it equals dt,0. There is no infinite-stack smoothness assertion or uniform derivative bound. Fixing the manifold and one metric fixes its volume independently of N.
