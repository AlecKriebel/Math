Let the positive terms be `x1*y2`, `x2*y3`, `x3*y1`; the negative terms are `x2*y1`, `x3*y2`, `x1*y3`. With columns ordered as all three positive terms followed by all three negative terms, the six exponent rows respectively constrain

```
x1: mu1+nu3       x2: mu2+nu1       x3: mu3+nu2
y1: mu3+nu1       y2: mu1+nu2       y3: mu2+nu3.
```

The prescribed identity augmentation adds `mu1+nu1`, `mu2+nu2`, and `mu3+nu3`. Thus the exact nine constraints are precisely

`mu_i+nu_j <= 1`, for every `i,j` in `{1,2,3}`, together with coordinate nonnegativity. All nine are retained. The objective is the sum of all six coordinates, and the domain in the question is rational points.

## Entire real polytope by an independent convex decomposition

Set `a=max_i mu_i` and `b=max_j nu_j`. The nine inequalities imply `a+b<=1`. If `a,b>0`, then

```
(mu,nu) = a*(mu/a,0) + b*(0,nu/b) + (1-a-b)*0.
```

Each nonzero normalized vector lies in its three-dimensional unit cube. Zero coefficients can simply be omitted when `a=0` or `b=0`. Conversely each of the two cubes, and therefore their convex hull, satisfies every pair inequality. Consequently

`P_R = conv(([0,1]^3 x {0}) union ({0} x [0,1]^3))`.

Its vertices are exactly the fifteen distinct zero-one cube vertices: the common origin and seven nonzero vertices in each cube. Each is extreme because a zero-one vector in `[0,1]^6` cannot be a nontrivial convex combination of different points in that box. This identifies the full polytope without any finite-sampling inference.

## Entire optimal face and universal fiber failure

For every feasible point,

`sum(mu)+sum(nu) <= 3a+3b <= 3`.

The value three is attained by either full-cube endpoint. Equality requires both `a+b=1` and every coordinate in its own block equal to its block maximum. Hence the full real optimal face is

`(mu,nu)=(t,t,t,1-t,1-t,1-t)`, `0<=t<=1`.

The full rational optimal face is exactly this segment with rational `t`: any rational optimizer has `t=mu1`, and conversely rational `t` gives rational coordinates. Each of the nine pair sums is one. Thus every optimal point has image `1_9`.

For any rational `t` choose `s=0` if `t!=0`, and `s=1` otherwise. Then `s` is rational, belongs to `[0,1]`, differs from `t`, and has the same nine-coordinate image. This proves that **every** rational optimizer, including each endpoint, fails the required singleton-fiber condition. The segment has only one image vector but no point with a singleton fiber.

More strongly, if a feasible point has the same image as an optimizer, its objective is the same because that objective is the sum of the three augmentation coordinates. Thus the feasible fiber and the optimal fiber agree for these images.

## Rank, kernel, and dual independently

For a homogeneous-kernel vector `(u,v)`, every equation is `u_i+v_j=0`. Fixing one `j` forces all `u_i` equal, and then every `v_j` is their negative. Therefore the kernel over either the rationals or the reals is the one-dimensional span of `(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)` and the rank is five.

The primal endpoint `(1,1,1,0,0,0)` is feasible with value three. A dual vector with zero weights on the six exponent rows and unit weights on the three augmentation rows has nonnegative entries, transpose image equal to the all-one objective vector, and value three. Weak duality alone certifies the optimum; no numerical solver or strong-duality assumption is required.

## Hypotheses and transformations

Evaluation at the all-one point annihilates each binomial and cannot annihilate a nonzero monomial. The ideal therefore contains no monomial. The six distinct degree-two monomials give three independent degree-two binomials, so none of the three generators can be generated by the other two: a homogeneous degree-two component of such a relation would have to be a constant linear combination. The full minimal-generator assertion follows from the three-dimensional quotient by the homogeneous maximal ideal, with all higher-degree ideal elements in that maximal-ideal multiple.

Changing the generator order, exchanging the two terms in any generator, or renaming the six ring variables permutes the LP coordinates/rows. It preserves feasible points, objective, and fiber cardinalities. Nonzero generator scalar multiples and invertible diagonal rescalings of ring variables preserve the exponent matrix and the algebraic hypotheses. One must not extend this to arbitrary unrelated coefficients: a cyclic coefficient product different from one makes a monomial belong to the ideal, as the explicit checker verifies.
