All four controls have outer ellipse x²/8+y²/5=1 and original foci (±sqrt(3),0), unit inversion radius, positive cyclic traversal, convex winding +1 and primitive period equal to the listed number of vertices. Their higher-period/star generalization is supplied by the paper's proof, not by finite sampling.

## Odd controls

At lambda=40/9, the caustic has squared axes 32/9 and 5/9. The ordered triangles are:

* H: (2sqrt(2),0), (-4sqrt(2)/3,5/3), (-4sqrt(2)/3,-5/3).
* V: (0,sqrt(5)), (-8/3,-sqrt(5)/3), (8/3,-sqrt(5)/3).

In focus order +sqrt(3), then -sqrt(3), H has inverted signed areas sqrt(2)/10-sqrt(3)/80 and sqrt(2)/10+sqrt(3)/80. V has areas sqrt(5)/16 and sqrt(5)/16. Both products are exactly 5/256. These two triangles were present in the preserved submitted controls.

For each edge with normalized line u*x+v*y=1, the direct confocal tangency equation is alpha²u²+beta²v²=1. The contact is (alpha²u,beta²v), and its edge fraction must lie strictly between 0 and 1. If d_in and d_out are unit edge velocities and n=(x/8,y/5), reflection is checked coordinatewise by d_out=d_in-2(d_in·n)n/(n·n). The portable verifier applies these tests to every edge and vertex, including the closing edge, before testing image areas.

## Same-caustic even counterexample

At lambda=40/13, the inner semiaxes are alpha=8/sqrt(13), beta=5/sqrt(13). The positive primitive four-period orbits are:

* Diamond: (2sqrt(2),0), (0,sqrt(5)), (-2sqrt(2),0), (0,-sqrt(5)).
* Rectangle: (8/sqrt(13),5/sqrt(13)), (-8/sqrt(13),5/sqrt(13)), (-8/sqrt(13),-5/sqrt(13)), (8/sqrt(13),-5/sqrt(13)).

The diamond's focal inverse areas are sqrt(10)/10 each, with product 1/10. The rectangle's are 13/40 each, with product 169/1600. Their difference is 9/1600, so extension to all even periods is false for a single fixed proper confocal pair.

More generally, for S=a²+b² and lambda=a²b²/S, the axis diamond and rectangle (±a²/sqrt(S),±b²/sqrt(S)) are genuine primitive four-period orbits. Tangency and reflection are shown in the paper. Their focal inverse products are respectively 4/(a²b²) and S²/(a⁴b⁴), differing by (a²-b²)²/(a⁴b⁴)>0. The finite verifier checks the specific rational-squared-axis example above. These even controls were derived during verification after candidate exposure and carry no independent-discovery or novelty claim.
