## Historical claim and conventions

Reznik--Garcia--Koiller, [arXiv:2004.12497v11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12497v11), dated 29 October 2020:

* p. 3 Eq. (1) defines signed cross-product/shoelace polygon area.
* §3.9 pp. 9--10 defines inversion of the ORIGINAL orbit vertices in unit-radius circles at the ORIGINAL billiard foci.
* Table 10 p. 12 row k903,a gives the product of the two inverse-polygon areas for odd N, dated **10/20**.

The shorter 2021 journal edition, DOI [10.1007/s40598-021-00174-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40598-021-00174-y), omits that row and is not a substitute for the expanded v11 target. Historical question marks are not claims of present-day openness. Outer-tangent/polar/pedal polygons, their foci, curved inverted edges, and unsigned enclosed regions are different observables.

Reznik--Garcia--Helman, [arXiv:2012.03020v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03020v2), dated 13 July 2021, p. 6 Proposition 7 gives the N=3 product, immediately followed by Conjecture 1 for odd N>=3. This is explicit antecedent credit. The paper's precise primitive/admitted-star and arbitrary-r indexing statements are its proved formulation, rather than quotations of the brief historical row. Repetition bookkeeping supplies no novelty distinction.

The RGH preprint's delta radicand on p. 3 has a minus-b⁴ defect at a²=8,b²=5. A qualified plus-b⁴ comparison is made only at that exact control, where delta=7 and product=5/256. A general corrected formula and the uninspected RGH journal typography are not claimed. The journal's bibliographic DOI is 10.1007/s40879-021-00489-2. The proof here does not depend on the triangle formula.

## Exact theorem and mechanism overlap

Alper Ferudun, *Products of the Two Focal Inversion Areas in Elliptic Billiards*, version 1.0, 2 October 2026, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23096994](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23096994), Theorem 1 and §§2--5, proves the same signed unit-focal-inverse odd product for strict proper confocal ellipse pairs, with admitted stars, reversal, positive product and repetitions. The complete six-page reading copy was examined in the bounded comparison. Its PDF is 84,112 bytes, SHA-256 `c7fbcc7e5a7b7746ad102cb7cf0f5e9eb6aa149009d88c3654fdbcc3484126c0`. No copy is distributed here. A DOI and this examination do not imply peer review.

Both arguments use the same essential divisor architecture: meromorphic inverse areas, a pole-order bound at adjacent singular vertices, reversal oddness, odd separation of focal pole grids, opposite-focus zeros, and compactness. The present note uses intrinsic flags and common isotropic tangents; Ferudun uses Jacobi coordinates and a quotient torus. These coordinate/presentation choices do not establish a new theorem or new method. The source also discusses boundaries beyond the strict theorem here; this note makes no broader verification claim. A localized printed finite even-control coordinate discrepancy in that source does not alter the direct overlap of its main odd-period theorem, and is not used to assert priority.

## Established methods and parameter source

* Akopyan--Schwartz--Tabachnikov, *Billiards in ellipses revisited*, [arXiv:2001.02934v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02934v2): geometric/complex-analytic billiard invariants and pole removal. The cited area ratio is a different observable.
* Ana C. Chavez-Caliz, *More About Areas and Centers of Poncelet Polygons*, Arnold Mathematical Journal 7 (2021), 91--106, [DOI 10.1007/s40598-020-00154-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40598-020-00154-8): compact flag cover, involutions, translation, and meromorphic area arguments. The correct DOI suffix is **-8**. Its even original/outer-tangent area product does not directly identify the two focal inverse polygons.
* Richard Schwartz, *Monodromy and Poncelet Families*, 9 August 2008, [author copy](https://www.math.brown.edu/reschwar/Papers/poncelet.pdf): earlier flag-torus and degenerate-flag methods. This is methodological antecedence, not an assertion that arbitrary rational metric observables are constant.
* Hellmuth Stachel, *On the motion of billiards in ellipses*, European Journal of Mathematics 8 (2022), 1602--1622, [DOI 10.1007/s40879-021-00524-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40879-021-00524-2), Theorem 4.3 p. 1614: modulus k=c/alpha, vertices (-a sn u,b cn u), step 2v and v=2nu K/N for coprime winding. The software parameter is k². The proof does not rely on the printed dn anti-period sign or a complex Jacobi lattice shortcut.

T-invariance alone permits nonconstant meromorphic functions on the finite-translation quotient. Reversal alone restricts Laurent parity. The target-specific complete pole inventory, adjacent-pole bound, odd-grid separation and opposite zeros are proved explicitly in the note rather than inferred from those general methods.
