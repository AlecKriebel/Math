# What would make a general antecedent a prior full solution

This is a priority test, not a new attempt to prove the target. An earlier theorem counts even if it never names this flag or prints D. “The candidate uses known results” and “an earlier classification already provides the complete answer by substitution” are different assessments; the latter must be established by exact scope and input identification.

The h-principle and ordinary homogeneous-map results already supply substantial reductions. Formal data are pairs (f, TMC≅f*TF), modulo simultaneous homotopy. The literal question already points to this formal reduction. A noncanonical frame description after orientable-source parallelizability does not make the construction new.

For orientable M, the correct compact frame target is E=(SU3×U3)/T², rather than F=SU3/T². The determinant character has weights (−4,−2), so E has π1=Z/2, π2=Z and π3=Z². Its finite universal cover is (SU3×SU3)/S¹. Koshkin's complete theorem classifies maps to that simply connected homogeneous cover and therefore applies to liftable subcases. For maps M→E that fail to lift, one must include the π1 character and the induced deck/monodromy identification; replacing E by its cover can lose actual regular-homotopy sectors. For nonorientable M, the fixed complexified tangent bundle has c1=βw1 and the formal problem is a twisted isomorphism/section problem. Neither issue is solved by dropping a hypothesis in Koshkin's theorem.

An equivalent prior classification can defeat the full claim if its actual theorem and proof, without an unsupported central extension, supply all of these:

1. The appropriate section or simultaneous-homotopy classification for a fixed complex rank-three source bundle over every ordinary three-dimensional CW model, including noncompact/infinite CW and nontrivial orientation character.
2. Classification of primary pairs satisfying the determinant compatibility, with the actual flag tangent root representation rather than a different almost complex structure or unordered flags.
3. The full homotopy action when the underlying base map moves. A classification only of isomorphisms over one fixed map, or ordinary maps into F, is incomplete for this target.
4. Integral secondary invariants and the stabilizer/monodromy action, retaining ordinary H3 torsion. A de Rham or rational classification cannot be equivalent for closed nonorientable M.
5. Transparent identification of the resulting quotient with the candidate's H3⊕coker D. If an earlier theorem gives this quotient up to an explicit change of generators, that is prior even though the printed coefficients differ.

The Derdzinski–Januszkiewicz n3 note meets the determinant-compatible existence portion. Its n2 injectivity proof does not meet item 4 in n3: the cylinder dimension rises from three to four, exposing π3(SU3). The Falbel–Veloso sphere proof meets a special secondary-fiber case. Duchamp's Euclidean classification has no moving-base action. The survey's local null-curvature theorem and scalar invariant do not assert the necessary global injectivity. These exact failures of transfer do not show that another stronger general theorem is absent.

This flag corpus investigation has not established an all-source equivalent antecedent. The independent general topology priority investigation is necessary to decide that stronger issue. Until that assessment is combined, a claim of novelty from absent specialized search hits would be invalid. The candidate's plausible added content is only an explicit evaluated specialization, not invention of the h-principle, K-theory, obstruction theory, primary/secondary classification, or formal frame description.
