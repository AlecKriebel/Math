**Conclusion: no positive novelty verdict is defensible yet.** Primary texts already establish all general mechanisms used here; the exact broad integral flag formula has not been found in the inspected texts. Lack of an exact formula does not show novelty. The candidate should be treated as a worked specialization of classical methods whose priority is unresolved, not as a new homotopy-theory technique.

1. Koshkin 2009, published pp331–346, was inspected completely, including all definitions and proofs. Theorem 3 (p343) assumes compact simply connected homogeneous X and, under the paper's standing convention, compact connected simply connected G and connected H. The formal framed target (SU3 x U3)/T2 has pi1=Z/2 (the determinant torus map has image 2Z), so the theorem does not literally classify it. But Lemma 4 (p341) explicitly allows any compact connected G and closed H: homotopy is equivalent to a nullhomotopic shift, and the proof of Theorem 3 gives [M,G]/image(pi0 Stab) as an orbit quotient before invoking simple connectivity to identify [M,G]. Therefore it would be wrong to claim that the quotient mechanism is an additional contribution just because Theorem 3 does not apply verbatim. The exact cokernel requires evaluation of the stabilizer inclusion and, for nontrivial E, a section/gauge variant.
2. Koshkin's published de Rham Corollary 1, pp344–345, deletes the nonorientable secondary invariant by asserting H3(M;Z)=0. This assertion is false for closed nonorientable three-manifolds, where ordinary integral H3 can be Z/2. The underlying integral Theorem 3 must be separated from this defective corollary. The inspected 2009 gauge-successor preprint retains simply connected homogeneous targets and closed sources (pp5–6); it is not the required extension.
3. Duchamp's complete three-page author-hosted preprint classifies totally real immersions into Cn by K1(M) after fixing an immersion (the proof is an explicit U(n)-stabilization argument). This supplies the Euclidean framing mechanism, not the monodromy of a varying map into the flag. Its PDF says Date:1984 but includes 1986 references, so the file does not prove a precise 1984 publication date. The author's bibliography calls it a preprint.
4. Gottlieb 1972, published pp23–50: inspected topology part pp23–33 and the proof of Theorem5.2 with relative Theorem5.6. Principal bundle-map space to universal bundle is weakly contractible; gauge transformations are weakly equivalent to the loop space of the classifying-map component (p32), with arbitrary CW base and no trivial-bundle restriction. Thus the gauge treatment at nontrivial E is itself prior, not a new mechanism. It does not compute the simultaneous T2 representation inclusion used in the candidate.
5. Derdzinski–Januszkiewicz, arXiv math/0612855v4 and author manuscript: inspected Sections1–4 including Theorem2.1, Remark3.2, Proposition3.1 and proof. Remark3.2 identifies the determinant condition for arbitrary dimension; crucially p12 explicitly says the surjectivity/existence argument still works for n=3 because SU3 is 2-connected. The full surface classification cannot be transferred to three-manifolds because the injectivity proof has a dimension bound; the three-dimensional existence mechanism is nevertheless explicit prior work.
6. Auckly–Kapitanski 2002 preprint/2003 publication, Proposition1, pp3–5 of preprint: classifies [M,G] as a set via H1 and H3 for a compact Lie group and closed three-manifold. Its proof, and Auckly–Speight 2004 preprint/2006 publication pp5–7, make the set/group distinction explicit. These provide determinant plus secondary coordinates, but do not compute the coupled T2 stabilizer action. Later de Rham claims again need orientability; no broad torsion inference should be taken from integral evaluation alone.
7. Membrillo-Solis–Theriault 2021 author's accepted manuscript pp1–5 supplies a modern nontrivial U(n)-gauge example, integral stable BU(n) reduction and gauge/mapping-space relation, but is a gauge homotopy-type classification over lens/Moore spaces; no flag-frame reduction appears in the inspected core. It is not an equivalent solution.

The defensible possible addition is narrow: the explicit coupled integral stabilizer action for the two torus representations, with determinant Bockstein, nonorientable ordinary-cohomology torsion, and ordinary noncompact homotopies. It could still be a routine unpublished-to-us specialization or have been written under a general bundle-reduction classification. General Postnikov/Whitehead primary proofs and broader successor citation coverage remain incomplete and prevent novelty certification.
