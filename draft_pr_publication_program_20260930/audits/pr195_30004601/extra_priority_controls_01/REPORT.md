# Additional bounded priority checks: two nine-factor examples

Root supplement, 15 October 2026 UTC. This extends the earlier dated priority audit. It does not replace its historical records or claim worldwide priority. The earlier audit left these generic initial ideals uncomputed. A full gin is unnecessary to exclude these two particular examples: absolute maximum ranks in a few low degrees suffice.

The original defining equations were checked in the primary texts:

- Nasrollah Nejad–Simis, *Closing two recent conjectures related to the Jacobian ideal of hyperplane arrangements*, arXiv:2606.18693v2, Example3.4: `xyzw(w+y)(w+z)(w-x+y)(w-x+z)(w-x+y+z)`. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18693v2
- Walther, *The Jacobian module, the Milnor fiber, and the D-module generated by f^s*, Invent. Math.207 (2017),1239–1287, Example5.7 (bracelet): `x1*x2*x3*(x1+x0)*(x2+x0)*(x3+x0)*(x1+x2+x0)*(x1+x3+x0)*(x2+x3+x0)`. https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07164

Both normal systems have rank four. The certificate records the normal rows, coherent ternary/binary section maps, pairwise distinct restricted factors, and exact Macaulay rank minors. Four degree-eight ambient derivatives are taken before restriction. No third-party code is used.

For both arrangements the exact quotient dimensions are

`H2(8,9,10)=(5,2,0)`, and `H3(7,8,9)=(36,41,43)`.

Every relevant matrix rank is the absolute maximum permitted by its dimensions: binary ranks4,8,11 in degrees8,9,10 and ternary ranks4,12 in degrees8,9. The ternary degree10 rank24 is also maximal, but is not needed for the exclusion. Nonzero exact minors imply that these maximum ranks hold on nonempty open sets. The chosen maps are compatible and extend to full invertible coordinate bases, so their common open set meets the revlex-gin open. The generic section identity therefore gives precisely these Hilbert values, rather than merely favorable sample bounds. The degree-seven ternary value36 follows because the ideal starts in degree eight.

Strong stability and the binary dimensions give pure-second-variable cutoff10. In the first three-variable truncation, multiplication by the third variable has kernels

`H3(7)-H3(8)+H2(8)=36-41+5=0`, and
`H3(8)-H3(9)+H2(9)=41-43+2=0`.

There are no ideal elements below degree eight. Hence there is no minimal generator whose largest variable is the third before degree10. For the broader wording “involving the third,” strong stability also excludes an earlier generator involving a later variable: replace later variables by the third and take a minimal divisor. If that divisor involves the third it contradicts the truncation conclusion; if it uses only the first two variables it already divides the original generator. Either contradicts minimality. Thus neither specific example, nor a linearly equivalent presentation, is an earlier refutation of the target bound.
