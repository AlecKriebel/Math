## Adversarial audit of the generic argument

The following reasoning, rather than the finite flags, justifies genericity.

1. The seven ambient factors are distinct in characteristic zero, and the four coordinate factors make the arrangement essential. On the stated open set of sections, the restricted factors are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional. Reducing a linear polar relation modulo one factor is legitimate: its quotient polynomial ring is a domain and every other factor has nonzero image. Thus the corresponding linear multiplier is a scalar multiple of that factor. The converse is exact, and the remaining condition is the sum of the seven scalars being zero.

2. Independence of the four polars is not assumed. The same reduction applied to constant multipliers forces every ambient normal applied to the constant vector to vanish. The four coordinate normals force that vector to be zero. Hence all four generators remain minimal in degree 6.

3. Independence of X and Y forces a common scalar on the four pencil factors. With remaining scalars b,c,d, the only compatibility conditions are `(a-d)X+(b-d)Z+(c-d)W=0` and `4a+b+c+d=0`. In three dimensions, independence of X,Z,W makes all four scalars equal and characteristic zero gives 7a=0 only for a=0. In two dimensions their relation space has dimension one; adjoining the trace equation removes exactly one dimension because its coefficient on the common scalar is 7. The map from scalars to actual multiplier polynomials is injective. This counts all linear syzygies, with both upper and lower bounds.

4. The binary height-two argument works over an algebraic closure. A common projective zero of the four ambient polars would, by restricted Euler, lie on a restricted factor. Pairwise nonproportionality means precisely one factor vanishes there, so the polar vector is a nonzero product times that factor's nonzero ambient normal. This is a contradiction. There is no hidden gcd or saturation assumption.

5. Hilbert–Burch therefore gives three free first syzygies with positive coefficient degrees. The double zero of the Artinian Hilbert numerator at 1 forces their degrees to sum to 6. Exactly one linear syzygy leaves only the multiset (1,2,3). This proves H2(6),H2(7),H2(8)=(3,1,0) on the entire specified open set, not just at one point.

6. The compatible explicit flag proves nonemptiness of these opens in the complete-flag parameter space. It extends to an invertible four-coordinate change; for example the rows `(1,0,0,0),(0,1,0,0),(2,3,1,0),(7,11,3,1)` have determinant 1. Nonempty opens in the irreducible characteristic-zero coordinate-change space intersect the generic-initial-ideal open. Thus the separately established generic-section/grevlex identity applies to the proved section values. This review checks the use of that classical identity, while the parent source audit is responsible for its exact source citation.

7. Strong stability makes the second-variable pure power standard whenever any binary degree-k standard monomial exists. Therefore the positive degree-7 binary quotient and zero degree-8 quotient give threshold exactly 8. The multiplication kernel in the ternary quotient has dimension `H3(k-1)-H3(k)+H2(k)`, equal to 0 at k=6 and 1 at k=7. With no elements below degree 6, the first nonzero such kernel produces a degree-7 minimal generator divisible by x3. Minimality also holds in four variables because a divisor of a monomial in the first three variables cannot involve x4.

No circularity, use of a single specialization as generic, reversal of semicontinuity, replacement of ambient polars, or unsupported upper rank bound was found.
