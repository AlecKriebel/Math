## 1. The arrangement and the ambient ideal

All seven coefficient vectors are nonzero, and no pair is proportional in characteristic zero. The factors x,y,z,w are present, so their common kernel is zero: the arrangement is essential. All factors are homogeneous linear forms, so it is central. The degree is 7. The homogeneous Euler identity is

\[
xQ_x+yQ_y+zQ_z+wQ_w=7Q.
\]

Consequently the ideal generated by Q and its four partials equals the ideal J generated by the four partials. Division by 7 is legitimate over every field in scope. The four generating forms have degree 6.

For a linear section C:K^r -> K^4, write the four coordinate restrictions as X,Y,Z,W, and write the seven factor restrictions as L_i. If f_j is Q_j restricted along C, differentiation of the product in the ambient four variables gives

\[
\sum_j u_j f_j=\sum_i (a_i\cdot u)\prod_{h\ne i}L_h.
\]

This identity involves four ambient polars. Replacing them with the r derivatives of the restricted product would change the ideal and invalidate the argument. The submitted proof does not make that replacement. My independent verifier constructs these ambient polars by the product rule before forming section polynomials.

## 2. Divisibility characterizes all linear syzygies

Assume the seven L_i are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional. For each i, the quotient K[v_1,...,v_r]/(L_i) is a polynomial ring in r-1 variables over K, hence an integral domain. Each other L_h has a nonzero image in that domain. Reducing a relation modulo L_i therefore forces L_i to divide a_i dot u. When u has homogeneous linear entries, there is a unique scalar lambda_i such that

\[
a_i\cdot u=\lambda_i L_i.
\]

The converse is also exact: after substitution, the syzygy expression equals (sum lambda_i) times the nonzero product of all seven L_i. It vanishes precisely when sum lambda_i=0. There are no additional relations lost by reduction modulo the factors, and no saturation, localization, or assumption about generic matrix ranks is used here.

The same argument for constant entries u_j gives a_i dot u=0 for every i, because a nonzero linear form cannot divide a nonzero constant. The coefficient rows of x,y,z,w then force every u_j=0. Hence the four f_j are linearly independent, nonzero degree-6 forms and form a minimal generating set for the section ideal. This holds for every section satisfying the stated factor condition, not merely for the exhibited flag.

## 3. Solve the scalar system without a generic-rank assumption

The factors X,Y,X-Y, with X,Y linearly independent, imply that their three scalar multipliers coincide. The fourth factor X-2Y has the same multiplier a. Set the multipliers for Z,W,X+Z+W to b,c,d. Every linear syzygy is therefore equivalent to

\[
u_x=aX,\quad u_y=aY,\quad u_z=bZ,\quad u_w=cW,
\]
\[
(a-d)X+(b-d)Z+(c-d)W=0,
\qquad 4a+b+c+d=0. \tag{A}
\]

For r=3 the independence of X,Z,W forces a=b=c=d. The last equation is 7a=0. Thus all four multipliers and all four u_j vanish. There are no linear syzygies for any ternary section in the admissible open set.

For r=2, pairwise nonproportionality makes X,Z,W span a 2-dimensional space and makes their relation space exactly 1-dimensional. Choose any nonzero relation rho_x X+rho_z Z+rho_w W=0. All solutions of the first equation in (A) have

\[
a=d+t\rho_x,\quad b=d+t\rho_z,\quad c=d+t\rho_w.
\]

The remaining scalar equation is

\[
7d+t(4\rho_x+\rho_z+\rho_w)=0.
\]

Its rank is exactly one, independently of whether the parenthesized coefficient vanishes. Thus the solution space in (d,t) has dimension exactly one. The resulting map to the tuple u is injective: u=0 forces a=b=c=0 because X,Y,Z,W are nonzero, and then d(X+Z+W)=0 forces d=0. A nonzero relation rho and a=b=c=d=0 imply t=0. This verifies the binary syzygy dimension without assuming an unproved lower or upper rank bound.

This covers the possible exceptional scalar value 4rho_x+rho_z+rho_w=0. In that case d=0 and t is free, so the dimension remains one. The trace condition cannot disappear in characteristic zero.

## 4. Binary height two and Hilbert–Burch

Extend K to an algebraic closure. Suppose all four restricted ambient polars vanish at a projective binary point. The restricted ambient Euler identity implies product L_i=0 at that point. Distinct binary linear forms have distinct projective zeros; hence exactly one L_i vanishes there. The four polars at the point are then the nonzero product of the remaining six factors times the nonzero ambient coefficient vector a_i. They cannot all vanish. This contradiction proves that the binary ideal has no projective zero, equivalently no nonconstant common factor and height two.

Using the algebraic closure creates no field assumption gap. Polynomial extension of scalars is faithfully flat, and existence of a nonconstant common factor over K would persist after extension. The ideal is proper because its homogeneous generators have positive degree. Its quotient is Artinian.

For clarity, the relevant graded minimal resolution is

\[
0\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{3}R(-6-a_j)
\to R(-6)^4\to R\to R/I_2\to0,
\quad R=K[s,t].
\]

The rank-three free first syzygy module follows from Hilbert–Burch for this height-two ideal; equivalently use the length-two free resolution of an Artinian quotient over the regular ring R. Minimality and independence of the four generators give a_j >= 1. In total degree 7, the syzygy space has dimension equal to the number of a_j equal to 1. Section 3 proves this dimension is one. Thus exactly one a_j is 1 and the others are at least 2.

The numerator 1-4t^6+sum_j t^(6+a_j) has a double zero at t=1 since the quotient has finite length. Its first derivative at 1 gives sum a_j=6. Therefore the other two a_j sum to 5 and are at least 2, forcing the multiset {1,2,3}. No alternative splitting type is compatible with these facts. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{HS}(R/I_2;t)
=\frac{1-4t^6+t^7+t^8+t^9}{(1-t)^2},
\]

and H_2(6)=3, H_2(7)=1, H_2(8)=0. As a supplemental consistency check, the quotient has length 25 and H_2(9)=0.

## 5. Nonempty compatible open flags and all quantifiers

## 6. From sectional dimensions to the generator

The ternary quotient has no elements of the ideal in degree below 6, four independent generators in degree 6, and no degree-7 relation among their linear multiples. Its three necessary dimensions are therefore

\[
H_3(5)=21,\quad H_3(6)=28-4=24,\quad H_3(7)=36-12=24.
\]

The revlex sectional identity identifies the generic i-variable section's Hilbert function with that of K[x1,...,xi]/(B intersect K[x1,...,xi]). In characteristic zero B is strongly stable. Among binary monomials of fixed degree k, x2^k is the last one under downward Borel moves. Thus x2^k in B implies that every binary degree-k monomial is in B; conversely H_2(k)=0 certainly implies x2^k in B. Since H_2(7)=1 and H_2(8)=0, the first pure x2 power is exactly 8. Vanishing in a smaller degree would persist under multiplication and contradict H_2(7)>0.

Let A_3=K[x1,x2,x3]/(B intersect K[x1,x2,x3]). The exact multiplication/cokernel sequence gives

\[
\dim\ker(x_3:(A_3)_{k-1}\to(A_3)_k)
=H_3(k-1)-H_3(k)+H_2(k).
\]

Its value is zero at k=6 and one at k=7. There is no appeal to a weak or strong Lefschetz property. Since no ideal element exists below degree 6, a minimal generator involving x3 of degree <=6 would give a nonzero monomial in the kernel at k=6 (or would itself have impossible degree below 6). Hence none exists.

At k=7, take a standard monomial m of degree 6 with x3*m in B. Any minimal generator dividing x3*m must involve x3; a divisor omitting x3 would already divide m. Its degree cannot be <=6, so the divisor must be x3*m itself and must have degree 7. Its minimality in the three-variable intersection is also minimality in B: a proper divisor of a monomial in x1,x2,x3 cannot involve x4. This establishes the claimed counterexample. In fact the one-dimensional monomial kernel shows exactly one such degree-7 minimal generator in the three-variable intersection, though its explicit exponent vector is not needed here.
