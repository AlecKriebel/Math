## 2. Arrangement and section conventions

Take `Q=xy(x-y)(x-2y)zw(x+z+w)` in K[x,y,z,w]. All seven factors are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional in characteristic zero. The four coordinate factors ensure rank four; thus this is a central essential arrangement in K^4, rather than a disguised K^3 arrangement. Euler's identity `7Q=xQ_x+yQ_y+zQ_z+wQ_w` means that omitting Q from the four-generator definition of J is legitimate. The degree-six partials generate J, and J has no homogeneous elements of lower degree.

For an injective section map K^r→K^4, r=2 or3, let X,Y,Z,W be coordinate restrictions. The restricted ideal is generated by all **four ambient derivatives** evaluated on that subspace. The chain rule yields only three linear combinations when differentiating a restricted ternary polynomial; it does not identify that three-generated Jacobian ideal with this four-generated ambient section. Four independent degree-six restrictions below rule out such an identification.

The proof imposes open conditions: independence of X,Y; nonzero pairwise nonproportional restricted factors; and, for r=3, independence of X,Z,W. These are polynomial nonvanishing conditions. The rational compatible flag `(x,y,z,w)=(s,t,u,s+2t+3u)`, then `u=2s+3t`, satisfies all of them. All seven binary coefficient vectors are distinct projectively, including Z=(2,3), W=(7,11), X+Z+W=(10,14). Thus the required open sets are nonempty. A nonempty Zariski-open set of injective matrices is dense; quotient dimensions proved constant on it are generic. Passing to a flag is also legitimate, since the displayed compatible flag lies in the joint open conditions. No one-sample-to-generic semicontinuity reversal is needed.

## 3. Independently checked syzygy mechanism

Write the original factor coefficient row as a_i and its restriction as L_i. For a linear-coefficient relation among the four restricted polars, the product rule gives

`sum_j u_j f_j = sum_i (a_i dot u) product_{h!=i} L_h`.

Modulo L_i the quotient is a domain, and every other factor remains nonzero. Consequently L_i divides a_i dot u. Degree one forces `a_i dot u=lambda_i L_i` with scalar lambda_i. Conversely these equations imply the sum equals `(sum_i lambda_i) product_h L_h`, so the single trace equation `sum_i lambda_i=0` is also necessary and sufficient. Pairwise nonproportionality is essential here and is explicitly available. The constant-coefficient version forces all a_i dot u=0; coordinate rows then force u=0. Therefore the four section polars are independent minimal generators in degree six.

The factors X,Y,X-Y,X-2Y force a common scalar a. The other three scalars may be b,c,d, yielding

`u_x=aX, u_y=aY, u_z=bZ, u_w=cW`,
`(a-d)X+(b-d)Z+(c-d)W=0`,
`4a+b+c+d=0`.

For r=3, independence of X,Z,W gives a=b=c=d, followed by 7a=0. Thus there are no linear syzygies. The degree-five, -six, -seven quotient dimensions are respectively21,28−4=24,36−12=24.

For r=2, X,Z,W span dimension two and have a one-dimensional relation space. Solutions of the vector equation form a two-parameter space. The trace equation removes exactly one parameter because its coefficient on the common scalar is7. The map to u is injective: all u zero forces a=b=c=0 and then nonzero X+Z+W forces d=0. Thus there is exactly one independent linear syzygy. This argument excludes a hidden extra relation for every section in the stated open set, rather than merely for the displayed flag.

As a distinct exact check, my standard-library rational Gaussian-elimination program constructs the full scalar-proportionality system from the seven ambient factor rows, with all u coefficients and all seven lambdas as unknowns. It finds rank19 on19 unknowns for the ternary flag and rank14 on15 unknowns for the binary flag. It also verifies the explicit binary syzygy and every pairwise projective-distinctness condition. ## 4. Binary height, Hilbert–Burch, and exact values

There is no projective common zero of the four binary polars after extension to an algebraic closure. If such a zero existed, the restricted Euler identity would force some L_i to vanish. Exactly one L_i can vanish at a binary projective point, since the factors are pairwise nonproportional. The ambient polar vector at that point would be `a_i product_{h!=i}L_h`, which is nonzero. This contradiction establishes absence of a nonconstant common factor over the algebraic closure and hence height two over K.

The binary ideal is minimally generated by four degree-six forms. Hilbert–Burch therefore gives three free syzygy generators, with positive coefficient degrees e1,e2,e3. Positivity follows from independence of the four generators. Its finite-length quotient has Hilbert numerator

`1−4t^6+t^(6+e1)+t^(6+e2)+t^(6+e3)`.

Vanishing of the numerator's derivative at1 gives e1+e2+e3=6. Exactly one linear syzygy means exactly one ei is1 and the other two are at least2. The only multiset is(1,2,3), including the possibility of repeated degrees in this enumeration. Hence

`H2(6)=3, H2(7)=1, H2(8)=0`.

The Hilbert-series calculation is exact, proves the needed rank upper bounds as well as lower bounds, and does not assume a full generic initial ideal computation. The independent code enumerates all allowed multisets and checks the resulting coefficients.

## 5. What the truncation data do prove

Put `B=rgin(J)`, `B3=B∩K[x1,x2,x3]`, `A3=K[x1,x2,x3]/B3`. Theorem6.2 identifies the section Hilbert functions with these truncations: a monomial ideal modulo the last variables is precisely its truncation to the first variables. B3 is strongly stable, and B has no terms below degree six because taking an initial ideal preserves the graded Hilbert function.

In the binary truncation, strong stability implies that if x2^d belongs to B, all degree-d binary monomials belong to B. Therefore H2(7)>0 forces x2^7 outside B. H2(8)=0 puts x2^8 in B, and divisibility excludes all smaller exponents. This proves p=8.

The cokernel of multiplication by x3 from degree k−1 to k of A3 is its binary quotient in degree k. Thus exact linear algebra gives

`dim ker(x3)=H3(k−1)−H3(k)+H2(k)`.

At k=6 this is21−24+3=0; at k=7 it is24−24+1=1. A minimal generator of degree six involving x3 would yield a nonzero standard degree-five monomial in the first kernel, impossible. Lower degrees are absent altogether. Since the quotient is monomial, the second kernel is spanned by killed standard monomials: there exists standard m of degree six with x3m in B3. Any minimal divisor t of x3m in B3 must involve x3, since otherwise it divides m. It cannot have degree at most six, so has degree seven. Finally a divisor of t in B cannot involve x4; hence t is also minimal in the full B. This proves the claimed strict inequality7<8.

These arguments do not require reconstructing B, identifying the actual monomial t, assuming any Lefschetz property, or transferring the known three-variable arrangement theorem to an arbitrary section. They suffice for the stated counterexample.
