## Exact claim and success criteria

For n>=3, G_n is the image of real binary tensors T with ||T||_F<=1 under d_i=det(M_i M_i^T). The assertion excludes every real polynomial P(d_1,...,d_n), of unrestricted degree and factorization, satisfying G_n={d in C_n:P(d)>=0}, where C_n=[0,1/4]^n. It also excludes the corresponding condition relative to H_n=C_n intersect {d_i<=sum_{j!=i}d_j}. The original source question concerns n>=4; n=3 is an additional established case. The claim concerns determinant-only variables and one additional sign condition after the stated ambient restrictions. It does not cover auxiliary variables, radical expressions, Boolean unions, or an unproved finite-conjunction extension.

The n=1 locus is {0}; n=2 is a diagonal segment and admits -(d_1-d_2)^2>=0. These exceptions are correct and prevent a spurious all-n extension. At higher n the submitted proof uses a closed face with zero determinants; it does not establish impossibility on an all-positive higher-dimensional domain. The manuscript, abstract, README and metadata respect that limit and credit Ferudun's stronger positive-interior theorem.

## Mathematical challenge and resolution

| Potential failure | Actual check and conclusion |
|---|---|
| Unit sphere theorem silently substituted for a unit ball theorem | Reconstructed the trace s=||T||^2 argument. For a_s(d)=2d/(s+sqrt(s^2-4d)), d(log a_s)/ds=-1/sqrt(s^2-4d). The ratio for a smaller determinant to a larger one increases with s, so a polygon violation at trace 1 cannot be repaired by lowering s. This is a deduction, not a numerical assumption. |
| Endpoint derivative or division invalid | The proof uses the derivative only for s>2sqrt(max d). Continuity extends the positive denominator ratios to the lower endpoint. Zero determinants contribute zero; max d=1/4 forces s=1; T=0 is separate. No endpoint is lost. |
| Necessity depends on complex-state realizations or incompatible local bases | The real Schmidt/projector proof is valid. Different-factor projectors commute, N has the complementary product vector as its one-dimensional kernel, and Bessel gives p+q<=1. The intermediate difference is (1-2a)(1-q)>=0. Degenerate eigenvalues allow arbitrary orthogonal eigenvectors. |
| Three-factor sufficiency only convex or numerically sampled | The explicit support 000,011,101,110 has no single-bit-adjacent words. Therefore off-diagonal Gram entries vanish, the bit-one squared masses are exactly t_i, and nonnegative weights sum to 1. Endpoints and zero weights are permitted. It realizes actual real tensors, including t_i=0 or 1/2. |
| F is zero, or is a polynomial only on a selected branch | F=P(t_i(1-t_i)) is a genuine global polynomial invariant under t_i->1-t_i. The open coordinate map is a diffeomorphism; an infeasible point forces F<0. Reflection invariance is a polynomial identity even where the reflected point lies outside the half cube. |
| A boundary zero need not force an odd factor | Vanishing on a relatively open plane patch gives linear-factor divisibility by the polynomial identity theorem. For exact multiplicity m, the residual restriction is a nonzero polynomial and cannot vanish throughout the patch. At a generic nonzero residual point F has strict opposite signs, so m is odd. Extra factors and their intersections are avoided rather than ignored. |
| Reflection gives only a lower bound on multiplicity | The affine reflection is a ring involution, transferring both divisibility and nondivisibility. The companion plane therefore has the same exact m. Its relatively open interior patch has a nonzero residual point where local nonnegativity forces m even. This contradiction is valid for arbitrary non-squarefree P. |
| Hull boundaries invalidate the local sign crossings | On t=(b+c,b,c), hull slacks are 2bc,2c(1-b-c),2b(1-b-c)>0. On sum t_i=1 they are 2t_jt_k>0. Thus both neighborhoods can be taken inside the strict hull. The infeasible t=(5/16,1/8,1/8) maps to (55,28,28)/256 with positive slacks (1,55,55)/256, excluding an identically zero hull pullback. |
| A zero determinant is not enough to peel a tensor factor | Gram determinant zero is rank <=1 for a real flattening. A nonzero tensor has rank 1 and factors S tensor u; normalizing u preserves ||S|| and every other full Gram matrix, by the exact contraction sum u_b^2=1. Repeated peeling preserves every remaining zero determinant. The zero tensor is harmless. |
| Restriction to the higher-n face becomes zero and evades the theorem | The exact slice is G_3 times zero, and H_n restricts to H_3 times zero. A zero restricted P includes the entire ambient face, including the explicitly infeasible point, so it also fails. No higher-n real sufficiency assumption is needed. |
| Source Q2 coefficient/sign set is wrong or floating radicals corrupt the witness | The visually checked published definition uses all 16 signs and coefficient 1/2. Independent rational multiplication yields the printed Q1, Q2 and positive difference exactly. |
| A lower norm rescues the positive witness | Its spectral bounds are lambda(d_1)>2/5 and the other sum <103/300<2/5. The already-proved trace comparison excludes every allowable s<=1. All determinant hull slacks are strictly positive. |
