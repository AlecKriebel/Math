## Exact claim and scope comparison

Write C_n=[0,1/4]^n, H_n={d in C_n: d_i<=sum_{j!=i}d_j}, and G_n for determinant tuples of real binary tensors with Frobenius norm at most one. A polynomial is only in the n scalar determinants; auxiliary coordinates, quantified variables, radicals, or unions of regions are different descriptions.

| Question | Submitted original theorem | Ferudun current full text | Older inspected sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arbitrary P in R[d], one additional condition P>=0 | Impossible for every n>=3 | Theorem 1.4: impossible for every n>=3 | General obstruction known; exact Gram application not found |
| Closed cube C_n | Explicit target | Explicit consequence | Seigal conjectures a particular Q1-Q2 for n>=4 |
| Closed hull H_n | Explicit strengthened target | Explicit consequence | Seigal gives the hull, not this impossibility |
| Positive interior U=int(H_n), including strict cube and polygon constraints | Three-factor crossings are inside U; higher factors transferred by zero slices | Theorem 1.4 proves directly in U for every n>=3 | No older exact application found |
| Merely d_i>0, with cube/hull boundary allowed | Submitted higher-factor zero slices do not establish this restriction | Stronger U theorem implies impossibility on any larger positive cube/hull domain | Distinct from the closed-face argument |
| Seigal's particular Q1>=Q2 | All-positive n4 rational counterexample | Corollary 1.2, different all-positive n4 witness and every n>=4 | Original conjecture; no older negative resolution found |
| Real tensors | Real construction and real polynomial obstruction | Real locus, normalized nonnegative real realizations; witnesses also exclude complex tensors | HSS necessity is complex-compatible; real sufficiency requires its real construction |
| Unit sphere versus closed unit ball | Ball target, sphere realization plus trace comparison | Theorem 1.1 identifies ball and sphere images | Prior normalized spectral theorems are ingredients, not automatically a ball result |

The submitted proof uses t_i with d_i=t_i(1-t_i), an actual facet t1=t2+t3, and its coordinate-reflected plane t1+t2+t3=1 crossing feasible interior. Ferudun uses r_i=1-2t_i and all coordinate sign changes: the actual polygon facet and an interior conjugate force incompatible vanishing multiplicities. This is the same branch/parity mechanism under an affine coordinate change, with a direct all-n construction instead of the submitted zero-determinant slice. Different rational counterexamples to the same conjectural formula are not distinct historical resolutions merely because their coordinates differ. The submitted n4 witness is (2401/10000,81/625,81/625,1/10000); the current paper uses (9/100,9/100,9/100,6/25).

## Older ingredients, implicit deductions, and documented applications

**Rainer Sinn (2011).** *The Algebraic Boundary of SO(2)-Orbitopes*, arXiv:1108.2992v1 [math.AG], submitted August 15, 2011. Lemma 4.2(b), printed p.8, gives a criterion excluding basic closed descriptions when a regular point of the algebraic boundary lies in the interior. Its full proof was read and rendered. It uses the same sign-change/multiplicity obstruction, applied there to orbitopes, not tensors. This is a known general ingredient. Its mathematical relevance is broader than excluding one polynomial because basic closed means a finite conjunction of polynomial inequalities. The source is a publicly posted primary preprint; a journal version was not verified. [Versioned primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.2992v1); [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.2992v1).

**Sinn (2015).** *Algebraic boundaries of convex semi-algebraic sets*, Research in the Mathematical Sciences 2:3, DOI 10.1186/s40687-015-0022-0. Full article HTML was available. The algebraic-boundary dimension argument and basic-closed counterexamples were inspected. The paper concerns convex sets and related dual geometry. It contains no inspected binary Gram-locus application; the nonconvex Gram locus should not silently be identified with its convex hull. [Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40687-015-0022-0).

**Averkov–Bröcker (2010 preprint).** *Minimal polynomial descriptions of polyhedra and special semialgebraic sets*, arXiv:1002.0921v1, February 4, 2010. Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 were inspected in full, with their hypotheses. The first starts with a bounded basic closed set; the second concerns ambient polynomial descriptions of polyhedra. Neither proves the target relative-cube impossibility for a nonlinear Gram locus. The PDF generation date is later than its arXiv submission; it was not used as the historical posting date. Journal DOI 10.1515/ADVGEOM-2011-059 is a bibliographic lead, not independently full-text inspected here. [Primary arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0921); [versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0921v1).

**Cynthia Vinzant (2020).** *The geometry of spectrahedra*, Proceedings of Symposia in Applied Mathematics 77, pp.11–35, DOI 10.1090/psapm/077/00681. The printed chapter date and author publication list were checked. Complete examples on printed pp.13–15 use a boundary component that also crosses the interior to rule out a finite conjunction. These are a disk-square union and a projected spectrahedron, not binary Gram loci. Later Gram matrices in the chapter concern sum-of-squares representations; the shared word “Gram” is not an exact application. [Primary chapter text](https://www.maths.dur.ac.uk/users/Anne.Taormina/Spectrahedra/VinzantSumofsquares.pdf); [author publication list](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~vinzant/Publications_Vinzant_2022.pdf).

**Procesi–Schwarz (1985) and Cimprič–Kuhlmann–Scheiderer (2008).** The publisher page for *Inequalities defining orbit spaces*, Inventiones Mathematicae 81, pp.539–554, DOI 10.1007/BF01388587, supplies only a preview in this audit; its complete proof was unavailable. The complete generalized quotient result in *Sums of squares and moment problems in equivariant situations*, arXiv:0808.0034v1, was inspected: Theorem 2.5 and Proposition 3.10 with its full proof. Under compactness, invariant basic closed sets have basic closed images in the **full invariant quotient**. The n determinants are selected invariants, not an established generating set for that quotient; forgetting other invariant coordinates is a further projection. Consequently this theorem cannot be used to claim that the determinant locus is basic closed. There is no inspected tensor application. [Original primary publisher page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01388587); [generalized primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.0034); [versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0034v1).

**Spectral inputs and original conjecture.** Higuchi–Sudbery–Szulc (2003), *One-Qubit Reduced States of a Pure Many-Qubit State: Polygon Inequalities*, equations (2), (12) and real construction (13)–(20), supply the exact normalized binary spectral region. Domanov–Stegeman–De Lathauwer (2017; inspected arXiv v2 May 23, 2018), *On the largest multilinear singular values of higher-order tensors*, Theorems 4–5 and their full proofs, provide real nonnegative tensor constructions. Their eigenvalue coordinates and sufficiency do not themselves document a determinant-coordinate one-polynomial impossibility. Seigal's published 2018 Conjecture 1.5, printed p.354, and Section 3, printed pp.363–366, were inspected; the OWR statement printed pp.1226–1227 proposes the polynomial characterization. The branch picture and multiple-region three-factor description make the new application plausible, but neither inspected source asserts the arbitrary-polynomial negative theorem. Seigal's 2019 thesis was located but full-file access failed, so its absence of such a theorem is not certified. [HSS primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0209085v2); [real tensor primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.03751); [Seigal published author PDF](https://seigal.github.io/seigal2018gram.pdf); [OWR primary report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46684); [thesis primary record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9jv5j0f4).

## Exact newly assembled implication

KNOWN_INGREDIENT_DERIVATION.md gives a complete proposed application of Sinn's 2011 lemma. It verifies the sphere-to-ball step, the irreducible sign-orbit boundary polynomial N, an interior regular point d=(2/9,2/9,2/9), regularity of G3 and its complement, and the higher-factor zero slice. An exact standard-library checker verifies N=-512(Q1-Q2) in three factors and gradients at actual-boundary and interior-branch points. This unverified audit-time hypothesis would exclude any finite simultaneous determinant-polynomial description in the closed cube or hull, a stronger conclusion than the submitted theorem. It is awaiting separate independent mathematical verification and is not promoted as a verified new contribution in this audit. It also does not establish higher-n positive-only impossibility through zero slices.

This comparison separates three statements: (1) the general lemma is known; (2) a specific application can be deduced after verifying additional hypotheses; (3) an earlier author actually documented that application. Only the first is historically verified in the older sources, while the exact current application is documented in Ferudun's manuscript. Newly assembling a derivation does not establish its earlier publication or its novelty.
