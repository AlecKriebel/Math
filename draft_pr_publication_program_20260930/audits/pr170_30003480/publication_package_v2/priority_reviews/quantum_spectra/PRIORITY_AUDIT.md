# Independent priority audit: PR170 / problem 30003480

Cutoff: 2026-10-12. Family: quantum marginals, real pure-qubit spectra, tensor singular-value feasibility, and determinant semialgebraic descriptions. This is a historical audit, not a new claim of proof correctness or exclusive firstness.

The searched primary literature supplies substantial older ingredients. No inspected pre-September-30-2026 source states the exact impossibility of describing the real binary-tensor unit-ball Gram locus by one determinant-coordinate polynomial inequality. A publicly accessible October 2, 2026 preprint does state and prove that result, in a stronger strictly positive domain. The supported original GitHub disclosure is September 30, preceding that deposit. These facts justify acknowledging an exact current overlap while avoiding both an unsupported earlier-resolution label and an exclusive priority claim.

## Exact target and domain distinctions

For a real binary tensor T, let d_i=det(M_i M_i^T), where M_i is the i-th principal flattening. Let B={T:||T||_F<=1}, C_n=[0,1/4]^n, and H_n={d in C_n: d_i<=sum_{j!=i}d_j for all i}. The audited submission has two logically distinct claims:

1. For every n>=3 there is no real polynomial P(d) with G(B)={d in C_n:P(d)>=0}; the same holds when C_n is replaced by H_n.
2. A strictly positive rational four-coordinate point lies in H_4 and satisfies Seigal's particular Q1>=Q2, but is not in G(B).

A spectral polytope on a unit sphere is not itself the first theorem. A counterexample to one specified polynomial is not a proof about every polynomial. A proof using a zero-determinant face does not by itself establish the statement on a domain excluding zeros. These distinctions determine the historical comparisons below.

## Independently derived translations and mechanism comparison

For a normalized real tensor, M_i M_i^T is exactly its one-body reduced density matrix. If t_i is its smaller eigenvalue then

\[
d_i=t_i(1-t_i),\qquad t_i=(1-\sqrt{1-4d_i})/2,\qquad r_i=1-2t_i=\sqrt{1-4d_i}.
\]

Thus the HSS polygons become sum_{j!=i} r_j-r_i<=n-2. Since tr(rho_i^2)=1-2d_i, the purity Q_i in the 2016 paper is exactly r_i. This calculation supplies the precise historical overlap without claiming that an old purity paper proved a polynomial impossibility theorem.

For a tensor with common Gram trace s=||T||_F^2<=1, put f_s(d)=(s-sqrt(s^2-4d))/2. Dividing the largest spectral inequality by f_s(D), where D=max d_i>0, gives ratios

\[
\frac{f_s(d)}{f_s(D)}=\frac dD\frac{s+\sqrt{s^2-4D}}{s+\sqrt{s^2-4d}}.
\]

The derivative of its logarithm is 1/sqrt(s^2-4D)-1/sqrt(s^2-4d)>=0. Continuity covers the endpoint; d=0 has ratio zero; D=0 is realized by a real product tensor. Increasing s to 1 preserves the largest polygon inequality, which implies the remaining ones. HSS real realization then provides a unit-norm tensor with the same determinants. Merely scaling the original tensor would instead change the determinants, so the spectral argument is essential. This derivation supports the candidate's ball reduction and explains the precise overlap with the October preprint.

For n=3, the HSS real state a|100>+b|010>+c|001>+d|111> becomes the submission's even-parity state by complementing each bit. Its squared coefficients are half the three polygon slacks and one minus half the eigenvalue sum. The realization is an older construction, not new historical resolution.

Under d_i=t_i(1-t_i), the involution t_1->1-t_1 leaves any determinant polynomial invariant. Under r_i=1-2t_i this is r_1->-r_1. The polygon facet t_1=t_2+t_3 becomes 1+r_1-r_2-r_3=0; reflection sends it, up to sign, to 1-r_1-r_2-r_3=0. In both proofs a facet forces an odd factor multiplicity, whereas a reflected hyperplane inside the feasible region forces even multiplicity. These are the same algebraic mechanism in conjugate coordinates. Extending the submission via rank-one zero faces is a proof variant; it is weaker in positive domains than a direct all-n facet argument.
