## 2. Arbitrary-field primaryness, Laurent reduction, and deficiency

The parametrization embeds A=R/a in K[s,t], so a is prime. A has dimension two: s^4,t^4 are independent and x,y are integral over K[s^4,t^4]. Each variable is outside a. For a hypothetical globally CM R/b, localization at any associated prime has depth zero and hence dimension zero; that prime must be minimal. The sole minimal prime is a. Thus `Ass(R/b)={a}` and b is a-primary. This is an all-localizations statement, not a vertex-only claim. In particular b=bR_a intersect R and b=bR_(wxyz) intersect R. The negative control `(u^2,uv)` in K[u,v] shows why radical equality alone is insufficient: u survives but vu vanishes, and localization at v contracts to a strictly larger ideal.

Every binomial in b lies in a and vanishes at (1,1,1,1), so after multiplying by a nonzero scalar its coefficients are one and minus one. A nonzero monomial cannot lie in a. In the Laurent ring L the generators become X^v-1, and addition/negation identities generate the subgroup Lambda of exponent differences. Monomials indexed by its cosets give `L/bL=K[Z^4/Lambda]`.

Decompose this finitely generated abelian group into a free part and finite cyclic parts. Each torsion factor is described by T^n-1. Its derivative nT^(n-1) is relatively prime in characteristic zero; after algebraic closure all such factors are products of fields. Laurent variables preserve reducedness. Faithful field extension then proves reducedness over the original K as well. No splitting of the finite algebra over K is presumed: Q[C3] includes a nonsplit irreducible quadratic factor, which is still separable. Fresh exact controls confirm that example and detect the characteristic-three repeated-root failure. The cited [Eisenbud–Sturmfels published author copy](https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/1996-002.pdf), Section2/Cor2.2, is classical corroboration; its algebraically closed decomposition hypothesis is handled by the direct argument.

Consequently bL=aL, and primary contraction gives b=a. To verify the contradiction universally, let u=s^2t^2 and B be the fourth Veronese ring. The exponent sums from {0,1,3,4} fill [0,8] in degree two; translating a full interval [0,4d] by those four shifts fills [0,4d+4]. Thus every degree d>=2 is complete, while degree one misses only u. This proves B/A=K(-1) in every degree, not just the tested range. Reducing exponents modulo four proves B is free over T=K[w,z] with basis 1,x,u,y. The relations wu=x^2, xu=wy, yu=xz, zu=y^2 make the quotient a copy of the residue field. Since rad(mB) is the homogeneous maximal ideal of B, the local-cohomology sequence gives H^1_m(A)=K(-1), supported in graded degree +1, and H^0_m(A)=0. A is not CM.
