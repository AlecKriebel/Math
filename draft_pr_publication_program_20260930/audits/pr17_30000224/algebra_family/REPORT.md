## 1. Primaryness and contraction: exact hypotheses

The map embeds A into K[s,t], so a is prime. The image has dimension two:
s^4 and t^4 are algebraically independent and every image generator is
integral over K[s^4,t^4]. Thus a has height two. Each variable has a nonzero
monomial image, and M=wxyz is outside a.

Assume the hypothetical R/b is globally Cohen-Macaulay. For any associated
prime p its localization has depth zero. CMness then makes its local
dimension zero, so p is minimal. Radical b=a makes a the sole minimal and
associated prime. Consequently every element outside a is a nonzerodivisor
on R/b, and b is a-primary. This uses CMness of the ring at its localizations,
not a bare assertion about its depth at one selected point.

For an a-primary ideal, f in bR_M intersect R implies M^n f in b for some n.
Since M^n is outside a, f is in b. Hence bR_M intersect R=b. The same argument
works for localization at R\a, and for a itself. This is the precise reason
the Laurent and generic-point arguments contract correctly.

A negative control prevents silently dropping primaryness: in K[u,v],
J=(u^2,uv) has radical (u) and generic length one, but J_v=(u)_v, whose
contraction is strictly larger than J. Indeed u is absent from J while vu is
in J, and v is outside the radical. The new probes check this membership
certificate exactly. It is a boundary counterexample to a weakened lemma,
not a replacement for the target ideal.

## 2. Laurent binomials over arbitrary K

Every generator of a binomial ideal b contained in a is either zero or a
nonzero scalar multiple of X^alpha-X^beta: evaluating at (1,1,1,1) forces
the coefficients to agree. A nonzero monomial cannot lie in a. No assumption
about homogeneous generators is needed. In fact, a binomial lying in a has
equal total degrees because both parameter images have total degree four
times the original total degree; this observation is not used to homogenize
an arbitrary b.

In L=K[w,w^(-1),x,x^(-1),y,y^(-1),z,z^(-1)], divide each binomial by a Laurent unit.
If Lambda is the subgroup of Z^4 generated by the exponent differences, the
ideal is generated by X^v-1 for all v in Lambda. Closure under addition and
negation follows from

    X^(v+h)-1 = X^v(X^h-1)+(X^v-1),
    X^(-v)-1 = -X^(-v)(X^v-1).

The quotient has one K-basis element per group coset, with multiplication
given by addition of cosets. Thus L/bL=K[Z^4/Lambda]. Write the finitely
generated abelian quotient as Z^d times a finite abelian group H. Over an
algebraic closure, the cyclic factors of H give equations T^n-1, each with
distinct roots in characteristic zero. The algebra is therefore a finite
product of Laurent polynomial domains. It is reduced. The injection into its
faithfully extended algebra shows the original K-algebra is reduced too.

This supplies the arbitrary-field step independently of the algebraically
closed-field convention in Eisenbud-Sturmfels Section 2. In particular,
one must not assume that the finite factor already splits over K: Q[C3]
has a nonsplit quadratic factor, which is nevertheless separable and reduced.
The direct argument matches the classical
[Eisenbud-Sturmfels Corollary 2.2](https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/1996-002.pdf),
printed pp.13-14.

Radicals commute with localization, so reducedness gives bL=aL. Primary
contraction gives b=a. Section 3 below makes R/a non-CM, a contradiction.
This establishes Proposition 3.1 in the full field and homogeneity scope
stated in the note.

Characteristic zero is a genuine boundary of this proof: in F3[C3], g-1 is
nonzero and its cube is zero. The probe checks the characteristic-three
coefficient cancellation. It does not claim this particular boundary algebra
constructs a target thickening. Powers q^N in characteristic zero have N+1
nonzero distinct terms, so rejecting binomial ideals does not reject those
non-binomial generators.

## 3. Defect, T-module structure, and an independent Cech proof

Use graded degree one for every degree-four parameter monomial. In degree
two, the sums of two members of {0,1,3,4} give every t-exponent from zero
to eight. If all exponents 0 through 4d are present for d>=2, adding the
four exponent shifts covers 0 through 4(d+1). Hence the fourth Veronese
B=K[s^4,s^3t,s^2t^2,st^3,t^4] differs from A by exactly u=s^2t^2 in graded
degree one, universally, not merely through the tested degree slices.

Let T=K[w,z]. Reducing the two nonnegative parameter exponents modulo four
gives uniquely the four residues (0,0),(3,1),(2,2),(1,3). This proves

    B = T*1 direct-sum T*x direct-sum T*u direct-sum T*y,
    A = T*1 direct-sum T*x direct-sum T*y direct-sum (w,z)T*u.

The second equality follows because all positive T multiples of u have
degree at least two and lie in A, whereas u itself does not. It gives the
independent minimal T-resolution

    0 -> T(-3) -> T direct-sum T(-1)^2 direct-sum T(-2)^2 -> A -> 0,

where the two last generators are wu=x^2 and zu=y^2, with sole relation
z*(wu)-w*(zu)=0. The T-Hilbert numerator is 1+2t+2t^2-t^3. B is free of
rank four over T. The parameters w,z form a regular sequence on B, so B is
CM of dimension two; the same argument at every maximal localization gives
global CMness. The radical of mB is n=(w,x,u,y,z), since u^2=wz is in mB.
The local-cohomology exact sequence of 0->A->B->K(-1)->0 consequently gives
H1_m(A)=K(-1).

A separate two-parameter construction verifies the grading and the support.
Put r=x/w=t/s and h=y/z=s/t. Then

    A_w=K[w,w^(-1),r],       A_z=K[z,z^(-1),h],
    A_wz=K[w,w^(-1),r,r^(-1)].

The last algebra has the Laurent monomial lattice
G={(a,b) in Z^2 : a+b is divisible by 4}. In A_w the t-exponent b is
nonnegative, while in A_z the s-exponent a is nonnegative. Linear
independence of distinct monomials makes their intersection exactly B.
Also x^4=w^3z and y^4=wz^3, so rad(w,z)A=m. The Cech complex for w,z gives

    H1_m(A) = (A_w intersect A_z)/A = B/A = K*u-bar,

and this quotient occurs in degree +1. No nonpositive-degree defect appears.
The new signed-lattice slices include negative degrees as an exact regression
check of this universal argument. The T-basis and degree-slice computation
also directly check the conductor action mu-bar=0.

## 4. Seminormality and Du Bois: failed hypotheses, not an obstruction

In Frac(A), u=x^2/w, with u^2=wz and u^3=x^2z in A. Yet u is not in A,
nor in A_m. For the latter claim, if g is outside m and gu is in A, the
nonzero constant term of g produces a nonzero multiple of u in degree one;
higher-degree terms cannot cancel it. This violates the square-and-cube
criterion for seminormality at the vertex.

Over C, the zeroth cohomology of the Du Bois complex is the seminormalization
(MSS p.14), so a Du Bois algebra is seminormal. The vertex fails that
necessary condition. This directly invalidates the Du Bois hypothesis of
[MSS Proposition 4.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02755v3), p.16, whose
ambient ring is regular local and essentially of finite type over C. A
smooth Proj does not repair the affine-cone hypothesis. No arbitrary
characteristic-zero field is assumed to embed into C.

Both displayed parameter charts are regular. They cover the punctured cone
because rad(w,z)=m. Thus the punctured-locus requirement of MSS Corollary
4.10, p.17, holds over C, but its required nonzero nonpositive-degree
H^i_m(A) for i<2 does not: H0=0 and H1=K(-1) is in degree +1. These are
precise failures of the two tests. They imply neither existence nor
nonexistence of the unexcluded b. The source note states this correctly.

## 5. Exact Koszul socle and depth chain

Localize at m=(w,x,y,z). For I=(wy,wz,xy,xz), all four quadratic generators
are minimal: their classes in I/mI are independent. Use the shifted Koszul
basis e_i of degree two and d_ij=f_i e_j-f_j e_i. The cycle

    c=-xz*e1+xy*e2

has degree four. Its differential is zero. Any degree-four boundary is a
constant combination of the six d_ij. Matching the xz*e1 term forces the
coefficient of d14 to be one, and its wy*e4 term cannot be canceled by
any other boundary. Hence c is nonzero in H1.

The distinct independent certificate is the whole multigraded slice at
alpha=(1,1,1,1). All four K1 vertices are present; the only K2 edges are
(1,4) and (2,3), because their products divide the monomial wxyz. The cycle
space has dimension three and the boundary space dimension two. The vector
(-1,1,0,0) connects the two boundary components, so its class is nonzero.
Multiplication by w,x,y,z adds respectively an edge (1,2),(3,4),(1,3),(2,4).
Each enlarged graph is connected, so its cycle space is entirely the
boundary space. Thus all four variable multiples of c are boundaries.
This proves the socle claim without importing the coefficients of the four
identities (6.1). Those identities were also reproduced by the author run.

The class remains nonzero after localizing at m: any s outside m acts on it
as its nonzero constant term, because mc=0. Therefore depth (H1)_m=0.
This is a statement about a nonzero socle submodule, **not** a claim that
the entire H1 is one-dimensional or finite length. The independent slices
have H1 dimensions 4,9,12 in degrees 3,4,5, respectively.

The exact sequence

    0 -> R/I -> R/(w,x) direct-sum R/(y,z) -> K -> 0

gives depth R_m/I_m=1. An independent minimal free resolution of I is

    0 -> R(-4) -> R(-3)^4 -> R(-2)^4 -> I -> 0.

It is the tensor product over K of the two parameter-ideal resolutions in
disjoint variables. Its four first-syzygy columns are
(-z,y,0,0), (-x,0,w,0), (0,-x,0,w), (0,0,-z,y); their relation has
coefficients (x,-z,y,-w). This is an exactness proof, not a matrix-identity
guess. It confirms depth I_m=2 and depth (Z1)_m=3. The depth chain is then

| Module at m | Exact reason | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| R/I | Skew-plane fiber-product sequence | 1 |
| I | 0->I->R->R/I | 2 |
| Z1 | 0->Z1->K1->I | 3 |
| H1 | Verified nonzero m-socle | 0 |
| B1 | 0->B1->Z1->H1 | 1 |
| Z2 | 0->Z2->K2->B1 | 2 |

For clarity, depth B1 is exactly one: H0(B1)=0 since it is torsion-free,
and H0(H1) injects nontrivially into H1(B1), while H0(Z1)=H1(Z1)=0.
The next sequence gives depth Z2 exactly two. Auslander-Buchsbaum over the
regular local ring R_m of dimension four yields pdim(Z2)_m=2.

## 6. Exact limit of the 2025 residual-intersection result

The [Hassanzadeh v2 theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05705v2), Theorem 1.2
on pp.2-3, requires specified alternatives beyond the residual-intersection
setup. Here s=2 and r=4. Its first alternative fails s>=r. It also fails
the local generator condition: at (w,x), y,z are units and I_(w,x) has two
generators, whereas that height-two prime lies among the required loci.

For the local second alternative, the demanded cycle bound at i=2 is
pdim(Z2)<=r-2-1=1, contradicted by the value two above. This failure suffices,
without assuming or needing to settle all possible proper-sequence choices.
For the displayed order, properness itself already fails: H1(wy,wz) is
R/(w) times the class (-z,y), and xy acts nontrivially on it.

The sliding-depth requirement is depth H1>=d-r+1=1, but its actual depth is
zero. Thus the SD premise of Corollary 3.19, p.22, also fails. All these
failures occur at m, the point that a globally CM target quotient must
handle. Applicability away from that point would not solve the question.

Generator padding cannot avoid this obstruction. For any generating list
of I_m with 4+k entries, invertible local changes of generators turn it into
the four minimal generators followed by k zero generators. The Koszul
complex is then K(f) tensor the exterior algebra on those zero generators.
Its cycle Z_(k+2) has Z2(f) as a direct summand. Its projective dimension is
at least two, while the theorem permits only
(4+k)-(k+2)-1=1. This is a universal additional check on the proposed
application, not a construction or a new route for the central problem.

The manuscript's claim is appropriately restricted to this shortcut; no
conclusion is drawn about every linkage or residual-intersection theorem.

## 7. Algebra premises for the geometry family

For generic length one, primary contraction from R_a gives b=a even when
b is nonhomogeneous, because a local Artinian algebra of length one is the
residue field. Hence generic length one is excluded in the original scope.
For generic length two, the maximal ideal of the local Artinian quotient
squares to zero: otherwise Nakayama makes it zero. Primary contraction
therefore gives a^2 contained in b globally, also without homogeneity.
The subsequent use of Proj, a line bundle, and the graded degree-two
restriction map requires homogeneous b. No extension of that exclusion to
nonhomogeneous generic length two is justified by this argument.

For q-contained b, put S=R/(q), P=a/(q), J=b/(q). S is a normal CM domain:
q is the Segre determinant, a hypersurface, with gradient (z,-y,-x,w), whose
singular locus is just the vertex. It satisfies S2 and is regular at every
height-one prime. P has height one. At any prime p containing P, the CM
quotient has dimension dim(S_p)-1 (the finite-type catenary dimension
formula), so the depth lemma gives depth J_p=dim(S_p). Outside V(P), J_p=S_p.
Thus J is a rank-one torsion-free S2 module and is reflexive.

A rank-one reflexive ideal equals the intersection of its height-one
localizations in Frac(S). The P-localization is P^r S_P for r>=1 in a DVR;
all other height-one localizations equal S. It follows that J=P^(r).
Scaling the grading preserves this symbolic power. Since K is infinite,
Vandermonde extraction of the homogeneous pieces of any element makes it a
graded ideal. Therefore b itself is homogeneous. This validates the
nonhomogeneous-to-homogeneous step specifically under q contained in b.
It supplies no homogenization theorem for unrestricted b.

The remaining sheaf identification with rC on Q and the conormal splitting
are reserved to the separate geometry family. The author arithmetic
identities for them reproduced, but those finite identities alone would
not prove the universal sheaf claims.

For the permitted algebraic-closure reduction, a remains prime after every
field extension: its image is the same monomial algebra with independent
monomial basis over the larger field. Since a is finitely generated and
radical b=a, some a^N lies in b, and this containment survives extension,
so radical equality survives too. Polynomial quotients that are CM remain
CM on field extension here (flat base change with geometrically regular
fibers in characteristic zero). No assumption that K embeds in C is needed.

## 9. Actionable findings and exact remaining gap

There is no must-fix correctness repair in the algebra material. Preserve
these qualification points in any accepted description:

1. Keep the no-homogeneity original statement and the homogeneous-only
   generic-length-two exclusion explicit.
2. Label all Koszul depths and projective dimensions as values at m. The
   nonzero socle concerns a submodule, not all of H1.
3. Keep the distinction between a positive-degree defect and the source's
   nonpositive-degree criterion. The failed Du Bois tests are not existence
   evidence.
4. Treat the geometric verdict as conditional until its independent family
   completes. Do not promote finite conormal identities into a universal
   theorem without the bundle/sheaf proof.
5. Acknowledge that generic length one is already excluded without
   homogeneity; do not extend the length-two bound to unrestricted b.

The unexcluded target is a non-binomial a-primary ideal b with nonzero
nilpotent q in R/b. If b is homogeneous, the separate geometry restriction
would force generic length at least three. Nonhomogeneous generic length
two is not excluded by that geometric argument. No construction or
universal exclusion of this class has been obtained. Normalization replaces
the reduced algebra and is not such a thickening. The shortcut based on the
2025 theorem is blocked by unsupported hypotheses, not a proof mechanism
for the residual class. The correct status stays partial/stalled and
unsolved, with four original attempts used out of five.
