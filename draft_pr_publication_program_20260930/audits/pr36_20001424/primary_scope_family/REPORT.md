## Independent mathematical reconstruction

The graph's unique four-cycle and pendant lengths `1,2,1,2` leave four abstract automorphisms. The cyclic orders separate them into identity (uniformly positive), opposite swap (uniformly negative), and two mixed-sign permutations. A sphere homeomorphism has one orientation sign, so only the first two can occur as induced embedded-graph actions. The specified antipode realizes the negative action and exchanges the graph's two faces.

The classifier therefore realizes a degree-11 map with ten fixed critical points, local-degree multiset `2^4,3^2,4^4`, and ramification sum twenty. This is PCF, is not polynomial, and has even reduced postcritical cardinality, consistent with the imported sufficient criterion. The drawing's coordinate rationality is not used to infer map algebraicity.

Any holomorphic dynamical automorphism acts on the intrinsic fixed rays and Tischler faces, hence preserves charge incidence and local orders. Its critical-point permutation is the identity; fixing three distinct points makes the Möbius transformation identity. Conjugation maps fixed rays to fixed rays of the conjugate map. The graph's mirror equivalence then supplies an antiholomorphic centralizer A. Since the holomorphic centralizer is trivial, A is unique and squares to identity.

The negative graph action fixes neither a vertex nor a charge edge. The simple graph makes edge incidence unambiguous. Tischler faces are paired without a fixed face. Choose one charge arc per pair and its actual A-image in the other face. This is a connected A-invariant charge graph with no fixed point. A reflecting involution would separate the sphere into exchanged disks, and a connected nonempty invariant graph disjoint from the fixed circle cannot lie in both. Thus A is fixed-point-free, and an R-model would yield a different reflecting centralizer, impossible. This argument uses an actual graph, not only its isotopy class.

For algebraicity, ten edges give finitely many graph classes and hence finitely many critically fixed conjugacy classes. Normalizing an ordered triple of distinct critical points gives finitely many normalized maps. On the nonzero-resultant degree-11 locus the ramification form W is nonzero of degree twenty; `W | H^20`, where the fixed-point form H has degree twelve, is equivalent to every critical point being fixed. Quotient variables of degree 220 express an algebraic condition; normalization and nonzero resultant give a constructible Q-locus. Its finite points are algebraic. This is a valid finiteness argument and has no flexible-Lattès exception.

After choosing an algebraic representative, a complex conjugator maps an ordered algebraic critical triple to an algebraic triple. It is therefore a PGL2(Qbar) map. This routine implication is implicit in CANDIDATE and explicit in the old review; I independently checked it before reading that review. The absolute Galois stabilizer contains an open subgroup from a coefficient field, so its fixed field K is a number field. Complex conjugation fixes the class, so K is contained in R in the chosen embedding. A K-model would give an R-model, already excluded. No exact K or coefficients are required for this existence claim, and total reality is not claimed.
