# Independently reconstructed universal assessment, before historical review

## Claim and decision

The literal question is “Are all PCF maps defined over their field of moduli?” A single PCF P1 rational conjugacy class over Qbar whose absolute moduli field does not define it falsifies the universal assertion. The candidate's degree-11 construction meets this criterion, conditional only on the cited established graph classification/realization theorem and its operative inverse construction, which I read. I find no central algebraicity or descent gap. This does not establish novelty, explicit coefficients, the numerical moduli field, or a minimal degree. It verifies the original construction, adding zero substantive research turns.

## Graph and analytic prerequisites, not assumed equivariance of an arbitrary bijection

The graph is a four-cycle with attached paths of lengths 1,2,1,2; paths at vertices 0,1 are inside and at 2,3 outside. Its 10 vertices and 10 edges give degree 11 under Hlushchanka's Theorem 2 and Section 5.1. Vertex degrees are four 3's, two 2's and four 1's, giving critical local degrees four 4's, two 3's and four 2's. The ramification total is 20. Every critical point is fixed by the realization construction. In particular P_f=Crit(f), of cardinality 10. The class is nonpolynomial because a polynomial class has a totally ramified fixed point, whereas all critical local degrees are at most 4<11.

The unique abstract cycle and pendant lengths force abstract symmetries id, h(j)=j+2, s0(j)=-j and s2(j)=2-j. Following the written cyclic orders at the four degree-three vertices shows that id preserves all, h reverses all, while s0/s2 have mixed signs. Thus the only positive embedded permutation is id and only negative one is h. The exact anti-Mobius J=-1/conjugate(z) realizes h and pairs all vertices and edges. This is a hand derivation; executable independent verification follows after the seal.

The source's inverse construction uses all fixed internal rays, intrinsically forming Tisch(f). Corollary 6 says each complementary face has two distinct critical boundary points; Section 5.2 puts one joining charge arc in each face; Lemma 7 gives connectivity. A (anti-)Mobius map commuting with f sends immediate basins to immediate basins. In local coordinates an invertible germ commuting with z^m is a rotation (or, in the antiholomorphic case, a conjugated rotation): if b(z)=az+... and b(z^m)=b(z)^m, a^(m-1)=1 and the first nonzero nonlinear coefficient would occur at incompatible least powers. The germ identity extends across the basin. Hence all fixed rays and their actual faces are carried to fixed rays and faces. The charge incidence and cyclic order are the order of these sectors, independent of the particular arcs chosen. Any charge graph is topologically equivalent to the special graph chosen in Proposition 8: two arcs in the same simply connected face joining the same distinct boundary corners are carried to each other by an isotopy fixing the boundary, extend these facewise. Therefore this is an action on the actual charge ribbon graph class; no abstract-bijection naturality assumption is being substituted.

Conjugation carries fixed rays of f to those of fbar, so the charge class of fbar is the mirror of that of f. The negative symmetry J of the graph gives a positive isomorphism to its mirror. Classification gives a Mobius L with fbar=L f L^-1. Consequently A=L^-1 c commutes with f, with c(z)=conjugate(z).

Every holomorphic automorphism has positive induced charge permutation id, so fixes all 10 critical points. Three fixed points force a Mobius map to be identity. Thus Aut(f)=1. Every antiholomorphic automorphism induces h; its square is a holomorphic automorphism, hence A^2=1, and any two antiholomorphic automorphisms differ by a holomorphic one, hence A is unique.

## Why involution is not a reflection

All charge edges are paired by h, equivalently all actual Tischler faces are paired by A. Choose one charge arc in one face of each pair, and use its A-image in the paired face. Since A^2=id this creates an actual invariant connected charge graph. Its vertices are paired and edge interiors in distinct open faces are paired, so no point of this graph is fixed. A reflection's fixed circle splits the sphere into disks exchanged by the reflection. A connected invariant nonempty subset disjoint from that circle would lie in one disk and be carried into the other, a contradiction. Thus A is fixed-point-free. If g=M f M^-1 had real coefficients, M^-1 c M would be a reflection commuting with f. Uniqueness of A contradicts that. Therefore the class has no real representative. The elementary real-model implication is the exact one needed from Hidalgo--Quispe Lemma 4; no unrelated pseudo-real family is assumed PCF.

## Finite normalized algebraic locus in full detail

There are finitely many critically fixed degree-11 conformal classes. This follows from the fixed-edge-count plane graph classification: 10 edges imply at most 11 vertices for a connected graph, finitely many abstract multigraphs and finitely many rotation systems. The specific class has 10 periodic critical points, so its Thurston orbifold has 10 infinite weights and characteristic 2-10=-8<0; flexible Lattes maps are irrelevant. In particular the finiteness statement is not an invalid general assertion that all PCF degree-11 classes form a finite set.

Restrict to maps with 0,1,infinity critical. For each conformal class, the critical set is finite. A normalized representative arises by choosing an ordered triple of distinct critical points of a representative and the unique Mobius sending it to (0,1,infinity). Thus there are finitely many maps themselves after projective coefficient scaling; continuous coordinate freedom is removed. Classes with fewer than three critical points contribute no points to this normalized locus, not a family.

Use homogeneous degree-11 forms F,G with resultant Res(F,G)!=0, and their common projective scaling. Define
W=F_X G_Y-F_Y G_X, deg W=20;
H=Y F-X G, deg H=12.
On this characteristic-zero resultant-nonzero locus W is nonzero and its zero divisor is ramification. H is nonzero for degree 11. A critical point of W multiplicity m<=20 is fixed precisely when H vanishes there. Consequently support(W) subset support(H) is equivalent to W dividing H^20. This exponent is sufficient, not a degree mismatch: deg(H^20)=240, so the homogeneous quotient Q has degree 220 and 221 coefficients. The 241 coefficients of W Q-H^20=0 are polynomial equations over Q. Add W(0,1)=W(1,1)=W(1,0)=0 and the resultant nonvanishing. In any projective coefficient chart, these equations and inequations define an incidence locus over Q; its image forgetting Q is constructible over Q by elimination/Chevalley. Alternatively quotient uniqueness and ordinary polynomial division on finitely many nonzero-leading-coefficient charts give direct constructible equations. Elimination by itself would give a closure, so I use the constructible-image statement rather than assume an arbitrary elimination ideal equals the locus.

Each finite constructible Q-locus has algebraic complex points. One proof: it is invariant under Aut(C/Q). Every coordinate in a fixed Q-defined affine chart has finite orbit. A transcendental coordinate has infinite orbit: extend it to a transcendence basis, replace it by arbitrarily many distinct rational translates, and extend the induced basis-field isomorphism to an automorphism of its algebraic closure C. Thus each coordinate is algebraic. Equivalently a zero-dimensional finite-type Q-stratum has number-field residue fields. Select the chart containing the chosen realized class. The finitely many normalization conditions cannot accidentally certify arbitrary transcendental graph coordinates; it is the actual rational-map coefficient locus that is defined over Q. Therefore f has a normalized representative in Qbar(z).

## Complex versus Qbar conjugacy

For f,g in Qbar(z), a complex Mobius conjugator exists over Qbar. In this case select three distinct algebraic critical points of f. Their images under the conjugator are distinct critical points of g and algebraic (they solve the homogeneous ramification form). The unique Mobius map between these ordered triples has algebraic coefficients. Thus the original complex conjugator belongs to PGL2(Qbar). More generally one can apply the Nullstellensatz to the nonempty conjugator equations over the algebraically closed field Qbar, but the critical-triple proof is elementary here. Accordingly fbar being C-conjugate to f implies it is Qbar-conjugate, and actual complex conjugation lies in the absolute arithmetic stabilizer used to define K.

## Absolute field of moduli and obstruction distinctions

Let E/Q be the finite coefficient field of the chosen algebraic representative and let S={sigma in Gal(Qbar/Q): sigma(f) is PGL2(Qbar)-conjugate to f}. This is a subgroup: composition of conjugators proves closure, and inverses give inverses. It contains Gal(Qbar/E). A subgroup containing an open subgroup is open; its complement is a union of open cosets, so it is closed as well. The Galois correspondence gives K=Qbar^S finite over Q, and S=Gal(Qbar/K). Complex conjugation c belongs to S, hence K is contained in the chosen copy of the real numbers. This claims a real embedded field, not that K is totally real.

A K-defined representative would have real coefficients because K is embedded in R. That contradicts the established no-real-model assertion. Therefore K is not a field of definition and the literal all-PCF assertion is false for this class.

No nontrivial automorphism/gerbe ambiguity is being ignored: Aut(f)=1 was proved independently. Choosing conjugators for sigma(f)=L_sigma f L_sigma^-1 makes them unique and gives L_sigma_tau=sigma(L_tau)L_sigma under this convention; the inverse conjugators give the standard opposite cocycle convention. A projective cocycle need not lift to a GL2 cocycle and Hilbert 90 does not trivialize PGL2 cohomology. At the real place A=a c and A^2=id only says a conjugate(a)=id in PGL2, allowing the nontrivial conic class (antipodal real structure). The candidate avoids any claim that an anti-involution is enough for a real P1 model. The obstruction is detected by the absence of a reflection. The canonical odd-divisor criterion has hypothesis Aut=1 but its parity hypothesis fails here: the reduced postcritical degree is 10. Ramification degree 20 and fixed-point degree 12 are also even. Thus there is no conflict with prior descent criteria.

## Boundary and falsification status

Degree>=2 and characteristic zero are used throughout. No statement is made about inseparable maps or positive characteristic. Critically fixed implies PCF but arbitrary PCF does not imply critically fixed or finiteness of normalized classes (flexible Lattes families are the standard warning). A graph realization specifies an exact conjugacy class without coefficients, and non-descent over a real subfield follows without computing K. Classification, intrinsic-ray naturality, and genuine invariant arcs are imported/derived prerequisites; no numerical graph check proves them. Algebraicity and finite arithmetic stabilizer are deductions, not empirical assumptions. No universal positive proof is attempted or needed once the exact counterexample is established.
