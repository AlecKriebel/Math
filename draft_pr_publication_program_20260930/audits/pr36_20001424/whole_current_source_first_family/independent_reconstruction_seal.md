## Elementary universal certificate, reconstructed

Take the printed 1995 cubic f(z)=i((z-1)/(z+1))^3. Numerator and denominator have disjoint roots, so degree is 3. A degree-one change followed by cubing and multiplication has precisely two critical points: +1 and -1, both local degree 3. Projective evaluation gives the orbit 1,0,-i,-1,infinity,i,1. Thus it is PCF, with a six-element reduced postcritical set; no numerical dynamical claim is needed.

Every rational-coefficient Galois automorphism acts on i as either +i or -i. T(z)=-1/z conjugates f to its coefficient conjugate. The absolute arithmetic conjugacy stabilizer is therefore all of Gal(Qbar/Q), whose fixed field is Q. Any commuting holomorphic Mobius transformation must permute {1,-1} and consequently {0,infinity}. Fixing the critical points forces H(z)=az, a=1. Swapping them forces H(z)=a/z, a=-1. The latter is T and fails commutation: at infinity, T(f(infinity))=i while f(T(infinity))=-i. The holomorphic automorphism group is trivial.

The map A(z)=-1/bar(z) commutes with f and satisfies A^2=id. It has no fixed points, since nonzero finite fixed points would require |z|^2=-1, and it swaps 0 and infinity. Any other commuting antiholomorphic transformation differs from A by a holomorphic one, so is A. If M f M^-1 had real coefficients, M^-1 c M would be a commuting reflection with a fixed circle. It cannot equal A. Hence there is no real representative and no Q representative. This is a complete negative answer in characteristic-zero P1, a subclass included in the literal all-PCF wording; no full-family positive proof is needed.

For all odd d>=3 the same calculation works. The six orbit values after i and -i depend on d modulo 4: when d=3 mod4 there is the six-cycle; when d=1 mod4 there are two 3-cycles. The cubic alone suffices. The degree-one boundary is excluded because it has no critical points and the automorphism argument fails there.
