No inspected primary result establishes that complete scope together with the common-null-set pointwise density criterion and the measurable support-band construction. This finding does not justify a first-priority assertion. Promotion still depends on the separate equivalent-method audit and on accurate attribution of the substantial prior content below.

## Exact target and source correspondence

OWR 19/2024, pp. 1078–1081, states two distinct questions in Open Problem (1). Its second sentence is: “Is it still true that a composition operator is generalized hyperbolic if and only if it has the shadowing property?” The first sentence asks whether the earlier characterization survives removal of bounded distortion. [Official primary report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/49477).

The measure-system setup on pp. 1079–1080 specifies a sigma-finite space, a bijective bimeasurable transformation, boundedness of both composition directions through measure-ratio bounds, and a wandering set of finite positive measure covering the space under its bilateral iterates. The range is every $1\leq p<\infty$. The generalized-hyperbolicity definition uses closed complementary subspaces, forward/backward inclusions, and spectra inside the unit disk. The candidate's scope matches this measure-system setup and does not substitute strict contractions in the original norm for the source's spectral-radius convention.

There is a textual nuance: the report's initial generic dynamics paragraph says “Let X be a separable Banach space” before reusing X for the measure space. The original D'Aniello–Darji–Maiuriello paper's measure-space setup is sigma-finite without a separability restriction. The candidate's unrestricted theorem covers the separable question as well as a broader nonseparable setting; it should not say that OWR explicitly formulated a nonseparable-Hilbert problem.

## Positive prior content and precise gaps

The primary comparisons are in [SOURCE_SCOPE.md](SOURCE_SCOPE.md), with hashes, URLs, versions, retrieval metadata, and search inventories in the accompanying manifests.

1. **D'Aniello–Darji–Maiuriello (2021).** Corollary GH proves the equivalence under bounded distortion. Theorem RN is not an unrestricted pointwise-density criterion: it assumes a uniform bound on $M_k/m_k$, which Proposition 2.6.6 proves sufficient for bounded distortion. Its proof compares density extrema with the total masses of wandering levels using that bound. Problem 5.0.1 keeps the equivalence and characterization as separate questions. The central uniformity problem is therefore not eliminated by invoking Theorem RN.
2. **Carvalho–Darji–Varandas (2024).** Theorem 2.4 gives the orbit-density sequence-space realization for arbitrary dissipative composition operators. Corollary 2.16 proves shadowing iff generalized hyperbolicity for shifts with finite-dimensional fibers and an appropriate basis with uniformly bounded projections. Corollary 2.7 only sends shadowing to scalar factors in a broader orthogonal setting. The paragraph after Corollary 2.15 supplies an infinite-dimensional example showing why shadowing of every scalar factor does not suffice. Question 5.1 remains open for general shifts in that version. This does not establish uniform control across arbitrary measurable fibers.
3. **Bernardes–D'Aniello–Maiuriello (July 2026).** Section 6, Theorem 6.1 and Example 6.2, explicitly address why bounded distortion is essential to the earlier aggregate characterization. The example has wandering-level masses (2^k) satisfying HC while a composition operator lacks shadowing. It disproves a mass-only sufficiency condition. It is not a shadowing operator that fails generalized hyperbolicity and does not negatively solve the equivalence question.
4. **Pituk (August 2026).** Theorem B establishes the equivalence for all invertible operators on separable complex Hilbert spaces, with removal of separability explicitly left open. This includes the separable complex $L^2$ slice regardless of distortion. Theorem A's general Banach counterexample uses surjective versus right spectra and an uncomplemented kernel; it is not asserted to be a scalar dissipative composition operator. The current June 26 v3 of Pituk's earlier spectral paper, arXiv:2511.12272, gives shadowing/right-spectrum/adjoint-uniform-expansivity equivalences on arbitrary complex Hilbert spaces, but does not supply a complementary invariant GH splitting.
5. **Messaoudi–Tofanin Neto–Saavedra–Tsokanos (August 2026).** Theorem 3.5 gives a pseudo-hyperbolicity/shadowing equivalence under complemented unimodular eigenspaces. Theorem 3.9 constructs counterexamples on \(\ell^p\), $1<p<\infty,\ p\ne2$, using uncomplemented subspaces. Pseudo-hyperbolicity does not provide the invariant closed direct-sum splitting required by the target. A Banach space being isomorphic to an $L^p$ space does not make its arbitrary constructed operator a composition operator.
6. **New follow-up routes.** The September 2024 Orlicz journal extension retains bounded distortion in Corollary 2 and retains a bounded density-extrema ratio in Theorem 3. The June 2026 weighted-composition paper provides a useful unrestricted weighted-to-unweighted conjugacy (Theorem 15), but its shadowing correspondence (Theorem 21) explicitly requires bounded distortion. The 2022 structural-stability paper's relevant Corollary SC2 and Theorems W/C also explicitly require bounded distortion, despite abbreviated abstract wording.
7. **Current adjacent function-space results.** Pinto's July 2026 continuous-function paper proves a scalar-sequence criterion and a continuous-function characterization. Its Theorem 32 assumes a metric distance-preserving map, uniform continuity of the weight, and admissibility conditions; the text explicitly assigns connectedness/compactness/regularity the role played by distortion in the $L^p$ precedent. Theorem 22 is a sufficient condition based on multiplication partitions, not an unrestricted measurable-$L^p$ converse. The September 3, 2026 Hardy v2 update, July 29 Paley–Wiener v3 update, and August Schwartz paper concern analytic or locally convex function spaces, not the full scalar measurable $L^p$ target.

## Candidate-specific novelty boundary

The potentially new content is the uniform passage from operator shadowing to
\[
\min\{\rho_{n-d}(w),\rho_{n+d}(w)\}\leq\eta\rho_n(w)
\]
for one $d$, one $\eta<1$, and a common exceptional null set across all integers, followed by a measurable support splitting and its finite-intersection passage from a power to the original operator. The candidate includes $p=1$ and permits arbitrary measurable fibers and sigma-finite spaces.

This is a narrower and more honest novelty hypothesis than claiming to invent orbit-shift coordinates, dual shadowing estimates, scalar weighted-shift dichotomies, or the generalized-hyperbolicity-to-shadowing implication. Those ingredients are related to established results, and the separate equivalent-method audit may still identify an older general theorem that subsumes the construction. This audit does not settle that broader question.
