## Exact claim and assumptions

The target is the second sentence of OWR 19/2024, Open Problem (1), printed
on p.1080. The preceding standing assumptions require a sigma-finite measure
space, a bijective bimeasurable transformation, bounded forward and inverse
composition, and a disjoint orbit decomposition generated by a wandering
set of finite positive measure. The source defines generalized hyperbolicity
by spectral containment in the open disk on closed complementary forward
stable and inverse stable subspaces. The candidate's spectral-radius
convention therefore matches. The separate aggregate-mass question is not
the target of the affirmative theorem.

For each fixed finite exponent p≥1 and either scalar field, the claim is:
two-sided shadowing is equivalent to generalized hyperbolicity, with
complementary measurable support bands available, and equivalent to a
common density drop at a common finite distance d. Neither bounded
distortion, separability, nor finite-dimensional fibers is used.

## Direct proof, including quantitative indexing

Let A be any bounded invertible Banach-space operator with shadowing.
Scaling the shadowing tolerance yields a constant K such that each bounded
forcing sequence has a bounded solution to y(j+1)−Ay(j)=b(j), with solution
norm at most K times forcing norm. Invertibility is needed to construct
the pseudotrajectory in both integer directions, but no boundedness of
the pseudotrajectory itself is assumed.

For finitely supported dual u(j), independently approximate each dual norm
by the real part of u(j)(b(j)) for unit b(j). Summing and shifting the
recurrence gives

    sum ||u(j)|| ≤ K sum ||u(j−1)−A* u(j)||.

This argument uses approximate norm attainment, not reflexivity or
actual norm attainment. With S=A* and u(j)=S^(−j)u on −b≤j≤−a, the two
uncancelled terms have norms ||S^a u|| and ||S^(b+1)u||. Thus, writing
a(j)=||S^j u||,

    sum_(j=a)^b a(j) ≤ K(a(a)+a(b+1)).

The interval [−j,j−1] gives a(0)≤K(a(−j)+a(j)). If both endpoints at
±d were at most 2a(0), the interval [−d,d−1] would have total at most
4Ka(0), whereas summing the preceding inequality for 1≤j≤d−1 gives
total at least (d−1)a(0)/K. Hence d>4K²+1 forces one endpoint norm
strictly above 2||u||. The signs and endpoint indices are correct.

On the wandering set W, define ν(n)(A)=μ(f^n A) and its derivative
ρ(n) with respect to ν=μ|W. Both inequalities for f ensure equivalence
of ν(n) and ν and finite densities, with a common countable null set.
The coordinate map

    (Uφ)(w,n)=ρ(n,w)^(1/p) φ(f^n w)

is onto and isometric. Its inverse is defined on the measurable,
pairwise disjoint levels f^n W. The normalized operator is

    (Bg)(w,n)=a(n,w)g(w,n+1),
    a(n,w)=(ρ(n,w)/ρ(n+1,w))^(1/p).

It is bounded and boundedly invertible. Its Banach adjoint on scalar
L^q(W×Z), including q=∞ when p=1, transports a coordinate n to n+1.
Its d-step multipliers on a vector originally at n are
(ρ(n)/ρ(n+d))^(1/p) and (ρ(n)/ρ(n−d))^(1/p). If both were below 2
on a positive-measure set, a finite-measure indicator on a subset where
both are at most 2−ε would contradict the dual estimate. This establishes
the pointwise common drop with η=2^(−p). The countable union over n
provides a single exceptional subset of W. Arbitrary nonseparable ν
introduces no additional quantifier or selection.

Assume such a common drop for some d and η<1. Assign A0 where the
negative d-step drop holds and C0 to its complement. On A0 the opposite
drop at n−d is impossible, so negative drops iterate. On C0 positive
drops hold and iterate, since entry into A0 at n+d would contradict the
drop already obtained. Thus B^d contracts L^p(A0), and B^(−d)
contracts L^p(C0), with m-step bounds η^(m/p).

A power split alone cannot be used as a one-step split. Define
M=intersection_(j=0)^(d−1) B^j L^p(A0). It is a measurable support
band, and B M⊆M because B^d L^p(A0)⊆L^p(A0). Its complementary
band N is the support band on the finite union of B^j C0, and
B^(−1)N⊆N. The forward bound on M follows from M⊆L^p(A0).
For g in B^j L^p(C0), commuting the powers gives an inverse bound
with prefactor D=max_(j<d)||B^j||||B^(−j)||. Decompose a general
g∈N along a measurable disjoint partition subordinate to that finite
union. Weighted coordinate permutations preserve disjointness, so
the p-th powers of the norms add and give the same D for the entire
band. This includes p=1. Finite remainder powers turn the d-step
bounds into exponential one-step bounds. This supplies the claimed
generalized hyperbolicity and measurable bands.

Finally, let P,Q be the two complementary projections for any
generalized-hyperbolic operator T. The proposed Green series is

    y(n)=sum_(j≥0) T^j P b(n−1−j)
         −sum_(j≥0) T^(−j−1) Q b(n+j).

Both sums converge uniformly. For truncation at J, subtracting T y(n)
from y(n+1) leaves b(n)−T^(J+1)P b(n−1−J)
−T^(−J−1)Q b(n+J+1). Both residual terms tend uniformly to zero.
This identity uses no commutation between P,Q and T. Subtracting y
from a pseudotrajectory yields an exact orbit in both directions.
Thus the direct proof establishes all three conditions.

## Independent older-method translation

The only imported non-elementary structural premise is the scalar
global-transition theorem printed in K20 Theorem 2.26. Its primary
statement assumes an aperiodic homeomorphism of a Stonean compact space.
At a spectral value where the difference is bounded below, it gives
closed invariant stable and unstable sets plus a clopen wandering
transition set, with global forward and backward tail closures landing
in the corresponding limiting sets. This is stronger than statements
about each transition orbit separately. The original K11 proof is
not claimed inspected. K20's official version header is 19 December
2020; the dynamically rendered internal 2026 date is not its version
date. [Primary K20v2 text](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.09303v2).

Set Ω=W×Z and S=B*. On L^q, S is M_b times the coordinate isometry
Uh(w,n)=h(w,n−1), with b=a(n−1). The complete sigma-finite measure
algebra has a Stonean Stone space K, and L^∞(Ω)=C(K). Multiplication
is faithful and isometric, including on L^∞; a nonzero level set of
a multiplier contains a positive finite-measure subset. Vector
annihilators are exactly the complementary support bands. Coordinate
residue partitions modulo r+1 show that the induced φ^r fixes no
Stone point for any r≥1. This also excludes periodic free ultrafilters.

The finite-forcing average independently gives
||u||≤K||(I−S)u||: use constant forcing v on a block of N times,
pair with u, sum the recurrence, and bound the two endpoint terms
by 2K||u|| and the N interior terms by NK||(I−S)u||. Divide by N
and let N grow. No central theorem has entered this step.

The needed transfer from C(K)=L^∞ to finite L^q is directly checkable.
Take bounded approximate fixed vectors f(m), with unit essential
supremum and defect at most m^(−3). Some coordinate n(m) and
positive-measure subset A(m) satisfy |f(m)|≥1/2 there. Multiply by
the finite cutoff 1_A r^|n−n(m)| for |n−n(m)|≤m, r=1−m^(−1/2).
The exact commutator identity leaves an interior slope term,
two endpoint terms, and the old approximation defect. Dividing by
the vector norm yields

    (1−r)/r + 4r^m + 2m^(−3)(2m+1)^(1/q) → 0.

The size of A(m) cancels, which is the necessary measurable uniformity
check. At q=∞ no transfer is needed because the spaces coincide.
Thus I−C is bounded below for the central weighted composition C.

If 1∈σ(C), apply the old scalar theorem. The union V of the stable
closed set and nonnegative transition iterates, and its complementary
union, are both closed by the global tail conditions; thus V is
clopen and φ(V)⊆V. A continuous product over some fixed length d
is strictly below one on the stable limiting set. The nested compact
global tail closures enter its neighborhood uniformly after one
finite J. Product bounds after time J then give uniform exponential
decay on all V. The inverse argument applies to V^c. No arbitrary
orbitwise or fiberwise rates are promoted to uniform ones. Empty
limiting sets cause no failure: nonempty transition tails in compact
K would then have a nonempty limiting intersection, a contradiction.

If 1∉σ(C), multiplication by exp(iθn) makes the spectrum rotation
invariant, so the full unit circle is excluded. Riesz stable and
unstable spaces are invariant under every multiplier, because
C^k(hf)=(h∘φ^k)C^k f and the inverse formula preserve exponential
decay. Their projection therefore commutes with every multiplier
and equals multiplication by its value at 1. Idempotence gives a
clopen indicator; commutation with C makes its set invariant. This
provides the same product bounds and covers pure contraction and
pure expansion. It needs neither the questioned broader spectral
equality nor inaccessible original proofs.

The orientation is decisive: C moves function supports by φ^(−1),
whereas the primal B moves vector supports by φ. Its norm factors
on initial supports are precisely

    ||B^k g||_p^p = integral (ρ(n−k)/ρ(n))|g(w,n)|^p dm,
    ||B^(−k)g||_p^p = integral (ρ(n+k)/ρ(n))|g(w,n)|^p dm.

These are the old central forward and inverse products to the p-th
power. The clopen split therefore gives the needed primal measurable
bands. Choose one common d making both band norms below one. Testing
finite-measure indicators gives the density-drop formula on each
complementary support, and countability of n gives the common null
set. Real shadowing complexifies by shadowing real and imaginary
parts; the real band indicators then restrict the split back. The
translation includes p=1 and nonseparable fibers.

## Source equation (33) and its precision correction

The frozen family's plain isometric-shift test distinguishes two
cocycle operators, but does not by itself distinguish their global
norms. I independently verified the root's stronger allowed module
example: stable periodic weights b=(1/4,1/2,3/4), coordinate multiplier
c=(4,1/4,1), and an expanding disjoint component of weight 2. The
coordinate operator U is similar to the plain isometry by multiplier
h=(4,1,1). At power two, the actual (bU^(−1))² stable norm is 1/2,
while the printed middle expression b₂U^(−2) has norm 3/4.

The operator identity

    (bU^(−1))^n = U^(1−n) (bU)^n U^(−n−1)

holds by shifting the full forward product by −(n−1). When the
projection commutes with U, the two outer U factors are subexponential
under σ(U)⊆unit circle and repair the growth estimate. Therefore this
is a repairable displayed equality error, not a refutation of the
older theorem. Neither narrowed proof route depends on it.

## Older subsumption versus older ingredients

Merely citing old ingredients would not settle priority. Here the
printed K20 scalar theorem supplies the central difficult conclusion:
one global clopen transition with uniform complementary tails.
K20 Theorem 5.4 also already prints the required stronger abstract
approximate-spectrum inclusion, and its disjoint cutoff mechanism
precedes the equation (33) issue. The present finite cutoff is a
concrete verification of that old mechanism, rather than a new
indispensable uniform splitting lemma. The other bridges are the
known orbit-density normalization, the elementary finite-forcing
dual estimate (also consistent with the prior 2026 duality theorem),
compactness, scalar product norm identities, and ordinary Riesz
decomposition. They specialize the older stronger structure through
routine exact hypothesis translation.

Accordingly, the full exact target and its density criterion are
verified derived corollaries of previously stated stronger machinery,
not just conjectures supported by related ingredients. This supports
the repository's explicitly qualified `already_solved` disposition
as a credited known-method corollary. It does not establish an earlier
literal printing, recognition, or advertisement of the precise OWR
answer. A new application of old theorems can sometimes be novel;
the present review found no indispensable new central mechanism
which would justify first-resolution or new-theorem priority here.
The elementary alternative proof may still be useful exposition.
