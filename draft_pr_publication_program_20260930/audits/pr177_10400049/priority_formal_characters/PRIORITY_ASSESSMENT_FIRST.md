# Independent first priority assessment: polynomial twists and formal characters

This assessment was written before any root/family priority conclusions or author follow-up were read. Initial input was limited to CANDIDATE.md, source_record.json, and source_manifest.json in the original submission. Mathematics is treated as passed; historical priority is a separate question.

## Assessment

No checked older primary body in this audit states a negative answer to the arbitrary-ideal question, or exhibits the candidate's nonempty-zero-set principal ideal. The older literature does legitimately entail the full negative conclusion after the short reduction below. That entailment is significant overlap and must be acknowledged; it does not by itself establish that the negative conclusion was earlier published. The polynomial restriction mechanism and its formal-parameter interpretation are established older mathematics. Priority for this application remains unestablished.

The strongest verified pre-question overlap is Lê–Murakami, Compositio Mathematica 102 (1996), Section 11, printed pp.62–63, Theorem 11, together with its framed-link universality on pp.50–51. The formula for the first braid generator is rho(sigma_1)=(exp(Omega_12/2), sym(sigma_1)). Their closure formula inserts a fixed factor c_m. Hence sigma_1^(2n) gives exp(n Omega_12), with an n-independent closure factor. This is an explicit older universal formal full-twist family on the right link domain. I read the actual printed formulas via rendered pp.62–63 because text extraction dropped them.

A second independently checked route is Stanford–Trapp, arXiv:math/9903057v1, dated 10 March 1999, Section 3, pp.3–4, Theorem E and its proof. Its implication from ordinary finite type to (n,q)-finite type concerns links in S^3 and mutually disjoint twisting disks. The proof converts each full-twist difference into a sum of singular-link crossing differences. Applying it to repeated two-strand full twists gives the finite-difference vanishing needed in the submission. This is an older published preprint theorem, with no journal publication asserted here.

## Exact reduction and what it establishes

Here is the reduction from the checked older full-twist theorem to the entire arbitrary-ideal refutation, rather than a merely similar statement.

Fix m>=2 and let L_n be the closure of sigma_1^(2n) with the remaining m-2 strands closed trivially and split. A type-d complex invariant pulled back along this family has vanishing (d+1)-st forward difference: use d+1 disjoint full-twist blocks and the implication of Theorem E. In characteristic zero this sequence has a unique polynomial P_v(t) of degree <=d. The same conclusion follows from the finite-degree part of the older universal closure formula. Ignoring framings pulls an ordinary invariant back to a framed finite-type invariant; no framed-only restriction is imposed on the ordinary invariant.

The assignment v -> P_v preserves addition, constants, and pointwise multiplication, since the corresponding polynomial identities hold on every nonnegative integer. It is therefore defined on the entire union of finite orders, even though each v has its own degree. No infinite-order invariant is admitted.

Set g=sum_{i<j} lk_ij^2 and f=g(4g-1). On actual links g is a nonnegative integer, so f vanishes exactly where g=0; that set contains the unlink. On L_n, g=n^2, hence P_g(t)=t^2. Evaluation P_v(1/2) is a complex character chi of the full invariant algebra, with chi(g)=1/4 and chi(f)=0. If g^N=fh held in that full algebra, applying chi would give (1/4)^N=0. Thus g vanishes on the nonempty actual zero set of fV but lies outside its radical. This verifies the full arbitrary-ideal negative answer for fixed m; restricting all-component invariants to the same family gives the identical argument for the all-finite-component algebra.

These last integer-value, multiplication, and radical-ideal steps are the candidate's elementary algebraic application. I found no passage in the checked older bodies that performs them. One must not convert the existence of old ingredients into evidence that their application was previously published. Conversely, one must not describe polynomial restriction itself as a new theorem.

## Algebra and spectrum boundaries checked

Willerton, Banach Center Publications 42 (1998), pp.457–460, explicitly uses rational oriented knots. Corollary 3 gives filtered pointwise multiplication, and Theorem 5 gives the Dean–Trapp twist-polynomiality result. Its Stanford tangle-map remark still states knot inputs. It supports attribution of the mechanism; the link bridge comes from the directly checked Stanford–Trapp theorem, not an unverified claim that knot and link hypotheses coincide.

Eisermann, Proc. AMS 128 (2000), pp.1555–1561, Lemma 4 restates complex-valued knot twist polynomiality. Corollary 10 explicitly treats fixed-component classical links and braid-index/genus boundedness. The paper warns that the unknotting-number theorem does not extend to links. It provides no radical-ideal result. The 2003 geometric-characterization paper, Theorems 28 and 30, is formulated for knots; its rational/torsion-free geometric-sequence characterization is not silently imported as a link theorem.

Bar-Natan, On the Vassiliev Knot Invariants (1995; downloaded author file later updated by 2007), Theorems 1 and 7–9, supplies the associated-graded correspondence and Hopf/symmetric-algebra structure of knot diagrams and weight systems. The graded dual is explicitly used. This is not a theorem that every actual link represents every character, nor a theorem about arbitrary ideals in the ordinary full link algebra. The later author-file version is identified rather than mislabeled as an unchanged 1995 body.

Hinich–Vaintrob, arXiv:math/0005197v1 (2000), Sections 6.1 and 7.1.1, similarly concerns the graded chord-diagram Hopf algebra for framed knots and its enveloping-algebra description. The union of finite-order invariants and the completed target of Z are distinguished in the body. No all-link arbitrary-ideal conclusion was found in these read sections.

Bar-Natan, Non-Associative Tangles, downloaded edition 7 June 1995, Sections 3.4–3.5, treats removal of framing and states the parallel link universality after Theorem 4. A formal completed target can be used to construct an ordinary finite-order character only because every individual invariant factors through a finite-degree projection. The full algebra homomorphism follows from polynomial equality on integer n. Closure is linear on diagrams; it must not be assumed multiplicative. Group-like assertions or fractional formal braid powers by themselves do not replace this bridge.

The 2010 MathOverflow discussion on Spec(Vassiliev Invariants), including the April 2010 answer about completed braids, is a primary discussion of the spectrum question, not a published link-ideal theorem. It explicitly raises nongeometric points and warns that a singular knot/formal linear combination generally fails multiplicativity. No answer there supplies this arbitrary-ideal refutation. Chrisman's 2009 twist-lattice theorem concerns long virtual knots and two virtual finite-type conventions, so it is not a direct classical-link priority result.

The separate linking-number-generated ideal, homogeneous ideals, positive characteristic, completed invariant algebras, and zeros taken over all characters are different questions. The two-component mechanism gives no necessary knot-only conclusion. A source claiming a theorem for one of these settings needs an explicit bridge before it can settle priority here.

Theorem E's assumptions and the universal braid formula are sufficient older mechanisms. What remains unverified is a dated primary publication that states the arbitrary-ideal negative answer or performs an equivalent complete reduction.
