## Complete reduction from either checked link-polynomial theorem

For every finite-order v, obtain P_v. Addition and multiplication identities hold on all integer n and thus hold as polynomial identities. This produces one unital algebra map from the entire union of finite orders V to C[t], without imposing a common degree bound. Evaluation at t=1/2 is a character chi on V. On this family g=sum lk_ij^2 restricts to n^2, so chi(g)=1/4 and chi(g(4g-1))=0. On actual links g is a nonnegative integer; hence the actual zeros of f=g(4g-1) are exactly algebraically split links and include the unlink. If g^N belonged to fV then chi would make (1/4)^N=0. Thus g is outside radical(fV) despite vanishing on the nonempty actual zero set. Restriction to this same fixed-component family proves the all-finite-component version as well. These multiplication, lattice-value and radical steps are the explicit application; I did not locate them in an earlier body.
