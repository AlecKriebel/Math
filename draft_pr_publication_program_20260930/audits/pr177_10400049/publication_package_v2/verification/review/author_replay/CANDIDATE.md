# A nonempty-zero-set counterexample to the link-invariant Nullstellensatz

**Publication proof companion.** The exact characteristic-zero counterexample is independently verified. It is an elementary application of credited old polynomiality; no new polynomiality method or historical-firstness claim is made. The standalone manuscript in this package is the publication-facing statement. This proof companion accompanies the publication-facing manuscript; dated audit stages are recorded separately.

Let V be the unital algebra of complex-valued finite-type invariants of classical links in S³, with pointwise multiplication. Each member has some finite Vassiliev order; no completion allowing infinite-order series is taken. The construction works either for a fixed number m≥2 of components or for all finite-component links. It is independent of component order and orientation.

Put

    g(L)=Σ(i<j) lk(L_i,L_j)²,
    f=g(4g−1),
    I=fV.                                           (1)

Then the common zero set Z of I among actual links is precisely the nonempty set of algebraically split links. If J consists of all finite-type invariants vanishing on Z, then

    g∈J but g∉radical(I).                            (2)

Consequently J and radical(I) need not be equal. This is a counterexample to the arbitrary-ideal assertion in Bar-Natan's Problem 2.27. It does not resolve or refute the separate example asking about the ideal generated by all pairwise linking numbers.

## 1. Exact source and coefficient convention

The full question is [Ohtsuki, Problems on invariants of knots and 3-manifolds](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/04/gtm-2002-04-024s.pdf), Section 2.11, printed p.422, Problem 2.27, attributed to D. Bar-Natan. Its bibliographic volume year is 2002, while the actual publisher publication date is June 1, 2004 (also printed on p.377); these dates are distinct. The preceding paragraph recalls the usual Nullstellensatz over an algebraically closed field. The question uses an arbitrary ideal in the algebra of finite-type link invariants and takes its zeros to be actual links. It states no restriction to homogeneous ideals, no completion, and no replacement of links by abstract characters of that algebra. The [author's archive of the June 2001 question](https://drorbn.net/AcademicPensieve/2001-06/) reproduces the same formulation.

Printed p.398 gives the finite-type convention for oriented knots: linear extension over formal combinations and the positive-minus-negative crossing resolution. We use its usual extension to disjoint unions of oriented circles, and our squared-linking invariant also descends after forgetting orientation and component order. Order at most d means vanishing on links with d+1 such double points. This is the convention used below.

We choose coefficients in C, an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Thus the counterexample does not arise from failing to algebraically close the coefficient field. The same proof works over any characteristic-zero field. No assertion about the characteristic-p version is needed or made.

## 2. The invariant and its actual zero set

Pairwise linking numbers are integers. A crossing change between components i and j changes lk(L_i,L_j) by one with a sign, leaves the other pairwise linking numbers unchanged, and a self-crossing change changes none of them. It follows directly that each linking number has finite type at most one and g has finite type at most two.

More explicitly, resolve any three prescribed double points. Each pairwise linking number on the eight resolutions is an affine function of the three binary resolution variables. Its square has total degree at most two, so the alternating third difference is zero. Summing over pairs proves the assertion for g, uniformly in the number of components. The same argument, or the finite-type product rule, makes f=4g²−g an invariant of order at most four.

Changing the orientation of a component changes signs of incident linking numbers, and permuting component names permutes the summands. Therefore g is well-defined without fixing either those choices.

On an actual link, g is a nonnegative integer. Since 4g−1 never vanishes on an integer,

    f(L)=0  if and only if  g(L)=0.

Moreover g(L)=0 holds exactly when every pairwise linking number is zero. The unlink belongs to this set. Since f itself generates I, a link annihilates every member of I exactly when it annihilates f. Thus Z is the claimed nonempty zero set and g∈J.

Proving that g is outside the radical requires information about the **full** algebra V. A calculation inside C[g] alone would not suffice: enlarging a ring can change ideal membership. The next lemma supplies a character defined on every member of V.

## 3. Polynomial restriction along the full-twist family

Let σ be the standard positive generator of the two-strand braid group. For each integer n≥0, let L_n be the closure of σ^(2n), oriented coherently. It has two components and linking number n. For a fixed component number m>2, add m−2 split unknotted components. In either case

    g(L_n)=n².                                      (3)

**Lemma.** If v has finite type at most d, there is a unique polynomial P_v(t)∈C[t] of degree at most d such that P_v(n)=v(L_n) for every n≥0. The assignment v↦P_v is a unital algebra homomorphism V→C[t].

**Proof.** Write Δa(n)=a(n+1)−a(n). Adding one full-twist block σ² and changing one of its two crossings gives σσ^(−1), which cancels to the identity braid. Hence the first difference is the value on a braid with one double point and one remaining ordinary crossing in that block.

Iterating in r disjoint blocks gives

    Δ^r[v(L_n)]
      = Σ(j=0 to r) (−1)^(r−j) binom(r,j) v(L_(n+j)). (4)

The right side is the alternating resolution of an r-singular link: formally the braid expression is σ^(2n)(σ²−1)^r, and

    (σ²−1)^r = σ^r(σ−σ^(−1))^r.

Each factor σ−σ^(−1) is one double point resolved by the stated convention. All resolutions have an even total braid permutation and still close to two components. Extra split components remain fixed. Thus the right side of (4) is zero for r=d+1.

In characteristic zero, a sequence with identically zero (d+1)-st forward difference is the restriction of its Newton polynomial

    P_v(t)=Σ(r=0 to d) binom(t,r) Δ^r[v(L_0)],        (5)

where binom(t,r)=t(t−1)…(t−r+1)/r!. The recurrence Δ binom(t,r)=binom(t,r−1) proves agreement at all nonnegative integers, and a polynomial vanishing at infinitely many distinct integers is zero, proving uniqueness.

Linearity of the assignment follows from the defining values and uniqueness. For v,w∈V, the polynomials P_(vw) and P_vP_w agree at every n≥0, because multiplication in V is pointwise. They are therefore equal. Constants are preserved. This proves the lemma. □

The polynomial degree may depend on the invariant; no common finite-dimensional truncation of V is assumed. The construction is consequently a homomorphism on the whole algebra of finite-order invariants.

## 4. A character which actual links do not realize

Define the unital algebra homomorphism

    χ:V→C,       χ(v)=P_v(1/2).                      (6)

By (3) and the lemma, P_g(t)=t². Therefore

    χ(g)=1/4,
    χ(f)=χ(g)(4χ(g)−1)=0.

Every member of I=fV lies in ker χ. If g belonged to radical(I), some positive power g^N would lie in I. Applying χ would give

    0=χ(g^N)=(1/4)^N,

which is impossible. This proves (2) and the counterexample. □

The character χ is not evaluation on any actual link: it assigns g the value 1/4, whereas actual-link values of g are nonnegative integers. This is precisely the obstruction to replacing algebraic points by the given geometric links in the proposed equality. It is not an assertion that the classical Nullstellensatz itself fails.

There is also an integer-valued version. If V_Z is the ring of integer-valued finite-type invariants, (5) gives a rational polynomial and (6) a ring homomorphism V_Z→Q. The same f and g prove the identical strict-containment statement. The main counterexample uses C, providing a legitimate algebraically closed characteristic-zero instance of the question; the source does not uniquely fix this coefficient field.

## 5. Boundaries of the answer

The counterexample uses a principal ideal with a nonempty actual-link zero set. It does not rely on an empty variety, a transcendental constant, a restricted polynomial subalgebra, or an assumption that every character is represented by a link.

The source's separate example using indexed pairwise linking numbers is understood in the ordered/labelled theory, where those numbers are invariants. In that theory our principal ideal fV differs from their generated ideal, although their actual zero sets are both the algebraically split links. The same symmetric witness and full-algebra difference proof also work when labels are forgotten. The argument does not identify the full ideal of invariants vanishing on algebraically split links and does not decide that labelled example.

A completed invariant algebra, a homogeneous-ideal restriction, a positive-characteristic formulation, or replacing actual links by all algebra characters would change the question. None is included in the printed arbitrary-ideal statement, and no conclusion about those replacements is claimed. The construction requires at least two link components; no knot-only claim is necessary to answer the stated link question.

The finite controls accompanying this proof verify the braid exponent resolution, the finite-difference/Newton identities, the linking-square symmetry and vanishing conditions, and the character computation. The proof for all finite-type invariants is the singular-link argument and polynomial identity in Sections 3–4, not a finite list of sampled invariants. A bounded primary-source audit found no explicit equivalent earlier arbitrary-ideal counterexample in the inspected bodies. This does not establish historical firstness; the contribution is an explicit elementary application of old polynomiality, not a claim about the intended strength of a repaired conjecture.
