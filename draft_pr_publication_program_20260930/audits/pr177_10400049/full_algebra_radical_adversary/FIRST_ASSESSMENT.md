## Exact target and independently reconstructed criterion

Let X be the specified set of actual links and let V be the unital algebra of C-valued invariants on X, every element having some finite Vassiliev order, with pointwise multiplication. Work either with a fixed component number m >= 2, or with all finite-component classical links; in the latter case each invariant has a finite global order. Ordered/oriented links and the subalgebra invariant under forgetting those choices both admit the argument.

For an ideal I in V put Z_X(I) = {L in X: h(L)=0 for every h in I} and J_X(I) = {v in V: v vanishes on Z_X(I)}. The exact claim being falsified is J_X(I) = sqrt(I) for every ideal I. A single explicitly defined ideal with an element in J_X(I) outside sqrt(I) is logically sufficient. No classification of all ideals or all characters is required.

General obstruction: suppose a in V has 0 in its actual value set, c is a nonzero scalar outside that value set, and a unital algebra character chi: V -> C has chi(a)=c. For f=a(a-c), actual zeros of f are precisely actual zeros of a, so a vanishes on Z_X(fV). But fV is contained in the prime ideal ker(chi), while chi(a^N)=c^N is nonzero for every positive N. Consequently a is outside sqrt(fV). This criterion uses a character on all of V. Merely checking that a has this property inside C[a] would be insufficient: enlarging the ring can create new ideal memberships.

## Actual invariant and zero set

Choose orientations and enumerate the components temporarily. Let ell_ij denote their integer pairwise linking numbers, and set

    g = sum_{i<j} ell_ij^2,       f = g(4g-1),       I=fV.

A component reversal sends ell_ij to epsilon_i epsilon_j ell_ij; a relabeling permutes the terms. Thus g and f are independent of the temporary choices. This proves descent to unoriented and unordered objects; it does not assert that individual signed linking numbers descend.

For a singular link with r marked double points, resolving them preserves its components. Each ell_ij is an affine function of the r binary resolution choices, because a mixed crossing change changes precisely that pair's linking number by 1 or -1 and a self-crossing changes none. Thus g is a polynomial of total degree at most 2 in these choices. Its alternating resolution sum vanishes for r=3. Similarly f=4g^2-g has total degree at most 4 and vanishes for r=5. These are uniform finite-order bounds even when the component number is unrestricted. This direct argument avoids relying on an unstated product theorem.

On actual links g is a nonnegative integer, so 4g-1 cannot be zero. Therefore f(L)=0 iff g(L)=0 iff all pairwise linking numbers are zero. The unlink supplies an actual zero for every fixed m, so the zero set is nonempty. Since f itself belongs to I (V is unital), Z_X(I)=Z_X(f): one implication uses f in I and the other uses pointwise multiplication of f by any v. Hence g belongs to J_X(I). Knots and any included empty link also have g=0, which adds actual zeros without affecting the obstruction on the two-component family.

These facts use integer-valued linking numbers in characteristic zero; nonnegativity is an argument about the integers before their embedding in C, rather than an ordering of C.

## Entire-algebra character: independent proof

Let s be the generator of B_2. For n >= 0 take L_n to be the coherently oriented closure of s^(2n), with m-2 split unknotted components when a fixed m > 2 is desired. All these links have m components and g(L_n)=n^2.

For every r >= 0 the formal braid expression for the r-th forward difference is

    s^(2n)(s^2-1)^r = s^(2n+r)(s-s^(-1))^r.

The right expression is the alternating resolution of an r-singular braid with r disjoint marked crossings and r residual ordinary positive crossings. Choosing j positive resolutions gives exponent 2n+r+j-(r-j)=2(n+j), coefficient (-1)^(r-j), and multiplicity binom(r,j). Every exponent is even, so every closure has two components before adding the fixed split components. There is no passage through odd-exponent one-component braid closures. As B_2 is generated by one s, the factor rearrangement is legitimate. The corresponding singular-link identity is therefore

    Delta^r v(L_n) = sum_{j=0}^r (-1)^(r-j) binom(r,j) v(L_(n+j)).

For v of order at most d this expression vanishes identically in n when r=d+1. Over any characteristic-zero field, the elementary recurrence for forward differences gives

    P_v(t) = sum_{r=0}^d Delta^r v(L_0) binom(t,r).

Its degree is at most d and its values equal v(L_n) for all n >= 0. One proof of agreement is induction using the recurrence Delta binom(t,r)=binom(t,r-1), or the order-(d+1) recurrence plus agreement on the first d+1 inputs. Uniqueness follows since an ordinary polynomial over a characteristic-zero field cannot vanish on all distinct nonnegative integers unless it is zero.

Every member of V has its own finite d; the proof does not require a common order bound, a finite-dimensional truncation, generation of V by linking numbers, or a classification of finite-type invariants. Linearity is obtained from polynomial uniqueness. For v,w in V their pointwise product has finite order (also directly from distributing the singular differences between the factors), and P_(vw) and P_v P_w agree on every n >= 0. Uniqueness then proves equality. Constants are preserved. Accordingly P: V -> C[t] is a unital algebra homomorphism on the **entire** algebra. It need not be injective or surjective.

Now chi(v)=P_v(1/2) defines a character of all V. Since P_g=t^2, chi(g)=1/4 and chi(f)=0. Every member of fV maps to zero. If g^N were f times any full-algebra invariant h, applying chi would force (1/4)^N=0, a contradiction for every positive N. This proves g not in sqrt(I). The proof for all N is the field computation, not the finitely many powers checked in the controls.

The character cannot equal evaluation on an actual link, because its g-value is 1/4 rather than a nonnegative integer. Its kernel is a proper prime ideal, regardless of zero divisors elsewhere in V. No algebraic-closure theorem for an infinitely generated ring is invoked. C is chosen explicitly; this is a negative answer within an algebraically closed characteristic-zero coefficient convention.

## Full-algebra adversarial checks

* **Order-zero/component-count invariants:** crossing resolution preserves the component number, so arbitrary functions of that number are order zero. Restricting to L_n with a fixed m makes each such function constant and makes chi evaluate it at m. The indicator e_k of component number k maps to 1 for k=m and 0 otherwise, consistently with e_k^2=e_k and e_i e_j=0 for i != j. Their presence does not obstruct extension of the character.
* **Any other idempotent:** if e^2=e in V, its image satisfies P_e^2=P_e in C[t]. Since C[t] is an integral domain, P_e is identically 0 or 1. Thus adding idempotents cannot invalidate the character already constructed. This reasoning does not require a classification of all idempotents of V.
* **Unordered/unoriented algebra:** pull an invariant back to oriented/ordered links and apply the same singular family argument; all operations and the uniqueness argument still hold. Restrict the resulting character to the subalgebra. Although its polynomial image may be smaller than C[t], the image still contains P_g=t^2 and the same obstruction works. Possible identifications of family members impose additional polynomial relations automatically; they do not invalidate the homomorphism.
* **Reciprocal danger in larger function rings:** in the algebra of all functions on links the function h(L)=1/(4g(L)-1) is defined everywhere and satisfies f h=g, defeating the proposed witness. It is therefore essential that this function is not finite order. On L_n it would restrict to 1/(4n^2-1). If it were finite order it would equal a polynomial H(n) for all n>=0; then (4t^2-1)H(t)=1 by infinitely many polynomial zeros, an impossibility by degree. The missing reciprocal is established by proof, rather than by sampling differences.
* **Smallest component number:** m=2 already suffices; adding split components does not alter the mechanism. Restriction to knots alone has g identically zero and this construction gives no knot-only answer.

## Boundaries and exact remaining gaps

**Integer-valued ring:** for V_Z the Newton coefficients Delta^r v(L_0) are integers, but binom(t,r) is an ordinary rational polynomial. Thus P:V_Z -> Q[t] and chi:V_Z -> Q are unital ring homomorphisms. The same f,g give the same nonmembership proof. No claim that chi takes integer values is needed. For any characteristic-zero field the analogous proof works. This does not justify an assertion for arbitrary coefficient rings merely having characteristic zero.

**Positive characteristic:** the ordinary characteristic-zero polynomial interpolation proof is unavailable for high degree because of factorial denominators and repeated integer residues. For example binom(n,p) modulo p has values 0 and 1 at n=0 and n=p although these have the same residue; these restrictions occur from the integer-valued finite-type invariant binom(ell,p) on oriented two-component links before reduction. The proposed g,f also fail their stated zero-set property: for odd p, an actual linking number n=(p+1)/2 has nonzero g=n^2=1/4 modulo p and f=0. For p=2, f=g as a ring element, so g is in I. This is a failure of the particular proof in changed conventions, not a positive-characteristic resolution of the source question.

**Homogeneous restriction:** the natural finite-type structure is a filtration, and a grading requires a choice or passage to an associated graded object. Within the linking-number polynomial subalgebra, f mixes degrees 4 and 2. Replacing it with its degree-4 part 4g^2 makes g belong to the radical immediately, and replacing it by -g makes g belong to the ideal. Thus this counterexample is excluded by a homogeneous-ideal rule; no answer to such a repaired problem has been established here.

**Completion:** a completion in which sum_{k>=0}(4g)^k is allowed makes 4g-1 invertible, with inverse -sum_{k>=0}(4g)^k, so g=f/(4g-1) belongs to fV. In such a ring a unital character with g-value 1/4 is impossible because it would send a unit to zero. The ordinary ring C[[g]] is an exact model. No assertion is made that every possible completion of the link-invariant algebra exists with the same topology or that its infinite series evaluate on actual links. The submitted claim expressly excludes infinite-order completion.

**Replacing actual links with algebraic characters:** the exhibited character is itself an additional zero of I and is not a zero of g. Consequently g would not belong to the ideal of functions vanishing on that enlarged set. This explains the particular mismatch but proves no blanket Nullstellensatz over all C-valued characters of an arbitrary infinitely generated ring.

**Separate linking-number ideal:** the counterexample's ideal fV is not the ideal generated by the ell_ij. The construction identifies only the actual zero set and a witness of strict inclusion for fV. It does not identify all finite-type invariants vanishing on algebraically split links, nor prove or refute the separate example. The latter remains an exact gap.
