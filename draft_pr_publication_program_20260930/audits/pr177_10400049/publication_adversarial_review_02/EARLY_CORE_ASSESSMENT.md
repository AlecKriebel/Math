## Independent reconstruction and attempts to break the mechanism

1. On any crossing-resolution cube, temporarily label the components, whose identities persist under crossing changes. Each pairwise linking number is an affine function of the binary crossing choices: intercomponent crossings affect precisely that pair by +/-1, and self-crossings do not affect any pair. The sum of their squares is a polynomial of total degree at most two on the cube, independent of the temporary labels. Thus its alternating extension vanishes for any three or more double points. The same argument is uniform in component count because only finitely many components and cube variables occur in any one link, and the degree bound does not depend on the number of pairs. The product f has degree bound four. Products of two finite-type invariants are finite type: in the Boolean cube expansion coefficients of degrees above the order bound vanish on every face; multiplying and reducing x_i^2 = x_i cannot increase total degree beyond d+e. This checks that the stated algebra really is closed under products without a global order cutoff or completion.

2. For actual links each lk is an integer, so g is a nonnegative integer. The second root of f is 1/4, which never occurs for such g. Consequently f=0 exactly when every pairwise linking number is zero, and a unit in the algebra means the zero set of its principal ideal equals that of f. The m-component unlink proves nonemptiness; in the all-component algebra, knots and the unlink cause no contradiction and also have g=0. Algebraically split is used in its usual pairwise-linking-zero sense, not the stronger geometric-splitting property.

3. Use the coherent two-strand closure of sigma^(2n), n >= 0, plus fixed split unknots. Each even exponent has two braid components; their linking number is n because 2n coherent positive intercomponent crossings contribute half their signed sum. The baseline n=0 is the two-component unlink and is valid. Resolving the r designated crossings of sigma^(2n+r) tau^r gives exponents 2n+2j, with binomial multiplicities and sign (-1)^(r-j), i.e. Delta^r a(n). The total underlying permutation has parity (2n+r)+r even, and every resolution stays in the same two-component domain. This prevents the common erroneous substitution of an odd-exponent knot for the formal half-parameter. Added split components remain unchanged, and the double points need not be between particular labelled components for the definition to apply.

4. Order <= d gives Delta^(d+1) a(n)=0 for every n >= 0. Newton interpolation gives a polynomial of degree <= d agreeing on every nonnegative integer. Induction by the difference recurrence justifies all n, not merely the first d+1 data. Uniqueness follows from infinitely many integer inputs over characteristic zero. Consequently restriction of a sum or product agrees with the sum or product of their polynomials on infinitely many inputs, which proves a unital algebra homomorphism on the entire algebra. No character extension from a smaller linking-number subalgebra is assumed.

5. Evaluation of this polynomial at 1/2 is therefore a character on all of V_m. It sends g to 1/4 and f to zero. If g^N = f h for any positive integer N and arbitrary h in V_m, the character gives (1/4)^N = 0. This is impossible over C. The argument excludes every positive power at once, regardless of the unknown order or content of h, rather than just finitely many tested powers. In the algebra on all component counts, restrict a global invariant to this same fixed two-component family; its global finite order supplies the same difference bound and hence the same character. Uniform finite-order g and f have already been checked.

6. Forgetting labels leaves g symmetric. Reversing any component orientation changes signs of incident linking numbers and leaves squares fixed. In an unoriented invariant algebra its pullback to oriented links gives the polynomial character on the subalgebra and the same g,f obstruction; no surjectivity or reverse extension is needed. Characteristic-zero field coefficients suffice for the rational Newton denominators and nonzero powers of 1/4; no assertion is made in positive characteristic, an infinite-order completion, or a homogeneous-only ideal formulation.
