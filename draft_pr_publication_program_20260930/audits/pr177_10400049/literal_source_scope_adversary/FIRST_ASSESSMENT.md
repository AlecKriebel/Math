The literal target is the universal equality between the radical of an arbitrary ideal in the ordinary algebra of finite-type link invariants and the ideal of invariants vanishing on that ideal's common zeros among actual classical links. ## Independent challenge of the mechanism needed for source match

1. **Actual zero set is correct over the chosen field.** Every pairwise linking number of a classical link is an integer. Thus the numerical sum `g` is an element of the nonnegative integers before its injection into `C`; `4g-1` cannot be zero on a link. The equality `g=0` is equivalent to all pairwise linking numbers being zero as integers. Consequently `f=0` precisely on algebraically split links. The unlink is present, so the zero set is not empty. Because `f` itself belongs to the principal ideal and every ideal element is a multiple of it, common zeros of the entire ideal are exactly the zeros of `f`.

2. **Finite-type order is valid on the same domain.** At any chosen collection of crossing sites, each oriented pairwise linking number is affine in the binary resolution choices; a self-crossing choice has zero coefficient. Its square therefore has total degree at most two in those variables, and its third alternating difference is zero. The finite sum of the squares has order at most two, independently of component count. Its polynomial `4g^2-g` has order at most four by the same binary-resolution degree argument. Forgetting orientation or labels does not change this argument because the function descends unchanged.

3. **The restriction argument covers the full function algebra.** A calculation confined to `C[g]` would be insufficient: extending a ring can change membership in a generated ideal. The submitted proof instead takes an arbitrary finite-order invariant `v` in the full `V`, restricts it to coherent closures of `sigma^(2n)`, and proves that restriction polynomial. This is the right obligation, not an assumption that all invariants are polynomials in linking numbers.

4. **The singular resolutions preserve the same link category.** For `r` disjoint full-twist blocks, replacing one crossing in each block gives the formal braid difference `sigma^(2n)(sigma^2-1)^r = sigma^(2n+r)(sigma-sigma^(-1))^r`. Each factor in the last product is the difference of resolutions of a single transverse double point. Every ordinary resolution has even total braid permutation, so the closure has two components. The singular skeleton similarly has an even total swap permutation, so it is an immersion of two circles. Coherent orientations and labels are transported through that skeleton; fixed added split components are unaffected. Braid cancellation of one positive and one negative crossing is an isotopy allowed in the classical category. A reversal of the singular-resolution sign convention only multiplies the alternating expression by a sign and cannot affect its vanishing.

5. **No interpolation bound for the whole algebra is assumed.** If `v` has order at most `d`, the preceding singular expression proves its `(d+1)`st forward difference vanishes for every `n >= 0`. In characteristic zero, the Newton formula with coefficients `Delta^r v(L_0)` and polynomials `binom(t,r)` therefore gives a polynomial `P_v(t)` agreeing for all nonnegative integers. The elementary difference identity proves agreement inductively, and infinitely many distinct integer points prove uniqueness. The degree bound can depend on `v`. This constructs a restriction map on the entire ordinary finite-order algebra, not only a finite list or truncation.

6. **Multiplicativity is derived, not presumed.** Linearity, unit preservation, and `P_(vw)=P_v P_w` follow because the two polynomial expressions agree at all nonnegative integer points and multiplication of invariants is pointwise. The product remains finite type by the same finite-difference degree/product rule. This makes evaluation at `t=1/2` a unital algebra homomorphism defined on every relevant invariant. Its existence does not require Kontsevich integration or any claim that characters are geometric links.

7. **The radical obstruction is exact.** Along this family, `g(L_n)=n^2`, so uniqueness gives `P_g=t^2`. Evaluation at one half sends `g` to `1/4` and `f` to zero. Every multiple of `f` is killed. If a positive power of `g` lay in the ideal, its character value would both vanish and equal the nonzero number `(1/4)^N`, a contradiction. This proves strict containment for the full algebra, rather than just failure of principal divisibility in a polynomial subalgebra. The same calculation shows that this character is not any actual-link evaluation.

I found no hidden circularity or unsupported transfer of the main difficulty in those seven obligations. The universal statement comes from the singular-link proof and polynomial uniqueness, not numerical sampling. No toy finite computation was performed or promoted as evidence.

## What is and is not resolved

Let `K` denote the ideal generated by the individual linking-number invariants in an oriented labeled fixed-component category. The candidate's `f` belongs to `K`, since `g` is a sum of squares of those generators; so `(f)` is contained in `K`. The fact that `(f)` and `K` have the same actual zero set does **not** imply equal radicals or equal ideals. The candidate's separator character does not annihilate `K`: on the two-component family it takes the pairwise linking number to `1/2`. Moreover `g` is already in `K`. Thus this construction neither refutes nor proves the separate question whether the whole algebraically-split-link vanishing ideal equals `K`.

Completion is a material boundary. In a completion where `g` is topologically nilpotent, `1-4g` has the geometric-series inverse, so `f=-g(1-4g)` generates the same ideal as `g`. That defeats this particular separator mechanism; it does not settle a completed version, and arbitrary formal series need not even have evaluation on actual links without extra choices. No completed algebra is specified by the acquired question.

Positive characteristic is another material boundary: nonnegative integers can collide in the coefficient field, the integer zero-set argument can fail, and the interpolation/uniqueness step using infinitely many distinct integer points and factorial denominators fails as written. A homogeneous-ideal restriction, a fixed-linking-matrix domain, or zeros taken among all characters similarly changes the claim. None may be silently inferred from the literal source wording.
