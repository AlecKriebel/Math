## Findings and exact logical distinctions
No inspected problem update explicitly announces a solution of the arbitrary-ideal assertion. No inspected primary paper explicitly states the candidate linking-square principal-ideal counterexample or its half-twist character. Appleboim develops relative finite-type theories with linking matrix fixed. Its diagram image theorem5.2 and open completeness/kernel questions in Section6 do not by themselves provide a full-ring ideal-membership theorem. Even if the linking-number-generated ideal example were proved, that would concern a different ideal and cannot preempt the counterexample to arbitrary ideals by itself. Mellor's extension/comparison and Milnor-type results concern those relative theories.

Ng's theorem3.2/corollary3.3 concerns matching every invariant through any fixed finite degree by ribbon knots when Arf vanishes. The 2003 correction/addendum addresses the index-two argument and integral versus rational coefficients, not link-invariant radicals. This supplies finite-order approximation evidence; it neither chooses fV nor proves arbitrary-ideal equality. Do not silently equate its Arf remark with the Zariski closure taken only against complex-valued ordinary finite-type polynomial functions: Arf is an F2-valued finite-type invariant, and its lifted parity is not thereby a complex-valued finite-type invariant.

There is, however, a substantive PRIOR INGREDIENT implication route. Bar-Natan/Ron2003 p1 explicitly records that additive rational-valued invariants generate the algebra of all rational-valued invariants; Ng1995 pp12-13 identifies the integer Conway coefficient a2 and its nontrivial primitive degree-two role. Let K be a trefoil with a2(K)=1. For an invariant polynomial in additive invariants, restriction to n-fold connected sums of K is polynomial in n. Multiplication is preserved, hence evaluation at n=1/2 is a character on the entire ordinary algebra covered by that generation fact. Then q=a2, F=q(2q-1) gives the same type of nonempty actual-zero-set radical counterexample: integer actual values force Z(F)=Z(q), while chi(q)=1/2 and chi(F)=0 imply q not in radical(FV). For links, use q=sum of the Conway a2 invariants of the components and restrict to split links consisting of n-fold trefoil sums on one component plus fixed unknots.

This is my deduction from explicit old ingredients, not an explicit claim made by those authors, and not evidence that anyone published the arbitrary-ideal answer earlier. Its exact pending gap is independent validation of the passage from rational-generation statements to all complex finite-order invariants and of the full-link algebra restriction. A singular crossing argument may avoid this scalar-extension issue, but that would be an additional proof, not a quoted primary theorem. Thus neither 'no explicit solution found' nor source silence warrants a strong novelty claim; this older mechanism materially narrows any plausible originality claim to the newly identified consequence and/or specific linking-square construction.
