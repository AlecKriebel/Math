## Exact claim audited

Let k be a field of characteristic zero, and let V be the ordinary unital k-algebra of finite-order Vassiliev invariants of knots, of links with a fixed positive number of components, or of all finite-component links. Products are pointwise, and each member of V has some finite order. Ordinary means that all crossing changes, including self-crossings, enter the Vassiliev definition. No completion or infinite sum is allowed.

For knots put q(K)=a_2(K), the coefficient of z^2 in the normalized Conway polynomial. For links put q(L)=sum_C a_2(C), summing over the component knots obtained by forgetting the other components. Put F=q(2q-1), I=FV, and J=I(Z(I)), where zeros are actual knots or links in the chosen domain.

Claim: Z(I) is nonempty, q belongs to J, but q does not belong to radical(I). Consequently J is strictly larger than radical(I). This refutes the assertion that the actual-link version of the Nullstellensatz holds for every ideal of the full ordinary finite-order invariant algebra. It says nothing decisive about the special ideal generated by linking numbers.

Verdict: the claim is valid over Q, C, and every characteristic-zero field, under the stated conventions. The rational knot case follows from the old additive-generation ingredient. That ingredient alone does not establish the full complex-valued link case without an additional extension argument. A direct crossing-change proof below establishes the full complex-valued link case independently and removes the need for scalar extension.

## Deduction from the old rational generation statement

Fix a trefoil T with a_2(T)=1. Let K_n be the connected sum of n copies of T, with K_0 the unknot. If p is an additive normalized rational invariant, then p(K_n)=n p(T).

The generation statement implies that any rational-valued finite-order invariant v can be expressed using finitely many additive generators, finitely many sums and products, and a constant. Hence v(K_n)=P_v(n) for some P_v in Q[t]. This polynomial is unique: two polynomials agreeing for all n>=0 agree identically over Q. No assertion that the generating set is algebraically independent is required. Every algebraic relation among the generators restricts to a polynomial relation vanishing for all n>=0 and hence vanishes identically.

The restriction map rho(v)=P_v is a Q-algebra homomorphism: uniqueness on the infinite sequence proves rho(v+w)=rho(v)+rho(w), rho(vw)=rho(v)rho(w), and rho(1)=1. Since q(K_n)=n, rho(q)=t. Thus evaluation of rho(v) at t=1/2 defines a character on the entire rational invariant algebra, not merely on Q[q].

The conclusion uses each invariant's finite algebraic expression, not a uniform finite generating set. Infinitely many available additive invariants cause no problem. Conversely, arbitrary infinite series would cause a problem and are excluded.

## Independent construction for the full coefficient and link algebra

The following proof applies directly to k-valued invariants, with no claim that V_k is a scalar extension of V_Q.

Take the positive trefoil represented by the closure of the two-strand braid sigma_1^3. Change one specified positive crossing to negative. The resulting braid reduces to sigma_1, whose closure is the unknot. Cut both diagrams outside this crossing to obtain long-knot factors T_+ and T_-, with T_- the trivial long knot. The change occurs inside the factor, so it can be repeated in disjoint connected-sum factors on one chosen link component.

For a fixed number m>=1 of components, let L_n be the split union of K_n and m-1 unknots. For the algebra of all links, use any one fixed m. The chosen component may be selected in these representative diagrams even when the link category is unordered; no invariant is required to remember that selection.

Let v have order at most d and let f_v(n)=v(L_n). Insert r additional long-trefoil factors in the chosen component and turn the specified crossing of each inserted factor into a double point. Resolving a subset S of these r double points positively and the others negatively produces L_{n+|S|}: each negative factor is the trivial long knot. With the usual positive-minus-negative convention, the value on this r-singular link is therefore

    sum_{S subset {1,...,r}} (-1)^(r-|S|) v(L_{n+|S|})
    = sum_{j=0}^r (-1)^(r-j) binom(r,j) f_v(n+j)
    = Delta^r f_v(n).

All singularities are disjoint self-double-points of the selected component. For r=d+1 the Vassiliev condition gives Delta^(d+1) f_v(n)=0 for every n>=0.

In characteristic zero the Newton polynomial

    P_v(t) = sum_{j=0}^d Delta^j f_v(0) binom(t,j)

is defined in k[t], with binom(t,j)=t(t-1)...(t-j+1)/j! and binom(t,0)=1. It agrees with f_v on all nonnegative integers. One way to check the last statement is to use Delta binom(t,j)=binom(t,j-1); the polynomial has the same first d+1 forward differences at 0 as f_v, and both satisfy the same recurrence Delta^(d+1)=0. That recurrence determines the whole sequence from its first d+1 values. Increasing a chosen order bound d adds only zero terms, so P_v does not depend on that choice. Its uniqueness follows from the infinite set of distinct integer points in k.

The ordinary finite-order invariants form an algebra: the crossing-difference product rule assigns each differentiated crossing to one of the two factors, so a product of orders d and e has order at most d+e. Using uniqueness on L_n gives a k-algebra homomorphism rho:V->k[t] exactly as above. Consequently

    phi_alpha(v)=P_v(alpha)

is a character on the entire k-valued ordinary finite-order invariant algebra for every alpha in k. This statement remains true when other invariants depend arbitrarily on component count at order zero: along the chosen fixed-m sequence their count stays constant.

There is no limiting argument, convergence assumption, or scalar-extension theorem in this construction. Each P_v is a finite polynomial.

## The invariant q and its required normalization

Use the Conway normalization nabla_U=1, nabla_split_unlink=0 for more than one component, and nabla_{L+}-nabla_{L-}=z nabla_{L0}. It gives integer polynomial coefficients. For the positive Hopf link H, changing a crossing gives the two-component unlink and smoothing gives the unknot; hence nabla_H=z. For the positive trefoil, changing the specified crossing gives the unknot and smoothing gives H; hence nabla_T=1+z^2, so a_2(T)=1. Thus the numerical normalization needed for the half-integer character is checkable by these two skein computations.

The Conway polynomial of knots is multiplicative under connected sum and has no term of degree one. Therefore a_2(K#K')=a_2(K)+a_2(K'), a_2(U)=0, and q(L_n)=n. This is the only connected-sum normalization needed for the polynomial witness.

The coefficient a_2 of a knot is an order-at-most-two invariant. Directly, applying the Conway skein relation at r singular crossings multiplies the fully smoothed Conway polynomial by z^r, whose z^2 coefficient is zero if r>2. This agrees with the order-two statement in Ng's body.

For links, q=sum_C a_2(C) is again an integer-valued invariant of order at most two. At an intercomponent crossing, q has zero crossing difference because each separate component knot is unchanged. For a cube of self-crossings, a summand a_2(C) depends only on crossings belonging to C; a derivative involving another component vanishes. Any possible remaining threefold derivative on one component is zero by order two. This establishes the order bound uniformly even if the domain includes all finite component counts.

The sum is symmetric in the components and a_2 is unchanged by reversing a knot's orientation. Thus q is well-defined for ordered or unordered oriented links and also for unoriented links under the usual Conway coefficient convention. Picking a component to construct L_n does not add ordering to the invariant itself.

## Radical obstruction and nonempty actual zero set

For every actual knot or link in the domain, q has an integer value. In characteristic zero no integer equals 1/2. Therefore

    F(L)=q(L)(2q(L)-1)=0 if and only if q(L)=0.

Since V contains the constant 1, Z(I)=Z(F). Every unlink of the allowed positive component count has q=0; hence Z(I) is nonempty. The displayed equivalence gives q in J.

Now use phi=phi_(1/2). The proved restriction character satisfies

    phi(q)=1/2,
    phi(F)=(1/2)(2(1/2)-1)=0.

Thus I is contained in the prime maximal kernel of phi, while q is not. If q were in radical(I), some positive power q^N would lie in I; applying phi would give (1/2)^N=0, impossible in a characteristic-zero field. Hence q is outside radical(I).

Equivalently, if q^N=Fh for a full-algebra invariant h, restriction to L_n would give the polynomial identity t^N=t(2t-1)P_h(t). Evaluation at 1/2 contradicts it. This alternative explicitly checks the ambient-ideal problem: it rules out a witness h from anywhere in the full algebra, not only from the subalgebra k[q].

## Adversarial checks and exact failures of overextended claims

- **Rational ingredient alone:** enough for the full rational knot algebra. It does not by itself prove that arbitrary complex-valued invariants lie in a scalar extension, nor does it establish generation of the full link algebra by additive knot invariants. Those extensions require proof. The crossing construction above supplies the needed full-algebra argument directly.
- **Subalgebra radical test:** proving nonmembership only in radical(F k[q]) would be insufficient; a larger algebra might acquire additional ideal witnesses. The constructed character is defined on the full algebra and closes this gap.
- **Invalid old ribbon theorem:** unused. Neither ribbon density nor any invariant-equivalence group theorem is needed.
- **Fixed-order spaces:** V_d is not generally an algebra. The ideal is taken in the union of all finite-order spaces. q has order at most two and F has order at most four; witnesses h would have some finite order of their own.
- **Finite fields and characteristic p:** the integer-value exclusion of 1/2 fails in positive characteristic (and 2 is zero in characteristic two). This note makes no positive-characteristic counterexample claim.
- **Completed algebras:** excluded. Arbitrary completed elements need not restrict to polynomials, and evaluation of a formal series at 1/2 may be undefined. The argument cannot be transferred to a completion without new definitions and analysis.
- **Link-homotopy quotients:** excluded. q detects component knotting and is not a link-homotopy invariant. “Ordinary” is a material assumption.
- **Framing or labeling:** labels are not needed. The construction also makes sense with a fixed orientation choice. The proof as stated uses ordinary unframed isotopy; any framed variant should explicitly fix the framings and forget them when defining q.
- **Meaning of zeros:** these are zeros on actual knots or links. If zeros were all algebraic characters, phi_(1/2) would be included, q would not vanish there, and this would not be a counterexample to that different statement.
- **Linking-number ideal:** the general counterexample does not falsify or prove the source's separate claim for that special ideal.
- **Priority:** old documented generation makes this counterexample a rigorous deduction from old mathematical ingredients. It does not establish a documented earlier explicit answer. Novelty of the precise counterexample remains a bibliographic question, with no absence guarantee from this local audit.
