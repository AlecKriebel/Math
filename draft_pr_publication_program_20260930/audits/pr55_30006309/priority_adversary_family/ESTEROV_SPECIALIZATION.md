## Universal coefficient setup

Let Q be the candidate's n-dimensional Delzant polytope, A=Q∩Z^n, n≥1, with the smooth complete very ample embedding and degree≥2. Take n equations

\[
F_i(c,x)=\sum_{a\in A}c_{a,i}x^a,\qquad i=0,\ldots,n-1,
\]

with separate coefficient variables c_{a,i}. Put V=R^{A×n} and

\[
\Delta_i=\operatorname{conv}\{(e_{a,i},a):a\in A\}\subset V\oplus R^n.
\]

These are Newton polytopes in the global parameter space. They have identical full-dimensional x-support A, hence are analogous in Esterov's terminology. The lattice index is one: primitive edge steps at any Delzant vertex belong to A and form an integral basis. This also holds for every induced face lattice.

The general-position condition on leading coefficients is satisfied in this universal setup. For any face truncations of q chosen equations in Definition 5.5, choose an active coefficient variable in each equation. Its derivative is the nonzero monomial x^a on the torus; it appears in no other equation. The corresponding q×q Jacobian minor is diagonal and nonzero. Thus zero is a regular value. This argument is stronger than the injective-face restriction required there.

Do not substitute identical coefficients into different equations. That would make the equations dependent. The operation here is linear projection of exponent polytopes, so there is no monomial cancellation.

## Identify and project the discriminant

For a nonempty subset J of the n identical supports, codim J=n−|J|. Every proper J has a strictly smaller value for a strict superset, so the sole admissible factor in Cayley Theorem 2.31 is the full Cayley configuration B=A×Vert(Δ_{n−1}). Consequently the reduced mixed discriminant in this universal coefficient space is the reduced B-discriminant. For n=1 this identification is the ordinary one-equation definition. The full affine lattice is generated by B; thus the exponent convention discussed in Esterov Definition 2.7 has index one. It agrees with the ordinary irreducible discriminant used in the candidate. The degree hypothesis is retained to avoid the degree-one trivial Hurwitz case.

Let π:V→R^A sum the n coordinates over each a. On V⊕R^n act by π on the coefficient factor and identity on x. All projected Δ_i become

\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(e_a,a):a\in A\}.
\]

Linear projection commutes with normalized Minkowski integrals: a support covector w after projection evaluates the same fiber support as π* w before projection. It commutes with mixed fiber integrals by polarization as well. Applying π to the finite mixed-fiber formula of Theorem 5.10 therefore reduces it to the repeated-argument formula in Definition 5.12, with l=n−1.

For a j-face F of Q, write Σ_F for the normalized Minkowski integral of Δ_F=conv{(e_a,a):a∈A∩F}. The binomial coefficient is zero when j<n−1. Smooth signed Euler coefficients are 1 for Q and −1 for facets. Hence, as an identity of virtual polytopes with an actual Newton polytope on the left,

\[
N:=\pi\operatorname{Newt}(D_B)=n\Sigma_Q-\sum_{F\text{ facet of }Q}\Sigma_F.\tag{1}
\]

Subtraction in (1) is a Minkowski difference, equivalently subtraction of support functions. It is not set subtraction and is not an unproved assertion that an arbitrary difference is convex: Esterov's theorem provides the actual Newton polytope.

## From fibers to characteristic vectors

For a generic lower height w on A, let T be its regular triangulation. The fiber of Δ_F over x∈F consists of convex coefficient distributions with barycenter x. Its minimum under w is the lower convex envelope g_w(x). Esterov's normalization is (j+1)! times lattice Lebesgue measure on a j-face. Thus

\[
\min_{z\in\Sigma_F}\langle w,z\rangle=(j+1)!\int_Fg_w(x)\,dx.
\]

On each j-simplex σ of T restricted to F, g_w is affine. Its integral is Euclidean volume times the average of its j+1 vertex heights. Therefore

\[
(j+1)!\int_Fg_w=\sum_{\sigma\in T|_F,\ \dim\sigma=j}
\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb Z}(\sigma)\sum_{a\in\operatorname{Vert}\sigma}w_a.
\]

The top-face expression is 〈w,η_{T,n}〉. Summing over facets gives exactly 〈w,η_{T,n−1}〉, because only boundary massive simplices contribute. Interior simplices are excluded. Equation (1) now gives

\[
\min_{z\in N}\langle w,z\rangle=
\langle w,n\eta_{T,n}-\eta_{T,n-1}\rangle.\tag{2}
\]

For each regular T, its height cone has a nonempty open interior modulo affine heights. The right side of (2) is linear throughout that cone. Since N is a polytope, its support gradient there is the vertex ξ_T=nη_{T,n}−η_{T,n−1}. Conversely every vertex of N is exposed by a generic w, and therefore has this form. This proves N=conv{ξ_T:T regular}. The argument allows unused lattice points and nonunimodular triangulation simplices; it does not add a unimodular assumption.

As a normalization control, for n=1 and Q=[0,2], the coarse and fine regular triangulations yield ξ=(1,0,1) and ξ=(0,2,0). They are the exponent vectors of ac and b² in the quadratic discriminant b²−4ac. Thus no translation, sign reversal, extra factorial or index factor appears in this specialization. This is a small exact check of conventions, not proof of the full family or a new assertion-count certificate.

## Priority implication and precise limitation

This demonstrates that a substantially stronger prior Newton-polytope theorem already implies the all-dimensional endpoint without K-energy. It undermines claims of the first non-Kähler derivation, a new equality, or absence of an earlier general combinatorial formula. It does not identify an earlier publication containing the candidate's exact product-face vector identity plus finite-difference and equal-column GKZ-fan proof. That particular proof may be a useful alternative, but its historical originality remains unestablished. Esterov's foundational proof uses algebraic/topological inputs, including elimination and Euler characteristics; this comparison does not certify a foundation-free elementary proof.
