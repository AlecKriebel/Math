# Independent product-volume derivation and adversarial scope

This derivation was formed from the literal candidate and primary source definitions before reading any author verifier, old reviewer or other new reviewer. It verifies the claimed infinite family mathematically, conditional on the established GKZ inputs cited below. Finite controls are supplementary.

Let Q have dimension n ≥ 1 and let A be its complete lattice configuration. The geometric identification uses a smooth polarized toric variety, a complete very ample embedding, and degree at least two, as in Sano's introduction and Theorem 1.4. Write S = Δ_(n−1), B = A × Vert(S), and P = Q × S. We use the saturated induced affine lattice in every face and normalized simplex volume, with a point of volume one. The map π sums coordinates over the second-factor vertices, corresponding to restriction from the full coefficient torus on B to the diagonal copy of the first-factor coefficient torus on A. These two tori must not be conflated with the n-dimensional intrinsic dense torus of X.

For smooth complete A, the saturated-face lattice equals the affine lattice generated by A ∩ F. At a vertex of any face F, the primitive directions of its incident edges extend to a lattice basis because Q is Delzant. Each corresponding first lattice step belongs to the complete configuration. Their differences generate the full induced lattice of F. The same assertion holds for P, since the standard simplex has only its vertices as lattice points and its face lattices are unimodular. Thus the candidate normalization matches the GKZ convention exactly. Arbitrary sparse or singular configurations are outside this verification.

## 1. A local product statement without a staircase assumption

Take an arbitrary j-simplex σ in a face F of dimension j, an l-face E of the standard simplex, and k = j+l. Triangulate σ × E into k-simplices using only products of the vertices of σ and E. No regularity, unimodularity of σ, or staircase ordering is assumed.

Let V be the normalized volume of σ in F. Euclidean volume is measured using fundamental parallelepipeds of the respective affine lattices. The ordinary product-volume rule gives normalized volume

    Vol_Z(σ × E) = k!/(j! l!) · V = binom(k,j) V.

For a vertex a of σ, its barycentric coordinate λ_a extends affinely in the product by ignoring E. Its value at every allowed product vertex is zero or one. On any simplex τ of the product triangulation, the affine-function mean is the mean of its vertex values. Consequently its volume-weighted contribution to λ_a is Vol_Z(τ) times the number of τ-vertices projecting to a divided by k+1. Additivity of volume-weighted centroids gives

    sum_τ Vol_Z(τ) · # {vertices projecting to a}
       = (k+1) · Vol_Z(σ × E)/(j+1)
       = binom(k+1,j+1) V.

The product centroid has barycentric coordinates 1/(j+1) on σ; this also follows from invariance under permutation of σ's barycentric coordinates. Such permutations preserve the chosen volume because they have determinant of absolute value one on the affine span. The centroid argument is an elementary finite polyhedral volume identity. It is unrelated to K-energy or limiting analytic slope formulas.

Vertex multiplicity is essential: one simplex may contain several vertices projecting to a. Replacing that count by a zero-or-one row indicator is incorrect. The induced-lattice normalization is essential: a primitive diagonal segment in Z² has normalized length one, despite squared Euclidean length two.

## 2. Global massive faces and no overcounting

Each k-face of P has the unique form F × E with dim F = j, dim E = l, j+l=k. A k-simplex contained in such a face cannot belong to a different k-face, since the intersection of distinct k-faces has dimension below k. Thus every massive k-simplex has exactly one carrier, and internal k-simplices in higher-dimensional faces do not count.

Let T triangulate Q, and let U refine every σ × S using only products of T-vertices and simplex vertices. Restrict to F × E. The top simplices of T restricted to F partition F, and their products with E partition F × E. Their intersections have dimension less than k and have zero k-volume. The local statement adds over this partition; then add over all F and E. There are binom(n,l+1) choices of E, yielding coordinatewise

    π η_(U,k) = sum_(j+l=k) binom(n,l+1) binom(k+1,j+1) η_(T,j).

The argument is valid for j=0, l=0 and k=0. An unused point of A contributes zero: the assumed vertex restriction prevents U from using any point in its column. A triangulation with new vertices at an unused point would violate this restriction, and its barycentric values need not be zero or one. The candidate explicitly excludes that case from the local identity.

## 3. The alternating cancellation for every n

With d=2n−1, the coefficient of η_(T,j) in π m_U is

    c_(n,j) = sum_(l=0)^(n−1) (−1)^(2n−1−j−l)
                  binom(n,l+1) binom(j+l+1,j+1).

Put r=l+1 and f_j(r)=binom(j+r,j+1). The r=0 term is zero. Since the two sign exponents differ by an even integer,

    c_(n,j) = (−1)^(n−j) Δ^n f_j(0).

Repeated Pascal differences reduce the lower binomial index one at a time. If j ≤ n−2, f_j has degree j+1<n, so the nth difference vanishes. If j=n−1 it equals one, with overall sign minus. If j=n it equals binom(n,1)=n, with positive sign. Therefore

    π m_U = n η_(T,n) − η_(T,n−1).

For n=1 the first range is empty; the formula says η_(T,1)−η_(T,0). Degree-one Δ_n has zero Hurwitz vector, compatible with a constant discriminant convention, but a nonconstant geometric form in that excluded regime is not asserted. Nonunimodular simplices do not change the cancellation, since their correct face volumes enter linearly throughout.

## 4. Genuine support-function proof of both inclusions

Let w be a generic height inducing a regular triangulation T of A. Lift it to B by setting h(a,v)=w(a). Its lower convex envelope at (x,y) is g_w(x): every convex representation costs at least g_w(x), and a minimizing convex representation of x combined with the barycentric representation of y realizes equality. Hence its regular subdivision is exactly the product of T's cells with S.

A sufficiently small generic perturbation h+εq refines this subdivision. For a finite configuration this follows by retaining the strict signs of all previously nonzero circuit relations, while a generic q resolves the zero relations. For all sufficiently small positive ε their signs are fixed, so one triangulation U works for the full one-sided interval. Every unused point of generic T is strictly above the lower envelope; all points in its column remain unused for small ε. The product vertex restriction needed above is therefore satisfied.

The precise established input is that the massive vector m_U is the discriminant vertex selected by U's lower-hull height cone. This is stronger than merely saying the set of massive vectors equals the set of vertices. GKZ's principal-determinant initial-monomial formula gives the secondary vertex selected by the height. The regular determinant's factorization in principal determinants labels the corresponding massive vector. Its Newton polytope is a Minkowski summand of the secondary polytope, so the secondary normal fan refines its normal fan. Under smoothness the regular determinant is the ordinary discriminant. Lower-hull heights select minima. In particular, m_U minimizes h+εq. Taking ε→0 in the finite family of weak inequalities shows that it also minimizes h.

Set H=conv{nη_(T,n)−η_(T,n−1): T regular} and D=conv{m_U: U regular}. Every T has a regular product-refining U, so its Hurwitz vector belongs to πD. This proves H ⊆ πD.

For a generic w, choose the U just constructed. Then

    min_(z ∈ πD) <w,z> = <w,πm_U> ∈ {<w,x>: x∈H}.

Since H ⊆ πD, this equality of an attained minimum forces min_H<w,·>=min_(πD)<w,·>. Generic heights are dense in the finite-dimensional height space. Both support-minimum functions are continuous, as minima over fixed finite vertex sets, so equality extends to every w. If πD had a point outside H, closed-convex separation would produce a functional with strictly smaller minimum on πD after changing its sign. That contradicts the equality. Hence πD=H.

No step assumes every projected vertex is extreme. The independent unit-square controls actually exhibit projected massive vectors (2,0,2,0), (0,2,0,2), and their nonextreme midpoint (1,1,1,1). This explicitly falsifies that tempting stronger statement without harming the support-function argument.
