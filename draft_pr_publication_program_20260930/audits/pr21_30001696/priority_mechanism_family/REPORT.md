### Why the direct shortcut fails, and how the outward collar repairs it

The collapsed coordinate sphere contains e_1. For every substantive i<=d-2, e_1 has s^-=0 but s^+=d-1; arbitrarily small normalized alternating-sign perturbations lie outside C_i. Thus e_1 is a boundary point of B(i,d). The complex is not an ambient neighborhood of that coordinate core. This is proved universally in CONTROL_PROOFS.md, with an exact-arithmetic transcription replay over d=2,...,20.

Consequently, applying Corollary 3.30 directly to B and that coordinate sphere is blocked at its neighborhood premise. This was the exact initial gap, not a proof that no repair existed.

**The repair is standard collaring.** The complementary facet complex is also a PL manifold and has the same boundary. Add a short outward collar of the entire boundary in that complement. The resulting compact PL manifold N is PL homeomorphic to B by compressing combined inward/outward collars, collapses onto B by the cylinder-base collapse, and contains every point of B in its ambient interior. Thus N collapses to the same coordinate sphere X and really is a neighborhood of it. Corollary 3.30 applies, and Theorem 3.8 identifies N with the standard regular neighborhood of the same embedded equatorial X. That standard neighborhood has the finite cell structure of X times the cone on the complementary cross-polytope sphere, giving the required PL product. No vector-bundle classification or pushed-in spine is needed.

## Family C: classical total positivity, Chebyshev spaces and spectral splitting

[Schwarz (1970)](https://msp.org/pjm/1970/32-1/pjm-v32-n1-p20-s.pdf), Theorem 3, pp.213--214, characterizes strictly totally positive differential systems by the tridiagonal adjacent-positive mechanism (with the stated nonvanishing-on-every-interval condition in the general time-varying case). The proof uses positivity/irreducibility of all compound systems. Theorem 4, pp.214--215, gives the zero-sensitive strict variation-diminishing inequality s^+(y(s))<=s^-(y(r)). These are directly relevant classical inputs to the candidate's Lemma 3.1 and strict trapping argument.

[Margaliot--Sontag (2019)](https://sontaglab.org/FTPDIR/margaliot_sontag_totally_positive_automatica2019.pdf), Theorem 3, p.4, restates the strict inequality. The Appendix Proposition 1 and cofactor proof, pp.11--12, relate the common nonzero sign of maximal minors of an n-by-m matrix to the s^+<=m-1 property for every nonzero linear combination of its columns. Thus the low-variation subspace mechanism is itself classical, rather than a novel inference specific to this PR.

[Weiss--Margaliot, arXiv:1902.01630v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.01630), Lemma 5 in Section 5.1, PDF pp.19--20, constructs the k-dimensional interior subspace and complementary (n-k)-dimensional subspace avoiding P_-^k apart from zero, using oscillatory eigenvectors and the sign bounds on their linear combinations. It explicitly identifies the cone rank. [Alseidi--Margaliot--Garloff, arXiv:1910.08125v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08125), Theorem 1 and its proof, pp.8--9, gives invariant E and a complementary subspace, the spectral gap, exponential relative decay, and eventual low variation for the corresponding discrete dynamics. The arXiv preprint was used because the attempted author-hosted accepted PDF had a hostname certificate mismatch; no TLS bypass was used and no publisher-version identity is asserted.

These results prove spectral sign separation and attraction, not a homeomorphism of the *entire closed spherical cone* to a disk product. ### The closest flow-hitting lemma is a point-attractor theorem

[Galashin--Karp--Lam, arXiv:1707.02010v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02010), Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2 on p.4 and Lemma 2.3 with its full proof on pp.5--6, require a continuous contractive flow on R^N that strictly decreases the norm of every nonzero point for positive time. The origin is its unique fixed point and all orbits approach it. Lemma 2.3 assumes a bounded smooth embedded submanifold Q and **f(t,closure(Q)) contained in Q**, t>0. The closure bar is present in the actual p.5 image; plain-text extraction loses it. Its conclusion is a closed ball and spherical boundary.

The proof takes a small ball at the origin, constructs unique orbit cross-section times and first-boundary times, proves their continuity with strict inequalities and openness, and constructs the homeomorphism. This is a genuine earlier hitting-time and straightening mechanism that should be credited precisely.

The normalized candidate flow has norm one and fixes each unit eigenvector and its negative. Its attracting S(E) is not a unique origin; a direct global application of Definition 2.1 fails. For i>=1, an application that concluded a closed ball would also contradict the known S^i homology. A fiberwise extension would require an invariant fiber structure and a compatible trivialization. The candidate supplies its own global orbit coordinates and an identity cutoff near the entire sphere; Lemma 2.3 does not by itself establish those sphere-attractor extension premises.
