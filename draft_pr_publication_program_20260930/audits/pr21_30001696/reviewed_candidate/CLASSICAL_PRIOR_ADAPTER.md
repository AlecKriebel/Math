# Exact-prior adapter: Klee–Novik plus classical regular neighborhoods

## Claim and hypotheses

Let `d >= 2`, `0 <= i <= d-2`. Let `M = |C_d^*|`, the standard PL `(d-1)`-sphere, and `K=|B(i,d)|` generated by NONCYCLIC words with at most `i` switches. Let `K'` be the complementary full-dimensional subcomplex, generated by the other facets. Let `E=|C^*_{i+1}|` on the first `i+1` coordinate pairs; it is the standard coordinate PL `i`-sphere. All maps, collars, homeomorphisms and collapses below are in the PL category. This concerns the WHOLE complex, not merely its boundary or homology.

Klee–Novik Theorem 1.2(c),(d), proved in §3 of both original arXiv and author-final, gives: K and K' are compact PL `(d-1)`-manifolds with boundary, K' is simplicially isomorphic to `B(d-i-2,d)`. Remark 3.7 (printed p.9) records `B(i,d)` collapsing to `B(i,d-1)` and then to `B(i,i+1)=C^*_{i+1}`. The original sources predate the candidate by more than a decade.

## 1. Independently expand the recorded collapse

For any `j >= i+2`, write `L_x=lk_{B(i,j)}(x_j)` and `L_y=lk_{B(i,j)}(y_j)`. Klee–Novik Lemma 3.4 gives shellable stars. Since each star is a cone and all its facets contain the cone vertex, deleting that vertex from its shelling order gives a shelling of its link. Each link is a proper full-dimensional subcomplex of the `(j-2)`-sphere `C^*_{j-1}` (some alternating completions are excluded because `i <= j-2`), so the elementary shellability criterion recalled in KN §2 shows it is a PL `(j-2)`-ball. A shellable ball is collapsible. Every facet of `B(i,j-1)` extends to at least one allowed facet by appending the same letter as its final letter, while deleting an appended last letter does not increase switches. Therefore `L_x union L_y = B(i,j-1)`, and

`B(i,j) = (x_j * L_x) union (y_j * L_y)`.

For a collapsible polyhedron L, its cone collapses to the base L (Rourke–Sanderson Ch.3 p.40, example C; also elementary cone-lifting of a collapse followed by deletion of the last cone edge). Apply this to each cone relative to its base. Free faces involving `x_j` do not occur in the other cone, which contains no `x_j`, and likewise for `y_j`. Consequently these relative collapses leave precisely `L_x union L_y = B(i,j-1)`. Iteration gives the claimed actual collapse `K collapses E`. This verifies the strongest KN statement directly from its shelling lemma, rather than using bare homotopy equivalence.

## 2. Common boundary and outward collar

Each ambient ridge of `C_d^*` lies in exactly two facets. It belongs to the boundary of K precisely when one ambient incident facet lies in K and the other in K'. Thus the boundary ridges of the two manifolds agree; their generated boundary complexes agree, and `K intersect K' = partial K = partial K'`. The intersection equality can be checked without an embedding classification theorem. For any face sigma in both complexes, the facet-adjacency graph of its ambient link is the connected hypercube on the remaining coordinate pairs. A path from a K-facet to a K'-facet crosses a ridge containing sigma with one facet in each complex; that ridge is a boundary ridge in both, so sigma is in both boundary complexes. Conversely a boundary ridge and all its faces lie in both complexes. No global unknottedness of the boundary is presumed.

The PL collar theorem (Rourke–Sanderson Ch.2, Theorem 2.25, p.24) supplies a collar `c:partial K' x [0,2] -> K'` with `c(z,0)=z`. Restrict to `[0,1]` and set

`N = K union c(partial K x [0,1])`.

This is a compact PL manifold with boundary and is PL homeomorphic to K: combine an inward collar of `partial K` in K with this outward collar, then linearly compress the combined collar interval, extending by identity away from the collar. Also N collapses K: triangulate the collar as a product and collapse its interval factor to zero relative to its base. Gluing K does not spoil the free pairs, since it meets the collar only at that base. Therefore `N collapses K collapses E`.

Crucially, N is now an actual neighborhood of E in the ambient sphere M. This fixes the flaw in a naive application with E meeting the boundary of K. The collar is a neighborhood of `partial K` from the K' side; from the K side all points are already included. Hence every point of K, and therefore of E, is in the ambient interior of N. This uses both complementary PL manifolds, not an unproved assertion that the original collapse spine is interior.

## 3. Apply the exact classical criterion

Rourke–Sanderson Corollary 3.30 (printed p.41; DjVu page49, visually inspected) says a neighborhood N of X in `int M` is a regular neighborhood iff it is a compact manifold with boundary and collapses X. Here ambient M has no boundary, E is compact, and every hypothesis has just been checked. Thus N is a regular neighborhood of E in M.

By the regular neighborhood uniqueness theorem, Rourke–Sanderson Theorem 3.8 (p.33) or stronger 3.24 (pp.38–39), N is PL homeomorphic, in fact ambiently equivalent fixing E, to any standard regular neighborhood `N_0` of the same coordinate equator E in M.

## 4. Standard neighborhood is the trivial product

Write `q=d-i-1 >= 1`, `F=|C_q^*|`, so `M=E * F`. Define the simplicial function f on M that is zero on the E vertices and one on F vertices. The epsilon-neighborhood `N_0=f^{-1}([0,1/2])` is a regular neighborhood of E by the definitions and epsilon-neighborhood construction of Rourke–Sanderson Ch.3 pp.32–33.

It is PL homeomorphic to `E x cone(F)`. This does not require a bilinear join-coordinate map to be PL. Check its polyhedral cells instead. For every nonempty simplex `sigma` of E and simplex `tau` of F, the truncated join cell `f^{-1}([0,1/2]) intersect |sigma*tau|` has the face lattice of the convex product `|sigma| x cone(|tau|)`: its vertices consist of E-vertices and midpoints of E/F edges, matching respectively `(vertex sigma, cone apex)` and `(vertex sigma, vertex tau)`. Faces are truncations of join faces together with the truncation face, matching the product faces. These correspondences agree on common faces. Taking barycentric subdivisions of both cell complexes therefore yields a simplicial isomorphism and a PL homeomorphism. (The empty F face gives the base cell sigma.) Since `cone(F)` is the standard PL q-ball,

`N_0 is PL-homeomorphic to S^i x D^q`.

Finally `K is PL-homeomorphic to N is PL-homeomorphic to N_0`, which is the complete universal product conclusion for the original question.
