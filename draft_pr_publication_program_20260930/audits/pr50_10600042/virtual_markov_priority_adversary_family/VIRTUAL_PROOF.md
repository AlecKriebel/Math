# Independent virtual parity-lift argument

This argument was formed from the submitted target and candidate before any historical or other new review conclusion was read. It imports the exact virtual Alexander/Markov theorem from Kamada, math/0008092v1, Proposition3.1/Theorem3.2 and §3. The old PR49 mathematics is unrelated; only its capture operator was reused. The present argument does not claim to reprove Kamada's theorem.

Let VB_m be the virtual braid group with classical letters sigma_i^{±1}, virtual involutions v_i and the ordinary oriented virtual closure. Its defining presentation has inverse cancellation, classical braid/far relations, symmetric-group relations for v, and the far/mixed detour relations. It has no welded forbidden relation. The right inclusion adds an unused right strand; the left inclusion s increases every generator index by1. Kamada's ordinary closure equivalence on tagged braids is generated by those relations, conjugation, right stabilization by sigma_m^{±1} or v_m, and both exchanges

 a sigma_{m-1}^{-1} b sigma_{m-1} ↔ a v_{m-1} b v_{m-1},
 s(a) sigma_1^{-1} s(b) sigma_1 ↔ s(a) v_1 s(b) v_1,

at total strand count m, where the unshifted blocks a,b use indices at most m-2. This reindexes Kamada's §3 formulas whose block braid count is one less than the total count.

Define P(m,w)=(m,w) if m is even and P(m,w)=(m+1,w sigma_m) if m is odd. The positive crossing is an actual right stabilization, so P preserves the oriented ordinary closure. A trivial right strand would instead add a split component. Even input vertices are fixed exactly, including the tag.

For every unrestricted edge, the following universal computation gives an explicit replacement with even endpoints. N denotes its even padded level. The names refer to the submitted formulas, without changing them.

| Edge at unrestricted total count m | m even | m odd |
|---|---|---|
| Relation in arbitrary word context | Same relation on N=m | Same relation in old word with sigma_m retained on N=m+1 |
| Conjugation by a | C, blocks supported≤N-1 | BC, old blocks≤N-2 and final sigma_{N-1} retained |
| Right stabilization from m | D from N=m to N+2, appended g_N sigma_{N+1} | T on N=m+1: b sigma_{N-1} ↔ b g_{N-1} |
| Right exchange at count m | R, old blocks≤N-2 | BR, old blocks≤N-3, exchange index N-2 and final sigma_{N-1} |
| Left exchange at count m | L, unshifted blocks≤N-2 | BL, unshifted blocks≤N-3, shifted blocks≤N-2 and final sigma_{N-1} |

All sign and generator choices are covered, including virtual stabilization. Reverse arrows cover destabilization. The conjugation convention a b a^{-1} agrees with Kamada's a^{-1} b a after replacing a by its inverse. The left shift is applied to both blocks and never to the separate terminal padding crossing. At odd count m, that crossing has index m=N-1; every old relation has indices≤m-1 and remains a defining relation after right inclusion. Context words and their inverses retain the same support. No natural inclusion of a word is mistaken for a closure-preserving operation.

Each even scheme is also sound, independently of completeness. C is conjugation. BC and T compare right stabilizations of conjugate or identical old braids. D compares two consecutive right stabilizations, with the second chosen positive. R/L are the two imported exchange moves; BR/BL first compare an exchange at the old level, then positively stabilize both ends. It is legitimate to use those odd auxiliary objects to prove soundness: the independently displayed replacement relation contains only even tagged objects and an explicit syntactic word test. It is not defined by existentially asking for an unspecified odd Markov path.

Given two even tagged virtual braids with equivalent oriented closures, Kamada supplies a finite unrestricted chain. Applying P to its vertices converts every edge by the table. The resulting even chain connects the original endpoints, and repeated vertices may be deleted. Conversely each even edge preserves closure, so such a chain implies equivalence. Kamada's virtual Alexander proposition followed by P represents every nonempty oriented virtual link. The proof works for disconnected links and arbitrary virtual crossings, not only knots or classical links.

At m=1 the empty braid pads to (2,sigma_1), the unknot. At total count2 the exchange blocks are empty and R/L are identities. Odd exchange first becomes possible at count3, giving buffered schemes at N=4; this explains the stated minimum. Positive-strand ordinary closures are nonempty; an isolated zero-strand empty-link state can be adjoined separately. The certificate-height bound is 2 ceil(M/2) for an unrestricted chain of maximum count M; it says nothing about finding that chain or solving the braid/link decision problem.

No step requires a finite block-length bound, a minimal list, a uniformly bounded geometric disk of support, or an odd state in the displayed even move category. The primary Problem42 states no such extra condition. The strongest established claim is a finite list of algebraic schemes presenting ordinary oriented virtual closure equivalence using only even-strand states. The corresponding classical assertion follows by omitting v and exchanges from the standard classical Markov input. These are consequences of prior theorems, with the parity conversion explicitly proved here; this logical fact alone says nothing about whether an identical even-only formulation was previously published.

# Adversarial support witnesses

The support bounds are mathematically substantive. If T at N=2 permitted b=sigma_1, it would relate sigma_1² (Hopf link, linking number1) to the empty two-strand braid (unlink, linking number0). The real T only allows the empty b at N=2, so excludes this.

If R at N=2 permitted a=b=sigma_1, its left side reduces to sigma_1², while its right side is sigma_1 v_1 sigma_1 v_1. Both have two closure components. For oriented virtual links, the signed counts of crossings where component i passes over distinct component j are invariant under virtual Reidemeister moves, up to simultaneous permutation of component labels: RII adds cancelling signs, RIII preserves each pair's signed crossings, and virtual moves/detours change no classical data. The two words give ordered matrices [[0,1],[1,0]] and [[0,2],[0,0]], which are different even after relabeling. Permutation-cycle count alone misses this defect.

The same forbidden enlargement of BR at N=4 with a=b=sigma_2 gives sigma_2² sigma_3 versus sigma_2 v_2 sigma_2 v_2 sigma_3. Their closures have three components: a split first component and the same differing ordered crossing data on the other two. Real BR only permits block indices≤1 and excludes these words. The independent finite search found no analogous BL invariant witness in its compact search set; this null result neither proves a larger support bound sound nor changes the valid universal BL argument.
