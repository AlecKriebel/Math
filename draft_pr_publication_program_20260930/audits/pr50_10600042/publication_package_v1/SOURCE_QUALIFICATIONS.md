# Scope, source and priority qualifications

The theorem concerns ordinary closures of oriented, unframed, nonempty classical and virtual links. It gives four classical / eight virtual reversible algebraic scheme families, apart from defining braid relations. Word blocks may have arbitrary finite length. Syntactic support bounds, explicit even-strand tags, both classical signs, virtual stabilization, both virtual exchanges and the left shift/buffered tail are essential. No welded forbidden relation is imposed. The empty link may be added as an isolated zero-strand state. Plat, framing and transverse classifications, minimality and uniform geometric locality are not asserted.

The paper proves each scheme's closure preservation and the universal edge conversion by positive padding. It imports the established representation and unrestricted Markov results below. The height formula `2 ceil(M/2)` applies to a supplied certificate, not to a search algorithm. The finite code diagnostics are supplementary evidence and countercontrols, not a proof of the imported theorems or formal certification.

## Double moves and historical qualification

Fiedler reports that conjugation and double Markov moves alone do not replace ordinary Markov equivalence. Our D followed by T realizes all four classical double-sign choices, but the proof also allows BC and T. It does **not** claim that those extra moves are generated by conjugation and double moves, or that it refutes Fiedler.

A bounded primary-source priority review did not verify an exact earlier answer to this formulation. That result is neither an exhaustive absence proof nor a first-discovery certificate. The note answers the **dated** ordinary-closure interpretation of the source question and does not assert that it remains open today. Elementary derivability alone does not decide historical priority.
