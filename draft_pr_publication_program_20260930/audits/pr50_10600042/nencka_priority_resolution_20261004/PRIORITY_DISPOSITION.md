## What the 1996 pages actually establish

The first page recalls ordinary Markov equivalence through same-link closure. It then introduces a new representation of braids by interwoved strings. The second page announces finite powers-of-two induction and a continuum-sized braid group, then states a generalized Markov equivalence generated by conjugacy and signed tails from B_(2^n) to B_(2^(n+1)). It does not explicitly identify this generalized equivalence with ordinary oriented unframed closure equivalence, specify a complete closure construction for the new representation, or prove the announced results. This leaves model identification and completeness unverified; orientation conventions are also not separately specified.

The printed tail has a plus/minus superscript on EVERY factor, from sigma_(2^n) through sigma_(2^(n+1)-1). A positive-only obstruction is inapplicable. The source does not disambiguate common versus independently chosen signs; the comparison below grants independent choices, the stronger reading.

**Transcription correction:** Theorem 3 calls f_i a natural induction map. It does not explicitly call it a zeroth induction map. The B1/n=0 issue comes from the unspecified convention for the natural numbers, not an explicit zeroth-map statement. The earlier review sentence attributing a zeroth-map statement to Theorem 3 should be corrected if reused.

## Careful comparison with the present even calculus

1. Powers of two are not a valid dismissal. For n>=1 they are even. Any finite classical braid can be positively stabilized to a sufficiently large power-of-two count. Consequently, a valid ordinary-closure completeness theorem on these counts alone could answer the literal classical even-strand question, even without covering every even count directly.
2. On the ordinary Artin interpretation, the long tail has 2^n consecutive new-generator factors. For n>=1, divide them into 2^(n-1) consecutive pairs. Every pair is one of the four signed double stabilizations; intermediate counts are even. Thus Nencka's displayed move set on n>=1 is a subcalculus of conjugacy plus all four signed double moves. This is a finite algebraic comparison, not an assertion that the undefined generalized relation equals ordinary closure.
3. Fiedler's first-page preview reports a negative answer even for the unknot and displays examples in B4, a power-of-two count. This makes the comparison substantially more pertinent than a generic abstract alone. But the full proof and precise separated endpoints were not checked here, so this audit does not promote a conditional comparison into an independently verified disproof of Nencka's theorem. In particular, pairwise nonconjugacy of displayed examples alone does not prove separation under double moves.
4. If n=0 is permitted, B1 participates and the B1-to-B2 signed stabilization introduces an odd allowed state. Such a chain would fail the strict only-even-states condition unless every B1 passage were eliminated. Its possible role cannot be silently removed when invoking an obstruction that assumes fixed even parity. On ordinary B1 there is only the empty braid, and its two signed B2 images have unknot closure; this observation does not establish that all such passages can be eliminated by the n>=1 subcalculus.
5. The present theorem's (BC) and (T) moves are additional schemes; it does not claim they are generated by conjugacy/double moves. The displayed Nencka announcement supplies neither these explicit buffered moves nor a virtual braid/exchange calculus. That supports a concrete difference between displayed formulations, not a global novelty conclusion or a claim that classical prior work is irrelevant to the literal 2014 item.

The identified 1996 preprint, 1998 article and 1999 chapter are concrete routes for that check. A full Fiedler proof would sharpen the conditional double-move comparison, but by itself would not identify an undefined generalized representation or resolve the n=0 convention.
