## Directly relevant prior obstruction

T. Fiedler's *Markov Moves Cannot be Replaced by Double Markov Moves*, Journal of Knot Theory and Its Ramifications 12(4) (June 2003),575–577, is essential prior context. Publisher-deposited Crossref metadata authenticates the title, date and negative abstract. The publisher-supplied first-page preview defines all four sign choices of a double stabilization and discusses fixed strand parity, including failure for the unknot. Direct official full-text access failed; this audit has not independently certified the full counterexample proof. Its scope is the smaller system of conjugation and double Markov moves. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216503002561), [publisher-deposited metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1142/S0218216503002561), [identified publisher preview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268868228_Markov_Moves_Cannot_be_Replaced_by_Double_Markov_Moves).

The following comparison is a fresh deduction from the candidate's displayed schemes, not an attribution to Fiedler. For an even N and classical b on N strands, D with g=σ_N^ε gives

\[
(N,b)\longleftrightarrow(N+2,b\sigma_N^\varepsilon\sigma_{N+1}).
\]

T at N+2, with prefix bσ_N^ε, can replace the final crossing by σ_{N+1}^δ. Its support bound is valid: that prefix uses indices at most N. Thus D followed by T realizes each double stabilization with ε,δ in {+1,−1}. This does **not** show that T or BC is generated by conjugation and double stabilization alone. The candidate contains additional moves; the published narrower negative result neither contradicts its claimed theorem nor supplies an earlier complete positive answer.

Required addition before a note is promoted: cite Fiedler and explicitly explain this difference. Avoid a description suggesting that conjugation plus double stabilization suffices. The original candidate already avoids a historical-discovery claim; keep that restraint.
