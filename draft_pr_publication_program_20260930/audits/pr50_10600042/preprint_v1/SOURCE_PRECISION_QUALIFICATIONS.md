**P1 — Fiedler and double Markov moves.** Thomas Fiedler, “Markov moves cannot be replaced by double Markov moves,” *Journal of Knot Theory and Its Ramifications* 12 (2003), no. 4, 575–577, DOI [10.1142/S0218216503002561](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216503002561), reports a negative result for the smaller conjugation-and-double-move system. In this note D followed by T produces `b sigma_N^epsilon sigma_(N+1)^delta` for every pair of signs. The proof also uses BC and T. It does not claim those extra moves are generated by conjugation and double moves, so it does not claim to refute Fiedler. The comparison is bounded by authenticated publisher metadata/abstract and the identified publisher first-page preview. Neither the priority family nor this author preparation independently certifies the full counterexample proof. Direct DOI/Crossref page retrieval during authoring failed; this does not broaden the verified access scope.

**Target and interpretation.** The affirmative result concerns ordinary closures of oriented, unframed, nonempty classical and virtual links. Both versions are stated because the source item's braid terminology is unqualified. Four classical / eight virtual reversible scheme families are explicit, apart from defining relations. Arbitrary finite word blocks and syntactic support restrictions are part of the statement. No minimum scheme count, uniformly bounded geometric support, plat or transverse result, framing preservation, or welded equivalence is asserted. Odd-strand braids are auxiliary proof objects only; displayed states and endpoints are even. An optional zero-strand empty-link state is isolated.

**Imported versus proved statements.** Alexander representation and unrestricted classical/virtual Markov equivalence are imported with exact source pointers. The note proves soundness of each displayed pattern, the universal positive-padding conversion of every generating edge, and the height bound for a supplied certificate. Finite Python diagnostics are supplementary; they are not proofs of the imported theorems or a search/decision procedure. Every even braid endpoint is fixed exactly by padding. All signs, virtual stabilization, both exchanges, whole relation contexts and low-strand cases are included.
