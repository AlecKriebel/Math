# Independent reconstruction and falsification of the final claim

The claim under review is an equivalence of ordinary oriented unframed closures of explicitly tagged braid words. At every allowed vertex, the strand count is an even positive integer at least two. Four classical and eight virtual algebraic scheme families, plus the appropriate defining relations, generate exactly closure equivalence. Blocks have arbitrary finite length; their restrictions concern the written indices. This is an affirmative answer to the ordinary-closure reading of the dated Problem 42, without a present-openness or priority assertion.

## Universal soundness

Let `E_N` denote the word states on even `N`. All soundness arguments can use odd auxiliary braids; these are proof objects rather than vertices of the resulting equivalence calculus. For C, closure is invariant under conjugating by any braid on `N`. For BC, the two prefixes are words on `N-1`: every index in both the prefix and conjugator is at most `N-2`. Their ordinary closures agree by conjugation, and their positive stabilizations are the two displayed endpoints on `N`. For T, the same prefix on `N-1` is stabilized in either of the permitted ways: positive versus negative classical crossing, or versus virtual crossing in the virtual case. Neither crossing change inside an arbitrary `N`-strand braid nor equality of two group words is being asserted. For D, make a permitted right stabilization from `N` to `N+1`, then a positive right stabilization to `N+2`. The old block remains on the first `N` strands.

For R and L, the source is Kamada's virtual exchange, on total count `N`, with blocks on `N-1` before the right or left inclusion. For the left inclusion, each block index is increased by one. For BR and BL, carry out the same exchange at total count `N-1`, then positively stabilize to `N`. Thus unshifted indices must be at most `N-3`; shifted BL indices are at most `N-2`. Its final crossing is `sigma_(N-1)` outside both shifted blocks. It is not included in their shift. Kamada's crossings are negative then positive on the classical side, and two virtual crossings on the other side. No forbidden welded relation has been used. Each argument is reversible because the original equivalences and stabilizations are allowed in both directions. Soundness uses only the appropriate imported unrestricted operations, not the completeness of the proposed even calculus.

## Universal completeness

Start from an unrestricted finite Markov certificate between the two even endpoints, guaranteed by the classical theorem or Kamada's virtual theorem. It may be regarded as a chain of actual words: every group equality required between representatives is expressed by finitely many defining relations in contexts. Define positive padding on each vertex by keeping an even vertex unchanged and replacing an odd vertex `(m,w)` by `(m+1,w sigma_m)`. This preserves its ordinary oriented closure and fixes the original two endpoints exactly.

For each unrestricted generating edge, verify an allowed edge between its two padded vertices. A defining relation retains exactly its old indices and entire old surrounding word, and, at odd count, the same appended positive tail on both sides. Right inclusion into the next braid group preserves the defining relations, including all the virtual mixed relations. No decision of the word problem or membership in an included subgroup is needed.

Conjugation at even count gives C; at odd count it gives BC, with old indices at most `m-1=N-2`. A stabilization `b -> b g_m` at even `m` pads its odd target and becomes D: `(m,b) -> (m+2,b g_m sigma_(m+1))`. At odd `m`, its even target is fixed and the source is padded, giving T: `(m+1,b sigma_m) -> (m+1,b g_m)`. This handles both classical signs and virtual stabilization. Reversal handles all destabilizations; the proof does not silently require a positive-only Markov certificate.

A right exchange at even total count is R; at odd total count it is BR with `N=m+1`, exchange crossing `m-1=N-2`, block indices at most `m-2=N-3`, and appended `sigma_m=sigma_(N-1)`. Likewise a left exchange gives L or BL. Its original left inclusion shifts only the old blocks, which have original indices at most `m-2`, to indices at most `m-1=N-2`. The padding crossing is added afterwards and is independent of this shift. This enumerates every type of edge in the cited unrestricted virtual theorem. The classical theorem requires no virtual edge.

The padded chain is therefore a finite chain in the displayed even calculus with exactly the same two endpoints. Conversely, universal soundness shows its closure cannot change. Alexander representation followed by padding provides even representatives for every nonempty link in the corresponding category. There is no circular inference from the finite code tests to either the unrestricted theorem or the new universal edge conversion.

## Boundary and limiting cases

At one strand the only word is empty; padding yields `(2,sigma_1)`, the ordinary unknot, rather than an idle second strand, which would add a component. At `N=2`, BC's blocks and R/L's unshifted blocks are empty. Both sides of a genuine exchange reduce to the identity; the allowed T relates the two one-crossing unknot representatives and, in the virtual case, the virtual-stabilized unknot. The first odd total exchange count is three, which pads to `N=4`; BR/BL are not applicable at `N=2`. At `N=4` their blocks can involve index one and their left-shifted blocks index two, with tail index three. Empty blocks throughout are permitted and cause no invalid generator. Even tags cannot be erased: an empty four-strand word and an empty two-strand word have four- and two-component closures.

The function `h(m)=m+(m mod 2)=2 ceil(m/2)` is nondecreasing on positive integers. Every lifted edge has the two padded endpoints just described; it needs no further intermediate state of a greater even count. Applying this vertexwise to a supplied certificate with maximum count `M` proves its height bound. This supplies no bound or procedure for finding the initial certificate. The optional zero-strand empty state must be isolated because no positive-strand ordinary closure is empty. The theorem is neither a plat closure classification nor a framed, transverse, welded or geometrically uniformly local theorem.

## Countercontrols and independent finite evidence

The true T support restriction cannot be dropped. At two strands a forbidden prefix `sigma_1^2` would relate `sigma_1^3` to `sigma_1` using its negative endpoint and inverse cancellation. Their closed Fox 3-coloring counts are nine and three. The coloring rule is the invertible dihedral-quandle rule, whose Reidemeister invariance follows from idempotence, involutivity and right self-distributivity. An additional fresh example uses the forbidden prefix `sigma_1^4`: the T endpoints reduce to `sigma_1^5` and `sigma_1^3`, with Fox 5 counts twenty-five and five. These are genuine falsifications of the enlarged scheme, not tests that the actual scheme fails.

For a forbidden R extension at two strands with both blocks `sigma_1`, cancellation gives `sigma_1^2` versus `sigma_1 v_1 sigma_1 v_1`. Both close to two components, so component count alone misses the error. Counting positive-minus-negative classical crossings from over-component to under-component gives the matrices `(0,1;1,0)` and `(0,2;0,0)`, inequivalent under component relabeling. In the oriented virtual category these counts are preserved: RI is self-component, classical RII contributes cancelling pairs of the same order, classical RIII retains each component order/sign, and detours create only virtual crossings. The analogous forbidden BR enlargement at four strands gives the same obstruction together with an isolated zero row/column. This also checks that the retained buffered tail does not cure wrong block support.

The new implementation uses integer words, union-find for closure components, and matrix rank over F3/F5/F7 for coloring counts; it imports neither author nor previous reviewer code. The rank procedure is independently compared with exhaustive color assignments at small counts. It checks 2,320 legal scheme instances across all four classical/eight virtual families, 1,057 literal lifts of unrestricted edges at both parities, and 861 defining-relation contexts including inverses. All 19,485 assertions passed. At more than five components its ordered-crossing signature is deliberately a weaker necessary invariant, not an isomorphism test. These computations are supplementary falsification attempts and do not replace the universal proof above or certify all link invariants/word equivalences.

## Strongest verified result and exact remaining gap

Within the stated ordinary oriented unframed classification, the edge conversion is universal and no unresolved mathematical step remains after importing the explicitly cited unrestricted representation/Markov theorems. Their complete proofs have not been newly formally certified by this reviewer. The finite evidence cannot establish historical priority or current openness. Fiedler's full negative counterexample proof remains outside the accessed preview and is not needed to prove the positive calculus. No stronger locality, minimality, decision or search-complexity theorem follows. None of those exclusions is falsely asserted by the final package.
