# Independent classical theorem and adversarial support checks

Fix ordinary oriented, unframed braid closure. Let a state be an even integer N>=2 and an Artin word w in sigma_1,...,sigma_(N-1) and their inverses. The strand tag is part of the state. Use ordinary Artin relations, including inverse cancellation, at any even level. Define four reversible schemes:

* C: b <-> a b a^-1 in B_N, with a,b using indices<=N-1.
* BC: b sigma_(N-1) <-> a b a^-1 sigma_(N-1) in B_N, with a,b using indices<=N-2.
* T: b sigma_(N-1) <-> b sigma_(N-1)^e in B_N, e in{+1,-1}, with b using indices<=N-2.
* D: (N,b) <-> (N+2,b sigma_N^e sigma_(N+1)), e in{+1,-1}, with b using indices<=N-1.

Then every nonempty oriented classical link is represented by an even state, and two even states have isotopic oriented ordinary closures exactly when they are connected by these schemes and relations at even levels.

**Imported input.** Classical Alexander representation and the ordinary unframed Markov theorem, with arbitrary conjugation and both signs of right stabilization. I verify the corollary below, not the foundational topological proof of those theorems. Embedding an Artin word on fewer strands into a larger group respects the same defining relations, so no hidden group-equivalence assumption enters the padding construction.

**Soundness.** C preserves closure by conjugation. For BC the two lower words b and a b a^-1 on N-1 strands have the same oriented closure; positively stabilizing each yields its displayed N-strand endpoint. T is the comparison of positive and signed right stabilizations of the same lower word. D is a signed stabilization followed by a positive one. The lower words and odd strand counts in this explanation are auxiliary objects used to prove closure preservation, not move states. The replacements themselves are explicit even-endpoint word templates with syntactic support constraints. Hence every legal chain preserves ordinary closure.

**Completeness.** For an unrestricted state x=(m,b) put P(x)=x for even m, and P(x)=(m+1,b sigma_m) for odd m. The closure agrees with x. It is well defined on braid-group equivalence classes: a relation in b remains the same relation in the larger group before the appended letter.

Lift any finite unrestricted Markov certificate between even endpoints by P. A conjugation at even m lifts to C; at odd m it lifts to BC at N=m+1. The conjugating and conjugated words in the latter case use only indices<=m-1=N-2. A stabilization (m,b)<->(m+1,b sigma_m^e) at even m lifts to D; at odd m it lifts to T at N=m+1. Stabilization inverses lift by reversing the arrows. At even m an Artin relation is retained unchanged; at odd m it is retained before the terminal positive crossing. Equal group elements represented by different words may require a finite relation chain; all such rewrites occur at the same even level. Remove repeated vertices. P fixes the original even endpoints exactly. Thus the resulting chain lies entirely among even states and connects those endpoints. Alexander followed by P once proves representation.

**Quantitative and boundary scope.** For a given certificate of maximum strand count M, the lifted certificate has maximum2 ceil(M/2), with no extra intermediate strand counts. This follows from the monotonic map m->m+(m mod2), and is attained when the maximum M occurs. Each explicit conjugation or stabilization generator lifts to one template or an identity. This does not bound the effort or length needed to find a certificate, or the number of relation rewrites hidden in a group-element certificate. For m=1 the empty word lifts to sigma_1 on two strands, representing the unknot. At N=2 the BC blocks are empty; T compares the two signed unknot representatives. The empty words on two and four strands represent different unlink component counts and are distinct tagged states. The empty link, if desired, is an isolated zero-strand state. No framed/transverse or plat-closure theorem follows from this statement.

**Non-cosmetic support hypotheses.** Dropping T's support permits b=sigma_1^2 at N=2, giving sigma_1^3<->sigma_1 after choosing e=-1. Both closures have one component, but their Fox3 coloring counts are9 and3. Thus the component diagnostic used by the author alone cannot detect this false move. For BC at N=4, retaining a=sigma_2 but wrongly permitting b=sigma_3 changes component counts4 to2. Conversely, retaining legal b=sigma_2 but wrongly permitting a=sigma_2 sigma_3 changes counts2 to4. The independent control result records explicit words. Omitting D's final crossing leaves an extra separate component; padding by an idle strand likewise changes closure.

**Why the coloring witness is rigorous.** A positive crossing acts on adjacent Fox colors by R(x,y)=(y,2y-x) over F_3, and a negative crossing by R^-1(x,y)=(2x-y,x). The rule is invertible and satisfies the braid/Yang–Baxter relation; a Reidemeister-I arc has uniquely extended color. These local identities give a bijection of colorings under ordinary Reidemeister moves. Equivalently on a braid, closed colorings are fixed vectors of its linear color action; conjugation preserves their number, and each signed stabilization admits exactly one extra color equal to the terminal original color. The computed count is3^(N-rank(M-I)) over F_3. For the two-strand positive generator R has order3 over this field: R^3 is identity, whereas R fixes only x=y. The respective counts9 and3 therefore distinguish the counterexample closures without a knot-equivalence oracle. The invariant is incomplete and is not used to prove the main theorem's completeness.
