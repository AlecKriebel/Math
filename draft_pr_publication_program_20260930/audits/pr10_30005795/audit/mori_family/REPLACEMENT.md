# Recommended Section 3 replacement (expository repair only)

This replacement preserves the candidate's finite-support route and signed-weight safeguard. Its main change is to make endpoint Q-linear effectivity explicit. Assume throughout that S is an integral prime surface, as in the report's variety convention.

## 3. Why the finiteness assumptions fit the Fano setup

A smooth complex Fano variety is a Mori dream space by Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan, Corollary 1.3.2. Its effective cone is covered by finitely many closed rational polyhedral Mori chambers. On each chamber, the fixed divisorial negative part is supported on the exceptional prime divisors of the associated contraction and its coefficients are linear; see Okawa, *On images of Mori dream spaces*, Proposition 2.8 and §2.3. Taking strict transforms on X gives a finite union of negative prime divisors. Along the compact segment \(-K_X-uS\), the coefficient functions are bounded and piecewise linear, and the intersection weight is bounded and piecewise polynomial. Thus every \(a_j\) above is finite. Since X is smooth and, as shown below, none of the negative primes \(E_j\) equals S, their restrictions \(D_j=E_j|_S\) are effective Cartier divisors on S.

For completeness, S is not a fixed divisorial component along this segment. On a smooth Fano variety \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\cong N^1(X)_{\mathbb Q}\). The Mori dream space effective cone is closed rational polyhedral and generated by classes of effective rational divisors. Consequently \(\tau\) is rational and \(D_\tau=-K_X-\tau S\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly equivalent to an effective \(\mathbb Q\)-divisor G. If G contained \(bS\) with \(b>0\), then \(G-bS\geq0\) would represent \(-K_X-(\tau+b)S\), contradicting the definition of \(\tau\). Choose an effective \(\mathbb Q\)-divisor \(A\sim_{\mathbb Q}-K_X\) avoiding S, using a sufficiently divisible basepoint-free multiple of \(-K_X\). For \(0\leq u\leq\tau\), the convex combination \((1-u/\tau)A+(u/\tau)G\) is an effective representative of \(-K_X-uS\) avoiding S. Hence the fixed divisorial coefficient along S is zero for rational u, and therefore for all real u by the chamberwise linear extension.

This discussion uses the usual Mori-dream-space/divisorial interpretation of the negative part, rather than asserting that all positive parts are nef on the original threefold. In the setting explicitly used in the report, \(P(u)|_S\) is nef, which is enough for \(w\geq0\). Even if one instead works with a chamberwise divisor on the original model and its intersection weight has either sign, replacing w by \(\max(w,0)\) in \(a_j\) gives a finite upper bound for the original integral, since all valuation orders of the restricted negative part are nonnegative.

## Evidence supporting this replacement

- [BCHM, published Corollary 1.3.2](https://www.ams.org/journals/jams/2010-23-02/S0894-0347-09-00649-3/S0894-0347-09-00649-3.pdf): Q-factorial dlt log Fano varieties are Mori dream spaces. The same result is Corollary 1.3.1 in [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610203), printed p. 9; do not mix the published/preprint numbering.
- [Okawa, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.1326v2), Lemma 2.7, Proposition 2.8, Remark 2.12 and Proposition 2.13: finite closed rational polyhedral chambers; exceptional cone generated simplicially by actual primes; negative part equals normalized fixed part for sufficiently divisible multiples; chamberwise Q-linear P/N maps. Section 2.1 explicitly notes that taking closures of effective/movable cones is unnecessary on Mori dream spaces.
- The rational endpoint is not a separate nonvanishing hypothesis: a rational point of the finite Cox degree cone is a nonnegative rational combination of nonzero homogeneous section degrees. Clearing denominators and multiplying the sections produces an effective representative of a positive multiple. `Pic_Q=N^1_Q` follows from the Fano vanishing of `H^1(O_X)` and the torsion of numerically trivial classes modulo `Pic^0`.
- [Official OWR report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/48650), printed pp. 836 and 839: the setup is a smooth Fano threefold and Du Val surface; the report explicitly uses nef \(P(u)|_S\).
- The original-model sign safeguard follows pointwise from `w<=max(w,0)` and `ord_F(N|S)>=0`; bounded finite pieces make both integrals finite. No auxiliary nef theorem is required for this acceptance route.
