## Exact hypotheses and proposed implications

Let X be a smooth projective complex Fano threefold, let S be a nonzero integral prime divisor (normality is immaterial to these two claims), put A = -K_X and D_u = A - uS, and define tau = sup{u in R : [D_u] belongs to the pseudoeffective cone}. Since A is ample, this is also the supremum with u restricted to be nonnegative. This review tests:

1. tau is positive, finite, and rational, D_tau has an effective rationally linearly equivalent Q-divisor, and S has zero coefficient in the divisorial fixed part of D_u for 0 <= u <= tau.
2. Every movable Q-divisor on X is nef; hence the positive part P_sigma(D_u) is nef on X, with no need to transport it to a genuinely different small Q-factorial model.

Success requires complete arguments at u = 0 and u = tau, an explicit bridge from numerical to rational linear equivalence, and an explanation of why absence of small contractions implies movability forces nefness. A failure outside these exact hypotheses is recorded as a boundary check, not as a counterexample to them.

## Threshold and endpoint: attempted failures and surviving proof

The finite-Cox premise is justified in this setting. Hu--Keel, *Mori Dream Spaces and GIT*, Corollary 2.16 gives the log Fano threefold case; Proposition 2.9 relates finite Cox generation to the Mori dream property, and Proposition 1.11(2) supplies finitely many effective generators for the effective cone. Source: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0004017>.

The implication does need Pic(X)_Q = N^1(X)_Q, rather than polyhedrality alone. For smooth Fano X, Kodaira vanishing gives H^1(X,O_X) = 0 (apply it to K_X + (-K_X)); thus Pic^0(X) = 0. The kernel of numerical equivalence on the Neron--Severi group is torsion, so rational numerical and rational linear equivalence agree. This explains why a rational class in the effective cone has a Q-linearly equivalent effective Q-divisor here. On a general variety with nontrivial Pic^0, the bare numerical-cone argument would leave a real gap.

Here is a checkable proof independent of any generic chamber assertion.

* Choose a Cartier ample H. A pseudoeffective class B satisfies B.H^2 >= 0 by continuity from effective divisors. Since S.H^2 > 0, every allowed u satisfies u <= A.H^2/(S.H^2). This proves finiteness; openness of the ample cone proves tau > 0.
* The pseudoeffective cone is the closed cone generated by finitely many rational effective classes. Intersecting its rational linear inequalities with the rational line A - uS shows that the finite endpoint tau is rational and belongs to the cone. The resulting rational feasible system has a rational nonnegative solution, giving [D_tau] = sum_i q_i[E_i] with q_i >= 0 rational and E_i effective Q-divisors. The Pic_Q = N^1_Q bridge gives E_tau := sum_i q_i E_i ~_Q D_tau.
* If any effective representative E_tau of D_tau had coefficient c > 0 along S, then E_tau - cS would be effective and numerically equivalent to D_(tau+c). This contradicts the definition of tau. In particular, the effective representative just constructed avoids S. This argument even excludes S from every effective R-divisor numerically equivalent to D_tau.
* Take E_0 ~_Q A effective and avoiding S, possible because some multiple of A is very ample. For every real 0 <= u <= tau, the effective R-divisor

  E_u = (1-u/tau)E_0 + (u/tau)E_tau

  satisfies E_u ~_R D_u and avoids S. For rational u it is a Q-divisor and the equivalence is rational linear. Hence S is not a stable base component for rational u, and sigma_S(D_u) = 0 for all real u in this interval, including the endpoint. At a non-big endpoint, add an arbitrarily small effective ample representative avoiding S in the definition of sigma; the coefficient is still zero.

This does not prove that N_sigma(D_u) = 0: other prime divisors can be fixed. It proves only the requested statement about S itself.

## Literal all-real-u formulation is false

If “for all u” includes negative u, there is an explicit counterexample. Let X = Bl_p(P^3), let H be the pullback of a hyperplane, and let S = E be the exceptional P^2. The graph of projection away from p embeds X into P^3 x P^2, and the restriction of O(1,1) is 2H-E. Thus 2H-E is ample, and the blowup canonical formula gives A = 4H-2E = 2(2H-E), so X satisfies the smooth Fano hypotheses.

For u < -2 put t = -u-2 > 0. Then D_u = 4H+tE. Every effective divisor in this class can be written cE + G, where G has no exceptional component and is the strict transform of an effective degree-four divisor downstairs. If its total multiplicity at p is m >= 0, its class is 4H-mE, and therefore c-m=t. Consequently c >= t. A degree-four divisor avoiding p supplies equality. The same calculation with effective R-combinations gives sigma_E(D_u)=t>0. In particular S is fixed for every u<-2. A degree-d hypersurface has multiplicity m <= d at p, so its strict-transform class is d(H-E)+(d-m)E; conversely E and H-E are effective. Thus the effective cone is generated by E and H-E, tau=2, and D_tau=4(H-E) is the pullback of a divisor on P^2. This also provides a non-big endpoint check for the surviving argument.

Repair: state the interval 0 <= u <= tau explicitly. For the conventional nonnegative threshold interval this boundary test causes no defect.

## Movable implies nef: direct proof and precise scope

The primary contraction statement has been independently verified in the original scan by the read-only source-checking subagent: Wiśniewski, *On contractions of extremal rays of Fano manifolds*, J. reine angew. Math. 417 (1991), 141--158, Theorem (1.1), p.143 (proof p.144). Its fiber-locus inequality is

  dim F + dim Locus(R) >= dim X + length(R) - 1,

where F is an irreducible component of a nontrivial fiber. The article also gives the one-dimensional-fiber exclusion for small contractions on p.145. Original-article locator: <https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/dms/resolveppn/?PPN=GDZPPN002208571>; DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1991.417.141>. The present browser could not reopen those two landing pages; the independent subagent inspected the original scan, not a secondary quotation.

For a smooth threefold a small contraction would have both dim Locus(R) <= 1 and dim F <= 1. The length is a positive integer for a K_X-negative ray, so the inequality would give 2 >= 3, a contradiction. Thus every K_X-negative extremal contraction is divisorial or of fiber type. Since A is ample, every extremal ray is K_X-negative.

If a movable Q-divisor M is not nef, the cone theorem supplies an extremal ray R with M.R < 0 and its contraction f_R. Choose a divisible positive m for which |mM| has no fixed prime component.

For any G in |mM| and any contracted integral curve C, G.C = m M.C < 0. An effective Cartier divisor has nonnegative intersection with a curve outside its support; hence every such C lies in Supp(G).

* If f_R is divisorial with exceptional prime divisor F, contracted curves cover a dense subset of F. Thus every G contains F, contradicting movability.
* If f_R is of fiber type, contracted curves pass through every point of a dense open subset of X. Thus every G would contain that dense open subset, impossible for a divisor.

Thus M is nef. By passage to closures, the closed movable cone is contained in the nef cone. Conversely a nef Q-divisor on Fano X is semiample by the basepoint-free theorem: clear denominators to a nef Cartier L, for which L-K_X is ample. Kawamata gives the more general klt, nef-and-big form in *On effective non-vanishing and base-point-freeness*, p.1, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003169>. Therefore the closed movable and nef cones agree. This includes rational classes on the boundary and real classes by continuity, so it applies to P_sigma.

For completeness, any projective SQM f:X --> Y pulls an ample Q-divisor on Y back to a movable, hence nef and semiample, divisor on X. Codimension-one isomorphism identifies the section rings; semiampleness therefore extends f to a morphism. A nontrivial birational small morphism would contract an extremal ray whose contraction is small, contradicting the same smooth-threefold result. Hence an SQM is a regular isomorphism. The conclusion allows regular automorphisms and does not claim that the birational map is the identity.

The key restrictions are smoothness, dimension three, and ampleness of -K_X. The argument must not be silently applied to a singular Fano threefold or to later singular models in a divisorial MMP. Those models may have flips. They are unnecessary here because movability forces nefness already on the original X.
