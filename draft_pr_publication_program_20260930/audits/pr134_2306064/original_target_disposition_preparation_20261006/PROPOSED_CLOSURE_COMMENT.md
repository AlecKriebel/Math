The all-real-alpha coefficient theorem in this PR is mathematically valid, including equality and its stated restricted sharpness. The issue is the claim to newly resolve the original open problem.

The literal Miller–Hayman6.64 question asks for a sufficient generalization of the n and n² coefficient tests for every real alpha. It imposes no continuity, polynomial-weight, or optimality requirement. A complete answer already follows from two verified1975 results: Silverman's sufficient absolute-coefficient starlike/convex tests (Theorem1 and corollary, p.110, DOI10.1090/S0002-9939-1975-0369678-0) and Mocanu–Reade's all-real starlike alpha-convexity radius R_alpha (p.397, DOI10.1090/S0002-9939-1975-0374404-5).

Use weights W_0(n)=n, W_1(n)=n², and W_alpha(n)=n R_alpha^{-(n-1)} otherwise. If their weighted sum is below1, h(w)=R_alpha f(w/R_alpha) is analytic and starlike on the unit disk by the coefficient condition itself. The radius theorem puts h in M_alpha on |w|<R_alpha; J_alpha[h](R_alpha z)=J_alpha[f](z) gives the desired membership on the whole unit disk. Both endpoints match the source's tests exactly.

This is an earlier mathematical consequence, not evidence that those authors expressly answered the named problem or established first named-answer priority. The PR's polynomial condition is not fully subsumed: a sufficiently small a_n=epsilon/n⁴ satisfies it but not the exponential condition. Its novelty as an alternative criterion remains unresolved. We therefore do not claim that every theorem in the PR was previously published or that no distinct improvement is possible.

The source-credit errors and optimization-sensitive independent checker were reasonably repaired and documented; those repairs preserve the valid proof but cannot restore the previously-unsolved-target publication framing. Under this program's requirement to publish a novel resolution of the original problem, the original literal target is classified already_solved as a verified consequence of older results, and this PR is closed without merging or publishing a preprint. The valid polynomial theorem and its unresolved alternative-novelty question are retained in the audit record.
