# PR91 bounded priority and source-scope note

Evidence cut-off: 2026-10-05. This note summarizes separate author/citation-family and general-operator source audits, plus selected further primary-source comparisons, for the matrix closed-ball theorem. It records a bounded comparison, not exhaustive literature coverage or historical firstness.

## Exact target and outcome

Let `A` be a contraction on `C^k`, `k >= 1`, in any complex norm, `U` its closed unit ball, and `K_A f = f composed with A` on all continuous complex functions `C(U)` in the supremum norm. Let `S` be the matrix eigenvalues on the unit circle and `Gamma = <S>` the multiplicative group with all integer exponents, with `<empty> = {1}`. The target is the exclusion

`point-spectrum(K_A) intersect unit-circle subset Gamma`.

Kari Valentina Küster's [2015 thesis](https://homepages.laas.fr/henrion/mfo16/kari-kuester.pdf), Theorem 3.1.14(i), printed p. 43 / PDF p. 52, gives the mixed inclusion in the other direction and the following paragraph expressly leaves its converse open. [*Pure Koopmanism*, 2016](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46612), Example 4 and Theorem 5(ii), printed p. 321 / PDF p. 25, give the same setting and inclusion. The note answers a question expressly left open in the 2015 thesis and stated only as an inclusion in the 2016 contribution. The bounded audit located no earlier explicit completion in its examined scope.

The key factorization `f = f composed with P` through the contractive peripheral matrix projection is a short checked specialization of the classical Jacobs–de Leeuw–Glicksberg mechanism. The general theorem is preexisting; an absent explicit mixed-ball table does not establish a new general theory or historical novelty. The elementary applicability steps are recurrence, `||P|| <= 1`, identification of composition by `P` with the reversible idempotent, and application of the prior all-peripheral calculation.

Prior results credited in the note are Küster 2015 Theorem 3.1.5 (all peripheral), Proposition 3.1.7 (nilpotent), Theorem 3.1.13 (open disk from a nonzero stable matrix eigenvalue), and Theorem 3.1.14(ii) (stable only); see also 2016 Theorem 5(i) and (iii). The entire full closed-disk row when a nonzero stable matrix eigenvalue exists is already immediate from the 2015 open-disk inclusion, spectral closedness and `||K_A|| = 1`. The remaining full-spectrum row is a routine peripheral/nilpotent direct-sum consequence.
