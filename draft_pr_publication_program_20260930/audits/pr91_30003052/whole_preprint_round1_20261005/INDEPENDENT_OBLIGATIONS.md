# Independent obligations and derivation (before package or older reviews)

Read order: task specification and directory filenames only; no prior mathematical, priority, provenance, or reproduction reports have been read.

Claim: for finite-dimensional complex X with arbitrary norm, ||A||<=1, U its closed unit ball, T f=f o A on complex C(U), J is the set of nonzero eigenvalues strictly inside the disk, S the peripheral eigenvalues, Gamma the group they generate (empty product 1), and Z_A={0} exactly when A is singular. Claimed point/full table: J nonempty gives open unit disk union Gamma / closed unit disk; J empty gives Gamma union Z_A / closure(Gamma) union Z_A.

Independent proof obligations:
1. Contraction excludes nonsemisimple peripheral Jordan blocks, including nonnormal arbitrary-norm models. Decompose X=E+F, B=A|E, stable A|F. Establish unbounded recurrence n_k with B^n_k->I, including finite and empty S, then P=lim A^n_k is contractive; this puts P(U) inside U.
2. If |mu|=1 and Tf=mu f, uniform continuity and A^n(I-P)->0 show mu^n(f(x)-f(Px))=f(A^n x)-f(PA^n x)->0. Hence f=fP, without selecting a mu-dependent recurrence.
3. On E, B preserves the norm by recurrence. Coordinate eigenforms and their conjugates yield eigenvalues in Gamma. Complex polynomial algebra separates points, so it is dense in C(P(U)). Cesaro projection at mu on a polynomial approximation forces every nonzero unimodular eigenfunction to have mu in Gamma. This argument must cover irrational dense Gamma and must not confuse its closure with its point spectrum.
4. If nonzero |lambda|<1, an eigenform ell o A=lambda ell gives f_mu=|ell|^(a+ib), zero at ker ell, with a=log|mu|/log|lambda|>0 and b chosen from Arg(mu)/log|lambda|. This continuous function gives every 0<|mu|<1, regardless of Arg(lambda). Zero requires a separate construction: A(U) proper implies distance-to-A(U) is a nonzero function in ker T. J nonempty implies properness even when A invertible (det modulus<1). Full spectrum is closed disk by the contraction bound and dense point spectrum.
5. If J empty, F is nilpotent. Qf=fP is a bounded commuting projection. On ran Q, T is the isometric composition operator of B with spectrum closure Gamma (dense monomial algebra and resolvent/Fourier or classical group-rotation reasoning); on ker Q, T is nilpotent. The zero summand exists exactly when F nonzero, equivalently A singular in this case. Need full-spectrum proof, not finite grid evidence.
6. Boundary checks: X={0}, A=0, A=I, pure nilpotent, diagonal mixed peripheral/stable, arbitrary-norm nonnormal contraction, irrational rotation, root-of-unity rotation, invertible strict stable contraction, and stable/peripheral plus nilpotent mixtures. A={0} on positive dimension has nonzero kernel T yet constants give 1.

Adversarial targets: hidden Euclidean norm, recurrence boundedness, discontinuity at ker ell, mu=0 omission, point/full closure mixup, nilpotent direct-sum kernel assertion, misuse of monomial approximation, and numerical tests presented as infinite-dimensional spectral proof.
