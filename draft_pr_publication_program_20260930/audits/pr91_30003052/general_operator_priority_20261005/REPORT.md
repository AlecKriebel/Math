## Checkable applicability of the general JdLG mechanism

This section is an audit inference/specialization, not a quotation or a claim that a source prints it. Use the candidate's finite-dimensional peripheral projection P, with A=B on E=ran(P), all other eigenvalues strictly inside the circle, and A on F=ker(P) having powers tending to zero. Contractivity gives semisimple peripheral blocks. Simultaneous recurrence gives A^{n_j} -> P in matrix norm; hence ||P||<=1 and P(U)=V:=U cap E. These elementary matrix facts are the identification obligations; they cannot be omitted by simply citing JdLG.

1. Let H be the matrix-norm closure of {A^n:n>=0}. It is a compact Abelian semigroup. Every L in H maps U into U. The map
   \[
   \Psi:H\longrightarrow\mathcal L(C(U)),\qquad
       \Psi(L)f=f\circ L
   \]
   is continuous for the strong operator topology: uniform continuity of each f and uniform convergence of L on compact U give convergence in the sup norm. It is injective, since the coordinate functions distinguish Lx for x in the spanning ball U. Because H is Abelian, the reversed order of composition creates no ambiguity. Its image is exactly the strong closure of the Koopman powers, is compact, and therefore is also their weak operator closure. No reflexivity of C(U) is needed. EFHN theorem4.17 is the general continuity correspondence underlying this step.

2. The set
   \[
   G_A=\{DP:D\in\overline{\{B^n:n\ge0\}}\}
   \]
   is a compact group and an ideal of H, with unit P. Every power orbit's sufficiently far cluster points have zero stable block and peripheral block in the indicated compact group. Thus the minimum group ideal is G_A. Equivalently, any minimum ideal intersects G_A (multiply its element by P), and the compact group has no proper nonempty ideal. Hence P is H's minimal idempotent. Its image under the semigroup isomorphism Psi is the minimal idempotent
   \[
   Q=\Psi(P),\qquad Qf=f\circ P
   \]
   of the compact Koopman-power semigroup. This checks the exact extra identification that is not explicitly carried out for the mixed ball in the inspected sources.

3. The JdLG construction identifies the reversible subspace with ran(Q). EFHN theorem16.33(a) places each unimodular eigenvector there. Therefore
   \[
   K_A f=\zeta f,\quad |\zeta|=1
          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f=f\circ P.
   \]
   Conversely, a B-eigenfunction on V pulls back by P with unchanged eigenvalue, since P is onto V and AP=PA.

4. B is a surjective isometry of E in the restricted norm: ||B||<=1 and a recurrent subsequence of B^{n_j-1} tends to B^{-1}, implying ||B^{-1}||<=1. Thus (V,B) has exactly Küster's all-peripheral closed-ball hypotheses. By theorem3.1.5 its point spectrum is Gamma=<S>. When E=0, V is a singleton, yielding {1}. This is the target exclusion, including arbitrary original norm and singular A. Edeko proposition2.7 independently describes why the peripheral factor has discrete spectrum, but does not identify Gamma by itself.

One can also verify step3 directly from any recurrent sequence: pass to a subsequence with zeta^{n_j}->c in the unit circle; then Qf=cf. Since Q is an idempotent, c^2=c and c=1. This is the same compact-semigroup mechanism stripped of JdLG terminology. It does not demonstrate that the target was explicitly answered earlier.

Status: **verified short corollary/specialization of standard theory**. It is not a blocked route, circular reduction, or unsupported stronger claim. Documentary gap: no inspected primary source explicitly combines these steps for the exact mixed closed-ball setting.

## Full-spectrum general-family comparison

Kitover–Orhon's exact selfmap assumption is injectivity into K with a proper image. For invertible A with a nonzero strictly contracting eigenvalue, A:U->U is an injective nonsurjective compact map. The attractor L=intersection A^n(U) is V, has empty relative interior in U when stable dimension is nonzero, and composition on L is an invertible isometry. The constant observable gives spectral radius1. Theorem3.1's disk mechanism therefore gives the closed unit disk (see preprint caveat below). Lemma4.1 says lambda I-K is surjective for 0<|lambda|<1; combined with the interior spectrum inclusion this forces those values to be point eigenvalues. Zero is a point eigenvalue because A(U) is proper and continuous functions can vanish on it without vanishing everywhere.

When A is singular, do not apply the injective theorem on U. Choose d with ran A^d stabilized and Y=A^d(U). A restricted to Y is injective into Y, and J nonempty makes it nonsurjective. Restriction R:C(U)->C(Y) is onto, ker(R) is invariant, and K_A^d annihilates ker(R). Thus nonzero spectrum agrees with that of the quotient C(Y); nonzero point eigenfunctions transfer through g->g composed with A^d and restriction. This finite nilpotent reduction is an additional elementary applicability check, not a statement explicitly printed in PartVII. It recovers the same contracting/full-spectrum row. No assertion is made that PartVII covers the original singular selfmap without this reduction.

For J empty, Q yields a reducing direct sum C(U)=ran(Q) direct-sum ker(Q). The second restriction is nilpotent, the first is the compact peripheral action. Its circle spectrum is the closure of Gamma: either Gamma is finite and some power is identity, or it is infinite and its closure is the circle. The optional zero is the nonzero nilpotent summand. These are standard direct-sum/nilpotent and compact-rotation deductions; they do not answer a novel difficult full-spectrum question independently of the point-spectrum issue.

### Preprint typography caveat

The inspected primary PartVII v1 defines U=open unit disk and D=closed disk on p.2. Theorem3.1(I), p.4, nevertheless writes sigma(T,C(M))=rho(T,C(M)) U or {0}; a nonzero-radius open disk cannot be a bounded Banach-operator spectrum. This is visible in the PDF, not merely an extraction loss. Step3 on p.5 also prints fn->0 where the approximate eigenvector residual is required. The intended disk inclusion and closedness lead to the closed disk, consistent with its example5.7, but the audit does not silently treat the displayed formula as literally correct. The source's referenced2011 proofs and the journal version were not available for full proof-level correction here. This is a source-text caveat on general full-spectrum attribution, and it has no bearing on the separately verified EFHN peripheral mechanism.

## Adversarial scope checks

- Compact matrix-power closure gives a **strongly compact semigroup of Koopman operators**. It does not make a Koopman operator compact in operator norm or justify compact-operator spectral theorems.
- All Koopman orbits being relatively compact does not mean the original mixed operator has discrete spectrum. Edeko proposition2.7 requires invertibility; the stable ideal is nonzero and can contain interior eigenvalues.
- A invertible as a matrix does not imply A maps the ball onto itself, nor that K_A is invertible. This is essential for PartVII's proper-image hypothesis.
- EFHN works on Banach spaces, so no invariant measure or Hilbert L2 model is substituted for C(U).
- A generic union-of-subgroups peripheral theorem (EFHN4.21) does not determine the group Gamma. Its direct use would leave the central exclusion unsupported.
- The JdLG theorem alone names an abstract Q. Identifying Q with composition by the given contractive P is mandatory. The steps above verify it and label it as an audit deduction.
- Peripheral dimension0, root-of-unity spectra, nilpotent stable blocks, nonzero stable Jordan blocks, and arbitrary complex norms do not invalidate the identification. N^n->0 is sufficient, and recurrence supplies the norm-compatible P.
