# Independent proof obligations before reading the package

Claim under review: for a complex-linear A on C^k contractive in an arbitrary complex norm, U its closed unit ball, K_A on all continuous complex functions on U has point/full spectra as given in the task. This is an infinite-dimensional bounded-operator statement. No manuscript, prior review, or ROOT adjudication has been read at this stage.

Independent derivation and attacks:

1. Power boundedness forces every peripheral Jordan block to have size one. Spectral splitting E_c direct-sum E_s therefore has B diagonalizable with unit-modulus eigenvalues and C with spectral radius <1. An unbounded recurrent sequence n_j must simultaneously approximate all peripheral phases by 1; A^{n_j}->P, the peripheral spectral projection. Hence ||P||<=1, P(U)=U intersect E_c, and B is an isometry on that induced ball. A proof must establish unbounded recurrence even for finite-order phases or empty peripheral set.
2. For |mu|=1, uniform continuity plus A^n(I-P)->0 gives f=f composed with P from f composed with A=mu f. This must hold for every point of U and must use exact eigenfunction equality, not an L2-a.e. or measurable argument.
3. On the peripheral ball, coordinate polynomials z^alpha conjugate(z)^beta form a dense complex subalgebra and have eigenvalues equal to all integer products Gamma. To show no extra eigenvalues when Gamma is dense requires a coefficient/projection argument (compact abelian action / Haar character projection or equivalent); spectral closure alone is insufficient for point spectrum. The closure of Gamma can contain values outside Gamma.
4. A nonzero stable eigenvalue rho permits a nonzero left eigenfunctional ell. For any 0<|mu|<1, write a+ib=log(mu)/log(|rho|), a>0, and h(x)=|ell(x)|^(a+ib) with h=0 at ell=0. Then h(Ax)=mu h(x) and continuity follows from a>0. The phase of rho does not matter. Zero eigenvalue must be separately constructed by a function vanishing on A(U); because strict stable directions make A(U) a proper closed subset even when A is nonsingular, zero belongs to point spectrum in this regime.
5. If all stable eigenvalues are zero, stable block C is nilpotent. For mu!=0, iterate past nilpotency and show every eigenfunction factors through P. If A is singular, A(U) is proper closed so 0 is an eigenvalue; if nonsingular, no stable block remains and A maps U onto U, excluding 0.
6. K has norm 1. When a stable nonzero eigenvalue exists, the open disk lies in point spectrum and closure forces full spectrum closed disk. In the nilpotent-only case, the complement ker(f->f composed with P) is invariant and its restriction is nilpotent, while the factor-through-P part is an invertible isometry generated by compact abelian rotations. Full spectrum is closure Gamma (plus 0 iff nilpotent complement nonzero). Must avoid asserting equality solely from polynomial density without a bounded resolvent argument.
7. Stress cases: k=1 rho=0, rho in (0,1), rho on unit circle finite order or irrational; nonnormal stable Jordan blocks; arbitrary non-product norms; peripheral-only finite-order multi-phase group; mixed irrational peripheral and nilpotent stable blocks; singular mixed stable; empty peripheral group {1}; dense Gamma with a boundary point outside Gamma.

Initial judgment: the displayed classification appears mathematically plausible, with two nontrivial obligations: exact point-spectrum exclusion on the compact rotation part and a full bounded-operator spectrum upper bound in the nilpotent-only regime. No counterexample found by these attempts. This is a pre-reading hypothesis, not a verdict.
