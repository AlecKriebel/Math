## Exact unrestricted question

For a homeomorphism h of R^n (also asked for a diffeomorphism), suppose there is one finite constant D, independent of x, such that the diameter of {h^m(x): m in Z} is at most D for every x. Suppose also that h^{n_k} tends to identity uniformly on every compact set, for a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers n_k. Must h be identity? The normalization D=1 can be achieved by spatial scaling; it is not the assertion that each orbit merely has its own finite bound. The full target ranges over n, including n>=3.

The source's second question is the closed-ball variant: a homeomorphism of B^n fixing its boundary pointwise, recurrent to identity in compact-open topology, must be identity? On a compact closed ball compact-open convergence is uniform in a compatible metric. A recurrence sequence cannot be the repeated exponent zero. A finite-order map is recurrent in the specified positive-sequence sense. n=0 is vacuous; n=1 is included. An open ball lacking its boundary is a different hypothesis.

The source additionally discusses local-open-set/manifold formulations with suitably small neighborhoods, a periodic case attributed to Newman, a stronger smooth recurrence variant, and a compact-cyclic-closure/Hilbert–Smith connection. None is permission to replace the literal uniform-orbit, compact-open target by compact cyclic closure, equicontinuity, pointwise recurrence, Euclidean-uniform recurrence, or a lower-dimensional special case. The informal phrase about diameter in compact-open topology is ill-typed as a metric assertion and remains a source remark, not a manufactured formal hypothesis.
