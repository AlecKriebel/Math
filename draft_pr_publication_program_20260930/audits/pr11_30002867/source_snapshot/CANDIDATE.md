# A Koszul-rank criterion for finite affine complete intersections

**Problem:** UnsolvedMath 30002867, OWR-13678-008, rank 28  
**Status:** complete candidate characterization; independent review and novelty assessment pending  
**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Model used:** gpt-6-astra, xhigh (not ultra)  
**Substantive proof attempts used:** 1 of 5

## 1. The exact question

Let $R=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$, let $I\subsetneq R$ have finite positive colength $N$, and let $M_1,\ldots,M_d$ be the matrices of multiplication by the coordinate classes on $A=R/I$, in any common basis. The question in Boris Shekhtman's *On one class of ideal projectors*, Oberwolfach Report 21/2015, p. 1183, Problem 2, asks for a characterization using these commuting matrices of the condition that $I$ is generated by $d$ polynomials. This is the **global affine** complete-intersection condition of Definition 5 in that report. No condition on highest-degree terms, projective closure, or zeros at infinity is part of this question.

The finite positive-colength convention excludes the zero projector and the unit ideal. We take $d\ge1$. The univariate case is included below.

## 2. The criterion

For $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d)\in\mathbb C^d$, set
$$
T_i(\lambda)=M_i-\lambda_i I_N,
\qquad
D_1(\lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}T_1(\lambda)&\cdots&T_d(\lambda)\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Define the $dN$-by-$\binom d2N$ block matrix $D_2(\lambda)$ by giving its block column indexed by $i<j$: it has $-T_j(\lambda)$ in block row $i$, $T_i(\lambda)$ in block row $j$, and zero in all other block rows. For $d=1$, this is the matrix with $N$ rows and no columns; its rank is zero.

The finite set
$$
\Sigma(M)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C^d:\operatorname{rank}D_1(\lambda)<N\}
$$
is the joint spectrum of the multiplication tuple. It equals the set of points of $V(I)$. In particular, it has at most $N$ elements. At each point of this set, $\operatorname{rank}D_1(\lambda)=N-1$.

**Theorem.** With this notation,
$$
\boxed{\quad I\text{ is a complete intersection}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{rank}D_2(\lambda)=(d-1)(N-1)
\text{ for every }\lambda\in\Sigma(M).\quad}
$$

More generally, the global minimum number $\mu_R(I)$ of generators is
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mu_R(I)=\max_{\lambda\in\Sigma(M)}
\big((d-1)N+1-\operatorname{rank}D_2(\lambda)\big).
\quad} \tag{1}
$$
The theorem uses only the input multiplication matrices, scalar shifts, and ranks. It does not require constructing a polynomial generating set for $I$, a Gröbner basis, or a primary decomposition. Computing the joint spectrum is part of the finite test. Concretely, one may form the finite Cartesian product of the individual spectra of the M_i and retain precisely the tuples for which D_1 has rank less than N. Over arbitrary complex inputs this is a mathematical finite characterization. Turning it into an executable exact algorithm additionally requires an effective coefficient field supporting arithmetic, equality tests, and algebraic root operations. It is not a numerically stable certification procedure for approximate input matrices.

For example, for three variables,
$$
D_2(\lambda)=
\begin{bmatrix}
-T_2&-T_3&0\\
 T_1&0&-T_3\\
 0&T_1&T_2
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

## 3. Proof

### 3.1. Support and the first rank

Write $\mathfrak m_\lambda=(x_1-\lambda_1,\ldots,x_d-\lambda_d)$. The cokernel of $D_1(\lambda)$, interpreted as a map $A^d\to A$, is
$$
A/\mathfrak m_\lambda A\cong R/(I+\mathfrak m_\lambda).
$$
Because $R/\mathfrak m_\lambda=\mathbb C$, this cokernel is $\mathbb C$ when $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_\lambda$, and zero otherwise. Thus $\Sigma(M)=V(I)$, and its points have first rank $N-1$. The finiteness and the cardinality bound follow because a finite algebra of dimension $N$ has at most $N$ distinct maximal ideals.

### 3.2. The second rank counts local equations

The sequence $x_1-\lambda_1,\ldots,x_d-\lambda_d$ is a regular sequence in $R$. Its Koszul complex is a free resolution of $\mathbb C_\lambda=R/\mathfrak m_\lambda$. Tensoring with $A$ gives a complex whose first two differentials are exactly $D_1(\lambda)$ and $D_2(\lambda)$. The commutation of the matrices also directly gives $D_1D_2=0$. Consequently,
$$
H_1(D_\bullet(\lambda))\cong
\operatorname{Tor}^R_1(A,\mathbb C_\lambda).
$$
For $\lambda\in V(I)$, tensoring $0\to I\to R\to A\to0$ with $\mathbb C_\lambda$ identifies
$$
\operatorname{Tor}^R_1(A,\mathbb C_\lambda)
\cong I/\mathfrak m_\lambda I.
$$
Indeed, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(R,\mathbb C_\lambda)=0$, and the induced map $I\otimes_R\mathbb C_\lambda\to R\otimes_R\mathbb C_\lambda$ is zero since $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_\lambda$.

Nakayama's lemma, applied after localization, therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{R_{\mathfrak m_\lambda}}(I_{\mathfrak m_\lambda})
&=\dim_\mathbb C I/\mathfrak m_\lambda I\\
&=dN-\operatorname{rank}D_1(\lambda)-\operatorname{rank}D_2(\lambda)\\
&=(d-1)N+1-\operatorname{rank}D_2(\lambda). \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
No assumption that $I$ is a complete intersection was used here.

The regular local ring $R_{\mathfrak m_\lambda}$ has dimension $d$, and $I_{\mathfrak m_\lambda}$ is primary for its maximal ideal. The height theorem implies that the number in (2) is at least $d$. It equals $d$ exactly when the localized ideal is generated by a system of parameters, hence by a regular sequence. This proves the asserted rank criterion for the **local** complete-intersection property. The next step supplies the required global conclusion.

### 3.3. From the local counts to the global minimum

Let $Q=I/I^2$, a finite module over the Artinian algebra $A$. For a finite module over an Artinian ring,
$$
\mu_A(Q)=\max_{\lambda\in V(I)}\mu_{A_{\mathfrak m_\lambda}}(Q_{\mathfrak m_\lambda}). \tag{3}
$$
To see this, decompose $A$ as the finite product of its Artinian local factors. Each local module requires its indicated number of generators. Conversely, pad each local generating list with zeros to the maximum length and combine the lists componentwise by the product decomposition. This produces a global generating list of that length.

At a point of $V(I)$,
$$
Q/\mathfrak m_\lambda Q
\cong I/(I^2+\mathfrak m_\lambda I)
=I/\mathfrak m_\lambda I,
$$
since $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_\lambda$. Thus (3) equals the maximum of the numbers in (2).

We now use the following classical efficient-generation theorem of N. Mohan Kumar:

> If $S=k[t_1,\ldots,t_s]$, $J\subset S$ is an ideal, and $r=\mu(J/J^2)\ge\dim(S/J)+2$, then $\mu(J)=r$.

The source is *On two conjectures about polynomial rings*, Invent. Math. **46** (1978), 225–236, Theorem 5. Its precise statement is also explicitly restated as Theorem 1.2 in M. K. Das, *On two conjectures of Murthy*, arXiv:1710.04281. Only this stable-range theorem is used, not the much stronger general Murthy conjecture.

When $d\ge2$, equations (2)–(3) imply $r=\mu_A(Q)\ge d\ge2$, while $\dim(R/I)=0$. All hypotheses of the efficient-generation theorem hold, so $\mu_R(I)=r$. This proves (1). Finally $\operatorname{ht}I=d$, so $I$ is a global complete intersection precisely when this maximum is $d$. Every local count is already at least $d$, so the maximum is $d$ precisely when every count equals $d$, giving the displayed criterion.

When $d=1$, every proper finite-colength ideal of $\mathbb C[x]$ is principal. The right side of (1) is $1$, because $D_2$ has no columns. The criterion and formula hold in this case as well. This completes the proof.

## 4. Invariance and scope checks

- A simultaneous change of basis $M_i\mapsto SM_iS^{-1}$ conjugates each block by invertible block-diagonal matrices and leaves all ranks unchanged.
- The criterion is for the regular multiplication representation. Arbitrary commuting matrices without the cyclic quotient-algebra hypothesis are outside the statement; merely applying the formula to such a tuple is not justified.
- The test uses the full matrices of size $N$, including every support component. One must not combine local matrix sizes with the global constant $(d-1)(N-1)$. A separately restricted local version uses its own length.
- Repeated eigenvalues and nonreduced ideals are covered. Diagonalizability is not assumed.
- Affine complete intersection does not mean strict complete intersection: an affine change or nonlinear embedding can change degree-form data while preserving the theorem.
- The theorem characterizes the complete-intersection property; it does not by itself construct the global $d$ generators or prove the separate Hermite-projector smoothing conjecture.

## 5. Diagnostic examples

The accompanying standard-library script checks 13 small quotient algebras by exact rational Gaussian elimination. It verifies pairwise commutativity, the identity D_1 D_2=0, the support rank, the generator-count formula, and vanishing of first homology at a point outside the support. The largest quotient has length 16; no exhaustive search was performed.

Two examples guard against misleading replacements for the target.

1. Let A=C[x,y,z]/(x^2,y^2,xz,yz,z^2-xy). It has basis 1,x,y,z,xy and one-dimensional socle spanned by xy. It is Gorenstein but its ideal needs five generators: the five displayed independent quadratic forms already minimally generate it. At its sole support point, D_2 has rank 6, whereas the CI target rank is 8. Thus a one-dimensional joint eigenspace alone is insufficient in three variables.
2. Let A=C[t]/(t^4), represented by x=t, y=t^2, z=t^3. The kernel is (y-x^2,z-x^3,x^4), so it is a global affine complete intersection. At the sole support point, N=4 and rank D_2=6=2(N-1), as required. Its degree filtration reaches all of A in degree one, however, so the associated graded algebra has Hilbert function (1,3) and its defining ideal is the square of the homogeneous maximal ideal. It is therefore not a strict complete intersection. The proposed test retains this example, as the original question requires.

The exact computation is a consistency check. The proof in Section 3 covers arbitrary d and N.

## 6. Status and novelty

This is a complete candidate answer to the precise characterization question. The local homological identity and the efficient-generation theorem are classical. The proposed contribution, if any, is the explicit block-rank formulation and its application to this particular question. No claim of a novel algebraic theorem or first resolution is made. The bounded search found related published algorithms, notably Kreuzer–Long–Robbiano (2019 preprint; 2022 journal article), which test local and strict complete intersections through Fitting ideals and border bases. Further expert or independent literature review is needed before attaching a novelty or priority claim to the formula.

A particularly important source trap was checked: Fasel's broad 2016 claim for all ideals and Mandal's related 2016 article have later errata. They are not valid substitutes for the stable-range result used above, and are not used in the proof.
