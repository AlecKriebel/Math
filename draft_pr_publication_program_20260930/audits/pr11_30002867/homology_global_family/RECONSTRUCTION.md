## Exact claim and hypotheses

Let R=C[x_1,...,x_d], d>=1, I proper with 0<N=dim_C(R/I)<infinity, and A=R/I. The input M_i is the action of x_i on the regular R-module A, in one common basis. For a closed point lambda put T_i=M_i-lambda_i Id, D_1=[T_1 ... T_d], and D_2(e_i wedge e_j tensor a)=e_j tensor T_i a-e_i tensor T_j a. The sought property is that I has d global polynomial generators. A stronger claimed formula is

    mu_R(I)=max_{lambda in V(I)} [(d-1)N+1-rank D_2(lambda)].

All ranks are over C. Equality with the maximum requires a global ideal-generation theorem; it does not follow from Nakayama in R alone.

## Independently derived local chain

1. The image of D_1 is m_lambda A. Thus coker D_1=A/m_lambda A=R/(I+m_lambda). If lambda lies in V(I), this quotient is C; otherwise it is zero. Consequently rank D_1=N-1 on the support and N off it.
2. The Koszul complex on x_i-lambda_i resolves C_lambda over R because these d coordinates form a regular sequence in the polynomial ring. Tensoring with A gives the indicated block matrices. Their signs agree with the convention d(e_i wedge e_j)=t_i e_j-t_j e_i. Hence H_1=Tor_1^R(A,C_lambda), and D_1 D_2=0 follows separately from commutativity.
3. Tensor the exact sequence 0 -> I -> R -> A -> 0 with C_lambda. Since R is free, its first Tor vanishes. The induced map I/m_lambda I -> C is zero when I subset m_lambda. The Tor exact sequence therefore gives Tor_1^R(A,C_lambda)=I/m_lambda I, with no complete-intersection assumption.
4. I/m_lambda I agrees with the residue quotient of I localized at m_lambda: localization does not change this C-vector space. Nakayama in the local ring R_m gives

       mu_m(I_m)=dim_C H_1=dN-rank D_1-rank D_2
                  =(d-1)N+1-rank D_2.

5. The support condition makes I_m an m_m-primary ideal of the d-dimensional regular local ring R_m. Krull height gives mu_m(I_m)>=d. Thus the rank bound is rank D_2 <= (d-1)(N-1). Equality means mu_m=d. A d-generated m-primary ideal in this Cohen–Macaulay local ring is a system-of-parameters ideal, so it is generated by a regular sequence. Conversely a local complete intersection of this height needs d generators.

No step uses diagonalizability, reducedness, a monomial basis, a degree filtration, or the number of support points being one.

## Artinian conormal glue, and its limitation

Write A=product_{lambda} A_lambda and Q=I/I^2=product Q_lambda. If r_lambda is the local minimum number for Q_lambda, r=max r_lambda is the global A-module minimum: pad each local minimal list by zeros and combine the lists using the mutually orthogonal central idempotents of A. The lower bound comes from localization. At every support point,

    Q/m_lambda Q=I/(I^2+m_lambda I)=I/m_lambda I

because I^2 subset m_lambda I. Thus r=max mu_m(I_m).

This constructs r conormal generators, not automatically r ideal generators in R. Explicit warning example: for I=(x,y) in C[x,y], x-x^2 and y generate I/I^2, but (x-x^2,y) also vanishes at (1,0), so it is strictly smaller than I. A direct invocation of global Nakayama here would be false. The candidate correctly supplies a substantive efficient-generation theorem instead.

## Global theorem application independently checked

The claimed stable-range theorem is:

> For S=k[t_1,...,t_s], if r=mu(J/J^2)>=dim(S/J)+2, then mu(J)=r.

An author preprint by M. K. Das explicitly states it as Theorem 1.2, attributing it to Mohan Kumar 1978, Theorem 5. That preprint alone was initially a restatement, not a direct inspection of the 1978 proof. The primary-source family subsequently recovered the original 1978 scan. This family independently inspected printed pp.234–235 at 05:06–05:07 UTC on 2026-10-01: Theorem 5 states the same field-polynomial result, more generally allowing a field or principal ideal domain base. No characteristic, infinite-field, or algebraic-closure restriction is present. The original proof uses monic inversion, patching, and polynomial-ring projective freeness. This closes the earlier direct-source limitation.

An independent, publicly available primary proof exists in Sadao Tachibana, *Efficient Generation of Ideals in Polynomial Rings*, Proceedings of the Institute of Natural Sciences (1987), pp.57–61, Theorem 2.3. Its hypotheses are a Noetherian base B, R=B[X], an ideal containing a monic polynomial, and mu(I/I^2)>=dim(R/I)+2. It gives exactly mu(I)=mu(I/I^2), and its pp.59–61 supply a proof via a prime-avoidance choice, local generation, patching, and monic-polynomial projective freeness. The paper identifies this as an alternative proof of Mandal's 1984 result. This is not Mandal's erroneous much stronger 2016 paper.

For the present finite-colength target the additional monic hypothesis is automatic, with no change of variables: set B=C[x_1,...,x_{d-1}], X=x_d. The characteristic polynomial chi_{M_d}(X) is monic and annihilates the multiplication action by Cayley–Hamilton. Applying chi_{M_d}(M_d) to the unit class 1 proves chi_{M_d}(x_d) in I. The ring B is Noetherian; dim(R/I)=0. For d>=2, r>=d>=2, so Tachibana's theorem applies directly. It therefore independently certifies the global equality needed here even independently of the now completed inspection of Mohan Kumar's original. For d=1, C[x] is a PID and every proper finite-colength ideal is principal.

The stable-range inequality is fully satisfied at its endpoint d=2,r=2, where r=dim(R/I)+2. No strict inequality or genericity assumption is being substituted.

The only external algebraic input needed beyond standard local commutative algebra is this established efficient-generation theorem (or the original Mohan Kumar theorem, once inspected). The conormal-to-ideal lifting is the central nontrivial step; it has not been silently turned into an unsupported hypothesis.

## Multiple support and off-support acyclicity

Let A=direct sum_{nu} A_nu, with local lengths N_nu. Fix lambda. On A_nu with nu!=lambda, at least one x_i-lambda_i has nonzero residue nu_i-lambda_i and is therefore a unit. A Koszul complex with a unit entry is contractible: exterior multiplication by e_i divided by that entry gives an explicit contracting homotopy. Thus each off-point summand contributes no H_j, and

    rank D_1 on A_nu=N_nu,
    rank D_2 on A_nu=(d-1)N_nu.

At lambda, the local block has rank D_1=N_lambda-1 and

    rank D_2=(d-1)N_lambda+1-mu_lambda.

Adding gives the global rank (d-1)N+1-mu_lambda. This independently confirms that the global N in the candidate is correct even for unequal local lengths. Replacing N by N_lambda without restricting all blocks would be wrong.

## Boundary and adversarial cases

* d=1: D_2 has no columns, its rank is 0, the threshold is 0, and the formula is 1. Different eigenvalues and nilpotent Jordan blocks cause no change.
* N=1: A=C at one point, all T_i=0 there, the threshold is 0, and the formula gives d. The kernel is that point's maximal ideal, indeed generated by d coordinates.
* d=2: D_2 is the vertical map a -> (-T_2 a,T_1 a). Its kernel is the local socle at lambda (off-point factors contribute none). If the socle dimension is t_lambda, rank D_2=N-t_lambda and the local generator number is t_lambda+1. The criterion is exactly the classical codimension-two Artinian Gorenstein/complete-intersection condition. This checks the endpoint case independently.
* Arbitrary commuting matrices: take N=3,d=2 and M_x=[[0,1,0],[0,0,0],[0,0,0]], M_y=[[0,0,1],[0,0,0],[0,0,0]]. They commute and have sole joint support (0,0). Their D_2 has rank 2=(d-1)(N-1), but their common annihilator is (x,y)^2, which requires 3 generators. D_1 has rank 1, not N-1. These matrices give the dual of the regular representation of C[x,y]/(x,y)^2, and are not cyclic. This explicitly falsifies extending the rank criterion to arbitrary commuting tuples. The candidate excludes exactly this extension.
* Regular versus cyclic: a finite-dimensional commuting tuple with one cyclic vector is a regular quotient module R/ann(v), and then its vector-space dimension equals the quotient length. Conversely regular multiplication has cyclic vector 1. Mere faithfulness of an arbitrary representation is insufficient. The preceding dual representation is faithful for its quotient but not cyclic.
* Nilpotents: x_i-lambda_i are allowed to be nilpotent on a local factor. Only the coordinate regularity in R, not in A, is used to resolve the residue field.
* Height extremes: finite positive colength forces height I=d. Lower-height ideals and positive-dimensional quotients are outside the statement, so no weaker stable-range bound is needed. The zero projector/unit ideal N=0 would invalidate the intended positive-colength conventions and is explicitly excluded. d=0 is also excluded.

## Minor wording clarification

An affine-linear coordinate transformation preserves the standard degree filtration and the strict-complete-intersection property; its induced invertible linear map transforms the highest-degree-form ideal. A nonlinear polynomial coordinate change or re-embedding may change that filtration and the strict-CI property. The candidate's phrase that an affine change can change “degree-form data” can mean transformed coefficients (which is true), but should avoid implying that affine-linear changes alter the strict-CI property. This is exposition only and does not affect the rank theorem.
