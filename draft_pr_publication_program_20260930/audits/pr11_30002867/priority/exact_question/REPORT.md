## Earlier sufficient algorithm and its exact mapping

[Abbott–Kreuzer–Robbiano, *Computing zero-dimensional schemes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2004.09.001), J. Symbolic Computation 39 (2005), 31–49, is available in an [author PDF hosted by Passau](https://symbcomp.fim.uni-passau.de/fileadmin/dokumente/fakultaeten/fim/lehrstuhl/kreuzer/Artikel/Computing_Zero-Dimensional_Schemes.pdf). Propositions 2.6/2.8 (author-PDF p. 6), Theorem 3.1 (pp. 7–8), and Remark 3.3 (p. 9) supply the computational bridge: multiplication matrices and the coordinates of a cyclic unit vector determine the normal-form-vector map, and their generalized Buchberger–Möller algorithm computes the defining Gröbner basis directly. A previous Gröbner basis is not required. The author's draft header has placeholder bibliographic data; the author institution's publication list and journal DOI identify the actual 2005 publication.

The report's matrix data need not name the unit vector. The following elementary adapter closes that input gap. It is an audit deduction, not a claim that the 2005 paper states this adapter as an answer to the 2015 question.

Assume the promised tuple comes from a proper ideal with finite positive colength N>=1, as in the frozen candidate. Let B be the subalgebra of Mat_N(C) generated by the given regular M_i. Faithfulness of the regular representation identifies B with A, so dim B=N and ker(p -> p(M))=I. Starting with B_0=span{I_N}, compute

`B_(r+1) = B_r + sum_i M_i B_r`.

If a step stops growing, the space is already closed under every generator and hence equals B. Otherwise its dimension increases at least one at every step. Thus B_(N-1)=B. Choose a basis of B by ordinary linear algebra, express I_N in it, and compute the matrices of left multiplication by M_i on that basis. This provides precisely the normal-form data required by the 2005 algorithm, using only the original tuple and finite linear algebra. It also works for N=1, repeated eigenvalues, and multiple support points.

Next use [Kreuzer–Long–Robbiano, *Algorithms for Checking Zero-Dimensional Complete Intersections*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09563), first posted 22 March 2019, v2 dated 6 August 2019, published in J. Commut. Algebra 14 (2022), 61–76, DOI [10.1216/jca.2022.14.61](https://doi.org/10.1216/jca.2022.14.61). Proposition 3.2 / Wiebe's criterion detects an Artinian local CI using the nonzero zeroth Fitting ideal of its maximal ideal. Proposition 3.3 computes that ideal from a coefficient matrix obtained by writing primary-ideal generators against maximal-ideal generators. Algorithm 3.4 tests each local factor; Algorithm 3.6 combines the factors after primary decomposition. Those algorithms inspect local CI.

Over the candidate's C[x] setting, local CI at every support point makes I/I² locally free of rank d over A. The Artinian product decomposition then gives I/I²≅A^d, so μ(I/I²)=d. The already verified stable-generation theorem of N. Mohan Kumar, *On two conjectures about polynomial rings* (1978), Theorem 5, requires μ(I/I²)>=dim(R/I)+2. Here the threshold is d>=2, and its conclusion is μ(I)=d. The [original institutional scan](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0046/LOG_0020.pdf), printed pp. 234–235, and the [separate core source audit](../../primary_target_family/mohan_kumar/report.md) make this dependency checkable. Conversely, d global generators necessarily give local CI here; d=1 is principal. Thus reconstruction + Algorithm 3.6 + the classical theorem gives an earlier complete algorithmic route to the **literal global property in matrix data**.

An executable algorithm requires exact effective coefficient data supporting the arithmetic, equality, and decomposition operations of these methods. For arbitrary complex entries the composition is a mathematical reduction and characterization; it is not a claim of computation from numerical approximations. This is the same distinction the candidate makes for its rank test.

That composition is an inference from primary results. Neither paper explicitly says it resolves Shekhtman's Problem 2, and KLR's polynomial/primary input differs from the candidate's direct shifted-block test. These differences explain presentational convenience; they do not supply a new mathematical characterization mechanism. No comparative complexity, numerical-stability, or performance result has been proved.
