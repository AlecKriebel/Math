# Independent audit of later Shekhtman sources

Audit scope: OWR 2015, p.1183, Definition 5 and Problem 2. The literal property is that a global zero-dimensional ideal I in C[x1,...,xd] can be generated by precisely d polynomials, and the input is its regular coordinate multiplication tuple. A local complete-intersection test, strict complete-intersection test, or smoothability characterization is not by itself the same claim.

**Finding:** no explicit matrix/Koszul rank criterion answering that literal question was identified in the inspected later sources or bounded searches. The positive result established here is narrower: the directly indexed citing continuation (2023) and the inspected 2024 and 2025 works address other properties. A 2026 chapter remains an access gap. This audit does not establish universal absence or certify novelty.

## Adversarial exact-question check

The potential shortcut through source 1 fails concretely. In two variables I=(x^2,xy,y^2) is the kernel of an ideal projector and is Hermite under the bivariate result recalled on pp.1–2 of the 2023 paper. However it cannot be generated globally by two elements: localizing at m=(x,y), the vector space I_m/m I_m has the three independent degree-two monomials x^2, xy, y^2. Any two generators would span a vector space of dimension at most two, a contradiction. Thus smoothability alone cannot distinguish the literal property. This is an independent mathematical check, not a claimed new theorem.

The potential shortcut through source 3 also fails: the relation-module equality holds for a tuple compared with itself, including the above non-complete-intersection regular tuple. Requiring comparison with some already-known d-generated model would move the central class-membership difficulty into the choice and certification of that model. That route is blocked absent a new construction or independent theorem.

For source 4, replacing a multi-generator ideal by one selected principal subideal replaces the quotient and may enlarge the variety; its existence-of-interpolation argument cannot recover the minimal number of generators of the original ideal. The published remark itself records the lost uniqueness. For a proper finite-colength ideal with d>1, a nonconstant principal subideal has infinite-colength quotient and its variety is larger.

## Final adversarial review of the combined report

After the independent source family was complete, I read the parent's combined exact-question REPORT.md on request. Its matrix-only adapter passes: for a promised regular tuple on a nonzero quotient, the generated algebra B has dimension N and the faithful map S/I -> B has kernel I. If B_(r+1)=B_r then every M_i preserves B_r, so B_r contains every polynomial in the generators and equals B. Otherwise dimension rises at each step from dim B_0=1; therefore stabilization occurs by r=N−1. In a basis of B, the identity matrix supplies the coordinate unit and multiplication by M_i supplies exactly the regular normal-form data for reconstruction. Repeated eigenvalues and several support points do not affect this argument; N=1 is covered by B_0=B. N=0 should be excluded by explicitly saying the ideal/quotient is proper and nonzero.

The local-to-global bridge should be written explicitly: local CI at all support points gives I/I² locally free of rank d over the Artinian algebra A=S/I. Decomposing A into its Artinian local factors makes this module globally A^d, hence μ(I/I²)=d. The efficient-generation theorem's threshold becomes d>=dim A+2=2; d=1 is the separate principal-ideal case. My review accepts the parent's already verified 1978 theorem scope; I did not independently retrieve its original fulltext here. A primary 2024 author paper independently restates the relevant threshold in its Theorem 1.2: [Efficient generation of ideals and an analogue of a result of Mohan Kumar](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021869324000978). Link the original 1978 theorem artifact in the combined report for auditability.

Thus the composed reconstruction/local-test/classical-generation algorithm has no mathematical input gap under those assumptions. The claim of earlier explicit disclosure is a separate historical statement: none of the later author papers inspected here explicitly states the candidate's shifted-Koszul D2 equation or advertises a solution to OWR Problem 2. The combined report correctly labels its sufficient prior algorithm as a derived composition, and does not attribute that composition or the later question to the 2005 paper. No falsifier of the adapter was found; this is a bounded review, not an exhaustive priority proof.
