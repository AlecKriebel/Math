## Exact target

The original source is [Shekhtman, OWR 2015/21](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46567), printed p. 1183: Definition 5 specifies a finite-colength ideal in `C[x_1,...,x_d]` generated by `d` polynomials, and Problem 2 asks for characterization using the commuting multiplication matrices in (1), p. 1183. The target is global generation of the original affine ideal. It includes nilpotents, several support points, and embeddings with redundant coordinate generators. It does not impose the stronger strict condition on the degree-form ideal.

The candidate's core rank mathematics has already passed the separate core audit. This family addresses priority, not whether its deductions are valid.

## Decisive prior mechanism, entirely in the matrices

1. Recover a basis of the faithful finite algebra `B=C[M_1,...,M_d]` by linear independence of monomial matrix evaluations. `B≅P/I` because the matrices act regularly. The coordinates of the identity element of the original quotient basis need not be known.
2. At a joint support point `λ`, put `T_i=M_i−λ_i Id`. Present the `B`-module `J_λ=(T_1,...,T_d)B` by calculating the coefficient kernel of `B^d→J_λ`. A vector-space basis of this kernel generates it as a `B`-module. Its entries are linear combinations of the basis matrices in `B`.
3. Compute the determinant matrices of all maximal minors of that presentation. They generate `Fitt_0^B(J_λ)`. At the selected support factor, `J_λ` is its maximal ideal. At every other factor, one `T_i` is a unit and `J_λ` is free of rank one, whose zeroth Fitting ideal is zero. Thus some determinant matrix is nonzero precisely when the selected factor is a zero-dimensional local CI, by [Wiebe's original Satz 3, p. 260](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN235181684_0179/LOG_0075.pdf) (1969).
4. Require this at every joint support point. [Mohan Kumar's original Theorem 4, p. 234](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0046/LOG_0020.pdf) (1978) bounds the global generator count of a locally CI ideal in a polynomial ring by the number of variables. Height gives the reverse bound `d`, so this is exactly the original global property.

This route is a finite matrix characterization without primary decomposition, polynomial ideal generators, or a supplied cyclic vector. The oldest decisive criterion is 1969 and the global bridge is 1978. The explicit translation is documented in [CHECKABLE_MAPPING.md](CHECKABLE_MAPPING.md). It is our checkable reconstruction of those published mechanisms, not an assertion that its notation occurs verbatim in the prior sources.

The nonminimal coordinate issue is covered by presentation independence of Fitting ideals and by the original definition on Wiebe p. 258. [Simon–Strooker, Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703880), pp. 9–10 (2007), explicitly allow nonminimal maximal-ideal generators. KLR Proposition 3.2 also relates the local ring criterion to the ideal in the given ambient regular polynomial local ring.

## Central KLR source independently closes the task

[Kreuzer–Long–Robbiano, arXiv:1903.09563v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09563v1), posted 22 March 2019, already contains Proposition 3.2, Algorithm 3.4 (p. 10), and Algorithm 3.6 (pp. 10–11). The last algorithm computes primary components of the original affine ideal and checks their local CI property through Wiebe's Fitting criterion. This is not its later Section 4/5 strict CI algorithm. The journal publication is *Journal of Commutative Algebra* 14 (2022), 61–76, DOI [10.1216/jca.2022.14.61](https://doi.org/10.1216/jca.2022.14.61).

The regular matrices supply the exact input of Algorithm 3.6: enumerate monomials in an actual multiplicative degree-compatible order, retain independent evaluations, and solve for each border monomial in that basis. The resulting border relations generate precisely `I`, as proved in the mapping. The degree cutoff `N−1` suffices. This preserves the whole nilpotent algebra and every component. Apply the published affine local algorithm and then original Mohan Kumar Theorem 4. The result is again an exact matrix-derived global characterization.

KLR Remark 5.6(e), p. 22, gives another equivalent local test in characteristic zero: the Kähler different has nonzero image in every local factor. Reconstruct the border generators, calculate Jacobian minors, and evaluate them at the matrices. Projection by each principal idempotent yields the test. Checking only one globally nonzero minor would fail to cover multiple support points; checking every factor closes the exact target. The different method adds no obstacle over `C`.

The university-hosted journal PDF retrieved here is a seven-page extract, omitting central Section 3. The relevant algorithm locators above were verified in full original arXiv v1 and v2, not assigned invented journal page numbers.

## Verification and adversarial checks

The general proof is in the mapping; finite examples are corroboration. The exact rational executable uses only the standard library and supplies no quotient unit vector or ideal generators to the recovery. All matrices undergo a nontrivial simultaneous similarity change. Eight cases pass:

- univariate nilpotent CI;
- two-variable rectangular nilpotent CI;
- two-variable square-zero non-CI;

- three-variable Gorenstein non-CI;
