# Independent adversarial audit of the matrix Fitting criterion

Scope: the local determinant criterion and its link to the specified regular
embedding. No sibling review was read. No canonical manuscript was edited, and
no Git operation or external communication was performed.

**Verdict:** no counterexample or unresolved local logical gap under the stated
hypotheses. The construction is valid if the algebra is unital, its relation
kernel is computed in the recovered algebra, and determinants are interpreted
as algebra elements. Three implementation/wording traps are recorded below.

## Claim and assumptions

Let R=C[x_1,...,x_d], let I be proper of finite positive colength N, and let
M_i be the simultaneous multiplication matrices on A=R/I in an arbitrary common
basis. Set B=C[M_1,...,M_d], **including the identity matrix**. For lambda in the
support, put T_i=M_i-lambda_i 1 and J_lambda=sum_i T_i B. Let U have as its
columns any C-basis of ker(B^d -> B), where the map is (b_i) -> sum_i T_i b_i.

The tested claim is

    some d by d minor of U is nonzero in B
      iff B_lambda is an Artinian complete intersection
      iff I R_m_lambda is generated by a regular sequence of length d.

For d=0 the only case is I=0,N=1, and the empty determinant is one.

## Proof of the recovered algebra and degree bound

Polynomial evaluation identifies B with A. Indeed, f(M) is multiplication by
the class of f; it is zero exactly when that class is zero. The abstract unit
of A proves faithfulness here, but its coordinate vector is not algorithmic
input. Thus dim_C B=N, and products of matrices give the algebra operations.

Let V_k be the span of all evaluated monomials of degree at most k, starting
with V_0=C1. If V_k=V_(k+1), then each M_i preserves V_k, so V_k=B. Before
stabilization the dimension increases at least one each step. Hence V_(N-1)=B.
Selecting independent flattened matrices and expressing products in that
matrix basis requires no vector in the regular module.

## Support and product-factor isolation

The horizontal matrix [T_1 ... T_d] is the regular multiplication map
A^d -> A with image J_lambda. Its rank is less than N precisely when J_lambda
is proper. In a proper quotient all x_i become lambda_i, so B/J_lambda=C.
In particular, the horizontal rank is exactly N-1 at every support point.

Write B=product_mu B_mu into its Artinian local factors. At mu=lambda,
J_lambda projects to the maximal ideal m_lambda. At mu != lambda, at least one
coordinate differs; T_i then has nonzero residue and is a unit in B_mu. Thus
J_lambda projects to the free rank-one module B_mu there.

The kernel K of B^d -> J_lambda is a B-submodule. A C-basis of K also generates
K as a B-module: its C-span is all K and C is contained in B. Consequently

    B^r --U--> B^d --> J_lambda --> 0

is a genuine finite presentation even though its generators can be redundant.
The d by d minors generate Fitt_0^B(J_lambda). Fitting ideals commute with
projection to product factors. For a free rank-one module Fitt_0(B_mu)=0,
whereas Fitt_0(0)=B_mu. It follows that

    Fitt_0^B(J_lambda)
      = Fitt_0^(B_lambda)(m_lambda) times product_(mu != lambda) 0.

Thus a nonzero global minor occurs exactly when the selected local Fitting
ideal is nonzero. No other factor can create a false positive.

Wiebe's theorem identifies that condition with the selected local ring being
an Artinian complete intersection. It does not require the chosen maximal
ideal generators to be minimal. This precise nonminimal version is also
stated in Definition 5.2 and Proposition 5.3 of the primary research paper
[Brochard–Mezard, About de Smit's question on flatness](https://imag.umontpellier.fr/~brochard/recherche/desmitconj_published.pdf).
See also [Simon–Strooker, sections 2.6–2.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703880).

## Fixed embedding versus intrinsic complete intersection

Let S=R_m_lambda; it is regular local of dimension d with residue field C.
The Artinian ring A_lambda is already complete. Its completed presentation is
S_hat=C[[t_1,...,t_d]] -> A_lambda. Let e be its embedding dimension. Choose
the first e coordinate images to minimally generate its maximal ideal. Each
remaining coordinate image is a power series in the first e. Subtracting
these series gives a formal change of coordinates, since its linear part is
invertible. The kernel consequently has the form

    (z_(e+1),...,z_d) + J C[[z_1,...,z_d]],

where C[[z_1,...,z_e]]/J is a minimal Cohen presentation of A_lambda.
An intrinsic Artinian CI has J generated by e regular elements. Therefore
the kernel in S_hat has d regular generators. Faithful flatness of completion
preserves the minimal number of generators of I S, so mu_S(I S)=d. Its height
is d, and S is Cohen–Macaulay; hence d minimal generators form a regular
sequence. Conversely a quotient by a regular sequence is intrinsically CI.
This bridge does not assume a preferred coordinate presentation or discard
linear defining equations when e<d.

The final global step is the separately audited Mohan Kumar theorem. Once its
all-local-to-global hypothesis is matched, global d generators of the
height-d ideal form a regular sequence in the Cohen–Macaulay polynomial ring.
No extra inference about globalization is supplied by the determinant test
itself.

## Boundary cases and falsification attempts

- **Redundant generators:** C[t]/(t^3), coordinates (t,t^2), yields a nonzero
  two by two minor t^2. Its embedding in C[x,y] has ideal (y-x^2,x^3), a
  regular sequence. Adding zero or dependent coordinate generators does not
  change the Fitting ideal.
- **Reduced singleton:** B=C and every T_i=0. Then K=B^d, U can be identity,
  and the determinant is one. The maximal ideal is zero and Fitt_0(0)=B,
  so this case passes correctly.
- **Multiple supports with nilpotents:** B=C[x,y]/(x,y)^2 times C, coordinates
  (x,1) and (y,0), gives zero minors at (0,0) and a minor equal to the field
  idempotent at (1,0). The nonselected factor gives no false positive.
- **Gorenstein is insufficient:** C[x,y,z]/(x^2,y^2,xz,yz,z^2-xy) is Artinian
  Gorenstein but has five independent quadratic relations in embedding
  dimension three. All 165 tested three by three minors vanish.
- **Arbitrary basis and no unit vector:** every computed example also recovers
  its full algebra from the multiplication matrices after a simultaneous
  shear (nontrivial when N>1), using only matrix products and flattened
  independence.
- **Cyclic hypothesis:** the explicit regular-representation promise is valid.
  It must not be replaced by the claim that commuting N by N matrices with
  dim B=N are automatically regular. For M_x=E_12,M_y=E_13 on C^3, B has
  dimension three but every vector has an orbit spanning at most two
  dimensions; no cyclic vector exists. The algebra extraction itself still
  works, but this example refutes that possible unstated implication.

## Actionable clarifications

1. A minor is computed **in the commutative algebra B** and produces an N by N
   matrix. Nonzero means some entry/coefficient is nonzero. It does not mean
   invertible, nonzero scalar determinant, or nonzero block determinant.
   Counterexample to those interpretations: B=C[t]/(t^2), d=1, T=M_t. The
   kernel generator is M_t and the algebra minor M_t is nonzero, while its
   ordinary scalar determinant is zero. Its only eigenvalue is also zero.
2. Compute ker(B^d -> B) using the recovered matrix algebra and its
   multiplication table. The vector-space kernel of [T_1 ... T_d] in the
   underlying regular module cannot be reinterpreted as algebra coefficients
   without an identification of that module with B. Such an identification
   need not be given, and is unnecessary for the stated algebra-kernel route.
3. Include the identity matrix/constant monomial, and retain the explicit
   regular multiplication input promise if any claim about module cyclicity
   or dimension N is made.

These are interpretation safeguards, not counterexamples to the precise
algebra-valued formulation. No circularity was found: B is recovered directly;
the kernel computation is linear algebra in B; product decomposition is used
to prove the criterion, not assumed as computational input.

## Reproducible computation

Run `python3 check_fitting.py` in this folder. The dependency-free script uses
exact rational arithmetic. The saved output is `check_results.json`.
All nine adversarial examples meet the expected CI/non-CI conclusion, including
d=0, and the generation bound is checked after common changes of basis.

Strongest verified result: the proposed local criterion, including the fixed
regular embedding bridge, is valid with the hypotheses stated above. Remaining
local gap: none. Global theorem applicability is assigned to the separate
source audit by the parent task.
