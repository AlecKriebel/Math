## Literal claim and equivalence relation

For actual smooth or locally flat PL embeddings S^2 in S^4, let X be the compact exterior S^4 minus an open tubular neighborhood. The open complement deformation retracts onto X. The question is whether isomorphic *unmarked full* homotopy 2-types imply ordinary homotopy equivalence of the complement spaces. The invariant consists of G=pi_1(X), the G-action on A=pi_2(X), and the first Postnikov class k in H^3(G;A). An isomorphism means a group isomorphism phi and a phi-semilinear module isomorphism psi carrying k to k'; agreement of abstract groups A alone, or (G,A) alone, is insufficient.

The target does not ask for classification of embeddings, boundary marked homotopy equivalence, homeomorphism, diffeomorphism, simple homotopy equivalence, or recovery of a meridian. Kirby explicitly observes that inequivalent Gluck-related knots can have homeomorphic exteriors. Such a pair is not a negative answer to the homotopy question.

Lomonaco Section X asks precisely about smooth (or locally flat PL) 2-knots. Theorem 10.1 assumes BOTH knots are quasi-aspherical, meaning H_3 of each universal cover vanishes. His proof passes to 3-dimensional CW models, realizes the full algebraic 2-type isomorphism, and then uses vanishing of H_3 to obtain all universal-cover homology isomorphisms. Theorem 10.2 has the separate hypotheses of domination by connected 3-dimensional CW complexes and H_3(universal cover)=0 for BOTH spaces. Theorem 10.4 includes finitely ended knots in the QA class; it does not assert that every infinitely ended knot is non-QA.

## Geometric data that cannot be inserted silently

An actual compact 2-knot exterior is an oriented compact 4-manifold with boundary S^2 x S^1 and ordinary integral homology of S^1. Its geometric meridian is the S^1 boundary loop, defines a specified infinite cyclic subgroup, generates H_1, and normally generates the group. The boundary inclusion is injective on pi_1 (because its generator maps to an infinite-order element), and is usually NOT surjective: surjectivity from Z would force the group cyclic. The tuple (G,A,k) as stated in the source is not supplied with the boundary map, a geometric meridian conjugacy class, a relative fundamental class, or an oriented degree-one map of pairs.

Normal generation of an abstract element t is a necessary algebraic condition for a meridian, but cannot by itself be used as a theorem that t is the geometric meridian of a specified smooth exterior in S^4. A group realization theorem, a realization with specified peripheral pair (G,<t>), realization of a full 2-type, and a pair of realizations with different homotopy types are four distinct tasks. A conditional argument that assumes the appropriate geometric realization may be correct while leaving the literal problem untouched.

Homotopy 2-type realization by a map on 3-dimensional CW models does not give a degree-one map (X,boundary X)->(Y,boundary Y). Degree and boundary behavior are additional constraints. No use of duality across a candidate map is legitimate without establishing those constraints or obtaining the needed universal-cover homology isomorphism by an independent argument.
