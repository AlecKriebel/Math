## Original and classical statements

1. **2026 Kirby survey**, Problem 4.36, printed pp.219–220. [Author-hosted full PDF](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf). The complete question and all three remarks were read, including visual inspection of those two pages. The invariant includes the first k-invariant. The source asks for homotopy type of actual 2-knot complements, not classification of marked boundary pairs. It notes the quasi-aspherical result, non-quasi-aspherical examples, and the distinction from both knot isotopy and the quadratic 2-type problem for closed finite-group 4-manifolds.
2. **S. J. Lomonaco, The Homotopy Groups of Knots I: How to Compute the Algebraic 2-Type**, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 95 (1981), 349–390. [Author-hosted full scan](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/lomonaco/5knots/Lomonaco-Pacific-Journal-Math.pdf). Section X, printed pp.373–375, gives the exact smooth/locally flat PL question, Theorems 10.1–10.2 on the quasi-aspherical subclass, Theorem 10.3 on relative finite splittings, and Theorem 10.4 on known subclasses. The relevant scan pages were inspected. These theorems are prior work, not results of this attempt.
3. **F. González-Acuña and J. M. Montesinos, Quasiaspherical knots with infinitely many ends**, Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici 58 (1983), 257–263. [Publisher archive scan](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1983%3A58%3A%3A19). The indexed first page explicitly states the relative splitting criterion and announces counterexamples to the proposed converse between infinitely many ends and failure of quasi-asphericity. Direct full-PDF retrieval returned a CAPTCHA HTML page; the full paper was not read. Only the title, opening theorem statements and announced existence conclusion are used here. The finite-module deduction in OBSTRUCTION.md does not rely on this paper's proof.
4. **J. A. Hillman, Four-Manifolds, Geometries and Knots**, Geometry & Topology Monographs 5 (2002), revised 2022; arXiv math/0212142v3. [Full current PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212142v3). Section 14.6 gives the infinite-ended knot-group presentation used in the calculation; Section 14.7 emphasizes weight classes and their geometric significance. The group is a known 2-knot group. Our calculation for the particular subgroup generated by t is conditional on the specified group-pair identification for any particular exterior; normal generation alone is not treated as that identification.

## Recent primary literature

5. **M. Jabłonowski, Fundamental Quandles Do Not Determine the First Postnikov Invariant of 2-Knots**, arXiv 2608.03818v1, 4 August 2026. [Full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03818v1). Theorem 4.6 states that no compatible group/module isomorphism carries one first k-invariant to the other. Its examples consequently have different full homotopy 2-types. They do not settle Kirby 4.36. This audit does not purport to independently certify the entirety of that preprint.
6. **A. Conway and D. Kasprowski, 4-Manifolds with a Given Boundary**, arXiv 2510.18836v2, 27 August 2026. [Full current PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18836v2). Introduction and Theorems 1.1, 1.3 and 2.8 require surjectivity of the boundary fundamental group. Since the boundary of a 2-knot exterior is S2×S1, this restricts an application to cyclic knot groups. It cannot be invoked as a general noncyclic classification theorem.
