# Independent mathematical reconstruction before current freeze

This note was produced after the source-first seal, from the full original partial artifact and the already-read primary source. It is not a current-candidate verdict.

For a negative stopped piece of length d beginning at backward elapsed time a, its endpoint sum in the unreversed signed concatenation is W(a+d). The backward stitched path traverses this same piece in the opposite order and therefore has value

R(a+u) = W(a)+W(a+d)-W(a+d-u), 0<=u<=d.

Subtracting W(a+u) gives the difference of the increments [W(a+d)-W(a+d-u)] and [W(a+u)-W(a)]. Both increments have length <=c. If a+u<=H and d<=c, then all four arguments lie in [0,H+c]. Consequently the factor-two modulus bound follows deterministically, simultaneously for every H. The bound is not a claim that reversed stopped pieces are Brownian: the Brownian W is made from SIGNED FORWARD stopped pieces, in the independently reordered negative-index family. Independence of full stopped-pair objects and the standard strong-Markov concatenation lemma are the operative stochastic input. The nonnegative family determines the positive X half and is independent of the negative family.

A Brownian increment with endpoint separation <=c lies in a length-2c grid interval. Its absolute value is bounded by twice the supremum relative to that interval's left endpoint. The reflection principle and Gaussian Chernoff tail bound produce 4(N+2) exp(−r²/(16c)) for horizon cN+c, with N=2^m. At r=8 sqrt(cm), this is 4(2^m+2)e^(−4m), a summable sequence. Borel–Cantelli and monotonicity extend the bound from dyadic radii to all large radii. No independence between different grid windows or between modulus events is needed.

Stitching the Brownian W negative half to the independent Brownian positive half produces Z with the 2BM(0) law. The path discrepancy grows no faster than a sample-dependent constant times sqrt(log(2+H)). On a scaled compact interval [−L,L], division by sqrt(r) makes that error tend to zero. Brownian scaling fixes the law of scaled Z for each r, so convergence of scaled X in the uniform topology follows. The same-space almost-sure vanishing discrepancy is stronger than the probability convergence required for the weak-law comparison.

The exact gap is unchanged: an altered path inside each negative piece and its weak scaling limit do not construct any finite origin on the original path. Additional law-level boundary check: only stopped pairs are assumed independent. To verify the signed forward negative concatenation law, for each fixed J attach an independent Brownian continuation after the first J pieces on an auxiliary extension. Repeated strong-Markov splicing gives Brownian law. On [0,H], this finite splice and the explicit infinite W agree whenever A_J>=H. Divergence makes the complementary probability vanish, so their finite-window laws agree in the limit. The continuation is a proof device; the explicit W uses only the original stopped negative pieces. Zero durations require no thinning or conditioning on selected indices in this argument. This separates admissibility under sign reversal from the false assertion that an arbitrary stopped-pair distribution itself is sign invariant.
