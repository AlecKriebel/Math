# Independent smooth-geometric verification of PR48

The independent mechanism was recorded before the original review was read. The complete submitted argument and all original scientific files were read afterward. These proofs verify an unresolved partial; they supply neither an example resolving KP-4.85 nor a theorem ruling out every such example. Classical perfectness and the stated published uniform-perfectness results remain imported theorems with their exact hypotheses verified against primary sources.

## Compact-total-support convention and displacement

Let D(N) mean the smooth diffeomorphisms of N joined to the identity by a smooth isotopy whose support, as a subset of N times the compact parameter interval, projects into one compact subset of N. Products, inverses and conjugates of finitely many elements remain in D(N): compose their isotopies, use smoothly parameterized inverses, and take the finite union of the resulting compact supports. This group is the standard compactly supported isotopy group in the smooth perfectness theorem. Tsuboi's 2012 introduction states perfectness at r not equal to dim(N)+1; the proof of Theorem 1.1(1) explicitly chooses a compact set containing both factor supports and their isotopy supports. Fukui--Rybicki--Yagasaki 1905.07664, introduction and definitions, expressly distinguishes all isotopies from compactly supported isotopies and states perfectness for the latter. Smooth r=infinity is permitted. This is an imported classical theorem, not a numerical deduction.

For N=M times R2 with M closed, perfectness yields h as a finite product of m commutators with factors in D(N). Choose a closed planar disk of radius R that contains all map supports in its interior; no uniform R or m is assumed. Choose L>2R. The swept set K={z+(Lt,0): |z|<=R, 0<=t<=m} is compact. A smooth bump eta with compact support and eta=1 on a neighborhood of K exists. The planar vector field eta(z)L partial_x is compactly supported and therefore complete. If z starts in the disk, its trajectory for 0<=t<=m is exactly z+(Lt,0), by uniqueness of the ODE solution and because this entire proposed trajectory stays in the constant-field region. Its time-one map u consequently takes the starting disk through m+1 pairwise disjoint closed disks. The isotopy id_M times u_t has one compact total support because M is compact. Endpoint supports of all the original factors are displaced to disjoint products under powers of F=id_M times u.

The algebra requires only pairwise commutation of the conjugate subgroups, not an injective product decomposition. For s_i=c_(i+1)...c_m one has s_(i-1)=c_i s_i, hence s_i s_(i-1)^(-1)=c_i^(-1) without moving c_i through later terms. Regrouping only different conjugate copies yields [C,F]=h product_i T^i(c_i^(-1)) and [A,B]=product_i T^i(c_i). Multiplication cancels corresponding inverse factors and gives h=[C,F][A,B]. Every factor is in D(N) by the finite-operation observation. Thus cl_D(N)(h)<=2. If h is the identity, length zero suffices. The construction depends on h's finite factorization; no single fixed displacement map or uniform support bound is claimed.

## Two sphere charts and all isotopy endpoints

Choose epsilon in (0,1/2), U={z>-1+epsilon}, V={z<1-epsilon} on the sphere. These are open disks covering S2. Choose a smooth latitude cutoff chi with chi=0 for z near -1 and chi=1 for z near +1, and with the transition contained in (-1+2epsilon,1-2epsilon). Then supp(chi) is compactly contained in U and supp(1-chi) is compactly contained in V. The two plateaus, rather than just pointwise vanishing at the poles, ensure compact containment.

Let f_t be a smooth isotopy in Diff^infinity_0(M), f_0=id and f_1=f. The inverse family f_t^(-1) is smooth: (t,x)->(t,f_t(x)) is a bijective map with invertible block-triangular derivative and a smooth inverse, also in boundary parameter charts. Set a_s(x,y)=(f_(s chi(y))(x),y) and b_s(x,y)=(f_s f_(s chi(y))^(-1)(x),y). Their inverses are (f_(s chi(y))^(-1)(x),y) and (f_(s chi(y)) f_s^(-1)(x),y), respectively. Both are smooth. A block-triangular derivative with the invertible first-coordinate derivative gives local invertibility; the displayed global inverses remove any surjectivity issue.

At s=0, both maps are the identity. For every s, a_s is the identity wherever chi=0. Wherever chi=1, b_s=f_s f_s^(-1)=id, so b_s is the identity there for the entire isotopy, not just at s=1. Their total supports lie in the fixed compact sets M times supp(chi) and M times supp(1-chi). Their endpoints satisfy b_1 a_1(x,y)=(f(x),y) in the convention that the rightmost map acts first. This uses no relation f_(t+u)=f_t f_u and no expression f_(1-chi) for b.

Charts identify M times U and M times V with M times R2. The preceding compact-isotopy result gives two commutators for each endpoint. Each factor's own isotopy has compact total support in its open chart. It is therefore the identity on a neighborhood of the chart boundary for every time, and extension by the identity is a smooth isotopy on the closed product. All four resulting ambient commutators lie in Diff^infinity_0(M times S2). This proves the bound four for f times id. It also proves the bound for every power, since f^n lies in Diff^infinity_0(M), with no uniform choice of its isotopy needed.

For homogeneous q of defect D, homogeneity gives q(id)=0 and q(h^(-1))=-q(h). The defect inequality applied to a conjugate of h^n differs from q(h^n) by at most 2D; division by n gives conjugation invariance. A commutator is (aba^(-1))b^(-1), so its q-value has magnitude at most D. A product of k commutators has magnitude at most kD+(k-1)D. Apply the bound with k<=4 to (f times id)^n to obtain |n q(f times id)|<=7D, hence q(f times id)=0. An abstract retraction onto a positive-genus surface group would send the four commutators to four surface commutators, contradicting BHW Corollary 1.3. The genus-one case is included in that primary theorem; its proof changes the fine curve graph to edges of intersection at most once in Section 7. No continuity of the proposed retraction is needed. Genus zero is outside this contradiction.

## General cutoff counterexample

For a smooth ambient isotopy F_t generated by V_t, the derivative of z->F_(tau(z))(z) is D_z F_tau plus (partial_t F_t at t=tau(z)) tensor d tau. In an autonomous rotation this is an invertible rotation times (I+V(z) tensor d tau). On S2 take tau=1/2+xy, which lies in [0,1] because |2xy|<=x2+y2<=1. At w=(0,1,0), the rotation field is v=(-1,0,0) and d tau(v)=-1. The derivative kills v. The independent angular-coordinate calculation writes the new angle as phi+1/2+(1-z2)sin(2phi)/2: its angular derivative at (phi,z)=(pi/2,0) is zero, its mixed derivative is zero, and its z-coordinate derivative is one. Thus tangent rank is exactly one. This is a genuine smooth map with a singular differential, and extending it on a sphere factor gives the same failure on a four-manifold product. It refutes the naive universal cutoff construction only.

## Full-group invariant probability and averaging

Fix a coordinate ball B whose closure lies in a larger coordinate ball C. In Euclidean coordinates choose a positive contraction factor lambda. A bump-supported radial vector field equal to a negative constant times the radial field on B contracts B by that factor at time one; it never uses lambda=0, which would destroy invertibility. Choose the contracted radius small enough for N disjoint targets inside C. For each target, a bump-supported vector field equal to its desired translation vector along the whole straight swept corridor moves the contracted ball into that target. C can be chosen as a Euclidean ball, so the small-radius corridor lies compactly inside C. Composing contraction and translation gives g_i in the full smooth identity component, with total support in C and with g_i(B) pairwise disjoint. Each map is independently constructed; no single diffeomorphism maps B onto N copies simultaneously.

If mu is an invariant Borel probability, every image has measure mu(B), and finite additivity on disjoint Borel sets gives N mu(B)<=1 for every positive integer N. Hence mu(B)=0. Compactness supplies a finite cover by such balls, contradicting total mass one. No absolute continuity, absence of atoms, positivity of a density, or regularity beyond Borel probability is used. For dimension one the same compactly supported contractions and translations work in intervals. For a connected zero-dimensional manifold, the single point admits its Dirac invariant probability, so the positive-dimension hypothesis is essential. A finite disconnected positive-dimensional manifold is handled componentwise with the same finite-cover contradiction.

Under the submission's explicit integrability assumptions, the cocycle defect is pointwise bounded by D_psi. Integrating this defect against a probability gives |E|<=D_psi. The other first-factor term is integral psi(c(f,gx)) dmu(x)=integral psi(c(f,y)) d(g_*mu)(y); subtracting Q(f) gives exactly the signed-measure error in (5). Its bound is not supplied by the target quasimorphism defect. Absence of an invariant probability blocks this implementation, not every modified averaging scheme and not every ambient quasimorphism.

## Handles, connected components, and remaining scope

The sum of two-critical-point Morse functions on S1 and S3 has product critical points and block-diagonal Hessians; indices add to 0,1,3,4. Distinct critical values may be arranged by rescaling the summands. Removing a maximum/minimum pair in the standard connected-sum Morse construction yields counts (1,k,0,k,1) for k copies. For k=0 the conventional example S4 has only indices 0 and 4. Thus the no-middle-handle hypothesis is supplied by explicit decompositions. Homology vanishing alone does not eliminate canceling handle pairs and does not establish this hypothesis.

A compact manifold has finitely many components because components are open in a locally connected manifold and compactness gives a finite subcover by connected charts. Identity-component diffeomorphisms cannot permute those components. Coordinate isotopies combine in a finite product, giving the asserted product of identity groups. For perfect factors, coordinate projections show cl(product)>=max(cl_i). Choose factorizations of maximum length, pad the shorter ones by identity commutators, and take coordinatewise pairs to give equality. Thus an unbounded width in such a finite product entails an unbounded width in at least one factor.

For a central extension, homogeneity and the defect inequality applied to (az)^n=a^n z^n prove exact additivity on commuting a,z. Vanishing on the central kernel makes q constant on fibers and therefore defines a homogeneous quasimorphism on the quotient; necessity is immediate. The subgroup inequality cl_G<=cl_H and the fragmentation upper bound cl<=2 frag have the directions stated. The latter uses usual isotopy fragmentation into locally isotopic pieces, not a blanket assumption that every ball-supported ambient-isotopic map is locally isotopic in its ball.

The strongest verified partial is the stabilized-element ambient bound and its two transfer consequences, together with the averaging obstruction and credited no-middle-handle examples. The exact gap remains an unbounded ordinary ambient commutator-length sequence in one full smooth closed orientable four-dimensional group, or a universal theorem forbidding such a sequence. The package does neither. These are known-technique consequences; no priority or novelty claim is promoted.
