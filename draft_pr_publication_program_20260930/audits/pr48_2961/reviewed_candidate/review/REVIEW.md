## 1. Exact target and source correction

The original K3 Problem 4.85, printed pp. 259–260, asks for a closed orientable smooth four-manifold whose **full smooth identity component** is not uniformly perfect. The question and remarks were checked visually. Webb's Oberwolfach Question 3 on p. 489 is the same target; record 30004403 is therefore a duplicate, not another independent attempt.

The note correctly distinguishes ordinary ambient commutator length from subgroup commutator length, stable commutator length, fragmentation and lengths in a covering group. Mather–Thurston perfectness applies at smooth regularity, so the question is unboundedness of finite ordinary lengths, not failure of perfectness.

For disconnected closed manifolds, the stated finite-product reduction is valid. The identity component cannot permute the finitely many components, and commutator length in a finite direct product of perfect groups is the maximum of the coordinate lengths, by projection in one direction and simultaneous padding in the other.

Sources: [K3 author version](https://math.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/surv-295-ruberman-watermarked-author-pdf.pdf), [Webb, OWR 8/2020, pp. 487–489](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46844).

Tsuboi's **published** Theorem 1.1(2), p. 142, gives the bound four for a compact even-dimensional manifold with a handle decomposition lacking middle-index handles. It includes dimension four and smooth regularity. The exceptional finite differentiability exponent is irrelevant here. The general new theorem in that paper, Theorem 1.2, starts in dimension six; the package does not apply it to dimension four.

The sum of two-critical-point Morse functions on \(S^1\) and \(S^3\) has exactly the required four critical points, of indices \(0,1,3,4\). The usual connected-sum handle construction gives the stated \((1,k,0,k,1)\) counts. Thus these are genuine examples in the cited class; no implication from \(H_2=0\) to a handle decomposition is being assumed. The K3 remark claiming no understood four-dimensional example besides \(S^4\) is too broad, as the older Webb report itself confirms.

Source: [Tsuboi, Comment. Math. Helv. 87 (2012), Theorem 1.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43279?nt=1).

## 2. Compression identity

Lemma 1 is correct, including the order of noncommuting factors within each copy of \(H\).

Write \(T(g)=FgF^{-1}\) and \(s_i=c_{i+1}\cdots c_m\). The decisive relation is
\[
 s_{i-1}=c_i s_i,\qquad
 s_i s_{i-1}^{-1}=c_i^{-1}.
\]
There is no illicit commutation of \(c_i\) with the later factors.

In the product \(C\,T(C^{-1})\), different subgroup indices may be regrouped because those subgroups commute. The index-zero factor is \(h\), the intermediate factors are \(T^i(c_i^{-1})\), and the last factor is \(T^m(c_m^{-1})\). Meanwhile \([A,B]\) is the product of \(T^i(c_i)\). Their product is \(h\).

This reasoning does not need the conjugate subgroups to form an injectively embedded direct product. Pairwise commutation suffices to validate the word identity. No direct-product uniqueness is implicitly required.

The statement agrees with Burago–Ivanov–Polterovich's strong-displacement bound, Theorem 2.2(i). The proof in the note gives the needed algebra explicitly.

Source: [Burago–Ivanov–Polterovich, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1412).

## 3. Compact support and the portable product

Lemma 2 legitimately applies to the group defined using isotopies with compact total support. The imported smooth perfectness theorem supplies a finite commutator decomposition in that group. There are only finitely many factor supports, and \(M\) is compact, so they fit inside \(M\times B_R\).

For a fixed finite number \(m\) of factors, the corridor through the first \(m\) translated planar disks is compact. A smooth vector field equal to \(L\partial_x\) on a neighborhood of that corridor and cut off outside a larger compact set exists. Its flow is complete and compactly supported. On the specified disks, its first \(m\) iterates really are translations, because their full trajectories remain in the region where the field is constant.

The resulting \(F=\operatorname{id}_M\times u\) has a compactly supported isotopy; compactness of \(M\) is essential here and is assumed. The subgroup generated by the original factors is supported in the original disk product. Its required conjugates have disjoint supports and commute. The factors' own isotopies need not remain inside \(B_R\) for this displacement argument; only their map supports are used at that stage.

The proof therefore gives at most two commutators in the stipulated ambient compact-isotopy group. BIP's product example and Theorem 1.18 give the same conclusion. No portability claim for the closed product \(M\times S^2\) is made.

Sources: [BIP, Definition 1.16 and Theorem 1.18](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1412), [Tsuboi, introduction, smooth perfectness convention](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43279?nt=1).

## 4. Two sphere cutoffs give the stated ambient bound

Proposition 3 is valid for every specified \(f\).

Choose enlarged hemispherical disks and a smooth latitude cutoff equal to zero near one pole and one near the other. Both support inclusions can be compact containments, even though neither disk is compact.

A smooth isotopy \(f_t\) from the identity to \(f\) has a smoothly parameterized inverse. Since the second coordinate is unchanged, both displayed maps \(a,b\) are smooth diffeomorphisms, with pointwise inverses. The identity \(b\circ a=f\times\operatorname{id}\) has the stated composition order and does not require \(f_t\) to be a one-parameter subgroup.

The isotopies in the note start at the identity. Outside \(\operatorname{supp}\chi\), \(a_s\) is the identity for every \(s\). Outside \(\operatorname{supp}(1-\chi)\), \(\chi=1\), so
\[
 b_s(x,y)=(f_s f_s^{-1}(x),y)=(x,y)
\]
for every \(s\). Their total supports are consequently compact subsets of \(M\times U\) and \(M\times V\), respectively.

Applying Lemma 2 in disk charts gives two commutators for each factor. Each commutator factor and its isotopy can be extended smoothly by the identity, because its total support is compactly contained in the chart. All four commutators belong to the full \(\operatorname{Diff}_0(M\times S^2)\). Thus the estimate is genuinely ambient and its smooth-isotopy requirements are satisfied.

The bound concerns the product subgroup only. The explicit rotation example correctly prevents applying the same construction to an arbitrary isotopy: at the specified sphere point the point-dependent-time map kills a nonzero tangent vector. An independent symbolic Jacobian calculation finds tangent rank one, rather than two.

## 5. Quasimorphism and transfer consequences

For a homogeneous quasimorphism of defect \(D\), conjugation invariance and inversion give a bound \(D\) on a single commutator. Adding the \(k-1\) product errors gives \((2k-1)D\) on a product of \(k\) commutators. Applying the four-commutator bound to all powers yields the stated \(7D/n\) bound and hence vanishing on the stabilized surface subgroup. This is a direct argument, not an appeal to an unproved converse of Bavard duality.

A retraction would send the ambient four-commutator factorization to one in the surface group, giving a uniform bound four. Bowden–Hensel–Webb Corollary 1.3 excludes that bound for every closed orientable surface of positive genus. The obstruction is to an abstract group retraction, so it does not depend on continuity of the proposed retraction.

Source: [Bowden–Hensel–Webb, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3](https://www.math.lmu.de/~hensel/papers/dagger_arxiv.pdf).

The averaging calculation is also correct. With the stated integrability assumptions, changing variables sends the first cocycle term to an integral against \(g_*\mu\). Subtracting \(Q(f)\) leaves precisely the displayed signed-measure error. The quasimorphism defect alone bounds only the separate error \(E(f,g)\). The note does not falsely claim that every possible noninvariant averaging construction must fail.

There is no invariant Borel probability measure for the full group in the stated positive-dimensional closed connected setting. A coordinate ball can be shrunk and moved by local smooth isotopies to arbitrarily many disjoint smaller balls in a larger chart. Invariance and disjointness force its measure to vanish. A finite such cover contradicts total mass one. This reasoning applies to atomic and singular measures as well as smooth densities.

Finally, homogeneous quasimorphisms are additive on commuting elements, so vanishing on the central kernel is exactly the condition for descent in the displayed central extension. The note preserves the required subgroup/ambient inequality direction. The fragmentation comparison is used in the standard isotopy-fragmentation setting and only as an upper bound. The main product proof supplies its local isotopies explicitly; it needs no assertion that an arbitrary ball-supported mapping class is locally isotopic to the identity.
