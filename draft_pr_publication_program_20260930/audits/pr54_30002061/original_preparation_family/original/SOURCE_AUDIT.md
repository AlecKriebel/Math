The full published [Adiprasito–Benedetti article](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10169029), Discrete & Computational Geometry 64 (2020), 608–626, DOI [10.1007/s00454-019-00137-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-019-00137-3), was retrieved. Its proof reduces to one original elementary collapse, then uses the previous endo-collapsibility corollary on the derived subdivision. It does not remove the extra subdivision.

The [arXiv version 1709.07930v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07930v1), posted 22 September 2017, numbers the result 4.0.3. Its displayed manuscript date is 2015, and its results previously belonged to a longer paper. These dates and theorem numbers are not conflated with the 2020 journal publication. The historical OWR statements are not automatically substituted for the final published wording.

## The reconstructed low-dimensional result is already known

The planar certificate in PARTIAL.md is elementary and carries no novelty claim. As an additional prior-art check identified during independent review, Adiprasito–Benedetti, [*Tight complexes in 3-space admit perfect discrete Morse functions*, arXiv:1202.3390v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3390v2), Lemma 2.5, states a stronger fact: a planar complex collapses to a subcomplex which is a deformation retract. The relevant statement and surrounding argument were read in the full downloaded preprint. The paper appeared in European Journal of Combinatorics 45 (2015), 71–84; the journal version numbers the corresponding planar lemma 2.9. The local disk-to-boundary-path case reconstructed here is subsumed by that known result.

Chillingworth's 1967 convex 3-dimensional work and its 1980 correction are credited background. The original article's primary bibliographic record and correction were located; its complete corrected proof is not re-audited in this package. The supplied certificate makes only its explicitly proved dimension ≤2 assertion. Existing three-dimensional results are not advertised as discoveries or confused with an all-dimensional relative theorem.

## Precise stalled mechanism

The elementary local-to-global reduction preserves the protected target by a direct coface argument. In the planar case a dual-tree order removes triangles and leaves a contractible graph, which is a tree and can be pruned relative to the protected path. In higher dimensions the leftover contractible complex is no longer a graph. No relative collapsing certificate for it is provided. Further proof search stopped after one substantive family rather than relabeling this known low-dimensional case as a general solution.

The proof does not infer noncollapsibility from nonshellability. In particular, Rudin's nonshellable tetrahedron triangulation is not a counterexample to the target. Nor is a general noncollapsible PL ball automatically a linear subdivision of one original simplex.

Bounded current searches for Hudson's problem, collapse preservation under subdivision, and the Lickorish/Chillingworth formulations located the credited extra-barycentric theorem and later citations, but no verified general resolution. This is not an exhaustive literature or priority certificate.
