## The actual prior theorem bodies

**AAH v2, Section 4, pp. 15–17.** Proposition 4.4 gives the translation cover from a translation surface decomposed into regular n-gons to the standard single/double n-gon. Theorem 4.5 gives regularity for a Platonic unfolding. These supply the double-pentagon framework; the current candidate must credit them rather than present that framework as a new construction. [Version-pinned primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04131v2)

**AAH v2, Section 5, pp. 17–18.** The source defines virtual-Weierstrass points and saddle connections, identifies regular edge midpoints, and equates virtual-Weierstrass symmetry with a regular midpoint of that kind. Proposition 5.1 reverses a connection by the rotational involution about its midpoint, obtaining a contradiction when a closed connection would force a fixed vertex. Theorem 5.2 applies this to odd vertex valence. The dodecahedron application is explicitly noted at the end of the section. Proposition 5.3 applies the every-saddle-is-virtual-Weierstrass assertion only to triangles and squares; it is **not** a theorem for arbitrary pentagonal connections. The current terminal-germ calculation is therefore a real necessary identification, not something that follows by importing Proposition 5.3 into the pentagon case. [Critical section](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.04131v2#S5)

**AAH v2, Section 9.3, pp. 34–36, Figure 10 and Proposition 9.1.** This is a classification into two unoriented affine saddle-connection families on the double pentagon. The proof normalizes a connection to a horizontal one using the lattice/cusp structure. It does not state Fuchs's later A0 angle condition or a dodecahedral graph-distance restriction. I inspected the full rendered Figure 10, not only its extracted caption. [Critical section](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.04131v2#S9.SS3)

**AAH v2, Section 10.2.1, p. 43.** The short base connection has a Weierstrass midpoint, so the closed-connection search in that family must be empty by Theorem 5.2. This identifies the old equal-endpoint obstruction, not a graph-distance-two theorem. The AAH short/long families are affine-orbit representatives, while Fuchs's word “short” means vertex-to-first-vertex; these uses must not be conflated. [Critical section](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.04131v2#S10.SS2.SSS1)
