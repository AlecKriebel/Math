# Source-only assessment (sealed before submission inspection)

The literal target is Fuchs, *Billiard Trajectories in Regular Polygons and Geodesics on Regular Polyhedra*, DOI 10.1007/s40598-020-00170-8, section 3, Conjecture 3.2. It says that a type A0 short dodecahedral geodesic cannot end at graph distance 2 from its starting vertex. Here distance is the minimum edge count in the dodecahedral graph, not Euclidean or intrinsic length. A short geodesic joins vertices, has no intervening vertex hit, and unfolds to a straight line through the prescribed sequence of adjacent regular pentagonal faces. No bounded length restriction appears in the conjecture. The length <120 and initial-angle <3*pi/10 conditions describe the preceding experiment.

Section 2.2 and Figure 8 define endpoint angles alpha and beta in an oriented polygon. Section 2.3 explicitly states that A0 is characterized by beta-alpha=2*pi/n without an alpha restriction. Thus for n=5 one must reproduce the directed endpoint-edge conventions giving beta-alpha=2*pi/5. A face-word, a coincident pair of terminal edge directions, or an arbitrary symmetry label is not a substitute unless this equivalence is proved. Section 3 says reflection in the initial face's vertex-angle-bisector plane interchanges A1 and A2. This is a statement about the types; it does not define A0 as an individual geodesic fixed by a half-turn.

Independent exact model planned: use vertices (±1,±1,±1), (0,±1/phi,±phi), (±1/phi,±phi,0), (±phi,0,±1/phi), phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. Edges are the pairs with squared distance 4/phi^2. Recover faces as supporting coplanar five-tuples (or simple chordless 5-cycles), verify 20 vertices, 30 edges, 12 regular pentagons, degree three, two faces per edge, and graph-distance histogram 1,3,6,6,3,1. Rational arithmetic in Q(sqrt(5)) suffices for this model. Derive any half-turn about an opposite-edge-midpoint axis from H=2*a*a^T/(a.a)-I, and check that it actually permutes the vertex and face sets.

For an exact counterexample, success requires a nonzero developed segment, exact positive ordered crossing times, strict crossing-interior parameters (unless explicitly justified boundary start/end edges), no intermediate vertex, correctly glued adjacency, terminal graph distance exactly 2, and the source A0 endpoint-angle condition. A finite candidate verifier can establish existence of one counterexample. It cannot prove the universal conjecture by checking finitely many paths. A symmetric extension through a vertex would require care: vertices are cone singularities and cannot be passed through as if regular points.

For a positive universal proof, success requires a rigorous invariant excluding all type A0 saddle connections at distance 2, including arbitrary length and repeated faces, or an equivalent exhaustion with a proved bound. A reduction that assumes an unproved symmetry property of every A0 path would transfer the central difficulty and is blocked without a new argument. Hashes establish byte identity only.
