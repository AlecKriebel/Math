## Frozen independent verdict

**No primary source retrieved in this bounded audit expressly announces a resolution of the exact three-pseudo-Anosov question. Consequently this family does not establish the first person to answer that question, nor certify that it remained open immediately before PR126. It also does not certify novelty.** A current search returning no explicit answer cannot perform either task.

The original package’s valid theorem is a literal existential result on the closed torus. It openly uses Wajnryb’s already-published braid-related pair and adds its conjugate as the third element. Its extra matrix entries, explicit dynamics explanation, and verification files give checkable exposition. They do not, by themselves, establish a substantive new theorem or the priority required for publication as a novel resolution. The historical issue is separate from whether published algebraic results already imply that extension; that additional published-content comparison belongs to the distinct algebraic family.

Do not describe Mortada, Szepietowski, Song, Ghaswala–McLeay, or Castel as the author of a known solution of this exact named question on the evidence below. Do not present the question as presently open merely because these retrieved papers do not expressly answer it.

## Exact target and chronology

[Wajnryb’s author-hosted chapter](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/mcgbook.pdf), Chapter 8, Section 2, printed page 124 / PDF page 131, asks for three distinct pseudo-Anosov elements with pairwise Artin relations. It immediately credits the referee with a length-three pair obtained from order-two/order-three elements, and discusses torus Anosov matrices. The chapter’s compact-surface convention is on printed page 122 / PDF page 129. The specific question does not state a faithful Artin embedding, minimal three-element generating set, prescribed higher genus, or nonconjugacy requirement. The embedding questions around it are distinct. I visually inspected the complete target page and its surrounding paragraph.

The published chapter is Bronislaw Wajnryb, “Relations in the mapping class group,” *Problems on Mapping Class Groups and Related Topics*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 74 (2006), 115–120, DOI [10.1090/pspum/074/2264536](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/074/2264536). Crossref metadata independently confirms that DOI, title, author, year and pagination; the response is saved here. Mortada’s text places the neighboring two-element question on published page 117. The manuscript’s different pagination must not be mistaken for a different problem.

1. **Jamil Mortada, “Artin Relations in the Mapping Class Group.”** [arXiv:1008.0124v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0124v3), revised 23 September 2011; *Geometriae Dedicata* 158 (2012), 283–300, DOI [10.1007/s10711-011-9633-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10711-011-9633-9), online 24 June 2011. The introduction, PDF page 1, explicitly identifies the two-element question as its target. Theorems 1.1–1.4, PDF pages 2–3, construct pairs of products of Dehn twists satisfying various alternating lengths and embeddings of dihedral Artin groups. The complete text has no pseudo-Anosov assertion; its final Corollary 5.1, PDF page 21, again concerns a pair of twist powers. This is an answer to a neighboring question, not an explicit solution of the three-element pseudo-Anosov question. I visually checked the first page against text extraction. The arXiv reference’s manuscript pagination differs from the independently verified published chapter pagination.

2. **Błażej Szepietowski, “Embedding the braid group in mapping class groups.”** *Publicacions Matemàtiques* 54 (2010), 359–368, DOI [10.5565/PUBLMAT_54210_04](https://doi.org/10.5565/PUBLMAT_54210_04). [Full institutional PDF](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/pubmat/02141493v54n2/02141493v54n2p359.pdf). Printed page 360, Definition 1/Question 2, explicitly identifies nongeometric braid-group embeddings as the problem being answered. Theorem 6, printed page 362, lifts embeddings from nonorientable surfaces; Corollary 8, printed page 364, uses crosscap transpositions. Theorem 10 and its proof, printed page 366, describe images supported on lifted subsurfaces and preserving a curve. It does not assert that these generators are global pseudo-Anosov maps or resolve the named three-element problem. This is a genuine follow-up to Wajnryb with a different target, rather than evidence of the first priority claimed here.

3. **Yongjin Song, “The braidings in the mapping class groups of surfaces.”** [arXiv:1205.1606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1606); published *J. Korean Math. Soc.* 50 (2013), 865–877, as cited in Ghaswala–McLeay’s published bibliography. Lemma 3.7 and its proof, preprint PDF page 9, establish a nongeometric embedding using mapping classes with specified local subsurface support; Theorems 3.8–3.10, PDF pages 9–10, concern a braided monoidal functor and stable homology. None is a pseudo-Anosov braid-triple theorem. The complete extracted text has no pseudo-Anosov assertion. A nongeometric generator need not be pseudo-Anosov, so the adjective alone cannot bridge this scope gap.

4. **Tyrone Ghaswala and Alan McLeay, “Mapping class groups of covers with boundary and braid group embeddings.”** *Algebraic & Geometric Topology* 20 (2020), 239–278, DOI [10.2140/agt.2020.20.239](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2020.20.239). [Full author PDF](https://tyjgh.github.io/coverswithboundary.pdf), also [arXiv:1804.10609](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10609). Printed pages 242–243, Theorem 1.3, construct nongeometric braid embeddings with standard generators represented by chain twists. Section 5.4, especially Proposition 5.6, gives a chain/bracelet condition yielding a braid-related pair. Questions 5.7 and 5.10, printed pages 274–275, ask about the converse for chain twists and classification of embeddings. The paper’s explicit Wajnryb attribution concerns his nongeometric-embedding question. It supplies no asserted solution or historical credit for the exact target here. Do not silently turn chain twists into globally pseudo-Anosov generators; boundary/reduction curves require an actual separate argument.

5. **Fabrice Castel, “Geometric representations of the braid groups.”** [arXiv:1104.3698](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3698), first submitted 2011; published as *Astérisque* 378 (2016), confirmed by the [publisher’s sample](https://smf.emath.fr/sites/default/files/2023-09/smf_ast_378__sample.pdf). Preprint Section 5, printed/PDF page 40, explicitly assumes an even strand number at least six and target genus at most half the strand number. Theorem 5.1 concludes that irreducible representations in that setting are cyclic, including the pseudo-Anosov case discussed in Section 5.3. These restrictions were visually checked. The theorem cannot be applied to the two-generator length-three braid group or the nonfaithful triangular presentation implicit in PR126. It is neither a contradiction to the verified torus construction nor an explicit solution of its named target.

6. **Susumu Hirose and Naoyuki Monden, “On generating mapping class groups by pseudo-Anosov elements.”** [arXiv:2310.13272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13272), first submitted 20 October 2023. Theorem 1.2, PDF page 2, gives two or three conjugate pseudo-Anosov generators of higher-genus mapping class groups under stated genus bounds. Its generation conclusion does not assert that every pair satisfies an alternating Artin relation. The ordinary braid relation recalled in Section 2, PDF page 3, concerns Dehn twists. Thus the title’s pseudo-Anosov generators are not evidence of this braid-triple question having been explicitly answered.

7. **Dan Margalit, “Problems, Questions, and Conjectures about Mapping Class Groups.”** [arXiv:1806.08773](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08773), first submitted 22 June 2018. This general problem survey is selective, discussing other main topics; complete-text searches found neither the Wajnryb name nor this braid-relation question. Its omission is not an authoritative current-status update for a question it does not discuss. Likewise the author’s separately retrieved research prospectus is not a complete registry of Wajnryb’s problems.
