# Universal verification of the finite-jet lifting step

This is a reconstruction, with no dependence on earlier review verdicts or
finite numerical success. Mathematical credit belongs to van den Essen,
Maubach and Vénéreau. The exact target is the surjectivity of coefficient
reduction on **existing** polynomial automorphisms of determinant exactly one,
for every commutative unital Q-algebra R and every m,n>=1.

Write B=R[t], A=B/(t^m), and x=(x_1,...,x_n). An automorphism fixes the
coefficient ring; t is a parameter, not a coordinate being transformed.
Composition below is ordinary point-map composition f o g=f(g(x)). One may
instead use the opposite convention for coordinate-ring maps: the argument
uses associativity and first-order addition, and surjectivity is unchanged.

## Coefficients over rings with zero divisors

There is a direct-sum decomposition of R-modules

    A[x] = R[x] + t R[x] + ... + t^(m-1) R[x].

It is a direct sum because the ideal (t^m) in R[x,t] consists exactly of
polynomials whose t-coefficients below m vanish. This remains true over
nonreduced rings and rings with zero divisors. In particular, the implication

    t^r F = 0 modulo t^(r+1)  =>  F = 0 in R[x],  0<=r<m,

is a statement about coefficients, not cancellation of t in A. The parameter
t itself is usually a zero divisor in A.

For r>=1 let K_r be the subgroup of determinant-one A-automorphisms congruent to the identity
modulo t^r. A map in K_r has unique expression

    rho(x) = x + t^r F(x) modulo t^(r+1), F in R[x]^n.

Substitution in a polynomial preserves congruences modulo t^r, by the
polynomial identity P(z+w)-P(z) belonging to the ideal generated by the
entries of w. Thus composition of two such maps adds F; inverse negates F.
Terms from substituting the perturbation into t^r F have order at least 2r.
The exact inequality used is 2r>=r+1. At r=1 equality occurs: t^2 cross
terms do not vanish modulo t^3, and the proof does not discard them there.
It recomputes the next coefficient from the actual residual automorphism.

The Jacobian matrix is I+t^r JF modulo t^(r+1). The permutation expansion
of its determinant has linear term t^r tr(JF), and all terms with at least
two perturbation entries have order >=2r. Therefore

    det J rho = 1 + t^r div(F) modulo t^(r+1).

For a determinant-one rho, unique t-coefficient extraction gives div(F)=0
in R[x]. There is no reducedness or domain assumption in this deduction.

## The finite correction lemma, independently justified

Every polynomial divergence-free vector F is a finite sum h_j v_j, where
v_j is a rational constant vector and h_j(x+s v_j)=h_j(x) identically.
Here is the algebraic reason, included to make the induction self-contained.

For n=1, derivative zero implies constant, because each positive integer
is a unit in R. For n>=2, integrate the first component in x_n and subtract
the vector (partial_n H)e_1-(partial_1 H)e_n. Its divergence is zero; the
first component becomes zero. Continue for i=2,...,n-1. The last component
has derivative zero in x_n, so is already a shear in direction e_n.
Termwise integration divides only by positive integers.

For each pair (i,n), decompose H as a polynomial in x_i,x_n with coefficients
in the other variables. Its degree-d homogeneous part is a finite linear
combination of (x_i+q x_n)^d for q=0,...,d. The coefficient matrix has entries
binomial(d,k) q^k. Its determinant is the product of the nonzero rational
binomial coefficients and the Vandermonde determinant of distinct integers;
it is invertible over Q, hence also over every Q-algebra. Coefficient zero
divisors cause no difficulty because this inversion happens over Q, not R.
A term c(other variables)(x_i+q x_n)^d gives the curl vector

    c d (x_i+q x_n)^(d-1) (q e_i-e_n).

The linear form and all other variables are fixed by translation in that
direction. All these expansions are finite. No completion, analytic limit,
formal exponential, or Jacobian conjecture is invoked.

For one shear datum (h,v), the polynomial maps over R[t]

    Phi(x)=x+t^r h(x)v,  Psi(x)=x-t^r h(x)v

are mutual inverses: invariance holds after substituting s=t^r h(x), so
h(Phi(x))=h(x)=h(Psi(x)). Their Jacobian matrices have the form
I+t^r v (grad h)^T. The determinant of I+a b^T over a commutative ring is
1+b^T a. This follows directly by determinant multilinearity: terms with
two columns contributed by the rank-one part cancel or have repeated
proportional columns, with no division or rank argument over a field.
The determinant is one because v dot grad(h)=0. Each correction is therefore
a genuine special polynomial automorphism with a finite polynomial inverse
before reduction, rather than a Jacobian-one endomorphism presumed invertible.

The finite product Phi_r of the shears for F satisfies

    Phi_r(x)=x+t^r F(x) modulo t^(r+1).

The terms of order 2r and above are retained in the polynomial product and
its inverse. Reduction identifies its rth coefficient with F.

## Arbitrary constant automorphisms and order of induction

For the supplied automorphism sigma in SAut_A, reduce both sigma and its
supplied inverse modulo t. The resulting sigma_0 and sigma_0^(-1) are
mutual polynomial inverses over R. The determinant reduces to one. Extend
both constantly to R[t]. This preserves a possibly nonlinear, possibly
wild automorphism in full. It does not factor sigma_0 into elementary maps.

Set accumulated lift L_1=sigma_0 and residual rho_1=sigma_0^(-1) o sigma.
Then sigma=bar(L_1) o rho_1, with rho_1 in K_1. At stage r<m, obtain Phi_r
as above and set

    L_(r+1) = L_r o Phi_r,
    rho_(r+1) = bar(Phi_r)^(-1) o rho_r.

These choices give the exact equality

    bar(L_(r+1)) o rho_(r+1)
      = bar(L_r) o bar(Phi_r) o bar(Phi_r)^(-1) o rho_r = sigma.

Both residual factors have determinant one. Their rth coefficients are
opposite, so rho_(r+1) lies in K_(r+1). This is an actual composition in A[x],
not its first-order truncation. The finite loop r=1,...,m-1 ends in K_m,
which is the identity subgroup because t^m=0 in A. Thus L_m is the desired
special polynomial automorphism over R[t]. A finite product of polynomial
automorphisms has a polynomial inverse, the reverse product of the known
inverses; degree growth does not negate finiteness.

For m=1 the constant lift suffices. For n=1 there is a separate direct
verification: derivative exactly one forces sigma(x)=x+a(t) over a
Q-algebra, so lifting its coefficient polynomial a(t) gives a translation.
The zero ring, if permitted by the Q-algebra convention, is vacuous and does
not create an exception. Arbitrarily many generators in R and lack of
Noetherianity have no effect: each input polynomial has finite support and
the construction uses only finitely many operations.

## Scope boundary and strongest verified claim

This establishes the exact Q-algebra theorem without imported full-journal
proofs or empirical assumptions. Outside characteristic zero, derivative
zero need not mean variable independence, integration denominators may fail,
and rational interpolation may fail. Over F_p, x -> x+t x^p modulo t^2 is
an invertible determinant-one map (inverse x-t x^p), but it cannot come from
a determinant-one automorphism of F_p[t][x]: over that domain a one-variable
polynomial automorphism is linear, and determinant one forces a translation.
This boundary example does not refute the stated theorem.

Nothing here resolves the Jacobian conjecture, asserts tameness of the
constant part, lifts every infinite formal t-series by one finite polynomial,
or answers the remaining non-Q-algebra cases. There is no gap in the stated
finite induction after the finite correction lemma above.
