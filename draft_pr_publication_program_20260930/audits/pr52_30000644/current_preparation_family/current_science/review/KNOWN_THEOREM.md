# The reduction map was already proved surjective

**Disposition: already solved in the original literature. No new solution or novelty claim. Independent source/proof review pending.**

The exact target 30000644 / OWR-1452-008 has an affirmative answer. The original Oberwolfach report, printed p. 26, places the following immediately after Question 1:

> Answer 1 (van den Essen, Maubach). YES

The report then gives the two-variable, square-zero proof and cites the general result of **Arno van den Essen, Stefan Maubach and Stéphane Vénéreau**, *The special automorphism group of R[t]/(t^m)[x1,…,xn] and coordinates of a subring of R[t][x1,…,xn]*, Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra **210** (2007), 141–146, DOI [10.1016/j.jpaa.2006.09.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2006.09.013). The source's question was extracted without its answer.

The following is a self-contained verification of the credited theorem, expanding the elementary mechanism in the report to arbitrary n and m. It is not represented as a transcription or a line-by-line audit of the complete JPAA article, whose full text was not recovered in this search.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let R be any commutative unital ring containing Q, let m≥1, and let Y=(y1,…,yn), n≥1. Set A=R[t]/(t^m). For a coefficient ring B, write SAut_B B[Y] for its B-algebra automorphisms with Jacobian determinant exactly one. Here SAut has this explicitly specified determinant-one meaning, not a potentially different definition in terms of a group generated by additive-group actions.

**Credited theorem.** The coefficient-reduction homomorphism

\[
 \operatorname{SAut}_{R[t]} R[t][Y]
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{SAut}_{A} A[Y]
 \tag{1}
\]

is surjective. No reducedness, field, Noetherian, or finite-generation assumption on R is needed.

## 2. Divergence-free vectors are sums of integrable shears

Call a pair (h,v) a shear datum if h∈R[Y], v∈R^n is constant with respect to Y, and

\[
 h(Y+sv)=h(Y)\quad\text{in }R[Y,s].
 \tag{2}
\]

The pairs used below have v with rational entries. For any a∈R[t],

\[
 \Phi_{a,h,v}(Y)=Y+a h(Y)v
 \tag{3}
\]

is a polynomial automorphism over R[t], with inverse obtained by replacing a by −a. Its Jacobian determinant is one: the Jacobian matrix is I+a v(∇h)^T, whose determinant is 1+a(∇h)^T v; the latter directional derivative is zero by (2). Alternatively its elementary two-coordinate form below verifies the same determinant directly. These identities hold over arbitrary commutative rings.

**Lemma.** If F=(f1,…,fn)∈R[Y]^n satisfies

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\partial_i f_i=0,
 \tag{4}
\]

then F is a finite sum of vectors h v with shear data satisfying (2).

**Proof.** For n=1, the derivative condition says f1 is independent of y1: every positive integer is invertible in R. Thus (f1,e1) is a shear datum.

For n≥2, eliminate the first n−1 components successively. At step i, integrate the current i-th component with respect to yn to obtain a polynomial H_i∈R[Y] with ∂n H_i=f_i. Termwise integration is legitimate over a Q-algebra. Subtract the vector

\[
 (\partial_n H_i)e_i-(\partial_i H_i)e_n.
 \tag{5}
\]

This sets component i to zero, changes only component n otherwise, and preserves zero divergence. After the last step, only a vector g e_n remains, with ∂n g=0. It is a shear datum because g does not depend on yn.

It suffices to decompose each vector (5). Regard H_i as a polynomial in yi and yn with coefficients in R[Y\{yi,yn}]. Each homogeneous binary monomial of degree d≥1 is a Q-linear combination of

\[
 (y_i+q y_n)^d,\qquad q=0,1,\ldots,d.
 \tag{6}
\]

Indeed, their coefficient matrix is a Vandermonde matrix multiplied by the nonzero rational binomial coefficients, hence invertible over Q. Degree-zero terms contribute zero to (5).

For a summand H=a(Y\{yi,yn})(yi+qyn)^d, vector (5) is

\[
 a d(y_i+qy_n)^{d-1}(q e_i-e_n).
 \tag{7}
\]

The coefficient a and linear form yi+qyn are both unchanged by translation in direction q e_i−e_n. Thus (7) is h v with the required shear datum. All decompositions are finite. ∎

## 3. Lifting one successive t-coefficient

Suppose ρ∈SAut_A A[Y] is the identity modulo t^r, where 1≤r<m. Write its unique next coefficient as

\[
 \rho(Y)=Y+t^rF(Y)\pmod{t^{r+1}},\qquad F\in R[Y]^n.
 \tag{8}
\]

Expanding the determinant to this order gives

\[
 \det J\rho=1+t^r\sum_i\partial_i f_i\pmod{t^{r+1}}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Terms with two factors of t^r vanish modulo t^(r+1), because 2r≥r+1. Since det Jρ=1, the vector F has divergence zero over R.

Apply the lemma to write F=Σ_s h_s v_s. Compose the finitely many genuine special polynomial automorphisms

\[
 \Phi_s(Y)=Y+t^r h_s(Y)v_s.
\]

Their composition Φ lies in SAut_{R[t]}R[t][Y] and satisfies

\[
 \Phi(Y)=Y+t^rF(Y)\pmod{t^{r+1}}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Cross terms again start at order 2r, so the coefficients at order r add. If bar denotes reduction modulo t^m, then bar(Φ)^(−1)∘ρ is identity modulo t^(r+1). Every correction has determinant exactly one before reduction and a polynomial inverse before reduction; no Jacobian-conjecture implication is being assumed.

## 4. Finite induction proves surjectivity

Take σ∈SAut_A A[Y]. Its reduction σ0 modulo t is a special R-automorphism, because reducing σ's polynomial inverse gives its inverse. Extend σ0 to R[t][Y] by leaving t fixed. After replacing σ by σ0^(−1)∘σ, it is enough to lift an automorphism congruent to identity modulo t.

Apply Section 3 successively for r=1,…,m−1. If σ=A_r∘ρ_r in the target group, with ρ_r identity modulo t^r and A_r already lifted, replace ρ_r by bar(Φ_r)^(−1)∘ρ_r and A_r by A_r∘bar(Φ_r). The residual becomes identity modulo t^(r+1). After finitely many steps it is exactly identity in A[Y]. The product of the corresponding lifts, with the original σ0 extension, is a special polynomial automorphism over R[t] reducing to the original σ.

For m=1 no corrections are required; the constant coefficient lift suffices. This completes the verification of (1).

## 5. Scope controls and disposition

The Q-algebra hypothesis is used for integration, for the assertion that zero derivative means independence of a variable, and for the rational Vandermonde decomposition. It cannot simply be dropped. For example, over a field of characteristic p, the map y↦y+t y^p modulo t² is invertible with Jacobian one, but cannot be the reduction of a determinant-one polynomial automorphism of the one-variable polynomial ring over the domain k[t], since every such automorphism is a translation. This is outside the stated target and does not contradict it.

The input σ is already an automorphism. The argument neither proves the Jacobian conjecture nor says every special R-automorphism is tame: its constant reduction σ0 may be arbitrary. It constructs finite polynomial lifts of finite t-jets, not one globally convergent lift of an infinite formal t-series.

The independent proof reconstruction verifies the exact theorem for the stated coefficient rings, but the mathematical credit remains with van den Essen–Maubach–Vénéreau.
