## Claim and comparison criterion

The reviewed target is the full joint distribution of the Lebesgue measures of the territories first visited by k independent Brownian motions on the unit circle, with continued physical walkers. The submitted theorem treats fixed distinct labeled starts, then the independent-uniform and equidistant starting laws requested on printed p.1452 of Georgakopoulos's problem. Its answer is an absolutely convergent joint Laplace series with every coefficient reduced to finite permutations and deterministic integrals of explicit interval-exit kernels. An outer factorial remainder is supplied. No named density, compact closed form, efficient quadrature, or certified inner-series algorithm is part of the claim.

A prior resolution would need either this joint circle law, or a previously stated general result whose hypotheses and explicit output directly recover it. A one-point ownership probability determines a mean after spatial integration but not all correlations. A variance or an interface count likewise does not determine the volume vector. A general kernel formula requiring an unknown history-state kernel does not supply the submitted explicit reduction.

**Miller (2013; preprint 2010).** [Painting a graph with competing random walks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.2168), Annals of Probability 41, 636–670, DOI 10.1214/11-AOP713; arXiv v1 dated 10 March 2010. **Chatterjee, Nabahi, and Terlov (2026).** [Interface between competing random walks on a cycle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21312), arXiv2608.21312v1, dated 21 August 2026, 17:21:34 UTC. Sections 3–4 use hitting-time differences and cycle first-passage events. This is especially close geometry and allocation, but a different observable. It does not present the complete volume-vector law claimed by PR141.

## Generic characterizations and related explicit methods

**Fitzsimmons and Pitman (1999).** [Kac's moment formula and the Feynman–Kac formula for additive functionals of a Markov process](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~pitman/kac.pdf), Stochastic Processes and their Applications 79, 117–134, DOI 10.1016/S0304-4149(98)00081-7. The full author-hosted PDF was retrieved. Section 1, pp.117–120, gives moments and transforms of temporal additive functionals through a known killed-process potential kernel. Section 3.6, p.124, equation (27), gives permutation sums for products of additive functionals. These are strong precedents for the moment method. First-arrival spatial territories are not the stated temporal additive functionals. Applying an enlarged history/range state would still require its explicit kernel; the theorem does not provide the submitted finite-target comparison construction.

**Salminen and Stenlund (2020/2021).** [On Occupation Times of One-Dimensional Diffusions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10959-020-00993-3), Journal of Theoretical Probability 34, 975–1011, DOI 10.1007/s10959-020-00993-3; online 17 February 2020. Full primary HTML was retrieved. Section 3, Proposition 1, and Section 4 concern temporal occupation moments and transforms for diffusions, with explicit examples. They reinforce that moment/transform methods are standard. Their integration is over physical time in a state set, not over locations according to which of several continued walkers first arrived.

**Régnier, Dolgushev, Redner, and Bénichou (2022).** [Complete visitation statistics of one-dimensional random walks](https://sites.santafe.edu/~redner/pubs/pdf/PhysRevE.105.064104.pdf), Physical Review E 105, 064104, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.105.064104; published 6 June 2022. The full author-hosted PDF was retrieved. Section IV, equation (11), and Appendix B, pp.064104-8 onward, characterize multitime spans of one walker, using new-site exit increments; Appendix B also discusses the Brownian span. This is an explicit multivariate-law precedent. A span records total visited extent, not the locations and cross-walker hitting-time comparisons needed for ownership. Those formulas do not directly give the target law.

**Dey, Kim, and Terlov (2023).** [Collaboration of Random Walks on Graphs](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14241v1), arXiv2302.14241v1. Full primary HTML was retrieved. Section 1.2, Theorem 1.1, compares the expected size of the union of ranges of several stationary-start graph walks with one longer walk. Its general Markov-chain setting is relevant, but neither its observable nor its explicit result is the joint vector of first-arrival territories.

**Cortés-Poza, Padilla-Garza, and Padilla-Longoria (2026).** [New and Old Links Between PDEs and Voronoi Patterns](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10440-026-00779-5), Acta Applicandae Mathematicae 202, article3, DOI 10.1007/s10440-026-00779-5; published 17 March 2026. ## What is and is not supported about priority

The contribution supported by this audit is the explicit reduction of all finite-query ownership probabilities, preserving each walker's dependent hitting times, and their integration into the complete joint circle transform for both requested starting laws. Interval heat kernels, strong Markov iteration, competing-clock probabilities, Taylor expansion, Fubini, and compact-support moment determinacy are established tools. The manuscript must not imply methodological novelty for these tools. The model itself has close earlier discrete painting versions, including a continuum-scaling one-point calculation.

No accessible earlier theorem was found that already supplies every coefficient of the claimed finite-k circle law, or a generic explicit theorem that does so without a further kernel/construction step. This is a bounded search conclusion, not a proof of firstness. Every dated recent paper above predates the original PR and must be treated as prior literature. Their dates cannot establish which tools the authors actually consulted or whether any derivation was independent; this audit makes no such assertion.

The 1996 article is a concrete close predecessor with incomplete access, but the accessible primary scope identifies partial observables and two walkers. It is therefore not presently an identified unread *full-resolution* candidate for the general finite-k target. This distinction must remain explicit. If its full text later supplies a general joint-law construction, or another exact candidate is located, priority must be reopened. It would be unsound either to declare the PR already_solved from its title alone or to claim that the full text has been excluded. The Dicker thesis has the analogous access limitation in a materially different regime.
