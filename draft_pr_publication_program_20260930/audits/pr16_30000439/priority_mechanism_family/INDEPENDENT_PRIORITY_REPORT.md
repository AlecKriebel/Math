## 1. Verdict and exact target

**Verdict: bounded priority search; no exact prior subsumption found; priority unconfirmed.** The checked primary texts establish nearly all of the candidate's ingredients. They do not establish, by their stated conclusions alone, an original linear triangle complex that fails affine embedding in four dimensions. The additional candidate mechanism is a nonembedding estimate uniform over all small facet deletions, followed by removal of every repeated edge. This is a substantive additional combination relative to the checked theorem statements. That judgment does not prove that the combination, or the exact conclusion, was absent from all earlier literature.

The target is the existence of a finite abstract **two-dimensional** simplicial complex, with its **original** triangulation fixed, satisfying

\[
 e_{\mathrm{PL}}(K)=3,\qquad e_{\mathrm{lin}}(K)=5.
\]

PL embedding permits a subdivision; affine embedding must be affine on the original simplices. There is no manifold, small-size, prescribed-placement, or explicit-facet-list condition. Consequently any earlier existence theorem for a linear 3-uniform triangle hypergraph with more than its vertex count in facets, and no affine embedding in \(\mathbb R^4\), would already imply the exact target using established PL and general-position facts. Finding such a theorem was the principal falsification criterion of this family.

## 2. Two strongest positive priors

### Newman: geometry-uniform affine obstruction, but no stated deletion quantifier

[Newman, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09576v3), Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2), puts the affine \(\mathbb R^{2d}\) threshold at expected \(d\)-face exponent one. For complete edges and independent triangles \(p=n^{-\alpha}\), \(\alpha<2\) gives nonembedding in \(\mathbb R^4\). Section 4 (pp. 10–13) supplies the balanced-Radon count (Theorem 9/Corollary 10), order-type bound (Theorem 11/Lemma 12), and Janson argument (Lemma 13/Theorem 14). Theorem 16 (p. 14), using Wagner's PL threshold, gives PL embedding in \(\mathbb R^{2d}\) and failure of affine embedding in that same ambient space when \(1<\alpha<d\). Thus \(d=2,\alpha=3/2\) yields PL \(\mathbb R^4\), not a stated PL \(\mathbb R^3\) result. The dense-side proof and conclusion were inspected in full. There is no quantification over deleting facets in those statements or that proof.

Version history: [arXiv metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09576) records v1 **2022-12-19 16:08:49 UTC**, v2 **2023-06-27 20:42:09 UTC**, v3 **2023-10-03 14:26:49 UTC**. Earlier v1/v2 theorem statements were checked. Their odd-dimensional result was explicitly withdrawn in v3 and is not treated as a valid prior theorem. The even-dimensional argument is positive prior evidence. The [journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X26003559), DOI `10.1016/j.disc.2026.115331`, is indexed for Discrete Mathematics 349(12), December 2026. Its accessible preview was examined during this audit; current v3 was the full text used. The full journal article was inaccessible, and exact first online availability and full-text equality with v3 are **unverified**. A future issue label is not a reason to ignore the older publicly available preprint.

### Lee–Nevo: all linear hypergraphs have the requisite PL embedding
+[Lee–Nevo v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14195v1), Lemma 3.1 (PDF p. 9), proves that every linear \((d+1)\)-uniform hypergraph is PL embeddable in \(\mathbb R^d\), for \(d\ge3\). Here linear means distinct hyperedges intersect in at most one vertex. The incidence graph is embedded in \(\mathbb R^d\) and its facet nodes are inflated. The lemma and proof were checked in v1, v2, v3, and the [current published full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00856-4) (Lemma 3.1, journal pp. 1948–1950). The implication for a linear triangle family is immediate: give each triangle a private apex, apply the \(d=3\) lemma to the resulting linear tetrahedral family, and restrict the PL embedding to the triangles. The candidate correctly imports this PL mechanism.

[Version metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14195): v1 **2023-07-26 13:49:45 UTC**; v2 **2024-10-19 08:52:06 UTC**; v3 **2026-03-09 09:31:17 UTC**. The publisher gives **3 July 2026** for online publication/version of record, accepted 12 June 2026, issue October 2026. The relevant lemma therefore has public availability in July **2023**, not only in 2026. No Newman citation was located in the current full Lee–Nevo text; that bounded text observation does not establish lack of influence or lack of another connection elsewhere.

### Why the two prior conclusions alone do not imply the target

This is an implication check made by the present audit. Newman's sampled \(Y_2\) need not be linear. At \(p=n^{-3/2}\), the expected number of selected triangle pairs sharing an edge is \(\Theta(n)\). Lee–Nevo applies after those conflicts are removed, while Newman's original nonembedding conclusion may disappear under removal. Affine nonembedding is not inherited by arbitrary subcomplexes. One cannot substitute a cleaned subcomplex into Newman's theorem without an additional survival argument.

The candidate provides that survival argument by forbidding a crossing-free placement **after every deletion of at most \(n^{5/4}\) triangles**, including a deletion chosen after observing the random sample. Its failure bound \(\exp(-\Omega(n^{3/2}))\) beats both the order-type factor \(\exp(O(n\log n))\) and the deletion-set factor \(\exp(O(n^{5/4}\log n))\). Cleaning removes \(O(n)\) conflicts with high probability while leaving \(\Theta(n^{3/2})\) facets. The inequalities explain what is added; they are not an independent assertion of literature novelty. Nonplanarity from the retained edge count and the universal affine \(\mathbb R^5\) placement then make the two dimensions exact.

## 4. Linear hypergraphs, Steiner systems, block designs, and high chromatic constructions

[Horsley–Pike, *On balanced incomplete block designs with specified weak chromatic number*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.6111) was inspected in full where needed (Theorem 1.1, definitions, construction statements). It gives specified weak chromatic numbers for sufficiently large admissible BIBDs, with the stated exceptional parameter pair. Parameter \(\lambda=1,k=3\) yields linear triples. The theorem specifies coloring and block incidence, not failure of affine simplex embedding. Public v1 was 27 September 2012, v2 3 September 2013; JCTA 123 (April 2014), 123–153, DOI `10.1016/j.jcta.2013.12.004`.

Lee–Nevo Remark 3.3 expressly points to these designs and older coloring papers (de Brandes–Phelps–Rödl 1982; Linek–Wantland 1998) as high-chromatic linear examples. Its Hales–Jewett construction is also linear. No affine \(\mathbb R^4\) obstruction is asserted for these examples. The earlier chromatic results cannot be converted by a general chromatic bound because the preceding affine-embedding chromatic supremum is infinite. Special geometric impossibility for a particular design would need an additional theorem; none was located in this bounded trail.

[Lubotzky–Luria–Rosenthal, *Random Steiner systems and bounded degree coboundary expanders of every dimension*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08331): arXiv v1 public **28 December 2015**; published online **20 April 2018**, DCG 62 (2019), 813–831. Both v1 and the full author-hosted published PDF were retrieved. Published §2.3, definition (2.18), and Theorem 2.9 (pp. 820–822) were checked. The expander is a union of **sufficiently many** independent Steiner systems, with bounded codimension-one degrees, not degrees one. The theorem does not guarantee linearity of the union or the candidate's affine obstruction. Its topological overlap in dimension \(d\) is an ambient \(\mathbb R^d\) statement, not the requested affine \(\mathbb R^4\) conclusion for \(d=2\). Deleting to one system would again require a survival theorem. The theorem therefore does not imply the target.

Search terminology required filtering: design-theoretic “embedding” often means inclusion of a partial design into a larger design; embeddings of a triple system on a surface concern face incidence of an associated surface; point-line realization treats blocks as collinearities. None equals convex filled-triangle embedding of an abstract simplicial complex.

## 5. Counting and order types: a tempting but incomplete shortcut

[Dey–Shah, *On the number of simplicial complexes in \(\mathbb R^d\)*](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/DeyTamal/countingscomplexes.pdf), Computational Geometry 8 (1997), 267–277, has a **conditional** Theorem 1 (p. 269): an \(O(n^{\lceil d/2\rceil})\) upper bound for non-improperly intersecting simplices implies \(\log\kappa_d(n)=\Theta(n^{\lceil d/2\rceil})\). The introduction and open problems preserve that premise. At \(d=4\), the desired \(2^{O(n^2)}\) bound on all geometric complexes is not unconditional in this paper. The scanned PDF was OCRed and the displayed theorem was checked against the page image; OCR is not substituted for the formula. Submitted 1 June 1995, accepted 29 January 1997; the author's bibliography also lists a CCCG 1995 preliminary form.

The following implication checks are deductions of this audit, not claims attributed to Dey–Shah:

1. A linear triple system has \(O(n^2)\) facets, but that fact does not establish Dey–Shah's **global** premise about unrestricted crossing-free complexes. A specialized counting extension for linear triples would need proof.
2. \(2^{O(n\log n)}\) order types count placements, not admissible complexes per placement. A fixed placement may support many different facet subsets. For example, on the moment curve in \(\mathbb R^4\), all triangles through one chosen vertex meet correctly: the union of any two uses at most five vertices, which are affinely independent. Their arbitrary subsets give \(2^{\binom{n-1}{2}}\) different complexes in one order type. These examples are not linear; they disprove only the one-complex-per-order-type shortcut.
3. One also cannot apply a bound for triangulations of a point set to all geometric complexes. Dey–Shah explicitly discusses the obstruction to extending a geometric complex to a triangulation without new vertices (Schönhardt type). A triangulation that allows extra vertices would not yield an original-simplex count.

Thus the proposed deterministic counting route is **blocked at an unsupported premise**, rather than providing an exact earlier implication. It can be reopened by a genuinely applicable counting theorem; negative search does not establish that no such theorem exists.

This family has **not** resolved that question globally. No “first,” “new method,” or certified-original label follows from this report.
