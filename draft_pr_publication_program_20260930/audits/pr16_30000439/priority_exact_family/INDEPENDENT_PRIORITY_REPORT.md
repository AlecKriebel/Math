## 1. Exact target and historical scope

For a finite abstract simplicial complex K, write e_PL(K) for the least Euclidean dimension admitting a PL embedding of |K|, and e_lin(K) for the least dimension admitting an embedding affine on every **original** simplex. Subdivision is permitted in the definition of e_PL but changes the object in an original-linear claim.

The target is existence of a finite K with dim K=2, e_PL(K)=3, e_lin(K)=5. A prior theorem giving K PL-embeddable in R3 and not originally linearly embeddable in R4 would already establish a gap of at least two: e_lin(K)=5 follows from general position, and e_PL(K)≤3 suffices for that broader conclusion. For the exact minimum 3, a nonplanar graph subcomplex provides the lower bound. There is no requirement that K be a manifold, that it have a small vertex count, or that a complete facet list be printed.

The original question is at printed p.702 of Brehm's contribution to *Discrete Differential Geometry*, Oberwolfach Report 12/2006, pp.701–702. It asks whether the two minimal dimensions for a **simplicial complex** can differ by more than one, and separately identifies dimensions 2 and 3 as particularly interesting. Its preceding manifold theorem is an example, not a restriction on the question. The report defines PL embeddability using derived subdivisions and distinguishes it from original-linear embeddability. Theorem 2 states a same-ambient separation for n≥2, m≥3, n≤m≤2n. [Original report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46044).

Three distinctions control this audit:

- PL in R4 plus no linear embedding in R4 gives e_lin≥5 but only e_PL≤4. It supplies no second dimension of separation without an additional PL upper bound.
- No original-linear embedding in R3 gives e_lin≥4. It supplies no obstruction in R4.
- Topological nonembeddability in R4 rules out PL embeddability in R3, so a stronger topological obstruction can exclude a source from the target rather than help it.

## 2. Earlier Brehm constructions: exact deductions

### 2.1 The 1983 Möbius example has exact minima 3 and 4

Brehm's four-page primary proof constructs a nine-vertex triangulated Möbius strip that does not even immerse linearly in R3. Its triangular boundary and a triangular interior curve must have linking magnitude at least two in an embedding, whereas two straight triangular curves cannot have that linking magnitude. This is an obstruction in ambient dimension 3. [Brehm 1983](https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/1983-089-03/S0002-9939-1983-0715878-1/S0002-9939-1983-0715878-1.pdf).

Brehm–Schild's three-page theorem says **every triangulation** of the torus and projective plane has a simplexwise-linear embedding in R4. I read all pages as rendered images, including the proof. It finds an induced essential cycle, embeds the complementary planar subcomplex through a convex 3-polytope, and uses a polytope construction and Schlegel diagram. It does not assert the corresponding result for all surfaces. [Brehm–Schild 1995](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/BrehmUlli/Brehm4.pdf).

**Audit deduction.** Cap the boundary cycle of any triangulated Möbius strip by coning it to a new vertex. The resulting abstract simplicial complex triangulates RP2 and retains the original Möbius complex as a subcomplex. Restrict a Brehm–Schild R4 linear embedding to it. Consequently every original triangulation of the Möbius strip is linearly embeddable in R4. The 1983 witness has e_lin=4 and e_PL=3, since the underlying Möbius strip embeds PL in R3 and cannot embed in the plane. It cannot be a previously known 3-to-5 example.

### 2.2 The main 1992 linking family has a one-dimension gap

Brehm–Sarkaria Theorem A gives, for each n≥2 and prescribed number r of derived subdivisions, an n-complex PL-embeddable in R^(2n) whose rth derived complex is not linearly embeddable there. The proof begins with the n-skeleton of a (2n+2)-simplex minus one n-face, then amplifies unavoidable linking. I read the full preprint, including the constructions, proofs, and all concluding variations §3.2. [Brehm–Sarkaria, MPI/92-52](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/1946/1/preprint_1992_52.pdf).

**Audit deduction, checkable separately from the construction.** If the removed face is s, omit a vertex of s and take the induced subcomplex on the remaining 2n+2 vertices. All faces of size at most n+1 are present, so this is Sk_n(Δ_(2n+1)). Its minimal nonfaces have size n+2, and any two intersect, since 2(n+2)>2n+2. Apply Frick–Hu–Scheel–Simon Theorem 1 with ambient sphere dimension d=2n−1 and vertex count d+3=2n+2. This subcomplex does not topologically embed in S^(2n−1), hence not PL in R^(2n−1). It survives in the linking-amplifier complexes. Thus the main family's e_PL is exactly 2n. For r=0, e_lin is exactly 2n+1 by general position. For n=2 its exact minima are 4 and 5, not 3 and 5. [Few-vertex criterion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.04855).

The §3.2 variants give other same-ambient separations, an infinite topological/non-PL example, and same-ambient immersion obstructions via cones. The n=2,m=3 case changes attachments to preserve PL embeddability; it still supplies no R4 original-linear obstruction. These statements do not answer the minimum-dimension question. Its appearance after these results in 2006 is historical evidence, not a current-status proof.

Theorem B's punctured RP^n examples have a same-ambient obstruction in R^(2n−1). The n=2 case is bounded above linearly by 4; higher cases lack the additional affine lower bound needed here.

## 4. Surfaces, nonmanifolds, and terminology

The Hougardy–Lutz–Zelke survey distinguishes generic R5 realizability of arbitrary triangulated surfaces from R4 realizability of all triangulations of the torus and projective plane. Orientable surfaces and surfaces with boundary admit PL R3 embeddings; a closed nonorientable surface cannot embed in R3. Known nonrealizable orientable-surface triangulations in this discussion have an R3 obstruction. Such a surface **could** meet the target if its same triangulation were also proved non-linear in R4, but an R3 obstruction plus a generic R5 upper bound does not prove that. I found no cited R4 obstruction of this kind in the checked route. [Primary survey](https://www.or.uni-bonn.de/~hougardy/paper/SurfaceRealization.pdf).

Polygonal maps with nontriangular faces may fail realizability in every Euclidean dimension because of coplanarity constraints. Those results do not concern an abstract simplicial complex, whose faces are simplices. Subdividing polygonal faces removes the original constraint. Likewise, nonpolytopality does not imply failure of linear realization: the few-vertex paper explicitly distinguishes an R^d linear embedding from inclusion in the boundary of a convex (d+1)-polytope.

The target's nonmanifold complexes are fully within the original question. Any proposed publication should preserve that scope and avoid presenting the result as a manifold or small-example theorem.

## 5. Adversarial joins, suspensions, products

This extension was independently checked following the parent’s additional audit request, without receiving a positive prior claim from another family.

**Suspension has a genuine original-linear theorem, but no gap amplification here.** AKM Claim 11 proves K embeds geometrically in R^d iff K*S0 embeds geometrically in R^(d+1), by placement of the two apices and a projection argument. Thus e_lin(ΣK)=e_lin(K)+1. For the older positive-codimension families, the same increase holds for PL minima: a PL embedding of ΣK in R^m gives a PL embedding of the apex link K into S^(m−1). If dim K<m−1 this compact lower-dimensional image misses a point and gives a PL embedding in R^(m−1). Conversely suspension of a PL embedding supplies the upper bound. For the Möbius example, a proposed PL embedding of its suspension in R3 would require a Möbius link in S2, impossible; therefore the first suspension has minima 4 and 5, and iteration retains a one-dimension gap. This is an audit deduction, not a statement attributed to AKM.

**Joins have upper bounds, not a general affine additivity principle.** Separate embeddings give e_lin(K*L)≤e_lin(K)+e_lin(L)+1 and an analogous PL upper bound. Lower bounds require independent evidence. A simple check against universal equality is C3*C3: each triangular circle has linear minimum 2, while their join is the boundary of the convex free sum of two triangles in R4. A Schlegel realization embeds the original 3-sphere triangulation in R4, and invariance of domain excludes R3, so its linear minimum is 4 rather than 2+2+1=5. There is no legitimate general step adding two known one-dimension gaps.

Read the complete Parsa–Skopenkov primary paper, the main statements and relevant proof context of Parsa's Smith-index work, and Melikhov's join/product equivalence statements. Their lower bounds arise from topological deleted-product/join obstructions. Their favorable theorems produce topological/PL embeddings or recover embeddings of factors in a metastable range; they do not prove affine minima are additive. Parsa even supplies examples of factor nonembeddability whose join is embeddable in the corresponding double dimension. For target dimension 2 and PL ambient 3, the inequality 2d≥3k+3 fails at (k,d)=(2,3), so the Parsa–Skopenkov theorem cannot be used there without new evidence. [Parsa–Skopenkov](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12285), [Parsa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.02563), [Melikhov](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.04015).

Products require a chosen triangulation; replacing a CW product by an arbitrary subdivided simplicial product changes original-linear constraints. The checked product theorems concern the polyhedron’s topological/PL embeddability and yield no original-affine gap amplification. Therefore this route does not establish a previous answer either to the exact 2D target or to the unrestricted “gap exceeds one” question. This is a bounded exclusion of the examined theorems, not a universal no-amplification result.
