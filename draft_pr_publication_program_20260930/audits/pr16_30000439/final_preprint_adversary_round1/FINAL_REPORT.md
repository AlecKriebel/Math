## Universal mathematical gates

### Balanced seven-point Radon and finite constants

For seven generic R^4 points, the homogenized matrix A has rank 5 and kernel dimension 2. Two coordinate zeros on a nonzero relation would leave a dependence among at most five columns, impossible. Every coordinate functional on ker(A) is nonzero. On a generic semicircle, its sign crosses at a distinct point, and the number of positive entries changes in unit steps from k to 7−k. Thus a 3/4 transition occurs. At that boundary, the six nonzero entries have 3 positive and 3 negative signs. The homogenizing row makes their two coefficient masses equal; normalization gives an intersecting disjoint triple pair. This is a proof for every generic seven-point set, including extreme or badly conditioned placements, not an assertion from sampled configurations.

A forbidden pair uses exactly six labels, hence belongs to exactly n−6 seven-sets. The double count gives |W|≥binom(n,6)/7, without assuming uniqueness in a seven-set. Removing at most m whole triangles loses at most mM forbidden pairs, whether a removed pair is counted once or twice in that upper bound. For n≥12, each factor n−j is at least n/2, so binom(n,6)/7≥n^6/322560. The stated threshold n^(7/4)≥107520 makes mM≤n^6/645120. Therefore the surviving lower bound c n^6 is valid.

The explicit integer choice n=2^256, p=2^−384 and m=2^320 is consistent with a fourth-power n and integral m. Independent rational arithmetic checks the actual lower witness count, stronger ordered denominator, entropy and joint success probability. In particular c^2 n^(3/2)/2>2^343, entropy<2^331, and their difference>2^342. Using log2<1 and log(m+1)≤m is sufficient. exp(−x)≤1/(1+x) avoids evaluating the enormous exponential. The remaining Markov and Chebyshev bounds and λ/2−m>n are strict. There is no limiting asymptotic assumption left in the finite existence claim.

### Order types, continuum and nongeneric placements

The [original Goodman–Pollack 1986 paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF02187696.pdf), Theorem 1 on printed p.220, bounds simple numbered configurations by n^(d(d+1)n). Its hypotheses are n>d and no d+1 points in a hyperplane. I inspected the actual scanned theorem and finite proof on p.222; it is not merely an O(n log n) estimate with an unknown constant. With d=4 these give n^(20n), at the explicit finite n. The proof counts connected components of the complement of the determinant-product zero set; the total degree is4 binom(n,5). It supplies a bound on realizable sign patterns, not on all abstract oriented matroids.

For six generic R^4 points the kernel is one dimensional, with nonzero signed five-point cofactors. Their signs determine the unique affine dependence, hence its Radon partition. A disjoint triple pair crosses exactly for the corresponding3/3 split. Thus one labeled simple order type determines all W and covers a continuum of placements without coordinate discretization or coordinate-size restrictions.

For a finite original-affine embedding, every pair of disjoint nonempty faces has positive compact-set separation, and the minimum over all such pairs is positive. Moving vertices by less than one third of this minimum preserves those separations, since each convex combination moves by at most the maximum vertex movement. If two distinct domain points then had equal images, cancel their common vertex coefficients. The remaining positive and negative supports are disjoint subfaces with equal positive total mass; normalization contradicts the preserved separation. This also rules out a degenerate simplex. It is essential that all disjoint faces, not just disjoint maximal triangles, are used. The manuscript uses the correct criterion. Generic assignments are dense because the finitely many bad determinants are proper polynomials. Unused labels can be inserted avoiding finitely many affine spans. Thus nongeneric, overlapping-face and unused-label cases do not escape the obstruction.

### Ordered Janson and all adaptive deletion sets

The Bernoulli ground coordinates are triangles. A forbidden-pair event requires two distinct triangle coordinates; two such events are dependent exactly when they share a whole triangle. Geometric vertex or edge overlap alone gives no dependence. Distinct auxiliary graph edges can share at most one endpoint, so their joint expectation is p^3. If d_σ is the degree of triangle σ in W(F), the ordered off-diagonal count is sum d_σ(d_σ−1)≤M(M−1)(M−2)≤M^3. The diagonal contributes μ, not μp or μ^2.

The [author-hosted Frieze–Karoński book](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/BOOK.pdf) is explicitly dated 7 August 2026. Section 34.6, Theorem 34.13/equation 34.36 and the proof on pp. 712–714 apply to fixed-subset inclusion events in an independent product measure. Although braces in its overlap notation could be misread as unordered, the proof's sum over i of E(I_iY_i), with Y_i summing all overlapping j, counts ordered pairs and diagonal terms. Setting t=μ gives exactly the conservative manuscript denominator 2(μ+Δ). The endpoint follows by the stated theorem or its limiting argument. Janson 1990 is credited; its original DOI page was unavailable in this fresh audit, so I do not claim to have newly read that full paper.

For fixed geometry and fixed F, μ≥c n^3 and μ+Δ≤n^(9/2), so the rate a n^(3/2) is valid. There are at most (m+1)M^m≤(m+1)n^(3m) deletion sets, taken from the entire triangle universe. The union bound over geometry AND every such F therefore includes a cleanup selected after observing H, even if selection depends on geometry or is randomized. No independence among these failure events is used. A fixed-F estimate alone would have been insufficient; the manuscript contains the needed universal quantifier.

### Cleanup, nonplanarity and exact upper dimension

Every selected edge-sharing triangle pair is uniquely determined by its common edge and two distinct third labels. Its expected count is binom(n,2)binom(n−2,2)p^2≤n/4. Markov needs no independence of collision indicators. Deleting the union of one chosen member from each offending pair uses at most B removals and hits every original pair; deletion creates none. The retained family is linear. The binomial count has varianceλ(1−p)≤λ, and Chebyshev plus λ/2−m>n leave more than n triangles. The three good events coexist by a failure union bound, without an independence assumption.

The generated complex has exactly three distinct edges per retained triangle and at most n vertices, violating the planar simple graph edge bound. A PL-R^2 embedding would restrict to a planar graph embedding. Hence e_PL≥3. The geometry below gives its opposite bound. The uniform R^4 obstruction gives e_lin≥5 by Euclidean inclusion. In R^5, moment-curve vertices have every≤6-point set affinely independent. Any pair of faces uses at most six vertices; uniqueness of affine coordinates gives exactly its abstract common face as the hull intersection. This covers all dimensions of faces, not only triangles, and gives e_lin≤5. The retained triangles ensure dim(K)=2.

### Incidence geometry, local fans and global separation

Every four distinct three-dimensional moment-curve nodes are affinely independent. Thus disjoint graph edges cannot meet, incident edges meet only at their common endpoint, graph edges avoid other nodes, and any two facet rays at a shared vertex have positive angle. Incidence cycles impose no further topological constraint.

Within a facet, the three vectors v_i−f form a basis. The two subdivision triangles in one coordinate plane are on opposite sides of the diagonal ray and meet in exactly the edge[0,epsilon(e_i+e_j)]. Different coordinate planes meet on the shared axis. A triangle tipped at its axis vertex contributes[0,e_i]; the other contributes only0. These cases give precisely all common subdivision faces, and each triangle is nondegenerate. Edge-barycenter positions are consistent globally because linearity makes each original edge private to one facet. These freely assigned abstract subdivision vertices need not be arithmetic Euclidean barycenters.

For global separation choose r using all finitely many node and nonincident-edge clearances. Stars S_f trimmed outside open r/2 vertex balls are disjoint compact sets. Their positive minimum separationδ exists; facet nodes are outside the balls, so stars are not wholly erased. Each fan point has a nearby incidence-segment point within epsilon L. Outside radius-r balls, that nearby point is outside radius-r/2 balls if epsilon L<r/2; two images would then violate δ if2epsilon L<δ. The chosen epsilon L<δ/3 has the required slack. Trimming only at radius r without this margin would not have sufficed; the manuscript uses r/2 correctly.

Inside a vertex ball, every fan triangle associated with a nonincident segment remains farther than3r−epsilon L, hence cannot enter. This excludes even triangles of an incident original facet that are not tipped at the shared vertex. For tipped triangles, alpha=1−lambda−mu epsilon≥mu(1−epsilon). Unless the point is exactly the tip, alpha>0. Relative to alpha(f−v_i), the perturbation length ratio is≤q=epsilon L/((1−epsilon)ell). For q<1 an error ball gives angular deviation≤arcsin(q). Distinct facet-ray cones with this half-angle<theta/3 are disjoint away from the shared tip. Boundary points on radius-r spheres satisfy the outside-ball separation or the same closed-cone estimate. The formula also handles mu=0 and lambda=1 endpoints. All finitely many strict conditions can hold for one positive epsilon; absent pairs omit conditions.

Empty families, single facets, isolated labels, disconnected components, arbitrary finite valence and cyclic incidence graphs are either directly covered by these arguments or irrelevant to the generated K. The lemma does not claim to handle arbitrary extra free edges outside a triangle-generated complex. Zero epsilon, repeated incidence parameters, shared original edges and infinite families lie outside its assumptions; none is silently used. No prescribed original vertex position or manifold regularity is required.
