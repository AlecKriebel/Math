# Independent verification of the reviewed rare-flip proof

This reconstruction was written after the source-first duplicated-block seal and after personally reading the original proof. It is a verification, not an additional original-author attempt or a claim of blind independence.

Let X_n=X_0 product_{k<n} xi_k, where X_0 is fair and independent of all mutually independent signs xi_k and P(xi_k=-1)=1/(k+2). Every X_n is fair, but the varying adjacent transition rates rule out joint stationarity. The limit law is the fair mixture of the two constant paths; it is stationary and nonergodic, within the source category. Prefix length r+1 is constant iff none of the r adjacent transitions flips. Its exact no-flip mass is (n+1)/(n+r+1), and each constant word has half this mass. TV in the supremum-over-events convention is exactly r/(n+r+1). The length-one boundary r=0 has zero TV. All finite-prefix laws converge; compact binary product space allows uniform cylinder approximation of bounded continuous functions, proving weak shifted path-law convergence.

For the sigma field F_m generated by X_0,...,X_m, marginal independence gives E[X_n|F_m]=X_m m(m+1)/(n(n+1)) for n>m. This includes m=0, with its zero transition factor. For a fixed bounded full-path-measurable g, upward conditional-expectation convergence gives g_m=E[g|F_m]->g in L1 because the finite histories generate sigma(X). Therefore |E[X_ng]|<=||g-g_m||1+[m(m+1)/(n(n+1))]||g_m||1. Hold m fixed while n grows, then let m grow. The result is E[X_ng]->0 for every fixed g. No uniformity in n-dependent g is proved or needed.

In an arbitrary fixed coupling the second path equals one constant fair sign B simultaneously at all coordinates, by a countable intersection. B may be anticipative and depend on additional randomness. The projection g=E[B|sigma(X)] is fixed, bounded and full-path-measurable. The tower identity gives E[X_n B]=E[X_n g]->0, hence P(X_n!=B)->1/2. There is no conditioning on B that would incorrectly preserve flip independence. Independently, the two-time mismatch P(X_n!=X_(2n))=(3n+1)/(4(2n+1))->3/8 prevents convergence to a single B in probability by a union bound.

For the specified geometric metric, I_n=1{X_n!=B} and D_n=d(theta_n X,constant B) satisfy E|D_n-I_n|<=sum_j 2^(-j-1)P(X_(n+j)!=X_n)<=sum_j 2^(-j-1)j/(n+j+1)<=1/(n+1). Tonelli applies to nonnegative summands, and the weighted index sum is one. Thus D_n tends in law to a fair mixture at 0 and 1 and ED_n->1/2. Scaling the metric by two changes the numerical limit to points 0 and 2. On the actual compact binary space all compatible metrics are uniformly equivalent, so each still excludes convergence to zero in probability; identical numerical limiting laws are not inferred.

For deterministic nonnegative s, E d(theta_(n+s)X,theta_n X)<=s/(n+1). If T_n is fully dependent but uniformly tight, the union/Markov bound gives P(d(theta_(n+T_n)X,theta_n X)>epsilon)<=P(T_n>M)+M(M+1)/(2epsilon(n+1)). First n grows with M fixed; then M grows. A fixed a.s. finite T automatically qualifies. The constant second path is unchanged by any shift. This preserves the original nonzero metric-error law under the stated finite/tight offset class. It does not use or claim stationarity after a path-selected random time.

Three hostile controls identify the quantifier boundary. First, for each n a different coupling B_n=X_n makes the zeroth mismatch zero and expected metric error <=1/(n+1); the target requires one fixed B. Second, with an independent fair B, the first-hit T_n=min{s>=0:X_(n+s)=B} is a.s. finite and gives P(T_n>M)=(n+1)/(2(n+M+1))->1/2 for every fixed M. It is not uniformly tight. Independence of B and the stopping event's finite flip history permits a post-hit future estimate E d(theta_(n+T_n)X,constant B)<=1/(n+1). Escaping offsets can repair the example. Third, changing flip probabilities to the summable 1/(k+2)^2 yields an eventually constant process a.s.; its eventual fair sign supplies a fixed synchronous coupling. Local freezing without divergent accumulated flips is insufficient for the original obstruction.

Eventual constancy is a Borel invariant event: union over starting times of intersection over later coordinate equalities. The original process assigns it probability zero because independent flip probabilities have divergent sum; each shifted law still assigns zero. The limit assigns one. Setwise convergence fails, and TV of the complete path laws is one. This distinguishes Problem 3.1 / 9900005 from the weak-convergence target 3.3 / 9900007.
