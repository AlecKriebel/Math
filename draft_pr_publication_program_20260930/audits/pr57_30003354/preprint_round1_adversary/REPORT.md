## Universal logarithm and Beltrami estimates

The numerator's formal homogeneous degree in x,y,rho is j+1, while its coefficient l1 norm is at most (3j+2j) times that of P_j. Starting with P_1=x or y proves the manuscript's 5^(j-1)(j-1)! bound for every finite j, independent of rho. Treating rho as a formal homogeneous variable while differentiating only x,y is legitimate.

For p(z)=z^(r+1), real derivatives of order m<=r+1 satisfy |D^m p|<=C s^(r+1-m), and higher derivatives vanish. The term with no derivative on L is bounded by C s^(r+1-k)|log s|; each term with at least one derivative on L is bounded by C s^(r+1-k). Thus the stated Leibniz bound holds for every fixed k<=r+1. When k<=r the exponent is at least one and epsilon times the bound tends to zero uniformly; at k=r+1 the exponent is zero, but s>=rho=e^-n gives epsilon(1+|log s|)<=2.
