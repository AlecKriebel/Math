## Literal target and primary interpretation

The complete authoritative raw and SQLite source row, as well as the preserved source snapshot, agree exactly in parsed JSON: numeric id9700035, codeAMR-096-0035, expected length of a SIRSN spanning subnetwork, with iid uniform points in a square of area k and asymptotic E len(span(Z1,...,Zk)) / k -> ell. The raw source and prior are saved unedited in sources/. The cache SHA256 and lengths agree with the upstream manifest; SQLite revision agrees as well. Their prior status is triage, not a theorem or an authoritative novelty certificate.

In Aldous2012 long preprint Section8.4.3, Problem35 on printed page50, the asymptotic is an unconditional demand for the given SIRSN. The immediately preceding explanation singles out the possibility of extremely long excursions far beyond the square as the difficulty. In the published Aldous2014 Section8.4.3, Problem9 on printed Page38/41, the same full-length asymptotic appears with an explicit request to identify extra assumptions if needed. These are related formulations, but an extra-assumption theorem cannot be promoted to an unconditional resolution of the literal indexed 2012 target without proving that those assumptions follow from the indexed axioms.

The finite spanning subnetwork is the union of the designated routes between every pair of sampled points. Overlap is counted once; length is Euclidean edge length, not travel time. The primary definition does not minimize over connected subsets. The original prior's 'minimal' / Steiner-type description is therefore inaccurate. A minimum connecting network, a subset built from selected pair routes, and the entire route union need not agree. The target includes all route portions in R^2, including exterior portions beyond the sample square.

## Assumptions that a proof must preserve

Aldous's route process has finite non-self-intersecting jagged routes, reversal symmetry, and pairwise route compatibility: meeting twice forces agreement on the segment between the meetings. Its finite-dimensional networks are consistent, measurable in endpoints, invariant in law under translations, rotations, and positive spatial scales. Sampled Poisson point processes are independent of routes; their all-pair union S(lambda) is locally finite in length and has the appropriate scaling coupling. D1 is Euclidean length between fixed unit-separated points, with 1 < E D1 < infinity. ell is the mean edge-length per unit area of S(1), not that of every continuum road nor a minimal connector. The full SIRSN axiom p(1)<infinity controls the dense sampled route union away from endpoint discs and implies ell<infinity via Lemma20; omitting p(1) instead gives the separate weak-SIRSN variant. Scaling gives ell(lambda)=sqrt(lambda)ell and p(r)=p(1)/r. No global shortest-cost or deterministic confinement assumption is part of the indexed axioms.

## Source-derived initial proof controls and exact gap

For a proposed full proof, separate local/interior mass from exterior mass. Translation invariance and finite edge intensity bound the mean interior length of a Poisson finite-window union by ell times window area. As centered source windows grow, their route unions increase to S(1); monotone convergence determines the interior density. For fixed-count samples, a Poisson count coupled to the same iid point sequence offers upper/lower comparisons; exponentially small count errors must still be multiplied by a genuine finite expectation bound, not silently discarded. From E D1<infinity and scale/rotation invariance, the total sum over pair-route lengths has finite expectation and an O(k^(5/2)) elementary upper bound, which can control such count-error events under independence of count and point/route sampling. This gives a plausible route to the interior density, not a sealed proof of the full target.

The exterior mass is the critical independent gap. Finite local edge intensity says nothing by itself about total mass far outside a growing source window. A full resolution must show E len(span(Z1,...,Zk) outside Q_k)=o(k). Finite almost-sure exterior mass, a finite mean for each fixed pair, bounded-in-probability excursion radius, or local convergence alone does not establish that expectation estimate. A uniform-integrability or quantitative global tail mechanism must be checked rather than inferred from local finiteness.

Kahn's gamma>d construction proves fixed-pair Euclidean-length moments of orders delta<gamma-1 and exponentially controlled travel-time diameter; its finite long-distance-network intensity and SIRSN status are explicit model results. Euclidean length and travel-time diameter are different variables. Kahn's line-speed proof may permit a further uniform Euclidean route-length bound using a common travel-time bound and maximum-speed constraints in expanding balls, but that extension would need a checkable derivation and cannot be attributed as the exact theorem statement. Blanc–Curien–Kahn proves planar geodesic nonpausing and identifies the geodesic frame for beta>2, and supplies confluence and restricted-road travel-time regularity tools. Those results are model-specific and do not supply the generic indexed full spanning-functional asymptotic on their face.

## Review success and falsification criteria

Accept an unconditional indexed resolution only after every local-to-global expectation step, Poisson/binomial comparison, length-measure union identity, and exterior tail bound is proved under the exact indexed axioms. Accept an explicitly conditional theorem as a partial result when its extra assumption is precisely defined and its role verified, preserving the indexed open gap. Check finite cases k=0,1,2; equal/overlapping route unions; measurable windows; boundary sets; fixed versus random endpoint choices; lambda scaling; any zero/probability boundary cases; and large-radius and rare-event limits. Numerical and finite controls can falsify identities but cannot establish the infinite stochastic theorem.
