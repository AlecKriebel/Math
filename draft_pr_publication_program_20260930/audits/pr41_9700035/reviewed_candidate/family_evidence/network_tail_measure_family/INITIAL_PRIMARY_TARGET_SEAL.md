Literal full target is the displayed expectation asymptotic E len[span(Z1,…,Zk)] ~ ell k, as k→infinity, for k independent uniform points in a square of area k, sampled independently of the SIRSN. It is Open Problem35 in the 2012 paper; the published version numbers it Open Problem9 and explicitly asks “Under what extra assumptions (if any)” the law holds. The indexed source_record demands the unconditional law. A valid conditional theorem can answer a scoped published question, but cannot establish that all SIRSNs satisfy it or discharge the indexed unconditional target.

The operative span is the union of all prescribed pair routes, with overlaps counted once. It is NOT a shortest connected subnetwork/Steiner minimum. The imported prior's Steiner description is incorrect; original imported record must be preserved as provenance and current interpretation corrected separately. Routes are finite-length non-self-intersecting polygonal/jagged curves (possibly infinitely many segments accumulating at endpoints), symmetric under reversal, with compatibility: routes meeting twice agree between the meetings. Finite-dimensional laws are consistent, measurable in endpoints, translation/rotation invariant, and exactly Euclidean-scale invariant. PPP sampled subnetworks have locally finite edge length. Full SIRSN requires finite E D1 and finite limiting major-road intensity p(1); ell=intensity S(1) is finite (indeed ell≤2p(1)). Weak SIRSN without p(1)<infinity must be distinguished.

Measure-theoretic route: define the union edge-length random measure by one-dimensional length on countably many compatible routes, without summing overlap multiplicities. For PPP sample exhausted by nested bounded windows, route unions increase to S(lambda); for each bounded Borel cell their length measures increase and are dominated by integrable S(lambda)-length. Stationarity gives expected local mass ell sqrt(lambda) area. No ergodicity is required for an expectation law. Finite-window endpoint consistency is enough: no unsupported continuity in a new dense endpoint sample or implicit minimum-cost property should be assumed. The primary itself outlines rather than supplies every full measurable-space construction; any new proof should identify exactly which asserted random-measure/exhaustion fact it uses.

Own pre-candidate proof/gap map: interior normalized length should approach ell by fixed-cell exhaustion away from the boundary, using integrable local S(1) domination and negligible boundary area, followed by Poisson/binomial monotone sandwiches. Full length also includes outside-square excursions. At exterior distance s, every such edge is in the major-road process E(infinity,s), whose expected length density is p(1)/s. Integrating this density over exterior annuli of radius R gives order R, and its integral over the entire plane diverges. Thus finite p(1) gives a useful bounded-buffer estimate but does not directly supply a global integrable major-road dominator. Union-of-route length is bounded by the sum of pair route lengths, but this very loose order k^(5/2) bound does not yield ell k. Large-excursion probability alone cannot be multiplied by a correlated network length expectation. A Holder step needs a suitable moment of the random length or an independently justified conditional estimate; Markov plus first-moment tail does not repair that dependence. Truncation and tail integration must keep all k-dependent factors and required exponents. Any extra-tail assumption must be explicitly recognized as additional to the SIRSN axioms and verified for concrete models before claiming their coverage.

Success criteria: (i) exact source target and functional; (ii) rigorous measure/exhaustion interior coefficient ell; (iii) genuinely o(k) expected exterior length; (iv) valid monotone de-Poissonization including square-area scaling; (v) no unsupported uniform integrability, missing count/event dependence, or false constant/exponent cancellation.
