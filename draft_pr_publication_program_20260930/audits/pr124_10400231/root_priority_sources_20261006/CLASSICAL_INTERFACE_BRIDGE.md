# Exact bridge from the classical interface to the candidate

This is a root verification/priority comparison, not a new central proof-search approach or an assertion of earliest publication. It records an independently checked formal consequence of Turaev1986 printed pp127,131–133, especially the proof of Theorem1.6.1. Source PDF/hash and actual page render provenance are in ACTUAL_ACQUISITION_AND_RENDER.json; root actually inspected the complete relevant page pixels.

Let M be a closed connected oriented3-manifold with H1(M;Z)=Z⊕T, T finite. Choose an embedded knot k whose free homology image is a generator. Turaev's closed-case proof states that its exterior V has H1(V;Z)→H1(M;Z) an isomorphism, and that the induced free-group-ring identification takes Δ_V to Δ_M. The cited drilling theorem and ordinary torsion factors were directly checked, rather than assuming that arbitrary knot exteriors have this property.

For V, Turaev collapses onto a finite2-dimensional CW spine X with one0-cell and a1-cell circle Y whose homology class has primitive free image. With Λ=Z[t,t^-1], the relative free-abelian cover chain complex C^θ(X,Y) consists only of C2→C1. Euler characteristic zero makes its boundary matrix B(t) square. Turaev proves det B(t)=Δ_V up to ±t^k and explicitly identifies B(1) with the ordinary integral relative boundary map. Hence coker B(1)=H1(X,Y;Z).

The relative long exact sequence gives H1(X,Y;Z)=H1(X;Z)/<[Y]>: the H0(Y)→H0(X) map is an isomorphism since both spaces are connected. Writing [Y]=(1,s) after choosing the free generator, the map Z⊕T→T given by (n,u)↦u−ns is surjective with kernel generated by (1,s). Consequently coker B(1)≅T, regardless of the torsion coordinate s. This strengthens the cardinality conclusion in the published proof by reading its full relative chain complex; no cyclic-torsion assumption is introduced.

For a prime p, coker(B(1) modp)=T⊗Fp has dimension r_p. Constant invertible row/column operations over Fp bring B(1) to diag(I,0); the last r_p rows of the transformed Laurent matrix all vanish at t=1 and are divisible by t−1. The determinant is therefore divisible by (t−1)^r_p. This reasoning includes the zero reduced determinant and the empty square matrix case. The integer content of Δ is retained, and multiplication by ±t^k does not affect the order.

For T=(Z/p)^3 and Δ_p=t+(p^3−2)+t^-1, the free-homology and symmetry/augmentation conditions of the later Conjecture12.26 hold, but tΔ_p=(t−1)^2+p^3t has reduced order exactly2 rather than at least3. Thus the candidate's negative answer is a direct formal application of the integral interface published in1986 and the elementary determinant rank-drop fact.

The source comparison establishes the antiquity of this mechanism and a formal implication. It does **not** establish that the modular inequality itself, this family, or an explicit negative answer to the later conjecture had already been stated. Those are separate priority questions. In particular, a theorem's proof can contain enough information to refute a subsequently proposed conjecture without the refutation having been recorded; conversely, a later explicit modular theorem could remove any substantive mathematical novelty in the current obstruction. The2002 book's mod-r statements and their b1=1 polynomial-part scope are being checked for precisely this distinction.
