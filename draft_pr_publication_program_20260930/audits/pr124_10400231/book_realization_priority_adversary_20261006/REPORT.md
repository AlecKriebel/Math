## Checkable old-source implication for the exact pair

Use the printed rank-one setting of II.3 (p.22), its Corollary II.3.4 (p.23), and the closed-case Alexander identity II.5.2, (5.b) (p.28). Let D = (t−1)(t^−1−1) = 2−t−t^−1 and Q = pr([τ]). The identity gives an Alexander representative QD−|T|t^a, where a = K_t(e)/2 is integral. Changing Euler structure by a free-generator power normalizes a to zero; this follows from the evenness of K and the Euler-structure transformation printed on p.22. The resulting representative is symmetric by II.3.2(iv) after projection. A nonzero symmetric Laurent polynomial has only its two sign choices as symmetric unit representatives. Evaluation at 1 fixes the representative as −Δ_p.

Consequently, if the original pair were realized,

    QD − p^3 = −Δ_p,
    QD = p^3 − Δ_p = D,
    Q = 1.

Cancellation is in the integral Laurent domain; D is nonzero. For H_p the splitting in II.3.3 has n=4 and p_2=p_3=p_4=p. Corollary II.3.4 therefore forces augmentation([τ]) to be divisible by p. Projection preserves augmentation, but Q=1 has augmentation 1. This is the contradiction. It applies to every prime p, including 2, using II.3 rather than the separate cohomology-mod-r theorem.
