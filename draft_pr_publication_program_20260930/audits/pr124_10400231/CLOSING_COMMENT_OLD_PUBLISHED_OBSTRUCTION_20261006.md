The counterexample is mathematically valid, but the priority audit does not support publishing it as a new resolution. Closing without merging, with the research classification `already_solved` scoped to **mathematical content already implied by published results**.

Turaev, *Torsions of 3-dimensional Manifolds* (2002), [II.3.3–3.4, printed p.23](https://books.google.com/books?id=83ZJs9Z9BY0C&pg=PA23), gives an integral polynomial-part restriction for the full group `H=Z ⊕ (Z/p)^3`: the augmentation of `[τ]` must be divisible by `p`. The ordinary integral Alexander normalization is explicit in [II.5.1–5.2, pp.27–28](https://books.google.com/books?id=83ZJs9Z9BY0C&pg=PA28), [book DOI10.1007/978-3-0348-7999-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-0348-7999-6).

For the submitted `Δ_p=t+(p³−2)+t⁻¹`, that formula and polynomial-part integrality force the free projection `q([τ])=t^k`, for some integer `k`. Its augmentation is 1, contradicting divisibility by `p`. Sign and Laurent-unit choices are handled by cancellation of the double pole at 1. This proves the whole original family from the published condition, including `p=2`. II.3.3 also yields the original general mod-p torsion-rank inequality. Thus the original connected-cut proof is a useful self-contained alternate derivation, but no substantive mathematical result beyond those published consequences was identified.

[VIII.5.4, p.118](https://books.google.com/books?id=83ZJs9Z9BY0C&pg=PA118), realizes the polynomial using cyclic torsion of order p³; it does not realize the prescribed elementary-abelian group. The separate modular theorem III.4.3 has an additional hypothesis at `p=2` that fails here; the integral II.3.3–3.4 argument supplies that case.
