## Restriction of scalars is genuinely finite-dimensional

For a finite generating set, finitely many algebraic matrix entries generate a number
field K. Generator inverses also lie in K by adjugate/determinant; no new extension is
needed for infinitely many words. Choose a rational basis of K. Acting on K^d induces
a rational matrix of size d[K:Q]. If the rational action is identity, it fixes every
coordinate vector over K and the original K-linear action is identity. Thus this is
an injective homomorphism, and it transfers faithfulness. The converse follows from
Q contained in Qbar. Finite generation and allowance for increased degree are material.

## Finite simultaneous specialization and the universal obstruction

A finite-dimensional representation generated by rational-function matrices and their
inverses is defined on the localization obtained by adjoining their finitely many
entry denominators and determinant inverses. For each prescribed nonidentity word,
choose a nonzero numerator of one entry of rho(w)-I. A finite product of these
numerators and admissibility factors is a nonzero polynomial. Over the infinite field
Q it has a rational point where it is nonzero: induct on the number of variables,
first avoiding the nonzero leading coefficient and then the finitely many univariate
roots. This gives one representation separating the specified finite words. It does
not produce a point avoiding countably many exceptional polynomials.

For the displayed example every element has unique form
M(m,f)=[[x^m,f],[0,1]], m integer, f in Z[x,x^-1], with multiplication
M(m,f)M(n,g)=M(m+n,f+x^m g). Conjugation generates U(x^k) for every integer k;
integer powers and commuting products generate U(f) for every integer Laurent f.
The rational-function inclusion is faithful by definition and the normal form is
unique because x is transcendental. For nonzero algebraic alpha, choose a nonzero
integer polynomial p annihilating alpha. U(p) is a nonidentity group element and
maps to identity. Negative exponents are admissible since alpha is nonzero; alpha=0
is excluded by the generator determinant. Evaluation is on this Laurent ring, not
a field homomorphism from all of Q(x). The example gives no conclusion about any
particular braid representation or about existence of other rational representations.

## Low-strand correction, with a universal quotient argument

Write B3=<s,t|sts=tst>, A=sts, B=st. Then s=B^-1 A and t=A^-1 B^2;
the relation is equivalently A^2=B^3=z. This element is central and has infinite order
since the exponent map takes z to6. Quotienting by z gives the presented free product
C2*C3. Its center is trivial, so the center of B3 is exactly the infinite cyclic
subgroup generated by z.

For U=[[1,1],[0,1]], V=[[1,0],[-1,1]], UVU=VUV=A0=[[0,1],[-1,0]],
B0=UV=[[0,1],[-1,1]], A0^2=B0^3=-I. On the real projective line,
A0 acts by -1/x and B0 by1/(1-x). Take X_A=(-infinity,0), X_B=(0,infinity).
A0 sends X_B into X_A; both B0 and B0^2 send X_A into X_B. The ping-pong argument
therefore injects C2*C3 into PSL2(R). To see the boundary word cases explicitly:
a reduced word starting/ending in the same factor sends the other factor's set into
that factor's set. A cyclically reduced mixed word can be rotated to start in A and
end in B^epsilon, then conjugated by a nonidentity B^delta with delta!=epsilon;
the resulting reduced word starts and ends in the B factor and cannot be identity.
Length-one words are also visibly nontrivial. Thus the projective kernel of the
untwisted B3 map is exactly <z>; surjectivity onto PSL2(Z) is not needed here.

The scaled map rho(g)=2^e(g) phi(g) respects the braid relation. Its determinant is
4^e(g). If rho(g)=I, e(g)=0, projective injectivity gives g=z^k, and e(g)=6k
forces k=0. In particular rho(z)=-64I, not I. This is a faithful GL2(Q) representation.
B1 is trivial and B2 is Z with faithful character k maps to2^k.

For a proposed GL2(Z) faithful representation, the infinite-order central image C
cannot be scalar: the invertible integer scalars are just +/-I. Any nonscalar
2x2 rational matrix has a cyclic vector v (otherwise every vector is an eigenvector
and linearity makes the matrix scalar). A matrix commuting with C is determined by
its value at v in the basis(v,Cv), so lies in Q[C]. This centralizer is commutative,
forcing the whole image to be abelian. B3 is nonabelian (its above free-product
quotient is nonabelian), a contradiction. This does not obstruct GL2(Q), whose
scalar matrices can have infinite order, or higher-dimensional integral matrices.
