## Independent proof A: restriction of scalars

Let G be generated as a group by g_1,...,g_s and rho:G->GL_d(Qbar) a homomorphism. Let K be Q adjoined with all finitely many entries of A_i=rho(g_i). Each is algebraic; a finite tower gives [K:Q]=m<infinity. det(A_i) is nonzero in K. The adjugate identity gives A_i^{-1}=adj(A_i)/det(A_i), whose entries lie in K; no extra independent inverse parameters are needed. Every finite word therefore lies in GL_d(K). Fix a Q-basis e_1,...,e_m of K. Every A in M_d(K) acts Q-linearly on K^d; define T(A) in M_{dm}(Q) as its matrix in the induced basis. T(AB)=T(A)T(B), T(I)=I, and T(A) invertible with inverse T(A^{-1}). If T(A)=I, then A fixes every coordinate standard basis vector of K^d, so A=I. Thus T preserves the exact kernel of rho. The converse follows by Q inclusion Qbar. Characteristic0 and finite generation make this particular finite-field descent work. No integrality statement follows: the blocks are generally rational, not integer. Infinite-degree coefficient fields arising from infinitely many generators need not admit one finite restriction degree by this argument. Nor does Qbar simply act as a finite-dimensional Q-vector space.

## Independent proof B: finite-set avoidance in several parameters

Let F=Q(x_1,...,x_r), G finitely generated, rho:G->GL_d(F). Invertible generator matrices exist over F. Choose a nonzero common polynomial h in Q[x_1,...,x_r] containing all denominators of entries of all chosen generators and their inverses; equivalently include generator-entry denominators and determinant numerators after common-denominator clearing. Include x_1...x_r if the formulation requires nonzero coordinates/Laurent units. The matrices and inverses then belong to the localized ring R=Q[x_1,...,x_r,h^{-1}], and all defining relations that hold over F hold in R (R injects into F). For every finite subset S of G whose members have nonidentity rho-image, choose one nonzero entry f_s of rho(s)-I; express f_s=p_s/h^{k_s} with p_s a nonzero polynomial after clearing localization denominators. P=h product_s p_s is a nonzero polynomial in the integral domain Q[x_1,...,x_r]. There exists an integer tuple a with P(a)!=0: induct on r, choose integer values making one nonzero coefficient in the last variable nonzero, then avoid finitely many integer roots in the last coordinate. The evaluation R->Q at a is defined; matrices remain invertible; the specialized homomorphism separates every s in S. More strongly, the allowed tuples contain the rational points of a nonempty principal Zariski-open set; every nonempty real open box meets it, since a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an open box and Q^r is dense. A pre-fixed lower-dimensional curve need not meet it; a finite base field need not contain any allowed point. If rho is faithful then arbitrary finite nonidentity S can be preserved, but no single tuple has thereby been proved to preserve all G\{1}.

The all-word exceptional locus is a countable union, for countable G, of proper algebraic identity loci (each can be bounded by a chosen nonzero scalar witness) plus inadmissibility loci. Being countable union is not a reason to be unable to choose a real generic point, but Q and Qbar are themselves countable. Even countably many finite exceptional sets can exhaust Qbar: each nonzero algebraic number is a root of a nonzero integer polynomial, and such polynomials form a countable set. The precise logical gap is (for every finite S, exists a_S, all s in S survive) does not imply (exists a, for every nonidentity s, s survives). Countable families of rational representations may yield a faithful map into an infinite product; the dimensions of finite subproducts need not be bounded and the product is not GL_D(Q) for some proved finite D.

## Independent proof C: one faithful two-generator matrixgroup and every algebraic specialization

Let x be an indeterminate and D=diag(x,1), U=[[1,1],[0,1]] in GL_2(Q(x)). Put U(f)=[[1,f],[0,1]]. D U(f) D^{-1}=U(xf), and U(f)U(g)=U(f+g). The subgroup H=<D,U> is exactly matrices M(k,f)=[[x^k,f],[0,1]], k in Z, f in Z[x,x^{-1}]. Every such element is generated because U(sum_i c_i x^i)=product_i D^i U^{c_i} D^{-i}, finite Laurent support and arbitrary signed integer c_i. Conversely multiplication is M(k,f)M(l,g)=M(k+l,f+x^k g), so this set is a group containing D,U. The parameterization is unique, since x^k=x^l as rational functions forces k=l and matrix upper-right entries force f=g. Therefore H identifies with Z[x,x^{-1}] semidirect Z (restricted wreath product Z wr Z) and its displayed representation is faithful by construction, not a free group representation. It is finitely generated though its additive Laurent base has infinite rank.

For every alpha in Qbar\{0}, evaluation is the ring homomorphism ev_alpha:Z[x,x^{-1}]->Q(alpha) taking x to alpha; hence the homomorphism H->GL_2(Q(alpha)), M(k,f)->[[alpha^k,f(alpha)],[0,1]], is defined. It is not a field homomorphism from Q(x) (a nonzero minimal polynomial becomes zero). For any nonzero algebraic alpha, its rational minimal polynomial clears to some nonzero p in Z[x] with p(alpha)=0; alpha being nonintegral merely makes the polynomial nonmonic, and introduces no obstacle. U(p) is a nonidentity element of H whose evaluation is I. Realization is the finite word product_i D^i U^{c_i} D^{-i} for p=sum_i c_i x^i. For a Laurent annihilator p, signed i are equally allowed. Thus every admissible algebraic specialization of this displayed representation fails faithfulness.

The entire kernel is {M(k,f):alpha^k=1 and f(alpha)=0}. If alpha is not a root of unity, k=0 in the kernel; if order(alpha)=m, all k divisible by m are possible and D^m gives an additional kernel element. In every case p yields a nontrivial kernel, including alpha=+-1, alpha a rational fraction, irrational algebraic units, and nonunits. Alpha=0 is inadmissible because D becomes singular and x^{-1} has no image. For transcendental alpha, the same ring evaluation is injective and alpha has infinite multiplicative order, so this displayed specialization is faithful (over a transcendental coefficient field); this boundary test rejects an overclaim about all specializations.

This only refutes a general assertion that finite-generation plus faithful rational-function linearity automatically yields a faithful algebraic specialization of that representation. It neither embeds this example into B_n nor supplies a braid-specific kernel, nor yet proves whether H has some other finite-dimensional rational representation. No such stronger claim is required or promoted in this validation.
