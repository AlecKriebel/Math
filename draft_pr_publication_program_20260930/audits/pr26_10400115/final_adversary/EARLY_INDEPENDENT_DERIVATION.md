## Number-field descent, including inverse generators

Let H be finitely generated by h_1,...,h_r, represented injectively in GL_d(Qbar). Generate K over Q by the finitely many entries of their matrices. Each entry is algebraic, so the tower formula makes [K:Q] finite. Each determinant is a nonzero element of K. Adjugate divided by determinant puts every inverse generator in M_d(K), so every group matrix belongs to GL_d(K). A Q-basis of K turns K^d into Q^{d[K:Q]}; the same K-linear automorphisms are Q-linear. If a map acts as the identity on the underlying Q-space, it acts as the identity on each vector of K^d and was the identity originally. Thus restriction of scalars is injective. Reverse inclusion Q subset Qbar is immediate. The equivalence concerns existence with dimension increase; it says nothing about maintaining d, integral coefficients, or finite generation of a group with infinitely many prescribed coefficient fields.

## The printed GL_2(Z) assertion fails, and only that assertion

Suppose B_3 injects into GL_2(Z). Its infinite-order central z would map to an infinite-order C. A scalar in GL_2(Z) is ±I, so C is nonscalar. For a nonscalar 2x2 rational matrix choose v with v,Cv independent (otherwise every line is invariant and C is scalar). If T commutes with C, express Tv=pv+qCv; then TCv=CTv=pCv+qC^2v, so T=pI+qC on this basis. Its centralizer is the commutative algebra Q[C]. The entire braid image, commuting with C, would be abelian; the quotient C_2*C_3 exhibits that B_3 is nonabelian. Contradiction. In GL_2(Q), scalar 2I has infinite order, so the crucial scalar step fails. In higher dimensions, a nonscalar centralizer need not be commutative. No extrapolation to these settings is justified.
