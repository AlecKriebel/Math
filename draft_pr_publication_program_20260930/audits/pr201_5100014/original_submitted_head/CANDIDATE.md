# k303,a: a two-pole proof candidate

**5100014 / AMR-050-0014. Author turn 1. Complete candidate, pending separate adversarial review.**

## 1. Target, conventions, and credited inputs

Let E be x²/a²+y²/b²=1, with a>b>0, and let C be the strictly interior confocal ellipse with squared semiaxes alpha²=a²−lambda and beta²=b²−lambda, where 0<lambda<b². Suppose their Poncelet family has primitive period N=2m, with m odd. Necessarily N≥6. Let P_i be the vertices in orbit order, T_i the intersections of consecutive tangents to E, and Q_i(M) the feet from a fixed real point M to the tangent to E at P_i. The Q_i are exactly the pedal vertices of the outer tangent polygon, up to cyclic indexing.

Write A, A', B_M for the signed shoelace areas of P, T, Q(M). The claim is that A' B_M is constant over the family for each fixed M. This is precisely Table 4, k303,a, in both [arXiv:2004.12497v11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12497v11), p. 6, and its [published companion](https://armj.math.stonybrook.edu/pdf-Springer-final/021-0174.pdf), p. 346. M is not restricted to a focus or the center. This statement does not concern pedals to the sides of P itself.

We use two published inputs from Chavez-Caliz, [More About Areas and Centers of Poncelet Polygons](https://armj.math.stonybrook.edu/pdf-Springer-final/020-0154.pdf), Arnold Math. J. 7 (2021), 91–106:

1. Section 2, p. 98, describes the smooth genus-one flag curve, its two involutions, and the Poncelet map as a translation. This is the standard Griffiths–Harris framework.
2. Theorems 3 and 6, pp. 93 and 104, prove that AA' is constant for even primitive period between concentric ellipses in complex-projective general position.

We also use the standard genus-one Riemann–Roch fact: meromorphic functions with at most simple poles at two prescribed distinct points form a two-dimensional vector space. The argument below proves the additional outer-pedal area relation. No novelty or priority assertion is made.

For completeness, the conics are transverse: with c²=a²−b², their four common points satisfy x²=a²alpha²/c² and y²=−b²beta²/c². All coordinates are nonzero. The coefficient determinant 1/(a²beta²)−1/(b²alpha²)=lambda c²/(a²b²alpha²beta²) is nonzero, excludes intersections at infinity, and makes their affine gradient determinants nonzero. Thus both published inputs apply.

## 2. The flag curve and its central involution

Let X consist of flags (P,l), where P lies on E and l is a tangent to C through P. Projection pi:X→E has degree two and branches at E∩C. It is a smooth connected genus-one curve. Define:

- sigma: replace P by the other intersection of l with E, keeping l;
- tau: keep P and replace l by the other tangent to C through P;
- T=tau sigma, the Poncelet translation, of exact order N.

We use composition from right to left. In particular, tau T^j=T^(−j) tau and sigma T^j=T^(−j) sigma. The vertices represented by x∈X are P_i=pi(T^i x).

Central inversion in the plane lifts to an involution I of X, commuting with T and preserving each real orientation component of X. On such a component, which is a circle, the finite group generated by I and T acts by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Averaging a Riemannian metric over this finite group conjugates it to a group of circle rotations. There is a unique nonidentity element of order two. Since both I and T^m are such elements, they agree on the real circle, hence on all of X by analytic continuation:

    I = T^m.                                                (1)

In particular, P_(i+m)=−P_i in affine coordinates. This includes primitive star orbits: their rotation number has odd numerator when N is even. Nontrivial translations of X have no fixed points.

Let Y=X/<T>. This is again a smooth genus-one curve, and X→Y is unramified. Areas of an N-gon are invariant under cyclic relabeling, so A and B_M descend to meromorphic functions on Y.

Reversing orientation is induced by tau: pi(T^i tau x)=pi(T^(−i)x). Consequently

    A(tau x)=−A(x),    B_M(tau x)=−B_M(x).                  (2)

The fixed point M is unchanged here.

## 3. At most two simple poles for A on Y

The outer ellipse E has two points at infinity, [a:ib:0] and [a:−ib:0]. Neither belongs to C, so each has two distinct preimages in X. The coordinate functions of P have simple poles there, since pi is unramified and E meets the line at infinity transversely.

The involution I fixes either projective point at infinity and has no fixed point on X. It therefore interchanges the two flags above each such point. By (1), these paired flags lie in the same T-orbit. Their images in Y constitute a set S of at most two points.

No two consecutive vertices can both be infinite. If they are distinct, their connecting line is the line at infinity, which is not tangent to C. If they are the same infinite point, the connecting line would be the tangent to E there. Its normal is proportional to (1/a,±i/b), and its C-dual quadratic value is

    alpha²/a² − beta²/b² = lambda(1/b²−1/a²) ≠ 0,

so it is not tangent to C. Thus every shoelace summand has at most one simple-pole factor. It follows that A has at most simple poles, all supported on S.

A is not constant. Choose a real oriented orbit with C on the left of every oriented side. Since the origin is strictly inside C, det(P_i,P_(i+1))>0 for every i, even for a star traversal; hence A>0. But (2) makes any globally constant A equal to zero. Thus A is nonconstant.

A genus-one curve has no nonconstant meromorphic function with at most one simple pole. Therefore S consists of exactly two distinct points, and A belongs to L(D), where D is their sum. Riemann–Roch gives dim L(D)=2, so 1 and A form a basis.

## 4. Potential poles of the outer pedal

Put B=diag(1/a²,1/b²), R(P)=BP and d(P)=R(P)·R(P), where the dot product is complex bilinear after complexification. The tangent to E at P is R(P)·z=1. Orthogonal projection of a fixed M onto this line is the rational expression

    Q(P;M)=M + (1−R(P)·M) R(P)/d(P).                      (3)

At either infinite point of E this expression is bounded. Indeed R has a simple pole with leading direction (1/a,±i/b), whose squared bilinear norm is 1/a²−1/b²≠0. The quotient in (3) therefore has a finite limit, including for arbitrary fixed M.

All remaining possible poles are at the four finite points with d(P)=0. They satisfy

    x²=a⁴/c²,    y²=−b⁴/c².                              (4)

These are four distinct simple zeros of d on E. Their tangents to E are also tangents to C: if R=BP, then

    alpha² R_x² + beta² R_y²
       = a² R_x² + b² R_y² − lambda d(P) = 1.

Conversely, the difference of the dual equations for a common tangent to E and C forces d(P)=0 at its E-contact point. Thus (4) describes precisely the four E-contact points of the common tangents.

These points do not lie on C. Direct substitution gives

    x²/alpha²+y²/beta² − 1 = −lambda²/(alpha²beta²) ≠ 0.

Hence pi is unramified over them, and each Q(P_i;M) has at most a simple pole on X. It remains to locate their T-orbits and control products of two singular feet in the area sum.

## 5. Odd m places those poles over S

Let z∈X be the common-tangent flag at one of the four points (4). Then sigma z=z. Since Tz=tau z, both flags over that point lie in the T-orbit of z.

Choose an integer k with 2k≡1−m (mod 2m), which exists because m is odd. Let w=T^k z. Using tau=T sigma gives

    tau w = T^(1−k) z = T^(m+k) z = Iw.

Projection pi is invariant under tau, so pi(w)=pi(Iw)=I pi(w). The only points of the projective conic E fixed by central inversion are its two points at infinity. Thus pi(w) is infinite. Every potential pedal-pole flag is therefore in a T-orbit lying over S.

This congruence is where N≡2 (mod 4) is used. It has no solution when m is even, so the proof does not silently extend to the excluded parity.

## 6. Double poles cancel in the shoelace sum

For the same z fixed by sigma, a point T^j z is fixed by sigma exactly when

    T^j z = T^(−j)z, equivalently N divides 2j.

Because T has exact order N, the only such j modulo N are 0 and m. Every flag over a point (4) either is fixed by sigma or is T times a sigma-fixed flag. Therefore, along this orbit, the only singular feet have indices

    0, 1, m, m+1,

after a cyclic relabeling. The first pair of corresponding vertices tends to the same point P, and the second pair to −P. Since m≥3, only the indices in each displayed pair are adjacent.

Take a local coordinate t on X at the considered flag. In each adjacent singular pair, formula (3) gives

    Q_0 = v_0/t + O(1),    Q_1 = v_1/t + O(1),

where both v_0 and v_1 are scalar multiples of the same vector R(P). This remains true if one residue vanishes for a special M. Thus det(v_0,v_1)=0, so det(Q_0,Q_1) has at most a simple pole. The opposite pair has the same property. Every other determinant term has at most one singular factor and hence also at most a simple pole.

It follows that B_M has at most simple poles on X, all lying above S. The quotient X→Y is unramified, so

    B_M ∈ L(D).

## 7. Finish the proof candidate

The basis in Section 3 gives constants c_M,d_M, independent of the orbit parameter, with

    B_M = c_M A + d_M.

Apply orientation reversal (2). It gives −B_M=−c_M A+d_M, while negating the preceding identity gives −B_M=−c_M A−d_M. Hence d_M=0 and

    B_M = c_M A.                                          (5)

The published even-period theorem supplies AA'=K for the fixed confocal pair and orbit family. Multiplying (5) by A' proves

    A' B_M = c_M K,

independent of the initial vertex. This proves the exact k303,a assertion if the preceding pole argument passes separate review.

All real tangent intersections are finite: parallel tangents to E would have antipodal contact points, whose center-crossing chord cannot be tangent to C. Formula (3) has positive denominator for real P, so the pedal is defined for every real M. No division by a pedal area is used. Signed zero pedal areas and self-intersecting pedals cause no exception.

The argument does not assert the adjacent odd-period k303,b statement. It also makes no claim for hyperbolic or degenerate caustics. Circular boundary cases are excluded by the source's a>b convention and are not asserted here.

## 8. Exact controls and limits

For a=2,b=1 and lambda=4/9, two symmetry-axis six-period orbits, already used as controls in the separate k108 work, give respectively

    A'_H = 16 sqrt(5)/5,
    B_(u,v),H = 5 sqrt(5)(u²+v²+9)/18;

    A'_V = 5 sqrt(2),
    B_(u,v),V = 4 sqrt(2)(u²+v²+9)/9.

Both products equal 40(u²+v²+9)/9. A third phase starting at (6/5,4/5), generated by exact reflection with Joachimsthal value 1/3, closes after six steps and gives

    A' = 29200 sqrt(557)/96361,
    B_0 = 173 sqrt(557)/730,
    A' B_0 = 40,    B_0/A = 9/8.

These finite checks corroborate the definitions and formulas only. They are not the proof for arbitrary N or arbitrary orbit parameter. `turn1_exact.py` and `turn1_generic_six.py` are author-written exact SymPy calculations. No downloaded source executable was run.
