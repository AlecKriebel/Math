## Exact quantifiers and real geometry

The hypotheses are a fixed pair with a>b>0 and 0<lambda<b², and exact order N=2m with odd m>=3. The conclusion quantifies over every fixed finite real M, then every initial phase of that fixed caustic family. No unsigned face area, moving M, hyperbolic/degenerate caustic, nonconfocal pair, or mere listed length is asserted. A primitive star has the same ordered cyclic shoelace algebra as a convex member. A chosen left-caustic orientation places the origin strictly on the left of each directed chord P_iP_(i+1). Its left-side test is det(P_i,P_(i+1))>0, so A>0 even with crossings. The reversed sequence makes A negative. This supplies nonconstancy as a complex meromorphic function, although the two orientations occur on distinct real components.

Parallel real boundary tangents of a centered ellipse have antipodal contact points. Their chord goes through the origin, so cannot support the strictly inner ellipse. Thus every real outer vertex exists finitely. The real normal squared norm is positive, independently of M. The proposed exceptions at zero or negative pedal area do not cause a division: none is used in the argument.

## Flag curve, exact order, and the half-period

Solving E and C gives x²=a²(a²-lambda)/(a²-b²) and y²=-b²(b²-lambda)/(a²-b²). The four sign choices are distinct, finite, and nonzero. The determinant of their conic gradients is a nonzero scalar multiple of xy lambda(a²-b²); hence all four intersections are transverse. A connected double cover of the conic with four simple branch points has genus one. Connectedness also follows because a degree-two cover with a branch point cannot split into two degree-one components. The incidence model is smooth at its finite simple branch points and the other finite/infinite unramified points.

Each involution has fixed points: tau above E∩C; sigma at the four common tangent flags independently identified by d=0 below. An involution with a fixed point on a genus-one curve has linear part -1. Their composition T=tau sigma has linear part +1 and is a translation. A single closed orbit forces the corresponding translation multiple to be zero; a smaller order would produce a smaller real flag period. Thus exact least period supplies exact order N, and all nonidentity powers of T are fixed-point-free.

Central inversion preserves each of the two real components determined by tangent traversal direction and commutes with both involutions. Each component is a circle because there are two distinct continuously varying real tangent choices above each real E-point. Inversion and the translation are orientation-preserving on it. Averaging any circle metric over their finite commuting group gives invariant arc length, so both I and T^m are nontrivial order-two rotations and are equal. Equality on a real circle has accumulation points in the complex curve, so extends globally. This proves I=T^m, including stars, without assuming the phase parametrization advances once around the ellipse.

The action of <T> is free. Riemann–Hurwitz therefore gives genus(Y)=1 and an unramified quotient. The cyclic area sums are invariant meromorphic functions and descend without pole-order change. Holding M fixed, tau reverses the vertex/foot list, so A and B_M are odd under the induced reversal. No reflection of M is needed.

## Original-area poles and Riemann–Roch

The two base infinities [a:±ib:0] lie off C. Their two flags are unramified, and the affine coordinate functions have simple poles there. I fixes each base infinity and exchanges its two flags: otherwise a nonidentity translation would have a fixed flag. Consequently the four flags give at most two quotient points S.

An adjacent pair of distinct infinities would require the chord to be the line at infinity, not tangent to C. A coincident pair would make the chord the E-tangent there. For normal (1/a,±i/b), the C dual value at w=0 is lambda(1/b²-1/a²), which is nonzero. Hence no adjacent pair has two coordinate poles. Each original shoelace term has at most a simple pole. Cancellation can reduce but cannot increase this bound.

The real positivity and reversal above rule out a constant A. On genus one, l(p)=1 for a point p, so no nonconstant function can have a single simple pole or none. Therefore S consists of exactly two different points and A has a genuine simple pole at each. For D their sum, deg D=2 and deg(K-D)=-2. Riemann–Roch gives l(D)=2. The independent functions 1,A are a basis. This step does not require guessing residues or assuming the two infinity orbits are different.

## Every foot pole, ramification, and the index list

Projection to n·Z=1 with n=HP gives Q=M+(1-n·M)n/(n·n). At a base infinity, n~r/t with r=(1/a,±i/b), r·r!=0. The numerator has at worst order two, denominator exactly order two, so Q is regular there even when M is nonzero. There are no missing foot poles at infinity.

The remaining zeros of d=n·n on E have x²=a⁴/(a²-b²), y²=-b⁴/(a²-b²). In the rational coordinate x=a(1-t²)/(1+t²), y=2bt/(1+t²), its numerator is b²t⁴+(4a²-2b²)t²+b². The quadratic in t² has nonzero discriminant 16a²(a²-b²), neither root zero, and no root t=±i. Thus all four d-zeros are simple.

At such a zero, the tangent normal satisfies both dual conic equations; conversely subtracting them gives lambda d=0. Its C-contact is P-lambda n, while P itself satisfies C(P)-1=-lambda²/((a²-lambda)(b²-lambda))!=0. Thus pi is unramified there, although rho is branched there. Confusing these two projections would invalidate the pole count; the manuscript keeps them separate.

For a sigma-fixed common-tangent flag z, Tz=tau z is exactly the other flag above P. The dihedral identities yield tau T^k z=T^(1-k)z. Since m is odd, 2k=1-m mod 2m has solutions, giving tau w=Iw for w=T^kz. Then pi(w)=I pi(w). The only projective fixed points of planar inversion on E are its two infinities. Thus each foot-pole orbit lies over S.

On that exact-length orbit, sigma T^jz=T^(-j)z is fixed precisely when 2j=0 mod 2m, so j=0,m. Every contact flag is either a sigma-fixed flag or its tau image (equivalently T times that fixed flag). Therefore the entire possible singular-index set is {0,1,m,m+1}, rather than merely a convenient subset. At m=3 the only cyclic adjacent pairs are still (0,1) and (3,4); for every larger odd m the same conclusion holds. The two vertices of each pair coincide at P or -P in the limiting complex configuration.

Every foot individually has a pole of order at most one. Its residue is a scalar multiple of the corresponding fixed limiting normal. Thus each adjacent singular determinant has zero t^-2 coefficient, since its two residues are parallel. This reasoning also covers 1-n·M=0, where a residue vanishes. Every other determinant has at most one singular factor. Therefore B_M has at most simple poles, supported on S, and belongs to L(D). Expanding B_M=c_M A+d_M and applying reversal gives d_M=-d_M, hence d_M=0. No quotient by an area, or by c_M, occurs.

## The original/outer product is independently justified

For chord L=[u:v:w], its E-pole W=[a²u:b²v:w] is the intersection of the two endpoint E-tangents. Thus its cyclic trace is the actual outer polygon area. Infinite W occurs only for w=0 on the dual C. Such a line has direction (±i alpha,-beta) and E-quadratic value lambda(1/a²-1/b²)!=0, hence two distinct finite opposite endpoint points. rho is unramified there and the dual-conic intersection with w=0 is transverse. So the W-coordinate poles are simple.

Two distinct adjacent w=0 lines would share their endpoint only at the origin, off E. If two successive lines coincided, the intermediate endpoint would be their C-contact at infinity, which is not on E. Hence A' has at most simple poles; call their possible quotient support R0.

For any original-infinity flag, tau x=Ix=T^m x, hence sigma x=T^(m-1)x. Solving 2k=m-1 makes a sigma-fixed representative in that orbit. For a w=0 flag, sigma x=Ix=T^m x, hence tau x=T^(m+1)x; solving 2k=m+1 makes a tau-fixed representative. Both congruences use odd m.

An orbit containing sigma z=z cannot contain any tau-fixed point, because the latter would force 2j=1 mod even N. Therefore S and R0 are disjoint. At a tau-fixed representative in R0, pi(T^jz)=pi(T^-jz), and all original vertices are finite because this orbit is outside S. The original signed area is zero by reversed cyclic order. This regular zero cancels every possible simple A' pole. At a sigma-fixed representative in S, rho(T^jz)=rho(T^-jz); all W are finite because S is outside R0. Thus A'=0 there, cancelling each A pole. All poles of AA' have been removed; a holomorphic function on compact Y is constant. Multiplying B_M=c_M A by A' proves the target, including c_M=0.
