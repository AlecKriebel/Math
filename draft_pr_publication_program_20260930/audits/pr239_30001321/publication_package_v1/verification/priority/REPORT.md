## Exact question being compared

The candidate concerns one transverse spatial dimension, discrete time, nearest-neighbor jumps, a fixed iid space-time law with 0<p<1 almost surely, an arbitrary deterministic interval partition of common length M_N -> infinity, and each fixed epsilon>0. The probability over environments of a coarse endpoint L1 discrepancy exceeding epsilon is O(N^(-K)) for every K. No uniform lower bound on p or 1-p, quantitative rate of M_N divergence, symmetry of the annealed drift, or uniformity over environment laws is imposed. The threshold may depend on the chosen sequence and law. Both real interval length and integer lattice-cell conventions are addressed by the candidate.

The comparison is to the negative d=1 Conjecture1 immediately following Theorem1 in Noam Berger's contribution, OWR38/2009, printedpp2151–2152, [DOI10.4171/OWR/2009/38](https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2009/38). ## Adversarial consequence check

A plausible attempt at reclassification is to declare an existing quenched local CLT or invariance principle automatically sufficient. This route is unsupported unless one supplies the missing uniform estimate for arbitrary shrinking macroscopic cell sizes and every inverse power of N. SCOPE_NONIMPLICATIONS.md gives explicit elementary counterexamples to those purely logical implications, not counterexamples to this RWRE theorem. A qualitative weak Gaussian limit permits persistent alternation between fine cells; almost-sure convergence alone permits logarithmically decaying bad-event probabilities. An L2 corrector estimate alone gives its second-moment tail, not arbitrary powers.

Conversely, the fact that these implications fail in general is not evidence of novelty. An older proof may contain stronger estimates than its principal theorem, or a result under different notation may already give the exact consequence. No absence-of-search-match conclusion closes that gap.
