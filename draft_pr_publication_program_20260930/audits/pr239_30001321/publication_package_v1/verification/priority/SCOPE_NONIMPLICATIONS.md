# Logical distinctions in the priority comparison

These elementary examples concern implications between theorem formats. They are not counterexamples generated by the space-time RWRE model.

1. Almost-sure convergence does not give superpolynomial environmental tails. Let a finite nonnegative integer T have P(T >= n) = 1/log(n+e) for n >= 1. Then Y_n = 1{T >= n} tends to zero almost surely, while P(Y_n > 1/2) decays more slowly than every inverse power. Thus a qualitative almost-sure invariance principle cannot by itself supply the required environmental probability bound.

2. A weak central limit theorem does not control shrinking coarse cells. Let alpha_n be the discrete Gaussian on the time-n parity sublattice, centered at vn with variance proportional to n. Choose M_n -> infinity with M_n/sqrt(n) -> 0, and put sigma_n(x) = +1 or -1 on alternating M_n-site cells. For fixed 0<c<1, define mu_n(x) = alpha_n(x)(1+c sigma_n(x))/(1+c S_n), where S_n = sum_x alpha_n(x)sigma_n(x). Adjacent-cell pairing, or a Riemann-sum estimate, gives S_n -> 0. Both diffusively rescaled laws have the same weak Gaussian limit. Nevertheless their coarse L1 difference is exactly c(1-S_n^2)/(1+c S_n), and hence tends to c. Parity only changes the Riemann-sum mesh. Consequently fixed smooth-test or weak distribution-topology statements need a separate uniform shrinking-cell argument to imply this PR's claim.

3. An L2 approximation to a stationary corrector and spatial covariance decay can suggest vanishing expected coarse discrepancy, but an L2 Markov inequality yields only its stated second-moment rate. Arbitrary inverse powers at fixed epsilon require additional concentration or arbitrarily high moment estimates with suitable uniformity. No logical implication from a theorem's title or from a Gaussian limiting field is used here.

The candidate's independent moving-strip concentration and preceding old-law anti-concentration are exactly the kinds of additional bounds that address these differences. This observation is about the structure of claims, not a proof that no prior author has already established equivalent bounds.
