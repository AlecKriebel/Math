## Exact claim being challenged

For a fixed iid array of nearest-neighbor right-jump probabilities with 0<p<1 almost surely, starting at zero, every deterministic positive real sequence M_N tending to infinity and every deterministic alignment b_N satisfy P(sum_cells |mu_N(I)-alpha_N(I)|>epsilon)=O(N^{-K}) for every fixed epsilon,K>0. Uniformity is in deterministic alignment for each N; law-dependent constants and sequence-dependent eventual thresholds are allowed. The norm is full l1, not half-l1. Nothing is claimed for random environment-selected partitions, varying laws, p in {0,1}, correlated/static environments, or unrestricted pointwise local limits.

   C sqrt[(s^(-2/3)+s^(-1))(1+log(2+s))] + C s^(-2/3).

   This deterministic alignment-uniform bound tends to zero whenever n and M both tend to infinity, with no monotonicity or rate assumption. Applying n=ell_N and M=M_N in the final conditional mean is therefore legitimate. This check is an alternative explanation of the written two-ordered-limit argument, not a necessary manuscript repair.

2. The final target-scale partition is never used in a sign-entropy count. Hence a target M_N=log(log(log N)) eventually, or any slower nonmonotone deterministic diverging sequence, creates only a potentially enormous eventual rho threshold; it does not change the final N^(1/32) deviation exponent. 3. For a genuinely excluded iid random-arrow law p in {0,1} with q in (0,1), a fixed environment gives a single deterministic path, so its quenched coarse law is a point mass.
