# Coordinate and theorem scope bridge

This is the reviewer's algebraic comparison of two already-read statements, not a new construction or proof-search route.

Candidate Section 3 uses C²+S²=1 and D²=C²/a²+S²/b². The supporting chord line is xC/a+yS/b=U. Set

    ν=(C/a,S/b)/D,   Hν=a²ν_x²+b²ν_y²=1/D².

Then

    ν_x²/Hν=C²/a²,     ν_xν_y/Hν=CS/(ab),
    ℓ=2ab√λ D²=2ab√λ/Hν.

For a focus F=(h,0), h²=c²=a²−b², put K=a²b²−λc² and κ=K/(b²−λ). The vector pair

    -2F + 2κ (F·ν)ν/Hν

becomes

    (-2h + 2hK C²/[a²(b²−λ)], 2hK CS/[ab(b²−λ)]),

which is exactly candidate equation (8). Averaging opposite pairs divides this vector by two. Candidate equations (5)–(6) have ⟨CS⟩=0 and

    Hcandidate=⟨C²⟩=(a²/c²)[1−bL/(2a√λ N)].

Its focal coefficient is consequently

    -1 + K Hcandidate/[a²(b²−λ)]
    = -1 + K/[(b²−λ)c²] [1−b²L/(2Nab√λ)],

identical to the later paper's visually inspected equation (4).

Scope matches for a>b>0, 0<λ<b² and even least period. The later statement permits repeated traversals of such a primitive orbit; both centroids and L/N are unchanged. It does not rehabilitate a twice-listed odd primitive orbit. Both statements separately handle the circle. Both concern unweighted antipedal vertex means, not area centers, pedal feet or outer-polygon antipedals. No division by c² is used for the circle.

This equivalence identifies the same mathematical conclusion and the same pair/moment mechanism. It does not establish either author's absolute priority, independent discovery or that the conclusion follows immediately from every cited classical theorem.
