## Why the all-period argument survives the attacks

The tangent map preserves circle orientation and commutes with central inversion; central inversion preserves the chosen side of the directed tangent chord. The submitted averaging argument produces a nonatomic, full-support invariant measure for the finite group generated by the two maps, so its cumulative coordinate genuinely conjugates that group to rotations. Uniqueness of the half-turn then gives the antipodal half-period for even least period. It does not assume a simple polygon or winding 1. The independently frozen proof instead uses freeness and cyclic order on a finite orbit, providing a separate check of the same consequence.

The first variation is taken on the outer ellipse, with each chord's endpoint-angle difference held fixed while the common midpoint angle changes. Caustic tangency is imposed only after differentiation. This avoids a factor-of-two error and avoids differentiating an identity that is invalid along the comparison deformation. Reflection makes each perimeter gradient normal to the ellipse, so both the vertical midpoint sum and the constancy of perimeter along the actual family follow. Self-crossing stars cause no change to this local calculation.

The focal line systems give precisely the announced pair mean and denominators. In particular a²cos²d−c²cos²t=g(b²−lambda)/b²>0; the origin and foci lie strictly inside the caustic and cannot lie on its tangent chords. The line intersections are finite and unique. Averaging the pair identity and using the two stationary sums gives the announced centroid, rather than merely a numerical invariant.

Reversal interchanges the two endpoint equations and preserves the edge intersection. The opposite focus follows either from the h=−c calculation or central symmetry. The proof excludes lambda=0, lambda=b², degenerate two-bounce trajectories and the singular c² denominator. A circle has coincident foci and origin and is treated separately; no value of the displayed H is asserted at c=0.
