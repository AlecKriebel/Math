# A perimeter formula for the focal antipedal vertex centroid (k405)

**5100023 / AMR-050-0023. Complete proof candidate, awaiting independent review.** Two substantive approaches; no novelty or human peer-review claim.

## 1. Exact statement and conventions

Put O=(0,0), a>b>0, c=sqrt(a²−b²), and f_sigma=(sigma c,0), sigma=±1. Fix the outer ellipse E: x²/a²+y²/b²=1 and the strictly nested confocal elliptical caustic

    E_lambda: x²/(a²−lambda)+y²/(b²−lambda)=1,  0<lambda<b².

Consider a Poncelet billiard family of even least period N. Both simple and star polygons are allowed. For an edge A=P_i, B=P_(i+1), define Q_(M,i) as the intersection of the full lines through A and B perpendicular to A−M and B−M. The vertex centroid is C_M=(1/N) sum_i Q_(M,i). These are the unprimed antipedals of the orbit, not the antipedals of its outer tangent polygon or the pedal feet.

Let L be the common orbit perimeter and put

    K=a²b²−lambda c²,
    H=(a²/c²) [1−b L/(2a sqrt(lambda) N)].

Then

    C_O=(0,0),
    C_(f_sigma)=(sigma c [-1+K H/(a²(b²−lambda))], 0).       (1)

In particular all three centroids are fixed over the family, proving k405 in the source's nondegenerate confocal-ellipse setting.

The primary definition is Reznik–Garcia–Koiller, *Eighty New Invariants in the Elliptic Billiard*, arXiv:2004.12497v11, Sections 1–2, 3.5 and Table 5 (printed p.7). The published companion *Fifty New Invariants*, Table 5, p.348, retains k405 with even N and M=O,f_1,f_2. The source's introduction specifies a pair of confocal ellipses, rather than a hyperbolic caustic. Its N-periodic polygon is understood with N distinct successive vertices (least period N). Repeating an even-period polygon leaves its centroid unchanged. Relabeling a repeated odd-period polygon with an even number of entries is not this parity convention and is not asserted here. Two-bounce degenerate caustics, infinite antipedal vertices and hyperbolic caustics are excluded. For a circle the foci coincide with O and the conclusion is immediate by central symmetry.

## 2. Why even-period orbits are centrally symmetric

Let T be the oriented Poncelet tangent map of the outer oval, choosing the tangent with the inner oval on the left. It is an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism, commutes with S(P)=−P, and T^N=id by the Poncelet family premise. Its order is N: any earlier identity would contradict the least period. Central inversion preserves the chosen side of each oriented chord, so it commutes with T rather than reversing it.

Here is an elementary finite-action justification of the half-period assertion, avoiding an assumed pairing. The group G generated by the commuting finite-order maps T and S is finite. Average arc-length probability measure over G. The resulting measure is G-invariant, nonatomic and positive on every nonempty arc. Its cumulative-coordinate homeomorphism conjugates every member of G to a rotation: an orientation-preserving map preserving this measure preserves lengths of all arcs in the new coordinate. A rotation group has exactly one element of order two. Both S and T^(N/2) have order two, hence they agree. Thus

    P_(i+N/2)=−P_i.                                         (2)

The existence and orientation property of the tangent map are part of the ordinary Poncelet construction: through a point outside a smooth strictly convex inner oval there are two tangents, and choosing the inner oval to the left selects one directed forward chord. The other tangent defines the inverse. For the present nonsingular nested ellipses these maps are smooth. No assumption of convexity of the full star polygon is used.

For M=O the two defining antipedal lines on an opposite edge are the negatives of the original lines. Their intersections therefore pair as Q and −Q, proving C_O=0.

## 3. Chord coordinates and the two sums

Orient every chord with the caustic to its left. Since O is inside the caustic, each directed edge has det(A,B)>0. Write its endpoint eccentric angles as s−delta and s+delta, with 0<delta<pi/2, and set

    C=cos s, S=sin s, U=cos delta, V=sin delta,
    A=(a(CU+SV), b(SU−CV)),
    B=(a(CU−SV), b(SU+CV)),
    D²=C²/a²+S²/b².

Its line is x C/a+y S/b=U. The support function of E_lambda gives

    U²=1−lambda D²,   V²=lambda D².                         (3)

Consequently its ordinary positive length is

    ell=2V sqrt(a²S²+b²C²)=2ab sqrt(lambda) D².              (4)

Summing (4), and using D²=1/b²−c² C²/(a²b²), yields

    (1/N) sum_i C_i²=H.                                    (5)

For completeness the other sum follows from first variation, rather than an unproved symmetry of chord directions. A billiard polygon is a stationary point of perimeter under independent motions of its vertices along E. Indeed the coefficient of a displacement dP_i in dL is the incoming unit vector minus the outgoing unit vector; the reflection law says this vector is normal to E. It therefore pairs to zero with every allowed tangent displacement. This proof applies equally to star polygons.

Apply this stationarity to the simultaneous eccentric-angle variation t_i -> t_i+epsilon. This is an allowed comparison deformation on E, although its deformed chords need not retain the caustic. For each original chord delta is fixed in this deformation and s changes to s+epsilon. Direct differentiation of the first expression in (4), before imposing any constraint on the deformed chord, gives at epsilon=0

    d ell/d epsilon
       =2V (a²−b²) S C /sqrt(a²S²+b²C²)
       =2 sqrt(lambda) c² S C/(ab).

Thus, since c>0 and lambda>0,

    sum_i S_i C_i=0.                                       (6)

The same first-variation identity proves that L is constant along the smooth Poncelet family: its derivative along that family is zero. Continuity makes the constant global on the circle parameterizing the family. Hence using L in (5) does not import a new unproved invariant.

## 4. The focal opposite-edge identity

For a focus f=(h,0), h=±c, let Q_f(A,B) denote the antipedal intersection in absolute coordinates. Put m=(aCU,bSU) and v=(−aSV,bCV), so A=m−v and B=m+v. With q=Q_f−f, adding and subtracting the two defining equations gives the 2 by 2 system

    q dot (m−f)=|m−f|²+|v|²,
    q dot v=2(m−f) dot v.                                  (7)

The identical system for the opposite edge replaces m,v by −m,−v while keeping f fixed. Solving these two systems and adding, then using h²=a²−b², C²+S²=U²+V²=1 and (3), gives

    Q_f(A,B)+Q_f(−A,−B)
      = ( −2h + 2h K C²/[a²(b²−lambda)],
                    2h K SC/[ab(b²−lambda)] ).             (8)

For an explicit intermediate calculation, before substituting the tangency identity the two coordinates of this sum are

    2h b² (C²−U²)/(b²−lambda),
    −2CS a h [c²(C²−U²)−b²] /
                         [b a² (U²−c² C²/a²)].

These expressions follow directly from (7); the first has already used the denominator simplification below. Now

    U²−c² C²/a²=(b²−lambda)D²,
    c²(C²−U²)−b²=−K D²,
    b²(C²−U²)=−(b²−lambda)+K C²/a²,

which proves (8) without any orbit-specific cancellation. In particular all denominators used are nonzero. More geometrically, both foci are strictly inside E_lambda and no tangent chord can pass through either focus; the two vectors A−f,B−f are independent. The origin is also strictly inside E_lambda. Thus every antipedal intersection in this proof is finite and uniquely defined.

By (2) the sum of Q_f over all edges equals one half of the sum over all edges of the left side of (8). Divide by N and use (5)–(6). This gives exactly (1), completing the proof.

## 5. Evidence, credit and limitations

The written proof establishes the identities for all admissible real parameters and even least periods. The exact-rational verifier separately solves antipedal line systems on many chords; checks (3), (4) with squares where appropriate, (8), and the differentiated length identity; and checks the half-period pairing for all tested reduced rational rotation numbers. Those controls are not a numerical proof of periodic billiard dynamics. The finite-action argument and first variation supply that all-period step.

The chord parametrization, support-function identity and focal-height denominator are reused from this campaign's separately reviewed k603 package (ID5100032, PR110). That result concerns equality of sums of focal distances; it does not establish the centroid identity here. Central symmetry, perimeter conservation and billiard stationarity are classical facts, whose needed arguments are supplied above. A targeted current primary-source search located the source conjecture and related invariant papers but did not establish priority for this formula; no novelty claim is made.
