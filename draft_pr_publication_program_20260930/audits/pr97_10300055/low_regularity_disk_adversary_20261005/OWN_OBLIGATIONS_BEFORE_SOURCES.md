1. **Low regularity identity.** For C1 alpha and omega, d alpha is a continuous form, but the equation makes its coefficients C1. Distributionally d(d alpha)=0. The ordinary C1 product rule for alpha wedge omega then gives alpha wedge d omega=0 pointwise. This is promising and does not require alpha C2.
2. **Pencil.** Constant s gives beta_s wedge d beta_s=omega wedge d omega. This is straightforward exterior algebra; a variable s contributes a dh term, so it must not enter the path. On a finite interval both beta_s and d beta_s have finite uniform bounds.
3. **Compactness and signs.** Contact volume has a positive minimum on each closed component after orienting by its sign. The defining form has a positive norm minimum, so beta_s/s tends uniformly to alpha. A finite S, not an infinite endpoint, must enter the contact neighborhood. Disconnected compact manifolds have finitely many components and admit a common S.
4. **Approximation.** Independent smooth approximations of alpha and omega must have errors small relative to 1+S. No Frobenius identity survives approximation, but none is needed if the entire finite path stays C1 close to the contact pencil. The endpoint must stay in a *neighborhood in which all contacts are tight*, not just be close to one tight approximation. The imported theorem's exact regularity, tautness, coorientation, excluded-leaf hypotheses and contact sign require checking.
5. **Gray.** eta+s a is smooth in x and s, each form is contact, and the manifold is closed. Standard smooth Gray is therefore enough. No low-regularity Gray flow is implicitly permitted.
6. **Overtwisted criterion.** The candidate calls a disk overtwisted when its boundary is Legendrian and the disk tangent plane is everywhere distinct from the contact plane along the boundary. This differs from the familiar model whose boundary tangent plane agrees with the contact plane. It may be an equivalent tb=0 criterion, but this must be verified from primary sources. For C1 plane fields, the meaning of tightness and allowed disk regularity must be specified; one cannot silently invoke a smooth Darboux theorem.

A global S1 coordinate has a smooth closed form dt; no real-valued global t function is required.
