## Claim being tested

For a closed oriented smooth three-manifold M with cooriented taut C2 foliation F without spherical leaves, global nowhere-zero C1 alpha defining F, and smooth contact omega with d alpha = alpha wedge omega, ordinary smooth ker omega is tight. A separate supplement admits C1 omega and proves absence of embedded C2 disks with Legendrian boundary and distinct disk/contact planes on the boundary, plus tightness of all sufficiently C1-close smooth contact forms. No contact antecedent is constructed; no weak-sign existence question is resolved.

## Reconstruction independent of prior audit authority

1. Apply d distributionally to d alpha = alpha wedge omega. Distributional derivatives commute, so d squared alpha = 0. The right side is C1, whose exterior derivative obeys the classical product rule: d alpha wedge omega minus alpha wedge d omega. Substitution leaves minus alpha wedge d omega, since alpha wedge omega wedge omega=0. This is continuous and therefore its distributional zero is pointwise zero. No second classical derivative of alpha is needed.
2. The three possible extra terms in (omega+s alpha) wedge d(omega+s alpha) vanish separately: omega wedge d alpha=omega wedge alpha wedge omega=0; alpha wedge d omega=0 as just derived; alpha wedge d alpha=0. Thus the contact volume equals omega wedge d omega for every real constant s. All forms are global. The defining relation persists under this constant shift. A spatially varying shift adds a dh term and is excluded.
3. Fix a metric, m=min|alpha|>0 and W=max|omega|. For s>2W/m, alpha+omega/s is uniformly bounded away from zero. Normalizing it gives uniform convergence to alpha/|alpha|, hence uniform convergence of kernels. Compactness is needed here and for all later minima; no infinity-time isotopy is used.
4. Vogel's actual 2011 p.42 theorem says that a contact structure sufficiently C0-close to a taut foliation is fillable and tight. It is an every-nearby-contact statement, not merely existence of one tight approximation. Dathe–Rukimbira Prop.3.3 corroborates it. The source's C2 threshold is retained. Select a finite S whose plane field lies in this open neighborhood.
5. On [0,S], beta_s=omega+s alpha is C1 and has fixed contact volume. On each component orient an auxiliary volume form so that c(x)>0. The compact union has a positive contact-volume minimum c0 and finite beta/dbeta bound B. Smooth alpha only, retaining smooth omega, and set lambda_s=omega+s a. Its uniform contact error is bounded by 2B delta+delta squared when both form/derivative errors are at most delta, so choose this <c0/2. Density of smooth sections in C1 follows by finite chart mollification and partition of unity. Simultaneously choose the smoothing small enough that the finite endpoint's kernel remains in the open C0 neighborhood. The approximant need not be integrable.
6. Lambda is now a jointly smooth family on the finite closed parameter interval; all lambda_s are smooth contact forms, with lambda_0 exactly omega. Apply precisely Vogel 2016 Theorem 2.9 (smooth family of smooth contact structures on a closed manifold). The smooth isotopy transports the absence of an overtwisted disk. Negative sign is handled by changing the auxiliary orientation on the relevant component. Compact M has finitely many components; choose a common S and uniform bounds. No sign-dependent tightness notion is introduced.

## Supplement reconstructed separately

For C1 omega, select the same finite S and a sufficiently C1-close smooth a. For every smooth eta sufficiently C1-close to omega, eta+s a is uniformly close to beta_s on [0,S]. The same margin and open endpoint neighborhood work for all such eta; Gray therefore makes every such eta ordinarily tight. This claim by itself does not assume a definition of low-regularity tightness.

To exclude an embedded C2 disk D with the stated boundary condition, smooth its embedding in C2 (and its boundary), preserving embeddings and distinct disk/contact planes by compact openness. Choose one fixed smooth tubular chart around a smooth approximation to the original C2 boundary; projection onto its circle coordinate has nonzero derivative, so nearby boundaries can be reparametrized as graphs gamma_j(t)=(t,u_j(t),v_j(t)) converging in C2 to gamma. Smooth theta_j approaches theta in C1. Then q_j(t)=theta_j(gamma_j'(t)) approaches theta(gamma'(t))=0 in C1: differentiation uses D theta_j and gamma_j'', explaining the C2 boundary threshold. Subtract q_j(t) chi(u-u_j(t),v-v_j(t)) dt. The chart is fixed, graph first derivatives are bounded, and cutoff derivatives are fixed, so the correction tends to zero in C1. Choose one uniformly small support tube whose closure remains in the chart; extension by zero is smooth. Since dt(gamma_j')=1 and chi(0)=1, the corrected form annihilates gamma_j' exactly. Contactness persists by the C1 margin, and transversality along the compact boundary persists from theta and D. This produces arbitrarily close smooth overtwisted forms, contradicting nearby-smooth tightness. No assertion about C1 disks or other low-regularity definitions follows.
