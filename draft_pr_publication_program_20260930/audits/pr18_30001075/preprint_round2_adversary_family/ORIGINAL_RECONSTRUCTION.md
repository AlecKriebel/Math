## Claim and proof obligation
For arbitrary pairwise disjoint convex subsets of R^3, the spatial union of complete affine lines meeting each set and contained in a support plane of each must have Lebesgue outer measure zero. Empty sets make the family empty. No closedness, boundedness, interior, unique contacts, strict convexity, measurability or smoothness is permitted as an extra hypothesis. Stronger countable surface containment is not established.

## Reconstruction
1. A contact in each set can be surrounded by mutually disjoint closed rational balls with the contacts strictly inside. Intersection of each ball with the closure of its convex set is a nonempty compact convex set. The original supporting inequality persists on the closure, and the selected contact still meets the plane. Thus the original line belongs to a compact-cap tritangent family. There are countably many triples of globally fixed rational balls. Crucially, disjoint balls do not require disjoint original closures.
2. A nonhorizontal line chart has transverse intercept u and slope v, with points (u+zv,z). A compact convex set projects to pi_v(C)={x-zv}. If this projected set has two-dimensional interior, line tangency is exactly the boundary condition u in boundary(pi_v(C)). The support gap g=max over unit n of n.u-h_C(n,-n.v) detects this boundary by sign. It is jointly Lipschitz, and the signed increment lies between min and max of n.(du+z dv) over C. A planar convex set projects with interior precisely for directions transverse to its affine plane; retained lines through it are transverse. Contained lines lie in the affine plane and have a null spatial union.
3. At two distinct contacts s<t of dimension>=2 compact sets, their projected normal cones each lie in an open hemisphere because an interior projected disk is strictly behind every support line through the contact. Local rational caps retain the normal cone: a putative new supporting functional positive elsewhere in the original set would already be positive along a short segment from the selected contact lying inside the cap. They also retain affine dimension by scaling an affine basis toward that contact.
4. Choose cap height widths epsilon*(t-s), where epsilon<alpha_i/16. Motions V_A and V_B move e_A at s and zero at t, and vice versa. They are independent even for parallel/opposite e_i. Extend to four linear line coordinates (a,b,r). Near the base line all maximizing normals retain n.e_i>=alpha_i/2 by compactness of S^1 and continuity of the support gaps. Own increments have lower slope alpha_i/4=m_i; cross increments have absolute slope<=epsilon. Residual slopes K_i are finite locally. No uniform global constant is needed.
5. Choose a common square |a|,|b|<=delta in the good coordinate box and a residual ball |r|<eta with K_i eta<(m_i-epsilon)delta/2 for both i. At +/-delta the own gap has strict opposite signs, uniformly over the other coordinate and r. Therefore each has a unique root rho_i, Lipschitz in the other coordinate and residual variable with slopes epsilon/m_i and K_i/m_i. The coupled root map sends the closed square strictly inside it and is a contraction in max norm with kappa=max epsilon/m_i<1/4. The unique fixed point is Lipschitz in r by comparison of fixed points. This provides a map from an open two-dimensional residual domain into line space covering the simultaneous zero family for these fixed caps locally.
6. Local caps cannot be treated as a neighborhood cover of original-set tangents: contact can leave them. The actual countability mechanism is different and valid: first fix each globally rational cap pair P; let T_P contain the lines where the quantitative construction is available directly for that pair. Every such line has a neighborhood where all tangents to P are on its graph. A countable subcover of T_P exists by second countability. All original common tangents belong to at least one T_P by localization. This order preserves membership in the same caps when passing to a selected neighborhood.
7. A compact tangency family is closed: along converging lines, compact contacts and unit supporting normals have converging subsequences; normal inequality and line incidence persist. Pulling back through a Lipschitz chart produces a Borel tangency parameter set. Removing affine-plane-contained lines and identical interval-hull lines is a Borel exclusion. Their swept loci are already null.
8. At almost every selected parameter q, the parameter set has density one and the map is differentiable. Density one gives selected parameters q+tau*h+o(tau) for every h. This follows because a hole of radius comparable to tau at q+tau*h contradicts density one in a ball centered at q of comparable radius. Shear the base line to the height axis. If M=A+z_i B were onto at an actual contact, choose h mapping to the transverse displacement from that contact to an interior/relative-interior center.
9. In dimension three, a point on the selected nearby line at height z_i+tau(c_z-z_i) differs by o(tau) from the convex interpolated ball center, and the convex set contains an open ball of radius tau*r there. This contradicts supporting tangency. In dimension two, transversality gives a smooth intersection-height formula; its first variation matches c_z-z_i. The nearby line passes through a relative interior disk. A support plane at a relative interior point must equal the affine hull, which cannot contain a transverse line. In dimension one, the affine line direction has nonzero transverse component, since an identical hull line was excluded. Incidence yields u+z_i v=(d_x-d_z v)lambda, lambda=O(|q-q0|); projecting perpendicular to d_x leaves an O(tau^2) term, proving rank<=1. In dimension zero the fixed-contact incidence gives M=0 directly.
10. Three contacts have distinct heights because the sets are disjoint. det(A+zB) is a polynomial of degree at most two. Rank<=1 at three heights forces it identically zero, so the complete spatial sweep G(q,z) has zero three-dimensional Jacobian at every height of every good parameter. On bounded parameter and height patches G is Lipschitz. The bad parameter set times a bounded interval is a null three-dimensional set. The measurable-subset area formula therefore makes every selected swept patch null, including all exceptional fibers. Countable patches exhaust all finite heights and parameters; extra chart lines create no issue since only the Borel tritangent subset is swept.
11. For the remaining dimensional cases, point incidence gives smooth direction charts. With no point and fewer than two dimension>=2 sets, there are two nondegenerate compact intervals. Their two endpoint parameters give smooth line charts whenever one direction coordinate is nonzero. Compact disjointness bounds the contact-point difference away from zero; an open rectangle can extend the interval parameters beyond endpoints without vanishing that chosen coordinate. The true compact tangency subset is selected afterward. These charts cover every retained common tangent.

## Attempted falsifications and disposition
- Nonclosed sets with intersecting closures: caps inside mutually disjoint balls prevent accidental closure intersections; no closure disjointness assumption used.
- Unbounded sets: each relevant actual contact localizes; the countable compact proof needs no radius common to all lines.
- Planar hulls, intervals and singleton sets: affine-dimensional proof covers each separately; contained hull lines are explicitly added as null loci.
- Normal-cone corner and nonsmooth support: maximizing-normal compactness replaces differentiability; local sign increment applies to an initial maximizer. No unique normal/contact is assumed.
- Opposite e_A/e_B and arbitrarily short contact separation: contact evaluation ensures two motions independent; only local constants depend on d and alpha.
- Fixed-cap countability: checked the quantifier order; arbitrary nearby original tangents need not meet the base cap, but the argument only passes between tangents of a fixed cap pair.
- Density at a lower-dimensional parameter subset: density is used only at almost every selected parameter; a measure-zero circle of vertical ball tangents gives no contradiction because all such parameters are exceptional.
- Contact segments or more than one contact: proof may select any one actual contact from each set; disjointness alone guarantees the three selected heights are distinct.
- Collapsing or noninjective line charts: area formula with multiplicity and a measurable subset gives image nullness without injectivity.
- Exceptional fibers over a null two-parameter set: product with bounded height intervals is a null three-dimensional domain set; Lipschitz images are null, and countable bounded patches exhaust all fibers.
- Two tangencies: explicit rectangle family has determinant z(z-1), hence positive volume; cannot weaken three distinct contacts to two.
- Repeated singleton sets: all lines through the common point fill space; cannot remove disjointness.
- Endpoints of intervals: extending affine contact parameters to open rectangles is legitimate because actual parameter subset is then selected, and disjoint compact sets supply nonvanishing direction.
- Arbitrary-set measurability: the compact tangency subsets are Borel; arbitrary-set locus is only required to have outer measure zero and is contained in countably many compact-cap null loci.
