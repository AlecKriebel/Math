## Actual theorem bodies inspected

**Nonsmooth inverse functions.** Clarke, *On the inverse function theorem*, Pacific J. Math. 64(1) (May 1976), 97–102, Theorem 1, printed p.98, gives a local Lipschitz inverse when every matrix in the generalized Jacobian is nonsingular. The proof was inspected through its uniform lower-Lipschitz and openness steps. The candidate's own-motion/cross-motion inequalities verify this condition once its geometric cap construction has been established. Thus Clarke can replace the contraction stage; it does not prove those cap inequalities or a countable covering of every bitangent. [Original publisher full text](https://msp.org/pjm/1976/64-1/pjm-v64-n1-p07-p.pdf).

**Focal geometry.** Guilfoyle–Klingenberg, *The Geometry of Focal Sets*, arXiv v2 (29 November 2005), §5, Theorem 4 and Proposition 4, give a quadratic focal equation along a differentiable line congruence and the connection to tangent surfaces. Their optical-scalar formula divides by a sweep determinant at a regular reference point. The candidate instead needs a determinant that vanishes identically on almost every line belonging to a measurable parameter subset. The classical quadratic mechanism deserves credit, but the theorem cannot simply be invoked for that singular regime or to create the arbitrary-convex charts. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411189v2).

**DC normal cycles and common normals.** Fu–Pokorný–Rataj, *Kinematic formulas for sets defined by differences of convex functions*, Advances in Math. 311 (2017), 796–832: Lemma 3.2, printed p.14, proves finite (d−1)-dimensional Minkowski content for differences of contact points sharing one outward normal on two arbitrary compact convex sets. Theorem A controls pairs consisting of a direction of a nondegenerate boundary segment and a supporting-plane normal. The proofs, printed pp.15–18, were inspected: parallel enlargement, cap packing and two parallel sections. These are substantial nonsmooth geometry results. Independent supporting normals on a tritangent are not the same input; replacing the objects by their convex hull can lose legitimate contacts. Theorem B's intersection/kinematic statement holds for almost every group motion, which cannot be substituted for a prescribed triple. [Primary version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.03388v4), [publisher metadata](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001870816307903).

**DC touching and structure.** Pokorný–Rataj, *Normal cycles and curvature measures of sets with d.c. boundary*, Advances in Math. 248 (2013), 963–985, Theorem 1.2, Proposition 7.1 and Lemma 8.4 establish normal cycles and null families of weakly tangent hyperplanes/flats in their invariant parameter measures. Pokorný–Rataj–Zajíček, *On the structure of WDC sets*, Math. Nachr. 292 (2019), 1595–1626, Theorem 6.20 and Corollary 6.22 give DC surface covers at the relevant topological dimension **if** a set is compact WDC or closed locally WDC. Their theorem proofs and weak-regularity definitions were inspected. No input theorem was found making the complete common-bitangent locus WDC of dimension at most two. Assuming that transfers the central covering difficulty. Invariant zero measure in four-dimensional line space also does not imply a null three-dimensional sweep. [2013 primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.2305v4), [structure primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.09520v2).

**Tangent hyperplanes to DC graphs.** Pavlica–Zajíček, *On the Directions of Segments and r-Dimensional Balls on a Convex Surface*, J. Convex Analysis 14 (2007), 149–167: Theorem 4.3 and Propositions 4.9–4.10, printed pp.158–160, give Hausdorff-size bounds for tangent hyperplanes to DC graphs, including planes through a fixed point or parallel to a fixed line. Their constructions and Fenchel-duality arguments were inspected. This controls tangent **hyperplanes of a specified DC graph**. An encoding of all arbitrary-convex common tangent lines into such a graph, preserving independent support planes and the spatial sweep, remains unverified. [Publisher full text](https://journalofconvexanalysis.com/articles/jca14012/jca14012.pdf?v=2).

**Planar support rectifiability.** Czédli–Stachó, *A note and a short survey on supporting lines of compact convex sets in the plane*, author manuscript dated 19 December 2016, Theorem 1, proves a rectifiable pointed-support curve for every compact planar convex set. Its first proof gives a local bi-Lipschitz parameterization of a convex subdifferential graph by x+slope. The arbitrary-nonsmooth planar result merits contextual credit. A fixed-plane theorem does not provide simultaneous uniform two-parameter charts as the slicing plane varies through all spatial bitangents. [Author full text](https://www.math.u-szeged.hu/~czedli/m/publ.pdf/czedli-stacho_a-note-and-a-short-survey-on-supporting-lines-of-compact-convex-sets-in-the-plane.pdf).

**Known measure-theoretic conclusion.** Simon's 2018 NTU notes, Ch.1 Corollary 3.10, Ch.2 Theorems 1.4 and 3.3, printed pp.18,45,57–58, were independently inspected. The area formula applies to locally Lipschitz maps on open domains and arbitrary measurable subsets and counts multiplicity; it needs no injectivity. This exactly supplies the candidate's concluding measure-theoretic implication once its covering and almost-everywhere rank hypotheses are proved. These tools are not a prior resolution by themselves. [Author notes](https://math.stanford.edu/~lms/ntu-gmt-text.pdf).
