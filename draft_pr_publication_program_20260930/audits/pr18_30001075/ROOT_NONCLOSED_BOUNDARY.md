# Independent boundary check for the background closed-locus assertion

Take K_i={(i,y,0):0<y<1}, i=0,1,2. These are pairwise disjoint convex nonclosed sets. For every n>=2 the point p_n=(0,1/n,0) lies on the horizontal line y=1/n,z=0, which meets all three sets and is contained in their common supporting plane z=0. Thus p_n is in the common-tangent locus and p_n tends to p=(0,0,0).

If a common tangent through p existed, its contact with K_0 would be a distinct point (0,y,0) with y>0. The two points force the whole affine line to have x=0,z=0, so it cannot meet K_1. Consequently p is not in the locus. The locus is not closed, although it is contained in the null plane z=0.

This is a counterexample to extending the older background closedness statement to arbitrary nonclosed convex sets. It does not contradict the candidate measure-zero theorem, whose compact-cap reduction and closed compact tangency-parameter sets are separate. Source-background wording should specify its closed/compact convention rather than export closedness to the stronger arbitrary-set scope. This is an audit boundary control, not a new problem attempt or priority assertion.
