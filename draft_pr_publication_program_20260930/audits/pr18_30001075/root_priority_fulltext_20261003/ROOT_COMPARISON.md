## Exact target being compared

PR18's current mathematical claim is Conjecture4 of Goaoc's problem in Oberwolfach Report44/2008, printed2552: for three pairwise disjoint arbitrary convex subsets of R3, the union of affine lines that meet each set and lie in a supporting plane of each is contained in a Lebesgue-null subset of R3. Supporting planes may be different for different sets; contacts are actual, not limiting contacts. The theorem imposes no closedness, boundedness, smoothness, full-dimensionality or genericity assumptions. It does not establish the stronger countable-union-of-2-manifolds Conjecture3.

The candidate route requires a countable cover of the relevant bitangent lines by Lipschitz two-parameter graphs, a rank restriction at each of three distinct actual contact heights on density points of the selected tritangent parameter subset, and a quadratic determinant identity followed by the equal-dimensional area formula. No theorem about topology or mere existence of transversals supplies those metric estimates automatically.

## Comparison with every principal published result

1. Theorem1.1 and Section2 (printed676,681–684) construct finite weak-net counterexamples. Lemma2.1 selects prescribed finite subsets of one ruling of a hyperbolic paraboloid using compact convex polygons; Lemma2.2 and subsequent inflation/perturbation turn that construction into finite families in general position. These are existence/counterexample assertions for meeting transversals. They neither bound the spatial sweep of all supporting tritangents of a fixed arbitrary triple nor yield the required bitangent chart cover. The added lifting on printed683–684 replaces R3 by V×W and lines by k-flats in R^(k+2), and then uses a generic affine3-flat section to reduce the finite separation construction to Lemma2.1. Its generality in k does not provide a nonsmooth metric regularity theorem for a fixed triple in R3.

2. Proposition1.2 and Section3 (printed677,684–689) give nine blue lines and thirteen red lines with a robust convex-selection obstruction. Claims3.1–3.2 use joint regions of three separated planar rays and projections along cube diagonals. Those claims force a convex set meeting certain finite lines to meet another chosen line. They do not parameterize the supporting tangents of arbitrary convex sets or give contact derivatives, countability, area estimates or any general null-sweep conclusion. The special quadratic surface in Section2 and the elementary projections here are prior geometric tools; their occurrence does not subsume the universal theorem.

3. Theorem1.3 and the full Section4 proof (printed678,689–693) establish acyclicity of connected components of the space of lines *meeting* pairwise disjoint *open* convex sets in R3. Lemma4.3 builds paths between non-transversal directions using strict separating planes and planar Helly. Lemmas4.4 and4.2 give contractible components of the direction set; Lemma4.1 transfers homology through contractible fibers of the direction projection. None of these steps estimates the metric size of the boundary tangent locus, constructs two-dimensional Lipschitz covers of that locus, or proves the three-contact rank restriction.

   Actual supporting tangents to a nonempty open full-dimensional convex set cannot meet the set: every point of that set is interior and a containing supporting halfspace has no interior point on its bounding plane. Replacing a compact set by its interior or by an inflation therefore changes the relevant locus. The article itself demonstrates this obstacle using four compact sets on a hyperbolic paraboloid, whose transversals encode an arbitrary compact subset of an interval; at printed693 the authors explain the drastic changes caused by interior replacement and inflation. Accordingly, its openness assumption cannot be silently removed to obtain PR18.

   Acyclicity alone is insufficient even for spatial measure: the two-parameter family of vertical lines with intercepts in an open planar disk is contractible in line space but sweeps a positive-volume cylinder on a bounded height interval. Such lines are meeting transversals of three disjoint open balls centered at distinct heights with a common small radius. This observation concerns an explicit subfamily; it does not claim to calculate the topology of every transversal component. Its intercept derivative is the identity, so the swept determinant is1. Moreover a two-dimensional family has zero four-dimensional ambient line-space measure while its three-dimensional spatial sweep has positive volume. Neither a topological conclusion nor ambient line-space nullness implies the target.

4. The post-Conjecture4.5 Mnëv discussion (printed693) asserts arbitrary semialgebraic homotopy types for spaces of k-transversals when 3≤k≤d−3. Its construction identifies affine dependencies/Radon partitions with orthant sections and oriented-matroid realization spaces. The range excludes k=1; it is a homotopy-type universality observation for meeting higher-dimensional flats, not a metric regularity or nullness theorem for supporting lines. The proof sketch contains neither the cap-chart estimates nor the contact-rank/area-formula step of PR18. ROOT has read the actual added sketch, rather than inferring its content from the acknowledgement.

5. Theorem1.4 and Section5 (printed679,694–696) concern colorful intersection conditions forcing a k_i-transversal to one color class. Lemma5.1 is a Radon-partition characterization for k+2 convex sets, and Lemma5.2 separates the origin from a collection of normals. The main proof constructs an equivariant map from a join of subdivided simplex boundaries and contradicts Borsuk–Ulam. The conclusion is existence of a flat, with no supporting tangency or spatial-sweep measure. In particular, three singleton color classes consisting of our pairwise disjoint sets do not satisfy the required nonempty colorful intersection hypothesis. Assuming a transversal already exists in another specialization would still give no estimate of all such lines.
