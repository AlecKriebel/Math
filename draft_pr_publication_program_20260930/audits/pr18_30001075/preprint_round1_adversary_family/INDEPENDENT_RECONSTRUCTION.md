## Exact claim and assumptions

For arbitrary pairwise disjoint convex subsets of Euclidean three-space, the union of all complete affine lines that both actually meet each set and lie in a supporting plane for that set is Lebesgue outer-null. A supporting plane must meet its set and contain it in one closed halfspace. Three supporting planes may differ. Empty sets are allowed and give an empty family. No closedness, boundedness, interior, smoothness, uniqueness, or point-contact assumptions are used. The conclusion concerns the spatial union, not the dimension of the family in line space and not containment in countably many manifolds.

## Independent proof reconstruction

1. **Countable compactification:** For each actual line choose one actual contact in each disjoint set. The three contacts are distinct. Fixed rational closed ambient balls can be chosen with contacts strictly inside and with pairwise disjoint balls. Intersect each ball with the closure of its convex set. The caps are nonempty disjoint compact convex sets; the original supporting halfspaces also support them. Thus all original tangents are contained in a countable union of compact tritangent families. The fact that original closures can touch causes no problem: disjointness here comes from the balls. The balls are fixed in the unrotated coordinates, so arbitrary later chart changes do not break countability.

2. **Projection gap and tangency:** In a line chart `x=u+zv`, the projection of a compact set is `x-zv`. Its support function gives `g=max_(unit n)[n.u-h(n,-n.v)]`. A projection with interior has negative/zero/positive gap on interior/boundary/exterior. Actual boundary membership supplies an actual contact; a support line lifts to a support plane containing the complete affine line. An existing supporting plane has normal `(n,-n.v)` with nonzero transverse normal. The signed increment is bounded between the minimum and maximum of `n.(du+z dv)` over the compact set. This sign is essential and is correct.

3. **Two-contact local Lipschitz cover:** Choose distinct contact heights `s<t`. A disk strictly inside each projected convex set places every active contact normal inside a fixed open hemisphere: `n.e_i >= alpha_i >0`. Small strict-interior rational contact caps preserve exactly those normals and the affine hull. Localized heights lie within `eps(t-s)` of their assigned contacts. Independent line motions interpolate the prescribed transverse displacements at heights `s,t`. Each gap has a positive own-coordinate increment at least `m_i=alpha_i/4`, cross increment at most `eps`, and finite residual Lipschitz constants. The chosen inequality `eps<alpha_i/16` makes cross/own ratios below `1/4`. For small residual parameters, signs at the closed-square edges bracket unique roots. The two root maps have cross Lipschitz ratio below `1/4`; their joint map is a contraction of a complete square. Fixed points depend Lipschitzly on the residual parameters and yield a map on an open two-dimensional domain. Contact dimension at least two and transverse planar directions ensure projection interior.

4. **Countability with fixed caps:** A original-set tangent near a center may miss that center's cap, so a naive neighborhood cover fails. The stated proof instead fixes each rational cap pair first, considers the tangent subspace where the construction for that pair applies, and takes a countable subcover in this second-countable subspace. Each neighborhood's graph contains that fixed pair's tangents in the neighborhood. Every original tangent lies in at least one such cap-pair family. This avoids a moving-contact countability assumption.

5. **Measurable selection and rank at density points:** Compact tangency is closed in line space by taking subsequential contact and unit-normal limits. Composing with continuous Lipschitz family maps, and excluding closed conditions of lying in planar hulls or equaling interval hulls, gives a Borel parameter subset. At a density-one differentiability parameter, every transverse parameter direction can be approached by tritangent parameters with error `o(tau)`. For a contact of height `z_i`, set `M_i=Du+z_i Dv`. If a full-dimensional contact has `M_i` onto, a chosen direction enters a convexly scaled interior ball; the line then meets interior, inconsistent with lying in a supporting plane. For a planar contact transverse to its hull, solve its intersection height by the nonzero denominator `N_z+N_x.v`, and the same direction enters a scaled relative interior disk; a supporting plane through a relative interior point must equal the hull, inconsistent with transversality. For interval contacts on a nonidentical line, incidence expresses `u+z_i v=(d_x-d_z v)lambda`, with `d_x !=0` and `lambda=O(|q-q_0|)`; components perpendicular to `d_x` are second order. For a singleton contact, `u+z_i v=0`. Thus every `M_i` has rank at most one.

6. **Null spatial sweep:** Disjoint sets give three distinct contact heights; `det(Du+z Dv)` is a polynomial of degree at most two with three distinct roots, so it vanishes identically. On bounded parameter-height patches the sweep `G(q,z)=(u(q)+zv(q),z)` is Lipschitz and has this determinant. The Borel tritangent subset times a bounded interval is measurable. Parameters failing density/differentiability form a two-dimensional null set; their product is three-dimensional null, so their image also contributes zero by the area formula (or the standard null-image bound for Lipschitz maps). Countable bounded patches handle the open, potentially unbounded domain and all heights. No injectivity or open tritangent parameter family is assumed.

7. **Low-dimensional coverage:** Planar contained lines and interval-hull-identical lines sweep finitely many null planes/lines. If two sets have affine dimension at least two, use step 3. If any is a point, all lines through it have ordinary direction charts on open two-dimensional domains. Otherwise two sets are compact intervals; lines through a point in each are smoothly parameterized by the two interval coordinates on open rectangles where a direction coordinate stays nonzero. Compact disjointness prevents equal endpoints; extensions outside the actual intervals are allowed because the actual compact tritangent subset is selected afterwards. These cases exhaust all dimensions.

## Falsification attempts and current findings

- Nonclosed/unbounded sets: countable strict-interior compact caps retain actual contacts; no measurability is exported from originals.
- Touching closures: disjoint localization balls restore strict compact disjointness.
- Contact segments and cap extra normals: the contact strictly inside the ball forces short segments toward any original witness into the cap; the reverse normal inclusion does not depend on uniqueness of contact.
- Opposite/equal contact normals and small height gaps: independence comes from evaluations at two distinct heights, not normal independence; constants need no uniform lower bound.
- Planar/interval/singleton cases: explicit exclusion and separate incidence arguments cover each degeneracy.
- Exceptional density set: a circle of tangent parameters for a ball can have full family derivative, but its zero two-dimensional measure excludes it from the rank claim. Rank cannot be claimed at all tangent parameters.
- Parameter-domain boundaries: singleton direction charts and interval affine extensions provide open-domain maps; selected Borel subsets avoid a boundary derivative assumption.
- Spatial area formula: its measurable subset form with multiplicity is sufficient even when lines/charts overlap.
- **Quantitative slip:** The displayed inequality `|rho_A(b,r)| <= kappa delta +(K_A/m_A)eta < 5 delta/8` uses `kappa=max eps/m_i`. The hypotheses justify the final `5/8` bound using the *individual* ratio `eps/m_A`, not always with the common maximum substituted on the left. For example `m_A=1/4`, `m_B=1/40`, `eps=1/200`, `delta=1`, `K_A=1`, `K_B=1/100`, and `eta=3/25` satisfy both root-domain inequalities, but `kappa delta +(K_A/m_A)eta=17/25 >5/8`. The correct own-ratio estimate is at most `(1+eps/m_A)delta/2 <5delta/8`; alternatively the displayed common-maximum bound is `<3delta/4`, which still suffices for the self-map. This is a local repair, not a counterexample to the theorem or contraction argument.
