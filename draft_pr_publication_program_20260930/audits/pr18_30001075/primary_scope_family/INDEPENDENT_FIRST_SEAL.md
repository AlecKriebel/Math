## Adversarial scope finding

The historical statement that the locus is closed cannot be read as a verified assertion for arbitrary nonclosed convex sets. For i=0,1,2 let Kᵢ={(i,y,0):0<y<1}. These are pairwise disjoint convex sets. Horizontal lines y=1/n,z=0 are tangent to all three because z=0 is a support plane. Thus (0,1/n,0) belongs to the common-tangent union. Its limit (0,0,0) does not: a line through this point meeting K₀ must be the vertical affine line x=0,z=0, which misses K₁. Hence the union is not closed. The candidate's nullness claim survives this test; its compact reduction appropriately avoids using historical closedness in that wider scope.
