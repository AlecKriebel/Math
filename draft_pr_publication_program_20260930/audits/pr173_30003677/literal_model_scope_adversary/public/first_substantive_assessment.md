## Independent deduction: the core stochastic mechanism

I reconstruct a sufficient proof using fixed times and finite exposures; I do not use the paper's late competition martingale, logarithmic approximation, or fixed-seed theorem as a substitute for the central claim.

Let the law D satisfy min D>=2, E[D^2]<infinity, and unbounded support. Let mu=E D and D* have law d P(D=d)/mu. Its forward-offspring mean is nu=E[D*-1]<infinity. For a degree-d0 root the unopposed fast infection's tree approximation is a CT branching process with d0 initial individuals: each active edge has rate lambda2 and replaces one individual with Y=D*-1. Its expected active population is d0 exp(lambda2(nu-1)t). Consequently its expected event count up to fixed T is d0 lambda2 int_0^T exp(lambda2(nu-1)s)ds<infinity. Applying the finite-mean branching-process construction, or first deriving this bound by truncation, proves nonexplosion. Each event has a finite degree, so the entire fixed-time exploration exposes only finitely many incident half-edges almost surely.

Separately, a uniform test vertex U has root law D. Along a nonbacktracking path of any fixed R steps, every next total degree has the size-biased law D*, independently in the local tree limit. A forward edge exists at every new vertex because D*-1>=1. Mark a vertex whenever its degree is at least fixed k>d0. The failure to encounter any marked nonroot vertex is exactly (1-p_k)^R, with p_k=P(D*>=k)>0. The total weak traversal time along the R edges has mean R/lambda1. Reversing at the first marked vertex makes the path a route from an initially infected weak seed to U; the undirected fixed edge weights justify reversal.

For fixed k,R,T, couple this path and the unopposed fast ball to disjoint limiting trees. A cutoff M on all incident half-edges exposed in both trees has probability tending to one as M->infinity. Conditional on at most M exposures, each matching query samples uniformly without replacement, its deviation from size-biased empirical sampling vanishes, and hits on previously exposed half-edges have total probability O(M^2/n). The fast root is chosen uniformly within a degree class of asymptotic density P(D=d0)>0, so the chance it was exposed by the path is O(M/n). The degree empirical law and first two moments converge for iid samples; a single +1 parity repair does not change these limits. Thus the path and the whole fixed-time fast ball are disjoint with probability 1-o(1). Independence between X1 and X2 and between matching and weights supplies independent weak path times. The path-ordering randomness is only used to witness a path and does not choose seeds.

On any fixed weighted graph, every actual type2 ancestor path of arrival time <=T has X2 sum <=T, so its vertices lie in the unopposed X2 ball B2(T). A seed-to-U path whose vertices avoid B2(T) and whose X1 sum is <=T is protected: inducting along it, each next vertex cannot have type2 by T and therefore is already type1 or becomes type1 by the cumulative arrival time. This is a valid competition argument without replacing the process by a two-distance Voronoi rule.

Combining these observations and Markov's inequality on path time gives

    limsup_n P(U not type1 by T) <= (1-p_k)^R + R/(lambda1 T).

This bound is genuine fixed-parameter convergence. For k=K_j=d0+j choose R_j>=j^2/p_j and T_j>=j^2 R_j/lambda1, increasing T_j. Choose deterministic N_j increasing sufficiently that the actual finite-n failure probability is <=3/j^2 for every n>=N_j, that n q_j>=4j^2 whenever n>=N_j, and log N_j>=j T_j; q_j=P(D>=K_j)>0. This is possible for each j by the displayed limit. Set J(n)=max{j:N_j<=n}, k_n=K_J, t_n=T_J. Then k_n,t_n,J(n)->infinity and t_n/log n<=1/J(n)->0. For the realized process, Z_n=1-N1(n,t_n)/n satisfies E Z_n=P(U not type1 by t_n)<=3/J(n)^2. Hence Z_n->0 in probability by Markov, with no independence or unjustified concentration of vertex indicators.

Seed resource bounds also follow. Initially |S_n| is binomial(n,q_J) up to one parity increment, so E(|S_n|/n)<=q_J+1/n->0. Its mean >=4J^2 and variance <=mean force its count to diverge in probability. The original seed half-edge total/n has expectation E[D 1_{D>=k_n}]->0. If parity repair moves a vertex across threshold the added contribution is bounded by D_repair+1, with expectation <=mu+1; divided by n this vanishes. Total degree>=2n. Thus both seed vertex fraction and incident half-edge fraction vanish while every weak seed degree diverges. These deductions establish the candidate's claimed existence theorem under its stated hypotheses.
