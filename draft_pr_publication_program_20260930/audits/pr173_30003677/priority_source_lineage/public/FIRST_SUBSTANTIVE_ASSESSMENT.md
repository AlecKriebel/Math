## Claim reconstructed

The target has seven simultaneous quantifiers: every fixed unbounded integer law D with D>=2 and finite second moment; every fixed pair 0<lambda1<lambda2; every fixed supported d0; deterministic thresholds kn diverging; all vertices whose repaired degrees meet kn seeded before matching or weights; both vertex and stub seed fractions vanish while the seed count diverges; a deterministic tn diverging but o(log n) already yields type1 fraction tending to one. Its population advantage is unrestricted except for vanishing density and mass, and both threshold and time schedules can grow arbitrarily slowly. It does not prove a fixed-hub result, a specified polynomial budget, or a quantitative tradeoff between a degree and a rate.

## Actual derivation and antecedent implication test

Fix a threshold k>d0. A size-biased vertex along a nonbacktracking ray has probability p(k)=E[D 1(D>=k)]/E[D]>0 of being a seed. Since minimum degree is two, the limiting ray continues indefinitely. After R steps, the probability of missing all such marks is (1-p(k))^R. Independent type1 edge costs along the reverse ray have total mean R/lambda1. A separate degree-d0 root's unopposed fast infection exposes finitely many vertices and incident stubs by a fixed T: its branching offspring is D*-1 with finite mean. Two independently sampled finite rooted exposures intersect with probability tending to zero. Thus a type1 path along the ray avoids the entire fast ball through T with probability 1-o(1). The deterministic ancestral-path argument then bounds the uniform-root failure probability by (1-p(k))^R + R/(lambda1 T) + o(1), with k,R,T fixed first. Choose R then T to make this small. Choose k increasing only after n is large enough for these fixed parameter bounds, and force log n to dominate each T by a growing factor. This yields the exact candidate time and mass requirements using a diagonal limit, tail integrability, and n P(D>=k) sufficiently large. The central mechanism is therefore fixed-time local separation plus slowly vanishing seed density, rather than competition at logarithmic times or a high-degree growth exponent.

A prior result stating joint marked finite-time/local-neighborhood convergence for two independent roots, with finite-mean nonexplosive size-biased growth and minimum degree two, would imply this existence theorem through this displayed reduction. A prior growing-seed theorem on the same degree model that allowed seed selection by degree, conditioned bounded-degree competitor roots, and arbitrarily slow schedules would also imply it. A final-size-only result on fixed-degree random regular graphs would not directly imply the diverging minimum degree or sublogarithmic time clause.

Ahlberg--Deijfen--Janson (ADJ), arXiv:1711.02902v1, fully read pp.1-16 by this seal, supplies the fixed-time branching-exposure mechanism in Section 2, pp.6-7: for fixed initial stub counts and fixed T its exposed half-edge population is tight; replacement sampling and finite-mean nonexplosion produce an exact coupling with probability 1-o(1). Adding a fixed auxiliary ray and conditioning one independently selected root to a degree class of positive limiting density is a finite-exposure modification, not a new macroscopic stochastic theorem. This produces a plausible transparent-corollary objection to substantial novelty even though ADJ's literal Theorem 1.1 starts from two uniform or fixed finite initial sets and does not state the candidate. The direct ray-plus-diagonal reduction above is independently checkable. Its only extra probabilistic ingredient is the same finite-exposure sampling argument for the additional ray; this does not assume a growing-parameter coupling. Whether an exact earlier published corollary has been stated is still unestablished.

## ADMP older theorem comparison at this stage

Antunovic--Dekel--Mossel--Peres (ADMP), arXiv:1109.2575v1, has been read pp.1-5, not its complete proof by this seal. Its title page is dated 12 September 2011. The separately retrieved current PDF is explicitly v2, dated 4 August 2014; pp.1-8 have been read. Theorem 2.6 is in v2 p.6, not the 2011 theorem numbering. Definitions 2.1-2.3 restrict seed generation to uniform disjoint sets, or sets grown from a small uniform center followed by uniform opponent selection. The theorem concerns fixed d>=3 regular graphs and final component sizes.

Set ADMP's B to the faster infection, beta=lambda2, fixed B0=1, and R to the slower infection, rho=lambda1, with R0=m(N) growing and o(N). In the second regime, m(N) N^(rho/beta)/N ->0, the slow infection loses, of order m(N) N^(rho/beta). In the first regime, (N/m(N))^(beta/rho)/N ->0, equivalently m(N)>>N^(1-rho/beta), the fast infection is of order (N/m(N))^(beta/rho)=o(N), so the slow infection occupies N-o_P(N). Taking m(N)=N^a with 1-rho/beta<a<1 already proves the general population-advantage phenomenon against every fixed rate disadvantage with vanishing vertex and stub mass on regular graphs. This is a strong priority antecedent, not merely a tangential citation.

It does not literally imply the candidate: constant d cannot meet kn->infinity; the random regular model is not the fixed unbounded D model; degree-threshold seeds are not the uniform/admissible rule; and its theorem is about the final allocation, not tn=o(log N). One may not replace d by d(N), identify the budget with the sum of high-degree stubs, or transfer its urn formulas without proving a new degree-dependent growth calculation. The proof still needs full reading to decide whether it contains a more general directly usable mechanism.
