## Exact strongest checked prior result and matching reduction

[Ganguly–Ramanan, arXiv:2205.01587v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01587v1), dated **3 May 2022**, Theorem 4.3 on p.12, proves local convergence of interacting jump dynamics for locally convergent marked initial data and finitely dissociable limits. Its Corollary 5.16 on p.24 verifies the needed property for finite-second-moment unimodular Galton–Watson trees. The stronger empirical theorem is Theorem 4.6 / Corollary 4.7 on pp.14–15. The paper was published as [EJP 29 (2024), paper 185](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-EJP1243); corresponding local and empirical results are Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.12 on pp.19 and21. Both original v1 and the published full PDF were obtained, and actual hypotheses and proofs were read.

The full checkable reduction is in **COROLLARY_DERIVATION.md**. In brief: for each fixed degree cutoff K, the initial weak degree-threshold marking converges locally, while the single independent degree-d0 fast seed vanishes from the marked local limit. The three-state irreversible process has rates lambda_i times the number of type-i neighboring channels. After the explicitly justified cap at parallel multiplicity two, all the prior theorem's rate and mark assumptions hold. The cap matches the actual finite-variance configuration graph with probability tending to one. The limiting process therefore contains only weak infection. A forward ray in the minimum-degree-two limiting tree encounters a high-degree seed with probability tending to one as its length grows, and its traversal time is controlled by a gamma-sum expectation. This gives the fixed-cutoff estimate. A deterministic diagonal recovers every candidate conclusion, including vanishing vertex and stub density, a growing seed count whose minimum degree diverges, occupation tending to one, and deterministic t_n=o(log n).

The reduction directly addresses arbitrary fixed positive rate disadvantage: lambda2 may exceed lambda1 by any finite factor. The rate2 effect is handled by local convergence of the full two-color process, not a comparison of final independent distance fields. No new competition-transfer lemma is needed beyond the older theorem. The terminal positive-fraction question is therefore already answered by this implication; the deterministic time refinement also follows and does not rescue a first-answer claim.
