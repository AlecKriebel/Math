## Independently reconstructed target and mechanism

Target: i.i.d. unbounded integer degrees D>=2 with finite second moment; one fixed-degree d0 fast seed chosen before matching; slower infection initially occupies every degree>=k_n vertex. For every fixed positive pair lambda1<lambda2, prove deterministic k_n,t_n tending to infinity, t_n=o(log n), vanishing initial vertex and stub densities, growing initial population, and type-1 occupation 1-o_P(1) by t_n.

For fixed K>d0, a nonbacktracking ray from a uniform test vertex survives indefinitely in the limiting configuration tree because every forward degree is at least one. Its successive non-root total degrees are independent with size-biased law D*. Thus its first R vertices miss the weak seeds with probability (1-p_K)^R, p_K=P(D*>=K)>0. The traversal-time sum has mean R/lambda1, so its exceeding T has probability at most R/(lambda1 T). A degree-d0 fast root has a finite exposed time-T neighborhood almost surely: expected genealogical rings are bounded by sum_r d0 nu^(r-1)(lambda2 T)^r/r!, nu=E[D*-1]<infinity. Any fixed finite exploration from the test root is disjoint from that ball with probability tending to one, under a uniform unmatched-half-edge exposure conditioned only on the degree list. A route from a seed outside the unopposed fast ball is protected from type 2. Consequently

limsup_n P(test vertex missing from type 1 by T) <= (1-p_K)^R + R/(lambda1 T).

Choose successively K_j->infinity, R_j p_Kj->infinity and T_j/R_j->infinity. Delay the deterministic transition to stage j until both the fixed-stage error is small and log n>=j T_j. Also require n P(D>=K_j)>=j^2. The stagewise schedule gives the claimed time bound and population divergence. Tail integrability gives vanishing stub density; the vanishing vertex fraction follows from k_n->infinity. This independently reproduces the candidate's central implication, rather than assuming its conclusion.

Exact prior-implication criteria: a prior theorem must either (A) allow the two initially infected populations with degree-conditioned selection, unmatched uniform pairing, and enough initial weak stub mass to force near-total terminal occupation, or (B) give marked finite-time local convergence for this irreversible two-color process for every fixed degree threshold, with no bounded-degree-only or positive-density competitor requirement; the displayed ray and diagonal then give the candidate as a corollary. A terminal theorem giving a positive weak fraction would already resolve the source existential question, even without the time refinement.

## First theorem-level comparison

1. **Ahlberg–Deijfen–Janson (2017 preprint; 2019 article), Theorem 1.1, pp. 2–4; setup pp. 5–6; proof/future-work pp. 13–16.** The empirical degrees converge in distribution and second moment, with all degrees at least two. Two uniformly selected starts are used; the extension is to fixed numbers of infected vertices. The later conditioning on initial active counts concerns those fixed-root degrees and does not by itself enlarge the theorem to growing degree-selected seed sets. The source's question really permits growing initial numbers and degree choices (OWR PDF pp. 16–17, printed 3442–3443). No direct prior resolution follows from this terminal theorem.

2. **Antunovic–Dekel–Mossel–Peres (2011 preprint; 2017 article), Definitions 2.1–2.3 and Theorem 2.6, pp. 4–6.** A growing slower population can overcome a fixed faster population when its initial size exceeds the appropriate power threshold, and terminal occupation then tends to one. But the theorem assumes fixed d-regular graphs and admissible uniform or previously grown initial sets. The candidate has unbounded degrees and degree-selected seeds. This is established mechanism-level precedent, not the required nonregular corollary.

3. **Antunovic (2014), Theorems 1.1 and 1.4, pp. 1–4.** The general starting-configuration language concerns an urn with fixed replacement parameter b=d and random d-regular graph exploration. It does not cover arbitrary empirical degree sequences or variable offspring. This apparent broad initial-stub lead does not match the target.

4. **Janson–Luczak–Windridge (2013 preprint / 2014 article), conditions (D1)–(D6), Theorem 2.6, pp. 3–7.** This genuinely allows deterministic degree lists and arbitrary initial degree classes, recording initial infected stub mass mu_I; the pairing is uniform. For fixed K the candidate weak seed set has positive mu_I, while k_n->infinity makes mu_I=0. The theorem is single-type SIR, not two-type competition. Its stated condition (D6) excludes the unrecovered SI case with minimum degree two and no recovered mass. A reduction through positive recovery then a limit, or a degenerate-case extension, remains to be checked. The single-type conclusion cannot simply be imported as a competitive lower bound.

5. **Alimohammadi–Borgs–van der Hofstad–Saberi, downloaded current primary manuscript, Theorem 3 pp. 14 and Corollary 2 pp. 20–21.** It permits degree-dependent initial conditions, but imposes a locally reachable property involving the supremum over all paths conditional on the graph. A single random ray's high-degree hit probability does not establish this property. In particular, deterministic threshold marking leaves conditional avoidance probabilities zero or one; low-degree induced infinite paths can survive. No direct application is certified. The current manuscript cites 2025 work, so its exact version/date must be independently resolved before priority use.

6. **Karrer–Newman (2011), primary pp. 1 and 4–6 read in part.** This discusses a slower disease initially leading through many carriers, but the model uses recovering epidemics and discrete generation/transmissibility parameters. The finite-size crossover argument is not a proved uniform competitive-FPP theorem for arbitrary degree-selected growing seeds. Remaining pages and theorem status are not yet fully checked.
