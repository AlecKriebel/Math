# Sources and bounded priority assessment

Prepared 11 October 2026 for *An exact period criterion for focal-distance products in elliptic billiards*. This note separates the recorded observation, proved prior statements, consequences of established machinery, and limits of the literature search.

## Exact result and publication positioning

The manuscript concerns original billiard vertices on a **noncircular ellipse**, a **strictly nested nondegenerate confocal elliptical caustic**, and the product of ordinary positive Euclidean distances to either fixed focus. For a family of **least period** `n`, it proves phase constancy if and only if `n ≡ 2 (mod 4)`. It includes every admissible coprime star winding and both orientations, equality of the two focal products at each phase for every even `n`, and all repeated traversals. Repetition raises the primitive product to a positive integer power, preserving constancy in both directions. Circular boundaries are treated separately. Hyperbolic and degenerate caustics are excluded.

Beyond the experimental record described below, the current bounded primary-source search found **no explicit prior theorem or proof establishing the exact target product or its full least-period classification**. This is a search finding, not certification of novelty or exclusive historical priority. The full result is a short application/corollary of the classical Jacobi parametrization, standard elliptic-function divisors, and compact-torus pole removal. The target-specific calculation identifies the focal factor's complete divisor and checks its orbit cosets. No new parametrization, elliptic-function method, Liouville principle, or cyclic-product framework is claimed.

## The original observation: 2020 preprint and 2021 article

Dan Reznik, Ronaldo Garcia and Jair Koiller, *Eighty New Invariants of N-Periodics in the Elliptic Billiard*, [arXiv:2004.12497v11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12497v11), 29 October 2020, Table 2, records `k114 = ∏ d₁,ᵢ`, with `dⱼ,ᵢ = |Pᵢ − fⱼ|`, condition `N ≡ 2 (mod 4)`, experimental discovery date April 2020, and proof column `?`. The preprint was first posted on 26 April 2020; the table finding here is verified in v11 rather than presumed unchanged across every version.

The version of record is Reznik–Garcia–Koiller, *Fifty New Invariants of N-Periodics in the Elliptic Billiard*, **Arnold Mathematical Journal 7** (2021), 341–355, [doi:10.1007/s40598-021-00174-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40598-021-00174-y), published online 18 February 2021. Section 3.1, p. 344, defines the factors; Table 2, p. 345, retains the unproved entry. Credit for the observed invariant belongs to these authors.

The row does not explicitly resolve least period versus a return length permitting repetitions. The manuscript proves its least-period interpretation and gives an exact doubled-triangle counterexample to the unrestricted listed-length interpretation. For a return length `N ≡ 2 (mod 4)`, the product is constant precisely when its least period is even. The source's question mark records its publication-time status; it does not establish absence of subsequent proofs.

## Published input and established proof mechanism

**Hellmuth Stachel, *On the motion of billiards in ellipses*, European Journal of Mathematics 8 (2022), 1602–1622**, [doi:10.1007/s40879-021-00524-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40879-021-00524-2), online 24 January 2022. Theorem 4.3 and Eq. (4.9), p. 1614, provide the canonical vertex parametrization `P(u) = (−a sn u, b cn u)`, the axis relations, and the periodic step. This is the strongest directly verified published input to the present theorem. It does not state `k114` or the focal-product converse. Nevertheless, the ordinary focal distance is the positive factor `a ± c sn u`; its two zeros and two poles, translated along the finite orbit, yield the manuscript's complete criterion in a short calculation. Stachel's introduction, p. 1603, credits Jacobi (1828), King (1994), and Tabachnikov (2005) for earlier canonical-coordinate work. The original Jacobi text was not independently audited here.

The Jacobi periods, poles, half/quarter-period shifts, elementary identities and addition formulas are standard: [NIST DLMF §§22.4, 22.6 and 22.8](https://dlmf.nist.gov/22), especially Tables 22.4.1 and 22.4.3. The focal factor is considered on the `sn` torus; no identical complex period lattice for the full vertex parametrization is assumed.

Explicit precedents for meromorphic Poncelet invariants and pole removal include:

- **Richard Schwartz, *The pentagram integrals for Poncelet families*, Journal of Geometry and Physics 87 (2015), 432–449**, [doi:10.1016/j.geomphys.2014.07.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomphys.2014.07.024). The [author manuscript dated 3 February 2014](https://www.math.brown.edu/~res/Papers/poncelet_monodromy.pdf), pp. 5 and 12–14, uses pole removal on a complex Poncelet torus. Its proved invariants are specified monodromy functions; no identification of the target as one of them was found.
- **Arseniy Akopyan, Richard Schwartz and Serge Tabachnikov, *Billiards in ellipses revisited*, European Journal of Mathematics 8 (2022), 1313–1327**, [doi:10.1007/s40879-020-00426-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40879-020-00426-9), online 9 September 2020. Version-of-record Theorem 4.1/proof, pp. 1319–1320, and Theorem 5.1/proof, pp. 1321–1322, use complexification, Liouville and pole cancellation for angular invariants, explicitly crediting Schwartz. Preprint theorem numbers differ.
- **Ana C. Chavez-Caliz, *More About Areas and Centers of Poncelet Polygons*, Arnold Mathematical Journal 7 (2021), 91–106**, [doi:10.1007/s40598-020-00154-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40598-020-00154-8), online 14 August 2020. Section 2, pp. 97–98, develops cyclic meromorphic functions on the elliptic flag curve; Theorem 6, pp. 104–105, proves an area product by pole cancellation.

Theorems 1–2 and Corollary 1 concern angular sums, limiting-pedal perimeters and focus-inversion perimeter, using Jacobi functions and Liouville. These are additional method precedents, not the target product.

## Distinct products and why they do not substitute for the target

Here `A,B` denote the caustic semiaxes.

| Quantity | Verified prior result | Relation to this manuscript |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original vertex-to-focus distances, `k114` | Recorded unproved in the 2020/2021 source table | Exact target, with least-period classification supplied here |
| Vertex-to-caustic-contact lengths, `k117` | Stachel, Theorem 5.6, p. 1621: full contact products constant for even periods; certain half-products when `n` is divisible by four | Different factors; the theorem does not state `k114` |
| Focus-to-side and center-to-side perpendicular distances, `k202,a/b` | Bialy–Tabachnikov, Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.3, pp. 1349–1353: products `Bⁿ` for even periods and `(AB)ⁿ⁄²` when `n` is divisible by four | Different geometric distances; no direct focal-vertex classification |
| Focus-to-outer-tangent-polygon vertices, `k115` | Ferudun's 2026 unrefereed note: constant for least period divisible by four | Outer vertices are consecutive boundary-tangent intersections, rather than billiard vertices |

The perpendicular-distance reference is **Misha Bialy and Serge Tabachnikov, *Dan Reznik's identities and more*, European Journal of Mathematics 8 (2022), 1341–1354**, [doi:10.1007/s40879-020-00428-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40879-020-00428-7), online 11 September 2020. Printed Theorem 4.3(a), p. 1350, declares a center foot but writes focus-to-foot factors. Its proof, Lemma 4.2, and source entry `k202,a` identify the intended factors as focal perpendicular distances to side lines. This notation defect is not interpreted as a theorem about ordinary boundary-vertex distances.

The distinction has an exact check: for caustic axes `(2,1)` and outer axes `(√6,√3)`, the primitive rectangular and diamond four-periodic phases have ordinary focal products `16` and `18`, while both contact products are `4` and both focal-perpendicular products are `1`. Thus those known numerical invariants alone do not determine the target product.

**Alper Ferudun, *Focal Distance Products of Outer Elliptic Billiard Polygons***, unrefereed preprint, 2 October 2026, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.23093258](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23093258), [author release](https://eulersolve.org/papers/amr-050-0007/), Theorem 1 and §§2–5, proves the distinct outer-polygon result using credited classical Jacobi and cyclic-divisor tools. It also distinguishes least period from repeated-list length. It is a relevant recent comparison, not a proof of `k114`.

## Additional coverage and the unresolved full-text gap

Full-text checks also covered Stachel's *The geometry of billiards in ellipses and their Poncelet grids*, **Journal of Geometry 112:40** (2021), [doi:10.1007/s00022-021-00606-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00022-021-00606-2), and the [author manuscript of *On the Diagonals of Billiards*](https://www.geometrie.tuwien.ac.at/stachel/SaoPaulo_Diagonals.pdf), **ICGG 2022 proceedings**, pp. 19–33, [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-13588-0_2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-13588-0_2). Their relevant symmetry, grid and diagonal results supplied no explicit target theorem or a more direct implication. Canonical-coordinate background was checked in Jonathan King's *Three Problems in Search of a Measure*, **American Mathematical Monthly 101** (1994), 609–628, [doi:10.1080/00029890.1994.11997003](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1994.11997003); Serge Tabachnikov's *Geometry and Billiards*, **Student Mathematical Library 30**, AMS, 2005, [doi:10.1090/stml/030](https://doi.org/10.1090/stml/030); and Mark Levi–Serge Tabachnikov's [2005 preprint of *The Poncelet grid and billiards in ellipses*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0511009), **American Mathematical Monthly 114** (2007), 895–908. Supplementary Garcia–Reznik full texts were checked for focal/product leads.

**Vladimir Dragović and Borislav Gajić, *Points with rotational ellipsoids of inertia, envelopes of hyperplanes which equally fit the system of points in Rᵏ, and ellipsoidal billiards*, Physica D 451 (2023), 133776**, [doi:10.1016/j.physd.2023.133776](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2023.133776), remains a disclosed coverage gap. The audit inspected its abstract and complete public [publisher introduction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167278923001306), but found no legitimate public full text; the [institutional record](https://researchrepository.mi.sanu.ac.rs/handle/123456789/5063?mode=full) reports no full text. Its advertised inertia/confocal-pencil scope and billiard-integral connections supply no positive evidence of the focal-product criterion. That limited salience assessment is an **inference from accessible scope**, not clearance of the paper. **Sections 3.1 and 5.2 remain uninspected** and could contain relevant unadvertised facts or citations.

The search used primary papers, author manuscripts, publisher and institutional pages, reference lists, and bounded title/identifier/citation searches. Bibliographic locators were used to find sources, not as mathematical authority. Missing keywords, zero search hits, unavailable pages, and old proof-table question marks do not prove absence. Unindexed texts and every later specialization of a classical general theorem were not exhaustively checked. Specific formulation novelty and exclusive priority therefore remain uncertified. No external outreach was prepared or initiated.
