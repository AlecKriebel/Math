# A short classical implication for the focal-distance product

This is an independently derived priority-test artifact, not a claim that the target theorem was already stated in the literature. Its purpose is to measure the gap between published machinery and the PR149 claim.

## Published input and scope

Let the outer ellipse have semiaxes `a>b>0`, the strictly nested nondegenerate confocal elliptical caustic have semiaxes `A>B>0`, and `c=sqrt(a^2-b^2)=sqrt(A^2-B^2)>0`. Write `k=c/A`, `K=K(k)`, and `K'=K(sqrt(1-k^2))`. Stachel, *On the motion of billiards in ellipses*, Theorem 4.3, printed p. 1614, provides the canonical parametrization and closure formulas

\[
 P(u)=(-a\operatorname{sn}u,\ b\operatorname{cn}u),\qquad
 P_i(u)=P(u+i\delta),\qquad
 \delta=2\Delta=\frac{4\tau K}{n},
\]

\[
 a=A\frac{\operatorname{dn}\Delta}{\operatorname{cn}\Delta},\qquad
 b=\frac{B}{\operatorname{cn}\Delta},\qquad
 \gcd(n,\tau)=1.
\]

Here `n` is the least period. Choose the orientation with `0<tau/n<1/2`, so `0<Delta<K`. All admitted coprime star windings are included. In particular `n=2` cannot occur in this strictly nested nondegenerate elliptical-caustic regime.

Primary links: [Stachel theorem and full article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40879-021-00524-2), [DLMF periods/poles and shifts](https://dlmf.nist.gov/22.4), [DLMF elementary identities](https://dlmf.nist.gov/22.6).

## Actual ordinary distance and its divisor

For the fixed focus `F=(c,0)`, elementary ellipse algebra gives

\[
 |P(u)-F|^2=(a+c\operatorname{sn}u)^2.
\]

For real `u`, `a>c` makes `f(u)=a+c sn(u)` strictly positive, hence this is the ordinary Euclidean distance, without any signed-distance or square-root convention.

Regard `f` on the torus

\[
 T=\mathbb C/(4K\mathbb Z+2iK'\mathbb Z).
\]

The full vertex parametrization need not have this smaller imaginary period: only the focal factor is being regarded on this torus. This distinction does not alter its real-orbit product. DLMF Tables 22.4.1 and 22.4.3 give two simple poles, at

\[
 p_0=iK',\qquad p_1=2K+iK',
\]

and the shift identities

\[
 \operatorname{sn}(v+iK')=\frac1{k\operatorname{sn}v},\qquad
 \operatorname{sn}(3K\pm\Delta)=-\frac{\operatorname{cn}\Delta}{\operatorname{dn}\Delta}.
\]

Consequently the two simple zeros are

\[
 q_\pm=3K\pm\Delta+iK'.
\]

Indeed `sn(q_±)=-dn(Delta)/(k cn(Delta))=-a/c`. They are distinct because `0<Delta<K`. They are simple: the critical values of `sn` are `±1` and `±1/k`, whereas `a/c>1/k` since `a>A`. These two zeros exhaust the zeros because `f` is an elliptic function of degree two.

## The cyclic product criterion and exact classification

Let `H=<delta>` on `T`. Coprimality identifies `H` with the real subgroup generated by `4K/n`. Define

\[
 N_H(f)(u)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}f(u+j\delta).
\]

The divisor of this product is the sum of the translates of `div(f)`. This elementary cyclic-norm property is standard, and can also be checked by adding the zero/pole order of each factor at each point. An elliptic function is constant precisely when this divisor vanishes: absence of poles implies constancy on the compact torus, and a nonzero constant has no zeros.

The zeros `q_+` and `q_-` differ by `delta`, so their translates make a single coset with multiplicity two. The pole cosets coincide precisely when `2K` belongs to `H`, i.e. when `n` is even. If `n` is odd, there are two distinct pole cosets with multiplicity one each and a single zero coset with multiplicity two, so the divisor cannot vanish.

For even `n`, constancy is equivalent to `q_+-p_0=3K+Delta` belonging to `H`. This is exactly

\[
 \frac{3n+2\tau}{4}\in\mathbb Z.
\]

When `n` is even, coprimality makes `tau` odd, and the displayed condition holds exactly for

\[
 \boxed{n\equiv2\pmod4.}
\]

This proves both directions for the stated noncircular, strictly nested elliptical-caustic regime. If the real product were constant but the complex meromorphic product nonconstant, the identity theorem on a neighborhood of the real axis would be violated; hence complex nonconstancy also proves real phase nonconstancy. For the other focus the factor is `a-c sn(u)=f(u+2K)`, so it has the same classification. In the constant case the two focal products are equal, since `2K` belongs to `H`.

For an `r`-fold traversal the listed product is `N_H(f)^r`; positivity makes it constant if and only if the primitive product is constant. Thus a condition only on listed length `N` is insufficient: twice a primitive triangle has listed length `6` but a nonconstant product. A noncircular primitive `n≡2 mod4` orbit remains constant under every repetition, regardless of the repeated listed length's residue. For circles `c=0`, every ordinary focal distance is the radius `a`, so all products are trivially constant.

## Priority interpretation

The exact focal divisor identification is the target-specific calculation. The uniformization, closure formula, Jacobi shifts, translated-divisor rule, pole removal, and compact-torus constancy are prior machinery. The calculation above is short enough that it supports calling the classification a readily derived corollary of the published canonical parametrization. It does **not** support asserting that Stachel directly proved `k114`, nor asserting that a direct statement of the classification was previously published. It rules out any credible claim to novelty of the Liouville/cyclic-product method.
