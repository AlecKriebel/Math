Closing without merging or publishing as a new solution of an open problem. The mathematical counterexample has passed extensive independent verification. The issue is the imported target's open-status/provenance and the claimed-resolution framing.

The authenticated source asks, without additional hypotheses, whether the supremum of local Lyapunov dimension on a smooth dissipative system's global attractor must occur at an equilibrium or an unstable periodic orbit. For manifold phase spaces this already has an elementary negative answer: on R × T² take w' = −w and angular speeds 1 and √2. The compact minimal global attractor is {0} × T², attracting every bounded subset uniformly at rate exp(−t). There are no equilibria or periodic trajectories, and the intrinsic spectrum is (0,0,−1), with dimension 2 everywhere. For the embedded-manifold phase allowed by [Parker–Goluskin, arXiv:2510.14870v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14870v2), pp.2,5,7, an explicit linear ambient extension has spectrum (0,0,0,0,−1) and dimension 4. Its global attractor is relative to that phase; it has no compact global attractor on all R⁵.

This is a checkable reviewer specialization of classical product/quasiperiodic theory. Relevant mechanisms appear in Zelik's author-hosted [*A remark on a uniform Lyapunov dimension of cascade systems*](https://sergey-zelik.co.uk/publications/dlyap.pdf), Examples3.1–3.3, pp.10–12, associated with the bibliographically authenticated [2008 cascade paper, DOI10.3934/cpaa.2008.7.971](https://doi.org/10.3934/cpaa.2008.7.971). The author copy is undated and differs in title/numbering from the journal version; exact journal-body identity was not verified. We do not claim that Zelik printed these exact equations, named them an Eden disproof, or proved PR111's full theorem.

PR111 genuinely proves a stronger explicit statement: a complete real-analytic field on all R⁵ with uniformly negative divergence, a compact minimal global attractor for the entire phase, one equilibrium and four periodic circles, and an irrational torus whose asymptotic dimension 203/50 strictly exceeds every equilibrium/periodic value. Both the pointwise Kaplan–Yorke and fixed-global-index conventions were checked separately. The finite-time statement is a lower bound, not an exact time-infimum equality. No authenticated earlier source matching this entire stronger theorem was found in the bounded audit; its substantive research novelty remains unestablished. This closure does not say that the theorem is mathematically invalid, duplicates a located prior theorem, or adds nothing.
