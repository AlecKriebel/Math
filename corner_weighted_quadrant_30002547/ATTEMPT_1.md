# Attempt 1: the complete nonnegative-parameter radius

## Scope and result

For each fixed real parameter a >= 0, let Q_m(a) be the sum of a^c over all 2m-step square-lattice walks from the origin to itself that stay in the nonnegative quadrant. The statistic c counts exactly the linear-word adjacent pairs NW and ES. In particular, there is no extra final/initial junction.

**Theorem.** For every fixed a >= 0,

    limsup_{m -> infinity} Q_m(a)^(1/m) = (2 + sqrt(2+2a))^2.

Consequently the radius in the half-length variable is (2 + sqrt(2+2a))^(-2), as conjectured in Albert–Bousquet-Mélou, Conjecture 11. This includes a=0. This proof does not address any negative a. No claim of novelty relative to all later literature is made.

The proof below is self-contained apart from elementary finite-dimensional Perron–Frobenius theory and the L² martingale maximal inequality, whose needed application is spelled out.

## 1. A finite-state direction chain

Use the state order E,N,W,S. Set M_ij=a when ij is ES or NW and M_ij=1 otherwise. The weight of an n-letter word d_1...d_n is

    w(d_1...d_n) = product_{j=1}^{n-1} M_{d_j,d_{j+1}}.

Put s=sqrt((a+1)/2), lambda=2+2s, and r=(s,s,1,1)^T. Direct multiplication gives Mr=lambda r. Also l=(1,1,s,s)^T is a left eigenvector with eigenvalue lambda. Every entry of both r and l is strictly positive, including at a=0. Each row and each column of M has at most one zero, so every entry of M² is positive when a=0; for a>0, M itself is positive. Thus M is primitive for all a>=0, and lambda is its Perron eigenvalue. Alternatively its characteristic polynomial is z²(z²−4z+2−2a), which agrees with the displayed value.

Define the stochastic matrix

    P_ij = M_ij r_j / (lambda r_i).

Its stationary distribution is exactly uniform: l_i r_i=s for all four states, hence pi_i=1/4. In particular the stationary average lattice step is (0,0). The symmetry M_ij=M_{−j,−i}, where − reverses a direction, also verifies this zero-drift property; the explicit calculation avoids relying on symmetry heuristics.

Let D_1,D_2,... be a stationary chain with transition P. For any n-letter word d,

    Pr(D_1...D_n=d) = (1/4) lambda^{−(n−1)} w(d) r_{d_n}/r_{d_1}.

Since a is fixed and r has four positive entries, the weighted mass of any set F of n-letter words satisfies

    c_a lambda^n Pr(F) <= sum_{d in F} w(d) <= C_a lambda^n Pr(F),

with positive finite constants independent of n and F. This statement includes zero-weight words at a=0.

## 2. Most of the mass stays within a sublinear square

Write v(E)=(1,0), v(N)=(0,1), v(W)=(−1,0), and v(S)=(0,−1). The two coordinates of v have stationary mean zero. Since P is an irreducible finite-state chain, the Poisson equation

    h − Ph = v

has a bounded vector-valued solution h. One elementary justification is that eigenvalue 1 is simple and the range of I−P is precisely the subspace of stationary-mean-zero functions.

Set

    Z_j = h(D_{j+1}) − Ph(D_j),
    M_k = sum_{j=1}^k Z_j.

With the filtration generated by D_1,...,D_{k+1}, M_k is a martingale with bounded increments. Telescoping yields

    S_k := sum_{j=1}^k v(D_j)
         = h(D_1) − h(D_{k+1}) + M_k.

The scalar L² maximal inequality, applied to both coordinates, gives a constant K_a with

    Pr(max_{k<=n} ||S_k||_infinity > H) <= K_a n/(H−2||h||_infinity)^2

when H>2||h||_infinity. Indeed the martingale increments are orthogonal in L² and uniformly bounded, so E|M_n|²=O_a(n), and E max_{k<=n}|M_k|²=O_a(n).

Take H=H_n=ceil(n^(2/3)). The probability that all vertices S_0,...,S_n lie in [−H,H]² tends to 1 and is at least 1/2 for all sufficiently large n. Hence the total weight T_n of these confined, arbitrary-endpoint words satisfies

    T_n >= c_a lambda^n.

This use of probability requires a>=0 and is not available for signed weights.

## 3. Endpoint grouping and a weight-exact mirror

Partition the confined n-letter words by their endpoint z and their first and last directions f,d. There are at most

    16(2H+1)^2

classes. Nonnegativity implies that one class C has total weight

    B_n := sum_{P in C} w(P) >= c_a lambda^n/[16(2H+1)^2].

All members have exactly the same endpoint z and first/last directions f,d. Restricting by endpoint automatically respects whatever parity that endpoint has; there is no need to demand a return at length n, so odd n cause no difficulty.

For a word Q=q_1...q_n, define revneg(Q)=(−q_n)...(−q_1). Because M_ij=M_{−j,−i}, w(revneg(Q))=w(Q). For any ordered pair (P,Q) in C², the concatenation

    P revneg(Q)

is a closed 2n-step walk. Its vertices during the first segment trace P; its vertices during the second segment trace Q in reverse, because both endpoints equal z. Thus every vertex remains in [−H,H]². The middle seam is d,−d, which is neither NW nor ES. Therefore this closed word has exactly weight w(P)w(Q).

## 4. Embed the centered loop into the quadrant without changing its weight

Choose a monotone prefix A from (0,0) to (H,H) as follows:

- If f=W, take A=N^H E^H, whose last direction is E.
- Otherwise take A=E^H N^H, whose last direction is N.

In both cases the pair (last(A),f) is neither NW nor ES. Both internal corners of A and of revneg(A) are unweighted. The other outer seam, (−f,−last(A)), is the reverse-negation of the first seam and is also unweighted.

The word

    A P revneg(Q) revneg(A)

is a quadrant loop: the two monotone outer pieces stay in the quadrant, while the middle centered loop is translated into [0,2H]². Its length is 4H+2n, hence its half-length is m_n=n+2H. Its weight remains exactly w(P)w(Q), including when a=0.

For each fixed n and its selected class C, this construction is injective on ordered pairs (P,Q): the fixed prefix length 2H and the fixed middle split length n recover P and revneg(Q) uniquely. No uniqueness across distinct n is needed. Therefore, by nonnegativity,

    Q_{m_n}(a) >= B_n² >= c'_a lambda^{2n}/(2H+1)^4.

Since H=o(n), this implies

    limsup_m Q_m(a)^(1/m) >= lambda².

The proof uses no cyclic corner at the final return to the origin.

## 5. Matching upper bound and radius

All quadrant loops of length 2m form a subset of all words of that length. Nonnegativity and the probability-to-weight comparison above give

    0 <= Q_m(a) <= C_a lambda^{2m}.

Thus the opposite limsup bound holds, proving the theorem and the claimed radius by Cauchy–Hadamard.

One can also obtain the ordinary root limit here. A nonempty quadrant loop starts with E or N and ends with W or S, so concatenating two quadrant loops introduces no counted corner. Concatenation at prescribed lengths is injective and gives Q_{p+q}(a)>=Q_p(a)Q_q(a), with Q_m(a)>=2^m>0. Fekete's lemma therefore makes the root limit exist, and the already proved limsup identifies it. This optional observation is not used for any negative parameter.

## 6. Audit checklist and limitations

- The weight convention is linear, ordered NW/ES only.
- The a=0 transition matrix is primitive and all likelihood comparison constants are finite.
- Stationary drift is identically zero, not presumed from the unweighted model.
- The maximal bound concerns the entire path, not just its endpoint.
- Endpoint classes number O(H²), and squaring their weight loses only a polynomial factor.
- No even-n restriction is imposed before mirroring; the final loop is always even.
- Every new seam has weight 1, including when a=0.
- The injection is on ordered pairs, and the length normalization is m=n+2H.
- Nonnegative coefficients are essential twice: selecting/comparing the weighted class and retaining its constructed loops inside the total Q coefficient.
- In particular, a positive square B_n² coming from some loops is not a lower bound on the full signed sum when a<0.
- This establishes only the nonnegative part of the recovered full target a>=−1. The full question remains unresolved unless the remaining signed interval is separately settled.
