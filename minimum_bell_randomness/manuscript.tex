\begin{abstract}
We study how many measurement settings are needed for one Bell score to
certify \(2\log_2d\) private random bits from projective \(d\)-outcome
measurements.  We do not determine the all-dimensional minimum, but prove
four exact structural results.  First, if either party has only one input,
the complete behavior is local and has a compatible pure realization in
which Eve guesses every joint output perfectly; thus both parties need at
least two inputs.  Second, we give a positive-factor theorem for a broad
first-harmonic polar-linear class.  Under explicit phase-product hypotheses,
every permutation of the scalar maximizing phases produces an admissible
order-\(d\) maximizer with the same Bell-visible correlators, although its
full target distribution can change.  Third, the phase-permutation
counterexamples previously found for the first cyclic family of Perito
et al.\ also annihilate the sum-of-squares certificate of their second
family.  For both augmented families and every \(d\geq4\), an exact
maximizer with trivial Eve obeys
\[
G(AB|E)\geq
\frac1{d^2}+
\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)}
>\frac1{d^2}.
\]
At \(d=4\), an exact \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})\) certificate gives
\(G=3/32>1/16\).  Finally, we compute all four ideal tables of the standard
two-input qudit self-test and prove that a natural separately bounded
third-setting repair cannot expose its common computational mutually
unbiased basis.  These results explain why several efficient designs fail,
but they neither rule out nor construct an all-dimensional \(2\times2\) or
\(2\times3\) protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Framework and results}

Fix an integer \(d\geq2\).  Alice has projective measurements
\(\{M^x_a\}_{a\in\mathbb Z_d}\), Bob has
\(\{N^y_b\}_{b\in\mathbb Z_d}\), and Eve purifies their finite-dimensional
state \(\lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE}\).  For a designated pair
\((x_*,y_*)\), write
\[
\sigma_E^{ab}
=
\Tr_{AB}\!\left[
(M^{x_*}_a\otimes N^{y_*}_b\otimes\Id)
\lvert\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi\rvert
\right].
\]
Maximal global private randomness means
\begin{equation}
\sigma_E^{ab}=\rho_E/d^2
\quad\text{for every }a,b,
\qquad
\rho_E=\sum_{a,b}\sigma_E^{ab}.
\label{eq:private}
\end{equation}
This is stronger than the scalar equality
\(p(a,b|x_*,y_*)=1/d^2\).

Let \(\mathfrak M_d\) denote the setting pairs for which one real linear
Bell functional has a quantum maximum whose every realizing strategy
satisfies \eqref{eq:private}.  Our universal lower bound is:

\begin{theorem}[One-input impossibility]\label{thm:oneinput}
For every \(d\geq2\) and every finite \(m\),
\[
(1,m)\notin\mathfrak M_d,
\qquad
(m,1)\notin\mathfrak M_d.
\]
In fact, if Alice has one input, every complete nonsignalling behavior has a
compatible pure projective realization satisfying
\[
G(AB|0,y,E)=1
\]
for each Bob input \(y\).  The symmetric statement holds when Bob has one
input.
\end{theorem}

For \(d=2\), the lower bound is tight.  The binary score
\[
A_0B_0-2A_0B_1+2A_1B_0+2A_1B_1
\]
has maximum \(3\sqrt3\) and certifies two private joint bits at
\((0,0)\).  This is the \(\delta=\pi/6\) case, up to an output flip, of
Wooltorton, Brown, and Colbeck~\cite{WooltortonEtAl2022}; Appendix
\ref{app:binary} records a short independent SOS check.

For \(d\geq3\), the exact minimum remains open.  The strongest
all-dimensional single-score construction located in our targeted audit has
two Alice settings and \(d^2+1\) Bob settings
\cite{FarkasMironowiczAugusiak2026}.  Thus the rigorous setting bounds
recorded here are
\[
2\leq m_A,\qquad 2\leq m_B,
\]
with no claim that the known upper construction is minimal.

\section{Proof of the one-input impossibility}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:oneinput}]
Nonsignalling makes
\(p(a)=\sum_b p(a,b|y)\) independent of \(y\).  For \(p(a)>0\), set
\[
r_y(b|a)=\frac{p(a,b|y)}{p(a)};
\]
for \(p(a)=0\), choose \(r_y(\,\cdot\,|a)\) arbitrarily.  Introduce the
finite hidden variable
\[
\lambda=(a,b_1,\ldots,b_{m_B}),\qquad
\mu(\lambda)=p(a)\prod_y r_y(b_y|a).
\]
Alice outputs the stored \(a\), and Bob outputs the stored \(b_y\) on input
\(y\).  Summing over the other entries gives
\[
\sum_{\lambda:\lambda_A=a,\lambda_y=b}\mu(\lambda)
=p(a)r_y(b|a)=p(a,b|y),
\]
so this deterministic local model reproduces the complete behavior.

It has a finite-dimensional pure projective realization:
\[
\lvert\Psi'\rangle
=
\sum_\lambda\sqrt{\mu(\lambda)}
\lvert\lambda\rangle_A\lvert\lambda\rangle_B
\lvert\lambda\rangle_E,
\]
where Alice's and Bob's PVMs group the computational labels by \(a\) and
\(b_y\).  Eve reads \(\lambda\) and knows both outputs for every target
input, so \(G=1\).  Exchanging Alice and Bob proves the symmetric claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem concerns the total number of test inputs.  It does not say that a
larger Bell test cannot use one designated generation input on each wing.
It is the standard locality fact for a scenario with one input on one wing,
written here because it gives the exact resource baseline.  It is stronger
than a one-score obstruction because the flagged realization reproduces the
entire behavior.
\end{remark}

\section{A polar-linear permutation theorem}

For an operator \(C\), write
\(\absop C=(C^\dagger C)^{1/2}\) and
\(\Reop T=(T+T^\dagger)/2\).

\begin{lemma}[Polar positive factor]\label{lem:polar}
Let \(C=V\absop C\) be the canonical polar decomposition and let \(B\) be
unitary.  Then
\[
\frac{\absop C+\absop{C^\dagger}}2\otimes\Id
-\Reop(C\otimes B)
=\frac12P^\dagger P,
\]
where
\[
P=
\absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}\otimes\Id
-V\absop C^{1/2}\otimes B.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polar partial isometry satisfies
\(V^\dagger V=s(\absop C)\) and
\(\absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}V=V\absop C^{1/2}\).  Consequently
\[
\absop{C^\dagger}^{1/2}V\absop C^{1/2}=V\absop C=C,
\]
while
\[
\absop C^{1/2}V^\dagger V\absop C^{1/2}=\absop C.
\]
Expanding \(P^\dagger P\), with all tensor factors displayed, gives
\[
\absop C\otimes\Id+\absop{C^\dagger}\otimes\Id
-C\otimes B-C^\dagger\otimes B^\dagger.
\]
No inverse and no extension of \(V\) to a unitary is used.
\end{proof}

Fix finitely many coefficient pairs
\(\alpha_r,\beta_r\in\bbC\), and set
\[
g_r(z)=\alpha_r+\beta_rz,\qquad
f(z)=\sum_r\absop{g_r(z)},\qquad
M=\max_{|z|=1}f(z).
\]
For unitaries \(A_0,A_1,B_r,B_*\), define
\begin{equation}
\cJ
=
\sum_r\Reop\!\left[
(\alpha_rA_0+\beta_rA_1)\otimes B_r
\right]
+\Reop(A_0\otimes B_*).
\label{eq:polar-linear}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact certificate and permutation blindness]
\label{thm:permutation}
Put \(U=A_0^\dagger A_1\),
\(C_r=A_0g_r(U)=V_r\absop{C_r}\), and
\(f(U)=\sum_r\absop{g_r(U)}\).  Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_r&=
\absop{C_r^\dagger}^{1/2}\otimes\Id
-V_r\absop{C_r}^{1/2}\otimes B_r,\\
G&=M\Id-f(U),\\
R_*&=\Id-A_0\otimes B_*.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(M+1)\Id-\cJ
=
\frac12\sum_rP_r^\dagger P_r
+\frac12R_*^\dagger R_*
+\frac12\bigl(G+A_0GA_0^\dagger\bigr)\otimes\Id .
}
\label{eq:polar-certificate}
\end{equation}

Now let \(\omega=\e^{2\pi i/d}\).  Suppose there are labeled phases
\(z_j,s_{rj}\in\mathbb T\) satisfying
\[
f(z_j)=M,\qquad
\prod_jz_j=1,
\]
\[
s_{rj}\absop{g_r(z_j)}=g_r(z_j),
\qquad
\prod_js_{rj}=1.
\]
At a zero of \(g_r\), \(s_{rj}\) may be any unitary polar-extension
phase.  For every permutation \(\kappa\) of the labels, the strategy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=X,&
A_1&=X\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),\\
B_r&=\overline{
X\operatorname{diag}(s_{r,\kappa_j})},&
B_*&=X
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:permuted-strategy}
\end{equation}
on
\(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_j\lvert j,j\rangle\)
attains \(M+1\), and every displayed observable has \(d\)-th power
\(\Id\).  All local first moments and the entire first-harmonic correlator
matrix of \eqref{eq:polar-linear} are independent of \(\kappa\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(g_r(U)\) is normal,
\[
\absop{C_r}=\absop{g_r(U)},\qquad
\absop{C_r^\dagger}
=A_0\absop{g_r(U)}A_0^\dagger.
\]
Functional calculus gives \(G\geq0\).  Sum
Lemma~\ref{lem:polar} over \(r\) and use
\[
\Id-\Reop(A_0\otimes B_*)=\tfrac12R_*^\dagger R_*
\]
to obtain \eqref{eq:polar-certificate}.

For arbitrary weights \(t_j\),
\begin{equation}
\left(X\operatorname{diag}(t_j)\right)^d
=\left(\prod_jt_j\right)\Id.
\label{eq:weighted-cycle}
\end{equation}
The two product hypotheses therefore prove every order relation in
\eqref{eq:permuted-strategy}.  Moreover,
\[
\alpha_rA_0+\beta_rA_1
=
X\operatorname{diag}(g_r(z_{\kappa_j}))
\]
has polar factor
\(X\operatorname{diag}(s_{r,\kappa_j})\).
At a zero of \(g_r\), this is a unitary extension of the canonical partial
isometry; it agrees on the support of \(\absop{C_r}\), which is all that the
positive factor uses.
The maximally entangled trace identity
\[
\langle\Phi_d|R\otimes S|\Phi_d\rangle
=d^{-1}\Tr(RS^T)
\]
then proves equality in every positive factor.

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle A_0\otimes B_r\rangle
&=\frac1d\sum_j\overline{s_{rj}},\\
\langle A_1\otimes B_r\rangle
&=\frac1d\sum_jz_j\overline{s_{rj}},\\
\langle A_0\otimes B_*\rangle&=1,\qquad
\langle A_1\otimes B_*\rangle=\frac1d\sum_jz_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are symmetric sums of the labeled data.  Every local observable is a
weighted shift and hence has zero trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact randomness obstruction]\label{cor:target}
Use the spectral convention
\(O=\sum_a\omega^a\Pi_a\).  For the target
\((A_1,B_*)\), define
\[
q_0=1,\qquad q_{j+1}=z_{\kappa_j}q_j,\qquad
\widehat q_m=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}q_j\omega^{mj}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
p_\kappa(a,b|1,*)
=
\frac{\absop{\widehat q_{-(a+b)}}^2}{d^3}.
\label{eq:target-table}
\end{equation}
If the Fourier magnitudes are not constant, the trivial-Eve realization
obeys
\[
G(AB|1,*,E)
\geq
\frac{\max_m\absop{\widehat q_m}^2}{d^3}
>\frac1{d^2}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:weighted-cycle} gives \(q_d=q_0\).  The eigenvectors of
\(A_0=X\) and
\(A_1=X\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j})\) are respectively
\[
u_c=d^{-1/2}\sum_j\omega^{-cj}\lvert j\rangle,
\qquad
v_a=d^{-1/2}\sum_jq_j\omega^{-aj}\lvert j\rangle.
\]
The transpose of Bob's outcome-\(b\) projector is Alice's
outcome-\((-b)\) projector for \(X\).  Taking their overlap proves
\eqref{eq:target-table}.  Parseval gives
\(\sum_m\absop{\widehat q_m}^2=d^2\); nonconstancy therefore forces one
magnitude squared to exceed \(d\).
\end{proof}

\section{Both cyclic families admit biased maximizers}

\subsection{The first polar-linear family}

Let
\[
z_k=\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi i(2k+\delta_d)}d\right),
\qquad
\delta_d=
\begin{cases}
0,&d\text{ odd},\\
1,&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
These are exactly the roots of \(z^d=(-1)^{d-1}\).

\begin{lemma}[Sharp scalar sum]\label{lem:scalar-sum}
\begin{equation}
\max_{|z|=1}
\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\absop{1+\omega^yz}
=2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right)
\label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
has equality precisely when \(z^d=(-1)^{d-1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(z=\e^{2is}\), \(h=\pi/d\), and \(a=h/2\).  Then the sum in
\eqref{eq:scalar} is
\[
2G_d(s),\qquad
G_d(s)=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\absop{\cos(s+yh)},
\]
and \(G_d\) has period \(h\).

If \(d=2m+1\), take \(-a\leq s\leq a\) and reindex by
\(k=-m,\ldots,m\).  Every \(s+kh\) lies in
\([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\), because \(a+mh=\pi/2\).  Hence
\[
G_d(s)=\sum_{k=-m}^m\cos(s+kh)=\csc(a)\cos s.
\]
Its maximum is attained exactly at \(s\equiv0\pmod h\), which is equivalent
to \(z^d=1\).

If \(d=2m\), take \(0\leq s\leq h\).  The first \(m\) cosines are
nonnegative and the last \(m\) are nonpositive; an endpoint term may vanish.
The two finite sums give
\[
G_d(s)
=
\sum_{y=0}^{m-1}\cos(s+yh)
-\sum_{y=m}^{2m-1}\cos(s+yh)
=\csc(a)\cos(s-a).
\]
Its maximum is attained exactly at \(s\equiv a\pmod h\), equivalently
\(z^d=-1\).  Multiplying by two proves the result.
\end{proof}

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:permutation} with
\[
g_y(z)=1+\omega^yz.
\]
The polynomial \(t^d-(-1)^{d-1}\) gives
\[
\prod_kz_k=1,\qquad
\prod_k(1+\omega^yz_k)=2>0.
\]
Thus the polar phases
\[
s_{y,k}=\frac{1+\omega^yz_k}{\absop{1+\omega^yz_k}}
\]
have product one, and every root ordering is an order-\(d\) maximizer of
\[
\overline{\cI}_d
=
\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}
\Reop\!\left[(A_0+\omega^yA_1)\otimes B_y\right]
+\Reop(A_0\otimes B_d)
\]
with exact value
\[
2\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2d}\right)+1.
\]
This recovers, in the general language above, the first-family
counterexamples of Ref.~\cite{KriebelCounterexample2026}.

\subsection{The target bias}

For \(d\geq4\), choose the final-two swap
\begin{equation}
\kappa=(0,1,\ldots,d-3,d-1,d-2).
\label{eq:bad-order}
\end{equation}
The second cyclic autocorrelation of the sequence in
Corollary~\ref{cor:target} is
\[
C_2=\sum_jq_{j+2}\overline{q_j}
=\sum_jz_{\kappa_j}z_{\kappa_{j+1}}.
\]
It vanishes in the canonical order, whereas \eqref{eq:bad-order} gives
\begin{equation}
C_2=(z_{d-1}-z_{d-2})(z_{d-3}-z_0),
\qquad
\absop{C_2}
=4\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)>0.
\label{eq:C2}
\end{equation}
Hence the target table is nonuniform.

For a quantitative bound, put
\[
x_m=\absop{\widehat q_m}^2-d,\qquad
\Delta=\max_mx_m.
\]
Then \(\sum_mx_m=0\), and Fourier inversion at lag two gives
\[
C_2=\frac1d\sum_mx_m\omega^{-2m}.
\]
The nonzero mean-zero sequence \(x_m\) has at most \(d-1\) positive entries,
so
\[
d\absop{C_2}\leq\sum_m\absop{x_m}
=2\sum_{x_m>0}x_m\leq2(d-1)\Delta.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:target-table} proves
\begin{equation}
G(AB|1,d,E)
\geq
\frac1{d^2}
+
\frac{2\sin(\pi/d)\sin(3\pi/d)}{d^2(d-1)}.
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
At \(d=2,3\), every root ordering in this particular family is
Fourier-flat.

\subsection{The second SOS-certified family}

We now show that the same Bob observables maximize the second family of
Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}.  Put
\(\eta=\e^{\pi i/d}\), so \(\omega=\eta^2\), and let
\[
\lambda_\ell
=
\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}\eta^{\ell(\ell-1)}}
{d\sin(\pi(\ell-\tfrac12)/d)},
\qquad 0\leq\ell<d.
\label{eq:lambda}
\]
This is \(\lambda_{0,\ell-1}\) in the source's cyclic indexing; the formula
at \(\ell=0\) is read literally with \((-1)^{-1}=-1\).
For Bob's first-family observables, define
\[
C_\ell=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\ell y}B_y.
\]
The Hermitian Bell functional is
\begin{equation}
\cF_d
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}
\Reop\!\left(
\overline{\lambda_\ell}A_\ell\otimes C_\ell
\right).
\label{eq:second-functional}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Permutation maximizers of the second family]
\label{thm:second}
For every permutation \(\kappa\), define
\[
q_\ell=\eta^{-\ell(\ell-1+\delta_d)},\qquad
D_\ell
=q_\ell X\operatorname{diag}
\left(\omega^{-\ell\kappa_j}\right)_j,
\qquad
A_\ell=\overline{D_\ell}.
\]
Then every \(A_\ell\) and \(B_y\) has \(d\)-th power \(\Id\), and
\[
C_\ell=d\lambda_\ell D_\ell.
\]
The strategy on \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\) attains
\[
\langle\cF_d\rangle=d.
\]
With \(B_d=X\), the augmented Hermitian functional
\[
\overline{\cF}_d
=\cF_d+\Reop(A_0\otimes B_d)
\]
has value \(d+1\).  For the ordering \eqref{eq:bad-order}, its target
\((A_1,B_d)\) has exactly the distribution
\eqref{eq:target-table}, and hence obeys the strict gap
\eqref{eq:gap} for every \(d\geq4\).  Its complete matrix of complex
first-harmonic correlators is independent of \(\kappa\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z_r=\eta^{2r+\delta_d}\), write
\[
s_0(z_r)
=\varepsilon_r\eta^{r+\delta_d/2},\qquad
\varepsilon_r=
\begin{cases}
+1,&2r+\delta_d<d,\\
-1,&2r+\delta_d>d.
\end{cases}
\]
There is no equality case.  Also
\(s_y(z_r)=s_0(z_{r+y\bmod d})\).  Therefore the diagonal weight of
\(C_\ell\) at a label \(r\) is
\[
\sum_y\omega^{\ell y}\overline{s_y(z_r)}
=
\omega^{-\ell r}S_\ell,
\qquad
S_\ell=
\sum_t\omega^{\ell t}\overline{s_0(z_t)}.
\]
Let
\[
n=\frac{d+1-\delta_d}{2},\qquad
r_\ell=\eta^{2\ell-1}.
\]
The first \(n\) signs \(\varepsilon_t\) are positive and the rest are
negative, while \(r_\ell^d=-1\).  Two geometric sums give
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_\ell
&=\eta^{-\delta_d/2}
\left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}r_\ell^t
-\sum_{t=n}^{d-1}r_\ell^t\right)\\
&=-\frac{2\eta^{-\delta_d/2}r_\ell^n}{1-r_\ell}\\
&=
\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}\eta^{-\ell\delta_d}}
{\sin(\pi(\ell-\tfrac12)/d)}
=d\lambda_\ell q_\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \(C_\ell=d\lambda_\ell D_\ell\), including its phase.

The weighted-cycle identity \eqref{eq:weighted-cycle} and
\(\sum_j\kappa_j=d(d-1)/2\) give
\[
D_\ell^d
=
q_\ell^d\omega^{-\ell\sum_j\kappa_j}\Id
=
(-1)^{
\ell(\ell-1+\delta_d)+\ell(d-1)
}\Id
=\Id.
\]
Indeed,
\(\ell(\ell+d-2+\delta_d)\) is even because
\(d-2+\delta_d\) is odd.  Thus the Alice observables are admissible.

For completeness, the coefficient identity
\[
\sum_\ell\absop{\lambda_\ell}^2=1
\]
follows from
\[
\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}
\csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(\ell-\tfrac12)}d\right)=d^2,
\]
the derivative of the finite cotangent-sum identity.  Fourier orthogonality
and \(B_y^\dagger B_y=\Id\) give
\[
\sum_\ell C_\ell^\dagger C_\ell=d^2\Id.
\]
Consequently, for arbitrary admissible observables,
\[
P_\ell=d\lambda_\ell\Id-A_\ell\otimes C_\ell
\]
satisfies the exact SOS
\begin{equation}
d\Id-\cF_d
=\frac1{2d}\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}P_\ell^\dagger P_\ell.
\label{eq:second-sos}
\end{equation}
For our strategy,
\[
P_\ell|\Phi_d\rangle
=d\lambda_\ell
\bigl[\Id-\overline{D_\ell}\otimes D_\ell\bigr]
|\Phi_d\rangle=0.
\]
Hence \(\langle\cF_d\rangle=d\).  Since \(A_0=X\) and
\((X\otimes X)|\Phi_d\rangle=|\Phi_d\rangle\), the augmentation contributes
one.  Finally,
\[
A_1
=
\overline{\eta^{-\delta_d}
X\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{-\kappa_j})}
=X\operatorname{diag}(\eta^{2\kappa_j+\delta_d})
=X\operatorname{diag}(z_{\kappa_j}),
\]
where the first equality is read entrywise in the weighted shift.  Thus the
target pair is literally the one in Corollary~\ref{cor:target}.  Moreover,
\[
\langle A_\ell\otimes B_y\rangle
=
\frac{\overline{q_\ell}}d
\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}
\omega^{\ell r}\overline{s_y(z_r)},
\]
and \(\langle A_\ell\otimes B_d\rangle\) is obtained by deleting the
\(\overline{s_y(z_r)}\) factor.  These are symmetric sums over the complete
root set, so every complex first harmonic is permutation-independent even
though higher harmonics and the full target behavior need not be.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source conventions]
Equations \eqref{eq:second-functional}--\eqref{eq:second-sos} match
Eqs.~(19), (22), and (23) of Ref.~\cite{PeritoEtAl2026}.  Its nearby
Eq.~(20) omits \(d\lambda_\ell\), and the prose there omits Alice's complex
conjugation, which its theorem restores.  We use a real part in the
augmentation so that the Bell operator is Hermitian.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An exact four-outcome certificate}

At \(d=4\), set \(\zeta=\e^{\pi i/8}\) and
\(\kappa=(0,1,3,2)\).  Then
\[
A_0=X,\qquad
A_1=X\operatorname{diag}
(\zeta^2,\zeta^6,\zeta^{14},\zeta^{10}),
\qquad B_4=X.
\]
The sequence in Corollary~\ref{cor:target} is
\[
q=(1,\zeta^2,-1,\zeta^6),
\qquad
\left(\absop{\widehat q_m}^2\right)_{m=0}^3=(2,6,2,6).
\]
Thus, for both augmented cyclic families,
\[
p(a,b|1,4)=
\begin{cases}
1/32,&a+b\text{ even},\\
3/32,&a+b\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
G(AB|1,4,E)=\frac3{32}>\frac1{16}.
\]
The accompanying verifier works exactly in
\(\mathbb Q[x]/(x^8+1)\), reconstructs the published \(\lambda\)'s, checks
\(C_\ell=4\lambda_\ell D_\ell\), every fourth-power relation, the full SOS
operator identity, both Bell values, and the displayed table.

\begin{corollary}[Value-only robustness is impossible]
For either augmented cyclic family and every \(d\geq4\), no valid
value-only bound
\[
G(AB|E)\leq\frac1{d^2}+f_d(\varepsilon),
\qquad f_d(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0
\]
can hold for all strategies with Bell deficit at most \(\varepsilon\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exact biased maximizer is feasible for every \(\varepsilon\geq0\) and
has the positive gap \eqref{eq:gap}.  This says nothing against bounds that
condition on additional observed statistics.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the standard
\texorpdfstring{\(2\times2\)}{2 x 2} qudit route does not immediately work}

The standard two-input self-test of a maximally entangled qudit
\cite{SarkarEtAl2021} uses Fourier-phase bases.  Directly from the geometric
amplitude on \(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\), its four joint tables have the form
\begin{equation}
p(a,b|x,y)
=
\frac{\sin^2(\pi\delta_{xy})}
{d^3\sin^2(\pi(a-b+\delta_{xy})/d)},
\qquad
(\delta_{xy})=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/4&3/4\\
-1/4&1/4
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:satwap-table}
\end{equation}
up to input swaps, conjugation, transposition, and output relabeling.
Therefore every table has
\[
p_{\max}
=
\frac1{2d^3\sin^2(\pi/(4d))}
>\frac1{d^2}.
\]
None of the four setting pairs is uniform, for any \(d\geq2\).  Its ideal
joint min-entropy is
\[
\log_2\!\left(2d^3\sin^2\frac{\pi}{4d}\right)
=\log_2d+\log_2\frac{\pi^2}{8}+o(1).
\]
The existing self-test certifies \(\log_2d\) local bits, not the desired
\(2\log_2d\) global bits.

Suppose a third Bob setting is perfectly correlated with either one of
Alice's two ideal bases by a separately bounded term.  Its cross table with
Alice's other basis has half-step offset:
\[
p_{\max}^{\mathrm{cross}}
=
\frac1{d^3\sin^2(\pi/(2d))}
>\frac1{d^2}
\quad(d\geq3).
\]
Thus the direct ``self-test plus perfect anchor'' composition already has a
nonuniform ideal maximizer.  The qubit exception is exactly the case in
which the two Alice bases are mutually unbiased.

The following proposition rules out a broader version of the same
coefficientwise construction.  Let \(F\) be the Fourier matrix,
\(\rho=\e^{\pi i/d}\), and
\[
R=\operatorname{diag}(1,\rho,\ldots,\rho^{d-1}).
\]
The real Hermitian operator system generated by the two ideal Alice PVMs is
\[
\cS
=
\left\{
F\operatorname{diag}(x)F^\dagger
+RF\operatorname{diag}(y)F^\dagger R^\dagger:
x,y\in\mathbb R^d
\right\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Computational-MUB exposure obstruction]
\label{prop:mub-obstruction}
If \(K\in\cS\) has a computational vector \(e_r\) as an eigenvector with
eigenvalue \(\kappa\), then either \(K=\kappa\Id\), or \(\kappa\) is neither
the largest nor the smallest eigenvalue of \(K\).

Consequently no nontrivial third-setting term
\[
\mathcal L=\sum_bK_b\otimes Q_b,\qquad K_b\in\cS,
\]
can expose the computational PVM on the maximally entangled strategy through
the common coefficientwise bound \(K_b\leq\Lambda\Id\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(K=C+RC'R^\dagger\), where \(C,C'\) are Hermitian circulants:
\[
C_{jk}=c_{[j-k]},\qquad C'_{jk}=c'_{[j-k]}.
\]
The eigenvector equations in column \(r\) are
\[
c_m+\rho^{\nu_r(m)}c'_m=0,
\]
where \(\nu_r(m)\in[-r,d-1-r]\) is congruent to \(m\pmod d\).
Comparing each equation with its Hermitian conjugate forces
\[
c_m=c'_m=0
\quad\text{unless}\quad
m\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\cup\{d-s,\ldots,d-1\},
\qquad
s=\min(r,d-1-r).
\]
For the surviving modes, the ordinary and wrapped diagonals cancel except
in the upper-right and lower-left \(s\times s\) corners.  After grouping the
first \(s\), middle \(d-2s\), and last \(s\) coordinates,
\[
K-\kappa\Id
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&T\\
0&0&0\\
T^\dagger&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(T\) is upper-triangular Toeplitz.  Its nonzero eigenvalues are
\(\pm\sigma_j(T)\).  Hence a nonscalar \(K\) has spectrum on both sides of
\(\kappa\).

For the resulting bound \(\mathcal L\leq\Lambda\Id\) to be saturated on
\(\lvert\Phi_d\rangle\) with computational \(Q_b\), every computational
outcome has nonzero weight and the corresponding \(e_b\) must be a maximal
eigenvector of \(K_b\).  The first part makes this impossible unless all
relevant \(K_b\) are scalar, in which case the term constrains no
measurement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:mub-obstruction} is deliberately narrow.  It does not
rule out a joint SOS, a different common MUB, a different two-input
self-test, or an arbitrary \(2\times3\) Bell functional.
\end{remark}

\section{A sufficient private-MUB composition lemma}

The preceding obstruction identifies a design route that fails, not the
property a successful construction would need.  The latter has a short
operator-valued formulation.

\begin{lemma}[Private-MUB composition]\label{lem:private-mub}
Let \(\{P_b\}\) and \(\{R_a\}\) be Alice PVMs and let
\(\{Q_{\pi(b)}\}\) be a Bob PVM.  Suppose, on a purification
\(\lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE}\),
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.7em]
\item the Eve state conditioned on \(P_b\) equals \(\rho_E/d\);
\item \(Q_{\pi(b)}|\Psi\rangle=P_b|\Psi\rangle\);
\item
\(P_bR_aP_b|\Psi\rangle=d^{-1}P_b|\Psi\rangle\).
\end{enumerate}
Then the target pair \((R,Q)\) satisfies
\[
\sigma_E^{a,\pi(b)}=\rho_E/d^2
\]
for every \(a,b\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every Hermitian test operator \(T_E\), conditions (ii), cross-party
commutation, and the projector relations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Tr(T_E\sigma_E^{a,\pi(b)})
&=
\langle\Psi|R_aQ_{\pi(b)}T_EQ_{\pi(b)}R_a|\Psi\rangle\\
&=
\langle\Psi|P_bR_aP_bT_E|\Psi\rangle\\
&=
\frac1d\langle\Psi|P_bT_E|\Psi\rangle
=\frac1{d^2}\Tr(T_E\rho_E).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equality against every Hermitian \(T_E\) proves the operator identity.
\end{proof}

Thus a purification-stable self-test that yields a private reference basis
and the state-supported MUB sandwich, plus a compatible third-setting
equality condition, would solve the \(2\times3\) problem.  State extraction
alone, average scalar overlaps, and observed uniformity are insufficient.

\section{Binary benchmark}\label{app:binary}

For binary Hermitian unitaries, set
\[
\mathcal W_2
=A_0B_0-2A_0B_1+2A_1B_0+2A_1B_1.
\]
The exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
3\sqrt3\Id-\mathcal W_2
={}&
\frac1{2\sqrt3}
\bigl(\sqrt3 A_0-B_0+2B_1\bigr)^2\\
&+
\frac1{\sqrt3}
\bigl(\sqrt3 A_1-B_0-B_1\bigr)^2
\end{aligned}
\]
proves the maximum \(3\sqrt3\), with cross-party tensor identities
suppressed only in this display.  Equality yields
\[
(B_0-2B_1)|\Psi\rangle=\sqrt3A_0|\Psi\rangle,
\qquad
(B_0+B_1)|\Psi\rangle=\sqrt3A_1|\Psi\rangle.
\]
Putting \(X_A=(A_0+2A_1)/\sqrt3\) and
\(Z_B=(B_0-2B_1)/\sqrt3\) gives, on the state,
\[
B_0=X_A,\quad Z_B=A_0,\quad
\{A_0,X_A\}=0,\quad\{Z_B,B_0\}=0.
\]
The two associated cross-party stabilizers make
\(\langle A_0T_E\rangle\),
\(\langle B_0T_E\rangle\), and
\(\langle A_0B_0T_E\rangle\) vanish for every Hermitian \(T_E\).
These are exactly the three nontrivial binary operator-valued Fourier
coefficients, so \(\sigma_E^{ab}=\rho_E/4\).
