# Exact value for the finite dodecahedral benchmark

This note connects the independently verified finite certificates to the known
value chi_c(D) = 20/7. It makes no novelty claim and no universal graph-class
claim. The graph D is the explicit 20-vertex, 30-edge graph in the certificate
files, generated by NetworkX's dodecahedral graph constructor.

## 1. A continuous formulation and a rounding lemma

Let G be a finite simple graph with at least one edge and n vertices. Give each
vertex a coordinate theta(v) in R/Z and let

    m(theta) = min over edges uv of dist_R/Z(theta(u), theta(v)),
    M = max over theta in (R/Z)^n of m(theta).

The minimum is over a nonempty finite set of continuous functions, so m is
continuous. Compactness gives an attained maximum M. Assigning all vertices
distinct equally spaced coordinates proves M > 0, and an edge implies M <= 1/2.

Rounding lemma. If all edge distances are at least b/a, where a,b are positive
integers with a >= 2b, then c(v) = floor(a theta(v)), with representatives
0 <= theta(v) < 1, is an (a,b)-coloring. Indeed, for either ordered edge uv let
its clockwise gap d lie in [b/a, 1-b/a]. Lift theta(v) by an integer so that it
is theta(u)+d. The difference of the corresponding integer floors lies in
[b,a-b], since b <= a d <= a-b and the endpoints are integers. Reducing this
floor difference modulo a is exactly c(v)-c(u) modulo a and leaves it in that
interval. Hence every edge satisfies the required circular adjacency condition.

Conversely an (a,b)-coloring divided by a supplies coordinates whose edge
distances are at least b/a. In particular every discrete ratio a/b is at least
1/M. Section 2 proves M=q/k for positive integers k,q; applying the rounding
lemma with a=k and b=q to an optimal theta then proves chi_c(G)=1/M=k/q.
This closes the equivalence with the usual infimum over discrete colorings
without assuming rational attainment as an external theorem.

## 2. Tight directed cycles give a numerator bound

Fix optimal coordinates theta. Draw an arc u -> v for each edge uv whose
clockwise gap from u to v equals M. If M=1/2, every edge supplies both arcs and
there is a directed 2-cycle. Suppose henceforth M<1/2.

If this digraph were acyclic, choose integer heights h(v) strictly increasing
along each arc, for example longest directed-path lengths ending at v. Perturb
all coordinates simultaneously by

    theta_epsilon(v) = theta(v) + epsilon h(v) modulo 1.

Every tight clockwise gap increases, because its increment is
 epsilon(h(v)-h(u)) > 0. For sufficiently small positive epsilon its opposite
gap still exceeds M: the original opposite gap is 1-M > M. For any edge not
tight in either orientation, both original clockwise gaps exceed M, and for
sufficiently small epsilon they continue to exceed M. These choices can be
made simultaneously because there are finitely many edges. Also choose epsilon
small enough that each edge's lifted clockwise-gap expression stays within
(0,1), so taking residues introduces no discontinuity. Consequently every edge
distance becomes strictly greater than M. This contradicts optimality.

The tight digraph therefore contains a directed cycle. Taking one with no
repeated vertex gives length k <= n (including the already treated 2-cycle).
Following its k clockwise gaps returns to the same point of R/Z, so

    k M = q

for some integer q. Positivity of M gives q>=1, and M<=1/2 gives q<=k/2.
Thus M=q/k and the preceding rounding argument gives

    chi_c(G) = k/q, with 2 <= k <= n and 1 <= q <= floor(k/2).

Reduction of k/q to lowest terms can only decrease the numerator. This proof
uses no graph-specific assumption besides finiteness and existence of an edge.

## 3. The rational gap for at most 20 vertices

For k<=20, every ratio k/q < 20/7 is at most 17/6. To check this directly, when
q>=8 the ratio is at most 20/8=5/2. For q=1,...,7 the largest permissible integer
k is min(20, floor((20q-1)/7)), namely:

| q | largest k | k/q |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 5 | 5/2 |
| 3 | 8 | 8/3 |
| 4 | 11 | 11/4 |
| 5 | 14 | 14/5 |
| 6 | 17 | 17/6 |
| 7 | 19 | 19/7 |

All listed ratios are at most 17/6. The accompanying check_numerator_gap.py
also enumerates every k=2,...,20 and q=1,...,floor(k/2) using exact fractions.

## 4. Application to the certificate graph

The explicit verified K_{20/7} homomorphism gives chi_c(D)<=20/7. If this
inequality were strict, Sections 2 and 3 would give chi_c(D)<=17/6. The optimum
unit-circle placement would then have all edge distances at least 6/17; the
rounding lemma with a=17,b=6 would supply a K_{17/6} homomorphism. This contradicts
the independently verified exhaustive UNSAT branch tree for exactly the same
edge-list graph. Therefore

    chi_c(D) = 20/7.

The finite UNSAT tree and its checker are computational proof artifacts, not
proof-assistant formalizations. Their trust boundary includes the interpreter,
checker implementation, and edge-list identification with the dodecahedron.
The source files and negative-control tests make that boundary explicit. This
calculation verifies a known extremal example; it does not resolve the bound
for all triangle-free planar subcubic graphs.

## 5. Literature cross-check, not a dependency of the proof

Hatami and Tusserkani, *On the Complexity of the Circular Chromatic Number*,
arXiv:cs/0701007v1, PDF page 2, Lemma A, states the characterization by acyclicity
of a tight-coloring digraph and attributes it to Guichard. Pages 1–2 also credit
Vince with attained rationality. The primary research article PDF was inspected
through the web reader on 2026-10-20:

https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0701007

Its bibliography identifies Guichard, *Acyclic graph coloring and the complexity
of the star chromatic number*, J. Graph Theory 17 (1993), 129–134, and Vince,
*Star chromatic number*, J. Graph Theory 12 (1988), 551–559. Those original papers
were not independently inspected in this pass. The self-contained proof above
does not require treating this bibliography or the cited lemma as a proof of
the numerator bound.
