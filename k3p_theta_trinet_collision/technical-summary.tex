\textbf{Resolution.}
The high-level K3P trinet question has a negative answer: the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet inequality fails.  A fixed binary semi-directed strict level-two theta trinet with a genuine nontrivial $3$-blob produces exactly the same three-leaf distribution as an admissible three-star tree.  The witness lies in the quartic number field $\mathbb Q(h)$, where $h=5^{-1/4}$ is isolated by $2/3<h<7/10$; exact calculations use the basis $1,h,h^2,h^3$.  Both inheritance probabilities are $1/2$, every nontrivial edge eigenvalue lies in $(0,1)$, and every transition probability is strictly positive.  This answers negatively the K3P high-level trinet question raised by Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, and Martin after their level-one K3P result and arbitrary-level JC/K2P results.

\textbf{Topology and root suppression.}
Use the rooted calculation network
\[
\rho\!\to\!1,\ \rho\!\to\!u,\ u\!\to\!p,q,\ p,q\!\to\!r_2,\ p,q\!\to\!r_3,\ r_2\!\to\!2,\ r_3\!\to\!3.
\]
After suppressing $\rho$, the two root edges combine into the semi-directed pendant edge $1-u$.  The remaining blob is the theta graph with paths
\[
 p-u-q,\qquad p-r_2-q,\qquad p-r_3-q.
\]
Its three incident cut edges lead to leaves $1,2,3$, and it contains exactly the two reticulations $r_2,r_3$.  Thus it is a strict level-two nontrivial $3$-blob.  No no-$2$-sub-blob convention is needed.

\textbf{Exact network parameters.}
In coordinate order $(A,C,G,T)$, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
K&=(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12),&
U&=(1,\tfrac h3,h,\tfrac13),&
V&=(1,h,\tfrac h3,\tfrac13),\\[-1mm]
S&=(1,\tfrac{3h^2}{4},\tfrac14,\tfrac3{10}),&
T&=(1,\tfrac14,\tfrac{3h^2}{4},\tfrac3{10}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Assign $K$ to $\rho\to1,\rho\to u,r_2\to2,r_3\to3$; $U,V$ to $u\to p,u\to q$; and $S,T$ to all incoming reticulation edges from $p,q$, respectively.  Root suppression gives
\[
K\odotv K=(1,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14),\qquad
p(K\odotv K)=(\tfrac7{16},\tfrac3{16},\tfrac3{16},\tfrac3{16}).
\]
All inverse Fourier edge probabilities are strictly positive.  The explicit $U,V$ satisfy
\[
U_C=U_GU_T,\qquad V_G=V_CV_T,
\]
so the closed-form witness is on the boundary of the smaller strict positive-rate continuous-time cone, but strictly inside the stated stochastic space $\ThetaZero$.

\textbf{Comparison tree and factorization.}
Set
\[
\alpha=(1,\tfrac{5h^3+h}{16},\tfrac{5h^3+h}{16},\tfrac{h^2}{4}),\qquad
\beta=\gamma=(1,\tfrac{h^2}{4},\tfrac{h^2}{4},\tfrac{h^2}{4}).
\]
These three edges have strictly positive transition probabilities and strictly positive continuous-time K3P rates.  For a consistent label $(x,y,z)$, write $x=y+z$.  Removing the four $K$ factors, the network core is
\[
M_{y,z}=\tfrac14(S_yS_zU_{y+z}+S_yT_zU_yV_z+T_yS_zU_zV_y+T_yT_zV_{y+z}).
\]
Define
\[
B=(1,\tfrac{h^2}{2},\tfrac{h^2}{2},\tfrac{h^2}{2}),\quad
P=(1,\tfrac{5h^3+h}{4},\tfrac{5h^3+h}{4},h^2).
\]
Exact reduction using only $5h^4=1$ gives
\[
\boxed{M_{y,z}=P_{y+z}B_yB_z}\qquad(y,z\in\mathbb Z_2^2).
\]
Therefore
\[
q^N_{xyz}=K_x^2K_yK_zM_{y,z}=\alpha_x\beta_y\gamma_z=q^T_{xyz}.
\]
There are sixteen consistent Fourier coordinates and forty-eight inconsistent coordinates, which vanish in both models.  Hence all sixty-four Fourier coordinates and, by invertibility of the finite Fourier transform, all sixty-four leaf-pattern probabilities agree exactly.

\textbf{Full-rank strengthening.}
Differentiate the fifteen nonconstant consistent Fourier coordinates in row order
\[
\begin{gathered}
ACC,AGG,ATT,CAC,CCA,CGT,CTG,GAG,\\
GCT,GGA,GTC,TAT,TCG,TGC,TTA
\end{gathered}
\]
with respect to the specified fifteen edge/mixing coordinates (three coordinates on $\rho\to1$, $a_G$ on $u\to p$, $a_C,a_G$ on each incoming reticulation edge, $a_T$ on each reticulation-to-leaf edge, and the $p$-parent weight at $r_3$).  Exact elimination gives
\[
\boxed{\det J_*=\frac{h(10h^2+1)}{2^{61}3^4 5^{14}}>0.}
\]
Thus the fixed theta-trinet parameter map has rank $15$, so its stochastic image contains an ordinary open neighborhood of the common distribution in the $15$-dimensional affine space of consistent three-leaf group-based Fourier coordinates, $q_{AAA}=1$.  Intersecting this ambient neighborhood with the tree model yields a relatively open tree-model neighborhood of collisions.  This is a statement about distribution-space openness, not a claim that generic network parameters are tree-equivalent.

\textbf{Strict continuous-time strengthening.}
For a continuous-time K3P edge with all three rates positive, the exact equivalent conditions are
\[
a_C>a_Ga_T,\qquad a_G>a_Ca_T,\qquad a_T>a_Ca_G.
\]
Increase the two saturated coordinates by a common $\varepsilon>0$:
\[
U_C\mapsto U_C+\varepsilon,\qquad V_G\mapsto V_G+\varepsilon,
\]
and solve for the fifteen pivot parameters while holding the fifteen nonconstant Fourier coordinates fixed.  A real-analytic implicit-function argument gives a branch satisfying the fixed-output identity exactly for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.  Its exactly verified tangent satisfies $J_*p'+F_{U_C}+F_{V_G}=0$.  In particular,
\[
\frac{d a_G^U}{d\varepsilon}=\frac6{1+10h^2},
\]
and the two formerly saturated margins have derivatives
\[
\left.\frac d{d\varepsilon}(U_C-U_GU_T)\right|_0
=\frac{10h^2-1}{1+10h^2}>0,
\qquad
\left.\frac d{d\varepsilon}(V_G-V_CV_T)\right|_0=1.
\]
Every other rate margin, stochastic inequality, and mixing inequality has strict slack at $\varepsilon=0$, so all persist for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$.  Hence the same fixed tree distribution has a nearby theta-network preimage in which every network edge has three strictly positive continuous-time K3P rates.

\textbf{What the result does and does not show.}
\begin{itemize}
\item It shows that the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet inequality fails and blocks the direct K3P extension of the JC/K2P global blob-detection corollary.
\item It shows that a nontrivial $3$-blob is not universally identifiable from three-leaf K3P distributions on $\ThetaZero$.
\item It does not settle K3P quartet distinguishability, classify all tree/theta intersections, or imply generic tree-equivalence of theta-network parameters.
\end{itemize}
