\begin{abstract}
We give an exact negative answer to the high-level K3P trinet question: the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet inequality fails already for a binary semi-directed strict level-two theta trinet with a genuine nontrivial $3$-blob.  The comparison model is a three-leaf tree.  At an explicit point in the quartic number field $\mathbb{Q}(h)$, where $h=5^{-1/4}$, every network and tree edge lies strictly inside the stated stochastic parameter space $\ThetaZero$, every transition probability is strictly positive, and both inheritance probabilities equal $1/2$.  A sixteen-entry Fourier factorization proves equality of all sixty-four Fourier coordinates and hence all sixty-four leaf-pattern probabilities.  The explicit closed-form witness lies on the boundary of the smaller cone in which every continuous-time K3P substitution rate is strictly positive, but this is not a boundary of $\ThetaZero$.  We further compute an exact nonzero $15\times15$ Jacobian determinant, proving that the fixed theta-trinet image contains an ordinary open neighborhood in the $15$-dimensional affine space of consistent three-leaf group-based Fourier coordinates, $q_{AAA}=1$.  Finally, a real-analytic implicit-function argument, whose Jacobian and tangent data are verified exactly, shows that the collision persists in the strict continuous-time K3P rate cone.  A dependency-free verifier reconstructs the topology, parameters, collision, Jacobian, and the algebraic hypotheses and tangent signs used in that analytic argument.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and statement of the result}

Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, and Martin proved the level-one K3P trinet inequality in their Lemma~4.2, proved arbitrary-level trinet inequalities for JC and K2P in Lemmas~5.5 and~5.6, and used those results to obtain their global JC/K2P tree--network distinguishability Corollary~5.8 \cite{BritsHoltgrefeVanIerselMartin2026}.  In their discussion, they asked whether the high-level trinet inequality and quartet distinguishability extend to K3P, noting that the K3P level-one proof requires several invariants and does not directly support the one-invariant induction used for JC and K2P.

The construction below answers the high-level K3P trinet question negatively at strict level two.  It therefore also rules out a direct K3P extension of Corollary~5.8 on the same parameter space.  The JC and K2P results of \cite{BritsHoltgrefeVanIerselMartin2026} are unaffected.

\begin{theorem}[Exact K3P tree--theta collision]\label{thm:collision}
There is a binary semi-directed strict level-two trinet $N_{\theta}$ with a nontrivial $3$-blob and a three-leaf tree $T$ such that
\[
  \cM_{\KthreeP}(N_{\theta};\ThetaZero)
  \cap
  \cM_{\KthreeP}(T;\ThetaZero)
  \neq \varnothing.
\]
At the exhibited collision, every edge has all three nontrivial Fourier eigenvalues in $(0,1)$, every transition probability is strictly positive, and both inheritance probabilities are $1/2$.
\end{theorem}

The result is stronger locally than the existence of one isolated collision.

\begin{proposition}[Full-rank local overlap]\label{prop:rank}
At the explicit network parameters in Theorem~\ref{thm:collision}, the parameterization of the fixed theta-trinet topology has rank $15$, equal to the dimension of the affine group-based Fourier space $q_{AAA}=1$.  Consequently its stochastic image contains an ordinary open neighborhood of the common distribution within that affine space.  Its intersection with the tree model contains a relatively open neighborhood of the common point inside the tree model.
\end{proposition}

The closed-form parameters have two zero continuous-time rate margins.  Nevertheless, the full-rank calculation permits an analytic perturbative strengthening with exactly verified algebraic data.

\begin{corollary}[Strict continuous-time collision]\label{cor:ct}
The same fixed tree distribution has a nearby preimage on the same theta-trinet topology for which every network edge is generated by a continuous-time K3P process with all three instantaneous substitution rates strictly positive.  The three comparison-tree edges also have strictly positive K3P rates.
\end{corollary}


\paragraph{Scope.}
The construction shows that the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet inequality fails, prevents a direct K3P extension of the JC/K2P global blob-detection corollary, and disproves universal identification of a nontrivial $3$-blob from three-leaf K3P distributions on $\ThetaZero$.  It does not by itself settle K3P quartet distinguishability, classify all tree/theta intersections, imply that generic theta-network parameters are tree-equivalent, refute every possible level-two K3P identifiability theorem, or classify observational equivalence for arbitrary networks.

\section{K3P Fourier notation and the continuous-time subcone}

Identify the nucleotide set with the Klein group
\[
  G=\{A,C,G,T\}\cong \mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2,
\]
where $A$ is the identity and every element is self-inverse.  We use the index order $(A,C,G,T)$ and bitwise XOR for addition.

A K3P edge is represented in Fourier coordinates by
\[
  a=(1,a_C,a_G,a_T).
\]
Its transition-probability vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-edge}
\begin{aligned}
 p_A&=\frac{1+a_C+a_G+a_T}{4},&
 p_C&=\frac{1+a_C-a_G-a_T}{4},\\
 p_G&=\frac{1-a_C+a_G-a_T}{4},&
 p_T&=\frac{1-a_C-a_G+a_T}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The parameter space $\ThetaZero$ used here requires
\[
  0<a_C,a_G,a_T<1,
  \qquad p_A,p_C,p_G,p_T\geq 0.
\]
All probabilities in our construction are in fact strictly positive.

For three leaves, a Fourier coordinate $q_{xyz}$ can be nonzero only when
\[
  x+y+z=A.
\]
There are $4^2=16$ such consistent labels and $64-16=48$ inconsistent labels.  For a three-star tree with pendant-edge vectors $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-param}
  q^{T}_{xyz}=\alpha_x\beta_y\gamma_z
  \qquad (x+y+z=A),
\end{equation}
and all inconsistent coordinates vanish.

\begin{lemma}[Strict continuous-time rate cone]\label{lem:ct-cone}
Let $0<a_C,a_G,a_T<1$.  The edge vector $(1,a_C,a_G,a_T)$ arises from a continuous-time K3P process with all three instantaneous substitution rates strictly positive if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ct-ineq}
  a_C>a_Ga_T,
  \qquad
  a_G>a_Ca_T,
  \qquad
  a_T>a_Ca_G.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For rates $\lambda_C,\lambda_G,\lambda_T\geq0$, absorbing elapsed time into the rates, the nontrivial eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ct-eigs}
\begin{aligned}
 a_C&=\exp[-2(\lambda_G+\lambda_T)],\\
 a_G&=\exp[-2(\lambda_C+\lambda_T)],\\
 a_T&=\exp[-2(\lambda_C+\lambda_G)].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Hence
\[
 \frac{a_C}{a_Ga_T}=\e^{4\lambda_C},\qquad
 \frac{a_G}{a_Ca_T}=\e^{4\lambda_G},\qquad
 \frac{a_T}{a_Ca_G}=\e^{4\lambda_T}.
\]
This proves necessity.  Conversely, define
\[
 \lambda_C=\frac14\log\frac{a_C}{a_Ga_T},\quad
 \lambda_G=\frac14\log\frac{a_G}{a_Ca_T},\quad
 \lambda_T=\frac14\log\frac{a_T}{a_Ca_G}.
\]
The inequalities in \eqref{eq:ct-ineq} make all three rates positive, and direct substitution recovers \eqref{eq:ct-eigs}.
\end{proof}

\section{The semi-directed theta trinet and its parameters}

Let $h$ be the unique positive root in the rational isolating interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h}
  5h^4-1=0,
  \qquad \frac23<h<\frac7{10}.
\end{equation}
Thus $h=5^{-1/4}$.  All exact calculations take place in the quartic number field $\mathbb{Q}(h)$, represented in the basis $1,h,h^2,h^3$.

We first give a rooted representation used to calculate displayed-tree probabilities.  Its vertices are the root $\rho$, tree vertices $u,p,q$, reticulations $r_2,r_3$, and leaves $1,2,3$, with arcs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcs}
\begin{gathered}
 \rho\to1,\quad \rho\to u,\quad
 u\to p,\quad u\to q,\\
 p\to r_2,\quad q\to r_2,\quad
 p\to r_3,\quad q\to r_3,\\
 r_2\to2,\quad r_3\to3.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Each reticulation chooses either parent with probability $1/2$.

  \caption{The rooted representation used for the displayed-tree calculation and the corresponding semi-directed trinet.  Suppressing $\rho$ composes the two $K$ edges into the pendant edge $K\odotv K$.  Double circles mark the two reticulations.}
  \label{fig:network}
\end{figure}

Define the edge eigenvalue vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:network-vectors}
\begin{aligned}
 K&=\left(1,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12\right),\\
 U&=\left(1,\frac h3,h,\frac13\right),&
 V&=\left(1,h,\frac h3,\frac13\right),\\
 S&=\left(1,\frac{3h^2}{4},\frac14,\frac3{10}\right),&
 T&=\left(1,\frac14,\frac{3h^2}{4},\frac3{10}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Assign $K$ to $\rho\to1$, $\rho\to u$, $r_2\to2$, and $r_3\to3$; assign $U$ to $u\to p$ and $V$ to $u\to q$; assign $S$ to both arcs out of $p$ and $T$ to both arcs out of $q$.

\begin{lemma}[Topology]\label{lem:topology}
After suppressing the degree-two root in the underlying semi-directed graph, the network in \eqref{eq:arcs} is a strict level-two trinet with a nontrivial $3$-blob.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Root suppression replaces $\rho\to1$ and $\rho\to u$ by one pendant edge $1-u$.  The maximal subgraph containing no cut edge has vertex set
\[
  \{u,p,q,r_2,r_3\}
\]
and consists of the three internally disjoint $p$--$q$ paths
\[
  p-u-q,
  \qquad p-r_2-q,
  \qquad p-r_3-q.
\]
Thus it is a theta graph.  Every core edge lies on a cycle, while the edges to leaves $1,2,3$ are cut edges.  The core is therefore a maximal nontrivial blob with exactly three incident leaf components.  It contains precisely the two reticulations $r_2,r_3$, so the trinet is strict level two.
\end{proof}

The theorem uses only these robust blob facts.  It does not require a convention-dependent claim that the network has no literal ``$2$-sub-blob.''\footnote{Under a bare two-boundary-vertex wording, an individual theta edge can be counted as a two-terminal induced subgraph, although contracting it creates a degree-four vertex rather than a degree-two suppression.  No proper nontrivial cyclic two-terminal contraction removes the theta blob, and the counterexample is independent of this terminology.}

The two root-adjacent $K$ edges compose coordinatewise under suppression:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2}
  K\odotv K=\left(1,\frac14,\frac14,\frac14\right).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:inverse-edge}, its transition probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2-probs}
  \left(\frac7{16},\frac3{16},\frac3{16},\frac3{16}\right),
\end{equation}
so the effective semi-directed pendant edge is strictly interior.

\begin{lemma}[Network-edge admissibility]\label{lem:network-adm}
Every effective edge of the semi-directed trinet lies in $\ThetaZero$, and every transition probability is strictly positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inverse Fourier probabilities for $K$ are
\[
  \left(\frac58,\frac18,\frac18,\frac18\right),
\]
and those for $K\odotv K$ are given in \eqref{eq:K2-probs}.  For $U$ and $V$ they are
\begin{align*}
 p(U)&=\left(\frac{1+h}{3},\frac{1-h}{6},
             \frac{1+h}{6},\frac{1-h}{3}\right),\\
 p(V)&=\left(\frac{1+h}{3},\frac{1+h}{6},
             \frac{1-h}{6},\frac{1-h}{3}\right).
\end{align*}
For $S$ and $T$ they are
\begin{align*}
 p(S)=\bigg(&\frac{31}{80}+\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac9{80}+\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac{19}{80}-\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac{21}{80}-\frac{3h^2}{16}\bigg),\\
 p(T)=\bigg(&\frac{31}{80}+\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac{19}{80}-\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac9{80}+\frac{3h^2}{16},
              \frac{21}{80}-\frac{3h^2}{16}\bigg).
\end{align*}
The interval \eqref{eq:h} makes every displayed quantity strictly positive and every nontrivial eigenvalue strictly between zero and one.
\end{proof}

The explicit closed-form witness is interior to $\ThetaZero$, but it is not interior to the smaller strict continuous-time rate cone.  Indeed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ct-boundary}
  U_C=U_GU_T,
  \qquad
  V_G=V_CV_T.
\end{equation}
All other inequalities in \eqref{eq:ct-ineq} are strict.  This distinction will be removed perturbatively in Section~\ref{sec:ct-extension}.

\section{The comparison tree}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mn}
  m=\frac{5h^3+h}{16},
  \qquad n=\frac{h^2}{4},
\end{equation}
and give the pendant edges to leaves $1,2,3$ the respective vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-vectors}
  \alpha=(1,m,m,n),
  \qquad
  \beta=\gamma=(1,n,n,n).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Tree-edge admissibility]\label{lem:tree-adm}
The three comparison-tree edges lie in $\ThetaZero$, all their transition probabilities are strictly positive, and all three lie in the strict continuous-time K3P rate cone.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The probabilities for $\alpha$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-probs}
 \left(
 \frac14+\frac{5h^3+h}{32}+\frac{h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{5h^3+h}{32}+\frac{h^2}{16}
 \right),
\end{equation}
and those for $\beta=\gamma$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-probs}
 \left(
 \frac14+\frac{3h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{h^2}{16},
 \frac14-\frac{h^2}{16}
 \right).
\end{equation}
All entries and all nontrivial eigenvalues are strictly between their required bounds by \eqref{eq:h}.  For the continuous-time inequalities, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ have margin $n-n^2>0$ in every character.  For $\alpha$, the first two margins are $m(1-n)>0$, and the third is

 n-m^2=\frac{h^2(29-5h^2)}{128}>0,

where $5h^4=1$ was used.

\section{Exact Fourier factorization}

For a consistent label $(x,y,z)$ on leaves $(1,2,3)$, write $x=y+z$.  Strip the four $K$ edges in the rooted representation: the two root-adjacent edges contribute $K_x^2$, and the two reticulation-to-leaf edges contribute $K_yK_z$.

\begin{lemma}[Displayed-tree formula]\label{lem:display-formula}
After stripping the four $K$ factors, the network Fourier coordinate is the matrix entry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-def}
 M_{y,z}=\frac14\left(
 S_yS_zU_{y+z}
 +S_yT_zU_yV_z
 +T_yS_zU_zV_y
 +T_yT_zV_{y+z}
 \right).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-network-M}
  q^{N_\theta}_{xyz}=K_x^2K_yK_zM_{y,z}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are four independent inheritance choices.  Choosing the $p$ parent at both $r_2$ and $r_3$ gives the first monomial in \eqref{eq:M-def}; choosing $p$ at $r_2$ and $q$ at $r_3$ gives the second; choosing $q$ at $r_2$ and $p$ at $r_3$ gives the third; and choosing $q$ at both gives the fourth.  Each choice has weight $1/4$.  The character carried by an edge is the group sum of the labels below it, giving exactly the displayed subscripts.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PB}
\begin{aligned}
 B&=\left(1,\frac{h^2}{2},\frac{h^2}{2},\frac{h^2}{2}\right),\\
 P&=\left(1,\frac{5h^3+h}{4},\frac{5h^3+h}{4},h^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact core factorization]\label{lem:factorization}
For every $y,z\in G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factorization}
  M_{y,z}=P_{y+z}B_yB_z.
\end{equation}
In the order $(A,C,G,T)$, the common matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-matrix}
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \dfrac{h+h^3}{8} & \dfrac{h+h^3}{8} & \dfrac1{10}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{h+h^3}{8} & \dfrac1{20} & \dfrac{h^2}{20} & \dfrac{5h^3+h}{80}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{h+h^3}{8} & \dfrac{h^2}{20} & \dfrac1{20} & \dfrac{5h^3+h}{80}\\[2mm]
\dfrac1{10} & \dfrac{5h^3+h}{80} & \dfrac{5h^3+h}{80} & \dfrac1{20}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The simultaneous $C/G$ symmetry reduces the sixteen entries to six representative types.  Substitution into \eqref{eq:M-def}, followed only by $h^4=1/5$, gives
\begin{align*}
 M_{A,A}&=1,&
 M_{A,C}&=\frac{h+h^3}{8},&
 M_{A,T}&=\frac1{10},\\
 M_{C,C}&=\frac1{20},&
 M_{C,G}&=\frac{h^2}{20},&
 M_{C,T}&=\frac{5h^3+h}{80}.
\end{align*}
For example,
\[
 M_{C,C}=\frac14\left(\frac9{80}+\frac1{80}+\frac1{80}+\frac5{80}\right)=\frac1{20},
\]
and
\[
 M_{C,T}=\frac14\left(\frac{9h^3}{40}+\frac{h^3}{40}
 +\frac{h}{40}+\frac{h}{40}\right)=\frac{5h^3+h}{80}.
\]
These values and their symmetric images give \eqref{eq:M-matrix}.  On the factorized side, the same representatives are
\begin{align*}
 P_C B_A B_C&=\frac{5h^5+h^3}{8}=\frac{h+h^3}{8},\\
 P_T B_A B_T&=\frac{h^4}{2}=\frac1{10},\\
 P_A B_C^2&=\frac{h^4}{4}=\frac1{20},\\
 P_T B_C B_G&=\frac{h^6}{4}=\frac{h^2}{20},\\
 P_G B_C B_T&=\frac{(5h^3+h)h^4}{16}=\frac{5h^3+h}{80}.
\end{align*}
Thus all sixteen identities hold.  The certificate records all sixteen entries and all four displayed terms for each entry separately.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:collision}]
For every consistent $(x,y,z)$, Lemmas~\ref{lem:display-formula} and~\ref{lem:factorization} give
\[
 q^{N_\theta}_{xyz}
 =K_x^2K_yK_zP_xB_yB_z.
\]
The definitions imply
\[
 K_x^2P_x=\alpha_x,
 \qquad
 K_yB_y=\beta_y,
 \qquad
 K_zB_z=\gamma_z.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-equality}
 q^{N_\theta}_{xyz}=\alpha_x\beta_y\gamma_z=q^T_{xyz}
 \qquad(x+y+z=A).
\end{equation}
All forty-eight inconsistent Fourier coordinates vanish in both models.  Hence all sixty-four Fourier coordinates agree.  The finite Fourier transform on $G^3$ is invertible, so all sixty-four leaf-pattern probabilities agree as well.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:topology}, \ref{lem:network-adm}, and~\ref{lem:tree-adm} establish the topology and stochastic admissibility.
\end{proof}

\section{The full-rank Jacobian}\label{sec:rank}

Let $F$ denote the rooted theta-network parameterization restricted to the fifteen nonconstant consistent Fourier coordinates.  The row order is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jac-rows}
\begin{gathered}
 ACC,AGG,ATT,CAC,CCA,CGT,CTG,GAG,\\
 GCT,GGA,GTC,TAT,TCG,TGC,TTA.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The selected parameter columns, in order, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jac-cols}
\begin{gathered}
 e_{\rho1}.a_C,\,e_{\rho1}.a_G,\,e_{\rho1}.a_T,\,e_{up}.a_G,\\
 e_{pr_2}.a_C,\,e_{pr_2}.a_G,\,e_{qr_2}.a_C,\,e_{qr_2}.a_G,\\
 e_{pr_3}.a_C,\,e_{pr_3}.a_G,\,e_{qr_3}.a_C,\,e_{qr_3}.a_G,\\
 e_{r_22}.a_T,\,e_{r_33}.a_T,\,\delta_3(p).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta_3(p)$ is the inheritance weight of the $p\to r_3$ edge, while the $q\to r_3$ weight is $1-\delta_3(p)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}]
Differentiate the four displayed-tree monomials directly with respect to the columns in \eqref{eq:jac-cols}, evaluate at the witness, and use the row order \eqref{eq:jac-rows}.  Exact Gaussian elimination in $\mathbb{Q}(h)$, using the basis $1,h,h^2,h^3$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jac-det}
 \det J_*
 =\frac{h(10h^2+1)}{2^{61}3^4 5^{14}}.
\end{equation}
This is strictly positive on $2/3<h<7/10$.  Thus $F$ is a submersion at the witness.  By the submersion theorem, the fixed theta-trinet image contains an ordinary open neighborhood of the common point in the $15$-dimensional affine Fourier space $q_{AAA}=1$.  Intersecting that ambient neighborhood with the tree model gives a relatively open tree-model neighborhood contained in the network model.

The conclusion concerns openness in distribution space.  It does not say that a generic point of the higher-dimensional network parameter space maps to a tree distribution.  The full $15\times15$ matrix, the row and column metadata, and the exact determinant are stored in \texttt{jacobian\_certificate.json} and independently reconstructed by \texttt{verify.py}.
\end{proof}

\section{Moving into the strict continuous-time rate cone}\label{sec:ct-extension}

We retain the same fifteen pivot parameters as in \eqref{eq:jac-cols}.  Introduce a scalar $\varepsilon$ by changing the two free coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-ct}
  U_C\longmapsto U_C+\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  V_G\longmapsto V_G+\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Let $F(p,\varepsilon)$ denote the vector of fifteen nonconstant Fourier outputs after the free perturbation in \eqref{eq:free-ct}, where $p$ is the vector of fifteen pivot parameters.  At the closed-form witness $(p_0,0)$, the derivative $D_pF(p_0,0)$ is the invertible matrix $J_*$.  The real-analytic implicit-function theorem therefore supplies a unique analytic solution $p(\varepsilon)$ near $0$ such that
\[
  F(p(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=F(p_0,0)
\]
for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.
