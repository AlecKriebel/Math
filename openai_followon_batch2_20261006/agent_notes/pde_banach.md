## A. Endpoint nonlinear Maurey extension into general L1 spaces (#332)

**Recommended scope.** Prove a universal inequality

`e(X, L1(mu)) <= C M2(X)`

for every metric space X of Markov type two and every real L1 target, where e is the full-subset Lipschitz extension modulus. State explicitly the consequences `e(Lp, L1) <= C sqrt(p-1)` and `e(Sp, L1) <= C sqrt(p-1)` for 2 <= p < infinity (subject to exact known Markov-type constants), including non-atomic targets. Bundle the universal one-half-Hölder extension theorem: every L1-valued 1/2-Hölder map on any subset of any metric space extends with universal loss. The latter follows because the 1/2-snowflake has Markov type 2 with constant 1. Do NOT claim extension into arbitrary subspaces of L1: metric Markov cotype is not inherited by subspaces and counterexamples are known.

**Why it is opened now.** The source proves N2(ell1) <= 12 sqrt(21), but its stated extension corollary covers only Hilbert -> ell1. The full Markov-type source and non-atomic target package is absent from that paper. Mendel–Naor's existing extension theorem provides the central transfer; this is a newly unconditional endpoint family, not a new general extension mechanism.

**Mechanism and exact work.** First extend the finite metric-Markov-cotype inequality from ell1 to L1(mu): approximate any finite tuple by simple functions on a common finite partition; this is a weighted finite-dimensional ell1. Project outputs to that finite-dimensional ell1 if necessary. Pass to the limit using weak-star compactness in L1(mu)** and its canonical norm-one projection onto L1(mu). All relevant inequalities are finite sums of norms and pass by lower semicontinuity, then the projection fixes the input tuple. Alternatively adapt the source's explicitly coordinatewise median construction directly to integrable functions. Then apply Mendel–Naor Theorem 1.11 and its ultraproduct/retraction argument; the latter needs a retraction back to L1, since nonatomic L1 is not being asserted to be a dual space. Their discussion before Corollary 1.13 explicitly addresses targets complemented in their bidual. Check the usual general-measure formulation, or state first for sigma-finite measure spaces with the extension to general L1 via the support generated by the inputs. Verify p-dependence from the exact smoothness-to-Markov-type theorem before putting sqrt(p-1) in a headline.

**Assessment.** High tractability, impact about 8.0–8.2 for the complete endpoint package; little independent proof difficulty compared with #332, and attribution must make that clear. Priority competition is very high. The original Hilbert -> ell1 problem must not be represented as our new result. No full literature-priority clearance has been performed.

## B. Sharp one-third Brenier stability for log-concave nonuniform sources (#374)

**Recommended exact scope.** Compact convex K with nonempty interior in R^d, d >= 2; rho=r dx a probability with positive log-concave density on K and bounds 0<m<=r<=M<infinity; targets mu,nu supported in a fixed compact Y. Prove

`||T_mu-T_nu||_L2(rho) <= C(K,Y,M/m) W2(mu,nu)^(1/3)`.

Also prove the corresponding Lipschitz L2 stability of suitably normalized Brenier potentials. Uniform sharpness over this source class follows from the uniform-source subclass in #374. Start with r smooth positive up to the boundary; extend to bounded positive log-concave densities by approximation. Gaussian or other unbounded support, merely bounded non-log-concave density, and sharpness for EACH fixed nonuniform density are not part of the immediate claim.

**Exact mechanism checked against source.** This is NOT a consequence of equivalence of L2 norms: changing the source changes the optimal maps. In the source's midpoint coupling proof, let alpha=rho(U), beta=rho(V) for opposite perturbed convex cells, and use triangular quantile maps X,Z for the normalized restrictions of rho. The diagonal Jacobian products become `alpha/r(X)` and `beta/r(Z)`. For H=(X+Z)/2, arithmetic-geometric mean gives

`det(DH) >= sqrt(alpha beta)/sqrt(r(X) r(Z))`.

Log-concavity gives `r(H) >= sqrt(r(X)r(Z))`, hence

`r(H) det(DH) >= sqrt(alpha beta)`.

The exact same triangular substitution yields

`sqrt(alpha beta) Law(H) <= rho|P`

on the intermediate cell P. This has exactly the normalization needed in moments.tex. The stationary-site moment derivative estimate, with the same universal factor 162, then follows by the unchanged bounded quadratic function identity. Weighted face masses and moments retain graph-Laplacian symmetry and connectivity because r>0. The moving-site argument in finite_stability.tex is otherwise independent of uniform density. The gradient interpolation can be obtained from the original Lebesgue version by m/M comparison; unlike in the cell second-variation step, fixed density-ratio constants here cause no obstruction.

**Remaining checks.** Establish C1 weighted cell differentiation (first assume a continuous density) and retain uniform estimates in smoothing/approximation. Track normalization and constants carefully. The source's midpoint measure domination needs no arbitrary density-ratio loss; such a loss would remain zeroth order and invalidate the second-variation argument. Thus a blanket claim for all bounded densities cannot be advertised on this mechanism.

**Assessment.** Medium-high tractability (a concrete checked adaptation rather than an unsupported new inequality), impact about 7.7–8.0 in quantitative optimal transport. The strongest promise is target-uniformity, independent of atom counts, masses, separations and density regularity. Sharp exponent alone is not new for the uniform-source subclass.

## Independent audit of computation agent's two leading targets

Checkpoint: 2026-10-06 21:55 PDT. Assigned triage/audit completion estimate: 100%.

**Binary-weight hafnian FPRAS: reduction passes.** I independently checked the DAG gadget in `computation.md`. In the split graph, a full perfect matching corresponds to one directed source-to-terminal path and identity edges off that path: at every internal vertex, selecting an incoming nonidentity edge forces an outgoing nonidentity edge, and vice versa. Any additional nonidentity component would be a directed cycle, forbidden by acyclicity. Deleting both terminals therefore leaves only the identity matching. Deleting exactly one terminal gives an odd number of vertices and no perfect matching. In the global replacement graph, each edge gadget contains an even number of internal vertices, so exactly zero or two original endpoints are consumed inside it. Each original vertex is consumed once, giving precisely an original perfect matching with product multiplicity. The binary DAG recursion adds two vertices per bit, and direct source-to-new-terminal edges are distinct, so the graph stays simple. For rational inputs, the product of all denominators has bit length bounded by the sum of denominator bit lengths; resulting integer weights and the expanded graph have polynomial total size. Exact-zero support checks carry over. Approximate sampling can simply push forward an approximately uniform perfect matching of the expanded unweighted graph, since each original matching has exactly its integer product weight many lifts; total variation contracts under pushforward. No need for an additional difficult weighted Markov-chain argument.

**Exponential exact TSP SDP complexity: reduction passes.** Independently opened Rothvoss's primary paper https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2369; page 3 explicitly states Yannakakis's projection from a face of an O(n)-city TSP polytope to the n-vertex perfect-matching polytope, and page 4 gives its LP consequence. The single PSD-cone matrix dimension does not grow. Therefore the source's exponential matching PSD lower bound yields `2^{Omega(N)}` for exact symmetric TSP SDP lifts. A Held–Karp subset-state path-flow extended formulation yields the `2^{O(N)}` upper bound after embedding nonnegative variables diagonally in a PSD cone, if a tight exponential-order statement is desired.
