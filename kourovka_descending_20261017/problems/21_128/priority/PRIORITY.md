## Assessment

**Treat 21.128 as provisionally open. No complete positive or negative solution was located.** The current official Notebook retains the precise question, the proposer’s published paper explicitly leaves it undecided, and the two relevant September/October 2026 preprints establish rigidity of whole-group profinite completions rather than abstract commensurability. This conclusion rests on inspecting primary statements and relevant proofs; neither an absent solution star nor an aggregator status is used as sufficient evidence.

The exact target is whether the spherical Artin groups \(A[F_4]\) and \(A[H_4]\) possess isomorphic finite-index subgroups, with possibly different indices. The official source is the [21st Notebook, October 20 main file](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21tkt-3.pdf), printed p.198, problem 21.128, proposed by I. Soroko. The downloaded SHA-256 is `f133b4547caae29822fb65455b2115c4af075aa168983996a2aac1c2b21bc118`, matching the problem contract. The diagrams are chains with labels \(3,4,3\) and \(5,3,3\), respectively. A solution must construct the required subgroups or give an obstruction preserved under arbitrary passage to finite index.

## The two new finite-quotient papers do not settle the target

[Gavazzi–Haladjian–Paris, *Smallest quotients and profinite rigidity of irreducible spherical type Artin groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25940), v1 submitted September 22, 2026 at 09:45:38 UTC, was inspected beyond its abstract. Theorem 1.1 assumes spherical diagrams \(\Gamma,\Omega\), with \(\Gamma\) irreducible, and an isomorphism of **the full profinite completions** \(\widehat{A[\Gamma]}\cong\widehat{A[\Omega]}\); it concludes diagram isomorphism. The other diagram may be reducible. Corollary 1.3 is relative rigidity within spherical Artin groups. Theorem 1.5 gives S3 as the smallest nonabelian finite quotient for F4 and \(W[H_4]/Z(W[H_4])\) for H4. Section 4 and its product comparison concern these whole groups. The theorem list, proof scope, and text occurrences of “finite index” were checked; no theorem classifying arbitrary finite-index subgroups or virtual profinite equivalence was found. The full text has no occurrence of “commensur”.

[Hughes–Ng–Ragosta–Scherich–Verberne, *Finite quotients of spherical Artin groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27171), v2 revised October 16, 2026 at 14:52:40 UTC, has the same relevant scope boundary. Theorem C assumes irreducible spherical Artin groups and isomorphic full profinite completions, then concludes group isomorphism. Section 11 proves that statement. Its finite-index separability observation for a subgroup related to F4 is not a virtual classification. The authors acknowledge the concurrent independent Gavazzi–Haladjian–Paris paper; the latter does not supply the missing commensurability theorem.

There is a direct check against the tempting inference. [Cumplido–Paris, Theorem 2.4(1)/Lemma 6.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/39000?nt=1) gives commensurable, nonisomorphic \(A[A_n]\) and \(A[B_n]\), \(n\ge2\). The new rigidity theorems distinguish their full completions. Thus different full completions cannot generally obstruct abstract commensurability even within spherical Artin groups. F4 and H4 already have abelianizations \(\mathbb Z^2\) and \(\mathbb Z\): odd braid relations identify adjacent generators in abelianization, while an even relation imposes no equality. This whole-group distinction likewise is not a virtual obstruction.

## Equivalent terminology checked

The search included generalized braid groups, pure braid/Artin groups, complex reflection groups, reflection arrangements, projectivized complements, central quotients, and French commensurabilité variants. F4 is Shephard–Todd G28 and H4 is G30; see the [official Magma reflection-group handbook](https://docs.magma-maths.org/LieTheory/ReflectionGroups/create-group-refl-complex.html). Accordingly the same target can be searched as the commensurability of \(B(G_{28})\) and \(B(G_{30})\).

For a complexified finite real reflection group \(W\), with hyperplane complement \(V^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the generalized braid group is \(\pi_1(V^{\mathrm{reg}}/W)\), and its pure subgroup is \(\pi_1(V^{\mathrm{reg}})\). [Bessis, *Finite complex reflection arrangements are K(pi,1)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610777), v5 January 22, 2014, Definition 1.5 and §6, supplies the geometric language and the covering sequence. Its K(pi,1) result does not compare commensurability classes.

Passing to the pure subgroups does not change this commensurability question, since they have finite index. [Cumplido–Paris, Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 3.2](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/39000?nt=1) also identify the equivalent comparison of central quotients. Their proof identifies the projectivized pure-complement group with the pure group modulo its infinite cyclic center, and splits the pure group as that quotient times \(\mathbb Z\). These are equivalent formulations, not a solution.

A close false match is [Deraux, *A new non-arithmetic lattice in PU(3,1)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04463), v5 July 1, 2019, published Algebr. Geom. Topol. 20 (2020), 925–963. Section 3 and the CHL lattice tables discuss G28/G30 and commensurability, but §3 explicitly makes these holonomy lattices **quotients** of a projective braid group. Arithmeticity, trace fields, Euler characteristics, or commensurability of these representation images do not automatically determine commensurability of the source Artin groups. No injective finite-index identification that would bridge that gap was located.

## Exact remaining gap and audit limits

Neither an isomorphism between finite-index subgroups of the two target groups nor a separating invariant stable under arbitrary finite-index passage was located. Whole-group finite quotients, full profinite completions, ordinary first Betti numbers, finite Coxeter-group orders, and classifications of quotient lattices do not supply that missing step. This audit adds no proof toward either answer.
