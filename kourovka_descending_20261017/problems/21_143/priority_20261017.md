## Exact mathematical target

Let F be the orientation-preserving piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of [0,1] with finitely many breakpoints, all dyadic, and slopes integral powers of 2. Let X = {x0, x1, x0^-1, x1^-1} be its usual finite symmetric generating alphabet and π:X*→F the evaluation map. We use the right Cayley graph and right multipliers. Source generator conventions sometimes invert the pictured maps; all equations must be translated consistently if using a different convention.

An affirmative answer requires a regular language L⊆X* with π(L)=F and, for every a∈X∪{1}, a finite-state automaton accepting exactly

    R_a = {conv(u,v) : u,v∈L and π(u)a=π(v)}.

Here conv pads shorter inputs at the right with a new symbol. The a=1 relation is equality. Equality must be recognized or its regularity justified; uniqueness makes it the regular diagonal. Inverse multiplier composition is another valid derivation when its hypotheses have been checked. Equivalently, a regular surjective L must satisfy a uniform synchronous fellow-traveller bound:

    ∃K<∞ ∀u,v∈L, if d_X(π(u),π(v))≤1,
             then ∀n≥0, d_X(π(u[0:n]),π(v[0:n]))≤K,

with paths stationary after their ends. The quantifier is over all pairs in the chosen L satisfying the endpoint condition, and over all times and all group elements. Verifying bounded radii or one multiplier is insufficient. See [Kharlampovich–Khoussainov–Miasnikov, Definition 7.1 and Proposition 7.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.3645), and [Hauze, Chapter 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01965), which cites ECHLPT Theorem 2.3.5 for the geometric equivalence.

Automaticity is independent of the choice of finite generating set. Thus a correct structure over any finite generating set suffices; a general negative argument must exclude every candidate L (equivalently it may exclude every candidate over standard X using this invariance). Uniqueness and prefix closure are useful additional design choices, not assumptions licensed merely by the problem statement. Automatic groups admit suitable unique, uniformly quasigeodesic representatives; those representatives need not have bounded **additive** length excess above geodesics. Choosing shortest words *inside an existing automatic language* is not the same as proving the global shortlex geodesic language automatic.

## Nearby notions: exact distinctions and checked status

| Notion | Extra or altered requirement | What is verified for standard F here |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary synchronous automatic | The preceding finite-state right multipliers on actual generator words. | Open in current official/proposer lists. |
| Biautomatic | In addition require the left relations π(a)π(u)=π(v) to be synchronously regular for the same structure. | No resolution established in this audit; do not infer a negative answer from F containing an infinite-rank abelian subgroup. |
| Geodesically automatic over standard X | Require representatives that are geodesics, in addition to automaticity. | Excluded. Stronger additive-near-geodesic restriction below also applies. This is a generating-set-qualified conclusion. |
| Regular geodesic representative language over standard X | Regular geodesic words covering all of F, even without multiplier conditions. | Excluded by Cleary–Elder–Taback (2006), Theorem 30 and its application to F. The full geodesic language is also nonregular (Theorem 29). |
| Synchronous combing by geodesics over X | Choose geodesic paths with a uniform synchronous neighbour fellow-traveller bound, no regularity assumed. | Excluded by Cleary–Taback, *Seesaw words*, Theorem 4.2. |
| Synchronous or asynchronous combing without geodesic restriction | Retain the relevant neighbour fellow-traveller condition but drop regularity; asynchronous paths can be reparameterized. | No complete current resolution certified by this audit. CHST's checked 2010 text explicitly leaves asynchronous combability open. Their tame combing is not such a conclusion. |
| Asynchronously automatic | Finite-state recognition with asynchronous reading / the corresponding reparameterized fellow-traveller condition. | No resolution certified here. It is a different target; ordinary automaticity is stronger. |
| Tame 1-combing | A continuous extension through Cayley edges with a controlled radial no-return condition, not uniform closeness between paths to neighbours. | Yes: CHST Theorem 5.4; see precise constants below. |
| Regular normal forms | Only require a regular surjective or bijective generator-word representative language. | Yes: Guba–Sapir; no multiplier conclusion follows. |
| Autostackable | Bounded synchronously regular convergent **prefix** rewriting, or the equivalent regular bounded flow function. | Yes: Corwin–Golan–Hermiller–Johnson–Šunić Theorem 1.1. |
| Graph/Cayley automatic | Replace evaluation of generator words by an arbitrary bijection from a regular language over a finite **symbol** alphabet; edge relations remain regular. | No full yes/no conclusion certified here. Two particular encodings have nonregular multiplier relations. |
| Deterministic non-blind 1-counter graph automatic | Allow a finite-state machine with an unbounded integer counter (and zero tests) for the graph multiplier languages. | Yes: Elder–Taback Theorem 3.6. A counter is unbounded memory; this is not ordinary or graph automaticity. |
| FA-presentable group multiplication | Require the full ternary multiplication relation on codes to be automatic, as a first-order algebraic structure. | No for F: finitely generated FA-presentable groups are virtually abelian, while F is not. This is **not** the meaning of the Kourovka question. |
| Automaton/finite-state homeomorphism group representations | Represent individual transformations by transducers or Mealy automata. | Such terminology concerns an action/encoding, not the automatic normal-form structure asked for here; it supplies no answer. |

The graph/ordinary/FA distinctions are explicitly made in [KKM, §§1, 7, 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.3645). The FA-presentability conclusion uses their virtually-abelian characterization, not the false inference that ordinary automatic groups must be virtually abelian. For biautomaticity, [Mosher's *Central quotients of biautomatic groups*, Theorem D](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/42756?nt=1) explicitly discusses the unresolved infinite-rank-abelian-subgroup issue; [Papasoglu's lecture notes, problems 11–12](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/280-2009/hyplectures_papasoglu.pdf) even state the connection to the question whether Thompson F is biautomatic. These old sources do not prove its 2026 status, so that table entry is deliberately limited to what this audit establishes.

## Strongest useful inherited results and exact gaps

1. **Additive excess obstruction.** [Hauze, 2017 dissertation / 2018 arXiv, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01965), printed p.3 and repeated p.15: over standard X, any language containing a representative w of every g with |w|≤|g|_X+c for one fixed c cannot be contained in an automatic-structure language. Equivalently, in any hypothetical automatic L over X,

       sup_g min { |w|-|g|_X : w∈L, π(w)=g } = ∞.

   This excludes geodesic and uniformly additive-near-geodesic proposals. It does not exclude multiplicatively quasigeodesic representatives. The source is a doctoral thesis, arXiv v1; its long proof has not been independently re-proved in this audit. Treat it as a published-in-repository theorem to be checked if a new argument depends on it, rather than claiming new verification of its whole proof.

2. **Regular prefix rewriting.** [Corwin et al., *Autostackability of Thompson's group F*, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11691), Journal of Algebra 545 (2020), 111–134, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.04.004): an explicit bounded synchronously regular convergent prefix-rewriting system over x=x0, y=x1 exists. Its irreducible language is the Guba–Sapir regular normal-form language; local replacements have lengths at most 5 and 10. This proves a well-founded bounded flow to a normal-form tree. The exact automaticity gap is global uniform synchronous fellow travelling / regular edge multipliers. A regular decision for *where a local prefix rewrite applies* is not a regular machine for *the entire normalized product*.

3. **Metric and tame combing control.** [Cleary–Hermiller–Stein–Taback, *Tame combing and almost convexity conditions*, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0279): Theorem 3.7 makes their nested-traversal generator-word paths (6,0)-quasigeodesics in the standard Cayley graph. Lemma 3.3 gives N(g)-2≤|g|_X≤4N(g), with N the number of carets in either tree of a reduced pair. Theorem 5.4 gives a tame 1-combing of the standard two-relator Cayley complex with radial tameness ρ(q)=4q+45. Their normal-form language is context-free and nonregular, as clarified in the Corwin paper. Uniform metric efficiency plus radial tameness does not furnish a uniform neighbour synchronous bound.

4. **One-counter graph structure and a proved encoding failure.** [Elder–Taback, Theorem 3.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.04313), Groups Complexity Cryptology 8(1) (2016), 21–33, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/gcc-2016-0001): the standard infinite normal form can be coded in a regular bijective language L∞ over {a,b,#}. Lemma 3.1 proves regularity, Proposition 3.3 bounds code length linearly by standard word length (their term “quasigeodesic” concerns code length, not generator paths). The x0^-1 multiplier is regular (Proposition 3.4); the x1^-1 multiplier needs one counter and is nonregular (Lemma 3.7). Thus this **particular encoding** is not graph automatic. A different code, or actual generator-word language, remains untouched. Their discussion of the tree encoding points to [Taback–Younes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.04315), not a stronger finite-state result.

5. **Geodesic obstruction and exact metric tools.** [Cleary–Taback, *Seesaw words in Thompson's group F*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0310466), Theorems 4.1–4.2, construct arbitrary swing and exclude a geodesic synchronous combing over standard X. [Cleary–Elder–Taback](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410616), Theorems 27–30, give infinite cone types and no regular geodesic representative language. [Belk–Brown, Theorem 4.2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305412) gives the exact forest-diagram formula |g|_X=ℓ0(g)+ℓ1(g). These supply falsifiable metric tests for proposed languages, not a theorem that every possible quasigeodesic regular combing fails.

6. **Almost convexity scope.** [Horak–Stein–Taback](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.1862) prove failure of minimal almost convexity for finite generating sets drawn from the standard infinite generators and containing x1, and failure of almost convexity for finite generating subsets of those generators. They do not quantify over all finite generating sets of arbitrary elements. Almost-convexity obstructions do not by themselves rule out a general nongeodesic automatic structure.

7. **Quadratic filling already known.** [Guba, *The Dehn function of Richard Thompson's group F is quadratic*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0211395), Inventiones mathematicae 163 (2006), 313–342, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-005-0462-z), establishes δ_F(n)≃n². Therefore proving a quadratic Dehn upper bound again does not resolve 21.143; trying to derive superquadratic ordinary Dehn growth would contradict the inherited theorem and needs a specific independently checked flaw in it.

8. **Historical “regular rewriting” terminology is unsafe without definitions.** [Guba–Sapir (1997), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700001038) prove regular normal forms. Their “regular” rewriting definition requires a finite alphabet and a regular set of left sides. It does not say the paired rewrite relation is synchronously regular in the later Corwin sense. The journal abstract's “regular terminating confluent presentation” must not be upgraded silently to a modern regular convergent rewriting system or to automaticity. Its bibliography also lists an old Gersten preprint entitled “Thompson's group F is not combable”; a bibliography title is not current evidence of that conclusion.
