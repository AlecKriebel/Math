## Exact target and finding

The hypothesis is

\[
\operatorname{vb}_2(G)=\sup_{[G:H]<\infty}\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2(H;\mathbb Q)<\infty
\]

for a hyperbolic virtually compact special group \(G\). The proposed conclusion is that \(G\) is virtually free or virtually a closed aspherical surface group. The bound is uniform over **all finite-index subgroups**, and the Betti numbers are ordinary, unnormalised rational dimensions.

**No primary-source full solution or counterexample to this exact statement was found in this bounded recheck.** The three principal arXiv records retain their October 17 versions. Two additional pertinent 2026 revisions, Ng's surface-subgroup paper and Linton's retract-embedding paper, were checked and do not supply the unrestricted conclusion. This is evidence about the searched sources, not a certificate that no solution exists.

## Additional restricted surface-subgroup input

[Andrew Ng, arXiv:2510.01876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01876), *Quasi-convex surface subgroups in some one-relator groups with torsion*, has current **v2, June 3, 2026**. [Theorem 1 in v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01876v2) assumes a word \(w\) in a nonabelian finitely generated free group is neither primitive nor a proper power. It produces integers \(d,N>0\) such that every \(F_k/\langle\!\langle w^{dn}\rangle\!\rangle\), \(n\ge N\), contains a quasiconvex closed hyperbolic surface subgroup. The v2 proper-power exclusion must be retained when citing its exact statement. This is newly located in this audit, not new since October 17.

**Restricted deduction using the previously recorded amplification criterion:** enlarge \(N\) so \(dn\ge4\); Ng's proof of Corollary 2 records hyperbolicity and virtual compact specialness in this range. A finite-index surface subgroup gives the virtual-surface conclusion. Otherwise choose its orientable double cover \(S_o\) and a compact-special finite-index subgroup \(G_0\le G\). Then \(S_o\cap G_0\) is an orientable closed surface group with rational \(b_2=1\), quasiconvex and infinite index in \(G_0\). Amplification gives unbounded \(\operatorname{vb}_2(G_0)=\operatorname{vb}_2(G)\). This is a source-backed restricted application, not a new unrestricted theorem or priority claim.

## Linton's retract embedding: useful source and a novelty exclusion

[Marco Linton, arXiv:2510.10178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10178), *Embedding finitely generated free-by-cyclic groups in {finitely generated free}-by-cyclic groups*, has current **v2, April 22, 2026**. [Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10178v2) embeds a finitely generated free-by-\(\mathbb Z\) group as a retract in a group with a finitely generated free kernel; the ambient group is relatively hyperbolic with respect to the original group. Corollary 1.4 makes the ambient group hyperbolic and the embedding quasiconvex when the original group is hyperbolic. The paper's Lemma 4.4 already establishes malnormality of retracts of torsion-free hyperbolic groups. A recovered proof of that lemma is therefore exposition, not a novel contribution. The root identified the [IMRN 2026 journal record](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2026/4/rnag020/8494558); browser retrieval of that journal page failed, so the mathematical statements here were checked in the primary arXiv text.

The homology direction is decisive. If \(R\) is a retract of \(A\), then

\[
\operatorname{vb}_2(R)\leq\operatorname{vb}_2(A).
\]

Indeed, for each finite-index \(R'\le R\), the inverse image of \(R'\) under the retraction is finite index in \(A\) and retracts onto \(R'\), giving a split injection on rational \(H_2\). Thus unbounded virtual homology in the constructed ambient group does **not** imply unbounded virtual homology in the original retract. Boundedness in the original retract does not imply boundedness in the ambient group. The embedding alone therefore does not close 21.139.
