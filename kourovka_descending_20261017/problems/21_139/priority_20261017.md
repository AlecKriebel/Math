## Exact claim and present status

Write

\[
\operatorname{vb}_2(G)=\sup_{[G:H]<\infty}\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2(H;\mathbb Q).
\]

The target is the implication: if a hyperbolic virtually compact special group has finite \(\operatorname{vb}_2\), then it is virtually free or virtually the fundamental group of a closed aspherical surface. The hypotheses concern every finite-index subgroup and unnormalised rational dimensions.

## Closest homology classification: wider than the original title

The 2022 title *Characterising surface groups by their virtual second betti number* belongs to [arXiv:2209.14925v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14925v1). The current [v3, May 21, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14925v3), by Jonathan Fruchter and Ismael Morales, is titled *Virtual homology of limit groups and profinite rigidity of direct products*.

Its Theorem A = Theorem 3.5 classifies finite virtual second Betti number for **finitely generated residually free groups**, and also for **HGFC groups** (hyperbolic fundamental groups of finite graphs of finitely generated free groups with infinite cyclic edges). Finite values occur only for free, free abelian, and closed surface groups. For a hyperbolic group, the higher-rank free abelian alternatives are excluded. This establishes the desired conclusion for those subclasses and for groups with a finite-index subgroup in either class.

It does not state that every hyperbolic virtually compact special group belongs to either class. Its Question 5.5 explicitly asks a related extension to hyperbolic groups of cohomological dimension two. The current paper therefore cannot be treated as a full solution of 21.139. Its statements hold over fields more generally, but this audit's finite-index arguments below specifically use rational coefficients.

## Wilton's 2026 version: subgroup theorem, precise remaining bridge

[Wilton, arXiv:2406.02121v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02121v3), January 22, 2026, *Surface groups among cubulated hyperbolic and one-relator groups*, is the latest version on the checked arXiv record. Theorem A gives a **one-ended quasiconvex subgroup of infinite index** in a cubulated hyperbolic group unless the group is free or a surface group. Here “cubulated” means the fundamental group of a compact nonpositively curved cube complex.

Applied after passage to a compact-special finite-index subgroup, this supplies a nonfree quasiconvex subgroup. It does **not** assert that this subgroup has positive second rational homology, even after passage to finite index. Nonfreeness and one-endedness alone do not supply the homological class needed for the amplification mechanism. Consequently, combining this theorem with virtual retraction results still leaves a central gap. Calling that gap automatic would promote the desired conclusion without proof.

## Checkable deductions and restricted extensions

**Rational finite-index invariance.** If \(G_0\leq G\) has finite index, then \(\operatorname{vb}_2(G_0)=\operatorname{vb}_2(G)\). One inequality follows because every finite-index subgroup of \(G_0\) has finite index in \(G\). For the other, set \(K=H\cap G_0\) for any finite-index \(H\leq G\). The transfer \(H_2(H;\mathbb Q)\to H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) followed by the inclusion map is multiplication by \([H:K]\), so it is injective. Thus \(b_2(H)\leq b_2(K)\). This justifies passing to torsion-free compact-special groups, and applying restricted classifications to a finite-index subgroup. The virtual conclusion also passes upward by transitivity of finite index.

**Homological amplification criterion (standard-source deduction).** In a torsion-free hyperbolic virtually compact special group \(G\), suppose there is an infinite-index quasiconvex subgroup \(H\) with \(b_2(H)>0\). This forces \(G\) to be non-elementary. Arzhantseva's free-product extension, as recorded in Fruchter–Morales Theorem 3.2, gives a quasiconvex \(H*\mathbb Z\) in \(G\). The kernel of the map \(H*\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/m\) killing \(H\) has \(m\) conjugate copies of \(H\) as free factors and hence second Betti number \(m b_2(H)\). These are quasiconvex subgroups. Virtual retractions (or their finite-index form, followed by rational transfer) make their second homology inject into that of finite-index subgroups of \(G\). Therefore \(\operatorname{vb}_2(G)=\infty\). The same argument applies if a finite-index subgroup of \(H\) has positive \(b_2\).

**Additional restricted case, deduced rather than quoted as 21.139.** [Morales, arXiv:2211.12390v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12390v3), June 22, 2023, Proposition 9.4 supplies a quasiconvex closed hyperbolic surface subgroup for nonfree torsion-free hyperbolic groups in \(\mathcal H_{\rm ab}\). Definition 1.3 builds this class from finitely generated residually free groups using virtually abelian amalgams, HNN extensions, and finite-index overgroups. If that surface subgroup has finite index, the target conclusion holds; otherwise its orientable double cover has positive rational \(b_2\), and the preceding criterion proves infinite \(\operatorname{vb}_2\). This verifies the target within this torsion-free restricted hierarchy, without identifying it with the full virtually compact-special class.

**L2 reduction, not classification.** [Kielak–Linton, *Virtually Free-by-Cyclic Groups*, GAFA 2024, Theorem 1.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-024-00687-6) identifies hyperbolic virtually compact special groups with \(\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Q}\leq2\) and \(b_2^{(2)}=0\) as virtually free-by-cyclic. Bounded \(\operatorname{vb}_2\) implies \(b_2^{(2)}=0\) by Lück approximation. The cohomological dimension bound is an extra assumption, and “free-by-cyclic” here expressly allows an infinitely generated free kernel. It therefore supplies neither an Euler-characteristic-zero conclusion nor a surface classification. The independent latest-source notes check the related 2026 *Coherent RFRS groups* paper.
