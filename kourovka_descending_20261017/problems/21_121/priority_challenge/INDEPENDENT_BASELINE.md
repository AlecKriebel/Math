The official21st Notebook extracted text, cached independently under21_122,
states on new-problems page197:

* A group is p-Jordan when every finite subgroup has a **normal abelian
  subgroup of order coprime to p** whose index is bounded by a common
  `J*|Sylow_p|^e`.
* Its p-Jordan exponent is the **infimum** of admissible exponents, allowing
  the multiplicative constant J to depend on the proposed exponent.
* Part(a) asks whether the infimum is attained.
* Part(b) asks whether the infimum is at most3 for every p-Jordan group.
* The star and the cited September2026 counterexamples are attached to(a).
  The note does not mark(b) solved. The lack of a star is not, by itself,
  proof that(b) is currently open.

The underlying group is arbitrary and abstract; neither finite generation,
linearity, local finiteness, profiniteness nor finite-dimensional realization
is imposed. The introductory linear example is a restricted class, not a
universal embedding hypothesis.

For a finite group F define j_p(F) as the minimum index of a normal abelian
p'-subgroup (the identity is permitted). The condition quantifies over
**every finite subgroup** of the ambient group. For H<=F, intersecting a good
normal abelian p'-subgroup of F with H bounds the index by j_p(F), but it
does not replace |Sylow_p(F)| with |Sylow_p(H)|. Therefore a family of factor
groups with favorable whole-group estimates alone is not sufficient.

Part(b)'s infimum inequality does not say that the bound at exponent3 itself
is attained. Even if admissible exponents are known for every e>3, it proves
e(Gamma)<=3. Conversely, an exponent strictly bigger than3 on some selected
factor orders does not refute(b) unless a genuine p-Jordan ambient group and
all its hereditary finite-subgroup bounds are established.

The Notebook wording does not explicitly specify a positivity convention for
e; standard p-Jordan sources must be checked for that convention, especially
when Sylow-p orders are uniformly bounded. This does not affect the distinction
between(b)'s infimum and attainment or the cited linear exponent3 example.
