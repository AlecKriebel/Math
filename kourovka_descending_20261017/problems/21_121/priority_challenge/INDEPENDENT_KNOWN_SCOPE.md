## What the cited mathematical PDF says about(b)

Section4.3 explicitly leaves the general upper bound3 unresolved. It gives:

* exponent2 under a soluble-finite-subgroup hypothesis and a uniform Jordan
  bound on finite p'-subgroups;
* a related exponent2 bound when each finite subgroup has normal Sylow-p;
* a nonsoluble finite-support A5 product with exact attained 2-Jordan exponent
  `log60/log4`, lying strictly between2 and3;
* permanence of admissible exponent sets under the stated finite-index and
  bounded-quotient operations, and finite-kernel passage with an additional
  generalized p-Jordan hypothesis where required.

These statements retain conditions that do not apply automatically to an
arbitrary abstract p-Jordan group. Neither example exceeds3. Neither normal
Sylow-p nor uniformly bounded soluble index follows from the problem's
definition. The source identifies the notation H_(p) as a Sylow-p subgroup;
its order is the p-part of |H|.

## Degenerate exponent convention

The original text does not expressly restrict the sign of e. With real e, a
finite ambient group has all real exponents admissible after changing J, so
its infimum is -infinity. With e>=0 the infimum is0. This convention does not
produce an informative new counterexample to(a), already solved with positive
finite infima, and cannot refute(b). More generally, bounded Sylow-p orders in
an already p-Jordan group give a uniform bound on j_p and therefore no obstacle
to(b). The meaningful remaining case has unbounded Sylow-p orders.
