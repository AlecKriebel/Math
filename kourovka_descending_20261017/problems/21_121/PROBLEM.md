# Kourovka 21.121(b)

Authoritative source:21st Notebook, October20,2026 revision, printed/PDFp197,
C.Shramov. Full statement and convention trail:priority/SOURCE_CONTRACT.md.
Part(a) has a prior negative solution; it is excluded from original discovery.

Fix any prime p. An arbitrary abstract group Gamma is p-Jordan if there exist
uniform constants J>0 and a finite real exponent s such that every finite
H<=Gamma has a subgroup A normal in H, abelian, of order prime to p, with
[H:A]<=J*|H|_p^s. Here |H|_p is the order of a Sylow p-subgroup. Let E_p(Gamma)
be the admissible real exponents. For a global convention one may restrict to
nonnegative real exponents; this does not change the upper-bound question.

The remaining question is whether inf E_p(Gamma)<=3 for every p-Jordan Gamma.
An affirmative proof may show admissibility of3+epsilon for every epsilon>0;
it need not establish attainment at3. A counterexample needs one p-Jordan
Gamma with finite optimal exponent strictly larger than3 and a proof controlling
all its finite subgroups. The constants may depend on Gamma,p,epsilon but never
on the individual finite subgroup. Finite generation, topology, fixed linear
dimension, bounded rank, solubility or geometric origin are not hypotheses.
