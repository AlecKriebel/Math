# Preliminary priority audit: Kourovka 21.130

Audit cutoff: 24 October 2026. Audit completed locally; no outreach, submission, upload, or novelty claim. Status: **no complete resolution found; provisionally treat the exact problem as open, subject to the limitations below**. This is a literature-screening conclusion, not a proof that no solution exists.

## Exact claim and success standard

For every finite additive abelian group \(G\) with odd \(|G|\), and every \(A\subseteq G\) of size \(n>2\), a cyclic ordering \((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) of the distinct members of \(A\) exists for which \(a_i+a_{i+1}\), indices modulo \(n\), are all different. A complete positive resolution must cover arbitrary subsets and arbitrary odd-order abelian groups. A negative resolution needs an explicit eligible \((G,A)\) and a checkable impossibility proof. Whole-group, interval, random-set, bounded-size, and path-only results do not meet this standard.

The target is exactly Sun's Conjecture 3.3(i), dated 3 September 2013, in the primary [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1679). The latest listed version is v9, uploaded 2 March 2020; the journal reference is *Nanjing Univ. J. Math. Biquarterly* 36 (2019), 134–155. Its PDF still presents this part as a conjecture on PDF page 9. The PDF, rather than the experimental HTML conversion, controls the transcription. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1679v9)

## Equivalent formulations and false matches

These are direct deductions from the exact claim, not additional literature results:

* Colour the edges of the complete graph on vertex set \(A\) by \(c(\{x,y\})=x+y\). The conjecture is precisely existence of a **rainbow Hamiltonian cycle** in this particular induced sum colouring. Cancellation makes the colouring proper. A general rainbow-cycle statement must provide one spanning cycle on these prescribed vertices; disjoint cycles, a path, or nearly all vertices are insufficient.
* Equivalently, there must be a permutation \(\pi:A\to A\) that is one \(n\)-cycle and for which \(x\mapsto x+\pi(x)\) is injective. A transversal or Snevily matching allows multiple cycles and fixed points. For \(A=B\), \(\pi=\mathrm{id}\) already gives an injective diagonal via odd-order doubling, illustrating how far that condition is from requiring one cycle.
* Lev's term **rainbow-sum cycle** is an exact match when stated on the given subset \(A\). The phrase **harmonious subset** needs a definition check: Javaheri–de Wolf require the edge-sum set to equal \(A\), which imposes \(\sum A=0\) in the additive abelian case. The target does not impose this. For example, \(A=\{1,2,3\}\subset\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\) has distinct cyclic sums \(3,5,4\), but cannot be harmonious in that stronger sense because \(\sum A=6\ne0\).

**Decision:** no complete prior proof or counterexample was identified. The strongest corroborated scope is the longstanding whole-group case and special recent families; the exact unresolved gap remains arbitrary prescribed subsets of arbitrary finite odd-order abelian groups. The case is suitable for continued independent investigation on a preliminary-open basis, with a renewed priority check before claiming a solution.
