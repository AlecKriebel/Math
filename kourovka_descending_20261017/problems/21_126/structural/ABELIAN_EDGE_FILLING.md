# Structural lemma: intrinsic filling with abelian edge groups

Prepared October24,2026 UTC. Proof draft undergoing independent adversarial review. This is not a full21.126 solution and is not claimed original in the literature.

## General statement

Let J embed in a finitely generated group A, with a chosen word metric on A. Suppose J is the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with finitely presented vertex groups J_v of polynomial Dehn upper bounds and finitely generated free-abelian edge groups E_e. Suppose every E_e, through its embedded image in J≤A, satisfies

 |x|_{E_e} ≤ C_e τ_A(x),   τ_A(x)=lim_{m→∞}|x^m|_A/m.

The edge word length is for a fixed basis. It is enough that E_e is undistorted in A: apply |x^m|_E≤C|x^m|_A+C and |x^m|_E=m|x|_E, then divide by m. The displayed condition is invariant under conjugacy in A because stable word length is. Then J has a polynomially bounded Dehn function. If all vertex bounds have degree≤d, d≥2, the proof below gives the intentionally loose upper bound n^{2d+1}.

In a RAAG A every finitely generated abelian subgroup is undistorted (Flat Torus Theorem applied to the proper cocompact semisimple action on the universal cover of the Salvetti complex). Thus the edge condition holds for every free-abelian edge group of a subgroup J≤A.

## Finite graph-of-groups reduction for subgroups

Suppose A itself has a finite graph-of-groups splitting with finitely generated free-abelian edge groups, and H≤A is finitely presented. Let T be its Bass–Serre tree. If H fixes a vertex, H is a subgroup of a vertex group and one uses that vertex's assumed polynomial-subgroup filling theorem directly. Otherwise the minimal H-invariant subtree T_H has finite quotient: choose a vertex x and finite generators S of H; the union of the finitely many segments [x,sx] is finite, and the H-orbit of this union is an invariant subtree with finite quotient containing T_H. Removing extraneous edges cannot increase the number of edge orbits.

Each edge stabilizer of H is a subgroup of a conjugate of an ambient edge group, so is finitely generated free-abelian. Each vertex stabilizer is finitely generated as well. Here is a direct argument that avoids assuming subgroup finite generation for arbitrary vertex groups. In the finite quotient graph, express a finite generating set of H in graph-of-groups normal form; at each vertex take the subgroup generated by the finitely many vertex elements occurring in these normal forms and the finitely many incident edge-group generator images. The resulting graph of subgroups has the same edge groups. Its fundamental group injects into H by the normal-form theorem and contains the chosen generating set of H, hence equals H. Normal forms then imply that every vertex subgroup is the whole original vertex stabilizer.

The vertex stabilizers are finitely presented. Choose finite generating sets for them as above. Their unknown presentations yield an infinite graph-of-groups presentation for H with only finitely many generators and finitely many edge relations. Fix a finite presentation of H on this same finite generating set. Each of its finitely many relators has a finite proof in the graph presentation, using only finitely many vertex relations. Retain those vertex relations and also the finitely many relations needed to make the incident free-abelian edge-group maps well-defined. This defines finitely presented replacement vertex groups H'_v mapping onto H_v. Each original edge group still injects into H'_v, because its composite to H_v is injective. The resulting finite graph-of-groups fundamental group H' maps onto H, while all chosen relators of H hold in H', giving an inverse identity-on-generators homomorphism H→H'. Hence H'≅H. Normal-form injectivity at vertices now forces H'_v≅H_v. This proves finite presentation without treating ambient finite presentation as inherited by arbitrary subgroups.

## Area argument and tracking original intervals

Use a standard finite graph-of-groups presentation: finitely presented vertex groups, stable letters for oriented edges outside a maximal tree, and finitely many edge identifications. For normal-form reduction it is cleaner to retain formal oriented edge letters for **all** edges and use graph-path words in the graph-of-groups groupoid; maximal-tree paths have bounded length and convert an ordinary word of length n into a closed graph-path word with length O(n). These bounded changes affect only constants.

A null graph-path word can be reduced by successive pinches e u e^{-1}, where u is a word in the terminal vertex group and represents an element of the corresponding edge image. Replace u by an edge-basis word, apply the edge relations, and remove e,e^{-1}. At the end the word lies in a single vertex group and is null. There are at most O(n) pinches because no step introduces edge letters.

Keep a formal record that every replacement block represents an interval of the **original** cyclic boundary word. Initially each token has its own interval. When a pinch combines adjacent blocks, its replacement has their concatenated original interval. These intervals are nested or disjoint, and each such interval has original length O(n).

Fix maximal-tree base paths for the finitely many vertices. Map each vertex token and each formal oriented edge token to its based loop in J and then A; these token images have a common finite length bound L. This gives a multiplicative map from graph paths to J using the fixed endpoint base paths. Every replacement block is exactly equal in A to the product of its original contiguous interval. Keep blocks intact, with no free reduction across their boundaries, so the interval record is preserved. A pinch between consecutive inverse oriented edge tokens encloses only vertex tokens and replacement blocks and is a closed path at its initial vertex. Its edge-group output consequently equals the image of that original interval, after the fixed base-path identification. Its stable word length is at most its A-word length, hence at most L times the original interval length, which is O(n). Equivalently one can identify the edge element at the other endpoint and use conjugacy invariance of τ_A. The edge condition therefore bounds the chosen edge-basis word length by O(n), and both incident vertex images of that basis word have length O(n).

Therefore each pinch output has at most Cn vertex letters. There are at most Cn such outputs during the whole reduction, so at every stage the current vertex-word length is O(n²), even without using the stronger disjoint-interval length accounting. Fill the equality between u and its selected edge-image word inside the terminal vertex group using O((n²)^d) cells. Applying the O(n)-letter edge word uses O(n) edge-relation cells. Repeating O(n) times and filling the final null vertex word of length O(n²) gives O(n^{2d+1}) area. This proves the asserted polynomial upper bound. The interval invariant is needed to bound edge words by the original boundary length, rather than by a potentially inflated current word.

## Consequences being tested

If an ambient RAAG splits as a finite graph of groups along clique RAAGs, and every finitely presented subgroup of each vertex group has a polynomial Dehn upper bound, then the same is true for every finitely presented subgroup of the ambient RAAG. The preceding subgroup-reduction paragraph supplies finite presentation of vertex stabilizers and the RAAG action supplies edge undistortion. The degree may increase; no uniform degree independent of the decomposition is claimed.

A chordal graph has a finite graph-of-cliques decomposition (equivalently use a perfect elimination order); its RAAG is a finite tree of free-abelian groups. The lemma would therefore give a polynomial bound for every finitely generated subgroup of a chordal RAAG. A stronger CAT(0) conclusion is already established by Casals-Ruiz–Duncan–Kazachkov, *Limit groups over coherent right-angled Artin groups*, Publ. Mat.67(2023),199–257, DOI10.5565/PUBLMAT6712305, arXiv2009.01899v2. Actual Lemma9.5 gives geometric coherence of coherent/chordal RAAG Salvetti complexes; Definition9.2 and the following paragraph imply all finitely generated subgroups are CAT(0). The primary source was inspected, so the chordal consequence belongs to the prior literature.

The5-vertex house graph is a4-cycle with a roof vertex attached to the endpoints of one edge. Its RAAG splits as A(C4)*_{Z²}Z³. All finitely presented subgroups of A(C4)=F2×F2 have a quadratic upper bound (also follows from Huang–Osajda), and all subgroups of Z³ are free-abelian. The lemma gives a polynomial upper bound for every finitely presented subgroup of the house RAAG. It addresses a concrete leftover ambient graph but does not supply a witness or resolve arbitrary ambient graphs.

Independent adversarial review of the interval tracking and subgroup-finiteness arguments is recorded separately; no complete Kourovka resolution or literature originality claim follows from this partial lemma.
