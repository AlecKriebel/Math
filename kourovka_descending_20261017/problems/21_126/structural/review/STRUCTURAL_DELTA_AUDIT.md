## Cyclic fibre products

The displayed semidirect-product map is an isomorphism: the common integer height fixes k uniquely, and each coordinate has its unique N-part. Its multiplication uses the same automorphism in every coordinate. Fibre product of P_m and P_l over that same height is exactly P_(m+l); it does not create a second monodromy. This statement does not apply to an independently changed quotient or a different extension operation.

The polynomial Dehn upper bound for N^m follows by O(n²) cross-factor commuting and factor fillings. The mapping-torus presentation presents the stated semidirect product because φ is an automorphism. Including inverse conjugation rules as finitely many redundant relators is legitimate; the constants translating those cells back to the original finite presentation are finite. Collection moves t letters across expanded N words at an exponential bound and leaves a null N^m word of exponentially bounded length. A polynomial filling of that word therefore still has exponential cost. No bound on extrinsic distortion is being substituted for intrinsic filling.

For the refined polynomial statement I specifically tested the dangerous distinction between lengths of reduced iterates and raw substitution size. The proof correctly uses shortest representatives V_(h,x) and includes an N-filling every time a raw substitution is replaced by the chosen representative. Both words in that equality lie in the same N factor, the boundary length is O((n+1)^r), and the assumed degree-d Dehn upper bound controls its area by O((n+1)^(rd)). At most n height steps per original letter and n original letters keep the total polynomial. Existence of shortest representatives is sufficient for an isoperimetric upper bound; no effective procedure for finding them is asserted. Forward and inverse growth are both assumed, which is essential. Negative heights use inverse rules. Thus the proof does not assume reduced-word growth bounds syntactic expansion cost for free.

Neither the exponential upper bound alone nor large monodromy gives the required intermediate branch. The note explicitly retains this gap.

## Forest and closure theorem

Tran2017 Theorem6.3 and Definition6.1 (printed pp2508–2509) explicitly provide a compact orientable graph manifold for every finite tree with at least three vertices. Z, Z², the empty graph, and free products through connected sums handle the remaining finite forests. The interior has the same fundamental group. The covering with group H is orientable and has finitely generated fundamental group, so Scott's compact-core theorem supplies a compact orientable core whose fundamental group is H. This is a use of the established compact-core theorem, not an assumption that the original cover is compact or tame in some stronger sense.

The Nil/Sol exclusion is valid. Prime summand groups inject as free factors; a Nil or Sol prime has a nonabelian solvable finite-index subgroup. Baudisch's theorem for pairs of elements in a RAAG precludes such a subgroup: two noncommuting elements would generate a nonsolvable F2. Thus neither exceptional prime geometry can occur in the compact core. The cached Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton2015 Corollary4.7, printed p219, explicitly supplies a quadratic inequality under exactly the orientability and no-Nil/no-Sol-prime assumptions used. Compact-core finite presentation and this argument concern intrinsic area, not automatic inheritance of an ambient Dehn bound.

Scott and Baudisch are standard external theorem inputs. Their original PDFs were not obtained by the author, as the note explicitly discloses. I do not claim to have independently reconstructed their proofs or to have inspected those inaccessible originals. The current review confirms the application of their stated standard scopes. This access limitation is retained rather than silently converted to a claim of complete source reproduction.

For the closure lemma, a finitely generated Kurosh decomposition has finitely many nontrivial factors and a finitely generated free part. Each factor of the finitely presented H is a retract, hence finitely presented. The free-product filling bound follows by decomposing into factor null words with total length at most the original word length; d≥2 permits a common n^d upper bound.

The product-with-Z argument correctly handles the nontrivial vertical intersection. If H∩Z=mZ, a lift of k determines a well-defined θ(k) modulo m. Conversely every pair with that congruence lies in H by correcting a chosen lift with an element of mZ. Thus H is exactly the index-m kernel in K×Z of (k,z)↦z−θ(k), not merely contained in an index-m group. Finite presentation passes first to K×Z by finite extension and then to K by retraction. Sorting factors gives δ_(K×Z) ≼ δ_K+n². If the vertical intersection is trivial, projection is an isomorphism and avoids these steps.

## Independent census reproduction
