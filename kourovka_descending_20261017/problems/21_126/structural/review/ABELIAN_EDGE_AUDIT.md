## Exact mathematical claim verified

Let J be a finite graph-of-groups fundamental group with finitely presented vertex groups having degree-at-most-d polynomial Dehn upper bounds, d≥2, and finitely generated free-abelian edge groups. Suppose J embeds in a finitely generated ambient group A and, for each edge group in that embedding, its basis length satisfies |x|_E≤C_E τ_A(x). Then δ_J(n)≼n^(2d+1).

The condition is genuinely stronger than an arbitrary abstract graph-of-groups description with abelian edges. It prohibits distorted edge groups in the chosen ambient metric. Undistortion of a free-abelian edge group implies the displayed stable bound by applying it to x^m, using |x^m|_E=m|x|_E, and dividing by m. Additive constants disappear. Stable word length exists by subadditivity and is conjugacy invariant. These facts do not assert additivity of stable word length on a nonabelian group.

## Independent verification of the central interval invariant

Choose a maximal tree in the finite quotient graph and a base vertex. The standard graph-of-groups presentation embeds each vertex group into J by its fixed tree path. Assign each oriented formal graph edge its corresponding element of J (tree edges map to1 in this standard presentation, and inverse edges map to inverses). Applying the embedding J→A then gives a fixed A-element for every vertex generator and every formal edge token. These assignments satisfy all edge-conjugation relations. There is a finite constant L bounding the A-word length of every such token image.

An ordinary word of length n becomes a closed graph-path word of length at most Cn by inserting the fixed tree paths. Do not free-reduce its vertex letters across recorded block boundaries. Initially every token records its singleton interval of the original graph-path word. At each pinch, take consecutive mutually inverse edge tokens e,e⁻¹, with their entire intervening vertex-word block u. If u represents an edge element, replace e u e⁻¹ by its edge-image vertex word and record the whole concatenated original interval as one new block. No edge tokens occur inside that new block. In a later pinch, a block is therefore consumed whole; no operation needs to cut it. This maintains disjoint current intervals and nested or disjoint historical intervals. The sum of original interval lengths along the current word remains the original length.

The product in A of a replacement block equals the product in A of its original interval, because each performed relation holds in J and hence A. For the interior vertex word u, the concatenation of its current intervals is likewise one original contiguous interval (it is the full word between its two edge tokens). Thus the A-element represented by u has an A-word representative consisting of at most Cn original tokens, of length at most LCn. Identifying u with the appropriate edge group may conjugate it by one of the fixed base-path/edge choices; stable word length is unchanged. In fact the fixed maximal-tree presentation makes the relevant equality directly in A, so a bound involving the possibly long variable conjugator is unnecessary.

Consequently every edge element extracted at every reduction step has basis length O(n). This assertion is about original token intervals, not the already expanded vertex strings. It remains true after deeply nested reductions. Standard graph-of-groups normal form ensures some pinch exists while a null graph-path word has edges; a null path eventually reduces to a null vertex word. Noninverse consecutive edges that do not pinch are not removed by the argument. Each successful pinch deletes two edge tokens and introduces none, giving O(n) steps.

At most O(n) original vertex letters and O(n) replacement blocks occur at any time, each replacement having at most O(n) vertex letters. Hence a current vertex word has length O(n²), even without optimizing the stronger interval estimate. Replacing u by an edge word costs at most O(n^(2d)) vertex-relator cells, since its difference word is null in the relevant vertex group and has length O(n²). Moving that basis word across the edge costs O(n) edge cells. Summing O(n) steps and the final vertex filling gives O(n^(2d+1)). This constructs an actual intrinsic diagram in a fixed finite presentation of J; no numerical distortion bound is substituted for an area lower or upper bound.

## Boundary cases and attempted countermodels

Trivial edge groups cause no problem: an empty edge word is substituted after filling u in its vertex group. Vertex loops, non-tree edges, multiple edges, and separating tree edges use the same groupoid invariant. The final graph path is based at its original vertex; cyclic rotation is unnecessary. Reversing an edge only switches the incident edge images and basis correspondence, changing constants. Finite presentation and finitely many vertices/edges allow one maximum d and finitely many metric constants. Nothing here allows infinitely many edge orbits or degrees unbounded within one decomposition.

Baumslag–Solitar and snowflake-type compression cannot invalidate the proof while satisfying its hypothesis: compression of a fixed edge element would violate the required stable basis-length bound. Free-edge hydra constructions are outside the free-abelian-edge hypothesis. The proof is compatible with a polynomial bound of larger degree than known sharp results; it asserts no lower bound.

## Finite subgroup graph and finiteness of vertex stabilizers

For finitely generated H acting on a tree without inversions, the translates of finitely many connecting generator segments form a connected invariant subtree with finitely many edge orbits. If H does not fix a vertex, its minimal subtree is contained in this one; passing to it cannot create new edge orbits. Thus the quotient graph is finite. If it fixes a vertex, the reduction stops at that ambient vertex and requires no graph argument.

With finitely generated incident edge groups, the normal forms of a finite generating set of H use finitely many vertex elements. Include those and all incident edge-generator images to form finite generating sets for candidate vertex subgroups. The graph of these subgroups has unchanged edge groups and injects into the original graph of groups: any image that belongs to an edge group was already in the unchanged edge subgroup, so reducedness is preserved. It contains the chosen generating set of H, so its image is all of H. Intersection with an original vertex group, using normal forms, is exactly the candidate vertex subgroup. This proves finite generation of every vertex stabilizer without assuming coherence of the ambient vertices.

For finite presentation, choose a finite presentation of H on the finite union of those generators and stable letters. Each of its finitely many relators has a finite proof using the original, possibly infinite, vertex presentations and the finitely many edge relations. Retain just the finitely many vertex relators used in these proofs, plus finitely many relations making all free-abelian edge maps well-defined. The latter are finite because every edge group is finitely presented. Each abstract edge map into a replacement vertex group is injective: its composite into the actual vertex stabilizer is the original injective edge map. Thus a genuine finite graph of finitely presented replacement vertex groups exists. Its fundamental group satisfies all chosen H relators and maps onto H; the two identity-on-generators maps are inverse. Vertex normal-form injectivity then forces each replacement vertex group to coincide with its actual stabilizer. This proves the claimed finite-presentation passage. No finite presentation of an arbitrary subgroup is inherited merely from ambient finite presentation.

## RAAG application and house graph

A finitely generated free-abelian subgroup of a finite-graph RAAG is undistorted: its geometric Salvetti CAT(0) action is proper and semisimple, and the flat-torus theorem gives an isometrically embedded invariant flat on which its lattice acts by translations. Comparing that orbit metric with its basis norm and the ambient word metric supplies the required stable bound. Conjugate edge stabilizers also satisfy it, with their own finite constants.

The house graph is the union of a four-cycle and a triangle along a common edge, so the asserted RAAG amalgam A(C4)*_(Z²) Z³ is exact. For a finitely presented subgroup H, the preceding reductions give a finite graph of finitely presented vertex stabilizers inside conjugates of those two factors and free-abelian edge stabilizers. The already checked Huang–Osajda result gives quadratic upper bounds in A(C4); finitely generated subgroups of Z³ are free abelian and have quadratic upper bounds. Thus d=2 yields the conservative bound δ_H≼n^5. The vertex-fixing case already has a quadratic bound. This closes both requested rate branches in this particular ambient RAAG only.

For chordal ambient RAAGs the same argument is consistent: their clique decomposition has abelian vertices. Finite generation of H alone gives finitely generated, hence finitely presented abelian vertex stabilizers, and the finite graph presentation then makes H finitely presented before applying the filling bound. The candidate rightly flags that stronger known coherent-RAAG results may subsume this consequence.
