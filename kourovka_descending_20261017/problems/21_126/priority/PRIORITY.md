The standard Bestvina–Brady distinction is especially important: the 2021 questions sheet explicitly notes that Brady–Soroko’s high-degree examples can lie **inside** a Bestvina–Brady kernel whose own Dehn function is at most quartic. Intrinsic filling growth need not pass to subgroups as an upper bound. Thus the 2026 kernel classification does not close the general problem.

## Recent distortion results and hydra examples

[Goswami–Verma, *Superexponential distortion of free groups in virtually special Groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18223), v1 August 25, 2025, was read beyond the abstract. Theorems 1.1/1.2 give virtually compact special groups containing finite-rank free subgroups with distortion exp^k(x^m), and distortion beyond every fixed exponential iteration. Their intrinsic subgroup Dehn functions are in the linear coarse class, since the subgroups are free. The paper does not give finitely presented RAAG subgroups with those **Dehn functions**. Its §8 also separates ordinary hydra from hyperbolic hydra and notes that general virtual specialness of ordinary hydra remains open. Its open questions concern distortion. No later arXiv version was listed at the audit date.

[Dani–Riley, *Fractional distortion in hyperbolic groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08645), v2 June 21, 2025, Theorem A, constructs free subgroups with distortion exp^k(n^{p/q}); the k=1 ambient examples can be virtually special. Corollary C constructs doubles and centralizing HNN extensions with matching large intrinsic Dehn functions. This is a real Dehn-function result, but no injection of those doubles/extensions into a RAAG is claimed. Virtual specialness of the input group does not by itself establish the required embedding of a newly formed double. This is the exact unfilled scope step; no original attempt to fill it was undertaken here.

[Dison–Riley, *Hydra groups*](https://doi.org/10.4171/CMH/294), Comment. Math. Helv. 88 (2013), 507–540, gives CAT(0) hydra groups with free subgroups of Ackermannian **distortion**. Section9 obtains Ackermannian **Dehn functions** for different HNN extensions Γ_k. [Pueschel, *Hydra group doubles are not residually finite*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02554), v2 July 30, 2015, Theorems 1.1/1.2, proves these Γ_k and the corresponding doubles non-residually finite for k>1. They cannot be RAAG subgroups, because RAAGs and their subgroups are residually finite. An ambient hydra’s embedding, even if verified in a particular rank, cannot remove that obstruction for its double.

[Brady–Dison–Riley, *Hyperbolic hydra*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1535), v1 May 8, 2011, likewise produces huge distortion of finite-rank free subgroups, not huge intrinsic subgroup Dehn functions. The 2025 paper explicitly observes virtual specialness using hyperbolic free-by-cyclic cubulation; that update changes the ambient embedding status, not the intrinsic Dehn function of the free subgroup. Rips-style constructions with merely finitely generated kernels must also not be substituted for finitely presented target groups; Dani–Riley’s introduction explicitly discusses that finiteness obstruction.

## Remaining gap and limits

For the super-exponential branch, the missing combination is a verified RAAG embedding for a finitely presented group with an intrinsic Dehn lower bound not dominated by any exponential. Existing huge-distortion free subgroups and abstract doubles supply different parts of this requirement, and the inspected sources do not join them into the requested witness. The intermediate branch needs both a subexponential upper bound and proof that no polynomial bounds the intrinsic Dehn function. No such RAAG witness was located. Nonintegral powers and a polynomial times a logarithm are polynomially bounded and do not answer that strong interpretation.
