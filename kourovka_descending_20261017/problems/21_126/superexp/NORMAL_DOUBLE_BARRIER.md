# Normal doubles: a verified embedding mechanism and its exponential ceiling

Status: independently derived elementary reductions; a fresh adversarial proof audit passed. See review/NORMAL_DOUBLE_AUDIT.md and the reviewed-input receipt for scope and exact versions. These are not a solution of Kourovka 21.126 or a novelty claim. The target is an intrinsic Dehn function not coarsely bounded by any exponential.

## 1. Normal doubles inject into a product

Let G be any group and N a normal subgroup; write Q=G/N. Identify the two copies of N in D=G *_N G by the identity. The fold map f:D→G is the identity on each copy. The quotient map p:D→Q*Q is the quotient G→Q on each corresponding factor. Then

    (f,p):D → G × (Q*Q)

is injective. Indeed, N is normal in D because it is normal in both generating vertex groups. Quotienting D by this subgroup gives Q*Q, by the presentation or universal property of an amalgam. Thus ker(p)=N, and f restricts to the identity on N, so ker(f)∩ker(p)=1.

If G and Q embed into RAAGs A(Γ) and A(Σ), then Q*Q embeds into the RAAG on two disjoint copies of Σ, and the product embeds into the RAAG whose defining graph is the join of Γ with those two copies. This establishes an actual RAAG injection of D; it does not merely establish residual finiteness.

If G is finitely presented and N is finitely generated, D is finitely presented: take two finite presentations of G and add the finitely many relations identifying the chosen generators of N. Finite presentation of N is unnecessary.

This generalizes the familiar cyclic-quotient Bieri embedding used by Brady–Soroko, §7, author copy August 28, 2020. The argument here is given in full to avoid relying on a split extension.

## 2. An elementary quotient-word reduction fact

For a RAAG Q on a finite graph, a null word of length m in the standard generators can be reduced to the empty word by free cancellations and swaps of adjacent commuting signed letters, while never increasing the length and using at most m² swaps.

One proof uses the standard graph-product normal form: a nonempty trivial word has two inverse letters with every intervening letter commuting with their generator. Move one inverse letter through the intervening block and cancel the pair. Repeat. There are at most m/2 pairs, and every pair requires at most m swaps. The deliberately loose m² bound is sufficient. The same statement holds for Q*Q, since this is the RAAG on the disjoint union of two copies of the defining graph.

No Dehn-area lower bound is asserted by this reduction algorithm. Its relevant extra feature is that every conjugating prefix during the chosen reduction has length at most m.

## 3. Finitely generated normal kernels over RAAG quotients have at most exponential distortion

Suppose G is finitely generated, N◁G is finitely generated by a finite symmetric set Y, and Q=G/N is a finite-graph RAAG. Choose a lift t_v∈G of each standard generator v of Q and include their inverses in T. The finite set Y∪T generates G. Let L≥2 bound the Y-word lengths of all conjugates t y t⁻¹, with t∈T and y∈Y. Such a bound exists because N is normal and Y,T are finite.

For each signed commuting pair t,u corresponding to commuting distinct generators of Q, choose a Y-word c(t,u) representing [t,u]=tut⁻¹u⁻¹ in G. Let M≥1 bound the lengths of these finitely many words. Empty families cause no problem: put M=1.

Take a word w of length n on Y∪T representing h∈N. Move its Y letters to the left, using t y = (t y t⁻¹)t. Each original Y letter crosses at most n T letters and is replaced by a Y-word of length at most Lⁿ. Consequently

    w = W₀(Y) V(T),   |W₀|≤nLⁿ,   |V|≤n.

Since h∈N, V projects to the identity in Q. Reduce that projected word by the procedure of §2. Whenever adjacent t,u are swapped, use

    tu = c(t,u)ut.

The inserted Y-word can be pushed to the left across the preceding T prefix, whose length is at most n. Its resulting length is at most MLⁿ. Free cancellations insert nothing. With at most n² swaps, the resulting Y-word for h has length at most

    (n+Mn²)Lⁿ.

Thus Dist_N^G(n) is at most exponential. This argument permits non-split extensions, non-free N, and N with no finite presentation. It needs no assumed Dehn bound on G. A finite graph suffices whenever Q is finitely generated, by finite support.

The bound is presentation-invariant up to the ordinary distortion equivalence. In particular, quadratic *area* of the quotient must not be fed into a crude exp(area) estimate and then misread as evidence for exp(n²) distortion: the length-nonincreasing reduction gives a linear prefix bound.

## 4. Intrinsic Dehn upper bound for the double

Suppose now G is finitely presented, N◁G finitely generated, and Q=G/N is a finite-graph RAAG. There is C≥1 such that

    δ_D(n) ≤ C exp(Cn) + C δ_G(exp(Cn))

for suitable choices of finite presentations and all sufficiently large n. Hence if G has a polynomial isoperimetric function, then D has an at-most-exponential isoperimetric function. This conclusion concerns the intrinsic Dehn function of D, not just distortion of N.

Proof. Present G on the finite generating set Y∪T used above. By adding finitely many consequences to a finite presentation, include all the conjugation relations tyt⁻¹=φ_t(y) and the signed commuting-pair relations [t,u]=c(t,u). The chosen Y-words have lengths bounded by L and M. These are genuine relations of G, so adding them does not change G and their finite addition changes Dehn function only within coarse equivalence.

Present D on common generators Y and two copies T₁,T₂ of T, with two copies of these finite G relators. The quotient D/N is the RAAG Q*Q. In particular all necessary quotient commuting-pair relations occur *within* one of the two copies; there is no illicit relation commuting a first-copy generator with a second-copy generator.

Let w be a null D-word of length n. The construction of §3, now for the T₁∪T₂ letters, rewrites w to a Y-word W with

    |W|≤(n+Mn²)Lⁿ.

Each elementary pushing substitution is one conjugation-relator cell, after free reductions. A word of Y-length k pushed across a T-prefix of length at most n takes at most k(1+L+⋯+Lⁿ) cells. There are at most n original Y letters and n² inserted Y-blocks of initial length at most M. Thus the total rewriting area is bounded by a polynomial in n times L^{n+1}, and therefore by C exp(Cn). Signed adjacent swaps use the corresponding signed pair relation, which has been included explicitly.

The fold D→G is the identity on Y. Since W=1 in D, it follows that W=1 in G. Fill W using the first-copy G presentation, at area at most δ_G((n+Mn²)Lⁿ). This proves the displayed bound after enlarging C. If δ_G(m)≤Kmᵈ+Km, then polynomial(exp(Cn)) is again an ordinary exponential; no exponential iteration appears.

## 5. Consequence for the attempted Rips route

A proposed construction was to take a virtually compact special hyperbolic Rips group G mapping onto a RAAG Q, with finitely generated kernel N, and then double along N. The injection in §1 would establish a virtual RAAG embedding after suitable finite-index passage. But the actual intrinsic Dehn upper bound in §4 already rules out a super-exponential result for a polynomial-Dehn G: the double is at most exponential. The same applies to any compact special G, since its CAT(0) cubical action gives a quadratic isoperimetric inequality. For virtual RAAG embeddings, first use the proved injection D→G×(Q*Q), then take its inverse image of a finite-index subgroup of this ambient product that embeds in a RAAG. This is a finite-index subgroup of D and preserves its finite presentation and Dehn equivalence class. No claim is made that replacing the two vertex groups separately by one finite-index G₀ gives an amalgam of finite index in D; that generally does not follow.

This is a route obstruction, not a general exponential upper bound for all finitely presented RAAG subgroups. If Q is only an arbitrary finitely presented RAAG subgroup, rather than itself a RAAG with the specified reduction property, §2 cannot simply be assumed in its intrinsic generators. If G already has exponential Dehn function, §4 permits a double-exponential upper bound and supplies no matching lower bound. Either change requires a new mechanism and is not a solution obtained here.
