# Parabolic-intersection doubles: injection and a canonical-embedding obstruction

Status: independently derived construction/filter; a fresh adversarial proof audit passed. See review/PARABOLIC_DOUBLE_AUDIT.md and the reviewed-input receipt for scope and exact versions. This does not resolve 21.126 and is not claimed original literature priority.

Let A=A(Γ) be a finite-graph RAAG and P=A(Λ) the standard full subgroup on an induced subgraph. Let G≤A and H=G∩P. Use two copies of Γ, identifying precisely the vertices of Λ and keeping all edges from the two copies. The RAAG B on this graph is naturally A *_P A, by its defining presentation.

The homomorphism

    G *_H G → A *_P A = B

is injective. A reduced amalgam normal form in G *_H G has non-H interior factors. Under G→A these remain outside P, precisely because G∩P=H. Thus its image remains a reduced nontrivial normal form in A *_P A. The factors themselves inject. If G is finitely presented and H finitely generated, the source is finitely presented. Hence a highly distorted finitely generated intersection H in a suitable finitely presented G supplies a *concrete* RAAG-embedding mechanism, rather than a speculative combination theorem.

The exact remaining construction task is therefore to find a G≤A and finitely generated H=G∩P for which the resulting double has the required intrinsic super-exponential Dehn lower bound. Huge distortion of an arbitrary subgroup H≤G does not establish the exact intersection equality. Even after equality, a suitable intrinsic Dehn lower-bound proof is required.

## Canonical compact-special embeddings do not supply distorted intersections

Assume G acts cocompactly on a convex subcomplex Y of the standard CAT(0) cube complex X of A, and Y contains the identity vertex. Let Z⊂X be the convex standard P-subcomplex containing that vertex. Then H=G∩P acts cocompactly on Y∩Z. This implies H is finitely generated and undistorted in G.

Here is an elementary proof of cocompactness in this based case. There are constants R_G,R_P such that every vertex of Y is at distance at most R_G from G·1, and every vertex of Z is at distance at most R_P from P·1 (in fact one may take R_P=0 in the standard vertex-transitive P-subcomplex). For z∈Y∩Z choose g∈G,p∈P near z. Then g⁻¹p belongs to a fixed finite ball in A. For each difference r arising, fix one pair (g_r,p_r) with g_r⁻¹p_r=r. Equality g⁻¹p=g_r⁻¹p_r implies

    g g_r⁻¹ = p p_r⁻¹ ∈ H.

Thus z is at uniformly bounded distance from H·1. The intersection Y∩Z is convex and connected. Following its edge-geodesics and choosing nearby H orbit points shows H is generated by the finite set of elements moving 1 a bounded distance and its intrinsic word metric is bounded linearly by the distance in X. The reverse inequalities follow from finite generation. This proves the assertion, including trivial H and P.

This is a special based instance of a known convex-cocompact intersection property: Fioravanti, *On automorphisms and splittings of special groups*, arXiv:2108.13212v4 (January 16, 2023), Lemma 2.8 and Lemma 3.22(3); published Compositio Math. 159 (2023), 232–305, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X22007850. The source was read directly; no novelty is asserted for this fact.

For a compact special cube complex X_G with a based local isometry X_G→Salvetti(Γ), the universal cover is such a convex G-cocompact Y. Let Z_G be the component containing the basepoint of the subcomplex consisting of all Λ-labelled cubes. It is a finite locally convex subcomplex and π₁(Z_G)=G∩P, by path lifting and convexity.

Doubling X_G along Z_G maps by a local isometry to the Salvetti complex of B. To verify fullness of vertex links at a glued vertex, observe that links are the union of two full links, identified exactly on their Λ-labelled directions. Directions outside Λ from different copies are not adjacent in the defining graph of B; hence a clique in the image lies in one copy, or entirely in the common Λ part, and lifts to a simplex. At unglued vertices the same check uses a single copy. Injectivity on link vertices follows because every incident Λ-labelled edge at a vertex of Z_G lies in Z_G; no two distinct directions of the two copies map to the same direction unless they were glued.

Thus this double is itself compact special and has quadratic isoperimetric upper bound. This proves that the canonical compact-special embedding cannot turn a highly distorted free subgroup from a hydra construction into the required parabolic intersection: every such intersection in that embedding is undistorted, and its double remains CAT(0).

The restriction is on the *chosen embedding*. A group abstractly compact special may have a different, distorted embedding into another RAAG. The argument does not rule out that possibility, arbitrary finitely presented G, or super-exponential Dehn functions in general RAAG subgroups. Claiming any of these stronger conclusions would be an unsupported extension.

## Centralizer-extension variant

Adding a new vertex s commuting with every vertex of Λ produces the RAAG A *_P(P×⟨s⟩). The same normal-form argument gives

    ⟨G,s | [s,h]=1 for h∈H⟩ → A *_P(P×⟨s⟩)

injectively when H=G∩P. If H is finitely generated this is a finite presentation whenever G is finitely presented. The canonical compact-special embedding has the same convexity obstruction. An arbitrary huge-distortion subgroup not known to be such an intersection does not meet this injection criterion. General virtual specialness of the original G cannot replace that missing equality or supply the extension's virtual specialness automatically.
