# Exact finite-regular-module reduction for the Pell candidate

UTC: 2026-10-24T22:42:17.003837+00:00. Supporting deduction, not a solution. Discovery estimate **4%**. The final equivalent finite-quotient assertion below remains unsupported; the reformulation itself is not evidence that it holds.

Use the presentation and pairs in PELL_CANDIDATE.md. The following are equivalent:

1. There is a vertex-injective homomorphism from G into a finite-dimensional Euclidean-by-finite group.
2. Some finite group Q generated by h1,h2,h3 has a rational solution A,B,T in Q[Q] of

       L(hi)(pi A+T)=qi B+T, i=1,2,3,

   with A nonzero. Here L is the left regular permutation action.
3. For some such marked finite group of order N, the integer 3N-by-3N block matrix with row blocks

       [ pi L(hi), -qi I, L(hi)-I ]

   has rational rank at most 3N-2.
4. G has a homomorphism into Z^(N+1) semidirect Q for some such Q, whose restrictions to both displayed vertex groups are injective discrete lattice maps and whose finite quotient kills a,b,t.

5. There is a finite list of rational triples (Aj,Bj,Tj), not all Aj zero, such that for each i the two multisets

       { pi Aj+Tj : j=1,...,n }, { qi Bj+Tj : j=1,...,n }

   agree, with multiplicities. Here n is unrestricted and need not equal the order of Q in the other formulations.

No assertion is made that any of these conditions holds for the candidate. In particular this does not identify arbitrary finite holonomy with abelian, dihedral, bounded order, or bounded dimension holonomy.

## Proof, including original vertex rotations

For (1)⇒(2), take a common positive power M that kills the linear parts of a,b,t, and let A0,B0,T0 be the resulting translation vectors. Vertex injectivity gives A0 nonzero. The same commuting-power and conjugation calculation as in the reviewed abelian lemma gives

    Si(pi A0+T0)=qi B0+T0,

where Si is the original linear part of si. Set Q=<S1,S2,S3>, a finite subgroup of the orthogonal group after averaging. One may at this point rebuild a new homomorphism by sending a,b,t to translations A0,B0,T0 and si to the pure linear transformation Si: the displayed equations are precisely all its required conjugation relations. This explains why the pure-translation ansatz is sufficient for existence without assuming that the original witness had pure-translation vertices.

Choose a real linear functional ell with ell(A0) nonzero. The map

    F(v)(g)=ell(g^-1 v), g in Q,

is Q-equivariant from the original real module into the single real left regular module: F(hv)(g)=F(v)(h^-1 g). It has F(A0)(1) nonzero. Hence F(A0),F(B0),F(T0) give a real solution of the displayed integer linear system with its A component nonzero. A rational matrix has a rational basis for its kernel. If every rational kernel vector had A=0, every real kernel vector would too. Therefore a rational kernel vector with A nonzero exists, proving (2).

Any orthogonal solution has either A=B=0 or both A,B nonzero. Indeed, if B=0, equality of squared norms gives

    pi^2 ||A||^2 + 2pi <A,T> = 0.

Divide by the nonzero pi and compare i=1,2, whose pi differ, to obtain ||A||^2=0. If A=0, the analogous comparison using distinct qi gives B=0. This applies in particular to the regular permutation module.

For (2)⇒(4), clear denominators by a positive integer d. Let Q act on Z[Q] by left translation and on an additional Z coordinate trivially. Send

    a ↦ (dA,0), b ↦ (dB,0), t ↦ (dT,1),
    si ↦ the pure linear element L(hi).

The scalar last coordinate is written separately from the regular-module coordinate. The equations in (2) give all relators, and the two commuting vertex relations hold because their images are translations. Since A and B are nonzero, (dA,0),(dT,1) and (dB,0),(dT,1) are real linearly independent pairs. The two Z² vertices therefore map injectively to discrete rank-two lattices. This also proves (4)⇒(1).

Finally, because h1,h2,h3 generate Q, the common fixed space of their left regular actions consists exactly of constant functions. A solution with A=0 has B=0 by the preceding norm calculation, and then T is such a constant. Thus the entire vertical-only kernel is the single constant-T line. A horizontal solution exists exactly when the full kernel has dimension at least two, which is precisely (3).

For (2)⇒(5), index the regular coordinates by Q; each left translation permutes them, giving the multiset equalities. Conversely, matching equal entries in each pair of finite multisets gives three permutations on n coordinates. Their generated group is finite and preserves the standard Euclidean metric. The same denominator-clearing and independent-height construction used in (2)⇒(4) yields a vertex-injective Euclidean-by-finite witness. Thus (5)⇒(1). Multiplicities are essential, and both repeated triples and repeated projected values are allowed.

## What this closes and what it does not

Arbitrary original vertex rotational parts and affine cocycles introduce no further existential obstruction beyond the finite regular permutation matrix problem: the common-power reconstruction and the extra independent height coordinate prove this, rather than assume it. In contrast, proving that every marked finite group has the maximal possible rank 3N-1 is exactly the outstanding central difficulty. It cannot be promoted to a theorem merely because abelian, dihedral, B3, B4, or low-degree permutation actions have been excluded. The all-finite-quotient matrix-rank route is **blocked at that unsupported universal claim** unless a new mechanism or a positive witness is found.
