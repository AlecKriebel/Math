## 1. Scalar reflection construction — partial success

Mechanism: normalize an edge-nonzero height so one tree edge is a common height vector. Map all horizontal vertex directions into one line and each stable letter into a reflection or identity. The resulting finite sign equations give an exact Euclidean-by-finite witness.

Evidence: THETA_SUBCLASS.md, general proof and50,625 exact rational checks. It handles every two-vertex, three-edge theta that is CAT(0). It does not need a chosen CAT(0) metric to produce the witness; CAT(0) is used only to rule out one degenerate slope configuration. Root has assigned a fresh adversarial review.

Gap: with three or more non-tree edges, the sign equations can have no nonzero horizontal solution even though positive-definite CAT(0) Gram matrices exist. The corrected Pell candidate is an exact example. Reusing the same sign ansatz cannot settle those candidates; this route is blocked there absent a new representation mechanism.

## 2. Finite abelian holonomy obstruction — partial success

Mechanism: simultaneous complex characters turn three edge equations into a3×3 determinant. The determinant has six monomials. Minimal vanishing-root relations and exact cyclotomic arithmetic reduce arbitrary finite abelian holonomy to a finite, independently checkable certificate.

Evidence: ABELIAN_HOLONOMY_DELTA.md and its independent checker/certificate. Parent assigned a fresh reviewer. The candidate itself has an explicit F5-by-Z presentation and compatible flat metrics.

Gap: finite nonabelian holonomy has no simultaneous character decomposition. Treating this missing assertion as a universal determinant calculation would transfer the central difficulty. Finite degree≤5 permutation absence is supporting evidence only, not a route to an unbounded theorem.

## 3. Infinite planar holonomy — rejected as a full obstruction

A chosen flat metric can produce irrational products of reflections. This alone cannot show that no vertex-injective Euclidean-by-finite representation exists, because that representation can have a different metric and a larger Euclidean dimension. The primary2505.18054v3 Example8.10 explicitly requires a dimension greater than the largest vertex rank. Thus no two-dimensional angle obstruction is promoted.

## 4. Graph-manifold completion — distinct unproved positive mechanism

A CAT(0) metric and a global edge-nonzero height resemble nonpositive-curvature compatibility equations for graph manifolds. If one could complete the tubular group into a suitable nonpositively curved graph-manifold group while preserving vertex/edge injections, known virtual specialness would help. No such construction is supplied. Three or more distinct slopes at a rank-two vertex and extending cyclic annuli to torus gluings are the precise missing compatibilities. An unqualified assertion for all tubular free-by-Z groups would incorrectly include Gersten, so this remains speculative.

## 5. Simulating full-rank irrational holonomy — structurally blocked

Known Euclidean generalized Baumslag–Solitar constructions can obstruct virtual specialness through infinite-order rational orthogonal holonomy on a rank-two normal abelian subgroup. Simply importing a full-rank HNN extension does not produce a free-by-cyclic group: its free kernel would contain a rank-two abelian subgroup or the group has cohomological dimension at least3. A common nonzero height invariant on a two-dimensional flat would also force an orthogonal map fixing a line, hence finite order. In a genuine tubular graph, a hyperbolic Bass–Serre element intersects a vertex stabilizer through a cyclic first-edge stabilizer, so it cannot transparently conjugate a rank-two finite-index vertex lattice to itself. This explains why the familiar full-rank obstruction does not automatically transfer.

## 6. Generic finite-holonomy matrix rank — unproved, not a counterexample

The four-edge candidate gives a square matrix on three vector variables for each permutation degree. Generic small finite representations have only the constant-height kernel, but an exact witness could first occur at much larger degree. Countably many exclusions over a real metric parameter do not yield an integral or rational edge specification outside all exclusions. Nor does approximation of an infinite orthogonal representation by finite data preserve an exact kernel. No Baire/category or numerical-rank argument is used to claim an eligible counterexample.
