## Independent argument checks

1. **Presentation and injectivity.** From `c_i^-1 t c_i=t w_i` and `[t,w_i]=1`, one gets `t c_i t^-1=c_i w_i` with exactly the convention displayed in the note. Fixing the `x_j` and replacing each independent `c_i` by `c_i w_i` is an automorphism, with inverse replacing it by `c_i w_i^-1`. The semidirect-product normal form therefore proves the free fiber injects, and the subgroup generated by its fixed free factor and `t` is `F × Z`. For every nonidentity `u` in that free factor, the normal form and torsion-freeness give `⟨u,t⟩ ≅ Z²`. No unproved injectivity assumption is being inserted.

2. **Hyperbolicity and the common character.** Properness rules out an infinite-order elliptic element, since all its powers would stabilize one point. Thus semisimplicity makes `t` hyperbolic. Its centralizer preserves the product splitting of `Min(t)` and its oriented real factor, giving one signed real character on the entire fixed free subgroup. Centralizing `t` prevents reflections on the real factor. Separate, independently chosen characters for different twists would not be legitimate; the note correctly uses a single one.

3. **Strictly positive transverse length.** This is the main point at which a careless proof could fail. Merely knowing `u` is hyperbolic in the entire space does not imply positive transverse length: it could conceivably translate parallel to `t`. Here that possibility is excluded by the genuine independent rank-two subgroup. The proper semisimple flat-torus theorem produces its common invariant plane with two independent lattice vectors. Its `t` direction agrees with the real factor of `Min(t)`. The transverse component of the `u` vector must therefore be nonzero, and its norm is the translation length of the induced action on the other factor. Equivalently, if the semisimple transverse action had length zero, it would fix a point of that factor. Then `⟨u,t⟩` would preserve a line and act on it by translations; rational dependence gives an infinite kernel, while irrational dependence gives translations arbitrarily close to zero. Both contradict properness. This also makes explicit why independence, properness, and semisimplicity are essential.

4. **The product norm is the global length.** The induced transverse action is semisimple; its axis, together with the real direction, provides a flat on which the minimum product displacement is attained. Equivalently use the common minset for `⟨u,t⟩`. Consequently the length of `t w_i` in the full space is exactly the product norm used in the note, not merely an upper bound in a convenient invariant subspace. Conjugacy with `t` therefore gives the stated equality. Positive transverse length forces the strict inequalities `-2 ell < lambda(w_i) < 0`; neither endpoint is admissible for a nontrivial twist.

5. **Positive dependence.** A real character of a free group factors through its abelianization. A nonnegative dependence supported on nontrivial twists makes a nonempty sum of strictly negative values equal zero. This is a contradiction even for real coefficients, without any rationality or integrality premise. A nontrivial word with zero abelianization is correctly excluded as a single-term certificate. The two displayed multiple-word examples have the advertised exact integer certificates. Trivial twists cannot be included in the strict sum; the note excludes them explicitly.

6. **Conjugate signed powers.** The character is unchanged by conjugation and scales by the signed exponent. Transverse translation length is unchanged by conjugation and scales by the absolute value. For nonzero exponent `n`, expansion and division of the length equation gives `n D + 2 ell lambda(u)=0`, where `D=d(u)^2+lambda(u)^2>0`. Subtracting the two equations gives `(n_i-n_j)D=0`. Thus equality of the signed exponents, rather than just their absolute values, is required. This excludes both unequal positive powers and opposite signs. The Gersten pair passes the halfspace test but fails this stronger condition, as asserted. A single exponent can satisfy its one equation, so the lemma does not falsely reject all nontrivial twists.

7. **Boundary and scope.** An empty twist family and an all-identity family are direct products of a finite-rank free group with an infinite cyclic group, hence RAAGs. The all-identity case supplies no strict inequality. The rank-one fixed factor is permitted. The assumption that `t` fixes the whole factor used for abelianization is indispensable. In the illustrative Nielsen automorphism `a -> a`, `b -> b a^k`, both `a` and `b a b^-1` are fixed. In their free rank-two subgroup, `[a,b]=a (b a b^-1)^-1` has exponent vector `(1,-1)`, although it has zero exponent vector in `F(a,b)`. Thus the larger-fiber abelianization cannot replace the fixed-subgroup abelianization. This agrees with the distinction made by Delage's §4. The note does not need a theorem classifying the entire fixed subgroup for this warning.

## Outcome and exact remaining gap

**PASS as a standard-derived necessary CAT(0) eligibility filter.** It provides no sufficient CAT(0) criterion and no obstruction to virtual RAAG embedding of a group that actually satisfies the CAT(0) hypothesis of 21.124. It is therefore useful for rejecting proposed ineligible counterexamples, but it does not resolve either part of that problem. Full 21.124 remains open in this audit.
