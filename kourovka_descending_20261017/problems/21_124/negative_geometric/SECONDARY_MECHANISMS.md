# Independent geometric obstruction mechanisms: scope and exact gaps

UTC: 2026-10-24T22:36:45.972531+00:00. No complete counterexample. These routes are separated from the successful partial affine constructions.

## Two-generator distortion

Carr's primary paper, *Two-generator subgroups of right-angled Artin groups are quasi-isometrically embedded*, arXiv:1412.0642, proves that a nonabelian two-generator subgroup of a RAAG is free and undistorted. Source checked live: https://www.math.emory.edu/~mpcarr/papers/2gen.pdf . Hence if a finitely generated group H embeds in a RAAG, every two-generator subgroup L≤H is undistorted in H: |x|_L is bounded linearly by |x|_RAAG, which is bounded linearly by |x|_H. No undistortedness of H in the RAAG is assumed.

In G_k, the free subgroup F(a,b) is distorted. For example, expanding (b t)^n t^-n gives the positive free word product_(i=0)^(n-1) b a^(ki), of free length n+k n(n-1)/2 but ambient length at most3n. This supplies a direct nonembedding obstruction, independently of the familiar G_2-type two-generator nonfree subgroup. It is not virtual persistence: for a finite-index H, H∩F(a,b) generally has rank greater than2, and Carr's theorem then gives no conclusion. The known virtual specialness of rank-two-free-by-cyclic mapping tori already falsifies the blanket assertion that a distorted rank-two fiber prevents virtual RAAG embedding.

Attempting to use powers a^e,b^e does not preserve the positive-word certificate: the expanded powers (b a^(ki))^e use individual b letters and generally are not words in the subgroup generated by a^e,b^e. The exact missing mechanism is a rank-two distorted subgroup inside every finite-index H, with its word metric controlled. No such construction was obtained. Replacing it with the original distorted F(a,b) is an invalid quantifier shortcut.

## Residual finiteness, including the broad infinite-free-kernel reading

This cannot supply a negative example at all under the current primary theorem. Linton2510.10178v2, Theorem1.2, embeds every finitely generated free-by-Z group (allowing an infinite-rank free kernel) into a finite-rank-free-by-Z group. The theorem was checked live at https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10178v2#S1.Thmtheorem2 .

The latter groups are residually finite by the elementary characteristic-cover argument: to separate a nontrivial free-fiber word, choose a finite-index characteristic subgroup of the finitely generated free group excluding it. Such a subgroup exists by residual finiteness of free groups and taking the intersection of all subgroups of bounded finite index. The monodromy descends to the resulting finite quotient, and has finite order there; adjoining a cyclic stable letter of a multiple of that order yields a finite semidirect quotient separating the word. Elements of nonzero cyclic height are separated by finite cyclic quotients. Residual finiteness passes to subgroups. Since CAT(0) groups are finitely generated, the broad free-kernel convention cannot evade this deduction. This is a prior implication, not an original theorem.

## Fixed-centralizer commutator twists and flat metrics

If a proposed eligible example contains an HNN relation identifying t with tv where v is a nontrivial commutator in a subgroup centralizing t, the semisimple CAT(0) product splitting of Min(t) already obstructs it (Delage Proposition3.1). Such examples cannot be certified CAT(0) from Samuelson's old Theorem4.4. In the eligible family here w=[a,b]=a(bab^-1)^-1 has nonzero abelianization in the fixed free subgroup F(a,bab^-1), so it passes this eligibility filter.

For commuting a,t, Euclidean flat-torus length equalities are necessary but not a RAAG-specific obstruction. Noncompact specialness also does not require a cocompact cubulation or a cubical coarse median structure. The affine construction shows that demanding all relevant finite parts be trivial loses real virtual retractions; the finite-holonomy theorem must retain its hypotheses.

## Generalized finite-holonomy escalation is not a proof

For any one homomorphism to a finite-by-abelian group, large enough powers of two chosen elements have commuting translation images. This kills a commutator depending on that homomorphism's finite exponent. Varying the commutator with the quotient does not exhibit one fixed nontrivial word invisible in every finite-index cover. The same phenomenon occurs in free groups, which embed in RAAGs. Accordingly an argument based only on arbitrarily large needed holonomy transfers rather than solves the central difficulty.

## Current exact gap

No finite-index-persistent obstruction compatible with a geometric CAT(0) action was found. The proved/proposed partials are useful filters and explicit virtual retractions, not a complete negative or affirmative answer to21.124. A genuinely new obstruction must survive arbitrary finite holonomy, or a different family must come with an independently verified geometric CAT(0) construction and a complete all-finite-index nonembedding proof.
