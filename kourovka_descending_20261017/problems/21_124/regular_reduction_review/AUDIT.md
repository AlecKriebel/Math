## 1. Arbitrary initial rotations and cocycles

Write an initial Euclidean-by-finite witness as a homomorphism into `V⋊F`, where V is a finite-dimensional real inner product space and F is a finite group acting linearly by isometries. A finite linear action can equivalently be made orthogonal by averaging any positive inner product. The conclusion requires only injectivity on each displayed vertex group; the initial vertex image need not be discrete or span a two-dimensional real subspace.

Take a common positive M killing the finite linear parts of the images of a,b,t, and denote the translations of their Mth powers by A0,B0,T0. Vertex injectivity implies A0≠0 and B0≠0: a nontrivial power of either vertex generator cannot have trivial image.

Raise each defining conjugation relator to its Mth power. Since a commutes with t, `(a^pi t)^M=a^(Mpi)t^M`; the analogous formula holds for b,t. Thus, writing Si for the original linear part of si, conjugation of a translation gives exactly

`Si(pi A0+T0)=qi B0+T0`.

The translational part of si cancels in conjugation of a translation. These equations justify **a new homomorphism**, with pure-translation vertices and pure-linear stable letters. The proof is not asserting that the original affine witness has unchanged generators after taking powers. This distinction is handled correctly.

The group `Q=<S1,S2,S3>` is finite and generated by the three marked elements. It can be smaller than the original finite linear image, because the original vertex rotations are no longer needed in the rebuilt witness. No extra finite quotient relations are being imposed on the original group: killing a,b,t leaves the three edge letters free.

## 2. One regular copy and rational descent

Choose a real linear functional ell with ell(A0)≠0. For g∈Q define

`F(v)(g)=ell(g^-1 v)`.

With left regular action `(L(h)f)(g)=f(h^-1 g)`, one has

`F(hv)(g)=ell(g^-1 h v)=F(v)(h^-1 g)`.

Therefore F is equivariant with exactly the claimed convention. Also `F(A0)(1)=ell(A0)≠0`. One regular copy need not embed the entire original module: it only has to retain this one nonzero component while transporting the equations. Arbitrarily large multiplicities of an irreducible representation in the original module create no problem.

The transported equations form a matrix over Z. A rational row-reduction gives a rational basis of its kernel, whose real span is the real kernel. If every rational kernel basis vector had zero A-block, every real kernel vector would also have zero A-block. Hence the existing real solution with A≠0 implies a rational one with A≠0. This argument does not rely on rounding an approximate solution or on a numerically inferred rank.

## 5. Multisets, permutations, and positive weights

A regular-module solution gives equality of the two coordinate multisets for each i, since each L(hi) permutes coordinates.

Conversely, equal multisets of length n can be matched with multiplicities: match each occurrence of `pi Aj+Tj` on the left to one equal occurrence of `qi Bk+Tk` on the right. The resulting bijection j↦k defines a push permutation Pi such that `Pi(pi A+T)=qi B+T`. Repeated triples and repeated projected values cause no problem; the matching is of occurrences, not merely distinct values. The three resulting permutations may be chosen independently, since the source imposes no relations among the edge letters beyond their separate displayed conjugations. Their generated finite group acts orthogonally on R^n. The same nonzero-B norm argument and added height give a vertex-injective witness. This establishes condition5⇒condition1; n need not equal the order of this finite group.

An optional weighted formulation is valid for **positive rational weights**: clear all weight denominators and repeat each rational triple according to its resulting positive integer multiplicity. Zero-weight atoms should first be removed, and the nonzero-A condition must concern an atom of positive weight. For fixed rational atoms, existence of strictly positive real balancing weights also admits rational positive weights, by a rational basis for the balancing kernel and approximation within its relatively open positive cone. None of these extensions is needed for the frozen unweighted condition5.

Signed weights do not have this interpretation. The independent checker includes a concrete falsifier for using them: for pairs `(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)`, take a signed atomic measure formed by convolving the six factors `delta_0-delta_v`, with v respectively `(1,0,-pi)` and `(0,1,-qi)`.
