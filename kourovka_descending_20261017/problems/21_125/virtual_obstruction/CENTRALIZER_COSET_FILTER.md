# A necessary finite-cover filter, and a uniform barrier to this obstruction

October 24, 2026. Partial research on Kourovka 21.125(b). **No Hydra group is proved non-virtually-special here.** The main obstruction under investigation fails to exclude finite covers in every rank. Discovery estimate for the original negative goal: 4%.

Write
\[
G_m=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_m,t\mid a_i^t=a_i a_{i-1}\ (i\ge2),\ a_1^t=a_1\rangle,
\qquad [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy.
\]
The original Hydra presentation identifies its free fiber with the free group on the displayed generators. In particular all powers of \(a_1\) are nontrivial. Throughout \(m\ge2\).

## An obstruction that really passes to a condition on every finite-index subgroup

**Lemma.** If a finite-index subgroup \(H\le G_m\) embeds in a RAAG, then \(H\) contains no element of any conjugate of the coset
\[
a_2 C_{G_m}(t).
\]
Consequently, in the coset permutation representation on \(H\backslash G_m\), every element of \(a_2C_{G_m}(t)\), including every \(a_2a_1^k t^r\), is fixed-point free.

**Proof.** Put \(x=a_2u\), where \([u,t]=1\). For every positive integer \(q\),
\[
[x,t^q]=u^{-1}a_1^q u\ne1,
\qquad [[x,t^q],t^q]=1.
\]
Suppose that \(g x g^{-1}\in H\). Finite index supplies a positive \(q\) such that \(g t^q g^{-1}\in H\). These two elements generate a nonabelian subgroup satisfying a nontrivial two-generator relation. Such a subgroup is not free: in a free group the centralizer of a nontrivial element is cyclic, so a nontrivial commutator cannot commute with its second argument (or apply the usual free-group conjugacy/centralizer theorem). Baudisch's theorem says every two-generator subgroup of a RAAG is free or free abelian. Thus an embedding of \(H\) into a RAAG is impossible. Finally, a right coset \(Hg\) fixed by \(x\) is exactly the condition \(g x g^{-1}\in H\). This proves both assertions. \(\square\)

Baudisch's theorem is established prior work, not a new lemma here. A complete alternate proof, with the exact theorem stated as Theorem 1.2, is in Mike Carr, *Two-generator subgroups of right-angled Artin groups are quasi-isometrically embedded*, arXiv:1412.0642. The proof applies to arbitrary ambient graphs by first restricting the two finite words to their finite induced support.

Two consequences are immediate.

1. Any finite-index subgroup containing \(a_2\) fails the embedding test. If \(m\ge3\), then \(a_2=[a_3,t]\in G_m'\); hence **no finite abelian cover** (no subgroup containing \(G_m'\)) can be a RAAG subgroup. This concerns these covers only, and permits deeper nonabelian covers.
2. If \(H\) is normal and its finite quotient is nilpotent of class less than \(m-1\), then it contains \(a_2=[a_m,{}_{m-2}t]\in\gamma_{m-1}(G_m)\), so it fails the test. Quotients of sufficiently large class are not excluded.

## A bounded exact census of the necessary condition

A homomorphism to \(S_d\) is determined by \(A=\rho(a_m)\) and \(T=\rho(t)\). Put \(E_0=A\) and \(E_{j+1}=[E_j,T]\). The defining relation is \(E_m=1\); the lower generators are \(\rho(a_i)=E_{m-i}\). The necessary condition above requires every \(E_{m-2}E_{m-1}^k T^r\) to be fixed-point free.

`check_strong_index.py` tests this exactly for every \(d\le8\). It takes one representative for each cycle partition of \(T\), and **every** \(A\in S_d\); simultaneous conjugation proves completeness. It follows the finite commutator trajectory until the identity or a repetition. A repeated nonidentity state cannot ever reach the identity. If the trajectory reaches the identity for the first time at depth \(\ell\), only \(m=\ell\) or \(m=\ell+1\) can have a nonidentity \(E_{m-2}\); thus this computation covers all ranks, not merely a chosen finite rank interval. The subgroup generated by \(E_{m-1}\) and \(T\) has exactly computed point orbits. The whole coset is fixed-point free iff \(E_{m-2}\) sends each point outside its orbit under that subgroup. This avoids numerical or sampling arguments.

The permitted ranks under this **necessary condition only** are:

| Index degree | Ranks not excluded |
|---:|:---|
| 1 | none |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 2, 3 |
| 5 | 2 |
| 6 | 2 |
| 7 | 2 |
| 8 | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 |

In particular, **any RAAG-embedding subgroup of \(G_m\) for \(m\ge4\) has index at least 8**. For \(m\ge8\), this particular census also excludes index 8. No existence or embedding assertion follows for a permitted rank. The search does not require transitivity, so every excluded degree excludes every actual subgroup of that index. The JSON stores exact witnesses for allowed states and the number of pairs tested; a separate weaker census checks just that \(a_2\) is a derangement.

## The full centralizer-coset filter nevertheless passes in every rank

Here is a theoretical barrier to promoting the preceding necessary test to an obstruction to *all* finite covers.

**Proposition.** For every \(m\ge2\) there is a transitive finite permutation action of \(G_m\) of degree \(2N\), where \(N\) is the least power of 2 with \(N\ge m-1\), in which every element of the entire coset \(a_2C_{G_m}(t)\) is fixed-point free. In particular \(2N<4(m-1)\).

**Proof.** Let \(V=\mathbb F_2^N\), with cyclic coordinate shift \(S\), and write \(\Delta=1+S\). Since \(N\) is a power of 2, \(\Delta^N=1+S^N=0\), and
\[
\Delta^{N-1}e_0=(1,\ldots,1).
\]
The latter identity follows from \((1+x)^{N-1}=1+x+\cdots+x^{N-1}\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).

On \(\Omega=(\mathbb Z/N)\times\mathbb F_2\), let \(T\) cyclically shift the first coordinate and let \(B_v\) flip the second coordinate at precisely those first coordinates where \(v\) is 1. Choose the direction of \(S\) so that \(T^{-1}B_vT=B_{Sv}\). The \(B_v\) commute, and \([B_v,T]=B_{\Delta v}\).

Put \(v=\Delta^{N-(m-1)}e_0\), and set
\[
\rho(t)=T,\qquad
\rho(a_i)=B_{\Delta^{m-i}v}\quad(1\le i\le m).
\]
All defining relations hold. Here \(\rho(a_1)=1\) and \(\rho(a_2)=B_{(1,\ldots,1)}\), the simultaneous flip of both layers. The vector \(v\ne0\); cyclically moving any coordinate with a flip shows the generated action is transitive.

It remains to check the *full* centralizer rather than just \(\langle a_1,t\rangle\). Write an arbitrary centralizing element in normal form \(w t^j\), with \(w\in F_m\). Commutation with \(t\) means \(\theta(w)=w\). In the integral abelianization of the free fiber the unipotent Jordan transformation \(a_i\mapsto a_i+a_{i-1}\) has fixed subgroup exactly \(\mathbb Z a_1\). Hence the exponent sums of \(a_2,\ldots,a_m\) in \(w\) vanish. Since all \(\rho(a_i)\) commute and \(\rho(a_1)=1\), it follows that \(\rho(w)=1\). Thus \(\rho(C_{G_m}(t))\subseteq\langle T\rangle\). Every \(\rho(a_2)T^j\) is fixed-point free: it changes the first coordinate if \(j\not\equiv0\pmod N\), and otherwise flips the second coordinate. This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

The stabilizer of a point is therefore a genuine finite-index subgroup passing this particular necessary filter in every rank. **It is not proved to embed in a RAAG or to be special.** Any attempt to prove a virtual negative answer must introduce materially stronger information than this centralizer-coset filter. The obstruction route is blocked at that precise gap.
