# Independent negative routes: evidence and exact gaps

October 24, 2026. Ordinary Hydra \(G_m\), provisionally unresolved ranks \(m\ge5\), from Kourovka 21.125(b). **No negative answer has been obtained.** The following mechanisms are distinct; none is a replacement for the actual finite-index specialness question.

## 1. Two-generator and commuting-conjugate obstruction

Each whole \(G_m\), \(m\ge2\), is generated by \(a_m,t\), is nonabelian, and has a nontrivial Engel relator. Thus Baudisch's RAAG theorem excludes a direct embedding of the entire group. This known observation does not exclude virtual embedding. Known positive ranks 2–4 already falsify any inference of that form.

A legitimate virtual *necessary condition* is proved in `CENTRALIZER_COSET_FILTER.md`: every hypothetical RAAG-embedding subgroup must avoid all conjugates of \(a_2 C(t)\). A bounded exhaustive permutation computation excludes every subgroup of index at most7 for ranks \(m\ge4\), and also index8 for \(m\ge8\). These are lower bounds on a possible embedding index, not nonexistence at arbitrary index.

**Exact route obstruction.** The same note constructs transitive permutation actions passing the entire centralizer-coset test in every rank, of degree less than \(4(m-1)\). This countermodel is proved uniformly, then checked exactly for ranks2–65. It is a finite-cover countermodel to the method, not a RAAG embedding of the cover. A materially stronger condition on all two-generator subgroups would be required to reopen the route.

### Why taking large powers does not preserve the Engel obstruction

For \(p,q>0\), put \(x=a_2^p\), \(y=t^q\). Then in the free fiber
\[
[x,y]=a_2^{-p}(a_2a_1^q)^p.
\]
When \(p=1\), this is \(a_1^q\), fixed by \(\theta^q\). For \(p\ge2\) its freely reduced word starts with \(a_2^{-1}\), whereas its \(\theta^q\)-image starts with \(a_1^{-1}\); the initial \(a_1^{-q}\) cannot cancel with the following \(a_2^{-1}\). Thus
\[
[[a_2^p,t^q],t^q]\ne1\quad(p\ge2).
\]
The familiar short Engel relation does not pass to arbitrary positive powers of both generators. `check_uniform_barrier.py` independently substitutes signed free words for \(1\le p,q\le12\), confirming the exact nonzero reductions. The unbounded argument is the initial-letter calculation above. No assertion is made that these powered pairs are necessarily free.

## 2. Central infinite-order commutators and Gersten-type metric obstructions

A possible RAAG obstruction would be a subgroup \(K\) with nontrivial central \(z\) and \(z^n\in[K,K]\) for some nonzero \(n\). This is unavailable inside every ordinary Hydra group for a stronger existing reason: the original Hydra groups act properly and cocompactly on CAT(0) spaces, by Dison–Riley, *Hydra groups*, Theorem1.3 and following construction.

Indeed, if \(z\) is a central infinite-order element of a subgroup acting properly by semisimple CAT(0) isometries, its minset splits as \(Y\times\mathbb R\). Every element of \(K\) preserves the splitting and translates the line factor; commutation with \(z\) rules out reversing that line. Signed line displacement is a homomorphism \(K\to\mathbb R\) nonzero on \(z\), so no nonzero power of \(z\) lies in \([K,K]\). This also rules out using nonabelian nilpotent subgroups by this mechanism.

Similarly an embedded Gersten obstruction to proper semisimple CAT(0) actions is impossible: restricting the known CAT(0) action to a subgroup would contradict that obstruction. Generalized examples that obstruct only cocompact cubulation would still not settle the noncompact specialness in this problem. The exact gap is a genuinely cube-specific obstruction compatible with all existing CAT(0) actions and persisting in every finite cover.

## 3. Residual and profinite obstructions

Baumslag–Mikhailov, *Residual properties of groups defined by basic commutators*, arXiv:1301.4629v2, Theorem1, proves that exactly the one-relator Hydra groups \(\langle a,t\mid[a,{}_{m}t]=1\rangle\) are residually torsion-free nilpotent. Its proof uses the free normal fiber and the nilpotent quotient by the fiber commutator subgroup (Proposition1 and §5.2). Reversing the displayed free-basis indices gives precisely the ordinary family here. This is prior work, not an experimental guess about a quotient.

Consequently failure of residual finiteness, failure of residual torsion-free nilpotence, generalized torsion, and basic orderability failures cannot supply the desired obstruction. Nonseparability of the highly distorted Hydra *subgroup* also does not imply failure of virtual specialness of its ambient group: arbitrary finitely generated subgroups of RAAGs need not be separable. No subgroup-separability property stronger than what actually passes to arbitrary RAAG subgroups has been assumed.

A sharper residual-finiteness growth or faithful-linearity obstruction would require a new quantitative theorem. Huge distortion of a free subgroup supplies no such theorem, and does not give a lower bound on the intrinsic Dehn function of \(G_m\). This route has no obtained quantitative lower bound and is presently blocked at that exact missing mechanism.

## 4. Virtual homology and cyclic virtual retractions

This approach was delegated independently before the other route results were sent. Its candidate was persistent rational homological invisibility of \(a_1\), or other deep standard commutators, in every finite cover. Such invisibility would obstruct a cyclic virtual retraction, a necessary property of RAAG subgroups.

The companion `homology/` investigation finds that base abelianization and a first characteristic abelian cover can both be misleading: a further explicit finite cover in rank5 has an integral invariant character nonzero on \(a_1^2\), hence retracts onto \(\langle a_1^2\rangle\). It also checks powers of the other standard generators and selected commutators. Only the frozen companion artifact and its independent review can justify these individual visibility claims; they are not a proof for every element, or of RFRS or specialness. No persistent invisible element has been produced. The exact remaining gap is a uniform statement about all covers for some fixed nontrivial element, rather than invisibility in a chosen tower.
