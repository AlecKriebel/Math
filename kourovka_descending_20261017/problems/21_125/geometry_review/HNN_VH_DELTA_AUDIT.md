## 1. Exact link cycle and its quantifiers

The presentation square at level \(i\) has boundary

\[
s\alpha_i\alpha_{i-1}^{-1}\alpha_i^{-1},\qquad\alpha_0=s.
\]

At the unique base vertex its four corner pairs of outgoing germs are

\[
\{\alpha_i^+,s^+\},\quad
\{s^-,\alpha_i^+\},\quad
\{\alpha_i^-,\alpha_{i-1}^-\},\quad
\{\alpha_{i-1}^+,\alpha_i^-\}.
\]

Consequently the proposed cycle

\[
\alpha_2^+\;—\;s^+\;—\;\alpha_1^+\;—\;
\alpha_2^-\;—\;\alpha_3^-\;—\;\alpha_2^+
\]

has five distinct vertices and all five edges. Their level/corner sources, numbering the corners from zero along the displayed square boundary, are respectively

\[
(2,0),\ (1,0),\ (2,3),\ (3,2),\ (3,3).
\]

Extra edges do not weaken the argument: the presence of any odd cycle prevents a graph from being bipartite. The cycle exists for every \(m\ge3\), with no appeal to a finite search.

A VH edge partition forces each link edge to connect unlike edge types, so each vertex link must be bipartite. Every covering map, including nonregular or infinite covers, induces an isomorphism on each local vertex link. Thus **no cover of this particular square complex is VH for \(m\ge3\)**. The argument also persists through ordinary product cubical subdivisions: the link of every retained old vertex is unchanged. It concerns that standard cube-by-cube subdivision, not arbitrary changes to the square structure or a different cubulation of the group. The author uses the correctly restricted phrase “ordinary cubical subdivision.”

The rank threshold is necessary for this local argument. For ranks 1 and 2 the link is bipartite; in rank 2, for example, one bipartition is

\[
\{s^+,s^-,\alpha_2^-\}\quad\text{and}\quad
\{\alpha_1^+,\alpha_1^-,\alpha_2^+\}.
\]

This does not itself supply a VH partition of the base complex, since opposite germs of an edge must receive the same type globally. It does show that the five-cycle proof must not be extended to rank 2. Known rank-two positive finite covers are compatible with the asserted lemma.

## 2. Genuine HNN structure and base case

For \(m\ge2\), put \(B=G_{m-1}\), \(z=\alpha_m\), \(C=\langle s\rangle\), and \(D=\langle\alpha_{m-1}\rangle\). The exact presentation is

\[
G_m=\langle B,z\mid z^{-1}sz=\alpha_{m-1}\rangle.
\]

Both associated cyclic groups are infinite, so mapping their indicated generators defines an isomorphism and a genuine HNN extension; Britton normal forms inject the base. The initial group is \(G_1=\langle s,\alpha_1\mid[s,\alpha_1]=1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\). Thus \(L_1=\langle\alpha_1\rangle\) is infinite cyclic and \(L_1\cap\langle s\rangle=1\). The displayed induction formula involving \(L_{m-1}\) is read for \(m\ge2\), with this explicit rank-one base case; no \(G_0\) or \(L_0\) is required.

Abelianizing \(G_r\) gives a free abelian group on \([s]\) and \([\alpha_r]\); all \([\alpha_j]\) for \(j<r\) equal \([s]\). This follows immediately from the given defining relations, which impose exactly those identifications and no torsion. For \(r=1\), \([s]\) and \([\alpha_1]\) remain independent. Hence for every applicable base \(B=G_{m-1}\),

\[
C\cap D=1.
\]

In particular a nonzero power \(s^n\) cannot belong to \(D\). This independent abelianization assertion is correctly used for the top base generator, rather than incorrectly asserting independence of \(s\) and every lower \(\alpha_j\).

## 3. Independent Britton proof of the free-product and intersection claims

Assume the induction hypothesis \(L_{m-1}\cap C=1\). The definition of \(L_m\) gives it as the subgroup generated by \(L_{m-1}\) and \(zCz^{-1}\). Consider a reduced word in the abstract free product

\[
L_{m-1}*\langle zsz^{-1}\rangle.
\]

Every nontrivial factor from the second group expands to \(zs^nz^{-1}\), with \(n\ne0\). Such a block cannot be a Britton pinch, because a subword \(zbz^{-1}\) pinches only when \(b\in D\), while \(s^n\notin D\). Between two such blocks, the subword \(z^{-1}\ell z\) has a nonidentity \(\ell\in L_{m-1}\). This pinches only if \(\ell\in C\), excluded by induction. Endpoint factors in the base create no further pinch. The expanded word is HNN-reduced.

If at least one conjugate-cyclic factor occurs, Britton's lemma says this word cannot lie in the base group. One way to see the stronger “not in the base” conclusion is to multiply on the right by any candidate inverse base element; the same stable-letter subwords remain reduced, so the product cannot be the identity. A nonidentity word involving only \(L_{m-1}\) remains nonidentity in the injected base. Thus the proposed free-product homomorphism is injective, and

\[
L_m=L_{m-1}*\langle\alpha_ms\alpha_m^{-1}\rangle,
\qquad L_m\cap G_{m-1}=L_{m-1}.
\]

Since \(C\subseteq G_{m-1}\), the second conclusion implies

\[
L_m\cap\langle s\rangle
=L_{m-1}\cap\langle s\rangle=1.
\]

This closes the induction for every \(m\ge1\). It also proves that \(L_m\) is free of rank \(m\), consistently with the earlier hyperplane graph. No separability or finite-quotient hypothesis occurs.

The stated exclusions of \(\alpha_j\), \(j\ge2\), follow too. At the top level, the \(z\)-exponent homomorphism kills all generators of \(L_m\) but takes \(\alpha_m\) to one. For an earlier \(j\), membership in the base group and the displayed intersection reduce the question inductively. There is no assertion excluding \(\alpha_1\), which is expressly a generator of \(L_m\).

## 4. The commutation and combination boundaries

The nonmalnormality observation in section B is valid, including \(m=2\): the injected original \(G_1\) contains \(\langle s,\alpha_1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\), with \(\alpha_1\notin\langle s\rangle\). Therefore \(\alpha_1\) centralizes a nontrivial subgroup of \(\langle s\rangle\) without lying in it. At higher ranks one must use this injected \(G_1\), as the note does; the abelianization of a higher group identifies the lower classes and would not alone prove their independence.

The individual attaching edge loops have geodesic lifts in the published CAT(0) model: their two opposite germs are not adjacent in the vertex link, so an iterated edge path is locally geodesic. This fact supplies no special HNN combination theorem. The note correctly records that missing step rather than assuming it.
