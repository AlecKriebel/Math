## Objection found and repaired

The initial final paragraph inferred that a positive virtual construction must use genuine fiber covers or other finite-index subgroups because cyclic height covers do not form a residual separating chain. That inference was unjustified. An actual embedding of **one** cyclic height cover into a RAAG would suffice for virtual specialness in the noncompact sense in the source contract. Failure to obtain a residual chain from that particular family does not obstruct such an embedding.

The author replaced that sentence. The current note states both facts accurately: all height covers contain the free fiber, so cannot themselves form a separating chain, but one height cover could still supply a positive virtual construction. The homology calculation proves neither embedding nor nonembedding of these covers. There is no outstanding substantive objection to this repaired scope.

## Independent proof checks

1. **The group and conjugation convention.** The primary Dison–Riley text, printed pp.510–511, explicitly gives the same presentation and free-by-cyclic description. With commutator convention `[x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y`, the relation gives `[a_i,t]=a_(i-1)`. Thus descending recursion from `a_m,t` generates every fiber generator. The fiber is genuinely free and injects in the mapping torus; this is not a presentation-level assumption.

2. **The direct embedding obstruction.** For `m>=2`, `[a_2,t]=a_1` is nonidentity. The commuting elements `a_1,t` generate a copy of `Z^2`: height kills the exponent of `t` in any putative relation, then the free-fiber normal form kills the remaining exponent of `a_1`. A free group has only cyclic abelian subgroups, so `G_m` is not free, and the commutator rules out abelianness. Carr's independently proved version of Baudisch's theorem, Theorem 1.2, states that every two-generator RAAG subgroup is free or free abelian. Its use here is correct. If an infinite defining graph is allowed, the two image words use finitely many vertices and lie in the induced finite-graph RAAG; hence that variant causes no exception. This is a direct obstruction only. It does not carry the two-generator property to arbitrary finite index.

3. **The cover presentation.** For positive integers `m,d`, the inverse image of `dZ` under the height homomorphism is exactly the set of normal forms `v t^(d k)`, with `v` in the full free fiber. It is therefore the mapping torus of `theta^d` with stable letter `u=t^d`, and its index is `d`. With fiber abelianization represented by column vectors in the order `a_1,...,a_m`, the image of `a_i` is `e_i+e_(i-1)` for `i>1`; the matrix really is `J=I+N` with a superdiagonal, rather than its inverse. Abelianization imposes the columns of `J^d-I` and leaves `u` free. Taking a transpose instead would give the same Smith invariants, but the stated convention is itself consistent.

4. **Rational rank and torsion order for every `m,d>=1`.** For `m>=2`, the first column and last row of `A=J^d-I` vanish. Deleting them leaves an `(m-1)` by `(m-1)` upper-triangular matrix with diagonal `d`, so the rank is exactly `m-1` over `Q`. Among minors of this maximum nonzero size, any other deletion retains either the zero last row or the zero first column; hence the only nonzero maximal minor is `d^(m-1)`. By the determinantal-divisor characterization of Smith invariants, the torsion of `coker(A)` has this order and its free rank is one. Adding the stable letter gives `Z^2` plus finite torsion. For `m=1`, `A=[0]`, the cokernel is `Z`, and the same torsion-order formula is `d^0=1`. Thus the rational first Betti number is two in every allowed boundary case. No upper bound on the minimum number of generators follows from that rational rank.

5. **Prime powers and exact generator number.** In characteristic `p`, repeated Frobenius gives `(I+N)^(p^r)=I+N^(p^r)`. If `q=p^r>=m`, this is `I`, so the fiber contributes all `m` mod-`p` homology dimensions and the stable letter contributes one. A group generated by `k` elements has mod-`p` abelianization dimension at most `k`; the displayed cover presentation supplies `m+1` generators. These upper and lower bounds coincide. The proof applies to arbitrary prime powers satisfying the inequality, not merely the finitely checked examples. If exponent zero is permitted, `q=1>=m` only adds `m=1`, where the assertion remains true.

6. **The elementary-abelian special case and its exception.** For `d=p>=m`, every nonzero Smith factor is divisible by `p`, since the entire matrix is zero modulo `p`. There are `m-1` such factors, and their product is `p^(m-1)`, so all equal `p`. This proves the stated elementary-abelian torsion. At `(m,d)=(3,2)`, the nonzero block is `[[2,1],[0,2]]`; its entry gcd is one and determinant four. Thus its Smith factors are `1,4`, and its mod-two rank is one. Adding the free fiber class and stable letter gives mod-two Betti number three. This correctly falsifies the unqualified version with every prime regardless of rank.

7. **Virtual and problem scope.** Torsion in the abelianization of a subgroup is not an obstruction to embedding that group in a RAAG. A rational or mod-prime Betti calculation is not a special cube complex construction, and the note makes neither assertion. No specific Hydra group of rank at least five is proved virtually special or proved not virtually special by this package. The result is a standard-derived control calculation, not a complete answer or an originality claim.

## Additional modular cross-check

The following stronger elementary formula was derived independently to stress-test the prime-power restriction:

`b_1(H_(m,d); F_p) = 1 + min(m, p^v_p(d))`.

Indeed, write `d=q s`, where `q=p^v_p(d)` and `p` does not divide `s`. Over `F_p`,

`J^d-I = (I+N^q)^s-I = N^q (s I + binom(s,2)N^q + ... )`.

The parenthesized factor is invertible, since its diagonal is the nonzero scalar `s` and all other terms are nilpotent upper-triangular terms. Hence `rank(J^d-I)=rank(N^q)=max(m-q,0)`. This argument also covers `q>=m`, when `N^q=0`. It is a proof for every positive `d`, rather than empirical evidence for a spectrum. It strengthens the homology control but supplies no virtual-specialness conclusion.

## Reproduction and limitations

`python3 independent_height_check.py` passes using only the Python standard library. It does not import or execute the author's checker. The independent code builds the matrix by binary matrix multiplication, computes determinants by sparse-row Laplace expansion, and checks mod-prime nullities using column bases. These mechanisms differ from the author's binomial-entry construction, Bareiss determinants, and row elimination.

The run independently reproduces all **84** recorded Smith cases (`1<=m<=7`, `1<=d<=12`), all **100** author prime-power cases, the elementary-abelian special cases, and the `(3,2)` exception. It additionally checks **4,320** modular cases (`1<=m<=24`, `1<=d<=30`, six primes through 13) against the general formula above. These finite computations check implementation and boundary examples. The infinite claims rest on the proofs in this audit and the note.

The source audit read the complete relevant statements in the local primary-source caches authenticated by the author's source manifest: Dison–Riley's Hydra presentation and Carr's Theorem 1.2. This review does not certify the broader current priority audit for Kourovka 21.125, and does not constitute a new literature claim.
