## Outstanding corrections

1. **Coset order.** In `FIBER_ABELIAN_COVER_OBSTRUCTION.md`, the s-midpoint
   component through coordinate g consists of g rho(P_r), obtained by right
   multiplication by transport elements. In standard group terminology these
   are **left** cosets. The invariant unambiguous criterion is
   g^{-1}h in rho(P_r). The frozen text says “right coset”, which generally
   asserts a different equivalence. This is not only a possible naming dispute:
   in the rank-two order-eight quotient with fiber (Z/2)^2 and height Z/2,
   P={1,a_1 s}. Taking g=a_2 and h=a_2 a_1 s gives g^{-1}h in P but
   h g^{-1}=s not in P. Thus the corresponding s-edges are joined by transport
   while standard right cosets distinguish them. The independently computed
   coordinates are recorded in `independent_results.json`. The obstruction's
   use of the identity coset and element membership remains correct.

2. **Parameter domain of the special analogues.** Explicitly impose m>=1 and
   0<=k<=m in `SPECIAL_ANALOGUE_LIFT.md`. This is the domain in the primary
   Brady–Soroko definition (§5.1, author-copy text lines 862–868). The displayed
   quotient is valid on this domain. If one extends the presentation beyond
   it without qualification, the asserted quotient fails: for j=m+1, a_j maps
   to u_1 instead of s. For example m=1,k=2 would require both
   u_3^{-1}u_1 u_3=u_2 and the Hydra relation u_3^{-1}s u_3=u_2, forcing
   u_1=s, false in the ordinary Hydra group. The missing domain does not
   restrict the intended arbitrary target rank, since one may choose m>=k.

3. **Second failed section requires k>=1.** In the same note, the second
   obstruction uses the first new stable letter a_(m+2). Explicitly state
   m>=2 and 1<=k<=m there. For k=0 no such letter or relation exists; the
   adjacent pair a_m,a_(m+1) does embed the rank-one Hydra Z^2.

4. **Sufficiency is not equivalence.** The final sentence of the same note
   calls a missing section “equivalent to the central embedding difficulty”.
   The argument proves only that a section into a virtually special analogue
   would be a sufficient mechanism. It does not prove that every virtually
   special Hydra group admits a section into this particular family. Replace
   “equivalent” by an explicitly unproved route-specific sufficient condition,
   or just state that no section has been constructed. This correction is
   necessary to avoid silently claiming an unsupported converse.

## Independent mathematical verification

### Marked HNN embedding

The lemma is correct, including for finite cyclic edge subgroups. Because
v^{-1}pv=q in A and B injects in A, the cyclic subgroups generated by p and q
have matching order and define the source HNN extension. Write a reduced
source normal form with stable letters t^{epsilon}. Under t -> zv, successive
opposite stable letters have intervening base coefficients b for the pair
z^{-1} ... z, and v b v^{-1} for z ... z^{-1}. A target pinch therefore requires
b in <p>, respectively v b v^{-1} in <p>, equivalent to b in <q>. These are
precisely the prohibited source pinches. Same-sign stable letters never form
a Britton pinch. Outer coefficients cause no new cancellation. A nontrivial
base word stays nontrivial by the assumed embedding. This establishes the
injection with all normal-form cases addressed.

If p=h x^{+/-1}h^{-1} for a standard RAAG vertex x, replace z by h^{-1}zh.
The target presentation then adjoins one vertex commuting with x, hence is
again a RAAG. Without the marked conjugacy hypothesis this argument does not
give an induction.

### Index-two rank-two subgroup and its injection

The cover is **top-letter parity**, not height parity. Its transversal is
{1,a}. Schreier generators may be taken as y=s, w=u, x=asa^{-1},
v=aua^{-1}, r=a^2. The two lifts of [s,u] give [y,w] and [x,v]. The two lifts
of a^{-1}s a=u give v=y and r^{-1}x r=w. Eliminating v yields exactly the
stated presentation. Parity is a surjection, so the subgroup has index two.

The subgroup generated by p and c^{-1}pc in F(p,c) is free on those two
elements. A nonempty alternating product of nonzero powers expands into a
word whose c transitions between the two distinct conjugate cyclic factors
cannot cancel. Thus F(x,w) times <y> injects in F(p,c) times <b> as stated.
The marked lemma with v=c sends r to zc; its relation becomes
c^{-1}z^{-1}pzc=c^{-1}pc. The target graph is the path c--b--p--z. The
injection is rigorous, rather than inferred from finite tests.

For this particular map, s=y maps to b and a^2=r maps to zc. The
abelianization vectors of b^2 and zc differ. Conjugating the image cannot
change those vectors, and a common automorphism induces an invertible map
on abelianization, preserving their inequality. This excludes only the
proposed marked continuation in that target.

### Finite-cover balancing

For every r>=1 the abelianization of H_r is Z^2 on s,u_r: each lower u_i
becomes s, with no relation between s and the top letter. Core(L) gives a
finite quotient Q_0. In the image Q of (pi,chi), with chi(s)=(1,0) and
chi(u_r)=(0,1) modulo a common multiple N of their Q_0 orders, both indicated
elements have exact order N. Their regular permutations have identical cycle
structure. A permutation conjugator therefore supplies the new stable
letter and defines a homomorphism of the whole next HNN extension. Its
kernel J is finite-index normal and restricts on H_r to the kernel of
H_r -> Q, hence lies inside L on that vertex group.

Normality supplies conjugate vertex stabilizers throughout the induced
finite Bass–Serre quotient. Edge stabilizers are infinite cyclic and their
intersection indices are exactly N. Finite covers preserve specialness and,
separately, compactness. No special-combination claim follows merely from
having special vertex groups and cyclic edge groups; the note correctly
leaves that missing step open.

### Top cyclic malnormality and stable-letter centralizer

For r>=2, if g a_r^k g^{-1}=a_r^l, abelianization forces k=l. With g=w t^n,
free-group unique roots then show phi^{-n}(a_r) conjugate to a_r. Its
a_(r-1) exponent is -n in the free fiber's abelianization, forcing n=0.
The centralizer of a_r^k in the free group is <a_r>, so g lies in <a_r>.
The restriction r>=2 is essential and is present in the note.

Both a_1 and a_2 a_1 a_2^{-1} are fixed by phi; they freely generate a
rank-two subgroup by reduced free-word normal forms. They commute with t,
and the free fiber intersects <t> trivially, giving the displayed
F_2 times Z. In every finite-index subgroup suitable nonzero powers of
these three elements remain; the first two still freely generate F_2.
Thus the relevant cyclic stable-letter intersection does not become
malnormal after passing to finite index. This defeats only an induction
requiring both incident cyclic groups to be malnormal.

### Special analogue quotient and failed sections

the quotient. For m>=2 the first proposed section fails because the

### Canonical transport and fiber-abelian obstruction

The boundary of a square based at g is s u_i u_(i-1)^{-1}u_i^{-1}.
Opposite u_i sides connect coordinates g and gs; opposite s,u_(i-1)
sides connect g and gu_i. A star joining midpoint type s to each lower
u_i therefore has outward transport u_(i+1). Bringing its s-loop back to
the root gives u_(i+1) s u_(i+1)^{-1}. The s-type loop from the first
square has transport u_1. These are exactly P_r; this derivation checks the
basepoint and inverse conventions rather than assuming the hyperplane is
embedded. The only coset issue is the terminology identified above.

In M_r=Z^r semidirect <t>, with multiplication
(v,k)(w,l)=(v+phi^{-k}w,k+l), direct substitution gives
u^{-1}s u=(phi^k Jv,-1). Recursive substitution yields
U=u_1=(e_1,-1) and V=u_r s u_r^{-1}=(-e_(r-1),-1).
Their commutator is (-e_(r-2),0). Conjugation by V acts on the fiber
by phi, so r-3 further commutators [V,-] give (-e_1,0). Multiplication
by U recovers s. The element V s^{-1} gives (-e_(r-1),0), and successive
commutators with s recover all smaller basis vectors. Thus the image of
P_r contains s and the entire lower rank-(r-1) fiber. Conversely every
generator of P_r has zero top coordinate, so in fact its image is exactly
Z^(r-1) semidirect <t>.

All u_i for i<r are in that subgroup. Passing to any quotient of M_r
preserves the explicit membership identities. For i<=r-2, therefore,
rho(u_(i+1)) belongs to rho(P_r); the adjacent s,u_i sides of the square
are in a single lifted hyperplane. This proves self-crossing in every such
square of each regular canonical cover in the stated family. It does not
preclude a different finite quotient, a different cube complex, or virtual
specialness of the group.

Primary definition checked: Noel Brady and Ignat Soroko, *Dehn functions of
subgroups of right-angled Artin groups*, §5.1, author copy dated August 28,
2020, [author-hosted PDF](https://sites.math.unt.edu/~soroko/Dehn-functions-RAAGs.pdf),
[arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04066). The locally inspected text

The family is named G_(m,k) in the primary source; renaming it B_(m,k)
to avoid confusion is harmless when explicit.

The literal specialness definition does not require compactness. A subgroup
of a RAAG is the fundamental group of the corresponding cover of its
Salvetti complex, which remains special. Conversely, a special complex's
local isometry to a Salvetti gives a RAAG injection. Thus an actual
finite-index RAAG injection would suffice for this literal virtual-special
target; no extra compactness claim is needed. Finite generation allows a
finite ambient defining graph by restricting to the generator-image support.
