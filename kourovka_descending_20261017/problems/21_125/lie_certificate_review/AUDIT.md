## Why the group passage is legitimate

For positive-degree u, exp(u) and log(1+u) are the truncated series through degree 10. All their denominators are units in F101. The identities that make exp and log inverse hold in this nilpotent associative algebra. The truncated BCH formula is Lie-valued, so exp(R) is a subgroup of 1+A. Its inclusion in that unit group is faithful because log is its inverse.

The BCH formula applies because the nilpotency class is at most 10 and 10<101. One primary statement of the required correspondence is Garaialde Ocaña–González-Sánchez, *Transporting cohomology in Lazard correspondence*, §2, Theorem 1(c,d). It identifies subgroups with Lie subalgebras and normal subgroups with Lie ideals in class smaller than p. Finite F101 Lie algebras are finite Z101 Lie algebras, within the theorem's hypotheses. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.4654v2#S2).

If I is a Lie ideal of R, then exp(I) is normal in exp(R). Independently, this follows directly from

    exp(−u) exp(v) exp(u) = exp(exp(−ad u)(v)),

where v∈I, u∈R, and every term on the right remains in I. Therefore the finite group Q=exp(R)/exp(I) exists and has order 101^(dim R−dim I). Equivalently it is the BCH group of R/I. **A Lie ideal is not being treated as an associative ideal:** there is no asserted realization of this quotient by simply forming the associative algebra A/I. Only exp(R), before quotienting, is embedded in the displayed associative unit group.

The recursive Hydra relations hold before quotienting. The final commutation relator belongs to exp(I) because its logarithm r belongs to I. Thus the displayed generators define a homomorphism G5→Q. This homomorphism is surjective: for H=⟨exp(x),exp(y)⟩≤exp(R), the subgroup correspondence makes log(H) a Lie subalgebra containing x,y. Since they generate R, H=exp(R). Surjectivity persists in Q. Hence the associated cover is connected, regular, and has precisely |Q| sheets. Without this generation argument one should claim only the image subgroup and an upper bound on its order.

As I⊆W and W is a Lie subalgebra, exp(W)/exp(I) is a subgroup of Q containing the image of the hyperplane subgroup L5. If log(aj)∉W, then aj exp(I)∉exp(W)/exp(I): otherwise the Lie-quotient coordinate log(aj)+I would lie in W/I, which would force log(aj)∈W. Thus every proposed separation survives imposing the Hydra relation. This is the decisive containment check.

I independently checked the author's new inter-osculation criterion. Write a=alpha_(m−1), b=alpha_m, and use left cosets gH for the lifts of the base hyperplane. The positive alpha_(m−2) and alpha_(m−1) germs at the identity have hyperplanes a^−1 H and b^−1 H. They cannot bound a square: writing the square boundary as s alpha_i alpha_(i−1)^−1 alpha_i^−1 shows that its only pair of two distinct positive labels at a corner is s+,alpha_i+.

Square m−1 supplies the crossing pair H,b^−1 H. Translating by a^−1 gives a^−1 H,a^−1 b^−1 H. The relation a=b^−1 s b implies b a^−1 b^−1=s^−1. As q(s)∈q(L), the second translated hyperplane is b^−1 H. They are distinct: equality a^−1 H=b^−1 H would imply b a^−1=s^−1 b∈q(L), and multiplication by q(s) would imply q(b)∈q(L), contradicting its dual separation. Thus these two distinct hyperplanes cross somewhere and have osculating germs at the identity. This is an inter-osculation.

For m=5 the germs are alpha_3+ and alpha_4+.
