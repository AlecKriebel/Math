# Exact generator and homology controls for Hydra height covers

Candidate partial control calculation, October24,2026 UTC. Fresh adversarial review is pending. No originality or full-problem claim.

Let G_m=F(a1,…,am)⋊Z with t^-1 a1 t=a1 and t^-1 ai t=ai a_(i−1). Write θ for this triangular automorphism. The homomorphism h:G_m→Z sends t to1 and every ai to0. For d≥1 put H_(m,d)=h^-1(dZ), an index-d subgroup.

## Direct embeddings and the indispensable virtual quantifier

For m≥2, G_m is generated by am,t: recursively a_(i−1)=ai^-1 t^-1 ai t. It is nonabelian because [a2,t]=a1≠1 in the embedded free fiber. It contains Z²=⟨a1,t⟩: these elements commute, and projection to h shows their powers are independent. Thus G_m is neither free nor abelian. Baudisch's two-generator theorem therefore excludes an injective homomorphism G_m→any RAAG. This is a credited standard application of that theorem, not a virtual obstruction.

The subgroups below show explicitly why the minimum generator number cannot simply be carried into finite index.

## Integral homology formula

Let J_m=I+N, where N has1 on the first superdiagonal and0 elsewhere. On the abelianization of the free fiber, θ induces J_m. The group H_(m,d) has the presentation of F_m⋊_(θ^d)⟨u=t^d⟩. Abelianizing gives

    H1(H_(m,d);Z) ≅ Z⟨u⟩ ⊕ coker(J_m^d−I).

For m=1 this is Z² for every d. For m≥2,

    H1(H_(m,d);Z) ≅ Z² ⊕ T_(m,d),
    |T_(m,d)| = d^(m−1).

Indeed J_m^d−I is strictly upper triangular, its first-superdiagonal entries all equal d, and it has rank m−1 over Q. The only possibly nonzero (m−1)-rowed minor is obtained by deleting the last row and first column: any other such minor retains either the zero last row or the zero first column. That remaining minor is upper triangular with diagonal d and determinant d^(m−1). The top determinantal divisor is the product of nonzero Smith invariants, hence the order of the torsion subgroup. The rank calculation leaves one free fiber class, plus the stable-letter class.

Consequently b1(H_(m,d);Q)=2 for every m,d≥1. This alone does not bound its minimum generator number from above.

## Exact generator numbers for specified covers

Let p be prime and q=p^r≥m. In characteristic p the binomial identity gives

    J_m^q=(I+N)^(p^r)=I+N^(p^r)=I,

because N^m=0. Hence

    dim_Fp H1(H_(m,q);Fp)=m+1.

The presentation on a1,…,am,u supplies m+1 generators. Mod-p homology requires at least m+1. Therefore the minimum number of generators of H_(m,q) is **exactly m+1**. This covers every m by choosing a sufficiently large prime power, including power-of-two covers.

In the especially transparent case d=p≥m, the torsion order p^(m−1), together with J_m^p−I≡0 modp and rank m−1, gives

    T_(m,p) ≅ (Z/p)^(m−1).

The rank restriction matters. For m=3,d=2 the nonzero Smith invariants are1,4, so T≅Z/4 and the mod2 first Betti number is3, rather than4. Thus one must not apply the elementary-abelian conclusion to every prime regardless of rank.

## Interpretation and remaining gap

The original group is two-generated, while explicit finite-index subgroups have minimum generator number m+1. Thus the direct two-generator RAAG obstruction does not settle the virtual question. The cover calculation does not assert that any of these subgroups embeds in a RAAG or is special. Torsion in its abelianization is not treated as an embedding obstruction.

All cyclic height covers still contain the entire free fiber, so their intersection cannot by itself form a residual separating chain for G_m. This does not prevent a positive virtual construction using a single height cover: an actual special realization or RAAG embedding of one such cover would suffice. The homology computation proves neither an embedding nor a nonembedding of these covers. No conclusion about arbitrary finite covers, virtual specialness, or the unresolved m≥5 cases is obtained here.
