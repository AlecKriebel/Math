## 1. The virtual target does not require compactness

For a finitely generated group G, the following virtual properties are equivalent under the literal noncompact convention printed before Notebook21.125:

1. Some finite-index subgroup is the fundamental group of a special cube complex.
2. Some finite-index subgroup embeds in a right-angled Artin group on a finite graph.

There is a terminology qualification. In the corrected original Haglund–Wise paper, Definition3.2 distinguishes **A-special** (including two-sided hyperplanes) from **special** (which permits one-sided hyperplanes). The unqualified assertion that every group of a special complex in that original weaker sense itself embeds in a RAAG should not be substituted for their theorem. Theorem4.2 gives the local-isometry characterization for A-special complexes. Modern treatments often use “special” for that A-special condition.

Here is why the virtual equivalence still holds for the original weaker definition, even for a noncompact complex with infinitely many hyperplanes. Let X be such a complex with finitely generated fundamental group K, generated by k elements. Each hyperplane W gives a homomorphism ell_W:K→F_2 counting intersections of an edge loop with W modulo2. Square homotopies preserve parity because a square has either zero or two edges dual to W, and self-intersection is excluded. The simultaneous homomorphism

    ell: K → product_W F_2

has elementary abelian image generated by at most k elements, hence finite of order at most2^k. Thus the intersection of all its coordinate kernels has finite index. The proof of Haglund–Wise Proposition3.10 applies to this cover: an orientation-reversing parallelism chain would give a closed path crossing its own hyperplane exactly once, contradicting membership in the kernel. All lifted hyperplanes are therefore two-sided. Covers preserve the other specialness conditions (their Corollary3.8), so this finite cover is A-special. This argument replaces the original proposition's finite-number-of-walls hypothesis by finite generation of the fundamental group; it does not assume compactness. If needed, take the nonpositively curved completion, which leaves the2-skeleton and fundamental group unchanged.

For completeness, one can avoid an infinite-dimensional CAT(0) lifting issue. The A-special typing map f:X'→Sal(Gamma) is a combinatorial local isometry. Choose finitely many based edge loops generating pi_1(X'), and let Gamma_0 be the finite induced subgraph containing every label appearing along those loops. Let Y be the component of f^-1(Sal(Gamma_0)) containing the basepoint. It contains the generating loops, so pi_1(Y)→pi_1(X') is onto. The restriction Y→Sal(Gamma_0) is a local isometry: the induced subgraph gives a full subcomplex and the link condition is inherited. Its finite-dimensional CAT(0) universal-cover lift is an isometric embedding, so pi_1(Y) injects in A(Gamma_0). If an element of pi_1(Y) dies in pi_1(X'), its typing image dies in A(Gamma); it then dies in A(Gamma_0), since this induced-subgraph RAAG is a retract of A(Gamma). Injectivity of the restriction proves that it was trivial in pi_1(Y). Thus pi_1(Y)≅pi_1(X') embeds in the finite-graph RAAG A(Gamma_0). This proves (1)⇒(2), allowing a further finite-index subgroup in the original terminology.

Conversely, if H embeds in A(Gamma), the cover of the Salvetti complex corresponding to H is a nonpositively curved A-special cube complex, with fundamental group H. Covering preserves specialness. The cover need not be compact. This proves (2)⇒(1).

The printed preceding problem21.124(b) calls virtual RAAG embedding “a weaker question”, although its definition of virtually special does not add compactness. Record this as a source/convention issue; do not silently impose compactness or attribute an undocumented intention to a proposer. The equivalence above is for the literal noncompact virtual target. A hypothetical embedding in a finite RAAG suffices for the affirmative direction; failure of a particular compact cubulation does not disprove it.

## 2. Every cyclic height cover contains a copy of G_2

Let m≥2 and d≥1. Let H_(m,d) be the inverse image of dZ under the usual height map G_m→Z. Put

    A=a_1^d, B=a_2, u=t^d.

The defining automorphism fixes a_1 and sends a_2 to a_2 a_1, so

    u^-1 A u=A,       u^-1 B u=B A.

The subgroup of the free fiber generated by A and B is free on these two elements: a reduced alternating product of nonzero powers of a_1^d and a_2 is a nonempty reduced word in the original free basis. It is preserved by conjugation by u and u^-1. Height and free-fiber normal forms then prove that

    <A,B,u> ≅ F(A,B) semidirect Z ≅ G_2.

In particular, A=B^-1 u^-1 B u, so this subgroup is generated by B,u. It is nonabelian because [B,u]=A≠1, and it contains the rank-two free abelian subgroup <A,u>; height and free-fiber normal forms establish their independence. It is therefore neither free nor abelian. Baudisch's theorem on two-generator subgroups of RAAGs excludes its embedding in a RAAG. Consequently **no H_(m,d) embeds in a RAAG** for m≥2.

This conclusion does not follow from the earlier homology calculation. The reviewed statement that homology alone does not exclude an individual height-cover embedding is correct. The present independent subgroup obstruction excludes that family by another argument. It does not exclude other finite-index subgroups; known small-rank positive results already require such a distinction.

More generally, any finite-index subgroup containing a_2 contains t^d for some d and hence contains the displayed G_2. Conjugating shows that a RAAG-embedding subgroup must omit every conjugate of a_2. The negative family's separately proved centralizer-coset filter strengthens this necessary condition; these observations are compatible, not separate full solutions.

For m≥3, a_2=[a_3,t] lies in G_m', so every subgroup containing G_m' fails this test. In particular no finite abelian-quotient cover can be the desired RAAG subgroup. In general

    a_2=[a_m,{}_(m-2)t] ∈ gamma_(m-1)(G_m).

If a finite-index **normal** subgroup embeds in a RAAG and its quotient is nilpotent, that quotient must have class at least m−1. For m≥3 such a quotient is nonabelian. If it is a finite p-group of order p^e, the elementary nonabelian bound class≤e−1 then gives e≥m. The m≥3 restriction matters: the cyclic order-two quotient in the known rank-two construction is a boundary counterexample to the unqualified degree bound. This last degree bound is for regular covers with a p-group quotient; it must not be asserted for an arbitrary nonnormal subgroup whose index is a p-power.

Baudisch's theorem and the original Hydra free-by-cyclic normal form are prior results. The primary statements already authenticated in SOURCE_MANIFEST.json are Dison–Riley(2013), pp.510–511, and Carr, arXiv:1412.0642v2, Theorem1.2.

## 3. Exact generator number in prime-power height covers

The fresh homology reviewer independently proved, for every m,d≥1 and prime p,

    b_1(H_(m,d);F_p)=1+min(m,p^v_p(d)).

For d=q=p^r (including q=1), this gives a lower bound1+min(m,q) on the minimum number of generators. The semidirect presentation gives an upper bound m+1. The ambient G_m is two-generated (also G_1=Z²), so Schreier's bound for its index-q subgroup gives an upper bound q+1. Hence the exact minimum number is

    d(H_(m,q))=1+min(m,q).

This is a small strengthening of the frozen control note. Its lower bound uses the proof in root_controls_review/AUDIT.md, rather than an extrapolation from finite matrix checks. It provides no positive embedding construction and no obstruction to virtual specialness beyond the particular height covers considered in Section2.

## Remaining gap

No finite-index RAAG embedding has been constructed for every m≥5, and no obstruction valid for every finite-index subgroup has been obtained for a particular such rank. Canonical hyperplane filters, nilpotency bounds and homology formulas do not close that gap. No paper, new DOI, tracker entry or solved_verified label is justified.
