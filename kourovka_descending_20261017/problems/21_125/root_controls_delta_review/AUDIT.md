## Independent check of the infinite-wall parity argument

Every hyperplane \(W\) of an original-sense special complex has a mod-two intersection character \(\ell_W\). A square contributes either zero or two boundary edges to any one wall, because embeddedness forbids two distinct crossing midsegments of that wall. Thus the parity cochain is closed; it defines a homomorphism from the fundamental group. Orientation is irrelevant here, so one-sided hyperplanes cause no gap.

If \(K\) is generated by \(k\) elements, the image of

\[
\ell:K\longrightarrow\prod_W\mathbb F_2
\]

is spanned over \(\mathbb F_2\) by at most \(k\) vectors and has at most \(2^k\) elements. This remains true with infinitely many coordinates. No finite-wall or compactness assumption is being smuggled into the finite-index assertion. The kernel \(K_0\) therefore defines a finite cover.

For clarity, the two-sidedness contradiction is as follows. An orientation-reversing parallelism chain in that cover, together with one initial dual edge, closes to a loop. Each step along a side of a square is dual to a wall different from the initial wall: otherwise that square would be a self-intersection of the projected original wall. The closed loop consequently meets the initial projected wall once modulo two. Yet its projection represents an element of \(K_0\), where every such character vanishes. This is impossible. Every lifted wall is two-sided. The remaining original specialness properties pass to covers by Corollary 3.8, so the new cover is A-special. If a cube completion is needed, Lemma 3.13 leaves the 2-skeleton and fundamental group unchanged.

This really proves the required extension of the finite-wall argument for finitely generated fundamental groups. It does not assert the extension for arbitrary infinitely generated groups. For the virtually special group in the delta, the starting subgroup and its further finite-index parity kernel are finitely generated by Schreier's bound.

## Finite typing graph and the repaired lifting argument

The initial proof invoked a CAT(0) lift before addressing a possibly infinite-dimensional typing Salvetti. I requested an explicit finite-dimensional reduction, since Haglund–Wise Lemma 2.11 includes finite dimensionality. The revised proof provides it correctly.

Let \(f:X'\to\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma)\) be the A-special typing map. Choose finite based edge loops generating \(\pi_1X'\), and let \(\Gamma_0\) be the finite induced graph on their edge labels. The connected preimage component \(Y\) of \(\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma_0)\) contains all those loops, so \(\pi_1Y\to\pi_1X'\) is onto. The induced-subgraph Salvetti has full links, hence the restriction \(Y\to\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma_0)\) is a local isometry into a finite-dimensional NPC complex. Its fundamental-group map is injective. If a class of \(\pi_1Y\) dies in \(\pi_1X'\), its typing word dies in \(A(\Gamma)\), and therefore in \(A(\Gamma_0)\), since the latter is a retract. Injectivity of the restriction forces the original class to be trivial. Thus \(\pi_1Y\cong\pi_1X'\) and embeds in the finite-graph RAAG.

Conversely an arbitrary subgroup of a finite RAAG is the fundamental group of the corresponding Salvetti cover, which is NPC and A-special; the cover may be noncompact. This proves the asserted virtual equivalence under both the modern A-special and the original weaker HW convention. It proves no compact-special equivalence.

## The embedded \(G_2\) in every cyclic height cover

I independently checked the original free-by-cyclic presentation and the commutator convention \([x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\). For \(m\ge2,d\ge1\), write

\[
A=a_1^d,\qquad B=a_2,\qquad u=t^d.
\]

The automorphism fixes \(A\) and sends \(B\) to \(BA\) under conjugation by \(u^{-1}\). Its inverse sends \(B\) to \(BA^{-1}\), so the free subgroup \(F(A,B)\) is invariant in both directions. A nontrivial reduced alternating word in powers of \(A\) and \(B\) remains a nontrivial reduced word in the original free fiber; thus this is genuinely a free basis of that subgroup. Height gives independence of \(u\), so semidirect-product normal forms prove an injective copy of the full \(G_2\), not merely a homomorphic image satisfying its relators.

The relation \(A=B^{-1}u^{-1}Bu\) makes this subgroup two-generated. It is nonabelian because \(A\ne1\), and \(\langle A,u\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\) by height and fiber normal forms. Hence it is neither free nor abelian. Carr's Theorem 1.2 states Baudisch's two-generator RAAG subgroup theorem, so the asserted nonembedding follows. Infinite defining graphs cause no exception: the two images use a finite induced subgraph. [Carr's primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.0642v2), [Dison–Riley's published presentation](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43323?nt=1).

The same proof applies to a finite-index subgroup containing \(a_2\), since it contains a nontrivial positive power of \(t\). Conjugating that subgroup proves the exclusion of every conjugate of \(a_2\). This does not exclude every finite-index subgroup and does not conflict with known positive small ranks.

## Coabelian covers and nilpotent quotient bounds

For \(m\ge3\), \(a_2=[a_3,t]\) belongs to the commutator subgroup, so every coabelian subgroup contains the forbidden \(a_2\). This is an algebraic RAAG nonembedding obstruction; it is stronger in scope than the earlier canonical-cubulation abelian-monodromy obstruction, without supplying a full virtual obstruction.

Repeatedly applying \([a_i,t]=a_{i-1}\) gives

\[
a_2=[a_m,{}_{m-2}t]\in\gamma_{m-1}(G_m).
\]

A RAAG-embeddable normal kernel must omit \(a_2\). If its quotient is nilpotent of class \(c\), this forces \(c\ge m-1\). For \(m\ge3\), \(c\ge2\), so the quotient is nonabelian. A finite nonabelian \(p\)-group of class \(c\) has an abelianization of order at least \(p^2\) and at least \(c-1\) nontrivial successive lower-central factors below it; hence its order is at least \(p^{c+1}\). The abelianization assertion follows, for example, because cyclic mod-\(p\) abelianization gives a cyclic group by the Frattini generator theorem. Therefore \(|Q|=p^e\) requires \(e\ge c+1\ge m\).

The normality, quotient, and rank restrictions are necessary and correctly stated. In rank 2, the canonical degree-two action \(S=1,\alpha_2=(0\ 1)\) has an A-special cover; an independent full incidence check confirms it. Thus one cannot extend \(e\ge m\) to \(m=2\). Nor may the bound be attached merely to the index of an arbitrary nonnormal subgroup with \(p\)-power index: its coset-action image need not have that order. The delta explicitly avoids that error.

## Exact generator number and independent checks

I rederived the general modular formula rather than treating the previous review's receipt as a proof. Write \(d=qs\), \(q=p^{v_p(d)}\), \(p\nmid s\), and \(J=I+N\), where \(N\) is the single nilpotent Jordan shift of size \(m\). In characteristic \(p\),

\[
J^d-I=N^q\bigl(sI+\tbinom{s}{2}N^q+\cdots\bigr).
\]

The second factor is invertible because it is the nonzero scalar \(s\) times the identity plus a nilpotent polynomial. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(J^d-I)=\max(m-q,0)\), including \(q\ge m\). Abelianization of the semidirect presentation gives

\[
b_1(H_{m,d};\mathbb F_p)=1+\min(m,q).
\]

For height index \(d=q\) a prime power, this is a generator lower bound. The semidirect presentation gives \(m+1\) generators; Schreier applied to the two-generated \(G_m\) gives \(q+1\). Their minimum equals the lower bound, proving exactly \(d(H_{m,q})=1+\min(m,q)\). Rank 1 and exponent zero \(q=1\) both give two generators, correctly. The argument does not claim an exact formula from one mod-prime lower bound for arbitrary composite index.

The standard-library script `independent_delta_check.py`, importing no author checker, separately checks 5,820 reduced free-word images including the identity, their distinctness and both twist directions; 40,740 height normal forms; and 600 modular homology cases, including composite indices and rank/prime boundaries. All pass. These finite tests check conventions and implementation; the infinite conclusions rest on the arguments above.
