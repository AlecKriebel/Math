## 2. Automorphism and iterates, independently derived

Let `F=F(a,b,c)` and set

\[
\alpha(a)=a,\qquad \alpha(b)=ba,\qquad \alpha(c)=ca^2,
\qquad \varphi(x)=b\alpha(x)b^{-1}.
\]

The map `α` is an automorphism: its inverse fixes `a` and sends `b` to `ba⁻¹` and `c` to `ca⁻²`, as direct substitution in both orders shows. Conjugation is an automorphism, so `φ` is also an automorphism, with inverse `α⁻¹∘c_(b⁻¹)`.

Write

\[
p_n=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}ba^i,\qquad p_0=1.
\]

The identity `φⁿ=c_(p_n)∘αⁿ` holds for every `n≥0`: if it holds at `n`, then composition with `φ` on the right gives conjugation by `p_n αⁿ(b)=p_n baⁿ=p_(n+1)`, followed by `α^(n+1)`. Also `αⁿ(a)=a`, `αⁿ(b)=baⁿ`, `αⁿ(c)=ca^(2n)`.

For `n≥1`, let `q_n=p_(n−1)`. The positive word `p_n=q_n b a^(n−1)` has length `n(n+1)/2`, and `q_n` has length `n(n−1)/2`. Exact free reduction gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varphi^n(a)&=q_n b a b^{-1}q_n^{-1},\\
\varphi^n(b)&=p_n b a b^{-1}q_n^{-1},\\
\varphi^n(c)&=p_n c a^{n+1}b^{-1}q_n^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

All displayed words are freely reduced, including `n=1`, when `q_n` is empty. At a nonempty `q_n` junction the adjacent letters are powers of `a` followed by `b`, or a negative `b` followed by negative powers of `a`; the `n=2` terminal `q_n=b` only gives adjacent equal negative `b` letters. None is an inverse pair. Therefore, for every integer `n≥1`,

\[
|\varphi^n(a)|=n^2-n+3,\quad
|\varphi^n(b)|=n^2+3,\quad
|\varphi^n(c)|=n^2+n+3.
\]

Thus the maximum is exactly `n²+n+3`, and hence has degree `2=m−1` for `m=3`. In particular every fixed word has polynomial growth, by substituting its letters and using the triangle inequality. If a definition requires both signs of iterates, the inverse has quadratic growth as well: direct use of `φ⁻¹=c_(a b⁻¹)∘α⁻¹` gives, for `n≥1`,

\[
|\varphi^{-n}(a)|=n^2+3n+1,\qquad
|\varphi^{-n}(c)|=n^2+5n+1.
\]

Here the conjugating word is `(a b⁻¹)(a² b⁻¹)⋯(aⁿ b⁻¹)`; these formulas follow by the same free-reduction argument. All inverse basis images have an immediate quadratic upper bound from this formula. For the more cancellative `b` image, the exact length is `2` at `n=1` and `n²−1` at `n≥2`: writing `r=(a b⁻¹)⋯(a^(n−2)b⁻¹)`, its reduced form is `r a⁻¹ b a^(-(n−1))r⁻¹`. These facts are auxiliary: only positive iterates are used in the printed hypothesis.

For completeness, growth degree does not depend on the chosen free basis. For finite bases `X,Y`, express each `Y`-letter as a bounded-length `X`-word and each `X`-letter as a bounded-length `Y`-word. Applying the first substitution before `φⁿ` and the second afterwards bounds `max_Y|φⁿ(y)|_Y` by a constant multiple of `max_X|φⁿ(x)|_X`. Interchanging `X,Y` gives the reverse inequality.

## 3. Mapping-torus isomorphism and the prior theorem

Use the convention

\[
T_\alpha=\langle F,t\mid txt^{-1}=\alpha(x),\ x\in\{a,b,c\}\rangle.
\]

Inside this group put `s=bt`. Then `sxs⁻¹=bα(x)b⁻¹=φ(x)`. The identity on `F` and `s↦bt` therefore defines a homomorphism `T_φ→T_α`. Its inverse is the identity on `F` and `t↦b⁻¹s`; the defining relations are respected since `b⁻¹φ(x)b=α(x)`. The two substitutions are inverse on every generator, proving an actual isomorphism, rather than just an embedding or quotient. Choosing the opposite stable-letter convention only replaces `t` by its inverse.

The resulting `T_α` has precisely Gersten's presentation. The [author-hosted Brady–Soroko paper dated August28,2020](https://sites.math.unt.edu/~soroko/Dehn-functions-RAAGs.pdf), Appendix, printed pp.39–42, proves that no finite-index subgroup of this exact group embeds in a RAAG (Corollary10.3); in particular it is not virtually special. This is a statement about every finite-index subgroup and does not require any degree assumption on `α`. The July2021 questions note repeats the same conclusion on p.1. No mismatch of stable-letter convention or monodromy presentation was found.

The mechanism is consistent with the classical obstruction: in `T_α`, `t,at,a²t` are conjugate, while `a,t` generate an algebraic copy of `Z²`. Under a proper semisimple CAT(0) action the flat torus theorem supplies translation vectors for this `Z²`; equality of the three squared translation lengths forces the vector for `a` to vanish, contradicting properness. The virtual extension is the induced-action argument proved in Proposition10.2 of the cited Appendix. This paragraph checks the mechanism but does not claim a new theorem or replace the cited external CAT(0) results with computation.

Since virtual specialness and virtual RAAG embeddability are invariant under isomorphism, `T_φ` is not virtually special. This defeats the universal assertion in the **literal ordinary-growth part(a)**.

## 4. Outer growth and the untouched part(b)

The words `φⁿ(x)` and `αⁿ(x)` are conjugate. The exact cyclically reduced lengths of their basis images are `1,n+1,2n+1`. Moreover `|αⁿ(w)|≤(2n+1)|w|` for every fixed word `w`. Thus all fixed conjugacy classes grow at most linearly, and the class of `b` grows linearly. The outer automorphism class has linear rather than quadratic growth. A question imposing maximal **outer** growth would therefore reject this example.

The example does not have Hydra monodromy. Its mapping torus is the old Gersten group and has the old CAT(0) obstruction; no conclusion about the Hydra groups in part(b) follows. Small Hydra cases and the remaining range require their own source and mathematical audits. This review does not establish current status beyond the statement that the present construction gives no answer to them.
