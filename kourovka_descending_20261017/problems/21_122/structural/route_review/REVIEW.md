## 1. Closed abstract verbal subgroup and finite width

Fix a finite group word w in k variables. Let X=G_w, put
Y=X union X^-1 union {1}, and let D be the abstract subgroup generated by X.
The set X is compact as a continuous image of compact G^k; therefore every
Y^n is compact and closed in the Hausdorff group G. Here Y^0={1}, and the
inclusion of1 in Y lets all shorter products be padded to a common length.

The exact assertion is

    D closed in G  <=>  there is one m>=0 with D=Y^m.

The reverse implication is compactness. For the forward implication D is a
compact group and is the countable union of its closed subsets Y^n. By Baire,
some Y^n contains a nonempty relative-open set O in D. Choose g in O. Then
g^-1 O is an identity neighborhood contained in Y^(2n), using symmetry of Y.
An open subgroup U of D can be chosen inside this neighborhood. Compactness
gives finitely many U-cosets; choose their representatives in D and bound
their finitely many Y-lengths by L. Then D=Y^(L+2n). This reasoning does not
assume that G or D is metrizable.

If |X|<continuum, then |D|<=max(aleph0,|X|)<continuum. There is no hidden
regularity assumption on the continuum in this estimate: all finite products
are bounded by the same fixed infinite cardinal max(aleph0,|X|). A closed D
is profinite, and every infinite profinite group has at least continuum
points. Hence closedness implies D finite, so both X and K=closure(D) are
finite. This proves exactly the conditional statement claimed in the note.

For completeness, an infinite profinite group has no finite nonempty open
set: such a set would make an open subgroup finite, and compactness would
then make the whole group finite. Every nonempty clopen set can consequently
be split into two disjoint nonempty clopen subsets. Recursing gives a binary
tree of nested nonempty compact sets. Compactness supplies a point on each
infinite branch, while distinct branches yield disjoint intersections. There
are continuum many branches, proving the cardinality fact used above.

## 2. One uniform quotient width and the countable chain

For each fixed m the precise equivalence is

    K=Y^m
    <=> for every open normal N in G,
        w(G/N)=(image_N(Y))^m.

The image of K in a finite quotient equals the abstract verbal subgroup of
that quotient: it is the image of the dense subgroup D in a finite discrete
group. If all quotient equalities hold for one common m, then every g in K
belongs to Y^m N for every open normal N. Since Y^m is compact,

    intersection_N (Y^m N)=Y^m.

To see this last identity directly, a point outside the closed set Y^m has a
coset neighborhood gN disjoint from it. Thus K is contained in Y^m; the
reverse inclusion is automatic. Conversely K=Y^m immediately gives all the
finite quotient equalities. The m concerns the quotient's verbal subgroup,
not every element of G/N, and must not depend on N.

A hypothetical counterexample to the exact set target has infinite X of size
less than continuum. It therefore has proper nonclosed D dense in K, and no
common finite width bound in its finite continuous quotients. Its quotient
word-value counts are unbounded as well: any prescribed finite number of
distinct values can be separated simultaneously by intersecting finitely
many open normal subgroups. Width and word-value cardinality are each
nondecreasing under refinement of a quotient. At each stage choose separate
quotients exceeding the previous two bounds and intersect their kernels
with the previous kernel. This constructs the claimed descending countable
chain with both bounds growing. It does not assert that the chain separates
all points of a possibly nonmetrizable G.

## 3. Finite value set and open full marginal subgroup

The marginal subgroup here is the full one: an element must preserve every
value when inserted in every variable, for every tuple. Right invariance
alone already defines a subgroup: products follow by two substitutions, and
inverses follow by substituting the inverse input. Word equivariance shows
normality; closedness follows by intersecting the equality sets of continuous
maps. Normality converts right insertions to left insertions.

If X is finite, it is a finite discrete subspace of G, so each actual-value
fiber of w:G^k->X is open. Around every tuple choose a product of right cosets
of an open normal subgroup, lying entirely in that fiber. Finitely many such
boxes cover G^k. Intersect their finitely many open normal subgroups to get N.
Given any tuple, a covering box containing it contains every coordinatewise
right N-perturbation of it. Thus N is marginal in every variable at every
tuple, and w*(G) is open.

Conversely, if w*(G) is open, successive substitutions make w constant on
every tuple of its cosets. There are only [G:w*(G)]^k such tuples, proving
that X is finite. Thus the equivalence concerns actual values in G, not just
their images modulo another subgroup. The trivial word, k=0 if admitted,
finite G, and width0 cause no exception.

Small image supplies one constant nonempty coset box, not this uniform family
of boxes. The note correctly leaves the propagation to a global open
marginal subgroup as the unproved step.

## 4. The S3 product: a set countermodel, not a wordset counterexample

Take P=product over positive integers of S3. Define X0 exactly as the identity
and the three transpositions supported in each single coordinate. It is
countably infinite. A neighborhood of the identity restricts only finitely
many coordinates after passage to a basic neighborhood, so its intersection
with X0 is cofinite in X0. Every other point of X0 is isolated by its one
nonidentity coordinate. An open cover therefore has a member covering all
but finitely many points, and X0 is compact.

Conjugation preserves coordinate support and the transposition class, so X0
is normal as a set. Each nonidentity member has three conjugates and has
normal closure equal to its embedded S3 factor, of order6. Its abstract
generated subgroup is exactly the restricted direct sum of all the S3
factors; this subgroup is dense in P. Since every A3 element is a single
commutator in S3, coordinatewise choices yield

    [P,P]=product over positive integers of A3.

This equality holds for the abstract derived subgroup as well as its closure.
It is infinite. An element with finite support has a finite conjugacy class.
An element with infinite support has infinitely many noncentral coordinates;
choose two distinct coordinate conjugates at each of a countable infinity
of these coordinates. Every binary sequence gives a distinct conjugate in P,
so its class has at least continuum size. Thus FC(P) is exactly the restricted
direct sum, as stated.

Crucially, for every fixed word v without parameters,

    P_v=product over positive integers of (S3)_v.

Inclusion in the right-hand side is coordinate evaluation. For the reverse
inclusion select a witness tuple for each possible finite-factor value and
assemble these tuples coordinatewise into elements of P. The single-factor
image is either {1} or has at least two elements; its infinite product is
therefore a singleton or has exactly continuum size. Consequently the
countably infinite X0 cannot be P_v. This example refutes only the asserted
inference from compact small normal FC sets. No transfer to actual wordsets
is made or licensed.

## 5. The D8 product and iterated verbal subgroups

In D8 every element is r^i s^j. Its square is r^(2i) if j=0 and1 if j=1;
thus square values are exactly {1,r^2}. Every commutator lies in this same
central subgroup, and [r,s]=r^2 shows that both values occur. For
Q=product D8, coordinatewise witness choices give exactly

    Q_(x^2)=Q_([x,y])=product {1,r^2}.

This set is already a closed central subgroup K, so it equals the respective
closed verbal subgroup. For the square word, K has exponent2; for the
commutator word, K is abelian. In either case w(K)=1 while K and the original
word-value set have continuum size. The note uses this only to refute an
unconditional inference from finite w(w(G)), even combined with central K,
to finite original values. The small-image antecedent is absent from these
examples and cannot be dropped from a proposed full-problem argument.
