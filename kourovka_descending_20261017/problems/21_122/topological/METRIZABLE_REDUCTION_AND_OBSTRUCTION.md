# Metrizable reduction and the exact inverse-limit obstruction

Status: elementary reductions and an explicit counterexample to an overbroad topological mechanism. **Not a solution of Kourovka 21.122.** No novelty claim is made for the reductions.

## Exact claim

For a fixed finite group word `w` and profinite group `G`, write `X = {w(g_1,...,g_d): g_i in G}`. The target is `|X| < 2^aleph_0 => |X| < infinity`. The closed subgroup generated by `X` is a different object.

## 1. An infinite counterexample descends to a countably based one

Suppose `X` is infinite, and choose distinct `x_0,x_1,...` in `X`. For every pair `i < j`, choose an open normal subgroup `U_ij` of `G` with `x_i U_ij != x_j U_ij`. Enumerate these subgroups as `U_1,U_2,...`, put `N_n = U_1 intersect ... intersect U_n`, and `N = intersection_n N_n`. Then `H=G/N` is a profinite group with a countable neighborhood basis `N_n/N`, and the images of all the chosen values remain pairwise distinct. The quotient map sends `X` onto `H_w`, because arbitrary tuples in `H` lift to tuples in `G`.

Thus a counterexample with `|X| < continuum` gives an infinite compact metrizable image `H_w` of cardinality less than continuum. Any uncountable compact metrizable space has cardinality continuum: using a countable basis, remove the union of basis opens whose intersections with the space are countable; if the original space is uncountable, the remaining closed set is nonempty and has no isolated points. Binary splitting into disjoint open sets with shrinking closures injects the Cantor space into it. Consequently `H_w` is countably infinite.

Conversely, a countably infinite word image in a countably based profinite group is itself a counterexample. Therefore the full question is equivalent to excluding countably infinite word images in metrizable profinite groups.

This equivalence does **not** justify restricting to topologically finitely generated groups.

## 2. Finite quotient formulation

Take `G = inverse_limit Q_n`, with finite groups `Q_n` and surjective bonding maps. Let `Y_n = (Q_n)_w`. The bonding maps restrict to surjections `Y_(n+1) -> Y_n`. Then

`G_w = inverse_limit Y_n`.

One inclusion follows by evaluation. For the other, fix a coherent sequence `(y_n)` in the right side. The subsets of `G^d` on which `w` projects to `y_n` are nonempty, closed, and nested. Nonemptiness follows from surjectivity `G -> Q_n`, and compactness gives a tuple in their intersection. Its value realizes the coherent sequence.

If the numbers `|Y_n|` are uniformly bounded by `M`, the limit has at most `M` elements: `M+1` distinct limit points would already be pairwise separated in a sufficiently deep common finite quotient. The converse holds too, since the projections to `Y_n` are surjective.

The unsolved step in this formulation is therefore:

> For a fixed group word and a surjective inverse sequence of finite groups, must unbounded `|(Q_n)_w|` force continuum many coherent word values?

It is invalid to replace this with the corresponding assertion for arbitrary surjective inverse systems of finite sets.

## 3. A fully explicit thin-branch obstruction

Let `A = product_(i>=1) F_2` with the product group topology, and let `e_i` be its coordinate unit vectors. Define

`f(0)=0`, and `f(a)=e_k` when `k=min{i:a_i=1}`.

At a nonzero point, the first nonzero coordinate is determined by a finite initial segment, so `f` is locally constant there. At zero, given `m`, any input whose first `m` coordinates vanish has output with the same property, so `f` is continuous at zero. Thus

`f(A) = {0,e_1,e_2,...}`

is a countably infinite compact set, homeomorphic to a convergent sequence together with its limit. It is scattered of Cantor–Bendixson height two. The map has all of the following properties:

* continuous map from a compact homogeneous profinite group to itself;
* identity preserving and equivariant under simultaneous conjugation (the group is abelian);
* `f(a^m)=f(a)^m` for every integer `m` (both sides are zero for even `m` and equal for odd `m`);
* locally constant on a dense open subset;
* every output has a finite conjugacy class;
* compatible with the standard finite quotient chain.

Indeed, on `A_n=F_2^n`, use the same first-nonzero map `f_n`. Coordinate deletion intertwines `f_(n+1)` and `f_n`. Its image is `Y_n={0,e_1,...,e_n}`; each `e_i` has one lift to the next image, while zero has the two lifts zero and `e_(n+1)`. Hence `|Y_n|=n+1` is unbounded but the boundary is countable. A concrete checker is supplied separately.

This is **not a word-map counterexample**. Every group word on an elementary abelian group is linear in its arguments. Here `f(e_1+e_2)=e_1`, whereas `f(e_1)+f(e_2)=e_1+e_2`, so `f` is not even a one-variable group word map. Its role is to show exactly why compactness, conjugacy equivariance, power compatibility, scatteredness, and unbounded finite-image size do not suffice. Any successful fusion argument must use an additional fixed-word group-theoretic mechanism excluding this thin splitting.

## 4. Consequences inside a hypothetical metrizable counterexample

The countable compact image is scattered, with dense isolated points. An isolated word value `x` has a rigid fiber at some finite quotient: there is an open normal `U` for which `G_w intersect xU = {x}`. Conversely, such a rigid fiber makes `x` isolated. Thus a countably infinite counterexample necessarily has infinitely many values that lift uniquely above a finite quotient, accumulating at another value. One cannot assume that each value continues to split in deeper quotients.

Every word value has finite conjugacy class under the small-image hypothesis, by the homogeneous conjugacy-class argument of Detomi–Klopsch–Shumyatsky, Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2. The example in section 3 already has this property, so this fact alone does not repair the missing branching argument.

## Primary-source boundary

* Detomi–Klopsch–Shumyatsky, *Strong conciseness in profinite groups*, arXiv:1907.01344v2, Proposition 2.1 gives a constant open region whenever a continuous profinite-space map has fewer than continuum values. Applying the proposition on each nonempty clopen source region proves density of local constancy; it does not prove global local constancy.
* Shumyatsky, *On profinite groups admitting a word with only few values*, arXiv:2401.15707v2, Theorem 1.1 proves `w(w(G))` finite, not the target image dichotomy.
* De las Heras–Zozaya, *Strong conciseness and equationally Noetherian groups*, arXiv:2502.07427v1, Theorem 1.1 proves the target when a dense equationally Noetherian subgroup is available. This extra hypothesis is not supplied by the metrizable reduction.

Research gap: exclude thin splitting for genuine fixed word maps, or construct a genuine word/group inverse system realizing it. The present artifact supplies neither and must not be promoted as solving the problem.
