## Exact external input

P. Shumyatsky, *On profinite groups admitting a word with only few values*,
[arXiv2401.15707v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15707v2), Theorem1.1, states that
if `w` has fewer than continuum values on an arbitrary profinite `G`, then
`w(w(G))` is finite. Both verbal subgroups here are **closed** subgroups. There
is no finite-generation, soluble, or metrizability assumption. This theorem
does not say that `G_w` or `w(G)` is finite.

## Derived law and its nontriviality

Let `w` be a nontrivial word on `k>=1` variables and suppose `|G_w|<continuum`.
Put `K=w(G)`, `F=w(K)`, and let `e>=1` be the exponent of the finite group `F`
(take `e=1` when `F=1`). Using `k` disjoint blocks of `k` variables, define

    v = w(w(x_11,...,x_1k), ..., w(x_k1,...,x_kk))^e.

Every inner word value belongs to `K`, so every outer value belongs to `F`.
Consequently `v=1` throughout `G`.

It is essential that this be a **nontrivial** word, rather than an empty
identity. Write the free group on the `k^2` variables as the free product of
the `k` free groups on the separate blocks. In block `i`, the element
`w_i=w(x_i1,...,x_ik)` is nontrivial, hence has infinite order. Normal forms in
a free product show that the subgroup generated by the `w_i` is the free
product of their infinite cyclic subgroups: a reduced nonempty word in these
cyclic factors cannot cancel between distinct free factors. Thus substituting
`y_i -> w_i` embeds the free group on `k` letters. The outer word `w` remains
nontrivial after that substitution, and its positive power `e` is nontrivial
because free groups are torsion-free. This also handles `k=1`.

Therefore any counterexample to21.122 must be a profinite group satisfying
some nontrivial group law. This is a useful reduction, **not** a solution:
there are many infinite profinite groups with laws outside the currently
known positive classes, and no set dichotomy for all such law groups is
proved here. Replacing the target with that remaining assertion would be an
equivalent transfer under the cited theorem, not new progress.

In particular a group containing an abstract nonabelian free subgroup cannot
be a counterexample. Independently, a nontrivial word has a nonidentity value
in that free subgroup, its conjugacy class there is infinite, and its compact
conjugacy class in the ambient profinite group is then at least continuum.
The conjugacy class is contained in the word-value set.

## Explicit weak-branch conjugacy obstruction

Let `B` be a closed faithful group of automorphisms of a locally finite rooted
tree, spherically transitive, with nontrivial rigid stabilizer at every vertex.
Assume the usual infinite branching tree with at least two children at each
vertex. For every vertex `v`, its rigid stabilizer `R_v` is infinite: within
its subtree choose infinitely many pairwise disjoint descendant subtrees;
their nontrivial rigid stabilizers lie in `R_v` and yield arbitrarily many
independent nonidentity elements. The subgroup `R_v` is closed, so it is an
infinite profinite group and has cardinality at least continuum.

If `g!=1` in `B`, choose a vertex `v` moved by `g`. The subtrees rooted at
`v` and `g(v)` are disjoint. An element of `R_v` commuting with `g` would also
be supported in the other subtree; faithfulness forces it to be the identity.
Hence `C_B(g) intersect R_v=1`. Conjugating `g` by elements of `R_v` gives an
injective map from `R_v` to the conjugacy class of `g`. Thus every nonidentity
conjugacy class has cardinality at least continuum. In particular, for every
word its value set is either `{1}` or has cardinality at least continuum.
No finite word search is a substitute for this argument.

The primary [Abért paper, arXivmath/0306364v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306364)
was freshly read. Its Theorem1 gives no-law results from a separating action;
Corollary4 gives abstract free subgroups in profinite weakly branch groups.
These established results independently exclude weak-branch groups, and the
derived-law reduction also excludes any profinite group with such a quotient.
This is existing theory applied to the present search, not a new theorem claim.

## Finite wreath probes and their limits

The exact checker builds the full binary-tree automorphism groups at depths
1,2,3, of orders2,8,128. Their root flips have conjugacy-class sizes1,2,8.
In general `B_n=(B_(n-1) x B_(n-1)) semidirect C2`; the centralizer of the root
flip consists of the diagonal pairs, with or without the flip. Therefore

    |root_flip^B_n|=|B_(n-1)|=2^(2^(n-1)-1).

This verifies unbounded conjugacy in the inverse limit. The root flip is not
asserted to be a value of any of the tested words. The separate general
weak-branch proof applies to whatever nonidentity word value is obtained.

For the fixed words `x^4` and `[x,y]^2`, the exact image sizes at these first
three depths are1,1,2. For `[[x,y],[x,y]^x]` and `[x^2,y^2]^2`, they are1,1,1.
The latter observations are only finite-depth evidence: law-freeness of the
full weak-branch limit precludes concluding that those words remain laws.
No counterexample is produced by slowing the first visible finite-level
branching in this family. The unrestricted branch route is closed.
