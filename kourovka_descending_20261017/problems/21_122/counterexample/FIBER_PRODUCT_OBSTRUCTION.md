# Finite-controller fiber products cannot give a counterexample

UTC: 2026-10-24T23:17:19Z. Status: new derivation in this research effort,
awaiting an adversarial review; no originality claim. Discovery estimate toward
a complete resolution of 21.122: **2%**. This is a restricted obstruction, not

## Exact source target

Kourovka21.122, P. Shumyatsky, asks whether for every fixed group word `w` and
every profinite group `G`, its **set** `G_w` of word values is finite or has
cardinality at least continuum. The source is the latest locally cached21st
Notebook, new-problems page197, lines9492--9496 of the extracted text. The
question does not assert that the closed subgroup generated by those values
is finite. Our restricted result makes no assertion about that subgroup.

## Proposition

Let `H` be a finite group. For any index set `I`, let `pi_i:F_i -> H` be
surjective homomorphisms of finite groups. Form the closed fiber product

    G={ (g_i) in product_i F_i : pi_i(g_i) is independent of i }.

For every group word `w`, the value set `G_w` is finite or has cardinality at
least continuum.

The result permits arbitrary finite kernels, nonsplit extensions, unbounded
factor sizes and derived lengths, and an uncountable indexing set. It requires
that the **common quotient itself is fixed and finite**. It does not apply to
an arbitrary subdirect product or arbitrary tower of finite quotients.

## Proof

The empty index set gives the trivial group; assume `I` is nonempty. The
trivial word is immediate, so suppose `w` uses `k>=1` variables. Fix a quotient-input tuple
`h=(h_1,...,h_k)` in `H^k`. At coordinate `i`, let

    S_i(h)={w(f_1,...,f_k): pi_i(f_j)=h_j for every j}.

It is a nonempty finite subset of `F_i` projecting to `w(h)` in `H`.
Because all lifts at different coordinates can be chosen independently, the
image of the word map on inputs with the fixed quotient tuple `h` is exactly

    X_h=product_i S_i(h),

viewed as a subset of `G`. The inclusion from left to right follows by taking
coordinates. For the reverse inclusion, choose a realizing `k`-tuple of lifts
in each coordinate; for every variable its assembled coordinates have the
same quotient `h_j`, hence give an element of `G`. Evaluating `w` produces the
prescribed coordinate values.

An infinite product of nonempty finite sets is finite exactly when only
finitely many factors have at least two elements. If infinitely many factors
have at least two elements, choosing two elements at a countably infinite set
of coordinates embeds `{0,1}^N` in the product. Hence each `X_h` is finite or
has cardinality at least continuum. Finally

    G_w=union_{h in H^k} X_h

is a **finite** union. If every `X_h` is finite, the union is finite; otherwise
one subset already has cardinality at least continuum. This proves the claim.

## Consequence for attempted finite-support constructions

One cannot arrange infinitely many **active** coordinates (indices `i` such
that `|S_i(h)|>1` for some quotient-input tuple `h`) while making every fixed
quotient-input tuple activate only finitely many coordinates in this model.
A deterministic nonidentity coordinate value is not an active coordinate:
for example `F_i=H=C2`, `pi_i=id`, `w=x` has no activation at all even when a
chosen output has nonidentity coordinates everywhere. If there are infinitely
many active coordinates altogether, a
fixed tuple in the finite set `H^k` is active at infinitely many of them.
Its independent lifts already give the Cantor family above. This rules out
the proposed common finite controller, regardless of whether word values in
individual coordinates have a small support or a rare input pattern.

The proposition includes the usual full direct product as `H=1`, but does
not rely on density of its FC-centre or equational Noetherianity of a dense
subgroup. It is not claimed that the proposition is new in the literature.

## Possible escape: an infinite controller

Replace `H` by an infinite profinite group and let `pi_i:E_i -> H` be continuous
surjective homomorphisms of profinite groups with finite kernels. The same
coordinate argument says that, for each fixed tuple `h`,
the restricted word image is a product of finite nonempty sets `S_i(h)`.
Thus a hypothetical small image forces

    {i : |S_i(h)|>1} finite for every h in H^k.             (A)

This is necessary, not sufficient. The union now ranges over an infinite
compact parameter space, so the finite-union conclusion fails. In addition,
the word image projects surjectively onto `H_w`; thus `H` itself must satisfy
the same small-image restriction. Taking `H` to satisfy `w=1` avoids only this
last projection obstruction and does not construct a counterexample.

For countably many indices the activation sets in(A) are clopen. A finite
kernel quotient of profinite groups has local continuous sections: choose
an open subgroup meeting the finite kernel trivially, then its image is open
and the restricted quotient is a homeomorphism. On a sufficiently small
rectangle in `H^k`, the finitely many lift choices are therefore continuous.
Equality of two lifted word values is clopen, since their quotient is1 and
their ratio lies in a finite kernel. Taking the finite union over lift
choices shows that activation is clopen locally, hence clopen globally.

Infinite point-finite clopen families do exist in a compact profinite space:
the annuli `p^n Z_p \ p^(n+1) Z_p` are disjoint. Consequently point-finiteness
alone does not force finitely many active coordinates. The unproved central
construction task is to realize such a family **by one fixed word in genuine
group extensions**, while also controlling the deterministic singleton values
at inactive coordinates. Arbitrary continuous functions on a Cantor space
cannot be substituted for word maps.

In particular, this escape route is **blocked at the realization step**;
asserting the desired word cocycles exist would transfer the original
difficulty rather than solve it. A materially new extension mechanism is
required before reopening it as a counterexample construction.
