## Exact target and checkable implications

For each integer \(n\geq3\), the question asks whether every group of type \(F_{n-1}\) embeds in a group of type \(F_n\), and whether it even embeds in a group of type \(F_\infty\). Type \(F_2\) is finite presentability. These are the conventions in the [official October 6 edition, printed page 198](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21tkt-1.pdf).

The OpenAI manuscript's Theorem 1.1 claims existence of one \(F_\infty\) group containing every finitely presented group. **Deduction, independent of the validity of its proof:** if that theorem holds, every \(F_{n-1}\) group with \(n\geq3\) is finitely presented and embeds in that target, which is also \(F_n\). It therefore answers both parts of 21.146 simultaneously, with no decidable-word-problem assumption. The simple-target companion, whose inputs have decidable word problem, is a distinct assertion and cannot replace this theorem for arbitrary finitely presented inputs. [Pinned manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-universal-group-of-type-F-infinity-September-23-2026/paper.pdf).

Conversely, an \(F_\infty\) overgroup for every finitely presented group yields a single universal such target by applying the assertion to a universal finitely presented group. The latter exists by Higman's embedding theorem. For a fixed \(k\), embeddings of every finitely presented group in type \(F_k\) groups are equivalent to such embeddings for every finitely generated recursively presented group, again using Higman. For a single \(k>3\), the question restricted to \(F_{k-1}\) inputs does not automatically give that stronger statement. Separate finite-stage upgrades do not by themselves supply finite skeleta for a direct-limit target.

## Earlier mathematics and equivalent-result search

The primary [Fournier-Facio–Zaremsky manuscript, arXiv:2607.21727v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21727v1), explicitly associates the higher-dimensional embedding questions with 21.146. Its current [abstract/history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21727) shows only v1, submitted July 23, 2026; the manuscript itself displays August 24, 2026. Theorem A is **conditional**: recursively presented \(FP_n\) overgroups for all finitely generated recursively presented groups would yield \(F_n\) overgroups for all finitely presented groups. Theorem B says the specified Higman rope-trick container has infinite-dimensional rational third homology when the input \(F/R\) is infinite and \(R\neq1\), so it fails \(FP_3(\mathbb Q)\). It obstructs that construction, including when its inputs have stronger finiteness properties; it neither proves a universal negative answer nor provides the requested unconditional higher-finiteness embedding. Finite inputs and \(R=1\) fall outside the obstruction's hypotheses.

The accepted May 22, 2025 version of [Belk–Bleak–Matucci–Zaremsky, Progress around the Boone–Higman conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16356v3) asks in Question 5.6 whether every finitely presented group embeds in an \(F_\infty\) group, and discusses Leary's finiteness-upgrading question. [EMS Press publication record](https://ems.press/journals/emss/articles/14298886). Thus there is positive primary evidence that the strong form was still posed as open before the September-dated claim.

Older search hits for a universal \(F_\infty\) **diagram group** concern a target containing all countable diagram groups, not every finitely presented group. See [Guba–Sapir, Theorems 5.6 and 5.7](https://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/~msapir/ftp/pub/2comp/dc.pdf). The words "universal" and "type \(F_\infty\)" alone do not identify an equivalent theorem. Likewise, Leary's \(FP_2\) embedding theorem, recalled by Fournier-Facio–Zaremsky, supplies no \(F_3\) or \(F_\infty\) overgroup without additional work.

No earlier unconditional solution of the exact question was located. This is a bounded search finding, not a proof of novelty or an exhaustive priority certificate.
