## Exact target and success criteria

For the standard finite-piece dyadic PL Thompson group \(F\), the claim is

\[
\forall H\leq F,\qquad H\in\mathrm{EA}\quad\text{or}\quad F\hookrightarrow H.
\]

The quantifier includes infinitely generated subgroups. A positive resolution must prove this universal statement. A negative resolution must construct an actual \(H\leq F\), prove \(H\notin\mathrm{EA}\), and prove that no abstract copy of \(F\) embeds in \(H\). Being nonsolvable, nonamenable, or missing a standard/local copy of \(F\) establishes only part of the required conclusion.

The finitely generated case is equivalent to the full claim: if \(H\) has no copy of \(F\), neither does any finitely generated subgroup. If the finitely generated claim holds, these subgroups are all EA; their directed union is \(H\), hence is EA. This elementary reduction is already recorded in the literature, for example Cameron's Lemma 2.4.26; it has no novelty claim. A theorem for arbitrary finite generating sets would suffice; a theorem for a specially restricted generating class would not.

Abundance, infinite generation, or unbounded derived lengths do not establish non-EA. |

The essential open gap remains a mechanism that forces either an EA decomposition or an abstract \(F\)-subgroup for **every** finitely generated subgroup outside the proven classes. Proving such a mechanism for all groups is central work, not an auxiliary lemma that can be assumed.

Wreath-product boundary: in the regular restricted product, the base is \(\bigoplus_{\mathbb Z}G\), consisting of finitely supported functions; a restricted permutation wreath product uses another specified permutation action. Brin's permutation action must be retained. Replacing the base by the full product \(\prod_{\mathbb Z}G\) changes the construction. In particular \(\mathbb Z^{\mathbb Z}\) is uncountable, so a group containing that base cannot embed in the countable standard \(F\). No counterexample is obtained through this change of ambient group.

## Recent primary-source and AI-claim audit

**Ambient nonamenability is a separate result.** The [OpenAI manuscript dated September 23, 2026](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Thompsons-group-F-is-nonamenable-September-23-2026/paper.pdf) states \(F\) is nonamenable as Theorem 1.1. Its proof uses dyadic pair transports and Hilbert-valued recursive colors to obtain a uniform boundary obstruction; it has no subgroup dichotomy theorem. The [catalogue at the inspected commit](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/CONTENTS.md) and [overview](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/overview.tex) contain no Brin–Sapir/21.144 resolution in the keyword audit. This audit did not execute or certify its Lean files.

Importantly, the [Kim–Lodha note, arXiv:2610.18360v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18360v1), submitted October 13, 2026 at 13:25 UTC, supplies a human-written streamlined proof of the ambient nonamenability claim (Theorem 0.1). Its Theorem 2.7 gives an abstract nonamenability criterion using two self-embeddings of a set and a specified family of transports. It does not claim that every non-EA subgroup of \(F\) admits those transports, or that every group satisfying its criterion contains \(F\).

**Logical scope check:** even accepting \(F\)'s nonamenability, a subgroup of a nonamenable group can be amenable, and “amenable” is weaker than “EA.” Ambient nonamenability neither proves the conjecture nor gives an \(F\)-free non-EA subgroup. The statement “amenability of \(F\) is still open” is therefore inappropriate as a current literature summary without discussing these October primary sources; their scope must also not be enlarged into a solution of 21.144.

Two other recent checks:

- Golan's [Every copy of Thompson's group F in F is undistorted](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17193), submitted August 17, 2026, resolves a distortion question **conditional on a copy already existing**. It does not supply existence in an arbitrary non-EA subgroup.

Its introduction still states Brin–Sapir as a conjecture. The full PDF timed out in both direct and browser retrieval here; no stronger theorem is inferred from its abstract or indexed snippets.
