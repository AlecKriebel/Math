## Residual certificates for absence ofF

Every nontrivial normal subgroup N ofF contains F-prime. Indeed N intersectF-prime is normal in infinite simpleF-prime. If the intersection were trivial, [N,F-prime] would be trivial, putting N in C_F(F-prime)=1. The latter centralizer is trivial by choosing a compact dyadic bump moving a point but fixing its purported image under a nonidentity centralizer. Thus N must containF-prime. Consequently every noninjective homomorphism fromF has abelian image.

If H is residually solvable, it contains noF: every homomorphismF->a solvable group kills the perfect subgroupF-prime. A nonidentity element of any alleged F-prime copy would be killed by every residual separating homomorphism ofH, a contradiction. The same argument works for residual finiteness, using simplicity. It also works for residual elementary amenability, using the classical fact that F is not elementary amenable and the preceding normal-subgroup property.

This gives a sufficient algebraic no-F certificate, not a construction of a nonelementary-amenable H insideF. Producing such an embedded residually-EA group would still be a major new mechanism. Known PL subgroup restrictions may rule out particular residual subclasses and are being audited separately.

## Marked limits: preservation without embedding

F has a finite presentation and every proper quotient is abelian. Thus containingF is open in a space of marked groups: if the limiting marked group containsF, two fixed words represent standardF generators; finitely many presentation relations eventually hold in the approximants, and a noncommuting witness eventually remains nontrivial. The resulting F-homomorphism has nonabelian image, hence is injective. Therefore groups containing noF form a closed set. The counterexample agent independently found the same argument.

This makes marked limits of solvable/EA groups tempting: the limit can leaveEA while retaining noF. But there is no automatic embedding of that limit in standardF. Free groups already illustrate the danger: they are marked limits of finite/solvable approximations but cannot embed in PL interval groups of the required kind. Finiteness of each proposed PL generator’s pieces is essential. A diagonal construction on infinitely many invariant cells generally creates an infinite-piece map; restricted finite-cell support stays in an EA direct sum when the factors areEA. A stable letter shifting the cells gives ordinary wreath-type extensions, stillEA when the acting/base groups areEA.

Status: blocked without a new finite-piece realization mechanism. Replacing it with an unsupported embedding theorem transfers the central difficulty.

## Nonsplit extensions as a potential counterexample route

An actual subgroupH<=F mapping ontoF is automatically nonelementary-amenable, sinceEA is quotient-closed. A nonsplit extension with an EA kernel could in principle avoid an embeddedF even while havingF as quotient. But an abstract extension is insufficient; one must embed it by finite dyadic PL maps AND prove it contains no otherF copy. Conversely, asserting every F quotient of an F subgroup lifts to a copy would be a substantial rigidity theorem, not a consequence of finite presentation. The free group on two generators surjects ontoF but has noF, showing that the lifting claim is false for general groups.

No suitable PL extension or applicable lifting theorem has been obtained. This route is held for a materially new realization or obstruction, not promoted to a result.
