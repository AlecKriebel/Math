## Exact target and dated assessment

Let \(G\) be infinite and finitely presented, and assume

\[
 [G:H]=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H\text{ is free}
 \qquad\text{for every subgroup }H\le G.
\]

This property is usually called **IF**. The quantifier includes infinitely generated subgroups. The conclusion sought is an **isomorphism** of \(G\) itself with a free group or a surface group, not merely a virtual classification. In this problem, surface groups are fundamental groups of closed aspherical surfaces: this includes \(\mathbb Z^2\) and the Klein bottle group, as well as negative-Euler-characteristic surfaces.

**The unrestricted classification is still posed as open in the checked primary literature, and no full solution or genuine finitely presented counterexample was found through this audit.** This is a dated, bounded search assessment, not a proof that no resolution exists. The latest checked official statement is unchanged and unstarred in the October 20 notebook, printed p.200, as recorded in `PROBLEM.md`; official provenance and the root's PDF check are at `../../sources/latest_20261024_manifest.json`. This subteam did not duplicate that PDF comparison.

## What the current positive theorems actually prove

[Wilton, arXiv:2406.02121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02121), *Surface groups among cubulated hyperbolic and one-relator groups*, remains **v3, January 22, 2026, 11:55:53 UTC**, accepted final version. [Question 0.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02121v3) is exactly the unrestricted target and is stated as open. The principal conclusions are:

| Additional hypothesis | Current statement | Consequence for 21.138 |
|---|---|---|
| Cubulated hyperbolic | Theorem A supplies a one-ended quasiconvex infinite-index subgroup unless \(G\) is free or surface. Here cubulated means \(G=\pi_1(X)\) for compact nonpositively curved cubical \(X\). | Full classification in this class. |
| Fundamental group of a compact virtually special cube complex | Corollary B supplies a one-ended cubically convex-cocompact infinite-index subgroup under the same alternative. | This restricted result does not require hyperbolicity. |
| Infinite one-relator | Theorem D supplies a nonfree infinite-index subgroup under the same alternative. That subgroup need not be finitely generated. | Full classification for one-relator groups, without adding a finite-index one-relator condition. |

The geometric hypotheses have not been deduced from arbitrary finite presentation plus IF. A theorem for cubulated groups therefore cannot be promoted to a theorem for every hyperbolic group, let alone arbitrary groups.

[Gardam–Kielak–Logan, arXiv:2202.11093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11093), current **v2, August 9, 2022, 13:34:07 UTC**, has a two-generator **one-relator** classification in Theorem 1.5; the one-relator hypothesis cannot be omitted from that statement. Its [Proposition 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11093v2) separately says that a finitely generated IF group admitting an epimorphism onto \(\mathbb Z\) is hyperbolic or surface. Thus indicability reduces a case to a hyperbolic gap; it does not establish the unrestricted classification. Theorem 3.4 records that a torsion-free virtually surface group is itself a surface group.

[Kreuzer–Moldenhauer–Rosenberger, arXiv:2512.19645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19645), current **v3, March 16, 2026**, [published March 17, 2026](https://gcc.episciences.org/17637), repeats the exact unrestricted target as **Conjecture 4.1** in [Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19645v3). It treats cyclic amalgams and selected HNN extensions, and expressly recognizes Wilton's one-relator result. This is further dated primary evidence for the distinction between restricted and arbitrary groups; it is not an independent certificate of global openness on October 24. Some displayed presentations in the HTML contain apparent typographical errors, so no conclusion here depends on those displays.

## Limit groups and the residually free extension

[Wilton, arXiv:1102.2866](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2866), *One-ended subgroups of graphs of free groups with cyclic edge groups*, current **v2, February 26, 2011**, published *Geometry & Topology* **16** (2012), 665–683, supplies the earlier restricted input. [Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2866v2) concerns one-ended hyperbolic graphs of free groups with cyclic edge groups; Corollary 5 concerns one-ended **limit groups**, meaning finitely generated fully residually free groups. Each non-surface alternative produces a finitely generated one-ended infinite-index subgroup. For a freely decomposable nonfree limit group, a nonfree Grushko factor already violates IF. Hence limit groups with IF are free or surface. The PDF's compilation date differs from the arXiv submission history; the version date here comes from the record, not the title-page compilation date.

**Checkable restricted extension, not a novel claim:** the same classification holds for finitely generated residually free groups, by a standard obstruction plus the preceding result. The [proof of Theorem 3.5 in Fruchter–Morales, 2209.14925v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14925v3), current **May 21, 2024**, explicitly invokes Baumslag's result: a finitely generated residually free group that is not a limit group contains \(F_2\times\mathbb Z\). Inside that product, \(\langle a\rangle\times\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z^2\) has infinite index, so it also has infinite index in the ambient group and is nonfree. IF excludes this alternative, forcing a limit group. This deduction does not assume arbitrary IF groups are residually free.

Fruchter–Morales **Corollary D** alone is narrower: it says residually free **Mel'nikov groups** are surface groups. It must not be quoted as an unrestricted IF theorem. The extension above gives its separate mechanism and hypotheses explicitly.

## Formulation traps and false counterexamples

**Baumslag–Solitar boundary.** For \(|m|\ge2\),

\[
 BS(1,m)\cong\mathbb Z[1/m]\rtimes\mathbb Z.
\]

The normal base has infinite index. It is abelian and noncyclic, so it is not a free group. Noncyclicity is checkable directly: denominators of elements in a cyclic subgroup have bounded powers of \(m\), while \(\mathbb Z[1/m]\) contains \(m^{-k}\) for arbitrarily large \(k\). Every finitely generated subgroup of the base is cyclic, which explains why a finitely generated-subgroup test can miss the obstruction. The [proof of GKL Proposition 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11093v2) explicitly excludes these groups by this base subgroup. The boundary cases \(m=1,-1\) are the torus and Klein bottle groups and are permitted surface examples.

An older [Ciobanu–Fine–Rosenberger author PDF](https://www.macs.hw.ac.uk/~lc45/GroupTheory/SurfaceGroupConjecture.pdf), corresponding to [arXiv:1208.3992v1, August 20, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3992), states literally that infinite-index subgroups in the solvable Baumslag–Solitar case are cyclic. That arbitrary-subgroup assertion is false for \(|m|\ge2\), as the explicit normal base above shows. Its Baumslag–Solitar alternative is therefore not evidence of a counterexample to 21.138. No wholesale validity claim about that paper's other results is made here.

**The similarly named conjectures differ.** A Mel'nikov group is a nonfree infinite one-relator group whose finite-index subgroups are again one-relator. In [GKL's introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11093v2), Surface Group Conjecture A concerns residually finite Mel'nikov groups and allows \(BS(1,n)\); Conjecture B is stated as Mel'nikov plus IF, without explicitly retaining residual finiteness; Conjecture 1.4 drops the finite-index one-relator condition but retains the **one-relator** ambient hypothesis. Wilton settles the last version. Counterexamples to the original finite-index formulation do not refute 21.138.

**Finite presentation is essential to the target.** Ol'shanskii's torsion-free monsters are finitely generated nonfree groups with every nontrivial proper subgroup infinite cyclic, hence satisfy full IF. The [original journal record](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=im&option_lang=eng&paperid=1758&wshow=paper) identifies the 1979 Russian / 1980 English construction. [Ol'shanskii–Sapir, math/0208237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208237), printed p.3, explicitly records that these monster groups are **not finitely presented**. They refute the version with only finite generation, not this problem. Torsion monsters and torsion-by-cyclic constructions also fail the full IF antecedent because a nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup has infinite index in an infinite ambient group.

The condition that **every proper subgroup** is free is stronger than IF, not an equivalent restatement. For example, closed surface groups have proper finite-index surface subgroups that are nonfree. A paper about that stronger antecedent requires a fresh hypothesis check before it can resolve 21.138.

## Elementary deductions and exact research gap

The following deductions are included as checkable bookkeeping, not novelty claims.

1. An infinite IF group is torsion-free: any nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup would be nonfree and of infinite index.
2. IF is inherited by every subgroup \(K\le G\): a subgroup of infinite index in \(K\) has infinite index in \(G\).
3. A finitely presented IF group is coherent. Every finitely generated infinite-index subgroup is a finitely generated free group and hence finitely presented; finite-index subgroups are finitely presented by Reidemeister–Schreier.
4. Any nonfree subgroup of an IF group has finite index. In particular, a surface subgroup forces virtual surface structure, and torsion-freeness plus the cited surface-extension theorem gives the required isomorphism conclusion.

The remaining task is to classify arbitrary infinite finitely presented IF groups, or construct one outside the permitted classes while proving IF for **all** its infinite-index subgroups. It is not enough to produce a locally free subgroup, check boundedly generated subgroups, prove only coherence, or assume cubulation, hyperbolicity, indicability, residual freeness, or existence of a surface subgroup. A route that merely transfers the difficulty to one of those unsupported assertions is blocked until a new mechanism supplies it.
