# Structural proof routes for 21.138

Research checkpoint: **2026-10-24T17:05:00Z**. Exact target: an infinite finitely presented group G in which **every** infinite-index subgroup is free must itself be free or a closed aspherical surface group. The quantifier includes infinitely generated subgroups. This note contains structural reductions and conditional obstructions; it does not prove the target or construct a counterexample. Best-guess discovery progress: **15%**, subject to independent challenge.

## 1. Elementary reductions, including the ends gate

Call the antecedent IF. Any infinite IF group is torsion-free: a nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup would have infinite index and would not be free. IF passes to subgroups, since infinite index in a subgroup implies infinite index in the ambient group. A finitely presented IF group is coherent: its finitely generated infinite-index subgroups are finitely generated free groups, and its finite-index subgroups are finitely presented by Reidemeister–Schreier.

**Proposition 1.** An infinite finitely generated nonfree IF group is one-ended.

Proof. The torsion-free version of Stallings' theorem on ends says that a group with more than one end splits over the trivial group. In a nontrivial free product A*B both nontrivial factors have infinite index, hence are free by IF; their free product is free. An HNN extension over the trivial group is A*Z, and A either has infinite index and is free, or is trivial. Both alternatives are free. This also covers the two-ended case Z. Thus a nonfree group has one end. No accessibility iteration or assumption of a finite-dimensional classifying space was used. ∎

**Proposition 2.** If an infinite IF group contains Z², then it is virtually Z². If it is finitely generated, it is the torus or Klein bottle group.

Proof. Z² is not free, so has finite index by IF. A torsion-free virtually Z² group is the fundamental group of a closed flat surface, hence Z² or the Klein bottle group; equivalently use the torsion-free virtually-surface theorem quoted in the priority audit. ∎

For the rest of this note, a **hypothetical counterexample** means an infinite finitely presented IF group which is neither free nor surface. Such a group is torsion-free, coherent, one-ended, and has no subgroup isomorphic to Z². None of these deductions implies hyperbolicity, cubulation, residual finiteness, or cohomological dimension two.

## 2. A nonabelian finitely generated normal subgroup forces virtual indicability

**Proposition 3.** Let G be an infinite finitely generated IF group and let N be a nonabelian finitely generated free normal subgroup of infinite index. Then G is virtually indicable.

Proof. Write N=F_r with 2≤r<∞. We first show C_G(N)=1. If c≠1 centralizes N, torsion-freeness makes <c> infinite cyclic. N∩<c> is trivial, because an element of this intersection would be central in the nonabelian free group N. Hence <N,c> is the direct product N×<c>. For any nontrivial a∈N, the subgroup <a,c> is Z² and has infinite index in N×<c>, since <a> has infinite index in F_r. It therefore has infinite index in G, contradicting IF. This proves the centralizer assertion.

Conjugation now gives an injection

    Q=G/N → Out(F_r).

Indeed, an element acting by an inner automorphism of N differs from an element of N by an element centralizing N. The kernel is exactly N because that centralizer is trivial. The group Out(F_r) has a torsion-free subgroup of finite index. Consequently a torsion subgroup of Out(F_r) is finite: its intersection with that finite-index torsion-free subgroup is trivial, so its cardinality is bounded by the index.

Q is infinite, so it has an element q of infinite order. If <q> had infinite index in Q, its full preimage P in G would have infinite index in G and would be free. N is a nontrivial finitely generated normal subgroup of P, whereas P/N=<q> is infinite. This contradicts the free-group normal-subgroup theorem that any nontrivial finitely generated normal subgroup of a free group has finite index. Thus <q> has finite index in Q. Its preimage is a finite-index subgroup of G mapping onto Z, as required. ∎

The free-group normal-subgroup theorem is valid for free groups of arbitrary rank. For this use one can also note that P is finitely generated: it is generated by finitely many generators of N and one lift of q. Its graph-theoretic proof uses the finite core of the covering graph of a finitely generated subgroup. Normality makes all nontrivial translated loops lie in that core, so the whole quotient graph must be finite.

The exact classical input on Out(F_r) is its virtual torsion-freeness, for example the torsion-free kernel of Out(F_r)→GL(r,Z/3). Vogtmann's author survey, printed pp.11 and 19, explicitly gives the virtual torsion-free subgroup and the consequence that torsion subgroups are finite; the author lecture in the manifest gives the mod-3 argument. This is an established theorem, not a new lemma or an assumed linearity statement. Out(F_r) itself is not asserted to be linear.

The root's separate note `root_virtual_indicability.md` combines Gardam–Kielak–Logan Proposition 3.5, Linton Corollary 1.5, and Wilton Theorem A to classify finitely generated virtually indicable IF groups. Subject to that input's separate review, Proposition 3 excludes N in any hypothetical counterexample. In fact the classified groups cannot contain such an N: nontrivial finitely generated normal subgroups of negative-Euler-characteristic surface groups have finite index; free groups have the same property; torus and Klein bottle groups contain no nonabelian free subgroup.

**Important scope distinction.** A normal subgroup which is finitely generated *as a normal subgroup* need not be finitely generated as a group. Every finitely presented quotient of G has finitely normally generated kernel, but Proposition 3 applies only when that kernel is finitely generated as an abstract group. Replacing one property by the other would invalidate the route.

## 3. The central case yields a precise torsion quotient, not a contradiction

**Proposition 4.** If a hypothetical counterexample G has a nontrivial central element z, then

    Q=G/<z>

is an infinite finitely presented torsion group, and every infinite-index subgroup of Q is finite cyclic.

Proof. The element z has infinite order. For each g∈G the subgroup <z,g> is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group of rank at most two. Rank two would give Z², excluded by Proposition 2. Thus <z,g> is infinite cyclic. In particular g and z are commensurable: g^m=z^n for some m≠0 and some integer n. Hence the image of every g in Q has finite order.

Adding the single relation z=1 to a finite presentation for G proves that Q is finitely presented. If Q were finite, G would be torsion-free virtually cyclic and hence infinite cyclic, contrary to the hypothetical-counterexample assumption. Therefore Q is infinite.

Let U≤Q have infinite index, with no finite-generation assumption. Its full preimage P≤G has infinite index and is free. It contains z in its center. A free group with nontrivial center is infinite cyclic, including for arbitrary-rank free groups. Since <z> is a nontrivial subgroup of the infinite cyclic group P, P/<z>=U is finite cyclic. ∎

The implication does **not** rule out the central case: nonexistence of infinite finitely presented torsion groups has not been proved here. This is a reduction to a stronger construction problem, with additional subgroup restrictions.

The proof also shows that every infinite-index subgroup of this G is cyclic. Indeed, if H has infinite index it is free; if H is nonabelian free, adjoining z gives H×Z unless a nontrivial central power lies in H, which is impossible in a centerless free group. The resulting Z² has infinite index and violates IF. Thus H has rank at most one. This stronger observation is not needed for Proposition 4.

### Exact conditional converse

**Proposition 5.** Suppose Q is an infinite finitely presented torsion group such that all of its infinite-index subgroups are finite cyclic. Suppose it admits a torsion-free central extension

    1 → <z>≅Z → E → Q → 1.

Then E is a finitely presented IF group, and it is neither free nor a surface group. Thus such a pair (Q,E) would refute 21.138.

Proof of finite presentation. Choose a finite presentation Q=<x_1,...,x_d | R_1,...,R_s>, lifts x̃_i∈E, and a generator z of the central kernel. The lifts and z generate E. Each R_j(x̃) is z^{k_j}. Define P by the finite presentation

    <x̃_1,...,x̃_d,z | [z,x̃_i]=1, R_j(x̃)=z^{k_j}>.

There is a surjection P→E, and P/<z> is Q. Consequently the kernel of P→E lies in the cyclic central subgroup generated by z. No nonzero power of z can belong to that kernel because its image has infinite order in E. Thus the surjection is injective, proving P≅E.

Proof of full IF. Let H≤E have infinite index and set U=π(H). If U had finite index in Q and H∩<z> were nontrivial, then [<z>:H∩<z>] would be finite and the index formula would give [E:H]<∞, a contradiction. If U had finite index but H∩<z>=1, then H would embed into the torsion group Q, hence would be a torsion group. E is torsion-free, so H would be trivial, contradicting U being finite index in the infinite group Q. Therefore U has infinite index and is finite cyclic by assumption.

The preimage π^{-1}(U) is torsion-free and has <z> as a finite-index subgroup. It is therefore infinite cyclic. Its subgroup H is cyclic or trivial, hence free. This covers arbitrary H, not just finitely generated ones.

Finally E is not a nonabelian free group because it has nontrivial center. It is not Z because E/<z>=Q is infinite torsion. A negative-Euler-characteristic closed surface group has trivial center. For the torus, the quotient by any central infinite cyclic subgroup has an infinite-order element. For the Klein bottle group <a,b | a^-1 b a=b^-1>, the center is <a²>; its quotient by any nontrivial central subgroup <a^(2k)> still contains b with infinite order, by the normal form a^i b^j. Thus neither can have the displayed torsion quotient Q. These exhaust the closed aspherical surfaces. ∎

No group Q and no torsion-free central extension satisfying these hypotheses has been produced. This is a mathematically precise obstruction and conditional construction mechanism, not evidence that such a counterexample exists. In particular, a finite torsion quotient cannot be substituted, and an arbitrary central extension may retain torsion and fail IF.

**Prior terminology and scope.** Gardam–Kielak–Logan, *Algebraically hyperbolic groups*, [arXiv:2112.01331v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.01331v3), Definition 2.8, call a finitely generated torsion-free cyclic extension of an infinite torsion group an **Adian extension**. Lemma 2.9 gives precisely the commensurable-powers mechanism used above, and Proposition 5.6 proves that every proper quotient of such a group is torsion. The extension mechanism itself is therefore established prior mathematics, not an originality claim. Theorem 5.5's proof cites Sozutov for the fact that torsion-free residually finite groups are Adian-free; Proposition 2.11 also excludes Adian extensions in cohomological dimension two. Neither residual finiteness nor that dimension bound has been deduced for arbitrary G here. Proposition 5 specializes the established mechanism to the exact full IF quantifier and finite-presentation gate.

If N=<z> is infinite cyclic and normal, then either it is central or there is g with gzg^-1=z^-1. In the latter case <z,g> is an embedded Klein bottle group: every word has normal form z^m g^n, and a putative relation z^m g^n=1, conjugated by g, also gives z^-m g^n=1. Torsion-freeness first gives m=0 and then n=0. IF makes this nonfree Klein bottle subgroup finite index in G; torsion-free finite-extension classification then makes G a torus or Klein bottle group, with inversion excluding the torus. Consequently **every infinite cyclic normal subgroup of a hypothetical counterexample is central already**. This strengthening was found by the independent adversary; no index-two ambiguity remains.

## 4. A direct power-balance lemma and an elementary structural alternative

This section was developed after the initial independent attempt. Its mechanisms overlap the established Adian/AH framework just cited. They supply a checkable reduction, with no first-priority claim.

**Proposition 6 (power balance).** In any infinite IF group, if a≠1 and

    t^-1 a^m t=a^n,    mn≠0,

then |m|=|n|. Neither finite generation of G nor finite presentation is required.

Proof. Suppose |m|≠|n| and let H=<a,t>. The element t commensurates <a>. For any h in its commensurator define δ(h)=v/u whenever h^-1 a^u h=a^v with uv≠0. This ratio is independent of the chosen powers: raising two such equations to common multiples gives vu'=v'u, because a has infinite order. Conjugating common powers also proves δ(hh')=δ(h)δ(h'). Thus δ:H→Q^× is a homomorphism with δ(a)=1 and δ(t)=n/m. Its image is the infinite cyclic multiplicative subgroup generated by n/m.

Its kernel K has infinite index in H and hence in G. Therefore K is free by IF. Let N be the normal closure of a in H. All H-conjugates of a lie in K and are commensurable with a. In a free group every element commensurable with a nontrivial a lies in the same infinite cyclic centralizer C_K(a): if b^u=a^v≠1, b centralizes a^v and the centralizers of a and a^v coincide. Consequently N is a nontrivial subgroup of one cyclic group, so N is cyclic. It is normal in H. Conjugation by t restricts to an automorphism of N, hence sends a to a or a^-1. The given equation then implies n=m or n=-m, a contradiction. This argument does not require K to have finite rank, and it does not replace a finitely normally generated subgroup by a finitely generated one without proof: cyclicity of N was proved inside the free group K. ∎

**Proposition 7 (structural alternative).** A hypothetical counterexample is either a CSA group whose maximal abelian subgroups are infinite cyclic, or an Adian extension with nontrivial center. In the latter case it has the exact quotient in Proposition 4. These alternatives do not classify either remaining family.

Proof. First every abelian subgroup A of G is cyclic. If A were noncyclic, it could not be free, so IF would give [G:A]<∞. Then A would be finitely generated, torsion-free abelian, and noncyclic, hence would contain Z², already excluded.

Suppose commutation is not transitive on nontrivial elements. Then there are nontrivial x,y,z with [x,z]=[y,z]=1 but [x,y]≠1. The finitely generated group K=<x,y,z> has nontrivial center and is nonabelian, so is not free. IF makes it finite index in G. For every k∈K, the subgroup <k,z> is abelian and hence cyclic. Thus every nontrivial k has a nonzero power equal to a nonzero power of z, and K/<z> is torsion. It is infinite, since otherwise torsion-free virtually cyclic K would be cyclic, contrary to [x,y]≠1.

For every g∈G, some positive power g^d belongs to K; it then has a nonzero power equal to z^e with e≠0. Choose a finite generating set g_1,...,g_d for G and corresponding nonzero exponents e_i. Each g_i commutes with z^{e_i}. A common nonzero multiple L of the e_i makes z^L central in all of G. Proposition 4 gives the claimed actual central Adian extension, rather than only an unspecified virtually central case.

Suppose instead commutation is transitive. Then each nontrivial centralizer is abelian and hence infinite cyclic. G has unique roots: if u^r=v^r≠1 for r≠0, u and v both centralize that common nontrivial element, so commute; torsion-freeness in their abelian subgroup then gives u=v.

Let A=<a> be a maximal abelian subgroup and suppose A∩gAg^-1≠1. Then g commensurates A and some nonzero powers of a satisfy g^-1 a^m g=a^n. Proposition 6 gives n=±m; unique roots gives g^-1 a g=a or a^-1. In the first case g centralizes a, so g∈A. In the second case <a,g> is an embedded Klein bottle group by the earlier normal-form argument and contains Z², impossible. Thus A is malnormal. This is exactly the CSA property. ∎

The intermediate terminology is consistent with GKL: no Z² together with Proposition 6 gives **weak algebraic hyperbolicity**. Their Theorem 5.2 identifies the remaining Adian obstruction, and Theorem 3.1 supplies the CSA conclusion in the algebraically hyperbolic case. The elementary proof above records how IF additionally makes any nonfree Adian subgroup finite index and then supplies an actual central power in G.

No claim follows that cyclic CSA FAb groups are surface groups. Torsion-free Tarski monsters already exhibit cyclic CSA behavior without finite presentation. Nor has the Adian possibility been excluded for finitely presented groups. These are precise residual classes, not a disguised proof of 21.138.

## 5. Routes explored and their gaps

| Route | Checkable mechanism | Status and exact gap |
|---|---|---|
| Ends/accessibility | Stallings splitting over trivial finite subgroups forces free factors to be free by IF. | Complete reduction to the one-ended case; no mechanism supplies any splitting of a one-ended group. |
| Cohomological dimension | Infinite-index subgroups have cd≤1 because they are free. | Does not bound cd(G). A finite presentation complex need not be aspherical; no converse to Strebel or PD₂ characterization has been supplied. |
| Nonabelian finitely generated normal subgroup | Centralizer vanishes, quotient embeds Out(F_r), an infinite cyclic quotient appears virtually. | Complete conditional mechanism. It does not apply to infinitely generated free kernels or mere finite normal generation. |
| Normal cyclic subgroup/center | Pass index≤2 to central case, then obtain the torsion quotient in Proposition 4. | Blocked at constructing or excluding a finitely presented torsion quotient with the required extension. |
| Finite extensions | Torsion-free virtually free/surface groups have the required exact isomorphism type. | Complete upgrade once a finite-index classification exists. It does not supply that classification. |
| Minimal nonfree subgroup | Every nonfree finitely generated subgroup has finite index; choose one of minimal generator rank. | Does not control the number of defining relators. A Freiheitssatz or one-relator presentation cannot be inferred. |
| Positive deficiency | A presentation with more generators than relators has infinite abelianization and is indicable. | Root's virtually-indicable theorem handles this. A hypothetical counterexample is FAb and has no positive-deficiency finite-index subgroup. |
| Graph-of-groups decomposition | An IF group acting on a finite graph of free groups with a cycle in the underlying graph maps onto Z. | FAb excludes such a cycle; an underlying tree with higher-rank free edge groups is not classified by this observation. Cyclic-edge and cubulated results cannot be silently extended to all free-edge embeddings. |
| Coherence/Howson | A finitely generated infinite-index join <A,B> is free, so intersections of its finitely generated subgroups are finitely generated. | If <A,B> has finite index, this argument gives no Howson property. Coherence alone is not subgroup separability or cubulation. |
| Solvable possibilities | An infinite finitely generated virtually solvable group cannot be FAb: otherwise successive derived groups remain finite index and finitely generated until 1 does. | Restricted case excluded by root's virtual-indicability theorem. Amenability alone does not justify the same derived-series argument. |

The dimension caution has a concrete modern source: Fournier-Facio–Sun, [arXiv:2503.01987v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01987v3), Theorems B and E, construct finitely generated torsion-free Tarski monsters, hence IF groups, with cohomological dimension infinity and controlled finite dimensions. Their introduction and Remark 4.4 explicitly exclude finite presentation: a finitely presented limit of the fixed finite generating set and epimorphic sequence would stabilize at a finite stage, whereas their finite stages are acylindrically hyperbolic and the simple limit is not. Remark 4.5 gives the stronger obstruction that their constructed M has infinite-dimensional rational H₂ and is not FP₂(Q). These facts were checked in the primary paper. This rules out trying to deduce a dimension bound from IF and finite generation alone.

## 6. Boundary checks and current result

- Z is free and two-ended; it is explicitly allowed in Proposition 1.
- Z² and the Klein bottle group are IF and one-ended, with nontrivial center and normal cyclic subgroups. They are excluded from Proposition 4's antecedent by the phrase hypothetical counterexample, not accidentally ruled out.
- Negative-Euler-characteristic orientable and nonorientable closed surfaces are one-ended IF groups with trivial center. Passing to an orientable finite cover does not change the required exact classification after the torsion-free extension theorem.
- Free groups of infinite rank do not evade the centralizer/center arguments, but a normal kernel of infinite rank does evade Proposition 3's finite-generation hypothesis.
- The known torsion-free Tarski groups satisfy IF and can be FAb/simple. They show why virtual indicability cannot follow from finite generation plus IF. Their failure of finite presentation remains essential.
- No finite computations are offered as substitutes for the unrestricted subgroup quantifier.
