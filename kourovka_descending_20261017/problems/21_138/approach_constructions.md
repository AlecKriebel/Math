## 1. Exact target and why familiar monsters do not qualify

The hypothesis IF means that **every** infinite-index subgroup is free, including infinitely generated subgroups. We seek an infinite finitely presented IF group outside the free and closed aspherical surface groups, or a proof that none exists.

An infinite IF group is torsion-free, since a nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup has infinite index and is not free. Also, every finitely presented IF group is coherent: a finitely generated subgroup is either finite-index and finitely presented by Reidemeister–Schreier, or infinite-index and finite-rank free.

Consequently **no finitely generated, non-finitely-presented group can embed in a finitely presented IF group**. In particular, embedding a non-finitely-presented torsion-free monster in a finitely presented group by a Higman-type embedding cannot preserve IF. This is a property of every target of such an embedding, not a failure peculiar to one encoding. The monster image would have to be finitely presented by coherence. If the monster is also nonfree, the simpler contradiction is that IF makes its image finite-index, which again makes it finitely presented.

Quotienting is different and does not preserve IF: a finite-rank free group surjects onto every group with a generating set of that rank. Thus replacing an embedding by a quotient supplies no IF proof. If the starting monster is simple, a nontrivial quotient is the same group and cannot repair finite presentation.

The accompanying independent note `reviews/construction_gate.md` checks old and recent monster constructions, including the finite-presentation obstruction in Fournier-Facio–Sun, arXiv:2503.01987v3. Its infinite-dimensional rational second homology is an additional obstruction even to type \(FP_2(\mathbb Q)\). These are genuine full-IF examples without the required finite presentation; they cannot be promoted to counterexamples here.

## 2. A finite-presentation gate for quotient-limit constructions

**Lemma 2.1 (epimorphic limit stabilization).** Let
\[
G_0\twoheadrightarrow G_1\twoheadrightarrow G_2\twoheadrightarrow\cdots
\]
be a sequence of epimorphisms with \(G_0\) finitely generated. If the direct limit \(G\) is finitely presented, the canonical map \(G_i\to G\) is an isomorphism for some \(i\), and every subsequent arrow is an isomorphism.

**Proof.** Fix a finite generating set \(X\) of \(G_0\), and write \(G_i=F(X)/N_i\), with \(N_i\subseteq N_{i+1}\). Then \(G=F(X)/N\), where \(N=\bigcup_iN_i\). Finite presentation of \(G\), with this fixed finite generating set, means that \(N\) is finitely normally generated in \(F(X)\). All members of one such finite set belong to some \(N_i\), so \(N\subseteq N_i\). Thus \(N=N_i\), and monotonicity gives the conclusion. The fact that a finitely presented group has finitely normally generated kernel for any finite generating set follows from finite Tietze transformations. \(\square\)

Thus an endlessly strict quotient construction cannot give a finitely presented terminal monster. Merely increasing the sparsity or geometric quality of the stages does not avoid this algebraic obstruction. This lemma is standard; Fournier-Facio–Sun explicitly use the same mechanism in Remark 4.4, so no novelty is asserted.

For lacunary hyperbolic constructions there is another precise gate: a finitely presented lacunary hyperbolic group is hyperbolic, by the Kapovich–Kleiner appendix, Theorem 8.1, to Ol'shanskii–Osin–Sapir, *Lacunary hyperbolic groups*, Geometry & Topology 13 (2009), 2051–2140. This statement alone does **not** exclude every hypothetical IF construction: it transfers an attempted counterexample into the still-unsettled general hyperbolic case. When a particular proposed limit is known nonhyperbolic, however, it excludes its finite presentation.

## 3. Strictly ascending free HNN extensions: the entire kernel matters

This gives a direct obstruction even when no simple power-root tower is visible.

**Lemma 3.1 (bounded-generator free union).** Let
\[
B_0\le B_1\le B_2\le\cdots,
\qquad B=\bigcup_iB_i,
\]
and suppose every \(B_i\) can be generated by at most \(r<\infty\) elements. If \(B\) is free, then \(B\) has rank at most \(r\), is finitely generated, and the chain eventually stabilizes at \(B\).

**Proof.** If a free basis of \(B\) had \(r+1\) distinct elements \(x_1,\ldots,x_{r+1}\), some \(B_i\) would contain all of them. The homomorphism \(B\to F(x_1,\ldots,x_{r+1})\) that kills every other basis element restricts to a surjection from \(B_i\). This contradicts the generator bound, since abelianization of \(F_{r+1}\) needs \(r+1\) generators. Therefore \(B\) has a finite basis of size at most \(r\), which is contained in one \(B_i\); that stage equals \(B\). \(\square\)

This proof deliberately does not use the false assertion that subgroup rank is monotone in a free group.

**Proposition 3.2.** If \(\phi:F_r\to F_r\) is an injective proper endomorphism, with \(r<\infty\), the ascending HNN extension
\[
G_\phi=\langle F_r,t\mid t^{-1}xt=\phi(x)\ (x\in F_r)\rangle
\]
does not satisfy IF.

**Proof.** The base embeds by the HNN normal form theorem. The height homomorphism \(G_\phi\to\mathbb Z\) sends \(t\) to 1 and the base to 0. Its kernel is
\[
N=\bigcup_{i\ge0}t^iF_rt^{-i}.
\]
Every inclusion is proper, because \(t^{-1}F_rt=\phi(F_r)\subsetneq F_r\), and each stage has rank \(r\). Lemma 3.1 shows that \(N\) is not free. Yet \(G_\phi/N\cong\mathbb Z\), so \(N\) has infinite index. \(\square\)

The kernel is locally free: each finite set lies in one stage. This shows why restricting computational or theoretical checks to finitely generated subgroups can miss precisely the required obstruction. The rank-one example recovers \(\mathbb Z[1/m]\) in \(BS(1,m)\), but the proof applies to arbitrary proper injective free-group endomorphisms, without a periodic conjugacy class or explicit cyclic root tower.

### 3.3 Noninjective endomorphisms do not evade the gate

Let \(\psi:F_r\to F_r\) be any endomorphism. The ranks of the free groups \(I_j=\psi^j(F_r)\) form a nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative integers. Once the ranks stabilize, the surjection \(\psi:I_j\twoheadrightarrow I_{j+1}\) is injective: after identifying the two finite-rank free groups, this is Hopficity. Hence \(\ker\psi^j\) stabilizes too. Let \(j\) be such a stage and set \(K=\ker\psi^j\). Then
\[
F_r/K\cong I_j
\]
is finite-rank free, and \(\psi\) induces an injective endomorphism of this quotient. In the mapping-torus presentation every element of \(K\) is trivial, since repeated conjugation gives \(t^{-j}xt^j=\psi^j(x)=1\). Adding these already valid relations therefore identifies the mapping torus with the ascending HNN extension of the stable quotient.

If that induced injective endomorphism is proper, Proposition 3.2 excludes IF. Thus the only surviving mechanism is an automorphism mapping torus of a finite-rank free group (or the rank-zero case \(G\cong\mathbb Z\)). This reduction itself does not settle the automorphism case. The parent's separate virtually-indicable route uses additional current theorems to handle it.

Boundary checks: killing every free generator gives \(\mathbb Z\); stable rank one with automorphism \(+1\) or \(-1\) gives the torus or Klein bottle group. Stable rank one with multiplication by \(|m|\ge2\) is proper and fails IF. These agree with the permitted surface convention.

## 4. Finite small cancellation versus iterative small cancellation

Finite classical \(C'(1/6)\) presentations cannot supply a counterexample. Such a group is hyperbolic; Wise's *Cubulating small cancellation groups*, GAFA 14 (2004), 150–214, DOI 10.1007/s00039-004-0454-y, supplies a proper cocompact cubical action. If IF holds, the group is torsion-free, so finite stabilizers are trivial (after eliminating inversions). It is therefore the fundamental group of a compact nonpositively curved cube complex, and Wilton's 2026 Theorem A classifies it as free or surface.

This invokes the stated published cubulation theorem, checked in the primary publisher abstract, rather than a new proof of it. It does not include every graphical, relative, or graded small-cancellation construction under the same name. Finite classical small cancellation is excluded by cubulation; endlessly strict quotient limits are excluded by Lemma 2.1. The interval between these mechanisms is not filled by an unsupported assertion that all finitely presented IF groups are cubulated.

## 5. Kazhdan random groups: a genuine FAb construction gate

Property (T) makes an infinite finitely generated group a natural candidate for the remaining finite-abelianization situation. However, generic groups in the standard density model do not work in the established property-(T) range.

**Observation 5.1.** A property-(T) group containing a closed hyperbolic surface subgroup \(S\) fails IF. Indeed, if \(S\) had finite index then it would inherit property (T). A closed hyperbolic surface group has a finite-index orientable subgroup mapping onto \(\mathbb Z\), so it cannot have property (T). Thus \([G:S]=\infty\), and \(S\) is nonfree.

Fix the number of generators \(m\ge2\) and a density \(d\in(1/3,1/2)\), and let relator length \(\ell\to\infty\) in the strict Gromov density model. Calegari–Walker, arXiv:1304.2188v2, Theorem 6.4.1, provide closed hyperbolic surface subgroups with probability tending to 1. Ashcroft, arXiv:2104.14986v2, Theorem B, provides property (T) with probability tending to 1 in this same strict model and for all relator lengths. A union bound gives both events with probability tending to 1, and Observation 5.1 excludes IF.

The choice of Ashcroft avoids silently passing between strict and lax density models or imposing a divisibility-by-three condition from older property-(T) statements. This is a deduction from established theorems, not a new probabilistic theorem or an individual certified presentation. It does not rule out every exceptional density-model presentation or every property-(T) group.

## 6. Simple groups and geometric encodings

For an infinite simple group every proper subgroup has infinite index. A proper finite-index subgroup would give a nontrivial map into a finite symmetric group; simplicity would force an injection into a finite group. Hence an infinite simple IF group would have **all proper subgroups free**. This is a strictly stronger demand than IF in general and does not follow for arbitrary candidates.

Higman's group and Baumslag–Gersten constructions have embedded \(BS(1,2)\) subgroups. The dyadic subgroup \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) is a nonfree subgroup of infinite index already inside \(BS(1,2)\), and therefore of infinite index in any overgroup. These examples fail IF regardless of the ambient group's finite-quotient behavior.

Disjoint-support constructions also have a direct gate: two commuting infinite-order elements with disjoint nonempty supports generate \(\mathbb Z^2\), because restricting any relation to each support forces both exponents to vanish. In an infinite simple group that abelian subgroup is proper and hence infinite-index, so IF fails. The independent reviewer checks this mechanism for the relevant finitely presented torsion-free simple homeomorphism examples; one must verify that the specified copies really occur in the candidate group rather than merely in a larger ambient homeomorphism group.
