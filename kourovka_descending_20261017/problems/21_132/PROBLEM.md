## Literal Golod condition in 9.76

The cited Problem9.76, printed p.38, defines a Golod group as the subgroup generated by 1+x_i+I (i=1,...,r; r>=2) in the adjoint group 1+F/I. Here F is a free nonunital associative algebra in the x_i over a characteristic-p field, and F/I is nil but not nilpotent. This definition does not impose a fixed r=2 or direct indecomposability.

Archive11.101, printed p.262, credits a centerless Golod-group construction to V. A. Sereda and A. I. Sozutov, *Associative nil-algebras and Golod groups*, Algebra and Logic45(2) (2006),134–138; Russian Algebra i Logika45(2) (2006),231–238, DOI10.1007/s10469-006-0012-9. Its centerless quotient theorem is relevant, but residual finiteness must not be inherited automatically through an arbitrary quotient.

## Why the direct-product mechanism is admissible

Whenever T is a known infinite finitely generated residually finite p-group with Z(T)=1, the group T x C_p has all the requested group-theoretic properties and center C_p. Finite generation, infinitude, torsion p-power orders, and residual finiteness are preserved by this finite direct product.

This mechanism also preserves the literal Golod construction. If T=<1+a_1,...,1+a_r> lies in 1+A, with A nil and nonnilpotent, form B=A direct_sum F_p z with z^2=Az=zA=0. The algebra B is nil, nonnilpotent, and generated by the a_i together with z; it is a quotient of the free nonunital algebra on r+1 generators. Its designated Golod subgroup is exactly

    <1+a_1,...,1+a_r,1+z> = T x <1+z> ≅ T x C_p.

Thus requiring a construction arising from Golod's nil-algebra method does not block a finite central direct factor.

## Complete existing public construction

The Maidenhead Project's public note *Residually finite Golod groups with centre of prime order*, document dated15September2026, explicitly proves this construction, including a centerless residually finite input group. Theorem1 states that every prime p admits an infinite three-generated residually finite Golod p-group with center C_p. Section4 explicitly acknowledges the direct factor and distinguishes unasked two-generator or indecomposability conditions.

https://maidenhead.solutions/proofs/21.132.pdf
https://maidenhead.solutions/

Its input is Golod's two-generated positively graded nil, nonnilpotent algebra over F_p. Successively quotienting by the ideal generated by the algebra center, and taking the union of those homogeneous ideals, gives a centerless graded quotient Q. Non-nilpotence survives by the finite generating words at each fixed length; finite-dimensional graded truncations separate elements. The square-zero summand Q direct_sum F_p z then gives the specified T x C_p and the precise finite center. The proof checks infinitude and finite p-group quotients rather than assuming that arbitrary quotients preserve residual finiteness.
