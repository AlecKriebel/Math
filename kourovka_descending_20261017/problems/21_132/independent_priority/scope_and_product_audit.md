## Exact question and restrictions

The October 20, 2026 official Notebook, printed p. 199 (PDF page index 198), asks for each prime a finitely generated residually finite p-group with finite nonidentity centre, based on developments of Golod's construction. It points to Notebook 9.76 and Archive 11.101. The whole relevant page was visually inspected in the retained rendering.

The entry specifies no generator bound, direct indecomposability, or just-infinite condition. Infinitude is suggested by its construction references and context rather than stated as a separate adjective. Retaining infinitude is the conservative interpretation. Notebook 9.76 (printed p. 38) defines the named class using the subgroup generated by the specified elements 1+x_i in the adjoint group of a nonnilpotent nil quotient of a free nonunital associative algebra in characteristic p. There is no homogeneity requirement in that definition. Archive 11.101 is printed p. 262 in this version.

Primary source: <https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21tkt-3.pdf>. Retained bytes and extraction have hashes recorded in `official_copy_receipt.json`.

## What the 2006 theorem actually supplies

Sereda--Sozutov, *Algebra i Logika* 45(2) (2006), 231--238, Theorem 1, pp. 232--233, states:

> Если P — группа Голода, то некоторая её факторгруппа является группой Голода и имеет тривиальный центр.

Thus every Golod group has a centreless quotient which is again a Golod group. Section 1 uses characteristic p and reduces to GF(p), without excluding any prime. The quotient is generated by the images of the original finite generator list; minimal rank preservation is not asserted. The proof takes the algebra quotient by its Levitzki radical, with the group quotient induced by the adjoint map.

Residual finiteness is not part of this theorem's assertion, and that proof does not explicitly preserve homogeneous defining relations. Therefore this audit does not infer residual finiteness of its centreless quotient. This is a scope gap in using that theorem alone for 21.132, not a claim that its quotient is non-residually finite.

## Why residual finiteness requires a separate argument

Hammoudi, *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* 212(1) (2003), 93--101, Corollary 8, p. 99, explicitly constructs, for every prime p, Golod p-groups having non-residually finite quotients. Consequently a general quotient-preservation shortcut is unavailable. This does not identify the particular 2006 quotient as such an example.

Primary source: <https://msp.org/pjm/2003/212-1/pjm-v212-n1-p07-p.pdf>. PDF bytes retained in `sources/hammoudi_2003.pdf`; fetch details and SHA-256 are in `hammoudi_fetch_receipt.json`.

## Public prior construction and elementary product verification

The public Maidenhead write-up *Residually finite Golod groups with centre of prime order*, dated September 15, 2026, already uses the product in question. Its Theorem 1 claims the infinite, three-generated, residually finite Golod construction for every prime, with centre C_p. The site labels the result team-checked; the PDF itself says human review remains pending. These are provenance labels, not independent correctness certificates.

September 15 is the document's displayed draft date. This audit establishes public availability when accessed on October 24, 2026; it does not establish the first posting date.

For the displayed step, take its centreless graded algebra Q, put B=Q direct-sum F_p z, and impose z^2=Qz=zQ=0. With T generated by 1+q_1,1+q_2, the displayed group is T times C_p. Cross products vanish, its centre is exactly C_p, and B is nil but nonnilpotent. The three algebra generators give precisely the presentation required in Notebook 9.76. Torsion follows from (1+b)^(p^s)=1+b^(p^s), including p=2. The finite degree quotients separate elements and have p-power cardinality. No error was found in this product step.

Primary source: <https://maidenhead.solutions/proofs/21.132.pdf>. Full cached extraction, all three pages/143 numbered lines, was read. Its homogeneous central-ideal iteration and elementary infinitude/centre/filtration arguments were also inspected without finding a gap. The external 1964 existence theorem invoked there was not independently re-proved or fully audited here.
