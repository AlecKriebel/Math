# Kourovka 21.140: exact statement and bounded priority audit

Checked 17 October 2026. This is a source and mechanism audit, not a
solution or a claim of exhaustive priority. Research-goal completion

below.

## Exact target and status

The target is the implication

\[
G\text{ torsion-free},\quad G\text{ of type }F_\infty,\quad
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G=\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F\hookrightarrow G.
\]

Here type F_infinity is the geometric condition of a classifying CW
space with finitely many cells in each dimension. The embedding is any
abstract injective homomorphism of the standard Thompson group F.
Failure of a particular interval action or a preferred inclusion is
insufficient. FP_infinity alone is not the stated hypothesis. Infinite
dimension of a chosen complex is insufficient to establish infinite
cohomological dimension. Torsion-generated infinite integral dimension
is excluded by the first hypothesis.

## Related mechanisms and boundaries

An apparent no-F construction must survive taking arbitrary subgroups.
For example, an obstruction to embedding F into a specific action model,
or a theorem that a group does not embed into V, does not prove that F
does not embed into that group. Nonamenability, simplicity, and absence
of nonabelian free subgroups each allow copies of F in the inspected
families. Conversely, amenability excludes F only with an independently
established nonamenability theorem for F. This audit does not need to
decide the September 2026 nonamenability claim: the current amenable
construction already fails the torsion-free gate.

For interval constructions, Kim–Koberda–Lodha's
[chain-group stabilization theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.04099v3),
Theorem 1.1, gives an additional trap: sufficiently large equal powers
of generators of any prechain group form a chain group; adjacent
generators in a chain group generate F by definition. Thus changing
slopes or parameters does not itself avoid F when the prechain mechanism
is retained. This does not assert that every interval group is a
prechain group.

The 2026 higher-finiteness embedding work must also be separated from
the old Higman rope trick. Fournier-Facio–Zaremsky,
[arXiv:2607.21727v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21727v1), submitted
23 July 2026, Theorem A is conditional: recursively presentable
FP_n envelopes would yield F_n envelopes of finitely presented groups.
Theorem B obstructs the specific rope-trick output at FP_3(Q) for the
specified nontrivial inputs. Invoking an envelope that preserves
torsion-freeness, no-F, and infinite cd without proving those properties
merely transfers the central difficulty. That route is **blocked** until
a new preservation mechanism is supplied.

## Priority judgment and exact remaining gap

**Keep as officially open; do not promote a solution or a novelty claim.**
This is an active and sharply formulated problem with useful recent
near-examples. The strongest relevant no-F examples inspected are the
2025 infinite-cd torsion-free Tarski monsters, and their failure of even
FP_2(Q) is a proven obstruction. The strongest finiteness tools inspected
retain F or add torsion. No inspected result bridges that gap.

A productive new route must give either a theorem forcing F in every
torsion-free F_infinity infinite-cd group, or a construction with
independently checkable certificates for all four counterexample gates.
Reopening the Tarski limit family requires a materially different way
to obtain geometric finiteness; rebranding the same limit cannot defeat
its second-homology obstruction. Reopening a Thompson-envelope route
requires a demonstrated way to remove all copies of F while preserving
the other gates. At present, neither is an established mechanism in this
audit, so this is a difficult research target rather than a ready
construction exercise.
