# Independent family screen for Kourovka 21.140

Checkpoint: 2026-10-17 21:26 UTC. Bounded source screen complete: 100%. Estimated progress toward settling 21.140: 5%; this is an exclusion audit, not a solution or novelty claim. Full primary PDFs were downloaded and the cited theorem/definition passages read. No external communication occurred.

The counterexample sought is a torsion-free group of type F∞ with infinite **integral** cohomological dimension and no abstract subgroup isomorphic to Thompson's F. Each condition is a separate gate. Finite presentation alone does not imply F∞. Excluding a nonabelian free group does not exclude F. Amenability alone supplies no exclusion of F from the sources checked here.

## Embedding checks used in the table

The Hyde–Lodha reduction is an abstract-subgroup check. Write m=n(n+1). Their Proposition 1.4 embeds F_m in Γ_n, so F'_m embeds in Q_n=[Γ_n,Γ_n]. For completeness, F embeds in every F_m: from the presentation recalled in their §3.1, send the standard generator x_i of F to the standard generator y_{(m-1)i} of F_m. The defining relations are preserved. The image is nonabelian (the standard elements y_0 and y_{m-1} do not commute in the usual interval action), so the map is injective because every proper quotient of F is abelian. It follows that F' embeds in F'_m. Finally, F' contains a copy of F supported in a proper dyadic subinterval of (0,1). The last two facts are respectively Cannon–Floyd–Parry Theorem 4.3 and Theorem 4.1 together with Lemma 4.4 (journal pp. 228–230; PDF pp. 15–17).

For a torsion-free Tarski monster M, no copy of Z² is possible: such a subgroup would be a noncyclic proper subgroup, while M itself is nonabelian. Thompson's F contains Z² by taking two nontrivial maps with disjoint supports. Therefore M contains no abstract F. The obstacle in the actual Fournier-Facio–Sun construction is precisely higher finiteness, not this subgroup exclusion. Remark 4.4 explains the non-finite-presentability through a nonstabilizing direct limit of acylindrically hyperbolic quotients. Remark 4.5 separately supplies the homological obstruction. Reopening this route requires a materially different mechanism that fixes that obstruction, not merely declaring the limit F∞.

## Adversarial audit of the two 2026 overgroup gates

The downloaded **A universal group of type F∞** (OpenAI, 2026-09-23), Theorem 1.1 on PDF p. 1, gives one H into which **every** finitely presented group injects. Both C₂ and F are finitely presented. Consequently that H has torsion and contains F. No torsion-free-input exception or word-problem restriction occurs in that theorem. This rejection depends only on its precise conclusion, not on accepting its proof. Corollary 1.2 and the HNN construction are consistent with that conclusion.

The downloaded **Simple F∞ overgroups of groups with decidable word problem** (OpenAI, 2026-09-23), §5 on PDF pp. 28–29, constructs H=SV_Λ for an action on a countably infinite set S. Its definition permits arbitrary finite brick partitions with piece maps h_β τ_a h_α^{-1}. Fix one s₀ in S, set a=1 on every piece, put nonempty prefixes only in coordinate s₀, and use any finite binary prefix-replacement bijection in that coordinate. This gives an injective V→H: a nontrivial replacement changes some s₀-coordinate sequence and fixes the others. Thus F≤V≤H. In particular swapping prefixes 0 and 1 in coordinate s₀ is a nontrivial involution. This independently verifies torsion without relying on the final statement that H is generated by two finite-order elements. S is infinite for every input in Theorem 4.1, so no empty-coordinate or trivial-input exception removes either obstruction.

These audits reject the displayed universal/simple overgroups as counterexamples. They do not reject a separately proved torsion-preserving or subgroup-avoiding embedding theorem, which these statements do not provide.
