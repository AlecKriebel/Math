## Verdict

**PASS after the explicit infinite-order and nonzero-exponent qualifications in the general finite-index lemma.** The author's first draft omitted the infinite-order condition. It has now been repaired in the reviewed file. The exponent should also be explicitly stated as `m != 0` in that general lemma, consistent with the main claim. Neither issue affects the two applications, whose central translations have infinite order and whose exponents are positive.

The accepted result is a barrier to embedding these particular **full lift groups**, or any finite-index subgroup of them, into a RAAG. It does not prove rationality of stable commutator length in RAAGs, does not rule out relevant proper subgroups of the lift groups, and does not settle 21.127.

## 1. Central elements in subgroups of finite RAAGs

For a nontrivial cyclically reduced element z, Servatius' centralizer decomposition has the form

    C_A(z) = <r_1> x ... x <r_k> x A_link,
    z = r_1^{e_1} ... r_k^{e_k},  k >= 1, e_i != 0.

The factors <r_i> are infinite cyclic. Projection onto any one factor gives a homomorphism to Z nonzero on z. If z is central in H, every element of H belongs to this centralizer, so restriction of the projection to H kills [H,H]. It cannot kill z^m for m != 0. The argument uses no finite-generation assumption on H. Conjugating H and z together when making z cyclically reduced is legitimate.

The conclusion about groups with nontrivial central elements whose abelianization classes are torsion is valid for direct embeddings. If the central element has finite order, RAAG torsion-freeness already prevents such an embedding; if it has infinite order, the projection argument applies.

Source verification: the exact displayed decomposition is reproduced in Charney's author-written survey, arXiv:math/0610668v1, section 2.3, printed pp.6–7, attributed there to Servatius. The original article's author page and publisher metadata were inspected, but its full text could not be retrieved through the publisher (403). Thus this review checks the theorem's precise statement through Charney, and independently checks its application; it does **not** claim to have rechecked Servatius' original proof. Primary article: H. Servatius, *Automorphisms of graph groups*, J. Algebra 126 (1989), 34–60, DOI 10.1016/0021-8693(89)90319-0.

## 2. Infinite defining graphs

Suppose an equality z^m = product_{j=1}^r [a_j,b_j] holds with z central in H <= A_Gamma. Take the finite vertex set containing the supports of z and all a_j,b_j. The induced finite RAAG embeds in A_Gamma and is a retract, by sending all other generators to the identity. In that finite RAAG, z commutes with each a_j,b_j. Apply the finite case to the subgroup generated by z,a_j,b_j. This contradicts the asserted equality. The claim therefore extends to arbitrary defining graphs.

## 3. Finite-index persistence, with the necessary hypothesis

Correct statement: Let z be an **infinite-order** central element of an arbitrary group H, and suppose z^m belongs to [H,H] for some **nonzero integer** m. Every finite-index subgroup K <= H has a nontrivial central element with torsion abelianization class.

Proof, including nonnormal K: Let N=[H:K], and let d be the least positive integer with z^d in K. Centrality implies that multiplication by z permutes the N cosets in cycles all of length d, so d divides N. The transfer on abelianizations satisfies

    tr([z]) = (N/d)[z^d] in H_1(K;Z).

Indeed each cycle contributes z^d after conjugation, and centrality removes that conjugation. Consequently

    0 = tr([z^m]) = (Nm/d)[z^d] in H_1(K;Q).

The coefficient is nonzero, hence [z^d] vanishes rationally. The kernel of the map from any abelian group to its rationalization is its torsion subgroup, even without finite generation. Therefore (z^d)^e lies in [K,K] for some positive e. Infinite order ensures this is a nontrivial power and z^d itself is nontrivial and central in K. The preceding RAAG obstruction applies.

**Falsification of the unqualified first draft:** H=C2, z its nonidentity element, and m=2 satisfy z^m=1 in [H,H], while the finite-index subgroup K={1} embeds in a RAAG. Thus infinite order cannot be dropped. Likewise m=0 must be excluded: otherwise H=Z would incorrectly be excluded.

## 4. Golden-ratio Thompson total lift

The frozen primary PDF arXiv:2110.06286v2 was downloaded independently. In section 3.3, printed p.10, Lemma 3.15 states that G'=H, where G is the total lift of T_tau and H is the total lift of T_tau', and that [G:G']=2. The definition of total lift in section 2.1 supplies the complete integer-translation kernel Z in H. The unit translation z is consequently central in G, has infinite order, and belongs to G'. This is exactly the obstruction above, with m=1. It excludes all finite-index subgroups of G as well.

## 5. Zhuang total lifts and divisible translations

Take integers p,q>1 (multiplicative independence is part of the cited examples but is unnecessary for this barrier). In T(1,A,<p,q>) with A=Z[1/p,1/q], rotation by any a in A has slope 1, allowed break points and preserves A modulo Z. In particular every translation by an element of B=Z[1/p] belongs to the total lift. This follows directly from the definition in Zhuang, arXiv:0710.0026v1, section 3, printed p.4.

For any finite-index subgroup K of the full lift, N=K intersection B has finite index in the additive group B: its cosets inject into the finite coset set of K in the full group. Since B is abelian, B/N is a finite abelian group. Multiplication by p is surjective on B, hence also on B/N; finiteness makes it injective. If x belongs to N, its unique p-th additive root x/p in B has class annihilated by p in B/N. Injectivity forces x/p into N. Thus N is p-divisible, with roots iterating indefinitely. It is nonzero, since a finite-index subgroup of the infinite torsion-free B cannot be zero. Equivalently, the finite quotient has order coprime to p. This reasoning is valid when p is composite as well.

A fixed nontrivial element g in a finite RAAG cannot have roots of unbounded degree: any root u satisfying u^n=g commutes with g, so write it using the centralizer decomposition. Its link factor has nth power 1, hence is trivial by torsion-freeness. The cyclic coordinates give n a_i=e_i for all i, and therefore n divides every e_i. In particular n <= gcd(|e_i|). This also justifies the root claim rather than merely invoking uniqueness of roots, which alone would not bound depth.

For an infinite defining graph, retract onto the finite support of g. The retraction fixes g and sends every asserted root to a root of the same degree in that fixed finite RAAG. Thus the same bound holds. The obstruction consequently excludes embeddings into arbitrary RAAGs, not only finite ones, of each full Zhuang lift and every finite-index subgroup.

## 6. Exact remaining gap

The input groups cannot themselves be transplanted by an injective homomorphism to a RAAG. A proper subgroup could omit the obstructing central/divisible translations. Even if such a subgroup embeds, its scl values need not be retained by a further embedding. No argument here computes all RAAG scl values, constructs an irrational RAAG value, or proves rationality. Both directions of the original 21.127 research question remain open after this barrier.
