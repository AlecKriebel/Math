# A positive-density obstruction to independent potential-disk promotion in P4

Partial research mechanism counterexample, 2026-10-24 UTC. This is **not** an irrational scl example and not a solution of Kourovka 21.127.

## The smallest non-cograph tree test

Let P4 be the path a--b--c--d and

    G = <a,b,c,d | [a,b]=[b,c]=[c,d]=1>.

The presentation gives

    G = Z²_<a,b> *_<b> Z²_<b,c> *_<c> Z²_<c,d>.

The subgroup injections follow either from the standard RAAG induced-subgraph retractions or the normal form theorem for free products with amalgamation. The presentation of the iterated amalgam is exactly the displayed RAAG presentation.

Chen's rationality Theorem 5.10 requires incident edge images in a vertex group to be central and mutually commensurable. The middle torus has the independent cyclic images <b> and <c>, with trivial intersection, hence infinite index in both. Centrality holds but commensurability fails. Susse's Theorems 3.4/3.5 handle free abelian factors over one common amalgam, rather than this changing-subgroup iteration. Those results therefore do not settle P4 by direct application.

## Sixteen explicit polygon blocks

Use indices i modulo 4. There are four ab blocks A_i, four cd blocks D_i, and eight bc blocks M_j (0 <= j < 8). Pair turns so that the entire piece incidence graph is the cycle

    A0,M0,D0,M1,A1,M2,D1,M3,A2,M4,D2,M5,A3,M6,D3,M7,A0.

Every A_i has two b turns; every D_i has two c turns. Each M_j has one b turn and one c turn.

The two exterior arcs of A_i are a and a^-1, with their placement chosen so that the forward arc is a for i=0,1 and a^-1 for i=2,3. The two exterior arcs of D_i are d and d^-1, with the same sign placement. Thus all a and d charges of these blocks vanish, and their b/c charges are zero.

Let

    (w0,...,w7) = (1,1,1,1,1,1,-3,-3).

In M_j, the arc directed from its b turn to its c turn has label b^{w_j} c^{w_j}; the other exterior arc is trivial. Thus M_j has b and c charges both equal to w_j. At zero turn windings the A/D blocks are actual disks and M blocks are annuli. Every polygon is locally a "potential disk" if this means that its winding lies in the subgroup generated by all incident edge images: those subgroups are <b>, <c>, and <b,c> in the three torus types respectively.

This last informal local potential-disk notion deliberately drops Chen's commensurability assumption; it is not asserted to be his disk-like-piece criterion outside its hypotheses.

## Uniform loss under unchanged-block graph covers and rewirings

For coordinate b, each component consists of one A_i and its two adjacent M leaves. Its total charge is the sum of two numbers selected from {1,-3}, and therefore is one of

    2, -2, -6.

In particular every b component is a bad tree. There are four such components and they are disjoint as collections of pieces. Each must contain a non-disk by the transport lemma.

All pairings must respect the endpoint types in the graph of groups: every b turn pairs one ab-side turn with one bc-side turn, and every c turn pairs one bc-side turn with one cd-side turn. Pairing two ab sides, two cd sides, or two middle-torus sides is not a legal gluing here. For N copies of these polygon blocks, allow **arbitrary endpoint-compatible** new pairings of the b turns and arbitrary endpoint-compatible new pairings of the c turns. Requiring the usual exterior turn types would only restrict these choices, so allowing all pairings gives a stronger impossibility statement. A b component still consists of exactly one A block with its two M leaves, since A has b degree 2 and M has b degree 1. Its charge is still 2, -2, or -6. Thus at least 4N of the 16N pieces must remain non-disks, for every winding assignment. Graph covers that duplicate these unchanged polygon blocks, and arbitrary rewiring of those copies, cannot promote a proportion greater than 3/4 of the blocks to disks. A general cover of an annular piece can merge multiple polygon boundaries into a longer block; that operation changes the prescribed block data and is outside this counting assertion.

For the displayed cyclic pairing, equality is attained: choose M1,M3,M5,M7 as the four non-disks. These four pieces meet every bad b component and every bad c component. The remaining a/d components have charge zero. The transport lemma therefore makes the other twelve pieces disks. Exact integer turn windings and residual non-disk vectors are supplied in `transport_certificate.json`.

There are sixteen paired turns, so the best relative Euler characteristic for this fixed block family is

    chi = 12 - 16 = -4.

The associated degree-one relative surface objective is 2. For N copies it is at least 2 after normalization, and equality holds for N disjoint copies. Counting all sixteen locally potential disks would instead predict chi=0 and objective 0. The error persists at positive density within unchanged-block covers. Moreover the free-retract argument in `FREE_RETRACT_BOUND.md` gives an absolute lower bound 1 for this exterior chain. Thus an unrestricted theorem promising absolute cost 0 by counting all these locally potential disks would also be false, independently of the fixed-block calculation.

## Exterior chain and an actual upper bound

The two exterior boundary words, writing uppercase for inverses, are

    g1 = a b c d a b c d A b c D A B B B C C C D,
    g2 = A B B B C C C d a b c d a b c D A b c D.

Both have zero exponent sum in each of a,b,c,d. RAAG abelianization is free abelian on these generators, so both belong to [G,G]. Both are nontrivial: after retracting onto the free subgroup <a,d>, each has a nonempty freely reduced word. Every arc at an ab endpoint has a^±1 and every arc at a cd endpoint has d^±1. Consequently the exterior loops are tight and hyperbolic for the displayed graph of groups, so global disk components do not interfere with the relative Euler calculation.

The relative surface with four annular M pieces has six boundary components, genus zero, and Euler characteristic -4. All its four inner loops lie in the same middle torus. Their winding vectors sum to zero in Z²: the sum of all initial charges is zero and each paired turn cancels. For any N, take the connected cyclic N-cover of this six-holed sphere in which the six oriented boundary loops have monodromies 1,-1,1,-1,1,-1 modulo N. These values sum to zero, so they define a homomorphism of the surface group to Z/N, and all six boundary lifts have degree N. Cap the four inner lifted loops by a four-holed sphere mapping into the middle torus: their winding vectors are N times the original four vectors, whose sum is zero, and the abelian target makes their product trivial. This cover need not duplicate the original annular polygon blocks individually; inner-loop monodromy can produce longer annular blocks, which the fixed-block lower-bound statement explicitly permits to lie outside its scope. The cap has Euler characteristic -2. The resulting admissible surface for the chain g1+g2 has degree N and Euler characteristic -4N-2. Hence

    scl_G(g1+g2) <= 2.

This explicit chain upper bound does **not** assert equality. A different polygon decomposition could have smaller cost. The fixed-family lower bound is not a lower bound on scl over all surfaces. Likewise, the counterexample to local promotion does not imply irrationality or exclude other finite optimization approaches.

## Check and remaining gap

Run `python3 check_transport.py` in this directory. It reconstructs every coordinate component, exhaustively verifies the minimum transversal number 4 among 16 vertices, constructs exact integer flows, derives both boundary words from their oriented arcs, verifies both abelianizations, and checks nontriviality after the free retraction. No floating point or external package is used.

The general graph-decomposition route remains blocked at the need to control simultaneously the component structure, winding charges, and number of polygon types across *all* admissible surfaces. The demonstrated mechanism prevents replacing this task by independent local disk counting followed by an unsupported large-cover promotion.
