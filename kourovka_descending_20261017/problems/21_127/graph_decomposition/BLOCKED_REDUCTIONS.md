# Additional structural routes tested

## Passing to finite covers does not repair the canonical splitting hypothesis

In the P4 torus splitting the middle incident edge groups are <b> and <c> in Z². For every finite-index subgroup K <= Z², their intersections with K remain infinite cyclic, have trivial intersection with each other, and hence remain noncommensurable. Equivalently their rational spans are distinct lines in K tensor Q. In a finite cover of this graph of tori every lift of the middle torus still meets lifts of both adjacent edge cylinders. Thus the same commensurability failure persists at every such vertex. This blocks applying Chen 5.10 merely by replacing the canonical graph of groups with its induced finite-cover splitting. It does not rule out a genuinely different splitting or other rationality mechanism.

## A free derived subgroup is not a direct rationality reduction

For chordal defining graphs there is a known free-derived-subgroup phenomenon, which might tempt one to transfer free-group scl. Even granting that hypothesis, it does not put the target elements in the correct domain for free-subgroup commutator length.

Let H=[G,G] for the P4 RAAG and g=[a,d]. The induced retraction G -> F(a,d) takes H onto F(a,d)' and g to the elementary commutator [a,d]. This last element is nonzero in the abelianization of F(a,d)': in the universal abelian cover of the two-petalled rose, which is the Z² square grid as a graph, its loop is the nonzero 1-cycle around one grid square. A graph has no 2-cells, so this nonzero cellular cycle represents a nonzero H1 class. Therefore g is not in [H,H]. Its ordinary commutator length in H is not finite, even though its scl in G is 1/2 by the RAAG gap and the commutator upper bound.

Thus neither free-subgroup rationality nor the observation that a derived subgroup is free answers the actual ambient scl question. A successful argument would have to control the action of the abelian quotient and ambient commutators; that is precisely additional mathematical content, not a harmless domain extension. The conclusion here does not depend on asserting the free-derived-subgroup theorem or citing its scope.

## Enlarging vertex groups while preserving Chen's edge homology test

Chen's first isometric embedding theorem, Theorem 3.4, requires injectivity on the sum of incident edge images in vertex homology. At the P4 middle torus this sum has rank 2. Mapping both b and c into commensurable subgroups of an abelian target forces their homology images into a common rational line. Consequently no such target modification can be homologically injective on that rank-2 sum. In particular, collapsing the two edge directions to obtain the commensurable-image hypothesis does not preserve the sufficient hypotheses for that scl-isometry theorem.

This is only an obstruction to that particular proposed use of Theorems 3.4 and 5.10. It is not a proof that no scl-preserving embedding into any rational-scl group could exist.

## Finite optimization after fixed data does not bound all surfaces

The transport lemma solves integer winding assignment for fixed polygon blocks and pairings. It leaves an unbounded family of possible polygon boundary circuits and matching patterns for a single input word. Each such fixed problem has a rational answer; an infimum of infinitely many rational answers can still be irrational. A new compactness, finite generation, or exact normalization theorem would be needed to turn the mechanism into a complete rationality proof. No such theorem was established here.
