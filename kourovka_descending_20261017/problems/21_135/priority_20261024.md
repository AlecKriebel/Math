## Known recognition results and their limits

1. **Simple and quasisimple source groups:** C. Bessenrodt, H. N. Nguyen, J. B. Olsson, H. P. Tong-Viet, *Complex group algebras of the double covers of the symmetric and alternating groups*, Algebra & Number Theory 9(3) (2015), 601–628, DOI 10.2140/ant.2015.9.601, Theorem B, proves that every finite quasisimple group is determined up to isomorphism by its complex group algebra. This includes all finite nonabelian simple groups. This is a known restricted answer when the source G is simple; it does not classify all groups with R(G)=1. Nontrivial-center quasisimple groups have a solvable center, so they themselves need not satisfy R(G)=1. The exact primary theorem was checked in the arXiv version, https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4388, and is explicitly credited as solving 21.59(b) in the latest notebook update.
2. **Almost simple source groups:** the latest notebook's stronger isomorphism question 21.59(a) remains unstarred. Vasil'ev §3, Theorems 3.4–3.5 and following discussion, reports restricted recognition families rather than a theorem for all almost simple groups. Examples include symmetric groups and specified linear/unitary families; all parameter restrictions must remain attached to those citations. Even a proof of 21.59(a) would cover only the almost simple subclass of 21.135. Conversely, a positive answer to 21.135 would not itself assert full isomorphism recognition of almost simple groups.
3. **Direct products:** recognition results exist for particular products of nonabelian simple groups, but no general theorem was verified for all such products or for all groups with trivial radical. The independent scope screen records precise product families and primary references; do not infer that simple-group recognition tensorizes to arbitrary products.
4. **Huppert's conjecture:** this asks, for nonabelian simple S, whether equality of degree SETS cd(H)=cd(S) forces H ≅ S x A with A abelian. It is a different, stronger statement for the simple-source case, with a weaker invariant. An extra abelian direct factor is invisible to degree sets but multiplies every degree multiplicity by |A|, and hence is visible to the multiset. Tong-Viet, *Huppert's conjecture for finite simple exceptional groups of Lie type*, Journal of Algebra 665 (2025), 48–71, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2024.10.042, completes the exceptional Lie-type families. It does not claim all finite simple groups, nor arbitrary radical-free groups. Vasil'ev's September 2026 discussion still leaves the higher-dimensional classical Huppert cases open.

## Brauer-style algebra questions and stronger inputs

Brauer's 1963 Question 2 asks when nonisomorphic groups can have isomorphic complex group algebras. The 2015 Bessenrodt et al. introduction discusses the separate longstanding solvability-preservation question. Moretó, *Multiplicities of character codegrees of finite groups*, Bull. London Math. Soc. 55(1) (2023), 234–241, DOI 10.1112/blms.12724 (first online September 8, 2022), again states in its introduction that solvability preservation under complex group algebra isomorphism is unknown, and carefully distinguishes the codegree multiset (“pseudo-algebra”) from degree data. It is not a proof of preservation of trivial radical. The publisher full HTML was read through the web tool; a separate direct-cache request returned HTTP403, recorded honestly in the source manifest.

https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/blms.12724

The full ordinary character table is stronger: its character values determine kernels, the lattice of normal subgroups, and their orders. Thus its information distinguishes a minimal normal subgroup of prime-power order from a product of nonabelian simple groups, detecting whether the solvable radical is trivial. This standard observation uses the full table, whose omitted nonidentity-column values are unavailable in 21.135. Escañuela González–Natale, *On fusion rules and solvability of a fusion category*, J. Group Theory 20 (2017), 133–167, DOI 10.1515/jgth-2016-0020, introduction, recalls the normal-lattice and solvability information of the full character table; arXiv primary copy obtained at https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05727. No claim is made that its fusion-category theorems answer 21.135.

“Semisimple” for a finite group in Vasil'ev's discussion means R(G)=1; it must not be confused with semisimplicity of the algebra C[G], which holds for every finite group by Maschke, or with a claim that G itself is a direct product of simple groups. The target includes radical-free overgroups of their socles and groups permuting several socle factors.
