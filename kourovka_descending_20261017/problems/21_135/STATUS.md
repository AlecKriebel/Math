If chi_1(H)=chi_1(G) and G has trivial solvable radical, must H have trivial solvable radical? A.V.Vasil'ev; official21st edition,20October2026update3, printedp199. ## Precise remaining gaps and blocked routes

Every nontrivial solvable radical contains an elementary abelian minimal ambient-normal subgroup. Clifford theory imposes exact inertia-orbit packets, square masses, action modules and common projective factor sets. The degree multiset alone does not identify the quotient-character subset, inertia factors, kernels, or factor sets. An explicit artificial centralC2packet for the radical-free A7^3spectrum satisfies all retained numerical conditions. It even supplies central algebra units, but no group basis or group extension. Adjoining its central involution to the original group merely doubles the order. Treating that packet as an actual group would assume the central missing realization step.

Higher-rank noncentral radical modules escape the small-GL criteria. The lcm defect is not uniformly1or supported on fixed small primes for arbitrary socle overgroups. Simple-family recognition does not tensorize without new arguments: mixed A5/A7products already admit the degrees used to eliminate exceptionalGL6images, including central covers. Arbitrary radical-free sources can be nonperfect, have larger least nonlinear degree, and permute socle factors. No general induction reconstructs these features from degree data.

Thus the remaining task is to rule out every actual radical-bearing Clifford configuration against every radical-free source, or to construct one genuine matching pair. Neither has been achieved. Assuming group-basis realization, generic quotient recognition, a uniform forbidden-degree list, or a universal defect-zero property merely transfers the main difficulty to an unsupported claim. Those routes are blocked until materially new mechanisms or evidence appear.
