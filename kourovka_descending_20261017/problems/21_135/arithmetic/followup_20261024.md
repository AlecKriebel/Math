## Tensor-root quotient filter

For D0={1,16,17^3,30^2,34}, order4080, every quotient of a hypothetical realizing group K must have a degree submultiset of D0. Because m(1)=1, its quotient also has exactly one linear character. Its order q divides4080, equals the sum of squares of that submultiset and is divisible by the lcm of its degrees.

The standalone spectrum_quotients.py exhausts all48 choices of multiplicities of16,17,30,34. The only survivors have q=1 or4080. Thus K could have no nontrivial proper quotient and would be simple. This is stronger than the δ=1 deduction of radical-freeness; it still does not exhibit K or prove a universal group-basis theorem. Excluding a simple realizing group uses further simple-group/character recognition information. The pre-existing almost-simple PSL2-socle recognition theorem already rules out the original counterexample candidate, so this arithmetic refinement is not a new solution of an open case.

## Bounded primary priority check on defect-zero claims

G. Malle, G. Navarro and G. R. Robinson, *Defect zero characters predicted by local structure*, arXiv1605.04205v1, May2016, Theorem3.7, treats an almost simple group G whose abelianization G/G′ is cyclic of p-power order, for p≥5. It classifies the absence of p-defect-zero characters: proper defining-characteristic extensions and the stated linear/unitary diagonal-extension cases. Theorem3.6 gives a positive result for nondefining field actions within that same setup.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.04205. A local primary PDF and retrieval/hash receipt were added to sources/manifest.json. This confirms that the G_p family in research_notes.md lies in established defect-zero territory; the present δ=p computation is recorded as a useful quantitative instance, without originality claim. This theorem is not a result for arbitrary radical-free groups, arbitrary abelianizations or arbitrary permutations of socle factors. Extending it to those classes without new proof would transfer the central difficulty to an unsupported assertion.
