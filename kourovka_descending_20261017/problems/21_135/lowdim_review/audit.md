# Independent adversarial audit: low-dimensional recognition partial result

Audit timestamp: 2026-10-24T17:59:36.263809+00:00. Reviewer did not author the root proof. This report concerns a restricted family, not a complete solution of Kourovka 21.135. Completion estimate: 100% of this bounded proof audit; 0% certification of a general solution or originality.

## Target and verdict

For all integers r,s>=0, let G=A5^r x PSL2(7)^s. If a finite group H has exactly the same multiset of irreducible complex character degrees as G, then H is isomorphic to G.

**Verdict:** the stated argument is valid, conditional on the cited classical classification of finite primitive subgroups of GL(3,C). No substantive objection remains in the proof. The pure A5^n claim is the special case s=0. This does not settle arbitrary radical-free finite groups, the quantifier in Kourovka 21.135. The bounded priority search is not a novelty certificate.

## Checks against hidden assumptions

1. The number of degree-one characters is one. Hence H/H' is trivial, so H is perfect; no perfectness of an arbitrary radical-free group is being assumed. The degree multiset gives |H|=60^r 168^s by the sum of degree squares.
2. The target factor degree multisets are [1,3,3,4,5] and [1,3,3,6,7,8]. Products of these degrees give exactly 2(r+s) degree-three characters. No product of two nontrivial degrees can be three. Degree ten is absent: every nontrivial factor degree dividing ten would have to be five, but there is no degree two, and products of two fives exceed ten. This exclusion works for arbitrary r,s, including one of them zero. Degree six is absent only in the pure A5 case, so the mixed argument must use degree ten instead.
3. A degree-three character of H induces a faithful irreducible representation of its finite image L. Since images of perfect groups are perfect, det(L)=1. If the representation were imprimitive, the block system in dimension three would consist of three lines. Its permutation quotient is a perfect subgroup of S3, hence trivial because S3 is soluble. The three lines would then be invariant, contrary to irreducibility. Thus the image is primitive. This argument does not assume faithfulness of the original H representation.
4. Bácskai--Flannery--O'Brien, arXiv:2107.12252v2, Theorem 11.9 classifies finite nonsoluble primitive GL(3,C) groups as scalar extensions of A5, 3.A6, or PSL2(7). In the A5 and PSL2(7) cases the displayed groups are direct products with the scalar center, whose derived subgroup is the simple factor. In the 3.A6 case the displayed scalar extension is generated by the perfect group 3.A6 and central scalars, so its derived subgroup is 3.A6. Perfectness of L therefore eliminates every additional scalar extension. Thus L is exactly one of A5, PSL2(7), 3.A6. No solvable Hessian case is permitted because L is nontrivial and perfect.
5. The exceptional image 3.A6 is impossible: its quotient A6 has an irreducible degree-ten character, so inflation along H -> 3.A6 -> A6 would give degree ten in H. This is a representation-theoretic quotient argument; it does not assume that every irreducible representation is faithful.

## Independent exact degree-ten certificate

The ordinary permutation representation of A6 on six letters is 1 plus a five-dimensional standard representation V. Exterior square W=Lambda^2(V) has dimension ten. For p in A6, its character is

    chi_W(p) = ((fix(p)-1)^2 - (fix(p^2)-1))/2.

The authored `verify_A6_degree10.py` exhausts all 360 even permutations using exact integers. It obtains the character histogram

    -2:45, 0:234, 1:80, 10:1.

Consequently its self inner product is

    (45*4 + 234*0 + 80*1 + 1*100)/360 = 1.

Thus W is irreducible over C. The histogram and norm are preserved in `A6_degree10_result.json`. This certificate is independent of GAP and of tabulated A6 character degrees. The exterior-square character identity is an exact eigenvalue identity, so there is no numerical approximation.

For the initial pure-A5 proof, the alternative exclusion by degree six in 3.A6 is also correct. Bauer et al., *Negative curves on symmetric blowups of the projective plane, resurgences and Waldschmidt constants*, section 8, gives the faithful degree-three and degree-six characters and identifies the latter as a symmetric square. Its class-size data gives degree-six norm (3*36+3*45*4+6*72)/1080=1. The final mixed proof does not require this auxiliary fact.

## Kernel count: exactly two characters per simple quotient kernel

Every degree-three character has quotient H/K isomorphic to A5 or PSL2(7). Conversely each such normal subgroup K contributes exactly two degree-three characters, by inflating the two degree-three irreducibles of that simple quotient. Both are faithful: their kernels are proper normal subgroups of a nonabelian simple group. Thus they have kernel exactly K. Inflation preserves irreducibility and distinguishes the two characters. No character can be counted for two kernels, because its kernel is intrinsic.

Let a and b be the numbers of distinct quotient kernels of types A5 and PSL2(7), respectively. Then 2a+2b=2(r+s), so a+b=r+s. Choosing a different isomorphism of a quotient with a named simple group permutes these two characters and creates no extra kernel or multiplicity.

## Distinct quotient kernels give the full product

For completeness, let f_i:H -> S_i be epimorphisms to nonabelian simple groups, with pairwise distinct kernels. The combined map to the product S_1 x ... x S_t is onto.

Induct on t. Suppose the image onto P=S_1 x ... x S_(t-1) is full, and let D be the image in P x S_t. If D contains 1 x S_t, then D=P x S_t. Otherwise D intersection (1 x S_t)=1, since that intersection is normal in the simple second factor. The projection D -> P is then an isomorphism, and D is the graph of an epimorphism f:P -> S_t. For each direct factor S_i of P, f(S_i) is normal in S_t; hence it is either trivial or all of S_t. At least one is all. Two cannot both be all, because distinct direct factors commute and S_t is nonabelian. The unique nontrivial factor maps isomorphically to S_t, so the new epimorphism from H has the same kernel as an earlier coordinate epimorphism. This contradicts distinctness.

This proof explicitly excludes diagonal subdirect products rather than assuming pairwise distinct kernels automatically suffice without explanation.

## Final order check and boundaries

The preceding lemma gives an epimorphism H -> A5^a x PSL2(7)^b. Its order divides |H|. The 5-adic valuation yields a<=r and the 7-adic valuation b<=s. Together with a+b=r+s, these force a=r and b=s. Source and target now have the same finite order, so the epimorphism is an isomorphism. If r=s=0, |H|=1 and the result holds separately without selecting any degree-three character. The proof does not need any classification of groups of order 60^r 168^s.
