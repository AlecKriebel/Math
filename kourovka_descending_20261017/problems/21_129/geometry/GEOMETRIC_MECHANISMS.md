# Geometric mechanisms and exact gaps

Date: 2026-10-24. None of the exploratory invariants below resolves Kourovka 21.129. The strongest original certificate is the obstruction in `PARABOLIC_PRESERVATION_COUNTERMODEL.md`.

## 1. The useful deletion signatures, and why they are insufficient

For a rank `n` connected spherical diagram, inspect its standard maximal proper parabolics `A[S minus {s}]`. A disconnected deletion with `p` components, of which `q` have at least two vertices, is virtually a product of `q` non-Euclidean irreducible central quotients and `Z^p`. The coarse type-I product theorem distinguishes the pair `(p,q)` for these abstract subgroup geometries.

For `D_n`, `n=6,7,8`, the underlying tree has arm lengths `(1,1,n-3)`. Deleting the trivalent vertex gives

`A[A_{n-3}] x A[A1] x A[A1]`, with `(p,q)=(3,1)`.

For `E_n`, the arm lengths are `(1,2,n-4)`. Deleting the trivalent vertex gives

`A[A_{n-4}] x A[A2] x A[A1]`, with `(p,q)=(3,2)`.

The trivalent vertex is the only deletion producing three components in either tree. Every other deletion has `p<=2`. Thus the **raw** maximal-proper-parabolic deletion spectra distinguish all three `D_n/E_n` pairs, using only product decomposition, without needing to distinguish the unknown `D/E` factors recursively. Exact graph enumeration is in `check_certificates.py` and `CERTIFICATES.json`.

Likewise, the `F4` deletion types are `B3,B3,A2 x A1,A2 x A1`. Those of `H4` are `H3,A3,I2(5) x A1,A2 x A1`. The irreducible `H3` parabolic has a different QI type from `A3/B3`: the latter central quotient is a mapping class group covered by QI rigidity, and the former is not commensurable to it by Cumplido–Paris. The other rank-two factors collapse into the known dihedral commensurability class.

Therefore, **if** arbitrary QIs coarsely preserved this raw maximal-parabolic family, these calculations would distinguish all four residual pairs. However, `PARABOLIC_PRESERVATION_COUNTERMODEL.md` proves that preservation hypothesis false for a self-QI of `A3/Z`. This route is blocked, not merely awaiting a citation.

A possible new mechanism would enlarge the raw family to all intrinsic product regions or twist-centralizer regions. Such a mechanism would require both:

1. a QI-invariant characterization of the enlarged family, including its coarse containment and intersection relations; and
2. an inventory proving that the displayed `D/E` or `F/H` distinction survives in that enlarged family.

Neither follows from the raw deletion calculation. In particular, smaller product subspaces are not adequate: a `D6` deletion `A3 x A2` has geometry `Q_{A3} x F x Z^2`, and `Q_{A3}` itself contains a product region `F x Z`, so `D6` already contains regions of geometry `F x F x Z^3` resembling the `E6` branching deletion. Existence of an abstract product of this geometry therefore cannot by itself distinguish the two groups.

## 2. Marking graphs, quasiflats, and the actual missing axioms

Ragosta (2025), Theorem A, supplies a geometric action of each irreducible central quotient on a marking graph. Its introduction explicitly leaves hyperbolicity and acylindricity of the parabolic complex open; Section 1.1.3 presents an HHG structure as a potential future application, not a theorem. A graph admitting a geometric group action is only a model of that individual group; it does not canonically compare different models under arbitrary QIs.

To run a mapping-class-style QI proof, one would still need a quasiflat theorem identifying coarse orthants/product regions and then prove their incidence structure rigid enough. Merely declaring the marking graph an HHG, or declaring standard flats intrinsically recoverable, transfers the original difficulty to an unsupported theorem. Status: blocked at geometric recognition, with the raw substitute independently falsified above.

Primary source: [Ragosta, arXiv:2508.10394v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10394v1), especially introduction and Section 1.1.3. Its text is cached in the neighboring priority sources.

## 3. Ordinary filling functions do not separate the residual pairs

For an irreducible spherical Artin group `A`, the pure subgroup `P` has a direct decomposition `P=Q x Z(P)` with `Q=P/Z(P)`, by Cumplido–Paris Proposition 3.1. Thus `Q` is a group retract of a finite-index subgroup of the biautomatic group `A`. The ordinary Dehn function of `P`, and hence of its retract `Q`, has a quadratic upper bound. The same applies to the finite extension `A/Z(A)`.

The explicit derivation in `NESTED_CENTRAL_RETRACTS.md` proves more: `Q` has an actual group retract `Z^(n-1)`, generated by nested parabolic full twists and split by consecutive differences of meridian winding coordinates. For rank at least three it therefore retracts onto `Z^2`, giving a quadratic lower bound by the same bounded-relator-image argument. Thus the central quotient has exactly quadratic ordinary Dehn function. In rank two the central quotient is virtually nonabelian free and has linear Dehn function.

All eight groups in the four residual comparisons have rank at least four, so this invariant has the same quadratic value for every one. This gives a concrete reason that passing to the center quotient and comparing ordinary filling does not provide the needed distinction. Higher filling functions would require a separate computation; no conclusion about them is asserted here.

The basic retract inequality is direct: choose finite presentations and a homomorphic retraction; replace every ambient generator in a filling diagram by its fixed bounded-length image in the retract, and fill the finitely many resulting relators with uniformly bounded area. The number of cells grows by a constant factor.

## 4. Contracting axes and the invariant that remains uncomputed

Calvez–Wiest prove that Morse elements in finite-type Garside central quotients have strongly contracting axes in the Garside Cayley graph. This is useful evidence for coarse type I, but applies uniformly across the exceptional types and `D_n`. It provides no type-dependent boundary or incidence calculation. Moreover, arbitrary QIs need not send periodic axes to periodic axes, so counting Coxeter-periodic elements or finite-order quotient elements cannot be promoted to a QI invariant by this theorem.

A materially distinct possible invariant is the topology or stable dimension of the Morse boundary. The exact gap is an independently verified computation differing for one of the residual pairs. No such computation has been derived here. Existence of Morse elements alone distinguishes none of them.

Primary source: [Calvez–Wiest, DOI 10.2140/agt.2024.24.4545](https://msp.org/agt/2024/24-8/p12.xhtml), published 17 December 2024; [arXiv:2106.14826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14826).

## 5. New finite-quotient results do not supply a QI bridge

The primary preprint Hughes–Ng–Ragosta–Scherich–Verberne, [arXiv:2609.27171v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27171v2), submitted 23 September 2026 and revised 16 October 2026, proves irreducible spherical Artin groups are distinguished up to isomorphism by their profinite completions (Theorem C). Its proof does not assert a QI or abstract-commensurability classification. It also notes concurrent independent work Gavazzi–Haladjian–Paris, arXiv:2609.25940.

This is fresh relevant literature, but finite quotients of a group are not invariant under passing to finite-index subgroups, much less under arbitrary QI. Known commensurable examples `A_n/B_n` already warn against that substitution. The missing bridge would be a QI-recognizable virtual algebraic structure; no such bridge is supplied by profinite rigidity alone.
