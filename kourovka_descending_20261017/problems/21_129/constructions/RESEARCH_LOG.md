# Construction route research log

Author: Alec Kriebel, ORCID <https://orcid.org/0009-0001-9320-500X>.

## Contract

We test the hypothesis that two spherical Artin groups (including reducible
ones) can be quasi-isometric without being abstractly commensurable. A negative
answer to Kourovka 21.129 requires an explicit eligible pair, a checked
quasi-isometry, and an independent proof of noncommensurability. A positive
answer requires all types and products. No such result is claimed here.

The current mechanism families are arrangement deletion/branched completion,
common symmetric-space models, finite covers, and central product operations.
An auxiliary independent agent studies the last family. No external outreach,
Git, publication, QUEUE, or PR actions are part of this delegated effort.

## 2026-10-24T19:56:28Z — checkpoint 1

Completion estimate for a full resolution: **5%**.

Started independent construction research. Primary source search confirms that
Cumplido–Paris proves componentwise commensurability classification and that
the pairs F4/H4 and Dn/En (n=6,7,8) were not resolved by that paper. They are
candidate test pairs only, not certified noncommensurable pairs. Soroko's 2021
paper does certify D4/F4 and D4/H4 as noncommensurable.

Initial finding: inclusion of one complex hyperplane-arrangement complement
into another after deleting a hyperplane kills a meridian with nonzero linking
number. The induced group homomorphism therefore has infinite kernel and
cannot be a quasi-isometric embedding. This is an elementary obstruction to
the natural map, not an obstruction to every possible quasi-isometry.

Common complex-hyperbolic orbifolds from the CHL construction need special
care: cone-angle completion imposes powers of meridians. An orbifold quotient
is not automatically a finite cover or a model for the original Artin group.
We are checking exact quotient formulas and a central-quotient relative
hyperbolicity obstruction before promoting that conclusion.

## 2026-10-24T20:02:39Z — checkpoint 2

Completion estimate for a full resolution: **7%**. Completion of this bounded
construction probe: **90%**, pending independent audit.

Developed a support argument showing that every proper standard parabolic
injects into an irreducible spherical Artin group's central quotient: positive
relations preserve support, whereas every nonzero positive power of the
global central generator has full support. Explicit dihedral presentations
then show that a standard generator and the local rank-two central generator
span Z² after passing to the global quotient. The enlarged generating set has
connected commutativity graph, so Anderson–Aramayona–Shackleton Theorem 2
excludes strong relative hyperbolicity with proper finitely generated
peripherals in rank≥3. Root independently reached a compatible network proof;
an adversarial agent is checking this derivation and its exact scope.

CHL Theorem 7.1 explicitly imposes finite-power meridian relations. Combining
this with Crisp–Paris Theorem 1 strengthens the kernel obstruction: a CHL
holonomy kernel contains F2 generated by powers of adjacent Artin generators.
This F2 survives passage to the central quotient since a free group has
trivial center. Even lifting the map through an abelian central extension
still kills its infinite commutator subgroup. Thus a simple attempt to
restore the missing center cannot repair the construction.

The independent central-product probe is complete and algebraically audited.
If Γ has p components, k nonsingletons, and C is a rank-r central subgroup,
then A_Γ/C is virtually (product of pure central quotients) × Z^(p−r). It is
commensurable to a spherical Artin group exactly when r≤p−k, subject to the
documented empty-diagram boundary. It then only consumes existing A1
components. The optional stronger QI exclusion is explicitly dependent on
KKL Theorem B and Cumplido–Paris Remark 5.2; the algebraic theorem does not
need that geometric input.

No explicit QI between certified noncommensurable spherical Artin groups has
been found. Natural deletion maps, CHL quotient maps, and central lattice
operations are marked blocked as counterexample mechanisms unless a
materially new noncentral mechanism is supplied. Proofs and exact limitations
are in `ARRANGEMENT_AND_LATTICE_PROBE.md` and
`central_product_probe/CENTRAL_QUOTIENTS.md`. Primary PDFs, text extractions,
and SHA256 custody metadata are preserved locally.
