## Scope and criterion

Audited the CHL-specific claims in §4 of
`../../ARRANGEMENT_AND_LATTICE_PROBE.md` against the archived primary source
`../../sources/CouwenbergHeckmanLooijenga2005.txt`. Success means identifying
which exact group the cited presentation concerns, whether its relations
imply finite powers of spherical Artin generators, and which projective and
parameter boundary qualifications are necessary. The elementary group
argument in Proposition 4.1 is also checked, but no other section is audited.

## Verdict

The infinite-kernel obstruction is valid for the Coxeter-orbit monodromy
representations at all finite parameters listed in the exceptional real
tables. The source explanation needs correction: the starting real Coxeter
group W need not be the Schwarz symmetry group G used in Theorem 7.1.
Consequently Theorem 7.1 is not, by itself, a presentation of a quotient of
the original spherical Artin group in every listed example. CHL §3.5 supplies
the required Artin representation directly and repairs the argument.

## Findings

### 1. Substantive source/group conflation: G need not equal W

CHL Definition 4.2, text lines 2641–2658, defines G from Schwarz rotation
groups G_L. It does not define G to be an arbitrary Coxeter symmetry group
already acting on the arrangement. At lines 3639–3644, §7.1 identifies
π₁(G\\V°) with an Artin group only under the additional condition that G is
a Coxeter group and H is its reflecting-hyperplane arrangement. Saying only
that the starting arrangement came from a real Coxeter group omits this
condition.

There is a concrete diagnostic within the cited tables. Their parameter q_H
means κ_H=1−2/q_H (lines 3927–3931), while Theorem 7.1 uses the denominator
q_L of the *reduced* fraction 1−κ_L (lines 3653–3665). For a hyperplane,

    q_L = q_H / gcd(2,q_H).

Thus at q_H=4, present for E6 and F4, the theorem's hyperplane exponent is 2,
whereas §3.5's standard Artin generator has order 4. These are different
loops, not inconsistent calculations. For this parameter 1−κ_H=1/2 has
numerator 1, so its Schwarz rotation group G_H is trivial; the reflection in
W is not supplied by this G_H. The Artin generator is a half-meridian in
W\\V°, while a pure meridian is its square.

The distinction also affects the target image. §3.5's image of A_W in GL(V)
should be denoted separately, say Γ_W, unless its identification with the
Γ of Theorem 7.1 has been established. The pure monodromy image has finite
index in Γ_W because π₁(V°) has finite index in A_W. No canonical quotient
A_W→the exact Γ of Theorem 7.1 follows merely from the two finite symmetry
groups acting on the arrangement.

### 2. Correct direct proof that standard generator powers vanish

CHL §3.5, lines 2121–2154, takes W to be a finite real irreducible reflection
group, lets κ be W-invariant, identifies A_W with π₁(W\\V°), and constructs
the monodromy representation. For |κ_i|<1 the standard generator σ_i is
semisimple with eigenvalues

    1 and −exp(π i κ_i).

For the table weights κ_i=1−2/q_i, with finite integer q_i≥2, the second
eigenvalue is exp(−2π i/q_i). Consequently σ_i has finite order q_i in this
linear monodromy representation, and in particular a positive power of
every standard generator lies in its kernel. No assertion G=W and no full
Theorem 7.1 presentation are required for this conclusion.

For the nonparabolic homogeneous affine structure, the nonzero dilatation
factor fixes a distinguished affine origin, so the affine holonomy and its
linear form can be identified. In the parabolic case one must distinguish
the affine holonomy from its linear part; see finding 4 below.

### 3. Parameter boundaries must be stated explicitly

The exceptional real examples are in §8, Tables 2–4, not §7.3 of the archived
preprint. Lines 3940–3955 give E6,E7,E8; F4; and H3,H4. F4 has two mirror
orbits, as stated in the draft.

All the listed q or q_i are finite integers at least 2. In particular the
F4 entries with q₁=2 have κ₁=0. They still satisfy |κ_i|<1, so the §3.5
semisimple generator argument applies, with order 2 on that orbit. However,
the main geometric Theorems 5.1, 5.14 and 6.2 are written with strict
κ∈(0,1)^H. A claim applying those precise hypotheses to the full F4
arrangement at a zero weight requires a reduction to the effective
arrangement or the appropriate symmetry extension; the table alone should
not silently erase that boundary.

The parameter κ_i=1, corresponding formally to q_i=∞, is *not* covered by
the finite-order proof. CHL lines 2151–2154 explicitly warn that
semisimplicity may fail there. Rational κ by itself is therefore insufficient
if the endpoint 1 is allowed. None of Tables 2–4's finite parameters creates
this problem.

F4 q₁=2,q₂=3 is explicitly parabolic (lines 3932–3936 and 3947), so blanket
references to all entries as ball lattices would also be wrong. The draft
only says “examples” in its first paragraph, which is fine.

### 4. Projective factorization through the full Q is conditional

The phrase “whenever it is defined” makes Proposition 4.1's projective
statement logically sound. Given a representation from Q killing a positive
power of the image of a standard generator, the rank-one parabolic
injection supplies its infinite order, exactly as the draft argues.

But one must not silently infer a homomorphism from the full Q=A/Z(A)
merely from the radial full twist mapping to a scalar. The radial loop
represents Δ². If Z(A)=⟨Δ⟩, killing Δ² projectively only says that the
projective image of Δ has order at most 2; a separate argument must kill Δ.

For the linear Coxeter monodromy a separate elementary argument is
available: along x(t)=exp(it)x the residue sum is κ₀I by Lemma 2.13
(lines 1110–1119), so parallel transport is scalar. If the center is
generated by Δ, the longest Coxeter element is −I, and the half-radial
path 0≤t≤π closes in W\\V°. Gluing the endpoints by −I still gives scalar
transport. If the center is generated by Δ², the full radial path suffices.
This checks the full center for the *linear* representation; the conditional
wording avoids needing to insert this derivation into the main probe.

For affine holonomy at κ₀=1 the radial loop is a nontrivial translation, not
a scalar (Theorem 4.5, lines 2698–2699 and 2724–2725; Theorem 5.14 proof,
lines 3163–3169). Embedding the affine group in one higher projective
dimension does not kill a nontrivial translation. Thus “projectivize the
affine representation” is not an automatic construction of a representation
of Q at the parabolic boundary. Passing to the linear monodromy or to the
specific translation quotient must be stated if used.

### 5. Elementary infinite-kernel inference survives

The exponent-sum map A→Z sending each standard generator to 1 is valid:
both sides of every Artin relation have the same length. A killed positive
power therefore generates an infinite cyclic subgroup of the kernel. Every
finite-index subgroup meets it in an infinite subgroup. For Q, the draft's
rank-one proper-parabolic argument gives the same conclusion in rank≥2.

These checks prove that the specified homomorphisms cannot be
quasi-isometric embeddings. They do not rule out unrelated quasi-isometries
or imply that all Artin groups mapping to a common holonomy image are
commensurable. The draft correctly preserves this limitation.

## Recommended repair

Use Theorem 7.1 only to describe the G-orbit presentation with its exact G.
Then use §3.5 and §8's finite rational weights for the finite-power claim
about A_W. Call its target the Coxeter-orbit monodromy image unless an
identification with Γ has been proved. Keep the full-Q claim conditional,
and correct the table section reference to §8. The argument needs no exact
exponent in the main text, but the semisimple eigenvalue calculation above
provides a checkable exponent if needed.

Strongest verified result: every standard generator has finite image in
the Coxeter-orbit linear CHL monodromy for the finite exceptional real table
parameters; hence the representation has infinite kernel on every
finite-index subgroup of A_W, and so does any induced projective
representation of Q. Exact remaining source gap before the original
wording can be promoted: identify the correct source group and image in
each use, rather than equating Schwarz G with W.
