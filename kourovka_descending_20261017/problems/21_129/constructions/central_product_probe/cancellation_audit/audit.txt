NOTATION AND EXACT TARGET
G ~ H means abstract commensurability: isomorphic finite-index subgroups.
VZ(G) = {g in G : C_G(g) has finite index in G}, the virtual center.
A_Gamma is a spherical Artin group, P_Gamma its pure finite-index subgroup.
For a connected component Gamma_i put Q_i=P_i/Z(P_i).
The target is to test whether adjoining finitely many central infinite cyclic
factors can hide a mismatch between products of the nontrivial Q_i, and to
check arbitrary central lattice quotients, including nonprimitive lattices.
No claim of newness or resolution of the remaining irreducible comparisons
is made.

LEMMA 1 (VIRTUAL CENTER CRITERION)
VZ(G)=1 iff every finite-index subgroup U of G has trivial center.
Proof: If z belongs to Z(U), its centralizer contains U, so z belongs to
VZ(G). Conversely, if g centralizes a finite-index subgroup U, then g is
central in <U,g>, which is a finite-index subgroup of G. Thus the condition
on all such centers forces g=1. This argument needs no torsion assumption.
Finite products of groups with trivial virtual center again have trivial
virtual center: the centralizer of a tuple is the product of its coordinate
centralizers, and finite index forces each coordinate centralizer to have
finite index. The empty product is the trivial group and also qualifies.

LEMMA 2 (CENTRAL STABILIZATION CANCELLATION)
Let VZ(G)=VZ(H)=1, and let a,b be finite nonnegative integers. Then

     G x Z^a ~ H x Z^b  iff  G ~ H and a=b.

Proof: Let K <=_fi G x Z^a and L <=_fi H x Z^b, and let f:K -> L be an
isomorphism. The projection pi_G(K) has finite index in G. If (g,z) is
central in K, then g is central in pi_G(K), hence g=1 by Lemma 1. Therefore

     Z(K)=K intersect (1 x Z^a),
     K/Z(K) isomorphic to pi_G(K) <=_fi G.

The central intersection has finite index in 1 x Z^a, so Z(K) is free
abelian of rank a. The corresponding statements hold for L with rank b.
Since f identifies centers and induces an isomorphism of central quotients,
a=b and G~H. The reverse implication follows by taking products of
isomorphic finite-index subgroups with Z^a. This proof applies when one or
both cores are trivial and when a or b is zero. It does not require finite
generation of G or H, strong indecomposability, or a splitting of K.

GENERAL FINITE-INDEX SPLIT FORM
Suppose X has a finite-index subgroup G x Z^r and Y has a finite-index
subgroup H x Z^s, with both cores having trivial virtual center. Then

 X x Z^a ~ Y x Z^b  iff  G~H and r+a=s+b.

This is immediate by commensurability and Lemma 2. It is the correct form
when X and Y already have central factors: a=b need not follow by itself.

LEMMA 3 (INDEPENDENT PRODUCT-MATCHING PROOF)
Let G=product_{i=1}^m G_i and H=product_{j=1}^n H_j, with all factors
infinite, strongly indecomposable, and having trivial virtual center.
Then G~H iff m=n and their factors match by commensurability up to a
permutation. Strongly indecomposable means that every finite-index subgroup
is indecomposable as a direct product.

Proof of the forward direction: choose f:U -> V, where U<=_fi G and
V<=_fi H. Put U_i=U intersect G_i and U_0=product U_i. Each U_i is finite
index in G_i; U_0 is finite index in U; f(U_0) is finite index in H. Write
N_ij=pr_{H_j}(f(U_i)) and B_j=product_i N_ij. The N_ij commute pairwise,
and B_j has finite index in H_j. Its center is trivial by Lemma 1. An
element in N_ij intersect product_{l != i} N_lj centralizes every factor
of B_j and so is trivial. Hence B_j is the internal direct product of the
N_ij. Strong indecomposability of H_j implies exactly one of these factors
is nontrivial. Thus each j is assigned to exactly one i.

No i can have no assigned j, because then f(U_i)=1, contradicting the
injectivity of f and the infinitude of U_i. For the nonempty set J_i of
indices assigned to i, f(U_i) is contained in product_{j in J_i} H_j.
Because f(U_0) has finite index in H and these blocks have disjoint
coordinates, f(U_i) has finite index in its block. Consequently it contains
the finite-index product of its intersections with the factors H_j in that
block. If |J_i|>=2 this gives a nontrivial direct product decomposition of
a finite-index subgroup of U_i, contradicting strong indecomposability of
G_i. Thus each J_i has one element; m=n; and f(U_i) is finite index in its
matched H_j. The reverse implication is obtained by multiplying matching
finite-index isomorphisms. Empty products cause no exception: any nonempty
product here is infinite and cannot be commensurable with the trivial group.

APPLICATION TO SPHERICAL PURE QUOTIENTS
CP Proposition 3.1(2) gives P_i isomorphic to Q_i x Z, with Z(P_i)=Z.
For rank-one Gamma_i=A1, Q_i=1. Otherwise CP Theorem 2.3(1) gives strong
indecomposability and infinitude of Q_i, while CP Lemma 4.1 gives trivial
centralizers of finite-index subgroups, hence VZ(Q_i)=1. Thus a product of
nontrivial Q_i satisfies Lemmas 2 and 3.

In particular, for Q=product_i Q_i and R=product_j R_j, with trivial
rank-one quotients omitted,

 Q x Z^a ~ R x Z^b
 iff a=b and the nontrivial factors match by commensurability.

Rank-one factors only affect the abelian rank and cannot compensate for a
missing or mismatched nontrivial quotient factor. If original Artin groups
are used instead of their cores, the existing centers must be counted:
A_Gamma is virtually Q x Z^p, where p is its number of connected components.
Hence stable equivalence with A_Omega is characterized by core matching and
p+a=q+b. Original groups can fail to be commensurable when a != b only
through such different abelian ranks; for example A_A2 and A_A2 x Z are
not commensurable, but A_A2 x Z is the latter group itself. With equal
stabilizations the original Artin groups do cancel.

CENTRAL-LATTICE CLAIM (ENDORSED)
Let Gamma have p connected components, k components with at least two
vertices, and a=p-k singleton components. Let C <= Z(A_Gamma), and let
r=rank(C). Write P=P_Gamma and L=P intersect C. Then:

 (i) P is finite index and has a decomposition P isomorphic to Q x Z^p,
     where Q is the product of the k nontrivial Q_i. Under any such splitting,
     Z(P)=1 x Z^p, because Q has trivial center. Since C is central, L lies
     in Z(P). Moreover [C:L] is finite, so rank(L)=rank(C)=r.

(ii) The image of P in A_Gamma/C is PC/C, with kernel exactly L, and
     [A_Gamma/C : PC/C]=[A_Gamma:PC] <= [A_Gamma:P] < infinity.
     Thus P/L is a finite-index subgroup of the quotient. This remains true
     even though C need not be contained in P.

(iii) Smith normal form of the subgroup L<=Z^p gives

       P/L isomorphic to Q x Z^(p-r) x T,

     for a finite abelian group T. In particular, A_Gamma/C is commensurable
     with Q x Z^(p-r). A nonprimitive L merely produces T, and the subgroup
     Q x Z^(p-r) x {1} has finite index. No torsion-free quotient assumption
     has been smuggled in.

(iv) It follows that (A_Gamma/C) x Z^r ~ A_Gamma.

(v) If A_Gamma/C ~ A_Omega for a spherical diagram Omega, then
     A_Omega x Z^r ~ A_Gamma. Applying CP Theorem 2.2 to Omega disjoint union
     r copies of A1 shows that the non-singleton components of Omega match
     those of Gamma up to commensurability, and that Omega has exactly a-r
     singleton components. Non-singleton irreducible spherical factors
     cannot be commensurable with Z: their pure quotient has trivial virtual
     center and is infinite, so their finite-index form Q_i x Z cannot be
     virtually cyclic. Therefore r<=a.

(vi) Conversely, if r<=a, let Gamma' be obtained by deleting any r singleton
     components. Its pure subgroup is Q x Z^(p-r), so A_Gamma/C~A_Gamma'.
     The orientation of C in the p-dimensional center is irrelevant for
     abstract commensurability.

BOUNDARY CHECKS
* C=1: r=0 and the statement recovers A_Gamma itself. Since the original
  center is torsion-free, a rank-zero subgroup C is indeed trivial.
* r=p and k>0: the quotient is virtually Q, with no abelian factor, and
  cannot be commensurable with any spherical Artin group; r>p-k.
* r=p and k=0: A_Gamma=Z^p and its quotient is finite. It is commensurable
  with the Artin group of the empty diagram, namely 1. If the target class
  excludes the empty diagram, this is an explicit exception to the terse
  formulation 'iff r<=a': no nonempty spherical Artin group is finite or
  virtually trivial. This convention must be stated.
* p=0: G=1, C=1, and the empty-target convention gives the expected result.
* Finite torsion in P/L or A_Gamma/C does not obstruct abstract
  commensurability, because the exhibited finite-index subgroup removes T.
  It would matter if the question instead required isomorphism or an
  embedding in a torsion-free group.

HYPOTHESIS CHECK AND COUNTEREXAMPLE
Centerless is weaker than trivial virtual center and cannot replace it.
Let D be the infinite dihedral group. Z(D)=1, but D is virtually Z.
Both G=D and H=D x D are centerless, and G x Z ~ H since both are virtually
Z^2, while G and H are not commensurable: their torsion-free finite-index
abelian subgroups have ranks 1 and 2. Thus even with nontrivial centerless
G,H, unequal central stabilizations can hide different virtually abelian
cores. This is exactly ruled out by VZ=1 in Lemma 2.
