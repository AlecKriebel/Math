# Kourovka 21.141: statement, conventions, and bounded priority audit

Audit date: **2026-10-17 UTC**. Researcher: Alec Kriebel (ORCID https://orcid.org/0009-0001-9320-500X). This is a literature/definitions audit, not a proposed solution. No individual was contacted.

**Finding.** The current official edition still presents 21.141 without a solution or AI-solution marker. The primary sources screened here do not establish the unrestricted conclusion. There are substantial affirmative special cases, especially procyclic edges and malnormal analytic edges. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no solution exists.

## 1. Exact claim and official status

At printed p.198 of the official 21st edition, October 6, 2026, the quantified question is:

- Fix a prime \(p\). Take coherent pro-\(p\) groups \(G_1,G_2\), a common polycyclic pro-\(p\) subgroup \(H\), and their free pro-\(p\) amalgam \(G=G_1\amalg_HG_2\).
- Must every topologically finitely generated closed subgroup \(K\leq G\) have a finite pro-\(p\) presentation?

The proposer is **P. A. Zalesskii**. The entry expressly says the analogous abstract-group assertion is known and that coherence is meant in the pro-\(p\) category. It does **not** add finite generation of the factors, torsion-freeness, nilpotence, malnormality, or an acylindricity assumption. The word “proper” is not printed in the question; see §2.

The [official landing page](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/) fetched during this audit points to the October 6 update-2. Its [main PDF](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21tkt-1.pdf) is the cached authoritative source authenticated by SHA-256 `c6056609ce6e48bacc28db5acc61b835f8095104e47d5c9e95b84abdfd6de692`. The [update-only PDF](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21upd-1.pdf) contains no 21.141 entry. The main PDF has an unmarked 21.141, unlike the explicitly marked neighboring 21.142. This supports “still listed without an answer in the latest official edition checked,” not an unrestricted claim about world knowledge.

## 2. Conventions verified from proposer-authored sources

**Closed, generated, presented.** Castellano–Zalesskii, *Subgroups of pro-p PD3-groups*, [arXiv:2005.00423v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00423v2), Notations and §2, explicitly uses closed subgroups, continuous morphisms, and topological finite generation/presentation. Its polycyclic convention is a finite chain
\[
1=H_0\trianglelefteq H_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq H_n=H
\]
of closed subgroups with procyclic factors. An infinite factor is \(\mathbb Z_p\); finite factors are cyclic \(p\)-groups. Thus “cyclic” here is topological, not abstract countable cyclic. The diagram makes normality successive; its accompanying prose says “normal subgroups of G,” so the chain, rather than a stronger ambient-normal interpretation, should be displayed in any formalized statement. Hirsch length counts the \(\mathbb Z_p\) factors. Proposition 2.3 shows that positive Hirsch length gives an open normal poly-\(\mathbb Z_p\) subgroup. Finite \(p\)-groups are included.

For precision, a finite pro-\(p\) presentation means \(K\cong F_p(d)/\overline{\langle\!\langle r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle\!\rangle}\), with finite \(d,m\) and continuous quotient map; neither \(K\) nor its dense abstract finitely generated subgroups are being assigned an abstract finite presentation. Snopce–Zalesskii explicitly defines pro-\(p\) coherence using finitely generated **closed** subgroups in [arXiv:2005.01685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01685), introduction before Theorem 1.6.

**Amalgam and properness.** As a categorical construction, \(G_1\amalg_HG_2\) is the pushout of continuous maps \(G_1\leftarrow H\to G_2\). A proper amalgam additionally has injective canonical maps \(G_i\to G\); compactness then makes these closed embeddings. This is not automatic in the pro-\(p\) category. The proposer’s 2024 paper makes this explicit in Definition 2.12/Remark 2.13: every amalgam considered there is proper. Zalesskii’s 2025 JSJ preprint repeats it in §2.4.

Accordingly, **the principal research interpretation should be the proper amalgam of the given factors**. The official entry alone does not explicitly resolve whether nonproper categorical pushouts were intended too. Do not silently claim that replacing factors by their images preserves the hypothesis: a quotient of a coherent pro-\(p\) group need not be coherent. Any final theorem should state properness or separately treat nonproper pushouts.

## 3. Primary results that already occupy nearby territory

The mathematical statements below are restated with all relevant hypotheses; they are not search-snippet conclusions.

### 3.1 Procyclic edges and the finite-decomposition condition

Chatzidakis–Zalesskii, *Pro-p groups acting on trees with finitely many maximal vertex stabilizers up to conjugation*, Israel J. Math. 247 (2022), 593–634, [corrected arXiv v2, February 12, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06867v2):

- **Theorem 6.8:** a finitely generated pro-\(p\) group acting on a pro-\(p\) tree with procyclic edge stabilizers has a finite graph-of-pro-\(p\)-groups decomposition, with finitely generated vertices and vertex/edge groups conjugate into the original stabilizers. Proposition 6.6 supplies \(|V|\leq2d-1\), \(|E|\leq3d-2\) for reduced decompositions and \(d\geq2\). No numerical bound is used here for \(d=0,1\).
- **Theorem 6.3:** finite generation, edge stabilizers in \(\mathcal F\), and \(\mathcal F\)-accessibility yield a finite decomposition with stabilizer groups. Accessibility bounds all finite proper reduced decompositions over that family; it is an extra hypothesis.

**Standard consequence, not new:** proper procyclic amalgamation preserves coherence. Apply Theorem 6.8 to finitely generated closed \(K\leq G\). Its vertices are finitely generated subgroups of coherent conjugate factors, hence finitely presented. Its edges are procyclic. The finite pro-\(p\) graph presentation completes the argument. This special case does not solve 21.141.

### 3.2 Finite edges

Wilkes, *On accessibility for pro-p groups*, J. Algebra 525 (2019), 1–18, [arXiv:1811.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02329), **Theorem 3.1**, bounds the number of edges of any finite proper reduced decomposition of a finitely generated pro-\(p\) group when every edge group has order at most \(M\):
\[
|E|\leq \frac{pM}{p-1}(d(G)-1)+1.
\]
Thus a fixed finite amalgam edge gives the bounded family needed in the preceding accessibility theorem. Together with coherent factors and finite graph presentations, this supplies the finite-edge case as a standard consequence. The paper also constructs a finitely generated inaccessible pro-\(p\) group; **that example does not itself refute 21.141**.

### 3.3 Malnormal analytic edges: direct published coherence result

Castellano–Zalesskii, *A pro-p version of Sela’s accessibility and Poincaré duality pro-p groups*, Groups Geom. Dyn. 18 (2024), 1349–1368, [DOI 10.4171/GGD/769](https://doi.org/10.4171/GGD/769), **Corollary 3.14**, applies when:

1. \(G=G_1\amalg_HG_2\) is proper under the paper’s blanket convention;
2. both factors are coherent pro-\(p\) groups;
3. \(H\) is an analytic pro-\(p\) group;
4. \(H\cap H^g=1\) for every \(g\in G_1\setminus H\).

Its conclusion is pro-\(p\) coherence of \(G\). Malnormality is required only in one factor. Polycyclic groups have finite rank (intersect their finite procyclic series with closed subgroups), hence fall within the analytic finite-rank setting. **Dropping condition 4 is not licensed by this theorem.**

Theorem 3.15 gives a finite subgroup decomposition for finitely generated subgroups of a finite reduced \(k\)-acylindrical graph of pro-\(p\) groups. Theorem 3.13 gives accessibility bounds. These retain acylindricity. The proof of Corollary 3.14 has an apparent “finite pro-p groups” typo in its graph description; the stated corollary and Theorem 3.15 do not require the vertex groups to be finite.

### 3.4 RAAG examples do not prove a general combination theorem

Snopce–Zalesskii, *Right-angled Artin pro-p groups*, Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 54 (2022), 1904–1922, [DOI 10.1112/blms.12664](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12664), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01685), **Theorem 1.6**, says for a finite simplicial graph \(\Gamma\) that the following coincide: chordality; coherence of its pro-\(p\) RAAG; and type \(FP_\infty\) for every finitely generated closed subgroup. Their proof uses the free-by-abelian structure, not unrestricted coherence preservation under arbitrary polycyclic amalgamation. The chordal decompositions are useful test examples. Nonchordal RAAG incoherence supplies no counterexample unless a qualifying decomposition with coherent factors and polycyclic edge is independently proved.

## 4. Recent sources screened, including 2026

- **Zalesskii, [JSJ Decompositions of pro-p Groups, arXiv:2511.22626v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22626v1), November 27, 2025.** Full contents/theorem statements, §3 and §8.2 were checked. Proposition 8.2 starts with a **finite** graph of pro-\(p\) groups. If its total group and all edge groups are finitely generated (respectively finitely presented), its vertex groups have that property. It does not construct the finite graph needed for an arbitrary subgroup in 21.141. The JSJ results require their stated accessibility/ellipticity hypotheses. No coherence combination theorem answering the unrestricted question was found in this paper.
- **Lopes–Zalesskii, [Pro-C groups acting on profinite trees, arXiv:2607.23875v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23875v1), July 26, 2026.** Full introductory theorems and later statements screened. Theorem 1.3 starts with a finite \(k\)-acylindrical graph of profinite groups, assumes each infinite procyclic pro-\(p\) subgroup has an open subgroup that is a virtual retract of the total group, and takes a finitely generated pro-\(\mathcal C\) subgroup for an extension-closed class excluding some finite group. Its conclusion constrains stabilizers to pro-\(\pi\) groups for a finite prime set when there is no fixed vertex. It does not remove the extra assumptions or prove the required finite presentations.
- **Peng–Yang, [Residual profiles and bounded generation in pro-p completions of amalgamated products, arXiv:2608.02193v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02193v2), August 11, 2026.** Theorem A assumes a proper nondegenerate amalgam and classifies bounded generation: it occurs exactly when the edge is boundedly generated and both indices and \(p\) equal 2. Theorem B concerns completed **abstract** amalgams with boundedly generated starting factors and uses their closed residual images. This is not a coherence criterion. The abstract’s broad claim about a proper residual amalgam should not be read as saying the original factors always embed. The full paper explicitly distinguishes those issues.
- **Kionke–Otmen–Toti–Vannacci–Weigel, [On probabilistic identities and coset identities in pro-p groups, arXiv:2507.19086v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19086v2), May 28, 2026.** Corollary 4.8 concerns a proper nonascending HNN extension, or a proper amalgam with maximum edge index greater than 2. It concludes random freeness. This imposes no finite-presentation conclusion for arbitrary finitely generated subgroups and does not solve 21.141.
- **Berdugo–Zalesskii, [Cyclic splittings of pro-p groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08787), J. Algebra 660 (2024), 291–309**, and **Casals-Ruiz–Pintonello–Zalesskii, [Pro-C RAAGs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13439), J. Algebra 664 (2025), 177–208** were checked at full theorem-statement level. Their splitting/RAAG hypotheses do not cover general coherent factors with arbitrary polycyclic edge.
- **Kochloukova’s [Higher dimensional algebraic fiberings for pro-p groups](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X23000895)** is in Canadian J. Math. 77 (2025), 300–323, first online 2023. The locally obtained [arXiv:2205.07418v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07418v1) is the older 2022 preprint, not the final article. Its Theorem 1.3 proves incoherence for \(K\rtimes F\), with \(K\) finitely presented, \(F\) nonprocyclic free of finite rank, and a closed normal \(N\trianglelefteq K\) with \(K/N\cong\mathbb Z_p\) and \(N\) not finitely generated. The published abstract states a stronger extension version. Neither is a counterexample here without a separate qualifying amalgam decomposition. The 2024 Kochloukova–Vidussi [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01447), §8, was also checked for context.

### 4.1 Prior normal-core lemma: credit required

**Jesus Berdugo–Pavel Zalesskii**, [*Pro-C generalized Baumslag–Solitar groups and profinite commensurators*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40863-025-00523-1), São Paulo J. Math. Sci. **20, article 2 (2026)**, published/version of record **February 27, 2026**; received June 30, 2025, accepted December 24, 2025. The DOI’s 2025 segment is not its publication date. No arXiv identifier for this article was located by exact-title/author/topic screening; reference [7], arXiv:2502.03626, is a different paper.

**Definition 3.1** takes the *closure* of the algebraic commensurator. **Proposition 3.2**: for a profinite \(G=\overline{\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\rangle}\), if each \(g_i\) individually commensurates closed \(H\) in both finite-index directions, then some \(V\leq_oH\) is normal in \(G\), and \(G=\operatorname{Comm}_G(H)=N_G(V)\). **Corollary 3.3**: a finitely generated closed commensurator equals \(N_G(V)\) for some \(V\leq_oH\). **Theorem 4.4** assumes a finite reduced graph, finitely generated total group, and finite edge indices at **both** endpoints. These assumptions are not supplied by a polycyclic edge alone. The normal-core mechanism is prior work and must not be claimed as a new lemma. It does not settle unrestricted 21.141. Verify algebraic commensuration of the chosen generators when invoking Proposition 3.2; membership in the closure alone is not its stated hypothesis.

## 5. Two category mismatches that must be excluded

**Abstract groups.** Li–Sánchez Saldaña, [A note on higher coherence of graphs of groups, arXiv:2511.16409v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16409v3), September 2, 2026, proves Theorem 1.3 for abstract graph-of-groups fundamental groups. For \(1\leq n<m\leq\infty\), vertices are \((n,m)\)-coherent and edges are \((0,m-1)\)-coherent if \(n=1\), or \((1,m-1)\)-coherent otherwise. The conclusion is \((n,m)\)-coherence. Its \(n=1,m=2\) instance uses slender edges, and hence covers abstract polycyclic edges. The proof uses an abstract cocompact invariant subtree. This does not supply that subtree in the pro-\(p\) category. Theorem 1.2 attributes the classical version to Karrass–Solitar, Trans. AMS 150 (1970), 227–255, [DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-1970-0260879-9](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1970-0260879-9); the AMS original was blocked by HTTP 403, so the original proof was not audited here.

**Locally compact topological groups.** Arora–Martínez-Pedroza, [Topological groups with a compact open subgroup, relative hyperbolicity and coherence, arXiv:2206.07141v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07141v4), May 9, 2023, defines coherence using closed **compactly generated** subgroups and **compact presentations**. Theorem B assumes a topological abstract amalgam of coherent **open** factors with compact intersection. In that sense every compact group is coherent. All pro-\(p\) groups are compact, so this theorem cannot answer the finite-pro-\(p\)-presentation question. “Coherence of augmented Iwasawa algebras,” “local coherence of representations,” and topos coherence likewise concern different predicates.

## 6. Exact remaining obstacle and boundary checks

For a proper amalgam, a finitely generated closed \(K\leq G\) acts on the standard pro-\(p\) tree. Its edge stabilizers lie in conjugates of \(H\) and therefore have finite procyclic series and uniformly bounded generator rank. This fact is useful but does not, in the screened sources, give a finite graph decomposition for \(K\) with the required vertex information.

A proof that merely assumes such a finite decomposition, assumes all vertex intersections are finitely generated, or assumes accessibility over all polycyclic subgroups has transferred the central difficulty to an unsupported claim. **That route is blocked until a new mechanism or applicable theorem establishes the missing finite-decomposition/vertex-control step.** The cited procyclic, finite-order, and acylindrical results are three mechanisms that establish it in restricted cases.

Check any proposed solution against:

- \(H=1\), finite noncyclic \(H\), and procyclic \(H\), all already occupied by standard consequences above;
- degenerate inclusion \(H=G_i\), where the pushout is the other factor;
- \(p=2\), torsion in \(H\), arbitrary finite series length, and factors that are coherent but not finitely generated;
- the difference between a closed subgroup and a dense abstract subgroup;
- the difference between the given factors and their images in a nonproper pushout;
- the difference between a finite graph theorem and existence of the finite graph.
