# A cyclic-fiber obstruction for proper pro-p amalgams

Checkpoint: 2026-10-17 21:16 UTC. Scoped-route completion estimate **95%**; overall Kourovka 21.141 discovery estimate **10%**. This is an independently derived partial lemma, not a claim of a new theorem or a solution of 21.141.

## Exact statement and conventions

Fix a prime p. Let

\[
G=A\amalg_H B
\]

be a **proper** amalgamated free pro-p product: the canonical maps from A and B into G are injective. Suppose A and B are coherent in the pro-p sense. Assume

> (HFG) Every closed subgroup of H is topologically finitely generated.

Then, for every continuous epimorphism \(\chi:G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z_p\), its closed kernel N, if topologically finitely generated, is topologically finitely presented.

The usual meaning of a polycyclic pro-p group—a finite subnormal series of closed subgroups with procyclic factors—implies (HFG). Indeed, intersect the series with a closed subgroup; its factors are closed subgroups of procyclic groups and hence are procyclic. A finite extension sequence of finitely generated pro-p groups is finitely generated. The lemma is explicitly formulated using (HFG), so it does not depend on an unresolved interpretation of the word “polycyclic” in the problem statement.

## 1. The finite-orbit cohomology argument

Assume \(\chi(H)\ne0\). A nonzero closed subgroup of \(\mathbb Z_p\) is open. Let T be the standard pro-p tree of the proper amalgam. Since N is normal,

\[
N\backslash G/H\simeq \mathbb Z_p/\chi(H),\qquad
N\backslash G/A\simeq \mathbb Z_p/\chi(A),\qquad
N\backslash G/B\simeq \mathbb Z_p/\chi(B).
\]

These sets are finite, so N has finitely many edge and vertex orbits on T. For orbit representatives write \(N_e=N\cap H^g\), and \(N_v=N\cap A^g\) or \(N\cap B^g\), respectively. Each \(N_e\) is finitely generated by (HFG).

With trivial discrete coefficients \(k=\mathbb F_p\), the standard-tree exact sequence of compact permutation modules is

\[
0\longrightarrow k[[E(T)]]\longrightarrow k[[V(T)]]
\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0.
\]

Each module is a finite direct sum of modules \(k[[N/N_x]]\), because there are finitely many N-orbits. Applying continuous Ext into k and Shapiro gives the relevant part of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence:

\[
H^1(N,k)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_v H^1(N_v,k)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_e H^1(N_e,k)\longrightarrow
H^2(N,k)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_v H^2(N_v,k).
\tag{MV}
\]

This derives the required sequence directly from the N-action; it does not assume an abstract Bass–Serre decomposition of an arbitrary closed subgroup.

Suppose N is finitely generated. Then \(H^1(N,k)\) is finite dimensional. Every edge term \(H^1(N_e,k)\) is finite dimensional too. Exactness gives

\[
\sum_v\dim_kH^1(N_v,k)
\leq \dim_kH^1(N,k)+\sum_e\dim_kH^1(N_e,k)<\infty.
\]

Thus each vertex stabilizer is finitely generated. It is a closed subgroup of a conjugate of A or B, so coherence makes it finitely presented. Consequently every \(H^2(N_v,k)\) is finite dimensional. The second part of (MV) now gives

\[
\dim_kH^2(N,k)
\leq\sum_e\dim_kH^1(N_e,k)+\sum_v\dim_kH^2(N_v,k)<\infty.
\]

For a finitely generated pro-p group, finite-dimensional \(H^2(-,\mathbb F_p)\) is equivalent to finite pro-p presentability. Therefore N is finitely presented.

Only first cohomology of the edge stabilizers is used: finite presentation of edge stabilizers is unnecessary for this lemma.

## 2. A zero character on the edge precludes a finitely generated fiber

Assume \(\chi(H)=0\), and first assume H is proper in both A and B. The two images \(\chi(A)\), \(\chi(B)\) generate \(\mathbb Z_p\). Therefore at least one is nonzero, and the following cases exhaust the possibilities.

### 2a. Both vertex images are nonzero

The maps \(A\to\chi(A)\) and \(B\to\chi(B)\) kill H, and hence give a continuous epimorphism

\[
f:G\twoheadrightarrow F:=\chi(A)\amalg\chi(B)\cong F^{(p)}_2.
\]

There is an epimorphism \(\varepsilon:F\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z_p\) induced by the inclusions of the two images, satisfying \(\chi=\varepsilon f\). Hence \(f(N)=\ker\varepsilon=:L\).

Here L cannot be finitely generated. For n at least zero, let \(U_n=\varepsilon^{-1}(p^n\mathbb Z_p)\). The pro-p Schreier formula gives

\[
d(U_n)=1+[F:U_n](d(F)-1)=1+p^n.
\]

If L were finitely generated, lifting a topological generator of \(U_n/L\cong p^n\mathbb Z_p\) would give \(d(U_n)\leq d(L)+1\), uniformly in n. Indeed the closed subgroup generated by L and that lift has full quotient and contains L, so equals \(U_n\). This contradicts the displayed unbounded ranks. Since a continuous quotient of a finitely generated pro-p group is finitely generated, N cannot be finitely generated.

### 2b. Exactly one vertex image is zero

Relabel so that \(\chi(A)=0\) and \(\chi(B)=\mathbb Z_p\). Because H is a proper closed subgroup of A, there is a continuous epimorphism \(\lambda:A\twoheadrightarrow C_p\) with \(\lambda(H)=1\). To see this without assuming A finitely generated, choose an open normal U of A with \(HU\ne A\), pass to the finite p-group A/U, and put the image of H in a maximal subgroup. Its preimage is a normal open subgroup of A of index p containing H.

The pair \((\lambda,\chi|_B)\) induces an epimorphism

\[
f:G\twoheadrightarrow Q:=C_p\amalg\mathbb Z_p
\]

that intertwines \(\chi\) with the epimorphism \(\varepsilon:Q\to\mathbb Z_p\) killing \(C_p\). Again \(f(N)=L:=\ker\varepsilon\).

For a direct certificate that L is not finitely generated, put

\[
M=\mathbb F_p[[\mathbb Z_p]]
=\varprojlim_n\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z],
\qquad W=M\rtimes\mathbb Z_p,
\]

where \(\mathbb Z_p\) acts by translation. W is a pro-p group. Map the generator of \(C_p\) to the unit basis vector \(\delta_0\in M\), and map the \(\mathbb Z_p\) free factor identically to the top subgroup of W. These images topologically generate W: the translates of \(\delta_0\) span every finite quotient of M. Hence there is an epimorphism \(Q\twoheadrightarrow W\) intertwining the projections to \(\mathbb Z_p\), and its restriction maps L onto M. The elementary abelian pro-p group M has quotients of dimension \(p^n\), so is not topologically finitely generated. Thus neither L nor N is finitely generated.

### 2c. Degenerate edge inclusions

If one edge inclusion is onto a vertex, the amalgam is isomorphic to the other vertex. It is coherent by hypothesis, and every finitely generated closed subgroup, including N, is finitely presented. This completes the lemma in all cases.

## 3. Finitely generated abelian quotients are also excluded

The cyclic statement extends to every continuous epimorphism

\[
\varphi:G\twoheadrightarrow Q
\]

onto a topologically finitely generated abelian pro-p group Q: if \(N=\ker\varphi\) is finitely generated, then N is finitely presented.

For a nondegenerate amalgam, first suppose that \(S=\varphi(H)\) is not open in Q. A closed subgroup of a finitely generated abelian pro-p group has finite index if it has the same \(\mathbb Z_p\)-rank. Therefore \(Q/S\) has positive rank and admits a continuous epimorphism \(\psi:Q\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z_p\) vanishing on S. Put \(M=\ker(\psi\varphi)\). There is an exact sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\ker\psi\longrightarrow1.
\]

The group \(\ker\psi\) is finitely generated because it is a closed subgroup of the finitely generated abelian pro-p group Q. Since N is finitely generated, so is M. But \((\psi\varphi)(H)=0\), contradicting Section 2. Hence S must be open.

It follows that N has finitely many edge and vertex orbits: their orbit sets are \(Q/\varphi(H)\), \(Q/\varphi(A)\), and \(Q/\varphi(B)\). The argument of Section 1 applies verbatim. If Q is finite, openness is automatic and the same argument applies immediately. Degenerate amalgams are coherent as before.

This corollary rules out finitely generated non-finitely-presented fibers over all finitely generated abelian pro-p quotients, including higher-rank quotients and quotients with finite torsion.

## 4. What has and has not been established

The lemma and corollary rule out a finitely generated non-finitely-presented fiber over any finitely generated abelian pro-p quotient as the witness of incoherence of a proper amalgam in the stated class. They do not show that the ambient amalgam is coherent: an arbitrary finitely generated closed subgroup need not be a normal kernel with such a quotient, and its quotient of the standard tree may be infinite.

Thus this route is **blocked as a counterexample mechanism** under the exact hypotheses. Reopening it requires a materially different witness, not merely a different presentation of a cyclic fiber.
