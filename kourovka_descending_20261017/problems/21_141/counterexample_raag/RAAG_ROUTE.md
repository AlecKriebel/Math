# RAAG route: rigorous obstructions and exact residual gap

Checkpoint: 2026-10-17 21:16 UTC. Scoped-route completion estimate **95%**; overall Kourovka 21.141 discovery estimate **10%**.

## A. Canonical induced-subgraph amalgams cannot work

Let \(G_\Gamma\) denote the right-angled Artin pro-p group on a finite graph \(\Gamma\). A full subgroup on an induced subgraph \(\Lambda\) embeds because killing all vertices outside \(\Lambda\) is a retraction.

Suppose \(\Gamma=\Gamma_1\cup\Gamma_2\), with induced intersection \(\Lambda\), and use the canonical splitting

\[
G_\Gamma=G_{\Gamma_1}\amalg_{G_\Lambda}G_{\Gamma_2}.
\]

This is a proper pro-p splitting: the pro-p presentations coincide, and the factor maps have the preceding vertex-killing retractions.

If \(G_\Lambda\) is polycyclic, then \(\Lambda\) is complete. Otherwise two nonadjacent vertices generate a closed rank-two free pro-p subgroup, which has an infinitely generated closed kernel of an epimorphism onto \(\mathbb Z_p\), as proved by Schreier growth in `FIBER_OBSTRUCTION.md`. Such a subgroup cannot occur inside a pro-p group all of whose closed subgroups are finitely generated. Conversely a complete finite \(\Lambda\) gives \(G_\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z_p^{|V\Lambda|}\), which is polycyclic.

If both vertex RAAGs are coherent, Snopce–Zalesskii's coherence theorem makes both \(\Gamma_i\) chordal. The union of two chordal graphs along a clique is chordal. Here is an explicit graph check: an induced cycle of length at least four can contain at most two vertices of the intersection clique; if two, they must be consecutive. Removing those clique vertices leaves a connected path, which must lie wholly on one side because there are no cross edges outside the intersection. The same argument applies if the cycle meets the clique in one or no vertices. The cycle would therefore lie in one chordal factor, a contradiction. Thus \(G_\Gamma\) is coherent.

This proves a complete obstruction for canonical full-subgraph amalgams. It does not classify noncanonical polycyclic embeddings into RAAGs.

## B. The standard cycle witness has the wrong edge group

For a chordless cycle \(C_m\), \(m\ge4\), choose nonadjacent vertices v,w. It is the union of two induced paths \(T_1,T_2\) meeting in the two isolated vertices \(\{v,w\}\). Consequently

\[
G_{C_m}=G_{T_1}\amalg_{F^{(p)}(v,w)}G_{T_2}.
\]

The two path RAAGs are coherent; the edge is a rank-two free pro-p group and is not polycyclic. Thus the familiar incoherent example fails exactly the edge hypothesis of 21.141.

To verify its incoherence in the correct category, let \(\chi\) send every standard vertex to \(1\in\mathbb Z_p\), and write \(K_X=\ker(\chi|_{G_X})\). The restrictions to the intersection and to each path are onto. Snopce–Zalesskii's Lemma 5.3 gives the proper pro-p splitting

\[
K_{C_m}=K_{T_1}\amalg_{K_{\{v,w\}}}K_{T_2}.
\]

Each path kernel is a free pro-p group of finite rank (their Lemma 5.4), whereas \(K_{\{v,w\}}\) is free pro-p of infinite rank. The amalgam is therefore topologically generated by finitely many elements. Its continuous-cohomology Mayer–Vietoris sequence has a map from the finite-dimensional sum of the two vertex \(H^1\)'s to the infinite-dimensional edge \(H^1\), followed by \(H^2(K_{C_m},\mathbb F_p)\). Exactness forces this \(H^2\) to be infinite dimensional. Hence \(K_{C_m}\) is not finitely presented as a pro-p group.

This is a pro-p proof; it does not assume that an abstract kernel commutes with pro-p completion.

## C. A stronger exclusion of arbitrary cycle-RAAG splittings

The lemma in `FIBER_OBSTRUCTION.md` applies to every proper coherent/polycyclic amalgam, regardless of whether its vertex groups or embeddings are graphical. Since \(G_{C_m}\) has a finitely generated non-finitely-presented \(\mathbb Z_p\)-fiber, it cannot be isomorphic to any proper amalgam with coherent pro-p vertices and an edge satisfying (HFG). In particular it cannot itself be a counterexample to 21.141 using a noncanonical splitting.

More generally, **any** proposed ambient group with such a cyclic fiber is ruled out as an amalgam of this form. This substantially narrows the usual Bestvina–Brady mechanism.

## D. Exact remaining gap

An arbitrary nonchordal RAAG is not necessarily covered by the last argument. There are even incoherent RAAGs for which **every** finitely generated kernel onto \(\mathbb Z_p\) is finitely presented. Here is a checkable example.

Put \(R=G_{C_4}\). Add a vertex u joined to all four cycle vertices, and then a vertex w joined only to u. The resulting six-vertex RAAG is

\[
P=(R\times\mathbb Z_p(u))\amalg_{\mathbb Z_p(u)}
(\mathbb Z_p(u)\times\mathbb Z_p(w)).
\]

It is incoherent because it contains the closed bad subgroup already lying in R. This displayed splitting is not a counterexample to 21.141: its first vertex is incoherent.

For any continuous epimorphism \(\chi:P\to\mathbb Z_p\), if \(\chi(u)=0\), the zero-edge-character argument of `FIBER_OBSTRUCTION.md` shows that its kernel is not finitely generated. The edge is proper in both factors.

If \(\beta=\chi(u)\ne0\), the kernel has finitely many edge/vertex orbits. Write \(\alpha=\chi|_R\). Projection onto R identifies its intersection with \(R\times\mathbb Z_p(u)\) with

\[
U=\{r\in R:\alpha(r)\in\beta\mathbb Z_p\},
\qquad r\mapsto(r,-\alpha(r)/\beta).
\]

U is open in the finitely presented pro-p group R, so is finitely presented. The kernel's intersection with \(\mathbb Z_p(u)\times\mathbb Z_p(w)\) is procyclic, and its edge stabilizers are trivial. The finite quotient graph therefore gives a finitely presented pro-p kernel (equivalently apply the same finite-orbit cohomology sequence to these finitely presented vertices and trivial edges). Thus \(\ker\chi\) is finitely generated exactly when \(\chi(u)\ne0\), and in that case it is finitely presented.

This proves that the cyclic-fiber obstruction cannot by itself exclude arbitrary noncanonical coherent/polycyclic splittings of every nonchordal RAAG: incoherence need not be detected by any finitely generated global cyclic fiber.

The exact unsupported leap would be to assume that a bad closed subgroup inherited from an induced cycle has a finite quotient of the proposed amalgam tree, or is itself the kernel of a global character with finitely generated kernel. Neither has been proved here. A new mechanism is required to address it.
