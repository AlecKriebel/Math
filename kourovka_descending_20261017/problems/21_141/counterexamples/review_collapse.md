# Adversarial review of collapse and normal-edge claims

Checkpoint: 2026-10-17 21:20 UTC. Completion estimate: **100% of this three-claim audit**, not of Kourovka 21.141. All groups and homomorphisms below are pro-$p$ and continuous; subgroups are closed, and generation and finite presentation are in the pro-$p$ sense.

**Verdict.** All three proposed claims are valid. The collapse example is not a counterexample to coherence. The normal-edge argument proves a special positive case. The image-intersection statement admits a direct completed-permutation-module proof, so no properness or Bass–Serre assumption needs to be imported into that argument. No novelty conclusion is made.

## 1. The two unipotent actions force the edge image to vanish

Let $H=\mathbb Z_p^2$ and

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, the formulas $U^t=I+tE_{12}$ and $L^t=I+tE_{21}$ define continuous $\mathbb Z_p$-actions, including when $p=2$. Therefore

\[
A=H\rtimes_U\mathbb Z_p,\qquad B=H\rtimes_L\mathbb Z_p
\]

are pro-$p$ groups. Each has a normal series of length three with factors $\mathbb Z_p$: use $0<\mathbb Z_p e_1<H<A$ and $0<\mathbb Z_p e_2<H<B$. Explicitly, the multiplication in $A$ is

\[
(x,y,t)(x',y',t')=(x+x'+ty',y+y',t+t'),
\]

so $A$ is the $3\times3$ upper unitriangular group over $\mathbb Z_p$; $B$ is isomorphic to it by exchanging the coordinates of $H$. Both are polycyclic in the topological sense and coherent, since their closed subgroups are again polycyclic and finitely presented. No powerfulness assertion is needed, especially at $p=2$.

Let $G=A\amalg_H B$ be the categorical pushout, with no injectivity assumption. Put $M=\operatorname{im}(H\to G)$. This image is compact, hence closed and abelian. It is normalized by both factor images, and hence by $G$, because those images topologically generate $G$ and the normalizer of a closed subgroup is closed.

The surjection $H\twoheadrightarrow M$ gives an equivariant surjection

\[
\mathbb F_p^2=H/pH\twoheadrightarrow V=M/pM.
\]

Its kernel is invariant under both displayed matrices. A nonzero proper invariant subspace of $\mathbb F_p^2$ would be a line. A $U$-invariant line is the unique fixed line $\mathbb F_pe_1$, while an $L$-invariant line is $\mathbb F_pe_2$. Thus the kernel is either zero or the whole space.

If the kernel were zero, conjugation would give a continuous homomorphism $G\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ whose image contains $U$ and $L$. A continuous finite image of a pro-$p$ group is a $p$-group. For odd $p$, however,

\[
UL^{-1}U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

has order four. For $p=2$,

\[
UL^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

has order three. This is impossible. Consequently $V=0$, so $M=pM$ and $M=1$. For the last implication, either apply compact Nakayama to the quotient of $\mathbb Z_p^2$, or note that every finite abelian $p$-quotient $Q$ of $M$ would satisfy $Q=pQ$ and hence vanish.

It follows from the pushout property that

\[
G\cong(A/H)\amalg(B/H)\cong\mathbb Z_p\amalg\mathbb Z_p=F_2^{(p)}.
\]

This is a nonproper amalgam with total edge collapse and a coherent free pushout, for every prime $p$.

## 2. A polycyclic edge normal in both factors gives coherence

**Claim.** Suppose $A,B$ are coherent pro-$p$ groups, and $H$ is a common polycyclic closed subgroup normal in both. Their categorical pro-$p$ pushout is coherent even when its factor maps are not injective.

The following facts suffice.

1. Every closed subgroup and continuous quotient of a polycyclic pro-$p$ group is polycyclic. This follows by intersecting or projecting a finite series with procyclic factors. Such groups are finitely generated and finitely presented. Finite presentation follows inductively from the extension property in item 4.
2. If $C$ is coherent and $N\triangleleft C$ is finitely generated, then $C/N$ is coherent. For a finitely generated closed $D\le C/N$, its full preimage $P$ is generated by generators of $N$ and lifts of generators of $D$. Hence $P$ is finitely presented by coherence. Its quotient $D=P/N$ is finitely presented after adding a finite generating set of $N$ as normal relations.
3. A free pro-$p$ product of two coherent groups is coherent. For a finitely generated closed subgroup $K\le C\amalg D$, the finitely generated pro-$p$ Kurosh theorem decomposes $K$ as a finite free pro-$p$ product of a finite-rank free group and finitely generated intersections with conjugates of $C,D$. Each intersection is finitely presented, and taking a finite free product preserves finite presentation.
4. Extensions of finitely presented pro-$p$ groups are finitely presented. One verification uses the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence: in an extension $1\to N\to E\to Q\to1$, the terms contributing to $H^2(E,\mathbb F_p)$ are bounded by $H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)$, $H^1(Q,H^1(N,\mathbb F_p))$, and $H^0(Q,H^2(N,\mathbb F_p))$. They are finite-dimensional when $N,Q$ are finitely presented: the middle term is finite because $Q$ is finitely generated and its coefficient module is finite-dimensional. Also $E$ is finitely generated. The pro-$p$ finite-presentation criterion then applies.

For item 3 the precise primary theorem read in this audit is Dan Haran, *On closed subgroups of free products of profinite groups*, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) **55** (1987), 266–298, **Theorem 9.7, p. 298** ([author PDF](https://www.tau.ac.il/~haran/Papers/closed_subgroups.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/plms/s3-55_2.266)). Its separability restriction holds for finitely generated pro-$p$ groups, which are countably based. Finite generation forces finitely many nontrivial Kurosh factors: otherwise partition the factor index space into arbitrarily many clopen pieces containing nontrivial factors. The corresponding finite coproduct decomposition would force arbitrarily large Frattini-quotient dimension. Each surviving factor is a retract of $K$, hence finitely generated. This avoids assuming an unrestricted Kurosh theorem for arbitrary closed subgroups of large cardinality. The earlier primary article [Herfort–Ribes, *Subgroups of free pro-p-products* (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100066548) also advertises the finitely generated case, but its full theorem text was not used here.

Now let $M$ be the common image of $H$ in $G=A\amalg_HB$. As in section 1, $M$ is closed and normal in $G$. It is a quotient of $H$, hence polycyclic. Universal properties give

\[
G/M\cong(A/H)\amalg(B/H).
\]

Items 2 and 3 show that $G/M$ is coherent. For a finitely generated closed subgroup $K\le G$, its image in $G/M$ is finitely generated and therefore finitely presented. Its kernel $K\cap M$ is polycyclic and therefore finitely presented. Item 4 proves that $K$ is finitely presented. This proves the claim.

**Scope boundary.** Normality in both factors is essential to this proof. It does not establish coherence for arbitrary polycyclic amalgamation, and it does not say that arbitrary quotients of coherent groups are coherent.

## 3. Image factors are a proper pushout over exactly the edge image

Let $G=A\amalg_HB$ be any categorical pushout of pro-$p$ groups, and write $\bar A,\bar B,C$ for the images of $A,B,H$ in $G$. These are compact closed subgroups.

Given compatible continuous homomorphisms $f:\bar A\to T$ and $g:\bar B\to T$ agreeing on $C$, compose with the quotient maps from $A,B$. They agree on $H$, so the original universal property supplies a unique continuous $G\to T$. Since $A\twoheadrightarrow\bar A$ and $B\twoheadrightarrow\bar B$ are surjective, the induced map restricts to $f,g$. Thus $G$ is the pushout $\bar A\amalg_C\bar B$, and both structure maps are inclusions. The passage to image groups creates no quotient-topology issue: continuous surjections from compact groups to Hausdorff groups are quotient maps.

The stronger equality $\bar A\cap\bar B=C$ can be checked directly, without invoking a proper-amalgam theorem. For open normal $N\triangleleft_oG$, let

\[
W_N=\mathbb F_p[G/CN],\qquad
W=\varprojlim_NW_N=\mathbb F_p[[G/C]],\qquad m=([CN])_N\in W.
\]

Transition maps send coset basis vectors to their images. The natural $G$-action on $W$ is continuous, and $W\rtimes G$ is pro-$p$, being the inverse limit of the finite $p$-groups $W_N\rtimes(G/N)$. Define

\[
f(a)=(a\cdot m-m,a)\quad(a\in\bar A),\qquad
g(b)=(0,b)\quad(b\in\bar B).
\]

The coboundary identity makes $f$ a homomorphism. Every $c\in C$ fixes $m$, so $f,g$ agree on $C$. The pushout property therefore extends them to a homomorphism $G\to W\rtimes G$. If $x\in\bar A\cap\bar B$, its two images force $x\cdot m=m$. The stabilizer of $m$ is exactly $C$: if $x\notin C$, closedness gives some $N$ with $x\notin CN$, and the two distinct basis vectors $[xCN]$ and $[CN]$ in $W_N$ differ. Hence $x\in C$, as required.

In particular, a mechanism requiring the two image factors to acquire an intersection strictly larger than the compact edge image cannot occur for a categorical pro-$p$ pushout. For an abstract amalgam followed by pro-$p$ completion, the same argument uses the **closure** of the edge image. This should be distinguished from intrinsic edge-completion injectivity, which can certainly fail.
