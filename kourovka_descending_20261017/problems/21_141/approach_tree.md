## 1. Exact target and success criterion

Let `G = G1 coproduct_H G2` be a **proper** free pro-p amalgam: the canonical
maps from `Gi` into `G` are embeddings. Every subgroup below is closed and
generation/presentation is topological. Suppose that each `Gi` is coherent and
that `H` has a finite subnormal series with procyclic factors. The target is:

> Every finitely generated closed subgroup `K <= G` is finitely presented
> as a pro-p group.

The properness convention is substantive. Replacing noninjective vertex maps
by their images does not preserve coherence automatically, so it cannot remove
that hypothesis from a theorem. The official problem's intended convention
should be retained from its primary pro-p usage; the combination results cited
here explicitly use proper amalgams.

The standard pro-p tree `T` has vertices `G/G1` and `G/G2`, and edges `G/H`.
For the restricted `K`-action,

`K_(gGi) = K intersect gGi g^-1`,
`K_(gH) = K intersect gH g^-1`.

Closed subgroups of a group with a finite procyclic series again have such a
series: intersect the series with the subgroup and use that the resulting
factor is a closed subgroup of the original procyclic factor. In particular
every edge stabilizer is finitely generated. This is an elementary induction,
not an inference that arbitrary subgroups of a finitely generated pro-p group
are finitely generated.

**Success for this mechanism:** exhibit `K` as the pro-p fundamental group of
a finite graph whose vertex groups are finitely generated subgroups of
conjugates of `Gi`, and whose edge groups are finitely generated. Section 2
then supplies a finite presentation.

## 2. Three elementary lemmas, with checkable proofs

### Lemma A: finite graph presentation

If a finite graph of pro-p groups has finitely presented vertex groups and
finitely generated edge groups, its pro-p fundamental group is finitely
presented. Properness is unnecessary for this formal assertion, though it is
needed when identifying vertex groups with subgroups of an ambient group.

Choose a maximal tree. Take all vertex generators, vertex relators, and one
stable letter for each edge outside that tree. For each edge take a finite
topological generating set `S_e`; impose the relation equating its two images,
with conjugation by the stable letter where needed, for every element of
`S_e`. Equality of the two continuous homomorphisms on `S_e` gives equality on
the entire edge group. This finite pro-p presentation satisfies exactly the
universal property of the fundamental group.

### Lemma B: finitely generated fundamental group plus finite graph and
finitely generated edges implies finitely generated vertex groups

Let `P` be the finitely generated pro-p fundamental group of a finite graph,
and fix a vertex `v`. In its vertex group `V`, let `N_v` be the closed normal
subgroup generated by all incident edge images. There is a continuous
epimorphism `P -> V/N_v`: use the natural quotient on `V` and kill every other
vertex group and all stable letters. Every edge relation is respected. Thus
`V/N_v` is finitely generated.

Choose finitely many lifts of its generators, and add finite generating sets
of all incident edge images. Let `W <= V` be their closed generated subgroup.
Its closed normal closure is `V`. If `W` were proper, a finite p-group quotient
would have its image proper; a maximal subgroup of that finite p-group gives
an index-p open subgroup of `V` containing `W`. Such a subgroup is normal, a
contradiction. Hence `W=V`, proving the claim. This argument avoids finite-word
normal forms for elements of `P`.

### Lemma C: lifting a finite graph through an action kernel

Suppose `1 -> N -> K -> Q -> 1` is exact and `Q` is the proper fundamental
group of a finite graph of pro-p groups. Take the full preimages in `K` of
every vertex group. For an edge choose the full preimage of its first image
in `Q`; its second embedding is obtained using a lift of the corresponding
stable letter. On a maximal tree use the literal common subgroup. These
choices define a finite graph with full preimage vertex and edge groups.

Let its fundamental group be `P`. The construction gives `P -> K`. The copy
of `N` in each vertex is identified along the maximal tree, and the remaining
edge relations make that common copy normal under the stable letters, with
the conjugation action prescribed by `K`. It is also normal in each vertex.
It follows that `P/N` is the original graph presentation of `Q`. The map
`P -> K` is onto: its image is compact and maps onto `Q`, while containing
`N`. Its kernel is contained in `N` because `P/N -> Q` is an isomorphism, and
is trivial on `N`. Thus `P -> K` is an isomorphism. Properness follows as well,
since each preimage vertex maps to its given embedded subgroup of `K`.

If `N` is finitely generated, preimages of finitely generated vertex groups
are finitely generated (lift generators of the quotient and append generators
of `N`). This kernel step matters because the primary source below has a
standing faithful-action convention.

The first paper's conventions 2.2 require faithful actions and proper graphs.
Its Theorem 5.1 gives a finite stabilizer graph under finiteness of conjugacy
classes of maximal vertex stabilizers. Theorem 6.3 gives the corresponding
conclusion from accessibility over the edge family. Its Theorem 6.8 provides
finite graphs with finitely generated vertex groups when all edge stabilizers
are procyclic; vertex groups embed into vertex stabilizers. Its introduction
and Definition 2.5 explicitly distinguish profinite connectivity from
abstract path connectivity. These are source inputs, not new results here.

The second paper's Corollary 3.14 already proves the coherence conclusion
when `H` is analytic and malnormal in one factor. Its Theorem 3.15 gives finite
subgroup graphs for finite reduced `k`-acylindrical pro-p graphs. An arbitrary
polycyclic amalgamation need not satisfy that acylindricity hypothesis.

**Deduction: the procyclic-amalgamation boundary is affirmative.**
For finitely generated `K <= G`, let `N` be the kernel of its action on `T`.
Then `N` is a closed subgroup of any edge stabilizer, so is procyclic and
finitely generated. The quotient `Q=K/N` acts faithfully with procyclic edge
stabilizers. Apply Theorem 6.8 to `Q`, then Lemma C. Each lifted vertex group
is finitely generated and lies in a conjugate of a `Gi`; consequently it is
finitely presented by coherence. Lifted edge groups lie in procyclic edge
stabilizers and are finitely generated. Lemma A makes `K` finitely presented.
If the action fixes a vertex, coherence applies directly to `K`.

For a general polycyclic `H`, the same reasoning works **provided** the
faithful quotient action has a finite graph as in the stated success
criterion. The finitely generated action kernel causes no further gap.

No novelty is asserted for these deductions of existing combination results.
The source's older 2020 author-hosted PDF has different theorem numbering;
use the cached v2 source for the numbers above.

## 4. Exact gap and rejected finite-core shortcuts

The required statement still lacking is:

> **Finite-decomposition lemma (unproved).** For every finitely generated
> `K <= G1 coproduct_H G2` under the target hypotheses, the faithful quotient
> of the restricted standard-tree action has a finite graph of pro-p groups
> with vertex groups conjugate into vertex stabilizers and edge groups
> conjugate into edge stabilizers.

It would suffice instead to establish accessibility over the edge-stabilizer
family, or finitely many conjugacy classes of maximal vertex stabilizers,
with the exact hypotheses required by the source. Neither follows here from
Noetherianity of a single edge group. Conjugate edge stabilizers live in
different copies of `H`, and finite generation of `K` gives no finite-word
support for an arbitrary topological generator.

The following attempted proofs are invalid:

* Joining a vertex to its finitely many generator translates by **finite**
  paths, then taking their translates. A pro-p geodesic can be infinite as
  an abstract graph, and pro-p generators need not be finite amalgam words.
* Assuming the quotient graph has a maximal subtree with a continuous lift.
  This is not automatic for an infinite profinite quotient.
* Repeatedly applying a splitting theorem until it stops. Termination is the
  missing accessibility assertion.
* Assuming a relation/rank bound for `K` that presupposes finite presentation.
  That would be circular for this problem.

Thus the generic finite-core route is **blocked** rather than a proof. It
may be reopened for a mechanism deriving finite decomposition from the
particular coherent vertex embeddings, rather than postulating it.

## 5. Elementary obstruction to extending the procyclic graph-size bound

This is an independently constructed diagnostic family, not a counterexample
to coherence. For each integer `n>=1`, use the path with vertices `0,...,n`.
At vertex `i` take

`A_i = Z_p^2 = <a_i,b_i | [a_i,b_i]=1>`.

For the edge between `i` and `i+1`, take `E_i=Z_p^2=<u_i,v_i>` and boundary
maps

`u_i -> a_i^p`, `v_i -> b_i`,
`u_i -> a_(i+1)`, `v_i -> b_(i+1)^p`.

Both edge images have index `p`, so every edge is reduced. There is a
compatible map of the graph to `Z_p^2` carrying

`a_i -> (p^i,0)`, `b_i -> (0,p^(n-i))`.

It is injective on each `A_i`; therefore the universal maps from `A_i` into
the pro-p fundamental group `P_n` are injective. This proves properness
without any residual-p assertion about an abstract amalgam.

Put `x=a_0` and `y=b_n`. Eliminating the other generators gives exactly

`P_n = <x,y | [x^(p^i),y^(p^(n-i))]=1, 0<=i<=n>_(pro-p)`.

The displayed abelian quotient is onto (because it contains the images of
`x,y`), so `d(P_n)=2`. The graph has `n` edges, although every edge has the
same isomorphism type `Z_p^2`. Thus a bound on graph size depending solely
on the generator number and the edge-group isomorphism type cannot hold
for arbitrary proper reduced finite graphs.

The standard action in this initial family is **not faithful**. Indeed
`C=<x^(p^n),y^(p^n)>` is `Z_p^2`, belongs to all vertex and edge groups, and
is central since it commutes with the generators of each `A_i`. It is the
entire kernel: a kernel lies in every `A_i`, and the displayed injective
coordinate map shows that their intersection is exactly `C`.

Fresh adversarial review in `tree_rank2_review.md` verified both faithful
strengthenings below:

1. The quotient `P_n/C` keeps the same proper reduced path, now with vertex
   groups `C_(p^(n-i)) x C_(p^i)` and edge groups
   `C_(p^(n-i-1)) x C_(p^i)`. The action is faithful, `d=2`, and all edge
   generator numbers are at most two, while `n` is arbitrary. Here the
   isomorphism type of the edge groups varies with `n`.
2. Replace `A_0` by `A_0 coproduct Z_p`, leaving the edge maps unchanged.
   The new fundamental group is `Q_n=P_n coproduct Z_p`, so `d(Q_n)=3`.
   The path remains proper and reduced, with all edge groups `Z_p^2`.
   Its kernel lies in `P_n` and, by normality, in a distinct conjugate of
   `P_n` by the new free generator. Malnormality of free factors in a free
   pro-p product makes that intersection trivial.

For the embedding of `A_0 coproduct Z_p` in the second construction, one can
first attach a separate `Z_p` vertex to `A_0` by a trivial edge. This enlarged
graph is proper, with fundamental group `P_n coproduct Z_p`. Apply the proper
finite connected-subgraph embedding lemma (Castellano–Zalesskii Lemma 2.15)
to its `A_0 --1-- Z_p` subgraph, then contract that subgraph. The resulting
graph is the claimed proper path. The intersection of distinct free factors
can also be checked on their explicit two-edge path in the trivial-edge
standard tree. No infinite abstract-path assertion is used.

The verified faithful construction is freely decomposable. It makes no
claim against a bound restricted to freely indecomposable ambient groups.

For a different quantifier, the finite quotient `R_n=P_n/C`, `n>=2`, is
freely indecomposable. To check this, let it act on the standard tree of a
putative nontrivial free pro-p splitting. Each finite vertex group `A_i/C`
fixes a vertex (the finite p-group fixed-point theorem, e.g.
Chatzidakis–Zalesskii Theorem 2.20). In a tree with trivial edge stabilizers
a nontrivial subgroup fixes at most one vertex. The adjacent finite vertex
groups share `E_i/C`, of nontrivial order `p^(n-1)`, so they fix the same
vertex. All vertex groups generate `R_n`, which therefore fixes that vertex,
contradicting a nontrivial free splitting. Thus even with faithfulness and
free indecomposability no bound depending only on `d` and the generator rank
of finite edge groups exists. In this variant edge isomorphism types and
orders vary. This observation is a self-check in addition to the review;
retain the fixed-type/free-decomposability distinction above.

Even the strengthened diagnostic does **not** exhibit one fixed group with
unbounded decompositions. The groups depend on `n`. It does not disprove
group-dependent accessibility, the finite-decomposition lemma, or coherence.
It blocks only a universal graph-size bound of the indicated form. Any use
of it must retain those quantifiers.

## 6. Additional positive boundary: an open normal core in the edge group

Suppose there exists `N0 normal G` such that `N0 <= H` and `[H:N0]<infinity`.
Then the target coherence conclusion holds under the other hypotheses.

The finite subgroup `H/N0` in `G/N0` has trivial intersection with some open
normal subgroup `U` of `G/N0`: separate each of its finitely many nonidentity
elements by a finite quotient and intersect the resulting kernels. Let `U0`
be the preimage of `U` in `G`. Its intersection with every conjugate of `H`
is exactly `N0`. Thus for every finitely generated `K<=U0`, every edge
stabilizer in the restricted standard-tree action is the same finitely
generated subgroup `N=K intersect N0`. This is its action kernel. Consequently
`K/N` acts faithfully with trivial edge stabilizers. Use Theorem 6.8, Lemma C,
and Lemma A just as in the procyclic case; lifted vertex groups are finitely
generated subgroups of conjugates of the coherent `Gi`. Hence `U0` is
coherent.

Coherence passes from an open subgroup `U0` to `G`: for finitely generated
`K<=G`, its open subgroup `K intersect U0` is finitely generated by the finite
pro-p Schreier formula and is finitely presented by coherence. A finite
extension of a finitely presented pro-p group is finitely presented. This
last assertion can be checked by adjoining finitely many coset symbols to a
finite presentation of an open normal subgroup and imposing their finitely
many conjugation and multiplication-table relations. Replace `U0` by its
finite-index normal core first if necessary.

This proves, in particular, the normal-amalgamation and finite-amalgamation
boundaries. It does not show that arbitrary polycyclic `H` has such an open
normal core.

## 7. Cylinder/commensurator mechanism: exact additional obstruction

At the **2026-10-17 21:18 UTC** checkpoint the root researcher had proposed contracting
commensurability classes of full-rank edge stabilizers. This subsection was
added after independence of the original route had been preserved.

Commensurable conjugates of a polycyclic edge group plausibly have convex
classes: a common full-rank subgroup fixes their pro-p geodesic, so every
intermediate full-rank edge stabilizer contains it with finite index. This
uses fixed pro-p subtrees, not finite abstract paths. Even granting that
observation, it does not establish that a cylinder is closed, that its
quotient is a profinite tree, or that its stabilizer is finitely generated.

The root located the relevant primary result of **Berdugo–Zalesskii**,
*Pro-C generalized Baumslag–Solitar groups and profinite commensurators*,
published **27 February 2026**, Sao Paulo J. Math. Sci. 20, article 2,
<https://doi.org/10.1007/s40863-025-00523-1>. Proposition 3.2 shows that a
finite set of commensurating elements normalizes one common open subgroup
of `H`; Corollary 3.3 applies when the **closure** of the algebraic
commensurator is finitely generated. Their Definition 3.1 deliberately uses
that closure. These are prior results, not new lemmas here.

The proposition's short proof checks: take an ambient open normal `W` such
that `W intersect H` lies in the finitely many pairwise intersections.
Conjugation by each selected generator sends `W intersect H` to a subgroup
containing it. A closed subgroup of a profinite group cannot be conjugate
to a proper subgroup of itself, since a suitable finite quotient would
preserve that strict inclusion but give conjugate finite subgroups the same
order. Equality follows, and then normality under the generated closed group.

Here is an explicit topology diagnostic showing that the closure issue is
real in general, even for a procyclic subgroup. For each `n>=1`, let

`B_n = (C_(p^n))^p semidirect C_p`,

where the last factor cyclically permutes the `p` base coordinates. Let
`h_n` generate the first base coordinate, `s_n` be the cyclic shift, and put

`B = product_(n>=1) B_n`, `h=(h_n)`, `H=closure<h>`, `s=(s_n)`.

The map `a in Z_p -> (h_n^(a mod p^n))` embeds `Z_p` as `H`: its kernel is
the intersection of all `p^n Z_p`, and its image is compact and generated
densely by the integer powers of `h`. Every finite-support element `b` of
`B` centralizes `h^(p^m)` for some `m`, since all affected coordinates then
vanish. Consequently the algebraic commensurator of `H` contains the dense
restricted direct sum of the finite `B_n`.

On the other hand, conjugation by `s` moves the support in every coordinate
to a different base coordinate. Equality `h^a=(h^s)^b` forces
`a=b=0 mod p^n` for every `n`; hence `H intersect H^s=1`. Thus `s` does not
commensurate `H`. The algebraic commensurator is proper dense, so not closed.
The closure `B` is infinitely generated; this does not contradict the cited
finite-generation result.

This also gives a concrete failure of automatic cylinder closedness in a
proper standard-tree setting. Form `G*=B coproduct_H B` using the same
embedding on both sides. The maps of both factors identically to `B` give
retractions, hence the amalgam is proper. Truncate `s` to its first `m`
coordinates, obtaining finite-support shifts `s_m -> s`. The edges
`s_m H` converge to `sH` in the standard edge space `G*/H`. Their stabilizers
are commensurate with the stabilizer `H` of the base edge, but the limiting
edge stabilizer has trivial intersection with `H`. Consequently that
commensurability class of edges is not closed.

This ambient example does not assert that `B` is coherent or is an amalgam
factor satisfying the target coherence hypotheses. It shows why a cylinder argument
must prove an extra finite-generation/closedness theorem rather than silently
identify algebraic commensurators with closed subgroup stabilizers. Even if
one obtains a closed cylinder stabilizer, its new incident edge groups can
be commensurators in vertex groups, which need not be polycyclic. An induction
on Hirsch rank is therefore **blocked** until it controls both these new
edge groups and the topology of the collapse.

## Root clarification after fresh followup audit (2026-10-17 21:28 UTC)

The faithful finite-edge quotient R_n is freely indecomposable for n>=2, with an elementary proof that avoids assumptions about uniqueness of fixed vertices along arbitrary infinite pro-p geodesics. Its generator number is two. A nontrivial free pro-p splitting would have two factors of generator number one by additivity of generator number, hence both factors procyclic. But the embedded finite vertex A_1/C is C_(p^(n-1)) x C_p, which is noncyclic for n>=2. A finite p subgroup of a free pro-p product is conjugate into a factor (equivalently it fixes a vertex of the standard free-product tree). It cannot embed in a procyclic factor. Thus no such free splitting exists. For n=1 the quotient is C_p coproduct C_p and is decomposable. This R_n family varies its finite edge isomorphism types; the distinct Q_n family has fixed nontrivial edge type Z_p^2 and is freely decomposable. Neither observation asserts a single fixed group is inaccessible. Fresh review: reviews/partial_adversary01.md, section6.
