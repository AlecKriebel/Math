## Claim and category

The target is coherence of a proper pro-p amalgam

\[
G=G_1\amalg_H G_2
\]

when both vertices are coherent and the edge is polycyclic in the pro-p
sense. Every subgroup and normal closure below is closed, generation is
topological, and every presentation is a presentation in the category of
pro-p groups. A proper amalgam means that both canonical vertex maps are
injective. A nonproper categorical pushout is not covered by the argument.

For the elementary partial results, the explicit edge hypothesis is
**polyprocyclic**: a finite subnormal series of closed subgroups with
procyclic factors. This makes the convention checkable rather than relying
on an unverified interpretation of the word “polycyclic.” The arguments
also apply whenever the kernel in question has every closed subgroup
finitely generated and finitely presented.

## 1. Three elementary closure lemmas

### Lemma 1: finite presentations pass through extensions

If

\[
1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow1
\]

is an exact sequence of pro-p groups, with N and Q finitely presented,
then E is finitely presented.

**Proof.** Write finite pro-p presentations

\[
N=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_a\mid R_1,\ldots,R_b\rangle,
\qquad
Q=\langle y_1,\ldots,y_c\mid S_1,\ldots,S_d\rangle.
\]

Choose lifts of the y_j in E. Each conjugate y_j^{-1}x_i y_j and
y_j x_i y_j^{-1} is an element of N, so choose pro-p words
u_{ij}^{+}(x),u_{ij}^{-}(x) representing these elements. Each lifted
S_k(y) is also in N; choose a pro-p word v_k(x) representing it.
Let P be presented by the x_i,y_j with relators

\[
R_l(x),\quad
y_j^{-1}x_i y_j=u_{ij}^{+}(x),\quad
y_j x_i y_j^{-1}=u_{ij}^{-}(x),\quad
S_k(y)=v_k(x).
\]

These are finitely many relators. Let N_0 be the subgroup of P generated
by the x_i. Both conjugation directions were imposed, so every y_j
normalizes N_0. The normalizer of a closed subgroup of a compact group
is closed; therefore N_0 is normal in P. There is a surjection N→N_0,
because the R_l hold in N_0. The natural map P→E exists and is surjective;
its restriction composed with N→N_0 is the original embedding of N in E.
Consequently N→N_0 is injective as well as surjective.

After quotienting P by N_0, its presentation is exactly the displayed
presentation of Q. Thus P/N_0→Q is an isomorphism. The kernel of P→E
is contained in N_0 and its intersection with N_0 is trivial, proving
P≅E. All maps are continuous by the universal property of a free pro-p
presentation. ∎

Both conjugation directions were imposed to give a direct normality
argument. In this profinite setting one direction is already sufficient:
a closed subgroup cannot be conjugate to a proper subgroup, as recalled
in Proposition 6a below. The extra relators are therefore redundant but
hold in E and do not affect the proof.

### Lemma 2: coherent quotients by finitely generated normal kernels

If A is coherent and N is a finitely generated normal subgroup of A,
then A/N is coherent.

**Proof.** For a finitely generated subgroup B≤A/N, let A_B be its full
preimage. The group A_B is generated by finitely many lifts of generators
of B and a finite generating set for N. Hence A_B is finitely presented
by coherence of A. A presentation for B is obtained by adding the
finitely many generators of N as relators. Their normal closure is N,
because N is normal and they generate N as a subgroup. ∎

Here A itself need not be finitely generated. The preimage A_B is closed
because B is compact and therefore closed in A/N.

### Lemma 3: a Noetherian coherent normal kernel preserves coherence

Suppose N is normal in E, every closed subgroup of N is finitely generated
and finitely presented, and E/N is coherent. Then E is coherent.

**Proof.** For any finitely generated L≤E, its intersection M=L∩N is
finitely generated and finitely presented. The continuous image LN/N
of compact L is a closed, finitely generated subgroup of E/N, so is
finitely presented. Apply Lemma 1 to

\[
1\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow LN/N\longrightarrow1.
\]

Thus every such L is finitely presented. ∎

### Polyprocyclic kernels satisfy the hypotheses

Every closed subgroup of a polyprocyclic pro-p group is polyprocyclic.
Indeed, intersect a finite subnormal procyclic series with the subgroup;
the resulting factors embed as closed subgroups of procyclic factors.
The images are closed because they are continuous images of compact
groups. Closed subgroups of a procyclic pro-p group are procyclic.

A procyclic pro-p group is either Z_p or a finite cyclic p-group and has
a finite pro-p presentation. Induction on the series, using Lemma 1,
shows that every closed subgroup of a polyprocyclic pro-p group is
finitely generated and finitely presented. Quotients are polyprocyclic
by the corresponding image-series argument.

## 2. Affirmative special cases

### Proposition 4: an edge normal in both vertices

If H is polyprocyclic and normal in both G_1 and G_2, and the proper
amalgam has coherent vertices, then G is coherent.

**Proof.** The normalizer of H in G is closed and contains G_1 and G_2,
whose union generates G. Hence H is normal in G. The universal property
of the amalgam gives

\[
G/H\cong(G_1/H)\amalg(G_2/H).
\]

Each quotient vertex is coherent by Lemma 2. Free pro-p products of
coherent groups are coherent; this follows, for example, from the
H=1 instance of Castellano–Zalesskii's Corollary 3.14 cited below.
Now apply Lemma 3 to the polyprocyclic normal kernel H. ∎

Properness ensures that the H appearing in each group is the same
embedded compact group. This proof does not apply Lemma 2 to arbitrary
images of coherent groups in a nonproper pushout.

### Proposition 5: malnormality after removing a normal kernel

More generally, suppose C≤H is polyprocyclic and is normal in both
vertices. Suppose H/C is analytic and malnormal in G_1/C. Then G is
coherent.

**Proof.** As above C is normal in G, and the quotient is the proper
amalgam

\[
G/C\cong (G_1/C)\amalg_{H/C}(G_2/C).
\]

Its vertex maps remain injective because G_i∩C=C. The quotient vertices
are coherent by Lemma 2. Castellano–Zalesskii, Corollary 3.14, proves
coherence of this quotient amalgam. Apply Lemma 3 to C. ∎

The normal-edge case is C=H; the existing malnormal-edge theorem is C=1.
No claim is made that every edge in the problem admits such a C.

## 3. An additional elementary commensuration mechanism

### Lemma 6: a closed finitely generated commensurated subgroup of a
### profinite group has finite-index normal core

Let A be profinite and H≤A closed and topologically finitely generated.
Assume H and H^a are commensurable for every a∈A, where H^a=a^{-1}Ha.
Then core_A(H)=⋂_{a∈A}H^a is open in H.

**Proof.** A topologically finitely generated profinite group has only
finitely many open subgroups of each given finite index: such a subgroup
gives a continuous permutation action on a finite set, and a map into
that finite symmetric group is determined by a fixed finite generating
set. Thus the open subgroups of H can be enumerated as K_1,K_2,....

For each n set

\[
F_n=\{a\in A:K_n\leq H^a\}.
\]

This is closed, since it is the intersection, over k∈K_n, of the closed
sets {a:aka^{-1}∈H}. The commensuration assumption implies ⋃F_n=A:
H∩H^a is one of the open subgroups K_n. By the Baire theorem one F_n
has nonempty interior. Choose a_0 and an open subgroup V≤A such that
a_0 V⊂F_n. Then

\[
N_0=\bigcap_{v\in V}(H^{a_0})^v
\]

contains K_n and is V-normal. Since K_n≤H^{a_0} is open in H and
H is commensurable with H^{a_0}, it is also open in H^{a_0}. Therefore
N_0 is open in H^{a_0} and commensurable with H.

Because V normalizes N_0 and is open in A, core_A(N_0) is the intersection
of finitely many conjugates of N_0. Those conjugates are all commensurable
with H, so their finite intersection N is commensurable with H. It is
normal in A and contained in H^{a_0}. Consequently N=N^{a_0^{-1}}≤H.
Thus N is an open subgroup of H contained in core_A(H), as required. ∎

This version assumes commensuration by every element of profinite A and
does not require A to be finitely generated. A stronger conclusion
under finite generation is already in the literature, as follows.

### Known Proposition 6a: finite generating commensurators

Berdugo–Zalesskii, Proposition 3.2, proves: if a profinite A is
topologically generated by a_1,...,a_m and H is commensurable with each
H^{a_i}, then H has an open subgroup normal in A. H need not be finitely
generated. Their proof is short. Choose U normal and open in A with
U∩H contained in every H^{a_i}. Then

\[
U\cap H\ \leq\ U\cap H^{a_i}=(U\cap H)^{a_i}.
\]

A closed subgroup of a profinite group cannot be conjugate to a proper
subgroup: a strict inclusion remains strict in a suitable finite
quotient, where conjugate subgroups have equal order. Thus each displayed
inclusion is equality. U∩H is normalized by all a_i and hence by A.

This existing proposition handles finite abstract generating sets whose
closures generate A; no continuity of an abstract commensurator map is
needed. The paper's term **profinite commensurator** means the closure
of the set of actual commensurating elements. Its closed-set convention
must not be silently substituted for actual elementwise commensuration.

### Lemma 7: common normal kernel for commensurated amalgamation

In the proper amalgam G=G_1 amalg_H G_2, suppose H is finitely generated
and commensurated by every element of each vertex. Then H contains a
subgroup N open in H and normal in G.

**Proof.** Lemma 6 supplies C_i=core_{G_i}(H), open in H and normal in
G_i. Choose an open normal U of G with

\[
U\cap H\leq C_1\cap C_2.
\]

Such a U exists because H inherits its compact topology from its proper
embedding in G and C_1∩C_2 is open in H. Put N=U∩H. For i=1,2,

\[
N=U\cap C_i,
\]

so N is normal in each G_i, and hence in G. It is open in H. ∎

Consequently, in the polyprocyclic commensurated-edge case, quotienting
by N reduces the edge to a finite p-group. The standard finite-edge
combination theorem together with Lemmas 2 and 3 proves coherence of G.
This is another restricted case, not a reduction of arbitrary edges.

For clarity, the finite-edge combination theorem can be seen by choosing
an open normal subgroup W of the proper amalgam intersecting the finite
edge trivially. Its finite graph of groups has trivial edge groups, so W
is a finite free pro-p product of conjugates of closed vertex subgroups
and a finite-rank free pro-p group. Those factors are coherent, and so
is W. For a finitely generated L≤G, Schreier's formula gives finite
generation of L∩W; it is finitely presented by coherence of W, and the
finite extension L/(L∩W) is finitely presented. Lemma 1 finishes.

## 4. Completed homology: verified reduction and exact obstruction

The entire **procyclic-edge** case is also already affirmative by
Chatzidakis–Zalesskii, Theorem 6.8 in arXiv:2007.06867v2 (12 February
2023): a finitely generated group acting on a pro-p tree with procyclic
edge stabilizers has a finite graph decomposition with finitely
generated vertex groups conjugate into original vertex stabilizers.
Applying coherence to these vertex groups and the finite graph
presentation proves finite presentability. One must respect the paper's
faithful-action convention; a nontrivial action kernel is procyclic,
and removing it, applying the theorem, and lifting by Lemma 1 gives
the same conclusion. The independent tree-approach note records the
version and theorem-number audit in greater detail.

Let k=F_p and Λ_L=k[[L]]. For a closed subgroup L≤G of a proper amalgam,
the standard pro-p tree gives an exact sequence of compact Λ_L-modules

\[
0\longrightarrow P_1=k[[G/H]]\longrightarrow
P_0=k[[G/G_1]]\oplus k[[G/G_2]]\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0.
\tag{T}
\]

Apply the continuous Tor functors k completed-tensor over Λ_L. The relevant
part of the long exact sequence is

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_2^{\Lambda_L}(k,P_0)\longrightarrow
H_2(L,k)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\Lambda_L}(k,P_1)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\Lambda_L}(k,P_0)\longrightarrow H_1(L,k).
\tag{MV}
\]

In the pro-p category, finite generation means finite-dimensional H_1,
and a finitely generated group is finitely presented exactly when its
continuous H_2(-,k) is finite-dimensional.

### Proposition 8: finite edge-orbit criterion

Suppose L≤G is finitely generated and L\G/H is finite. If every edge
stabilizer is finitely generated and all vertex groups G_i are coherent,
then L is finitely presented.

**Proof.** There are finitely many edge orbits and therefore finitely many
vertex orbits. Each permutation module in (T) is a finite direct sum of
induced modules k[[L/S]], with S the corresponding closed stabilizer.
Continuous Shapiro gives

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_j^{\Lambda_L}(k,k[[L/S]])\cong H_j(S,k).
\]

The edge stabilizers are finitely generated, so Tor_1(k,P_1) is finite.
Since H_1(L,k) is finite, exactness of (MV) makes Tor_1(k,P_0) finite.
All finitely many vertex stabilizers are therefore finitely generated.
They are subgroups of conjugates of coherent G_i, so are finitely
presented. Thus Tor_2(k,P_0) is finite. Exactness again makes H_2(L,k)
finite; hence L is finitely presented. ∎

For polyprocyclic H, every closed edge stabilizer is finitely generated,
so the proposition applies whenever this orbit hypothesis is verified.
The orbit hypothesis is not automatic: a cyclic subgroup of a
nonabelian free pro-p product can already have infinitely many edge
orbits.

### Exact relation-rank calculation for the rank-two diagnostic family

The independently constructed path family P_n in `approach_tree.md`
also obstructs a simple uniform homological estimate. Its vertex groups
are A_i=Z_p², 0≤i≤n, and its edge groups are E_i=Z_p², 0≤i<n, with maps

\[
(u_i,v_i)\mapsto(a_i^p,b_i),\qquad
(u_i,v_i)\mapsto(a_{i+1},b_{i+1}^p).
\]

The compatible map a_i↦(p^i,0), b_i↦(0,p^{n-i}) into Z_p² is injective
on each vertex, so the graph is proper. Eliminating generators gives

\[
P_n=\langle x,y\mid
[x^{p^i},y^{p^{n-i}}]=1\ (0\leq i\leq n)\rangle_{\mathrm{pro}-p},
\]

and its abelian quotient Z_p² shows d(P_n)=2.

For Z_p², H_1(-,k)=k² and H_2(-,k)=Λ²k²=k; these follow directly from
the two-variable Koszul resolution of k over k[[s,t]]. The induced
map on H_2 is the determinant of the induced H_1 map. Each edge
embedding above has determinant p, hence induces the zero H_2 map.
In the finite-graph Mayer–Vietoris sequence, therefore,

\[
\bigoplus_{i<n}H_2(E_i,k)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{i\leq n}H_2(A_i,k)
\]

is zero. Meanwhile the H_1 boundary map is injective: the u_i basis
vector maps, up to sign, to the distinct vertex coordinate a_{i+1},
and v_i maps to the distinct vertex coordinate b_i. Exactness gives

\[
H_2(P_n,k)\cong k^{n+1}.
\]

Thus P_n has minimal pro-p relation rank exactly n+1, despite d(P_n)=2,
all edge groups having generator rank two, and each individual vertex
having relation rank one. This disproves a bound on relation rank
depending only on those three quantities for arbitrary finite proper
graphs of groups. It is **not** a counterexample to coherence: every
P_n has a central polyprocyclic kernel with virtually free quotient,
as explained in the tree approach. It also does not disprove a bound
allowed to depend on additional data about a fixed ambient amalgam.

There is also an exact failure of the tempting inverse-limit
counterconstruction. Let F=F_pro-p(x,y), and let N_n be the normal
closure of the displayed relators for P_n. For n≥m and 0≤i≤n, choose j
with

\[
\max(0,m-(n-i))\leq j\leq\min(m,i).
\]

The interval is nonempty because n≥m. In P_m the elements x^{p^j}
and y^{p^{m-j}} commute; hence their powers x^{p^i} and y^{p^{n-i}}
commute. Thus N_n≤N_m and there are natural epimorphisms P_n→P_m.

On the other hand, Magnus expansion over k shows each defining
commutator lies in Zassenhaus degree at least p^i+p^{n-i}. Consequently

\[
N_n\leq D_{p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+p^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}(F).
\]

The Zassenhaus filtration is separated, so ⋂N_n=1. Compactness then
makes the natural map

\[
F\longrightarrow\varprojlim_n F/N_n=\varprojlim_n P_n
\]

an isomorphism: injectivity is the intersection statement and surjectivity
follows since every finite compatible tuple is attained in its largest
index quotient. Thus this inverse limit is the free pro-p group of rank
two, whose H_2 vanishes. The growing relation ranks n+1 disappear in the
limit; they do not establish an incoherent subgroup. This observation
was independently supplied and checked by the root researcher.

### Why the unrestricted argument stops

For a general finitely generated L, the double-coset orbit spaces can be
infinite profinite spaces. Replacing the permutation modules in (T) by
an ordinary direct sum over individual double cosets is unjustified.
One needs the appropriate profinite family of stabilizers and its
completed homology. Even if every individual relevant fiber is finite,
the completed family need not be a finite-dimensional vector space.
For example k[[X]] is infinite-dimensional for an infinite profinite
space X although every free rank-one fiber is one-dimensional. This
example is a module obstruction, not a counterexample to the group
statement: boundary maps can cause cancellation.

Moreover, vertex coherence only controls a vertex stabilizer **after**
its finite generation is known. Sequence (MV) supplies that conclusion
in the finite-orbit situation; no such control has been proved for all
infinite orbit families.

Noetherianness of k[[H]] for analytic H cannot by itself turn an
arbitrarily profinite-indexed family into a finitely generated module.
An alternative ring-amalgam route has two unproved steps here:

1. Group coherence does not, by its definition, assert coherence of the
   entire completed group algebra k[[G_i]]. No equivalence sufficient
   for the argument has been established in this note.
2. An algebraic theorem about coherent amalgams of rings would need a
   separate proof in the completed/pseudocompact category. Completion
   is a substantive operation, not an automatic preservation theorem.

**Route status: blocked as a proof of the unrestricted claim.** Calling
the missing infinite-family control a “finite core” or a compactness
lemma would transfer the central difficulty to an unsupported claim.
Reopen this route only with a new argument controlling those modules
or a materially stronger verified structural hypothesis.

## 5. Boundary and falsification checks

- H=1 is covered by Proposition 4 and agrees with the known coherence
  theorem for free pro-p products.
- If H=G_1 and the other edge embedding is an isomorphism onto its image,
  the proper pushout is G_2; its coherence agrees with the target.
- Finite polyprocyclic factors and torsion cause no problem in Lemma 1:
  a finite cyclic p-group has a one-relator pro-p presentation.
- Quotienting a coherent group in Lemma 2 requires the kernel to be
  finitely generated. No unrestricted quotient closure is asserted.
- Proposition 5 requires C normal in **both** vertices. Merely normal in
  H is insufficient to form the indicated quotient amalgam.
- Lemma 6 uses Baire category and a countable open-subgroup family,
  which is justified by finite generation; it does not assume an
  unsupported uniform bound on all conjugate intersection indices.
- All image-subgroup closedness needed in Lemmas 1–3 follows from
  compactness, rather than assuming an arbitrary image is closed.
- Proposition 8 does not assume vertex stabilizers finitely generated;
  that is the first conclusion of its exact-sequence argument.

**Fresh followup audit, 2026-10-17 21:28 UTC.** The relation-rank and
inverse-limit claims passed the independent review in
reviews/partial_adversary01.md, section 6. An elementary alternative to
the Zassenhaus argument is worth recording. For any finite p quotient of
the free pro-p group on x,y, choose p^e divisible by its exponent. If
n>=2e, then for every i between 0 and n, either i>=e or n-i>=e. Thus
each relator [x^(p^i),y^(p^(n-i))] is trivial in that finite quotient.
The defining closed normal subgroup N_n is eventually contained in every
open normal subgroup of the free group. Its intersection is therefore
trivial. Since the N_n form a descending chain, compactness gives
F_pro-p(x,y) isomorphic to the inverse limit of F_pro-p(x,y)/N_n.
This proof uses only finite p quotients. It does not identify an inverse
limit solely from unbounded finite-stage relation ranks.
