## Exact objects, hypotheses, and success criteria

F denotes the standard binary Thompson group: all increasing homeomorphisms f:[0,1] -> [0,1] for which there is a **finite** subdivision of [0,1] with all interior subdivision points in Z[1/2], such that f is affine on every resulting interval and each affine slope is 2^k for some integer k. Composition is the group operation. In particular all elements fix 0 and 1. Breakpoints are dyadic; arbitrary real breakpoints or infinitely many affine pieces define a different object. Equivalent finite-tree and presentation descriptions are given in [Cleary–Taback, section 2](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2003/9-11p.pdf) and [Belk–Brown, sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305412v1).

Fix the finite generating set S={x_0,x_1}, with inverses allowed, and d_F(g,h)=|g^{-1}h|_S. On F x Z use

    d_a((g,m),(h,n)) = d_F(g,h) + |m-n|,

and on F x F use

    d_b((g_1,g_2),(h_1,h_2)) = d_F(g_1,h_1) + d_F(g_2,h_2).

These are the word metrics of the usual union of factor generating sets. Changing any finite generating set changes the metrics by uniform multiplicative factors and does not change the questions.

For either Y=F x Z or Y=F x F, a positive answer requires a function q:F -> Y and constants A>=1, B>=0, R>=0 such that

    for every g,h in F,
      A^{-1} d_F(g,h)-B <= d_Y(q(g),q(h)) <= A d_F(g,h)+B,
    and for every y in Y there exists g in F with d_Y(y,q(g)) <= R.

The constants must be independent of all inputs. The function need not preserve multiplication, be continuous, or be bijective. A quasi-isometric embedding supplies only the first line. A negative answer must exclude **all** such functions and constants, not merely homomorphisms or natural geometric candidates. These conventions match [Cleary–Taback, section 3, p.144](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2003/9-11p.pdf).

## Strong metric descriptions and legitimate equivalent formulations

1. **Relative tree-pair complexity.** If N(w) is the number of carets in either tree of a reduced binary tree pair for a nonidentity w, [Cleary–Taback Theorem 2.1, p.144](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2003/9-11p.pdf) gives N(w)-2 <= |w|_S <= 4N(w)-4. Identity conventions can be handled separately. Thus the applicable pairwise estimate is obtained from the reduced pair of **g^{-1}h**. Comparing the sizes of the separate diagrams of g and h, or preserving individual size without controlling cancellation, is insufficient. [Burillo–Cleary–Stein, *Metrics and embeddings of generalizations of Thompson's group F*, Trans. AMS 353 (2001), 1677–1689, arXiv:math/9809185v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9809185v3), supplies an earlier systematic caret/normal-form comparison and mutual embeddings of the generalized F(p).

2. **Exact forest metric.** [Belk–Brown, *Forest diagrams for elements of Thompson's group F*, IJAC 15 (2005), 815–850, arXiv:math/0305412v1, Theorem 4.2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305412v1) computes |w|_S as the sum of the specified space weights and the total number of forest carets. This gives an exact combinatorial model for d_F by applying the formula to g^{-1}h. The forest pointers and space weights matter; plain total caret count of two independent forests is not that exact metric.

3. **Diagram metric.** F is the diagram group D(<x | x=x^2>,x). Let d_D(g,h) count the cells of the reduced spherical diagram for g^{-1}h. [Dranishnikov–Sapir, *On the dimension growth of groups*, arXiv:1008.3868v4, Example 5.2 and Lemma 5.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3868v4), records that this metric and the standard word metric are (6,2)-quasi-isometric, attributing the comparison to Burillo and Arzhantseva–Guba–Sapir. Since both metrics are integer-valued and positive on distinct elements, the additive constants can be absorbed into multiplicative constants, giving a bilipschitz equivalent metric on this **same underlying F**. This does not give a bilipschitz equivalence with a product.

For a tree/forest representation equipped with the transported word metric, or for the diagram metric model M of F, the original clauses are respectively equivalent to M ~QI M x Z and M ~QI M x M, with a sum product metric. This follows by composing the known model comparison and its inverse, and taking products; the unproved product absorption remains exactly the original difficulty. Caret complexity by itself was not asserted to satisfy the metric axioms.

Another equivalent reformulation uses specific undistorted subgroups. Let L and R be the affine clone subgroups supported in [0,1/2] and [1/2,1]. Then H_b=L x R=Stab_F(1/2) is abstractly F x F and undistorted. Choose a nontrivial u in R and set H_a=L x <u>. By [Guba–Sapir Theorem 33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0005114v1), <u> is undistorted in R; hence H_a is undistorted in L x R and therefore in F. It is abstractly F x Z. Thus clauses (a) and (b) ask whether F is quasi-isometric to H_a and H_b, respectively, equipped with the **restricted ambient metric**, via some arbitrary map. They do not ask whether the literal subgroup inclusion is a quasi-isometry. Changing to another undistorted copy does not settle the question.

## Restricted algebraic obstruction, with compact proof

This deduction is routine background, not a claimed new contribution. Write G_{m,n}=F^m x Z^n for m>=1, n>=0, and N=(F')^m. If K<=G_{m,n} has finite index, its action on finitely many cosets kills every F' factor: F' is infinite simple, so any homomorphism from it to a finite permutation group is trivial. Hence N<=K. Since N is perfect, N<=K'; since G_{m,n}/N is abelian, K'<=N. Therefore K'=N, and K/K' is a finite-index lattice in Z^{2m+n}. The prerequisite F/F'=Z^2 and infinite simplicity of F' are recorded in [Burillo–Cleary–Röver, section 2 and Proposition 2.1](https://web.mat.upc.edu/pep.burillo/Papers/comF.pdf).

Consequently finite-index subgroups of F, F x Z, and F x F have abelianization ranks 2, 3, and 4. The three groups are pairwise noncommensurable. Moreover, a homomorphism which is a quasi-isometry has finite kernel and finite-index image: the lower metric bound makes the kernel a finite bounded set, and coarse surjectivity expresses the target as finitely many cosets of the image. All these groups are torsion-free, because an increasing interval homeomorphism of finite order is the identity. Thus such a kernel is trivial, and the rank calculation excludes a homomorphic quasi-isometry in **either direction** for either clause. This does not exclude arbitrary quasi-isometries; abelianization rank and commensurability are not asserted to be quasi-isometry invariants.

## Remaining gap and route gates

- **(a):** produce one uniform quasi-isometry between F and F x Z, or prove that none exists. Existing undistorted F x Z subgroups and a surjective endpoint character do not supply a coarse product decomposition. A homomorphic candidate is excluded by the preceding calculation.
- **(b):** produce one uniform quasi-isometry between F and F x F, or a distinguishing quasi-isometry invariant/obstruction. The undistorted stabilizer Stab_F(1/2) already realizes the target internally; its infinite index is no obstruction to the existence of some other quasi-isometry onto it.
- Mark “upgrade the known product embedding to a quasi-isometry” blocked until a new uniform coarse-surjectivity mechanism is supplied. Mark “all quasi-isometries are algebraic/tree induced” blocked until an independent applicable rigidity theorem is proved. A bilipschitz model of the same F only reformulates these gaps.
- No implication between the two unresolved clauses is asserted. No mathematical conclusion here depends on accepting any public claim about the separate amenability question.
