## Precise claim and external input

**Proposition.** Every countably infinite locally finite p-group embeds in a group of the form

\[
G=\langle 1+c_1,\ldots,1+c_r\rangle\le 1+C,
\]

where r is finite and at least two and C is an associative F_p-algebra generated by c_1,...,c_r that is nil and not nilpotent. Thus G is a Golod group in the literal Notebook 9.76 sense. Residual finiteness is not claimed.

The sole external structural theorem is Alahmadi–Alsulami–Jain–Zelmanov, *Matrix wreath products of algebras and embedding theorems*, arXiv:1703.08734v2, Theorem 4.3 (printed p.2, proof pp.17–18): a countable-dimensional stable nil algebra over F_p embeds into a finitely generated stable nil F_p-algebra. Here stable nil means all finite matrix algebras are nil. Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08734 . Actual PDF and extracted text are retained in sources/.

## 1. A locally finite p overgroup whose derived subgroup contains H

Let H be countable locally finite p. Define K_0=H. Given K_j, let

\[
K_{j+1}=K_j^{m_j}\rtimes\langle t_j\rangle,
\qquad m_j=p^{j+1},\quad |t_j|=m_j,
\]

where t_j cyclically permutes the m_j factors. Identify K_j with its diagonal image in the base K_j^{m_j}; this map is injective and a group homomorphism. Let K be the resulting direct limit. Each K_j is countable locally finite p: a finite set projects into a finite p-subgroup in each of the finitely many coordinates, and adjoining the cyclic top group still gives a finite p-subgroup. Every finite subset of K lies in one K_j, so K is countable locally finite p.

We show K=K'. Fix h in a stage, of order p^a. At some later transition m_j is divisible by p^a. Choose the convention t_j f t_j^{-1}(i)=f(i-1), where coordinates i are in Z/m_j. Let f(i)=h^i. Since h^{m_j}=1,

\[
f\,t_j f^{-1}t_j^{-1}(i)=h^i h^{-(i-1)}=h
\]

also at the wraparound index. Therefore the diagonal image of h is the commutator [f,t_j], using [x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}. Hence every element of K belongs to K'. In particular the initial H embeds into K'.

**Simplification for the tree family's target.** That H is generated by elements of order p. It is enough to take K=H^p⋊C_p once: the diagonal image of each order-p generator is the commutator just displayed, and hence the diagonal image of the entire H lies in K'. This avoids the direct-limit overgroup for the intended application.

## 2. A countable stable nil algebra containing K in its adjoint group

Let A=Δ(F_p[K]) be the augmentation ideal. Each finite collection of elements of A is supported in a finite p-subgroup P≤K, and is contained in Δ(F_p[P]). This finite-group augmentation ideal is nilpotent. For completeness, induct on |P|: choose a central element z of order p. The central ideal J=(z−1)F_p[P] has J^p=0. The quotient algebra by J is F_p[P/⟨z⟩]. If the augmentation ideal of the quotient has nilpotence exponent q, then Δ(F_p[P])^q⊂J and Δ(F_p[P])^{pq}=0. The trivial group starts the induction.

Consequently A is locally nilpotent and countable dimensional. For any n and any matrix in M_n(A), its finitely many entries belong to one Δ(F_p[P]); thus that matrix is nilpotent. Therefore A is stable nil. The map k↦1+(k−1)=k injects K into 1+A.

Apply the external theorem to embed A into a finitely generated stable nil F_p-algebra B. The induced unital map F_p+A→F_p+B is injective, and embeds K into U=1+B. Since H embeds into K', its image lies in U'.

## 3. Finitely generated relative elementary group

Write R=F_p+B and choose nonunital algebra generators s_1,...,s_d of B. For distinct i,j in {1,2,3}, set

\[
e_{ij}(b)=I_3+bE_{ij}.
\]

Let E=E_3(R) be generated by all elementary matrices over R, let Q=E_3(F_p), and let X={e_{ij}(s_l):i≠j,1≤l≤d}. The identities

\[
e_{ij}(a)e_{ij}(b)=e_{ij}(a+b),\qquad
[e_{ik}(a),e_{kj}(b)]=e_{ij}(ab)\quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct})
\]

show that ⟨X⟩ contains all e_{ij}(b), b∈B: positive-degree monomials are obtained by induction, sums by the first identity, and F_p scalar multiples by integral powers. Thus E=⟨Q,X⟩.

Put

\[
L=\langle qxq^{-1}:q\in Q,x\in X\rangle.
\]

The group Q is finite, so this is a finite generating set. Conjugation by Q permutes this generating set. Since X⊂L and E=⟨Q,L⟩, L is normal in E. It contains every e_{ij}(b), b∈B, by the identities above. Conversely, all its displayed generators belong to the normal closure of these elementary matrices. Hence L equals the relative elementary group E_3(R,B), defined as that normal closure. This proof uses no equality of an unspecified congruence kernel with a relative elementary group.

## 4. Explicit embedding of U' into L

For u∈U define D(u)=diag(u,u^{-1},1). Direct multiplication gives

\[
D(u)=e_{12}(u)e_{21}(-u^{-1})e_{12}(u)
e_{12}(-1)e_{21}(1)e_{12}(-1).
\]

Since u−1 and u^{-1}−1 lie in B, modulo the normal subgroup L the right side equals its value at u=1, namely I_3. Thus D(u)∈L.

For u,v∈U,

\[
D(u)D(v)D(uv)^{-1}
=\operatorname{diag}(1,u^{-1}v^{-1}uv,1)\in L.
\]

As u^{-1}v^{-1}uv runs through commutators and their products generate U', the homomorphism

\[
U'\longrightarrow L,\qquad h\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(1,h,1)
\]

is well-defined and injective. Combining with Sections 1–2 embeds H into L.

## 5. Exact canonical Golod realization

List the finitely many generators qxq^{-1} of L as I_3+c_1,...,I_3+c_r and let C be the nonunital F_p-subalgebra of M_3(B) generated by their differences c_i. Each c_i is the scalar conjugate s_l(qE_{ij}q^{-1}), so its entries lie in B and c_i²=0. Since B is stable nil, M_3(B) is nil; its subalgebra C is nil. Products and inverses of the units I_3+c_i lie in I_3+C, the inverse being a finite geometric polynomial because c_i is nilpotent. Therefore L is exactly the subgroup generated by these specified units in 1+C. Every nonidentity listed generator has order p.

If C were nilpotent, finite generation would make it finite dimensional: words of length below a nilpotence exponent span C. Because the field F_p is finite, 1+C would then be finite. But L contains the infinite group H. Thus C is not nilpotent. Also L cannot be cyclic: every 1+c has finite p-power order, so a cyclic subgroup is finite. A finite generating list for L therefore has at least two nontrivial entries after removing repetitions and identity entries. Taking a free nonunital F_p-algebra on this finite list and quotienting by the kernel gives exactly the presentation in Notebook 9.76.

## Adversarial checks and limitations

- Stable nil, rather than mere nil, is needed in Section 5; this is precisely supplied by the first clause of AAJZ Theorem 4.3.
- Algebra embedding by itself does not place K in the canonical finitely generated unit subgroup. Sections 3–5 supply the missing finite-generator realization explicitly.
- The map u↦D(u) is not claimed to be a homomorphism. The actual embedded map is h↦diag(1,h,1), restricted to the derived subgroup.
- Finite generation of the relative elementary group uses a finite base field. No claim for arbitrary infinite ground fields is required.
- Three matrix indices are used in the multiplication identity; two indices would not justify the same inductive proof.
- The resulting Golod group is not claimed to be residually finite or itself an AT-group.
- The proposition is phrased for infinite H to force C nonnilpotent. For a finite locally finite p-group H, first embed it in the infinite locally finite p-group H×(⊕_{n≥1}C_p) and then apply the stated proposition.
