## Adversarial checks and independent reconstruction

1. **One seed, all fields.** The construction, rather than the abstract statement of AAJZ Theorem 4.3, is essential. The prime-field seed is chosen once using P=F_p[t_1,t_2,...] tensor M_infinity(F_p). This P is countable dimensional and locally polynomial growth: finitely many elements use finitely many variables and a finite matrix corner. Nilness of B0 tensor P kills each finite generic matrix sum. Evaluating variables at arbitrary extension-field scalars proves nilness of every M_n(B0 tensor E), with this same B0. No uniform exponent across all matrices or all fields is needed. Nonunitality of M_infinity is harmless because the matrix under evaluation lies in one finite corner.

2. **Auxiliary wreath extension.** In A_tilde=N+M_2(A), the matrix summand is an ideal and the quotient is N, with N^3=0. Scalar extension commutes with this construction because N is finite dimensional and its actions are given by the fixed multiplication table. For every finite n the ideal becomes M_{2n}(A_E), which is nil, and the quotient M_n(N_E) is nilpotent. For any element x, nilness of its quotient gives x^r in the ideal, and nilness of that particular ideal element gives (x^r)^s=0. No bounded nil index is asserted. Absolute stable nilness of the augmentation ideal is sufficient and follows from supports in finite p-subgroups over any characteristic-p extension field.

3. **Infinite basis and tensor injection.** The map S_F tensor E to S_E is defined on algebraic tensors. Finitely many summands have finitely many row supports, whose union stays finite. Express a tensor using F-linearly independent extension scalars alpha_l. Zero image forces every entry sum sum_l a_l tensor alpha_l to vanish. Coordinate functionals E to F then give every original entry zero. This proves injection without equating an infinite product/Hom tensor extension with all maps after extension. The proof does not depend on finite dimensionality of A_tilde or V and is valid for uncountable E.

4. **Products and actions.** For finite-row matrices M,N, the entry of MN sums over the finite row support of N. Thus the entrywise map preserves multiplication. Left multiplication by b on V_E takes the finitely many output basis vectors into a finite-dimensional subspace; precomposition does not enlarge output support. These facts establish compatibility with both B0 actions. Every required product is a finite sum, including when a row contains infinitely many nonzero entries. The external direct-sum decomposition of the semidirect algebra preserves the B0 component under extension; no assumption about its represented intersection with S is needed.

5. **Finite-row nilness and matrix stability.** The corner map for a fixed finite row set T is multiplicative, with square-zero kernel. More explicitly, a matrix has block form [[C,D],[0,0]], so if C^k=0, then the original matrix has power k+1 zero. Infinitely many coefficients in D do not demand a common nil exponent. Finite n-by-n block matrices in S_E embed into finite-row matrices indexed by {1,...,n} times I. Hence M_n(S_E) is nil because A_tilde_E is stable nil. The quotient M_n(R_E)/M_n(S_E) is exactly M_n(B0_E), using the external semidirect sum. The same elementwise nil-extension argument proves M_n(R_E) nil.

6. **Containing algebra.** Both added generators c_gamma and e_11(u) lie in S_F. The algebra generated by them and the fixed finite generating set of B0 is therefore a subalgebra of the ambient external semidirect sum. Flatness of E/F preserves its injection. The preceding arguments imply M_n(B1 tensor E) nil for every E,n. This does not require rebuilding B1, reselecting gamma, or extending gamma to a surjection for each E. The original AAJZ containment is an F-algebra inclusion and remains the one used in the group construction. Similarly flatness embeds C tensor E into M_3(B1 tensor E), proving absolute nilness of this C.

7. **Hammoudi and exact generators.** The primary proof of Hammoudi Theorem 3, printed pp.96-97, constructs homogeneous J inside I in the same nonunital free algebra on the original finite d>=2 symbols. Its relations have sufficiently sparse degree counts for Lemma 1. Therefore x_i+J maps to the specified c_i, not to an unspecified generating list. The induced map on the generated unit subgroups is onto L. Corollary 3 explicitly states the matching d-generator group conclusion. The hypothesis is absolute nilness, which has been established for this C; merely stable nilness is not substituted.

   A potential hidden problem in that construction is mixing words of different lengths in one generic polynomial. It does not break homogeneity: the coefficient with multiplicities mu_w has degree sum_w mu_w*length(w), independent of product order. At stage k the finite list of words has length at most k and cardinality q. Absolute nilness applied over the rational function field in q scalars makes one generic power zero modulo I; coefficient comparison puts each coefficient relation in I. Every such relation has degree at least n, and there are at most binomial(n+q-1,q-1) of them. For fixed q this grows polynomially in n, whereas the allowed bound epsilon^2*(d-2*epsilon)^(n-2) grows exponentially, including d=2. Choosing n beyond previous relation degrees preserves all old bounds. Thus the source's homogeneous, sparse-relator, same-generator mechanism passes independently of the theorem's short statement.

8. **Homogeneous cover and residual finiteness.** A homogeneous quotient D has finite-dimensional graded components and algebraic direct-sum grading. Every nonzero element survives some quotient by degrees >=m; that quotient is a finite-dimensional nilpotent algebra over F_p. Its adjoint group is finite of order a power of p. Thus these maps separate elements of Gamma. Nilness makes all 1+d invertible and of p-power order. The source is infinite because it maps onto L containing infinite H. Alternatively, its nonnilpotent algebra property follows from the Golod degree bound. No residual-finiteness assertion about an arbitrary quotient is used.

9. **Subgroup inverse image.** If rho:Gamma ->> L and iota:H -> L is injective, then P=rho^{-1}(iota(H)) has the surjection iota^{-1} rho|_P onto H. Given h, any rho-preimage of iota(h) lies in P. No section, embedding H into Gamma, inverse-limit lifting, or finite-generation claim for P is needed.

## Optional convention clarification

Section 3's phrase allowing either side of the auxiliary ideal can be made explicit, since the subsequent containment specifically uses A_tilde*u. With the standard convention that matrix row is output and column is input, left multiplication by u on N=F u+F u^2 has matrix L_u=[[0,0],[1,0]]. For a coefficient matrix in M_2(A), the right action is precomposition by L_u. Consequently

    E_12(a) * u = E_11(a),

so the corner a -> E_11(a) embeds A in A_tilde*u. Also u*E_12(a)=E_22(a), giving the other side. This removes the notation ambiguity directly and verifies the side required by the c_gamma/e_11(u) containment. It is not an obstruction: both embeddings already exist in the very same auxiliary wreath product.
