## Scope, assumptions, and falsification targets

The exact claim is: if H is countably infinite and locally finite p, then there exist a finite list c_1,...,c_r with r>=2 and a nonunital associative F_p-algebra C generated by that list, such that C is nil and nonnilpotent and H embeds in <1+c_1,...,1+c_r> <= 1+C.

The principal failure modes checked were: failure of diagonal embeddings in a nonabelian wreath product; failure at the wraparound coordinate; lack of stable nilness of the group augmentation ideal; a theorem giving nil rather than stable nil; finite generation of an algebra being confused with finite generation of its adjoint group; incomplete normality of a relative elementary subgroup; a reversed noncommutative coefficient product; misuse of the nonhomomorphic map D; and a hidden stronger meaning of “Golod group” in Notebook 9.76.

This audit does not prove that the independently supplied H is an original 1986 AT-group, establish novelty, or replace the source theorem by a new self-contained proof. There was no Git operation, publication, or outreach.

## 1. The locally finite perfect overgroup

Let m=p^{j+1} and let K_j^m semidirect C_m use the cyclic shift t f t^{-1}(i)=f(i-1). The map h -> (h,...,h) is an injective homomorphism even when K_j is nonabelian, since the base multiplication is coordinatewise.

A finite subset of K_j^m semidirect C_m has only finitely many base entries. Those entries generate a finite p-subgroup P of K_j. The finite group P^m semidirect C_m contains the subset. Thus every stage, and then the injective direct limit, is locally finite p. Countability is preserved at each stage and by the countable union.

If h has order dividing m, put f(i)=h^i. For 1<=i<m,

    [f,t](i)=h^i h^{-(i-1)}=h.

At i=0, the same coordinate is h^{-(m-1)}=h^{1-m}=h. This is exactly where h^m=1 is needed. The increasing powers m_j eventually divide no element orders incorrectly: for order p^a, any j+1>=a works. Therefore every element of K, represented at an earlier stage, becomes a commutator at a later transition. K=K', so the embedded initial H lies in K'. No finite p-group is asserted to be perfect; perfection is only claimed of the direct limit.

For the proposed special target generated by order-p elements, the one-stage variant also works: the diagonals of all listed generators lie in the derived subgroup, so their entire generated subgroup lies there.

## 2. Stable nilness of the augmentation ideal

Let A be the augmentation ideal of F_p[K]. A finite collection of its elements has finite total support in K. Local finiteness places that support in a finite p-group P. Hence those elements lie in the augmentation ideal Delta(P).

For completeness, the stated central-element induction is valid. A nontrivial finite p-group has a central element z of order p. In F_p[P], set J=(z-1)F_p[P]. Centrality and characteristic p give

    J^p=(z-1)^p F_p[P]= (z^p-1)F_p[P]=0.

The quotient by J is the group algebra of P/<z>, and the image of Delta(P) is its augmentation ideal. If that image has nilpotence exponent q, then Delta(P)^q is contained in J and Delta(P)^{pq}=0. Starting with the trivial group proves the required nilpotence.

Every matrix T in M_n(A) has finitely many entries, whose supports are contained in one such P. If Delta(P)^N=0, the entire matrix algebra M_n(Delta(P)) has Nth power zero: each entry of a product of N matrices is a sum of products of N entries. Therefore T^N=0. This proves stable nilness directly and avoids any appeal to the unresolved implication nil => stable nil.

Countability of K gives countable dimension of A. The embedding A -> B from AAJZ extends to an injective unital map F_p+A -> F_p+B, because unitizations are direct vector-space sums. The original group algebra is F_p+A by the augmentation decomposition. The map k -> 1+(k-1) is injective into 1+A. Its image in 1+B preserves commutators; consequently the image of H lies in U' for U=1+B.

## 3. Finite generation and full normality of L

The independent reviewer in `noncommutative_check/REPORT.md` supplied a separate full audit of Sections 3-5. I also checked the equations and group inclusions directly.

For distinct indices, put A_0=aE_ik and T_0=bE_kj. Then A_0^2=T_0^2=T_0 A_0=0 and A_0 T_0=abE_ij. All higher terms in the commutator product vanish, giving

    [e_ik(a),e_kj(b)]=e_ij(ab).

The coefficient order is ab. Induct simultaneously over all six ordered pairs (i,j): if a word is w's_l, select the third index k and use the previous-degree e_ik(w') and generator e_kj(s_l). This produces every e_ij(word). Multiplication within the same root subgroup implements sums and integer powers implement F_p-scalars. Thus X generates every B-elementary matrix, and E=<Q,X>.

The finite set {qxq^{-1}:q in Q,x in X} is closed under Q-conjugation. Therefore Q normalizes its generated subgroup L. Since X is contained in L and E=<Q,L>, and L normalizes itself, **all E normalizes L**. This resolves the possible distinction between Q-normality and E-normality.

Let N be the E-normal closure of all B-elementary matrices. They are all in L and L is E-normal, so N<=L. Conversely X is among those matrices, so all qxq^{-1} lie in N and L<=N. Therefore L=N. There is no unsupported equality between N and a full congruence kernel.

Finiteness of Q=E_3(F_p) is the precise finite-field input. The listed finite X would not give the same scalar-generation proof over an arbitrary field. The argument deliberately uses F_p.

## 4. The derived-unit embedding

The two blocks used in the proposed factorization multiply as

    e_12(u)e_21(-u^{-1})e_12(u) = [[0,u],[-u^{-1},0]],
    e_12(-1)e_21(1)e_12(-1) = [[0,-1],[1,0]],

and their product is diag(u,u^{-1}). Only the two-sided inverse identities are used. Nilness implies u^{-1}-1 is in B for u in U. Every factor can thus be replaced in E/L by its value at u=1; their product then is I. Hence D(u) is in L.

Retaining the order of all noncommuting factors,

    D(u)D(v)D(uv)^{-1}=diag(1,u^{-1}v^{-1}uv,1).

With [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}, the middle entry is [u^{-1},v^{-1}]. Inversion bijects U, so every commutator occurs. The set of h for which diag(1,h,1) lies in L is a subgroup containing every commutator, hence it contains U'. The map h -> diag(1,h,1) is an actual homomorphism and is injective by its middle entry. The map u -> D(u) itself is generally nonhomomorphic, and the proof never needs it to be a homomorphism.

## 5. Literal canonical Golod realization

For scalar q and x=e_ij(s_l), the difference

    c=qxq^{-1}-I=q(s_l E_ij)q^{-1}

has entries in B. It is even square-zero, since (s_l E_ij)^2=0. This does not make the algebra generated by several such c nilpotent.

All c lie in M_3(B), which is nil by stable nilness of B. Their finitely generated nonunital subalgebra C is therefore nil. Closure of 1+C under products follows from (1+c)(1+d)=1+c+d+cd; closure under inverse follows from the finite geometric polynomial for a nilpotent c. Thus L is precisely the subgroup generated by the specified 1+c_i in 1+C.

If C^N=0, the words of lengths 1,...,N-1 in the finite generating list span C. So C is finite dimensional, then finite as a set over F_p. This would make 1+C and its subgroup L finite, contradicting the embedded infinite H. Hence C is nonnilpotent.

Every 1+c has finite p-power order: choose p^a at least a nilpotence exponent of c and use (1+c)^{p^a}=1+c^{p^a}=1. An infinite L therefore cannot be cyclic. Removing repetitions and identities leaves at least two list entries; neither the generated group nor the generated algebra changes. The quotient of the free nonunital algebra on this list by its kernel is C, exactly as in Notebook 9.76.
