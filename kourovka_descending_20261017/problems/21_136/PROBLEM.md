> Let G be a profinite group with fewer than 2^{aleph_0} conjugacy classes of elements of infinite order. Must G be a torsion group?

The following source annotation credits the affirmative finitely generated case to J. S. Wilson, Archiv der Mathematik 120 (2023), 557–563.

## Exact scope and success criterion

The target quantifies over every profinite (compact Hausdorff, totally disconnected) group, without assuming finite generation, countable generation, metrizability, or a countable base. Conjugacy is in the ambient G. Only conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements are counted; finite-order classes can be arbitrarily numerous. The cardinal bound is strict and is the actual continuum 2^{aleph_0}; it must not be replaced by countability or treated under an unstated continuum hypothesis.

“Torsion” means that every individual element has finite order. It does not demand a uniform exponent or that the group be finite. A full affirmative answer must eliminate every infinite-order element under these exact hypotheses. A negative answer must give an actual profinite group with an infinite-order element and strictly fewer than continuum ambient conjugacy classes of such elements. A restricted finitely generated/metrizable/p-adic analytic proof alone does not meet this target.

## Known result and a source warning

Wilson, *Profinite groups with few conjugacy classes of elements of infinite order*, Arch. Math. 120 (2023), 557–563, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-023-01851-4, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14753, Theorem A: under the target class-count assumption, a **topologically finitely generated** profinite group is finite. The introduction explicitly presents the unrestricted locally finite conclusion as a conjecture. Wilson's Theorem B concerns odd-prime Sylow subgroups and still requires that Sylow subgroup to be finitely generated.

Caution: the published Corollary 3.1 literally omits finite generation and would then assert finiteness for arbitrary pro-p groups. That literal statement fails for an infinite elementary abelian product. Its preceding sentence specifies finitely generated pro-p groups, and its proof invokes Theorem C with that hypothesis. We therefore record an evident omitted hypothesis rather than citing Corollary 3.1 as an unrestricted theorem. No author-confirmed correction has been obtained or solicited.
