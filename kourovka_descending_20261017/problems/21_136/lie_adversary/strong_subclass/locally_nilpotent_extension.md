# Extension of the local-isolation obstruction to abstract locally nilpotent kernels

Checkpoint: 2026-10-24T17:43:16Z. Completion estimate for this extension audit: 100%. Status: self-contained proof; the commutator formula and its class indexing were independently checked by an additional agent. No novelty claim is made.

This is a new note. Earlier subclass notes are left unchanged. No Git operations, publication, external communication, or other approach-note consultation was performed.

## Exact hypothesis and terminology

Write (LI) for the following condition on an infinite-order element x of a profinite group G:

    Some open neighborhood U of x has the property that every
    infinite-order y in U is G-conjugate to x.

An abstract group A is locally nilpotent if every finitely generated abstract subgroup of A is nilpotent of some finite class. The class may depend on the finite generating set. No topology is used in this definition.

This is strictly stronger than being pronilpotent as a profinite group. In particular, a pro-p group is pronilpotent but need not be abstractly locally nilpotent. The proof below uses abstract local nilpotence, not pronilpotence.

## Precise extension theorem

**Theorem.** Let A be a closed normal subgroup of a profinite group G. Suppose A is either torsion or abstractly locally nilpotent. If x in G is infinite-order and satisfies (LI), then xA has infinite order and satisfies (LI) in G/A.

Equivalently, if (LI) is impossible in G/A and A has either of the two stated properties, then (LI) is impossible in G.

The quotient group G/A need not be abelian, solvable, pronilpotent, or of bounded exponent. There is no global restriction on the number of infinite-order conjugacy classes.

## Quotient descent and the finite-image reduction

Let pi:G->B=G/A be the quotient map and let b=pi(x). If b has infinite order, (LI) descends immediately: pi(U) is an open neighborhood of b, and every infinite-order z in pi(U) has a lift y in U. The lift y is infinite-order, hence conjugate to x, so z is conjugate to b.

It remains to rule out finite order r=ord(b).

If A is torsion, this is immediate. The element x^r lies in A and therefore has finite order d; then x^(rd)=1, contrary to the infinite order of x.

For the rest of the proof, A is abstractly locally nilpotent.

## The quotient-centralizer argument

Suppose b has finite order r>=1. Every element of pi(U) is torsion: an infinite-order z there would lift to an infinite-order y in U, making z conjugate to the finite-order element b.

Choose an open subgroup V of B with bV contained in pi(U), and set

    C=C_B(b),
    K=C intersect V.

The subgroup K is open in C, although it need not be open in B. For each k in K, b and bk commute and both have finite order. Hence k=b^(-1)(bk) has finite order. Thus K is torsion. Every element of C has a positive power in K, since K has finite index in C; consequently C is torsion. No uniform bound on orders in C is needed.

Choose an integer n>1 such that

    x^n belongs to U,
    n=1 modulo r.

To see this explicitly, select an open normal subgroup W of G with xW contained in U. Let e be the order of xW in the finite group G/W, and take n=1+re. Then x^n belongs to xW and b^n=b.

Since x^n has infinite order, (LI) gives g in G such that, with right conjugation notation,

    x^g=g^(-1)xg=x^n.

The equality b^pi(g)=b^n=b puts pi(g) in C. The torsion property of C gives a positive integer m with

    h=g^m in A.

Put

    a=x^r in A,
    q=n^m>1.

Then a has infinite order, and iterating the conjugation relation gives

    a^h=a^q.

Both a and h lie in A. This is the exact point at which abstract local nilpotence is used.

## The two-generator nilpotence obstruction, with exact indexing

Let H be the abstract subgroup generated by a and h. Since A is abstractly locally nilpotent, H is nilpotent of some finite class c>=1. Use

    [u,v]=u^(-1)v^(-1)uv=u^(-1)u^v,
    [a,_0 h]=a,
    [a,_(j+1) h]=[[a,_j h],h].

For every integer t, the relation a^h=a^q gives

    [a^t,h]=(a^t)^(-1)(a^t)^h=a^(t(q-1)).

Induction therefore yields, for every integer j>=0,

    [a,_j h]=a^((q-1)^j).

The commutator [a,_j h] has weight j+1 and lies in the lower central subgroup gamma_(j+1)(H). Nilpotency class at most c means gamma_(c+1)(H)=1. Thus the correct vanishing index is j=c: c occurrences of h, of total commutator weight c+1. We obtain

    1=[a,_c h]=a^((q-1)^c).

Because q>1 and c>=1, the integer (q-1)^c is positive. This contradicts the infinite order of a. The finite-image case is impossible, proving the theorem.

Only the two-generator subgroup generated by a=x^r and h=g^m must be nilpotent. No global nilpotency class bound, finite generation of A, or continuity of an auxiliary representation is assumed.

## Finite normal-series corollary

**Corollary.** Suppose G has a finite chain

    1=N_0 <= N_1 <= ... <= N_t=G

in which every N_i is closed and normal in the ambient group G, and every factor N_i/N_(i-1) is either torsion or abstractly locally nilpotent. Then no infinite-order element of G satisfies (LI).

**Proof.** Induct on t, starting with the trivial group. For t>=1, N_1 is an allowed kernel. The quotient G/N_1 has the shorter chain consisting of the images of N_1,...,N_t, with the same remaining factor properties. Induction rules out (LI) in G/N_1, and the extension theorem rules it out in G. [End proof.]

The ambient-normality requirement is explicit because it makes quotient induction immediate. This note does not assert the same conclusion from an arbitrary chain that is merely subnormal, with each term normal only in its successor, unless a suitable ambient-normal refinement is separately supplied.

In particular this recovers all virtually solvable profinite groups: take an open normal solvable subgroup M, use its closed derived series, and append G/M, a finite torsion factor. Each abelian derived factor is abstractly locally nilpotent.

## Why pronilpotence cannot replace the hypothesis in this proof

Consider the compact semidirect product

    P=Z_3 semidirect Z_3,
    (u,s)(v,t)=(u+4^(-s)v,s+t).

The powers 4^s extend continuously from integers to s in Z_3. This is a pro-3 group: it is the inverse limit of the finite groups

    P_j=(Z/3^j Z) semidirect (Z/3^j Z)

with the same multiplication. The action is well-defined because 4^(3^j)=1 modulo 3^j. In particular P is pronilpotent.

For a=(1,0) and h=(0,1), direct multiplication gives

    a^h=(4,0)=a^4,
    [a,_j h]=a^(3^j)=(3^j,0).

The element a has infinite order, and these iterated commutators are nontrivial for every finite j, although they converge to the identity. Thus the abstract subgroup generated by a and h is not nilpotent, and P is not abstractly locally nilpotent.

This example invalidates replacing finite-step commutator vanishing by mere pronilpotence. It is not asserted to satisfy (LI), and it is not a counterexample to the extension theorem.
