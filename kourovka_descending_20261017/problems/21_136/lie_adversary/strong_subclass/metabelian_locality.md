# Bounded torsion cosets in every open subgroup of a metabelian pro-p group

Status: self-contained deduction; parent independently audited the norm identity, splitting argument, and iteration without finding a gap.
Checkpoint: 2026-10-24T17:24:47Z. Estimated completion of the assigned subclass check: 100% (the general solvable case remains open here).

## Exact hypothesis and conclusion

Let p be a prime. Say a pro-p group G has property P0 if every open subgroup H of G contains a coset xU, with U an open subgroup of H and x in H, on which every element has order dividing some p-power m (depending on H and the selected coset).

The question's property P, that every nonempty open coset contains an open bounded-torsion subcoset, implies P0. We prove:

**Theorem.** A virtually metabelian pro-p group satisfying P0 has finite exponent and is locally finite.

Here metabelian means that the closed commutator subgroup is abelian. No finite-generation assumption is imposed. The proof is purely algebraic after two elementary compactness steps. It gives no assertion for arbitrary solvable groups of derived length at least three.

## Elementary inheritance and the abelian case

P0 passes to open subgroups and continuous quotient groups. For the quotient assertion, lift an open subgroup to G, apply P0 there, and project the selected open coset; the quotient map is open and orders cannot increase.

If B is abelian and has P0, choose one coset xU of exponent dividing m=p^n. Since x and xu both have m-th power one, every u in U has m-th power one. Since B/U is a finite p-group of some exponent p^r, B has exponent dividing p^(n+r).

## Norm identity

Let A be a closed normal abelian subgroup of an ambient pro-p group G, let H be an open subgroup of G, and let

    V = A tensor_(Z_p) Q_p.

Conjugation by H defines Q_p-linear automorphisms of V; it is not necessary that A be contained in H. Suppose xU is a coset inside H, with U open in H, of exponent dividing m, and b belongs to xU. For each a in A intersect U, both b and ba belong to xU. Thus b^m=(ba)^m=1. Writing A additively and denoting conjugation by b as alpha_b, the usual power formula gives

    (1 + alpha_b + ... + alpha_b^(m-1))(a) = 0.

The subgroup A intersect U has finite index in A, so it spans V over Q_p. Consequently

    N_m(alpha_b) = 0 on V,
    N_m(T) := 1 + T + ... + T^(m-1).

The direction chosen for conjugation does not matter: replacing alpha_b by its inverse multiplies the norm by an invertible automorphism.

If all automorphisms induced by elements of H have exponent dividing q=p^e, we may enlarge m to a p-power multiple of q. Then

    N_m(alpha_b) = (m/q) N_q(alpha_b),

and characteristic zero gives N_q(alpha_b)=0.

## The exponent-drop lemma

**Lemma.** Suppose a group H acts on a Q_p-vector space V through a commuting family of automorphisms of exponent dividing q=p^e, with e>=1. If U<=H and x belongs to H satisfy

    N_q(alpha_(xu)) = 0 for every u in U,

then the action of U has exponent dividing q/p.

**Proof.** Fix u in U. The commuting automorphisms alpha_x and alpha_u generate a finite abelian group E of exponent dividing q. Extend scalars to a field L containing all q-th roots of unity. The representation of E on L tensor V decomposes into its character subspaces. This remains true if V is infinite-dimensional: the finitely many Fourier idempotents of the finite abelian group E sum to the identity.

On any nonzero character subspace, write xi=chi(alpha_x) and eta=chi(alpha_u). For each integer i, u^i belongs to U, so the hypothesis gives

    N_q(xi eta^i) = 0.

Since xi eta^i is a q-th root of unity and the field has characteristic zero, its norm is q if xi eta^i=1 and zero otherwise. Therefore xi does not belong to the cyclic group generated by eta. If eta had order q, that group would contain all q-th roots of unity, including xi. Hence eta has order at most q/p. This holds on every character subspace, giving alpha_u^(q/p)=1. Since u was arbitrary, the lemma follows. [End proof.]

## Proof of the theorem in the metabelian case

Let A be the closed commutator subgroup of G, and B=G/A. The abelian quotient B inherits P0 and therefore has exponent q=p^e. On V=A tensor_(Z_p) Q_p, all conjugation automorphisms commute, because A acts trivially on itself; their exponents divide q, because g^q lies in A for every g in G.

Starting with H_0=G, apply P0 to obtain a bounded-torsion coset x_0 H_1 inside H_0. The norm identity and exponent-drop lemma imply that H_1 acts on V with exponent dividing p^(e-1). Apply P0 inside H_1 and repeat, always keeping the original A and V fixed; A need not lie in H_i, but A intersect H_(i+1) remains open in A and spans V. After e steps, an open subgroup H_e acts trivially on V. (If e=0, use H_e=G immediately.)

Apply P0 once more inside H_e to obtain a bounded-torsion coset xU of exponent dividing p^n. The norm identity now reads

    p^n Id_V = 0,

because H_e acts trivially. Thus V=0.

Localization at p kills A exactly when each element of A is killed by some p-power. Hence A is a torsion abelian pro-p group. The closed subgroups A[p^n] cover A. By the Baire theorem, one has nonempty interior; as it is a subgroup, it is open. Its finite quotient then shows that A has bounded exponent, say p^s. Since B has exponent p^e, G has exponent dividing p^(s+e).

To prove local finiteness without invoking a general periodic-profinite theorem, let F be any finitely generated abstract subgroup of G. Its image in the bounded-exponent abelian group B is finite. Thus F intersect A has finite index in F and is finitely generated by Schreier's theorem. It is a finitely generated torsion abelian group and therefore finite. Hence F is finite.

## Passing to a finite extension

If an open subgroup M of G is metabelian, it inherits P0 and has bounded exponent by the preceding proof. Its core C is an open normal subgroup of G contained in M, so C has the same exponent bound. The finite p-group G/C then gives a finite exponent bound for G.

Every finitely generated subgroup F of G has F intersect M of finite index; this intersection is finitely generated and finite because M is locally finite. Thus F is finite. This completes the theorem.

## Additional elementary subclasses

1. If G has an open subgroup that is nilpotent of finite class, P0 also implies finite exponent and local finiteness. In a finite-class nilpotent group, torsion elements form a subgroup: any finitely generated nilpotent group generated by torsion elements is finite, as its lower central factors are generated by finite-order multilinear commutators. A single bounded-torsion coset xU in that nilpotent open subgroup therefore makes all of U torsion; finite index then makes the whole nilpotent subgroup torsion. Finite-class nilpotent torsion groups are locally finite by the same argument. Compactness gives bounded exponent by induction on nilpotency class: the compact torsion center is bounded-exponent abelian, and its quotient is compact torsion nilpotent of smaller class. Then pass to the finite extension as above.

2. If G has an open torsion-free subgroup, P0 implies G is finite. A bounded-torsion coset inside a torsion-free open subgroup can only consist of the identity. Hence that open subgroup has an open singleton and is finite; being torsion-free, it is trivial. In particular this applies whenever an independent structural theorem supplies an open torsion-free subgroup, for example a finite-dimensional p-adic analytic pro-p group.

## Exact remaining gap

The metabelian argument uses commuting conjugation actions essentially. For a nonabelian quotient of bounded exponent, a coset of automorphisms with vanishing norms need not lower the action exponent in one step. For example, in the faithful complex two-dimensional representation of Q8, every element of the coset i<j> has no eigenvalue 1, although <j> still has exponent 4. Thus replacing finite abelian character decomposition by an unsupported general representation claim would be invalid.

No counterexample to P in arbitrary pro-p groups or solvable groups of derived length at least three is established here. No novelty claim is made for the subclass theorem pending literature comparison.
