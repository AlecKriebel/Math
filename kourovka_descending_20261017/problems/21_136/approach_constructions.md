## 1. Normal locally nilpotent kernel and torsion quotient

Here locally nilpotent means **abstractly locally nilpotent**: every finitely generated abstract subgroup is nilpotent. A pronilpotent profinite group need not have this property.

**Proposition.** Let G be a profinite group and A a closed normal abstract locally nilpotent subgroup. If G/A is torsion and A is not torsion, then G has at least continuum many conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements.

The proof does not require finite generation, metrizability, a bound on the primes dividing G, or the continuum hypothesis.

### Preliminary compact abelian facts

(i) A compact abelian torsion group H has finite exponent. Indeed H is the union of its closed subgroups H[n]={h:h^n=1}. Baire's theorem gives an H[n] with nonempty interior. As a subgroup it is open. The finite quotient H/H[n] has exponent d, whence H has exponent dividing nd.

(ii) Any infinite profinite group has at least continuum many elements. Construct a strictly descending chain of open normal subgroups in an infinite metrizable quotient, or directly a binary tree of nested nonempty clopen subsets with disjoint siblings, then use compactness. No assertion that the original group is metrizable is needed.

(iii) For a procyclic group C with supernatural order product p^{a_p}, its automorphism group is the product of the unit groups of Z/p^{a_p}Z (with Z_p when a_p is infinite). A generator is carried to another generator by a unique such unit. When C is infinite, Aut(C) has unbounded exponent: either some a_p is infinite, giving a quotient Z_p^×, or infinitely many primes occur, giving unit-group quotients F_p^× of unbounded orders p−1.

### Case 1: an infinite p-component

Choose x in A with C=closure(<x>) isomorphic to Z_p. Put U=Z_p^×. The elements x^u for u in U are all infinite-order generators of C. Two such generators are conjugate in G if and only if their ratio of exponents belongs to the image H of the continuous normalizer action

    N_G(C) -> Aut(C)=U.

For g in N_G(C), write x^g=x^lambda. Because G/A is torsion, g^m=h belongs to A for some positive integer m. The abstract subgroup generated by x and h is nilpotent of some finite class c. With [x,h]=x^{-1}x^h, induction gives

    [x,_c h] = x^((lambda^m−1)^c) = 1.

The map Z_p -> C, a -> x^a, is injective. Hence (lambda^m−1)^c=0 in the integral domain Z_p, and lambda^m=1. Thus every element of the compact abelian image H is torsion. In fact H lies in the finite torsion subgroup of U (of order p−1 for odd p and order 2 for p=2). Every fusion orbit of the continuum many generators has at most this finite size. There are continuum many G-classes of infinite-order elements.

### Case 2: infinitely many prime components

It remains to consider an infinite-order a in A whose procyclic closure C has no infinite p-component. Then C has infinitely many nontrivial finite p-components. Let I be this infinite set of primes, let U=Aut(C), and let

    r: U -> V = product_{p in I} F_p^×

be the surjective coordinatewise residue map. Again put H=image(N_G(C)->U), which is compact and hence closed. If g acts on C by lambda in H, then g^m=h lies in A for some m; the subgroup generated by a and h is nilpotent of class c. Thus (lambda^m−1)^c acts trivially on C. In each p-component this implies lambda_p^m=1 modulo p. Consequently r(lambda)^m=1 in V. Every element of r(H) is torsion.

By (i), the compact abelian group r(H) has finite exponent. The group V has unbounded exponent because it surjects onto cyclic groups of orders p−1 for infinitely many unbounded primes p. Thus r(H) cannot have finite index in V: a finite-index bounded-exponent subgroup of an abelian group forces the whole group to have bounded exponent. The quotient V/r(H) is an infinite profinite group, so by (ii) it has at least continuum many elements. The quotient U/H maps onto V/r(H), and generator fusion classes in C are exactly the cosets of H in U. The claimed continuum lower bound follows.

**Important distinction.** In Case 2 the full image H need not be torsion. Nilpotence of lambda^m−1 on a finite p-power component allows nontrivial unipotent units, whose orders may be unbounded across components. Only its residue image r(H) is asserted torsion. Omitting this distinction would create a gap.

The proposition applies when A is abelian as a special case. There the simpler argument g^m in A centralizes C shows H itself torsion for every infinite procyclic C, and (iii) immediately excludes finite index.

**Corollary.** If a profinite group with fewer than continuum many infinite-order conjugacy classes has a finite series of closed subgroups, all normal in G, whose factors are abstract locally nilpotent or torsion, then it is torsion. In particular this holds for every solvable profinite group of finite derived length, and for every group with an open such subgroup.

For a finite series, induct on its length through the quotient by the first nontrivial term. The class-count bound passes to continuous quotients. Once the quotient is torsion, a torsion kernel makes the whole group torsion; a locally nilpotent kernel is torsion by the proposition. For solvable groups use the closed derived series, whose factors are abelian. For open subgroups the class-count bound is inherited: one ambient conjugacy class splits into at most [G:O] O-classes inside an open subgroup O. A finite extension of a torsion group is torsion. This corollary says nothing about arbitrary prosolvable groups with unbounded derived lengths, nor about series with merely pronilpotent factors.

## 2. Why the affine fusion model fails

Let G=Z_p ⋊ Z_p^× with multiplication (a,u)(b,v)=(a+ub,uv). Conjugation by the multiplier group fuses translations according to their p-adic valuation. Hence the infinite-order translations alone have only countably many ambient conjugacy classes. This is a genuine warning against counting only a convenient normal subgroup.

Nevertheless the continuous quotient onto Z_p^× has continuum many infinite-order elements (take 1+pZ_p for odd p and 1+4Z_2 for p=2). Every multiplier u of infinite order gives an infinite-order element (0,u), and its multiplier is invariant under conjugation because the quotient is abelian. Thus G itself has continuum many required classes.

Replacing the acting group by a compact torsion group does not repair the construction: its image in Z_p^× consists of torsion units and is finite. The translations then already have continuum many orbits. More generally, the proposition rules out every normal abelian-by-torsion attempt, including non-finitely generated or nonmetrizable versions.

## 3. Direct products and continuous coordinate permutation fusion

For a direct product of finite groups, an infinite-order element must have coordinate orders unbounded. Select a countable sequence of distinct coordinates with strictly increasing orders. At the nth selected coordinate retain the chosen element or replace it by 1 according to an infinite subset S of N; keep all other coordinates trivial. Every such element has infinite order. Distinct S have distinct coordinate-order sequences, which are conjugacy invariants of the direct product. This gives continuum classes. If coordinate orders are uniformly bounded, the product has bounded exponent and is torsion.

The same obstruction survives a compact group K that acts continuously by permutations of the finite factors (possibly transporting each factor by isomorphisms). Delete trivial factors first; their labels carry no group data and may be permuted without affecting continuity of the base action. Every orbit of the remaining nontrivial coordinates is finite. One way to justify this without assuming it is to view a coordinate projection as a continuous map from the compact base to a finite discrete group: its compact-open orbit under K is compact in a discrete space, hence finite. Distinct nontrivial coordinate labels give distinct such projection maps, even after factor isomorphisms. Equivalently compactness gives an open subgroup of K fixing that coordinate projection as a map. Hence K has finite orbits on nontrivial coordinates.

Group the factors into these finite K-orbits. The order of the component in each finite orbit block is invariant under conjugation in the whole semidirect product. If the base contains an infinite-order element, its block orders are unbounded. Binary support coding on a countable sequence of blocks again gives continuum infinite-order ambient classes. If the base is torsion and K is torsion, the extension is torsion. Thus a permutation wreath construction of this kind cannot create a first counterexample.

An unrestricted regular action of an infinite profinite group on an infinite **discrete** coordinate set is not a continuous compact permutation action: a continuous orbit of a compact group in a discrete space is finite. Writing a full regular wreath product without checking joint continuity does not produce an admissible profinite example.

### Stronger ambient obstruction: unbounded abelian torsion

The coordinate-invariance argument above is unnecessary when the finite groups form an actual subgroup of a larger profinite group: the following proof accommodates **arbitrary ambient fusion** and requires neither normality nor a coordinate action.

**Power-coding lemma.** If a profinite group G contains an abelian torsion subgroup T of unbounded exponent, then G has at least continuum many conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements.

The subgroup T need not be closed. Put A=closure(T), a closed abelian subgroup. For every open normal N of G, the group T intersect N has unbounded exponent. Indeed a finite exponent there, combined with finiteness of T/(T intersect N), would bound the exponent of T.

Set N_0=G. Recursively, after choosing finite-order b_1,...,b_(i−1) in T with orders m_1,...,m_(i−1), define

    E_i = lcm(i!, m_1, ..., m_(i−1)).

Choose b_i in T intersect N_(i−1) whose order m_i does not divide E_i; this is possible by the preceding unbounded-exponent observation. Choose an open normal N_i contained in N_(i−1) such that b_i^E_i does not belong to N_i. Residual finiteness and b_i^E_i != 1 supply this choice.

For every binary subset S of the positive integers put q_k=product_{1<=j<=k, j in S} b_j. The compact nonempty sets

    q_k (A intersect N_k)

are nested: q_(k+1) differs from q_k by either 1 or b_(k+1), which lies in A intersect N_k. Compactness gives at least one x_S in their intersection. No countable-base assumption or convergence of an infinite product is made. Since A is abelian, for every i its image in G/N_i satisfies

    x_S^E_i = 1                if i is not in S,
    x_S^E_i = b_i^E_i != 1     if i is in S.

All earlier factors are killed by E_i and all later choices leave the chosen N_i-coset fixed. Thus the conjugacy-invariant property x^E_i in N_i recovers every bit of S, even for conjugacy in all of G. Distinct S supply different ambient classes.

If S is infinite and x_S had finite order L, choose i in S with i>=L. Since L divides i! and hence E_i, the selected-bit condition would contradict x_S^E_i=1. Therefore all infinite S give infinite-order elements. There are continuum many infinite subsets of the positive integers, proving the lemma.

**Consequences and limits.** Under the small-class hypothesis, every abelian torsion subgroup has a finite exponent; the bound may depend on the subgroup. In particular G cannot contain a closed direct product of finite cyclic groups of unbounded orders, or a closed product of finite groups with unbounded exponents: in the latter choose cyclic subgroups of unbounded orders in different factors and apply the lemma. This remains true when the ambient G does not preserve those factors. It does not force a uniform exponent for all torsion elements of G, because the torsion elements need not form an abelian subgroup. It does not prove that all closed abelian subgroups are torsion: an affine group can fuse unit powers in a Z_p subgroup, whose finite-order torsion subgroup is trivial.

## 4. Rooted-tree iterated wreath groups: dense torsion still leaves continuum classes

Let T_p be the rooted p-ary tree and W_p the full iterated permutational wreath limit of C_p, realized as the closed subgroup of Aut(T_p) whose local vertex permutations are powers of a fixed p-cycle. It is a pro-p group. Finite-depth automorphisms extended trivially below their depth are torsion and dense, while the standard odometer has infinite order. Thus it is an honest test of the dense-torsion obstruction emphasized by Wilson, rather than an all-torsion false candidate.

Use the incomparable vertices v_n=1^n0 for n>=0. In each subtree below v_n place the standard p-adic odometer a_n. Their supports are disjoint. For every S subset of N the infinite product

    g_S = product_{n in S} a_n

is well defined: its restriction to every finite level depends on finitely many n. It belongs to W_p by closedness. If S is nonempty, g_S has infinite order because its restriction to any active subtree has infinite order.

At level m, for n in S with n<=m−2, the action on descendants of v_n is a single cycle of length p^{m−n−1}. Different n give different nontrivial cycle lengths. Everything outside active subtrees is fixed. Thus the number of cycles of length p^j at level m is exactly the indicator of m−j−1 in S, for 1<=j<=m−1. These cycle counts are invariant under conjugation even in the larger ambient Aut(T_p). Distinct S are distinguished at some finite level. The nonempty subsets S therefore give continuum many infinite-order conjugacy classes.

The exact finite-level implementation in construction_artifacts/verify_tree_support.py independently builds the permutations and compares their complete cycle histograms with this formula. The universal proof above establishes the all-level claim; the finite checks are consistency checks and do not replace it. No claim about fusion in an arbitrary overgroup lacking the tree action is made.

The power-coding lemma gives an additional proof that does handle arbitrary profinite overgroups: inside W_p put, on disjoint subtrees of increasing depths, finite-depth odometers of orders p, p^2, p^3, and so on. Their abstract direct sum is an abelian torsion subgroup of unbounded exponent. Hence every profinite group containing this W_p contains continuum many ambient infinite-order classes, without requiring that conjugators respect the original tree action. The earlier infinite-odometer family remains useful as a direct explicit cycle-type certificate within Aut(T_p).

### A second inverse-limit test: infinite unitriangular groups

Let U be the profinite inverse limit of the finite upper unitriangular groups UT_n(F_p), with the leading-principal-block quotient maps. Equivalently U consists of all infinite upper unitriangular matrices indexed by the positive integers; each entry of a product uses a finite sum, so the product topology makes this a compact group. Finitely supported matrices give a dense torsion subgroup. The first superdiagonal map

    U -> product_{i>=1} F_p,   g -> (g_(i,i+1))_i

is a continuous homomorphism to an abelian group and therefore is invariant under conjugacy. For any 0/1 sequence a let N_a have precisely the entries a_i on its first superdiagonal and zeros elsewhere. In characteristic p,

    (I+N_a)^(p^k) = I+N_a^(p^k),
    (N_a^r)_(i,i+r) = a_i a_(i+1) ... a_(i+r−1).

Reserve disjoint mandatory runs of 1s of unbounded lengths, separated by infinitely many independent coding positions. Every coding sequence then gives an infinite-order element I+N_a, while different sequences have different first-superdiagonal images and hence different U-classes. Again there are continuum many infinite-order classes. This argument was independently obtained by the parent at the same checkpoint.

Alternatively U contains commuting finite block-diagonal Jordan groups of unbounded p-power orders on disjoint coordinate intervals, so the power-coding lemma excludes it even inside an arbitrary profinite overgroup. The example shows why bounded-exponent abelianization plus dense torsion does not make an inverse-limit group a viable counterexample.

## 5. Approaches blocked or not yet justified

* Adjoining finite-order conjugators to fuse all unit powers of a normal Z_p fails: finite-order automorphisms of Z_p form a finite group. Adjoining infinite-order affine conjugators merely transfers the required class count to the multiplier quotient.
* Repeating torsion-kernel/torsion-quotient extensions finitely many times remains torsion. An inverse limit of these extensions can acquire infinite-order elements, so this elementary extension argument cannot be applied through an infinite limit. The rooted-tree example demonstrates this failure and supplies its own continuum-class witnesses.
* A hypothetical inverse system of finite groups with only countably many infinite-order conjugacy paths would be a genuine candidate. A bare tree of finite conjugacy data can have countably many such paths, but realizing that tree by compatible finite group epimorphisms is the central unsupported difficulty. No such system has been constructed here. Merely assigning conjugacy fusion at successive levels does not prove existence of finite groups or compatibility with their multiplication.
* The local normalizer mechanism demands substantial non-solvable or unbounded-depth behavior from any counterexample. It does not show that one may inherit the ambient class bound in an arbitrary finitely generated closed subgroup. Using Wilson's finite-generation theorem on such a subgroup would be invalid because conjugacy in the ambient group may fuse its classes.
