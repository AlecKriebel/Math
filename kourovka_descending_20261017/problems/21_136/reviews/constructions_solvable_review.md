## Verdict and exact scope

**PASS, with a strengthening and explicit scope restrictions.** The proposed finite fusion argument for a p-infinite element in a closed normal abelian kernel is correct. Its extension to a closed normal **abstract locally nilpotent** kernel is also correct. The finite-derived-length soluble conclusion follows. In fact, neither kernel result needs Wilson's finite-prime lemma: arbitrary infinite procyclic subgroups can be handled directly, as proved below.

The strongest statement verified here is the following extension principle.

> **Extension principle.** Let G be a profinite group, A a closed normal abstract locally nilpotent subgroup, and suppose G/A is torsion. If A has an element of infinite order, then G has at least continuum many conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements. Consequently, if G has fewer than continuum such classes, then both A and G are torsion.

Here abstract locally nilpotent means that every finitely generated abstract subgroup is nilpotent, with a class bound allowed to depend on the subgroup. It must not be replaced by pronilpotent. A pronilpotent group is not necessarily abstract locally nilpotent. The result does not settle the arbitrary profinite, arbitrary pro-p, or arbitrary prosoluble case.

## 1. The proposed p-infinite finite fusion argument

Suppose x in A topologically generates B isomorphic to Z_p. Use x^g = g^{-1}xg. Its topological generators are exactly x^u with u in U = Z_p^times; there are continuum many, and all have infinite order.

If (x^u)^g = x^v for units u,v, then g normalizes B, because both source and image generate B topologically. Its action on B is multiplication by lambda = v/u. Since G/A is torsion, g^m is in A for some positive integer m.

If A is abelian, g^m centralizes B, so lambda^m = 1. Thus lambda lies in the finite torsion subgroup of Z_p^times, of order p-1 for odd p and of order 2 for p=2. Each ambient G-conjugacy class therefore meets the generator set in at most p-1, respectively 2, points. Partitioning a continuum-sized set into uniformly finite classes yields continuum many classes in ZFC; no continuum hypothesis is used.

The statement remains valid for abstract locally nilpotent A. Put h=g^m. The subgroup generated abstractly by x,h is nilpotent, say of class c. Since h normalizes B and x^h=x^(lambda^m), repeated commutators give

    [x, h, ..., h] = x^((lambda^m - 1)^c) = 1,

with c copies of h, using [y,h]=y^{-1}y^h. The repeated commutator has weight c+1 and therefore vanishes. The exponent is a p-adic integer; injectivity of alpha -> x^alpha and the fact that Z_p is an integral domain imply lambda^m=1. The same finite fusion bound follows.

This calculation uses continuity of conjugation on B to apply the scalar action to all p-adic powers. It does not assume that the abstract cyclic group generated by x is closed, and it does not assume B is normal in all of G. Its normalization follows individually for every conjugator being considered.

## 2. Three elementary compact-group facts needed for the strengthening

### 2.1 Compact abelian torsion groups have finite exponent

Let K be compact Hausdorff, abelian, and torsion as an abstract group. The closed subgroups K[n!] = {k : k^(n!)=1} cover K. The Baire theorem for compact Hausdorff spaces gives one of these subgroups nonempty interior; a subgroup with nonempty interior is open. It has finite index r, because its cosets form an open cover of the compact group. The finite quotient has exponent dividing r, so K has exponent dividing r n!. No metrizability or countable-generation hypothesis is used.

The conclusion would be false without compactness. It is not a claim that a compact abelian torsion group must be finite: an infinite elementary abelian product has finite exponent.

### 2.2 An infinite procyclic group's automorphism group has unbounded exponent

Write an infinite procyclic group C as

    C = product over p in pi(C) of C_(p^e(p)),

where e(p) is a positive integer or infinity, with C_(p^infinity)=Z_p. Continuous automorphisms preserve these characteristic Sylow factors, so

    Aut(C) = product over p of (Z / p^e(p) Z)^times,

with the factor Z_p^times when e(p)=infinity.

If some e(p)=infinity, that automorphism group has an infinite-order element: multiplication by 1+p is a unit and no positive integer power of the ordinary integer 1+p is 1 in Z_p. This works also for p=2. If every e(p) is finite, infinitude of C requires infinitely many distinct primes. For each such prime, Aut(C) has a quotient F_p^times of order p-1. These orders are unbounded. A finite exponent for Aut(C) would have to be divisible by every such p-1, which is impossible. These two cases exhaust the possibilities.

### 2.3 Infinite closed profinite quotients have at least continuum points

An infinite profinite group has no isolated points: an isolated identity would make it discrete and hence finite by compactness. Recursively split nonempty clopen subsets into two disjoint nonempty clopen subsets. Compactness along every branch of the resulting binary tree supplies distinct points for all infinite binary sequences. Thus an infinite profinite group has at least continuum points.

In particular, if V is a profinite abelian group of unbounded exponent and K is a closed bounded-exponent subgroup, then K has infinite index. Otherwise the finite quotient V/K, together with the exponent bound on K, would give an exponent bound for V. Consequently V/K has at least continuum points.

## 3. Abelian kernels: all infinite procyclic groups, without a finite-prime hypothesis

Assume first A is abelian, and choose any infinite-order x in A. Let C be its closed procyclic subgroup. Since A is closed, C is contained in A. Let

    H = image of N_G(C) in Aut(C).

The normalizer is closed and compact, its action is continuous, and hence H is compact and closed. The usual topology on Aut(C) is the product topology in Section 2.2; equivalently one can evaluate an automorphism at the fixed generator x. In particular H is a compact abelian group.

Each g in N_G(C) has some positive power in A, which centralizes C. Its action therefore has finite order. Thus H is torsion and has finite exponent by Section 2.1. By Section 2.2, Aut(C) has unbounded exponent. Therefore Aut(C)/H is an infinite profinite group and has at least continuum points.

The generators of C are parametrized regularly by Aut(C). Two of them are conjugate in G exactly when their parameters are in the same H-coset. One implication is the normalization argument from Section 1; the converse is the definition of H. It follows that continuum distinct G-conjugacy classes already meet the infinite-order generator set of C. This argument applies even when C has infinitely many finite Sylow factors and no Z_p factor at all.

This proof is independent of Wilson's Lemma 4.2 and of the finite generation result in that paper.

## 4. Locally nilpotent kernels: residue actions repair the possible unipotent obstruction

Retain any infinite-order x in A and C equal to its closed procyclic subgroup.

If C has a Z_p Sylow factor, that factor is a subgroup of C, hence lies in closed A. Apply Section 1 to one of its topological generators. This gives the required continuum classes immediately. This step does not require a finite prime set.

Otherwise all Sylow factors of C are finite, and pi(C) is infinite. Define

    V = product over p in pi(C) of F_p^times,

and let rho: Aut(C) -> V be coordinatewise reduction modulo p. This is a continuous surjection. Let H be the compact normalizer-action image as above and K=rho(H), which is compact, closed, and abelian.

For any g in N_G(C), write its scalar action as lambda, and choose m positive with h=g^m in A. The abstract group generated by x,h is nilpotent of some class c. The same commutator calculation as before gives

    (lambda^m - 1)^c = 0

in the profinite exponent ring product_p Z/p^e(p)Z. Reducing each coordinate modulo p produces (rho(lambda)_p^m - 1)^c = 0 in the field F_p, so rho(lambda)_p^m = 1 for every p. The integer m may depend on g but is common to all its prime coordinates. Therefore every element of K is torsion. Section 2.1 makes K bounded exponent.

The full V has unbounded exponent, because its cyclic quotients F_p^times have unbounded orders p-1. Thus V/K is infinite profinite and has at least continuum points. Surjectivity of rho induces a surjection

    Aut(C)/H -> V/K.

It follows that Aut(C)/H has at least continuum points as well. As in Section 3 this is exactly the number of ambient G-classes meeting the generators of C. The extension principle is proved.

**Important correction to a tempting stronger claim:** for a locally nilpotent kernel, one may not assert that H itself is torsion from (lambda^m-1)^c=0 when C has finite p-primary factors. Their residue rings have nonzero nilpotents and permit nontrivial unipotent units. Only the residue-action image K is shown torsion in this case. The proof above explicitly accommodates this issue.

## 5. Finite-derived-length soluble induction

Let P(G) denote the strict bound of fewer than continuum conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements. This property passes to continuous quotients: an infinite-order quotient element has only infinite-order lifts, and mapping a lift's conjugacy class to its quotient class is well defined and surjective onto the quotient's infinite-order class set.

Suppose G has a finite closed normal series with abelian factors. Induct on its length. The trivial group is torsion. At a nontrivial inductive step choose a closed normal abelian subgroup A such that G/A has shorter such a series. The quotient inherits P and hence is torsion by induction. Section 3 forces A to be torsion. Finally, a torsion-by-torsion extension is torsion: for any g, some positive power g^m belongs to A and then (g^m)^n=1 for a suitable n depending on g. No uniform exponent bound is claimed.

Consequently every finite-derived-length soluble profinite group satisfying P is torsion. Finite abstract derived length gives the required closed series as well: closure commutes with taking the closed commutator subgroup, by continuity of the commutator map. More generally the same induction works for a finite closed normal series whose factors are abstract locally nilpotent, using Section 4 instead of Section 3.

This is a restricted-class result. A prosoluble group can have unbounded derived length and lacks the finite induction used here. Neither a transfinite induction through closures nor inheritance of P by arbitrary closed subgroups has been proved.

## 6. Falsification checks and limits

- **p=2:** the unit torsion subgroup has order 2, not p-1. The stated finite orbit bound accounts for this.
- **Ambient fusion:** every conjugator between generators of the same C normalizes C. The proof uses ambient G-conjugacy throughout and never assumes arbitrary closed-subgroup inheritance of P.
- **Infinite prime support:** C=product_p C_p can have infinite-order generators and no p-infinite elements. Section 3 handles this directly; Section 4 uses the residue-action product. Merely asserting that every infinite-order profinite element contains a Z_p component would be false without additional hypotheses.
- **Need for a torsion quotient in the finite fusion step:** the compact affine group Z_p semidirect (1+pZ_p), for odd p, has abelian kernel Z_p and normalizer action containing the infinite group 1+pZ_p. Its kernel's generators can fuse in infinite sets. Here the quotient is not torsion, so the reviewed proof correctly does not apply. The affine group still has continuum infinite-order classes from its quotient and is not a counterexample to 21.136.
- **Nilpotent versus pronilpotent:** the iterated-commutator argument needs an actual finite nilpotency class for the abstract two-generator subgroup generated by x and h. This is supplied by abstract local nilpotence, not merely by residual nilpotence, pronilpotence, prosolubility, or a dense locally nilpotent subgroup.
- **Cardinality:** all bounds use a continuum-sized procyclic generator set or an infinite profinite quotient. There is no replacement of the strict continuum bound by countability, and no appeal to the continuum hypothesis.
- **General obstruction:** neither existence of a nontrivial closed normal locally nilpotent subgroup nor a finite normal series of the type used above is available for an arbitrary profinite counterexample candidate. This remains an exact gap, not a new solution claim.
