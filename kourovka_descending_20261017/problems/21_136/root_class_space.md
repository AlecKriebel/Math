# Countably based reduction, class space, and a normalizer obstruction

Checkpoint: 24 October 2026. Full-problem discovery estimate **10%**. These are reductions and necessary conditions, not a resolution or a novelty claim. The source target remains every profinite group, with strictly fewer than continuum conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements.

## 1. A counterexample survives in a countably based quotient

**Lemma 1.** If a profinite group G contains an infinite-order element x, some countably based quotient of G contains the infinite-order image of x.

**Proof.** The orders of the images of x in the finite continuous quotients of G are unbounded. Otherwise their orders would lie among1,...,M for some M; their least common multiple would kill x in every quotient and hence in G. For each positive integer i choose an open normal subgroup U_i such that xU_i has order greater than i. Put N_i=U_1 intersect ... intersect U_i and N=intersection_i N_i. Then G/N is the inverse limit of the finite groups G/N_i. Compactness proves that the natural map onto this inverse limit is surjective; its kernel is N. This is a countably based compact metrizable profinite group. The image of x has unbounded orders in the quotients G/N_i and therefore has infinite order. QED.

The bound on infinite-order conjugacy classes passes to every continuous quotient: a lift of an infinite-order quotient element has infinite order, and conjugate lifts have conjugate images. Hence any counterexample to21.136 yields a countably based counterexample with the same strict cardinal bound. This is a genuine reduction of the unrestricted problem, not an assumption that the original group has a countable base.

## 2. In a countably based group the cardinal bound becomes countability

**Lemma 2.** The space of infinite-order conjugacy classes in a countably based profinite group is Polish. Consequently it is either countable or has cardinality continuum.

**Proof.** Choose a compatible bi-invariant metric d on G, for example the ultrametric from a descending open normal neighborhood basis. For conjugacy classes define

    delta([x],[y]) = min { d(x, g^-1 y g) : g in G }.

The minimum exists by compactness. Conjugation invariance of d makes this independent of the representatives, symmetric and subject to the triangle inequality. If it is zero then some conjugator realizes equality, so it is a metric. The projection from G onto its class space C is continuous. Its compact image shows that C with this metric is compact and has the quotient topology; it is metrizable and separable.

For each integer n>=1 let E_n={g:g^n=1}. It is compact and invariant under conjugation; its image in C is compact and hence closed. The infinite-order class space is

    J = C minus union_(n>=1) image(E_n),

a G_delta subspace of a compact metric space, and is therefore Polish. The perfect-set theorem for Polish spaces says that an uncountable Polish space contains a copy of Cantor space. Thus an uncountable J has cardinality at least continuum, and at most continuum because it is separable metrizable. QED.

Thus the full conjecture is equivalent to this precise special statement:

> Every countably based profinite group with countably many conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements is torsion.

No continuum hypothesis is used. This reduction does not make the group topologically finitely generated.

## 3. What an isolated infinite-order class would mean

If J is nonempty and countable, it has an isolated point. Indeed a nonempty countable Polish space is Baire; if it had no isolated points, its countably many closed singleton sets would all be nowhere dense, a contradiction. Equivalently there are an infinite-order x and an open set U in G with

    x in U,    U intersect {infinite-order elements} subset x^G.

One may replace U by a clopen coset containing x. The conjugacy class x^G is compact. Its relative openness among infinite-order elements does not make it open in G, and it does not imply that U has bounded exponent. Dense torsion can make the set of infinite-order elements thin.

An approach that merely assumes that every infinite-order class is nonisolated relative to J would settle the remaining problem only after proving that assertion. It is presently an unsupported central step, not an available lemma.

### The stronger torsion-coset property forced by countability

No conjugacy class of an infinite-order element has interior in a profinite group. Indeed, if Nx lies in x^G for an open normal N, then for every open normal L<=N the class of xL in G/L has at least |N/L| elements. Its centralizer has size at most [G:N]. The images of C_G(x) in all these quotients therefore have uniformly bounded size, forcing C_G(x) finite. This contradicts its containing the infinite procyclic group generated by x.

In the countably based small-class situation, the infinite-order elements are consequently a countable union of closed nowhere-dense conjugacy classes. Torsion elements are comeagre. More precisely, inside **every** nonempty clopen coset, Baire's theorem applied to those classes and the closed torsion sets E_n supplies a nonempty open subcoset contained in some E_n. Thus

    union_(n>=1) Int({g:g^n=1}) is dense and open in G.       (P)

This is stronger than density of torsion plus the existence of one bounded-exponent coset. Conversely, if the interior of the torsion set is dense, Baire inside a compact open torsion coset gives a smaller open coset of bounded exponent. Hence (P) is equivalent to density of the interior of the torsion set. This does not yet force all elements to be torsion; that inference remains unproved.

The argument counts all infinite-order element classes and is not automatically a statement about the subset of p-elements under ambient fusion. It uses countably based reduction and the Polish perfect-set theorem to handle the strict continuum threshold without the continuum hypothesis.

## 4. Necessary openness of procyclic normalizer actions

**Lemma 3.** Suppose G has fewer than continuum conjugacy classes of infinite-order elements. For every infinite procyclic closed subgroup C<=G, the image

    H = image( N_G(C) -> Aut(C) )

is open in Aut(C), and therefore has finite index.

**Proof.** Choose a topological generator x of C. Its generators are exactly x^u for u in Aut(C), under the identification of automorphisms of a procyclic group with units in its completed exponent ring. Every such generator has infinite order.

If two generators x^u,x^v are conjugate by g in G, conjugation sends the closed subgroup generated by one onto that generated by the other, so g normalizes C. Conversely an element of the image H supplies the corresponding conjugacy. Thus the number of G-conjugacy classes meeting the set of generators is exactly [Aut(C):H].

The normalizer is closed in compact G. Its action image is compact and closed in Aut(C): evaluation at x identifies the automorphism group topologically with the compact set of generators. Aut(C) is an abelian profinite group. If H had infinite index, the quotient Aut(C)/H would be an infinite profinite group and would have at least continuum elements. One elementary proof of the last fact constructs a nested binary tree of nonempty disjoint clopen sets; compactness supplies a distinct point on every infinite branch. This contradicts the hypothesis, so H has finite index and is open. QED.

For C=Z_p this gives a concrete necessary condition. Its automorphism group is Z_p^times. An open image contains1+p^k Z_p for some k (take k>=2 for p=2), so some g in G satisfies

    g^-1 x g = x^(1+p^k).

This condition forces the presence of a compactly realized power-conjugation mechanism for every Z_p subgroup. It does not yet contradict profiniteness: the familiar compact affine group Z_p semidirect (1+p^k Z_p) realizes that relation. That affine group's own infinite-order elements still have continuum conjugacy classes through the abelian quotient, so it is not a counterexample.

The general procyclic statement does not assert that an ordinary integer m>1 is a unit on C when all primes occur in its order; only the Z_p specialization uses the displayed integer power.

### A useful normal-kernel deduction

Suppose A is a closed normal abelian subgroup and G/A is torsion. If A contained an infinite-order element, put C equal to its infinite procyclic closure. For every g in N_G(C), some positive power g^m lies in A and centralizes C. The induced automorphism of C therefore has finite order. The entire normalizer image H is torsion.

An infinite procyclic C always has an infinite-order automorphism. If it has a Z_p factor, use multiplication by1+p on that factor (by5 for p=2) and the identity elsewhere. If all its Sylow components are finite, infinitely many primes occur; choose automorphisms whose reductions modulo these primes have orders p-1. Their product has infinite order because these finite orders are unbounded. Lemma3 says that H is open. A finite extension of a torsion group is torsion, so Aut(C)/H finite would make Aut(C) torsion, a contradiction. Hence A is torsion. Since G/A is also torsion, G is torsion.

This proves the original conclusion in the restricted **abelian-normal-by-torsion** situation, without finite generation or a restriction on the primes. By induction on the closed derived series it proves the conclusion for profinite soluble groups of finite derived length. It does not prove it for arbitrary prosoluble groups.

## 5. Exact research gaps and test requirements

- Quotient reduction is valid; closed-subgroup inheritance is not automatic. Ambient conjugacy can fuse many subgroup classes.
- The normalizer condition alone has not been shown sufficient for the small-class hypothesis or impossible in an infinitely generated profinite group.
- An isolated point of J is a class-space statement, not an open conjugacy class in G. A Baire argument applied to all of G would have to account separately for the torsion sets E_n.
- Wilson's finite-generation result cannot be applied to a finitely generated closed subgroup unless its own class-count bound is independently established.
- A construction must control all elements in the compact inverse limit, including elements outside the dense union of finite stages. Controlling that dense union only does not settle the infinite-order elements of its completion.
