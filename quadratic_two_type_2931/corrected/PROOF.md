# A necessary test and the unresolved lifting problem

## 1. Scope and notation

Let M and N be closed connected orientable topological 4-manifolds with finite fundamental group. Fix orientations for discussing forms. Their quadratic 2-types consist of the fundamental group, the second homotopy group as a module over its integral group ring, the first Postnikov k-invariant, and the equivariant intersection pairing. An isomorphism includes compatible group/module isomorphisms preserving the latter two data. Write B for a common Postnikov 2-stage, and f:M→B and g:N→B for the Postnikov maps after identifying their 2-stages.

The target is existence of an ordinary homotopy equivalence. It does not require that a specified isomorphism of quadratic 2-types, or a specified polarization, be realized. Nor does it ask for simple homotopy equivalence, a homeomorphism, or an equivalence after stabilization. An orientation-reversing equivalence also answers the target affirmatively.

## 2. Proved necessary fundamental-class condition

**Proposition.** If M and N are homotopy equivalent, there are a self-homotopy equivalence a:B→B and a sign ε∈{1,−1} such that

    a_* f_*[M] = ε g_*[N] in H_4(B;Z).

Consequently, if the two fundamental-class images lie in different orbits under all self-homotopy equivalences of B together with multiplication by −1, then M and N are not homotopy equivalent.

**Proof.** Let h:M→N be a homotopy equivalence. Functoriality of the Postnikov 2-stage gives a self-homotopy equivalence a of B for which a∘f and g∘h are homotopic. A homotopy inverse of h induces a homotopy inverse for a. Since M and N are closed connected oriented 4-manifolds, their top integral homology groups are infinite cyclic, generated by their fundamental classes. The isomorphism h_* therefore sends [M] to ε[N] for one of the two signs. Apply H_4 to the homotopy-commutative square:

    a_* f_*[M] = g_* h_*[M] = ε g_*[N].

The last assertion is the contrapositive. ∎

This proof is a basic necessary condition, not a sufficiency theorem or a new solution of KP-4.55. It is compatible with the much stronger cited classification by the Postnikov 2-stage and its fundamental-class image, recalled in [HKPR, Introduction and Section 2.1, Theorem 2.1]. That classification is a cited input rather than independently reproved here.

**Quantifier warning.** Finding one quadratic-2-type isomorphism that fails to carry the fundamental-class image correctly does not exclude a different self-equivalence a. To disprove ordinary equivalence using the proposition, one must exclude every a and both signs.

## 3. What nonzero quadratic-functor torsion does and does not imply

For a fixed finite fundamental group, fixed 2-stage B, and fixed equivariant form λ already realized by an oriented Poincaré-4-complex, the cited polarized classification describes the resulting nonempty set of polarized Poincaré-4-complex types by a torsor for

    T = Tors(Z ⊗_{Z[π]} Γ(π₂(B))).

Here Γ is Whitehead's quadratic functor. The torsor assertion is a published input [HH, Theorem 3.1]; no new Γ computation is claimed. Forgetting the polarization requires taking the appropriate self-equivalence orbits [HH, Remark 3.2]. Forgetting orientation can identify additional classes.

**Elementary logical lemma.** Knowing that a parameter set S is a torsor for a nontrivial group T does not imply that a quotient S/~ has more than one element. Indeed, take S=T with its regular translation action and let ~ identify all points. The regular action is free and transitive, yet S/~ is a singleton. Conversely, the equality relation leaves all points distinct. Thus torsor size alone determines neither possibility.

This is an abstract logical example. It does not claim that either of these equivalence relations is the actual topological self-equivalence relation for a particular B.

A second restriction is independent of that quotient: a Poincaré complex need not have the homotopy type of a closed topological manifold. Let S_man be the subset of polarized Poincaré types that admit such a realization. Even distinct ordinary-homotopy classes in S do not disprove the manifold statement unless two of them meet S_man. The exact question is whether the image of S_man under forgetting polarization and orientation can have more than one ordinary homotopy type within a fixed quadratic-2-type isomorphism class.

## 4. A counterexample certificate that is still missing

A valid negative solution along this route would supply all of:

1. Two actual closed orientable connected 4-manifolds, with finite fundamental groups, or independently justified manifold realizations of two Poincaré types.
2. An explicit isomorphism of their quadratic 2-types, including the module action, k-invariant, and equivariant form.
3. A proof of ordinary homotopy inequivalence. For example, the proposition supplies this if all self-equivalences of the common B, and both signs, fail the displayed fundamental-class equality.

No such pair, realization proof, or exhaustive self-equivalence calculation is supplied here. For the (Z/2)^3 test case, the recorded nonzero torsion therefore stops short of the requested counterexample. Conversely, no argument is supplied showing that all manifold-realizable points form a single ordinary-homotopy class. These are the exact unresolved steps.
