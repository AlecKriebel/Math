# KP-4.55: terminal research audit

Date: 2026-10-06. Problem ID: 2931. Rank: 920.

## Outcome

**Stalled partial.** No proof of the general implication and no counterexample were obtained. The elementary necessary condition in `PROOF.md` is proved, but it is not a solution. Current-source checks are bounded, not a proof that no later or unindexed result exists.

## Exact source target

K3, printed page 234, Problem 4.55 asks whether the quadratic 2-type suffices for ordinary homotopy classification of closed, connected, orientable 4-manifolds when their fundamental groups are finite. The section is about topological 4-manifolds; smoothness is not an additional hypothesis. K3's (Z/2)^3 discussion concerns polarized Poincaré complexes, and separately asks about forgetting polarization and about manifold realization. Those distinctions are essential. The ordinary equivalence sought need not realize a specified identification of invariants. [K3]

The adjacent definition includes π₁, its module π₂, the equivariant intersection form, and the first k-invariant. Choosing orientations makes the form explicit. Both possible degrees must be considered when excluding ordinary equivalence. K3 Remark (1) has its KPR24/KNR22 labels interchanged; the titles and theorems in the primary papers identify the intended references. [K3, KPR, KNR]

## What is already proved in the literature

- KPR's Theorem 1.1 covers every finite group whose Sylow 2-subgroup is abelian and generated by at most two elements, not merely abelian fundamental groups themselves. Its Theorem 1.2 gives the torsion-free Γ-coinvariant criterion. Section 5 reports that relevant coinvariants can have torsion for (Z/2)^3. These reported computations were not independently rerun here. [KPR]
- KNR proves the analogous classification when the Sylow 2-subgroup is dihedral. [KNR]
- The periodic-cohomology results of Hambleton–Kreck and Bauer, recalled by KPR, give further known positive cases. No new extension of their hypotheses is proved here. [KPR]
- HKPR's recent 3-manifold-group theorem is conditional on admissibility and does not cover arbitrary finite groups. Its Introduction notes that finite 3-manifold groups were already covered by the periodic-cohomology theorem. [HKPR]

## Nearby results do not close the gap

1. Hambleton–Nicholson, published in 2026, constructs homotopy-distinct stably diffeomorphic doubles. However, Theorem 6.16 says quadratic bias is determined by the quadratic 2-type on its stated class of doubles. Thus differing bias proves the quadratic 2-types differ; equal bias provides no converse. The paper explicitly leaves open whether its additional torsion parameter is required for homotopy classification. [HN]
2. Hambleton–Ünlü's Corollary D, combined with KNV's Theorem C, concerns ordinary versus simple homotopy equivalence. Its pairs already satisfy ordinary equivalence. Moreover, KNV Remark 1.4 constructs smooth examples over π*π, which is infinite for the nontrivial finite π at issue. These are two independent reasons this route does not refute KP-4.55. We use the cited implications only and do not claim a new verification of the surgery computations. [HU, KNV]
3. Kupers–Powell's Theorem A and Corollary B address realizing Whitehead torsion and upgrading an existing smooth h-cobordism under extra assumptions. An h-cobordism already supplies a homotopy equivalence. The result does not construct one from a bare isomorphism of quadratic 2-types. [KP]
4. Pavlov's August 2026 preprint classifies the infinite groups Z/p⋊Z after adding another form. Its polarized-classification generalization explicitly defers the finite-group case to Hambleton–Kreck. It does not supply a finite orientable counterexample or an unrestricted finite-group theorem. [P]

## Five approaches and stopping point

1. Extend the torsion-free criterion: it yields the known subclasses, with no argument for the remaining groups.
2. Promote nonzero torsion to inequivalence: blocked by the self-equivalence orbit calculation, including possible orientation reversal.
3. Realize two polarized candidates by manifolds: no pair of distinct ordinary-homotopy orbits with established manifold realizations was obtained.
4. Use quadratic bias to distinguish a fixed quadratic 2-type: Theorem 6.16 prevents this particular strategy.
5. Import surgery, stable-equivalence, or Whitehead-torsion distinctions: their hypotheses or target relations do not establish the required implication.

The remaining work is mathematical, not a missing administrative step: construct and separate two manifold-realizable candidates with the same full quadratic 2-type, or prove that no such separation is possible. Further repetition of the checked approaches would not remove this gap.
