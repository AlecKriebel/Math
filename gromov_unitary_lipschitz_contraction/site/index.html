Geometry · Gromov question [?24](i)

A Lipschitz-nonincreasing deformation of small unitary-valued maps

An explicit deformation to constant maps, with a Lipschitz bound at every step. The proof fits in four pages.

Alec Kriebel · ORCID · Version 1.1 · 23 September 2026

The result

One formula. Every dimension.

Fix a point p on the unit-round sphere. For a map Φ into U(N), write A = Φ(p) and W = A*Φ. When Lip(Φ) ≤ ½, set

Ht(Φ) = A (W + tI)(I + tW)−1.

The homotopy starts at Φ, ends at the constant Φ(p), and fixes every constant map. It depends continuously on the entire input map and remains in the original U(N).

Lip(Ht(Φ)) ≤ 1 − t21 + t2 Lip(Φ), 0 ≤ t ≤ 1.

Exact scope

The target uses either the unnormalized Hilbert–Schmidt Riemannian distance or the operator-norm length distance. Both strict and closed Lipschitz bounds are preserved.

This answers the literal threshold ½ question on page 36 of Gromov’s 2017 problem list, catalogued as AMR-066-0025.

The constant depends on Φ. The theorem does not contract U(N) itself to a single prescribed point.

The proof in three steps

01

Confine the spectrum

The diameter π and the bound ½ place every eigenvalue of the normalized image in the closed right semicircle.

02

Control the inverse

A positive matrix inequality bounds (I + tW)−1. An ordered resolvent identity gives the contraction factor without assuming the matrices commute.

03

Integrate along curves

Apply the derivative bound to the image of a spherical geodesic. This proves the intrinsic Lipschitz estimate, including for nonsmooth maps.

Check it yourself

Proof first. Small scripts alongside.

The analytic argument is self-contained apart from standard finite-dimensional spectral and curve-length facts. No numerical computation is required.

The package includes 10 exact algebra checks and 7,679 deterministic numerical checks, all passing. They cover noncommuting boundary matrices, both norms, endpoints, and failure outside the semicircle.

Context & priority

An elementary quantitative answer.

The Cayley and Möbius transformations are classical. A standard logarithm chart already gives contraction to constants when no Lipschitz bound is required during the deformation. The contribution presented here is the explicit quantitative deformation preserving the stated sublevels.

The audit compared the construction with earlier matrix-transform and unitary-group geometry literature. No earlier explicit resolution or exact quantitative retraction was located. The search does not exclude folklore, unindexed work, or results under other terminology.
