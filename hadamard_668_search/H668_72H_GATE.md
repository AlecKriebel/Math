# H(668) seventy-two-hour research gate

## Objective

The sprint asks whether the five exact shell-two profile orbits support a
credible route to an exact length-333 Legendre pair.  It does not treat a
single second-placement-digit witness as progress: after the first affine
digit leaves `F_3^36`, eighteen generic independent quadratic equations
would heuristically leave about

```text
3^(36-18) = 3^18 = 387,420,489
```

points per profile.

The finish line remains an explicit pair of binary sequences whose complete
periodic autocorrelations replay exactly, followed by the standard exact
construction and verification of a `668 x 668` Hadamard matrix.

## Result on 24 July 2026: construction gate failed

All required lanes were executed, but none of the four forms of evidence
listed below was obtained.

The strict construction gate therefore fails: there is no consecutive
higher-digit witness, no exact structured lift, no feasible complete exact
lift, and no whole-profile exclusion.

One secondary rate gate did succeed.  The compiled dense-shell
729-character kernel runs at a conservative 12,668,666 exact evaluations
per second per core, 718 times the four-week arithmetic threshold, with an
independent 1,458-character replay.  This makes an end-to-end
`n_9=1,0` **profile classifier** a technically credible scoped paper
project.  It does not make an exact phase lift feasible, and it does not
retroactively pass the `H(668)` construction gate.

The decision is:

1. stop headline search on the present five-profile placement lift;
2. preserve the five-orbit, structured-family, carry, and local-radius
   package as a paper project;
3. treat a dense-shell classifier as a separate bounded classification
   project, with its next gate requiring measured end-to-end skeleton
   generation, symmetry canonicalization, witness recovery, and exact
   replay—not merely the already-fast character kernel;
4. require a genuinely new construction principle before resuming the
   five-profile `H(668)` lift itself.

## Required lanes

1. Test at least three genuinely different structured placement families on
   all five exact profile orbits.  Families obtained only by adding a proper
   fixed common-multiplier supergroup do not count: the July 2026
   classification excludes every such supergroup of `<10>`.
2. Derive and attempt at least the two consecutive digits after the audited
   quadratic digit.  Every emitted point must be replayed through the exact
   Eisenstein coefficients, not accepted from a solver status.
3. Determine a rigorous finite exactness cutoff and estimate the complete
   remaining search, including row margins, all required digits or the
   equivalent prime-167 equations, symmetry, memory, and wall time.
4. Re-audit current literature and narrow every priority claim that overlaps
   public work.

## Evidence that passes the gate

At least one of the following is required:

- a profile placement that survives two consecutive higher digits, together
  with measured branching showing that exact replay is computationally
  reachable;
- an exact structured-family lift through every phase equation;
- a theorem that reduces the complete exact lift to a certified finite
  search feasible on the 16 GB host;
- a whole-profile exclusion whose proof covers every placement and row
  margin, not a bounded timeout.

A second-digit point, another low-rank pencil, a near miss, or an `UNKNOWN`
solver result does not pass.

## Stop condition

If none of the preceding evidence appears, stop spending headline research
tokens on the construction attempt.  Preserve the five-orbit classification,
shell descent, quadratic algebra, and priority audit as a scoped paper
project.  Any later restart should require a genuinely new construction
principle.

## Host limits

The machine has 16 GB physical RAM and ten CPU cores.  A single research job
may use several gigabytes when useful, but concurrent resident usage should
stay below 12 GB to leave operating-system and application headroom.  Avoid
large proof traces while free disk space is near 24 GB.  More hardware is not
currently the principal bottleneck; the gate is mathematical contraction.
