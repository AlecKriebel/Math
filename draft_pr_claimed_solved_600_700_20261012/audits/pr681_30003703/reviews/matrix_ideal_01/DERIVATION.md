## 2. Universal identity; a stronger independent proof

All coefficient rings in this paragraph are commutative and unital, but may have nilpotents, zero divisors, or positive characteristic. For an arbitrary 2×2 matrix X, put t=tr(X) and d=det(X). Cayley–Hamilton is X²=tX−dI. For U=[X,Y],

    XU+UX = X²Y−YX² = tU.

The same holds with V=[X,Z]. Since t is central,

    XUV = (tU−UX)V = tUV−U(tV−VX) = UVX.

Likewise XVU=VUX. Thus [[U,V],X]=0. This proves P(X,Y,Z)=[[[X,Y],[X,Z]],X]=0 for arbitrary 2×2 matrices, a harmless strengthening of the native traceless statement. No division by 2, eigenvalue argument, or density assumption is involved. Substitution Z=[X,Y] proves Q(X,Y)=[[[X,Y],[X,[X,Y]]],X]=0.

P is homogeneous of degree 5, while Q has weighted degree 6. In Q the substituted Z has degree 2. Treating this substitution as degree 1 would be incorrect. The stronger identity is not needed to repair anything: the native proof's traceless Cayley–Hamilton argument is correct.

The independent symbolic control computes P and Q in an ordinary polynomial ring over the integers on all independent matrix entries, without any truncation that could erase their terms. It also checks XU+UX=tU generically.

Commutativity of the coefficient ring matters. Reinterpret the 4×4 matrices X=diag(1,0,−1,0), Y=E12 and Z=E23 as 2×2 block matrices over M_2(Z). The block trace of X is zero, but P=−2E13 is nonzero. This does not affect Satoh's commutative function algebra; it prevents conflating the general associative filtered-ring lemma with a matrix identity over arbitrary noncommutative coefficients.

## 3. Exact filtered commutator calculation

Let I^r be a multiplicative descending filtration by two-sided ideals of a unital associative ring. For invertible U=1+u and V=1+v with u∈I^r and v∈I^s, r,s≥1, there is an exact identity

    [U,V]−1 = (uv−vu) U^(-1)V^(-1).

Indeed UV−VU=uv−vu and (VU)U^(-1)V^(-1)=1. Furthermore U^(-1)−1=−uU^(-1)∈I^r and similarly for V. Consequently the displacement lies in I^(r+s) and has leading class [u_r,v_s]; the error after replacing inverses by 1 has degree at least r+s+min(r,s), hence at least r+s+1. Replacing either input by a higher-degree error likewise affects only degree ≥r+s+1.

This argument uses actual ideal multiplication. It requires neither completeness, separation, a formal embedding, nor a converse theorem about orders of vanishing. It works for Mat_2(R) with I^r=Mat_2(J^r), whose matrix coefficients in the associated graded are in the commutative graded ring gr_J R. Nested application gives the displayed Lie polynomial with its correct bracketing and weights.

## 4. Actual membership in the target ideal

Let M_i be the tautological SL2 matrix and B_i=M_i−I. Every entry of B_i is in J, and det(I+B_i)=1 gives tr(B_i)=−det(B_i)∈J². Thus X_i, its degree-one class, is traceless over gr_J R. The written native argument is valid even without the stronger identity of section 2.

For w=[[[x1,x2],[x1,x3]],x1], the inner weights are 2 and 2; their commutator has weight 4 and the last has weight 5. The degree-five displacement is P(X1,X2,X3)=0. Every actual entry therefore belongs to J^6, by the definition of J^5/J^6.

For v=[[[x1,x2],[x1,[x1,x2]]],x1], the inner weights are 2 and 3, then 5, then 6. The degree-six displacement is Q(X1,X2)=0. Every actual entry belongs to J^7. The calculation treats all four entries and not just traces or conjugacy invariants.

A possible additional relation in R cannot invalidate an identity already proved in every commutative coefficient ring. In particular the proof does not require gr_J R to be a polynomial ring, the coordinate ring to be reduced, or the augmentation ideal to be a regular maximal ideal.

## 5. Independent finite universal quotient model

For each generator start with the determinant quotient

    S = Z[a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i]/(a_i+d_i+a_i d_i−b_i c_i),
    m=(all a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i).

In S/m^N, u_i=1+a_i has finite inverse sum_{j=0}^{N−1}(−a_i)^j. A different coordinate system from the native verifier is the Gauss chart

    M_i = [[u_i, u_i B_i],
           [u_i C_i, u_i B_i C_i + u_i^(-1)]].

Here a_i,B_i,C_i are independent commuting variables of order 1, and every monomial of total degree ≥N is zero. The determinant is exactly 1 in this finite ring. Conversely a_i=M11−1, B_i=M12/u_i, C_i=M21/u_i recover these coordinates; the determinant relation forces M22=u_i B_i C_i+u_i^(-1). All substitutions preserve the augmentation ideal and are inverse in the finite quotient. This is an isomorphism of finite rings, not a collection of numerical arcs or a limit in a completion.

Sending universal generator entries to their tautological functions gives S⊗Q→R, surjectively because all word entries are polynomial in generator entries and adjugate entries. The ideal m maps onto J. Therefore a universal finite quotient membership result yields the required actual membership in R without proving this surjection injective.

It does not import the authored or previous-audit scripts. Computing one degree beyond each required vanishing finds:

## 6. Remaining bridge to the stated counterexamples

The target ring is generated by entries of the n generator matrices: inverse matrices are adjugates, since determinants equal 1. A spare-generator transvection x_t↦x_t u fixing every other generator, where u avoids x_t, is a genuine automorphism, with the explicit inverse x_t↦x_t u^(-1). Its displacement in generator entries is M_t(M(u)−I). The J^6 and J^7 bounds therefore imply triviality on all of R modulo the same ideal powers, and consequently on J/J^6 and J/J^7. This proves D_n(5) membership with u=w,t=4, and D_n(6) membership with u=v,t=3. All higher-rank generators can remain fixed. Action-side inverse conventions leave a kernel unchanged.

The native independent free-group certificate is also checkable by hand: in free associative variables P has coefficient −1 at XXYXZ, because only −X(XY)(XZ) contributes. In Q put U=XY−YX and V=X²Y−2XYX+YX². The coefficient at XXXYXY is +1, uniquely from X(X²Y)(XY) in XVU. These nonzero integral coefficients, together with the elementary inclusion of Gamma_r under the Magnus map into 1+terms of degree ≥r, exclude w∈Gamma_6 and v∈Gamma_7. This uses neither an injectivity theorem nor a converse dimension-subgroup assertion. Hence the corresponding automorphisms are outside A_n(5) and A_n(6).
