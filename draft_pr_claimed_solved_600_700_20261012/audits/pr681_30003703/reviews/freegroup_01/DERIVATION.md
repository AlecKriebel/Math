## 2. Integral nonmembership reconstructed without a dimension-subgroup converse

Use `[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}`. Define

    w = [[[x1,x2],[x1,x3]],x1],
    v = [[[x1,x2],[x1,[x1,x2]]],x1].

The native proof's integral Magnus argument is valid: the universal property gives `E(x_i)=1+X_i` in the units of integral noncommutative formal series, and a direct filtered commutator calculation implies `E(Gamma_r) subset 1+T_{>=r}`. Only that implication is used. A nonzero term below degree `r` disproves membership in `Gamma_r`; injectivity of `E` and a converse dimension-subgroup theorem are unnecessary.

The leading polynomials have the advertised coefficients:

    coeff_11213([[[X1,X2],[X1,X3]],X1]) = -1,

For the first, the chosen monomial ends in `X3`, so it is absent from the term ending with `X1`; in the other term its only occurrence is `-X1*(X1 X2)*(X1 X3)`. For the second, write `U=X1 X2-X2 X1` and `V=X1^2 X2-2X1 X2 X1+X2 X1^2`; only `X1*V*U`, choosing the first monomial in both `V` and `U`, contributes to the indicated target. ### A distinct explicit integral nilpotent quotient certificate

For an even more direct independent test, let `U_d(Z)` be the upper unitriangular integer matrices of size `d`. Let `N_{>=r}` denote strictly upper triangular matrices supported at distance at least `r` above the diagonal. Products satisfy `N_{>=r}N_{>=s} subset N_{>=r+s}`. The exact commutator formula then gives

    [1+N_{>=r},1+N_{>=s}] subset 1+N_{>=r+s}.

Induction gives `Gamma_r(U_d(Z)) subset 1+N_{>=r}`, hence `Gamma_d(U_d(Z))=1`. Any free-group homomorphism into this group therefore annihilates `Gamma_d(F_n)`.

In size six assign

    x2 -> I + E34,

Consequently `w` is not in the integral group `Gamma_6(F_n)` in every rank containing these generators. This computation and the preceding distance-filtration proof constitute a separate nilpotent quotient certificate, rather than assuming any Magnus theorem.

## 3. Genuine automorphisms, both compositions and every rank

If `u` is a word in generators other than `x_t`, let `phi` fix the other generators and send `x_t` to `x_t u`. Let `psi` instead send `x_t` to `x_t u^{-1}`, fixing all other generators. Both maps fix `u`, since none of its letters moves. On `x_t`,

    psi(phi(x_t)) = x_t u^{-1} u = x_t,
    phi(psi(x_t)) = x_t u u^{-1} = x_t.

Both compositions fix every other free generator. By freeness, these are two-sided inverse endomorphisms, hence genuine automorphisms. This is not an inference from an invertible abelianization matrix or an inverse modulo a finite quotient.

For `w` choose `t=4`; this requires and proves all ranks `n>=4`. For `v` choose `t=3`; it proves all `n>=3`. Extra generators are fixed. The integral quotient certificates above still apply because extra free generators can be sent to the identity. Equivalently a free-factor retraction preserves all lower central terms. Thus there is no unstated stability assumption. The program checks both compositions at four ranks for each word, including 17, and deliberately confirms failure of the naive inverse when the moving generator is chosen to be `x1`, which occurs in both words. That control does not assert that every such other endomorphism is noninvertible; it rejects this proposed inverse.

For the actual automorphism, `x_t^{-1} phi(x_t)=u`. The exhibited nonmembership of `u` immediately disproves triviality on `F_n/Gamma_{k+1}` at `k=5` or `6`. This is exactly `phi notin A_n(k)`; no quotient of the automorphism group's own lower central series is involved.

## 4. Checking the full `D` action and the off-by-one bridge

I independently checked the short universal matrix argument, while the other mathematical review family supplies the main independent computational tests of the ideal bridge. Put `M_i=I+B_i`. The determinant identity implies `tr(B_i)=-det(B_i) in J^2`, so its degree-one class `X_i` is traceless. Cayley-Hamilton makes `X^2` scalar for a traceless 2-by-2 matrix. Both `[X,Y]` and `[X,Z]` anticommute with `X`, so their commutator commutes with `X`. Therefore the degree-five polynomial `P` vanishes. Substitution `Z=[X,Y]` gives the weighted degree-six polynomial `Q`; its substituted argument has degree two, not one.

The exact group-commutator displacement is

    (1+u)(1+v)(1+u)^{-1}(1+v)^{-1}-1
      = (uv-vu)(1+u)^{-1}(1+v)^{-1}.

If `u` and `v` are in ideal powers `r` and `s`, its leading class in power `r+s` is their associative bracket. This applies directly to matrix ideals over `R`; no completion, reducedness, smoothness or polynomial identification of `gr_J(R)` is needed. The zero leading classes imply actual membership

    M(w)-I in Mat_2(J^6),
    M(v)-I in Mat_2(J^7).

Now `R` is generated by the entries of the free-generator matrices: entries of products are polynomial in them, and inverse matrices are adjugates because determinant is one. If a substitution fixes these finite algebra generators modulo an ideal `I`, it fixes every polynomial modulo `I`. Explicitly,

    prod_j f_j^phi - prod_j f_j
      = sum_l (prod_{j<l} f_j^phi)(f_l^phi-f_l)(prod_{j>l} f_j).

Every summand is in `I`. Applying this with `I=J^{k+1}` establishes the identity on the entire algebra quotient, hence on every element of `J/J^{k+1}`. Invariance of `J` also follows by evaluating at the trivial representation, which precomposition by an automorphism fixes.

For the moving matrix, `M_t^phi-M_t=M_t(M(u)-I)`, so its entries belong to the required ideal; all other matrices are fixed exactly. Thus the two actual automorphisms lie in `D_n(5)` and `D_n(6)`, respectively. This is stronger than checking characters, traces, the first graded layer, or a few representations. The published primary action convention agrees directly; replacing the whole action by the inverse convention would also leave the kernel unchanged.
