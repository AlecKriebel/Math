## Universal identity without field assumptions

Use [g,h]=ghg^-1h^-1 and [U,V]=UV-VU. For arbitrary2x2 X over a commutative ring, Cayley–Hamilton says X²=tX-dI, where t=tr(X), d=det(X). Set U=[X,Y], V=[X,Z]. Then XU+UX=[X²,Y]=tU and XV+VX=tV. Therefore

    XUV=(tU-UX)V=tUV-U(tV-VX)=UVX,
    XVU=VUX.

Consequently P(X,Y,Z)=[[[X,Y],[X,Z]],X]=0. The cancellation is an integer polynomial calculation; it divides by no integer and requires no diagonalization, density, reducedness, smoothness or trace pairing. Q(X,Y)=P(X,Y,[X,Y]) also vanishes. It has weighted degree6, since the substituted third argument has degree2.

The coefficient ring is commutative: the function algebra R and gr_J(R) qualify. An arbitrary associative coefficient ring need not qualify. Matrix sizes greater than2 need not satisfy P. No whole filtration assertion in positive characteristic or for another matrix size is inferred from the universal identity.

The identity is classical. Burde1411.0950v2 Eq.(4), [z,[[z,x],[z,y]]]=0, is exactly -P after z=X,x=Y,y=Z. Theorem4.14 attributes its role in an sl2 identity basis to Razmyslov. ## Real automorphisms at every allowed rank

For u avoiding x_t, the maps fixing every other generator and taking x_t to x_tu and x_tu^-1 fix u. Their two compositions fix every generator, so they are inverse automorphisms. This is an identity in the discrete free group, not invertibility only on abelianization, a completed group or a finite quotient.

For w choose t=4, requiring n>=4. For v choose t=3, requiring n>=3. In larger rank leave all other generators fixed. The same explicit inverses work, while the displayed integral coefficients remain unchanged; alternatively retract to the smaller free factor. No stability theorem is required.

    M(phi_u(x_t))-M(x_t)=M(x_t)(M(u)-I).
