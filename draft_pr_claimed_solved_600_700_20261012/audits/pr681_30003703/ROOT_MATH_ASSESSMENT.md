## Universal algebra and ideal calculation

The group commutator convention is UVU^-1V^-1. For U=1+u and V=1+v the exact identity is [U,V]-1=(uv-vu)U^-1V^-1. Since U^-1-1=-uU^-1, in a multiplicative ideal filtration the leading class has degree r+s and equals the bracket of the leading classes. Terms from either inverse or any higher input class lie at least one degree higher. This requires neither convergence nor separation of the filtration.

For traceless2x2 X, Cayley–Hamilton gives X²=-det(X)I. Consequently X[X,Y]+[X,Y]X=0, and likewise for [X,Z]. Their two products each commute with X, so P(X,Y,Z)=[[[X,Y],[X,Z]],X]=0. The substitution Z=[X,Y] gives Q(X,Y) of weighted degree6. No diagonalization, density, division, or trace-pairing nondegeneracy is used.

A useful optional strengthening, independently noticed by the matrix family and checked by the parent, removes the traceless restriction entirely: with t=tr(X), Cayley–Hamilton gives XU+UX=tU and XV+VX=tV for U=[X,Y], V=[X,Z]. Hence XUV=tUV-U(tV-VX)=UVX, and similarly XVU=VUX. Thus P=0 for arbitrary2x2 matrices over any commutative coefficient ring. This is an algebraic simplification, not a novelty claim or an extension of the target to a different representation group. The native traceless proof is already correct and remains unchanged.

Write generator matrices M_i=I+B_i. Their entries B_i lie in J; det(I+B_i)=1 gives tr(B_i)=-det(B_i) in J². Thus the degree1 classes are traceless in the commutative associated graded ring. Applying the group-commutator lemma to w=[[[x1,x2],[x1,x3]],x1] of weight5 gives M(w)-I in Mat2(J5), with degree5 class P=0. By the definition of J5/J6 it belongs to Mat2(J6). The word v=[[[x1,x2],[x1,[x1,x2]]],x1] has weight6; its degree6 class Q vanishes, giving Mat2(J7). This is actual ideal membership, not pointwise vanishing on sample representations or just a completed-local assertion. No claim that gr_J R is freely polynomial is necessary.
