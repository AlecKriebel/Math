## Finite represented-algebra obstruction

Let (A,theta,i,p) be a general unital dilation with ip a conditional expectation. Let (E,xi,pi) be the GNS correspondence of p, and suppose C=pi(A) is finite dimensional. The embedding j=pi i of B is injective because p i=id. Since p(a)=<xi,pi(a)xi>, p factors through pi as a unital completely positive map pbar:C->B with pbar j=id and the B-bimodule property. Identify j(B) with B.

If every theta_t preserves ker pi, beta_t(pi(a))=pi(theta_t(a)) is a unital endomorphism semigroup on C. Assuming continuity of t->pi(theta_t(a)), finite dimensionality gives norm continuity of beta. For small epsilon, S=integral_0^epsilon beta_r dr is invertible since S/epsilon tends to id. Then beta_t S=integral_t^(t+epsilon) beta_r dr differentiates on the right at zero. Its derivative delta satisfies the Leibniz rule.

For a projection q in B, differentiating q^2=q gives q delta(q) q=0 and (1-q) delta(q)(1-q)=0. B-bimodularity of pbar yields q pbar(delta(q)) q=0 and (1-q) pbar(delta(q))(1-q)=0. Since pbar beta_t(q)=T_t(q), a semigroup with nonzero diagonal block in T'_0(q) contradicts kernel invariance. The symmetric bit-flip semigroup has T'_0(q)=diag(-1/2,+1/2), so qualifies. The amplitude-damping example may likewise qualify after checking orientation.

More generally, L=pbar delta|B obeys Leibniz: L(bc)=L(b)c+bL(c). Thus compression of an invariant finite represented-algebra semigroup has derivation generator, whereas irreversible Markov generators generally do not.

## Decision boundary

No earlier paper explicitly stating the exact OWR negative conclusion has yet been located. A short implication from an earlier theorem is stronger priority evidence than mere keyword absence. A final assessment must distinguish a known negative theorem from an unstated formulation-specific corollary, and evaluate whether an explicit M2/C2 example with exact positive-time witnesses remains a useful, honestly framed contribution. Publication remains on hold pending that assessment.
