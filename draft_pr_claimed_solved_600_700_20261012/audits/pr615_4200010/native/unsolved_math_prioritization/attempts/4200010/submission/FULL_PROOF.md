# A positive-volume non-almost-periodic Hamiltonian band with zero exponents

Problem 4200010 / AMR-041-0010; Knill's 2000 item 10.

Status: complete candidate proof, AI-assisted and unrefereed. Independent audit and historical priority review are pending. The construction uses classical skew-shift and suspension mechanisms; no first-discovery claim is made.

## 1. Claim and conventions

There is a connected compact smooth symplectic four-manifold `(M, omega)`, a globally defined autonomous `C-infinity` real Hamiltonian `H`, and an invariant open set `B` of normalized Liouville volume `1/4` such that:

1. Every Lyapunov exponent of the Hamiltonian flow at every point of `B` is zero.
2. No measurable invariant subset of `B` with positive Liouville measure has an almost-periodic Koopman representation.
3. Each connected energy component in `B`, with its conditional invariant volume, is ergodic and is not almost periodic.

Here a finite measure-preserving flow is almost periodic if, for every vector `f` in its complex `L2` space, `{f composed with Phi_s : s in R}` has relatively compact closure in `L2`. This is the usual discrete-spectrum / almost-periodic unitary-representation meaning. We prove noncompactness directly, so the spectral equivalence is not needed.

A 'good set' means an invariant measurable region on which the restricted Liouville system is almost periodic, rather than a factor generated by selected eigenfunctions. A 'bad set' has at least one positive Lyapunov exponent. Statements 1 and 2 imply that `B` is contained in the remaining region modulo a null set. Thus the remaining region has positive full four-dimensional Liouville measure, not merely positive hypersurface measure at one energy.

The source does not specify an ambient Euclidean cotangent space, analyticity, a kinetic-plus-potential Hamiltonian, or contact type. The result concerns its stated unrestricted smooth Hamiltonian formulation. Those additional restrictions are not asserted.

We use the convention `i_(X_H) omega = dH`. The opposite convention reverses time and leaves every conclusion unchanged.

## 2. The area-preserving skew shift

Let `a = sqrt(2)` and `T2 = R2/Z2`, with area form `sigma = dx wedge dy` and normalized Haar measure `m2`. Define

`F(x,y) = (x+a, y+x) mod Z2`.

This is well-defined because integer changes in `x,y` induce integer changes in both image coordinates. Its inverse is

`F^(-1)(x,y) = (x-a, y-x+a)`.

Its derivative is the constant determinant-one matrix

`A = [[1,0],[1,1]]`,

so `F* sigma = sigma`. For every integer `n`, induction, also applied to the inverse, gives

`F^n(x,y) = (x+n*a, y+n*x + a*n*(n-1)/2) mod Z2`,

and `D(F^n) = [[1,0],[n,1]]`.

### Ergodicity

The Fourier characters `e_(k,l)(x,y) = exp(2*pi*i*(k*x+l*y))`, `(k,l) in Z2`, form an orthonormal basis of `L2(T2,m2)`. The Koopman map is

`U_F e_(k,l) = exp(2*pi*i*k*a) e_(k+l,l)`.

If `u composed with F = u`, unitarity gives equal absolute values for the Fourier coefficients of `u` along the orbit

`..., (k-l,l), (k,l), (k+l,l), ...`.

When `l != 0` this orbit is infinite and has distinct indices, so square-summability forces all its coefficients to vanish. When `l=0`, invariance forces the `(k,0)` coefficient to vanish unless `exp(2*pi*i*k*a)=1`; irrationality of `a` implies `k=0`. Thus all invariant `L2` functions are constant, which is ergodicity.

### A direct obstruction to almost periodicity

Let `g(x,y)=exp(2*pi*i*y)`. Then

`g composed with F^n = exp(2*pi*i*(y+n*x+a*n*(n-1)/2))`.

For different integers `n,m`, integration in `x` gives

`<g composed with F^n, g composed with F^m> = 0`.

These vectors have norm one. Their pairwise distances are `sqrt(2)`, so this orbit is not relatively compact. This also emphasizes that ergodicity alone was not used as a substitute for failure of almost periodicity.

## 3. A compact symplectic suspension

Define the mapping torus

`N = (T2 x R)/((z,t) ~ (F(z),t-1))`.

It is a connected compact smooth three-manifold. The gluing action is free and properly discontinuous, because its integer translates shift the real `t` coordinate by integers. A fundamental domain is `T2 x [0,1)`. The forms `sigma` and `dt`, pulled back from the factors, are invariant under this action and descend to `N`. Write the descended two-form as `beta` and the descended one-form as `tau`. Both are closed, and `beta wedge tau` is a volume form with total volume one.

The flow

`psi_s[z,t] = [z,t+s]`

is smooth, complete, preserves this volume, and has first-return map `F` on `t=0`. For integer `n` its fundamental-domain formula is

`psi_n[z,t] = [F^n(z),t]`.

Take a second circle `S1_E = R/Z` and let

`M = N x S1_E`,

`omega = beta + tau wedge dE`.

This is a globally defined closed two-form. Moreover

`omega wedge omega / 2 = beta wedge tau wedge dE`

is a nowhere-vanishing positive volume form. Thus `omega` is symplectic, and the total Liouville volume of `M` is one. No primitive of `omega` and no global real-valued lift of the circle coordinate `E` is claimed or needed.

## 4. A genuinely real-valued global Hamiltonian

To avoid treating a circle coordinate as a real Hamiltonian, choose a smooth cutoff explicitly. Set

`eta(r)=0` for `r<=0`, and `eta(r)=exp(-1/r)` for `r>0`;

`q(r)=eta(r)/(eta(r)+eta(1-r))`.

Then `q=0` on `(-infinity,0]`, `q=1` on `[1,infinity)`, and `q` is smooth. On the representative interval `0<=E<1`, put

`chi(E)=q(16*(E-1/16)) * q(16*(7/16-E))`,

`h(E)=E*chi(E)`.

This function is zero on neighborhoods of both ends of `[0,1]`, so it extends to a globally smooth real-valued function on `S1_E`. On

`I=(1/8,3/8)`

we have `chi=1` and `h(E)=E`, hence `h'(E)=1`.

Define `H([z,t],E)=h(E)`. Since

`i_(partial_t) omega = dE`,

its Hamiltonian vector field is

`X_H = h'(E) partial_t`.

The invariant open band `B=N x I` therefore has flow

`Phi_s(q,E)=(psi_s(q),E)`.

Its Liouville volume is exactly `|I|=1/4`. Completeness on all of `M` follows from smoothness and compactness. On `B`, `dH=dE` never vanishes, so every energy there is regular locally, and each `N x {E}` is a compact regular energy component. Other components elsewhere in `M` at the same energy do not affect these statements.

## 5. Exact zero Lyapunov exponents in full phase space

The following frame on the covering space `T2 x R x S1_E` descends to a global smooth frame on `M`:

`V1 = partial_x - t partial_y`,
`V2 = partial_y`,
`V3 = partial_t`,
`V4 = partial_E`.

Indeed, the derivative of the gluing map `(z,t,E) -> (F(z),t-1,E)` sends `V1(t)` to `partial_x+(1-t)partial_y=V1(t-1)` and fixes `V2,V3,V4`. The frame is also invariant under integer translations in `x,y` and `E`.

Equip `M` with the smooth Riemannian metric for which this frame is orthonormal. On `B`, the exact derivative formula is

`D(Phi_s) V1 = V1 + s V2`,
`D(Phi_s) Vj = Vj` for `j=2,3,4`,

where the right-hand frame is evaluated at the image point. It follows that

`||D(Phi_s)|| <= 1+|s|`,
`||D(Phi_s)^(-1)|| <= 1+|s|`.

For every nonzero tangent vector `v` at a point of `B`,

`||v||/(1+|s|) <= ||D(Phi_s)v|| <= (1+|s|)||v||`.

Taking logarithms, dividing by `|s|`, and letting `|s|` tend to infinity proves that every Lyapunov exponent is zero. This controls all four tangent directions, including the transverse energy direction, and is not merely a return-map or tangential calculation. On a compact manifold every smooth Riemannian metric is uniformly equivalent, so the conclusion is metric-independent.

## 6. Ergodicity of the suspension energy components

Let `v` be an `L2` function on `N` invariant under every `psi_s`. In a fundamental-domain chart, invariance under sufficiently small translations in `t` implies that `v(z,t)=w(z)` almost everywhere. One rigorous formulation uses the vanishing distributional derivative in `t`: strong continuity of translations implies invariance for every real parameter from invariance on the rationals, and a locally integrable function with zero distributional derivative in an interval is constant almost everywhere on that interval. The mapping-torus seam then forces `w(Fz)=w(z)` almost everywhere. By the ergodicity of `F`, `w` is constant.

Thus the normalized volume `nu=beta wedge tau` on `N` is ergodic for `psi`. Its conditional measure on `N x {E}` is exactly the normalized Liouville disintegration in the coordinates above. Each such energy component is consequently ergodic.

It is not almost periodic: take the measurable fundamental-domain function

`G([x,y,t])=exp(2*pi*i*y)`, `0<=t<1`.

There is no requirement that an `L2` observable match values across the measure-zero seam. Its integer translates under `psi` are orthonormal, by the formula in Section 2 and integration in `t`. This proves assertion 3.

## 7. Excluding every positive-volume almost-periodic region

Let `C` be any measurable invariant subset of `B`, with invariance understood modulo Liouville-null sets, and suppose `mu(C)>0`. We first justify the form of such a set, rather than assuming that an arbitrary invariant subset contains whole energy components.

For each rational `s`, the invariance equation for the indicator of `C` holds in `L2(N x I)`. Fubini and a countable intersection give a full-measure set of `E` for which the section `1_C(.,E)` is invariant under every rational-time `psi_s`. Strong continuity of the Koopman group on `L2(N,nu)` extends this invariance to all real times. Ergodicity of `psi` then makes `1_C(.,E)` constant, either zero or one, for almost every `E`.

The function `E -> nu(C_E)` is measurable. Therefore, for some measurable `J subset I`,

`C = N x J` modulo null sets,

and `mu(C)=Leb(J)>0`.

Restrict the function `G` of Section 6 to `C` and normalize it by `mu(C)^(-1/2)`. At distinct integer times its translates are orthogonal in `L2(C,mu|C)`:

`<G composed with Phi_n, G composed with Phi_m>_(L2(C)) = mu(C)*delta_(n,m)`.

The normalized translates all have norm one and pairwise distance `sqrt(2)`. Their orbit is not precompact. Consequently, the restricted flow on `C` is not almost periodic. Since `C` was arbitrary, assertion 2 follows.

Finally, if an almost-periodic invariant region of all of `M` met `B` in positive measure, its intersection with `B` would itself be an almost-periodic invariant region: restriction to an invariant measurable subset is an intertwining orthogonal projection on `L2`, which carries relatively compact orbits to relatively compact orbits. This contradicts the preceding paragraph. Thus every good region meets `B` only in a null set. Since the bad set has empty intersection with `B` by Section 5, the complement of good and bad has measure at least `1/4`. QED.

## 8. Scope, nonclaims, and provenance

- The construction is autonomous, smooth, compact, and symplectic. It does not rely on a merely area-preserving map being Hamiltonian on its original torus.
- The Hamiltonian is globally real-valued and periodic in its circle argument; writing `H=E` on the entire circle would have been invalid.
- The positive-volume assertion is a four-dimensional band and is not a zero-volume isolated energy example.
- The suspension has nonconstant eigenfunctions such as `exp(2*pi*i*t)` and hence is not weakly mixing. This does not make its whole Koopman representation almost periodic: the orthogonal orbit above disproves that. Weak mixing is a guess in the source, not a requirement in its existential question.
- The per-energy-component conclusion also avoids the ambiguity that a continuum of integrable frequencies may produce continuous spectrum after averaging energies. The components here already fail almost periodicity.
- No assertion is made about genericity, robustness, analyticity, standard cotangent symplectic forms, contact-type energies, or natural mechanical Hamiltonians.
- The explicit skew shift, its Fourier calculation, and the mapping-torus construction are classical mechanisms. The packet establishes an elementary consequence for the stated target; it does not certify historical novelty or that the author intended an unstated restricted class.
