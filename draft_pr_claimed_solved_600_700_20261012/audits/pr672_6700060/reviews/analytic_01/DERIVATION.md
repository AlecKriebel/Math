# Independent analytic derivation for PR 672 / 6700060

This review concerns proof quality, not priority or the repository's final disposition. The frozen head is `13d9b2d944ce8ec121381a4bc21e48d2bb19846e`. The controlling README restricts the application to ambient-smooth, face-preserving maps with ambient-smooth inverse and nonsingular differential through corners. Those restrictions remain in force.

I independently retrieved and read the complete eighteen-page proof of [Bi, arXiv:2608.06320v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06320v1), and the relevant Section 2 definitions, energy estimates and proofs of Propositions 2.14–2.15 in [Brendle, arXiv:2301.05087v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.05087v4). The exposition below records my own checks of the implication; it is not a replacement for general smooth elliptic theory.

## 1. What must actually be established

For the needed application, it is enough to show the following statement. Let P be a compact full-dimensional convex polytope, let g be a smooth positive-definite neighborhood metric, and assume nonnegative scalar curvature, nonnegative outward mean curvature on every facet, and no larger interior dihedral angles at ridges. Then the g and reference normal inner products agree on every closed intersection of two facets. Flatness alone would be insufficient, because a Euclidean competitor already gives a flat pullback metric.

The proof first treats odd n>=3. It obtains a nonzero parallel section A of Hom(Delta,S) and the boundary relations c(nu_a)A=A omega(N_a). It then recovers the required scalar inner products from Clifford anticommutators. Even n is handled by an interval product. The construction uses ordinary smooth boundaries throughout the existence and energy steps; it does not require a limiting corner Dirac operator to be Fredholm.

## 2. Smoothing without hidden simplicity

Use irredundant affine inequalities u_a<=0 with Euclidean unit normal covectors du_a. Pick p inside P, put d0=min(-u_a(p))>0, and bound |x-p| by R. If an index is active at scale lambda, u_a(x)>-2/lambda, then for large lambda

    du_a(x-p)>=d0/2.

Consequently, for nonnegative t_a supported on the active set,

    |sum t_a du_a| >= (d0/(2R)) sum t_a.

The estimate transfers to the g-covector norm by fixed metric equivalence. In particular, active positive combinations do not cancel. It is a point-dependent supporting-direction estimate, not an assumption that all facet normals belong to one fixed hemisphere.

For a smooth convex cutoff Phi flat below -2 and increasing thereafter, normalized by Phi(0)=1, define F_lambda=sum Phi(lambda u_a). The sublevel P_lambda={F_lambda<=1} is convex, contained in P, and contains a fixed interior ball. On the boundary, the finite vector of cutoff values ranges in a compact set with sum one. Its derivative sum has a positive minimum. The preceding noncancellation therefore gives |dF_lambda|_g>=c lambda. The level boundary is smooth, including transitions to its flat facet patches.

The claimed O(lambda^-1) approximation also follows quantitatively: for boundary y, moving toward p by A/lambda makes every affine defining function at most -A d0/lambda. With A fixed sufficiently large, that point is in the inner sublevel. The radial intersection lies between it and y. Convex bodies between fixed concentric inner and outer balls have uniformly bi-Lipschitz radial parametrizations: their gauges are uniformly Lipschitz and bounded away from zero on the sphere. These facts provide the uniform Lipschitz geometry used later, rather than an unsupported uniform curvature bound on the smoothed boundaries.

The linear face-distance error bound is justified by normal cones. For projection y of x in P onto a nonempty intersection face F_I, v=x-y belongs to the normal cone of F_I at y. That cone is generated by active inequality normals outside I and the span of the equality normals in I. If there were unit such v with all equality evaluations approaching zero, a fixed face type and a convergent subsequence would give w with

    w=sum mu_j du_j + sum ell_a du_a,
    mu_j>=0, du_j(w)<=0, du_a(w)=0.

Pairing with w yields |w|^2<=0. Finitely many active-set types give a uniform contradiction and hence dist(x,F_I)<=C max_I|u_a(x)|. This argument does not require all active defining covectors to be independent.

Let E be the union of pair intersections and K the union of triple intersections. A ridge of an irredundant full-dimensional convex polytope is contained in precisely two facets: a transverse pointed two-dimensional cone has exactly two boundary rays. Therefore every nonempty triple intersection has ambient codimension at least three. At a nonsimple vertex many facets may meet, but this assertion still holds. An added redundant inequality could invalidate it. My exact diagnostics include the nonsimple octahedron (four incident facets at each vertex) and the adverse cube inequalities x<=1, y<=1, x+y<=2, whose triple intersection has codimension two because the last inequality is redundant.

If several cutoffs are active, compactness rules out an empty common intersection for large lambda. The face-distance bound places their common face within O(lambda^-1). Thus outside an O(lambda^-1) neighborhood of K at most two are active. On each fixed compact facet-interior patch, precisely its own cutoff remains, and the smoothed boundary eventually equals the facet exactly.

## 3. The two-face estimate has the correct leading coefficient

At a two-active-facet point away from K, write L=ker du_a intersect ker du_b, and choose the g-unit e perpendicular to L in the smoothed tangent space, directed toward increasing u_b. Expanding the level-set Hessian gives

    II_lambda = kappa e* tensor e* + B_lambda,
    kappa>=0, kappa<=C lambda, |B_lambda|<=C.

The rank-one form matters. Hence H_lambda=kappa+O(1), while the normal derivatives in L directions are O(1). Bounding all derivative columns by O(lambda) would lose the favorable coefficient and would not suffice.

The affine projection x_ab to the ridge plane lies in the actual ridge away from K. If it were outside, the closest ridge point would belong to its relative boundary, a subset of K, contradicting the separation. Transporting the two ridge normals to x changes the current endpoint normals by O(lambda^-1). Their derivatives are bounded. The cutoff weights have first derivatives O(lambda), so differentiating the changed weighted normal costs O(1), not O(lambda). The normalized weighted sums have a fixed positive denominator by noncancellation.

Let alpha be the transported g-normal angle and let s measure the spherical position of the transported rounded normal. The two defining covectors are independent. Metric positivity and compactness keep alpha uniformly away from zero and pi. Differentiation gives ds(e)=kappa+O(1), with ds restricted to L bounded and d alpha bounded. The reference normal arc has length alpha0. Interior-angle nonincrease is exactly alpha>=alpha0. Sending fractional arclength s/alpha to that reference arc therefore multiplies the leading derivative by alpha0/alpha<=1. The resulting map zeta obeys

    ||d zeta||_tr <= H_lambda+C.

The one-active-facet case has d zeta=0 and nonnegative facet mean curvature. The transition where a cutoff derivative vanishes is smooth: near an endpoint the arc coordinate is an arctangent of beta_b sin(alpha)/(beta_a+beta_b cos(alpha)), with positive denominator. There is no use of an arccos derivative at its singular endpoint.

## 4. The higher-corner replacement preserves both degree and coefficient

Put q=(x-p)/|x-p|. For every active reference normal, q dot N_a has a fixed positive lower bound by the original noncancellation estimate. The reference arc point is a positive sine combination of its endpoints whose coefficients sum to at least one. It follows that q dot zeta>=c>0.

With a regularized distance r comparable to distance to K and bounded derivative, use delta=lambda^-1/4 and a cutoff transitioning from r=delta^2 to r=delta, with derivative bounded by C/(r|log delta|). Interpolate from q to zeta on the minor sphere geodesic. Because q dot zeta>=c, all triples used lie in a compact subset away from antipodal pairs. The homotopy is explicit and begins at the radial degree-one map q.

For fixed q and transition parameter t, the polar-coordinate differential of sphere contraction is t in the radial direction and sin(t theta)/sin(theta) in angular directions. The verified hemisphere bound theta<pi/2 makes both at most one. Thus this interpolation does not amplify the large derivative of zeta. Differentiating q contributes O(1); differentiating the transition contributes the logarithmic weight. A global sphere contraction assertion without the hemisphere restriction would be false, but that broader assertion is not used.

The cutoff's inner radius delta^2 greatly exceeds the geometric error lambda^-1=delta^4. Therefore zeta is only evaluated where its two-face derivative estimate is valid. The resulting eta has degree one and agrees exactly with each reference facet normal on every eventual fixed facet patch.

## 5. The small-form estimate really prevents concentration

The Hessian expansion and noncancellation imply H_lambda>=-C everywhere. Combining this with the preceding construction bounds the positive boundary defect W_lambda=(||d eta||_tr-H_lambda)_+ by a finite sum of: a bounded ridge strip of width O(lambda^-1), a bounded K-neighborhood of width O(delta), and

    1_{delta^2<r<delta}/(r|log delta|).

Uniform radial Lipschitz maps give uniform W^(1,2) extension operators. For scalar norms, Kato plus Poincare with a fixed interior ball B and the boundary trace theorem control both the L2 interior norm and the boundary Lq norm by gradient energy plus mass on B, with q=2(n-1)/(n-2). The ridge strip has surface measure O(lambda^-1). Holder gives precisely the small coefficient lambda^-1/(n-1).

For the K term, pull back radially to the fixed boundary. The O(delta^4) displacement is negligible relative to the inner annulus radius delta^2. Distances and surface Jacobians change by fixed factors. A nearest triple-intersection face sufficiently close to the current facet must intersect that facet, since disjoint compact faces have positive distance. Applying the face-distance bound to their intersection reduces the weight to a finite sum of weights for triple intersections incident with this facet. This avoids a false assertion that every nearest corner face already lies in the facet.

Choose coordinates (y,z,t) in R^(n-3) x R^2 x R with the facet in t=0 and the incident face G in z=t=0. This is valid even when G has dimension less than n-3. Put a(y)=dist((y,0,0),G). Then the exact distance identity is

    dist((y,z,0),G)^2=a(y)^2+|z|^2.

For b=1_{c delta^2<sqrt(a^2+|z|^2)<C delta}/(sqrt(a^2+|z|^2)|log delta|), polar integration with s=sqrt(a^2+rho^2) gives, uniformly in a>=0,

    integral_R2 b^2 dz <= 2pi/|log delta|^2 integral_{c delta^2}^{C delta} ds/s
                        <= C'/|log delta|.

The bounded neighborhood indicator has L2_z norm O(delta). For almost every fixed y, the three-dimensional Sobolev trace estimate controls the squared L4 norm on its z-plane by the W^(1,2) norm of the (z,t) slice. Holder in z and integration in y give the desired coefficient delta+|log delta|^-1/2 multiplying the full W^(1,2) norm. The compatibility of this sliced trace with the full trace follows first for smooth functions and then by density. It is not a pointwise smoothness assumption on a general Sobolev section.

Thus

    integral_boundary W_lambda |psi|^2
      <= epsilon_lambda (integral_Plambda |nabla psi|^2 + integral_B |psi|^2),
    epsilon_lambda=C(lambda^-1/(n-1)+delta+|log delta|^-1/2) -> 0.

For n=3 there is no y-variable and the same trace estimate applies directly. No n-dimensional endpoint exponent is incorrectly substituted for the three-dimensional slice exponent.

Two hostile scaling controls show why the actual construction is essential. An ordinary transition of derivative size 1/delta on the transverse annulus delta/2<|z|<delta has squared L2 norm 3pi/4, independent of delta. A critically rescaled test function in three dimensions has bounded gradient energy but a nonvanishing boundary contribution to this weight. Also, if K had ambient codimension two, the one-dimensional transverse logarithmic squared integral would be 2(delta^-2-delta^-1)/|log delta|^2, which diverges. The supplied proof uses the logarithmic scale and genuine codimension three and avoids both failures.

## 6. Smooth-boundary existence, including the signs

For odd n choose matching irreducible Clifford modules with the same normalized volume convention. On Hom(Delta,S), let chi(A)=-c(nu)A omega(eta). It is self-adjoint and squares to one. The intended plus condition is equivalent to c(nu)A=A omega(eta). In the flat case eta=nu, the identity homomorphism satisfies the plus condition, fixing the convention.

For nonzero tangential xi, the inward decaying symbol space is the positive eigenspace of L=i c(nu)c(xi). L is self-adjoint, L^2=|xi|^2, and L anticommutes with chi. Thus the decaying space and the prescribed negative-chi target both have half the dimension; the decaying space has zero intersection with the positive-chi space. The boundary symbol is bijective. Brendle Proposition 2.14 then invokes ordinary smooth elliptic boundary theory to obtain the Fredholm map, regularity and smooth cokernel relations. This is an imported established analytic foundation, not a finite matrix calculation masquerading as a PDE proof.

At the flat Gauss-map base point, the identity gives a nonzero kernel. Green's formula sends a cokernel relation to a harmonic section with the minus condition. Normal Clifford multiplication commutes with chi, so no sign flip is lost in this deduction. Convexity gives H=||d nu||_tr, and the boundary energy estimate with eta replaced by -eta makes that harmonic section parallel. Its constant matrix anticommutes with every normal Clifford generator; the Gauss map is surjective even without strict convexity. In odd dimension the product of all generators is a nonzero scalar, and anticommutation with each would imply anticommutation with that scalar. Hence the matrix vanishes. The cokernel is zero and the index is positive.

For general g and a sphere map homotopic to the Gauss map, interpolate the metric through positive metrics and the map through its homotopy. Curvature signs need not hold along this index homotopy. The boundary target bundles vary smoothly and can be identified with a fixed bundle by transport of their smooth projections. Fredholm index invariance applies. Each smooth convex P_lambda has sphere boundary, and eta_lambda has degree one, hence is homotopic to its Gauss map. This gives the nonzero harmonic A_lambda required by the smoothing argument.

My exact controls check the boundary involution and symbol with three distinct rational sphere vectors, the flat identity sign, and the normal/chi commutation. Separate dimension 3, 4 and 5 controls compute the full anticommutant: it is zero in odd dimensions and one-dimensional in dimension four. The even-dimensional countercontrol confirms that one cannot silently use the odd-dimensional cokernel proof there.

## 7. Energy, normalization and closed-corner passage

Differentiating chi against the boundary Dirac operator gives -sum c(e_j)A omega(d eta(e_j)); derivatives of nu cancel the two H/2 terms. Singular-vector bases for d eta and unitary Clifford multiplication bound this term by ||d eta||_tr |A|^2. Integrated Schrodinger–Lichnerowicz therefore yields

    integral |nabla A_lambda|^2 +(1/4) integral R|A_lambda|^2
       <= (1/2) integral_boundary W_lambda |A_lambda|^2.

Normalize integral_Plambda |A_lambda|^2=1. For the fixed ball B its mass is at most one. With R>=0, the trace estimate absorbs the gradient term and gives E_lambda<=epsilon_lambda/(2-epsilon_lambda)->0.

Uniform extensions to a fixed bounded smooth neighborhood are bounded in H1. Rellich supplies strong L2 convergence as well as weak H1 convergence. Each compact subset of the interior eventually belongs to P_lambda. The limiting section is parallel there. Crucially, normalization is preserved because

    |integral_Plambda |E_lambda A_lambda|^2 - integral_P |A|^2|
      <= || |E_lambda A_lambda|^2-|A|^2 ||_L1 + integral_{P\Plambda}|A|^2 ->0.

The first term follows from strong L2 convergence and bounded L2 norms; the second from absolute continuity and interior exhaustion. Weak convergence alone would not justify this step, but the proof has exactly the stronger convergence required.

On a fixed compact facet-interior patch, both the boundary and eta equal their fixed facet counterparts for all large lambda. The extensions' traces there are genuine traces of A_lambda. Compactness of the fixed local H1-to-L2 trace map passes the boundary relation to A. This avoids a convergence claim on moving traces through the corner layer.

Choose a small convex neighborhood of P within the metric domain. Transport A(p) along straight segments from p. Smooth dependence of the transport ODE gives a smooth extension. Inside P this agrees with the parallel section; outside P it need not be parallel. Continuity then extends both parallelness on P and the facet relations to closed facets. The section has a positive constant norm on the connected interior and hence a nonzero value at every boundary point.

At a closed facet intersection, composing both boundary relations and adding the reverse order gives

    -2<nu_a,nu_b>_g A=-2<N_a,N_b>A.

A is nonzero there, so the scalar factors agree. This cancellation requires neither an unproved invertibility assertion nor a restriction to relative ridge interiors. The stronger source proof also establishes invertibility: A* A is constant and commutes with all facet Clifford generators; the normals span R^n, so irreducibility gives A* A=tI with t>0. The finite bivector controls are consistent with the faithful spin Lie-algebra representation used for the source's additional flatness conclusion.

## 8. Dimension and category boundaries

For even n>=4 take P x [-1,1] with g+dt^2. This is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope in odd dimension with a smooth neighborhood metric. Scalar curvature is unchanged. Side facets gain one zero principal curvature, so their mean-curvature traces are unchanged. End facets are totally geodesic. New side/end normal products are zero for both metrics, hence their angles are pi/2. Old comparisons persist. The odd-dimensional angle conclusion restricted to the middle slice gives the needed even-dimensional result.

The separate planar turning argument in the packet is consistent: with a counterclockwise Jordan boundary, the signed curvature equals outward-normal mean curvature in the specified positive-circle convention. The exterior turning sum of the reference polygon is 2pi. Nonincrease of interior angles increases each corner turning, while nonnegative smooth-arc curvature adds a nonnegative integral. The turning theorem forces all angle inequalities to be equalities. Dimension one has no relevant codimension-two angle.

No step above supplies a smooth positive-definite pullback from a map with degenerate corner differential. Such a map is outside the controlling category. Likewise this review does not certify Bi's journal status, current community acceptance, priority, or a novel theorem of the PR author. These are separate questions. Within the stated analytic route, I found no unresolved mathematical gap.
