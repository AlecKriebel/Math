## Independent reconstruction and falsification attempts

The assumptions are finite, distinct, linear central hyperplanes over a characteristic-zero field, a free logarithmic derivation module, and nonzero coefficient-degree exponents in `{1,2}` with at most one `3`. Success means a maximal chain of modular elements in the complete intersection lattice. A finite enumeration alone would not meet that criterion.

1. **External mathematical premises.** Terao factorization supplies `n=sum(d_i)` and `b_2=sum_{i<j}d_i d_j`. Rank-two Möbius values are `m-1`. The formality premise is generation of the linear relation kernel by supported rank-two relation spaces, not topological formality. DiPasquale's arXiv v1 Corollary 4.10, read directly, gives total formality for free multiarrangements and thus the required ordinary-arrangement implication. Möller–Mücksch–Röhrle also identify the original freeness-to-formality result as Yuzvinsky Corollary 2.5. These premises are established inputs; the paper does not pretend to prove them anew. Direct retrieval of the original Terao/Yuzvinsky DOI pages through the web reader failed, a source-access limitation rather than a newly discovered premise problem.

2. **Rank and product reductions.** Essentialization removes the common center and zero exponents while preserving the lattice. Empty/rank-zero, rank-one, and rank-two lattices satisfy the conclusion. Product factors are free with union of exponent multisets; combining their modular chains gives a maximal modular chain of the product. In an essential irreducible factor, choose independent normals as coordinates. Tangency to those coordinate hyperplanes forces every degree-one derivation to be diagonal. Every other normal must be an eigenvector; different eigenvalues split all normals into direct-sum coordinate blocks, producing a product. Therefore degree one consists only of the Euler line. Essentiality excludes degree-zero derivations, so free-basis grading indeed gives exactly one exponent `1`. Neither algebraic closure nor a real/complex embedding is used: the diagonal eigenvalues already lie in the ground field.

3. **Multiplicity bound.** For a rank-two pencil written `x,y,x+c_i y`, the `c_i` are distinct nonzero ground-field scalars. Tangency gives `(x+c_i y)|(P-Q)` for each degree-at-most-`d` derivation with first coefficients `xP,yQ`; `x` is coprime to every such factor. If `m>d+1`, the distinct-factor product has degree larger than `P-Q`, so `P=Q`. Applying this to *every* free-basis column makes its first two rows proportional over the fraction field, contradicting full derivation-module rank. The argument works in the ambient polynomial ring, including additional coordinates. It neither assumes the localization exponent list nor invokes the failed withdrawn factor-size assertion.

4. **Connected incidence graph.** Rich lines are complete represented rank-two flats with at least three elements. Distinct ones meet in at most one element because normals represent distinct projective points. If the element/rich-line incidence graph were disconnected, formality would split the relation kernel by components. A relation connecting the component spans then decomposes into supported relations inside each component, forcing each component's vector sum to vanish separately. The component spans are in direct sum; irreducibility rules this out. This includes isolated element vertices, which would split off rank-one factors. The graph's connectivity is proved, not assumed from a finite test.

5. **Exact counts.** Each pair of normals belongs to exactly one rank-two flat, hence `C(n,2)-b_2=sum_L C(|L|-1,2)`. For exponents `(1,2,...,2)`, this gives `n=2r-1`, `b_2=2(r-1)^2`, `u=r-1`, `v=0`, and graph cycle dimension zero. For `(1,2,...,2,3)`, it gives `n=2r`, `b_2=2r^2-2r-1`, `u+3v=r+1`, and graph cycle dimension `3-3v`. Formality adds `r<=u+2v=r+1-v`, so `v<=1`. The quadruple-line case and the all-2 case are trees; the remaining triple-only case has cycle dimension exactly three. All algebraic signs and off-by-one constants check.

6. **Forced minimal-dependency core.** In the triple-only case, `r+1` local one-dimensional relation generators span a relation kernel of dimension `r`; a minimal dependent subfamily exists. Its dependence coefficients and its triple coordinates are nonzero. Thus every support element occurs at least twice. The induced incidence subgraph is connected, since otherwise the dependence splits into smaller dependencies. With `t` triple vertices and `s` support elements, `s<=3t/2` and `beta=2t-s+1<=3`, giving `t<=4`. Every triple needs three distinct other triples to cover its three coordinates, because any other rich line meets it at most once, giving `t>=4`. Equality forces `t=4,s=6`, degree two at every support element, and one distinct intersection for each pair of triples. This is precisely the incidence subdivision of `K_4`. Minimal dependence also gives relation rank exactly three. No numerical six-point search or rank-three classification is needed for this deduction.

7. **Exhausted cycle space and extra lines.** The connected core already has cycle dimension three. Contracting it preserves the remaining cycle dimension, including loops/parallel edges, and yields a connected multigraph of cycle dimension zero: a tree. Consequently any outside component attaches once. Core triple vertices are saturated because all rich lines here have exactly three elements, so attachment is only at core element vertices. A noncore rich line containing two core elements would create parallel edges after contraction and an additional cycle; it is impossible. The added explicit sentence in the final manuscript correctly closes the needed inference that the retained core has exactly the four indicated rich lines. Deleting normals does not create a collinear triple previously absent.

8. **Terminal-pencil lemma.** With `L={a,p_1,...,p_(m-1)}` and each removed `p_i` belonging to no other rich line, all other local relation spaces are supported on `E'`. A relation in `R_L` supported on `E'` would be supported on the single nonzero normal `a`, hence is zero. Thus `R=R_L direct_sum S`, and restricting yields `R'=S`. Every other rich line remains intact; the pencil reduces to one element, so this proves deletion formality. The relation dimension decreases by `m-2`, the element count by `m-1`, and the normal rank by one. With `U=span(L)` and `W=span(E')`, dimension counting gives `U intersect W=K a`; no removed normal lies in `W`, so it is an exact represented flat/coatom.

9. **Modularity and chain lifting.** For any flat generated by normals, zero or one outside generator has intersection with `W` spanned by the inside generators. At least two outside generators span `U`, and the intersection is the inside span plus `K a`. Distinctness of the normals is essential here and is stated. This is the represented-intersection characterization of modularity: the matroid meet realizes the full vector-space intersection dimension. A flat modular within `W` is also a global flat (closure cannot escape `W`), and intersecting it with the represented `W intersect F` establishes global modularity. Appending the full span therefore lifts a maximal modular chain. The induction never assumes arbitrary deletion preserves freeness or formality; only the directly proved terminal-pencil deletion is used.

10. **Termination and rank-three base.** Root the tree at a triple or at the unique quadruple line and remove farthest nonroot triple lines, whose outward element vertices are leaves. Repeat to a rank-two pencil. In the cyclic case prune all attached trees; formality persists and the core relation kernel has rank three, leaving six normals of rank three. For a fixed star triple `L_i`, every other triple meets it in a represented element. Every ordinary rank-two flat is a pair of disjoint indexing edges, since adjacent edges lie in a star triple. A perfect matching on four vertices contains exactly one edge incident with `i`, so its normal plane also meets `span(L_i)` in a represented point. Distinct rank-two planes in rank three have one-dimensional intersection; identical planes cause no issue. Rank zero, rank one and full-rank flat intersections are immediate. Thus `L_i` is modular, an atom within it is modular, and the resulting rank-three chain lifts back through every pruning step.

The proof is valid over the stated arbitrary characteristic-zero field. It does not silently restrict to `Q`, `R`, an algebraically closed field, or generic realizations. Positive characteristic, affine arrangements, repeated/multiarrangement hyperplanes and two cubic exponents are outside the theorem. The explicitly checked non-Fano `(1,3,3)` boundary is therefore consistent rather than a counterexample to the stated conclusion. The withdrawal example is also covered by the replay; its false old factor-size inference is not revived.
