## Independent proof audit

1. **External inputs and reductions.** Terao factorization supplies `n = sum d_i` and `b2 = sum_{i<j} d_i d_j`. Yuzvinsky's formality implication is about linear relations among normals, not topological formality. His primary Corollary 2.5 and DiPasquale's primary Corollary 4.10 support the use made here. Essentialization removes exactly the zero exponents and preserves the lattice. Free product factors have the union of the exponent multisets, and a product of maximal modular chains yields a maximal modular chain. Empty/rank-zero, rank-one and rank-two arrangements require no further induction. These reductions do not assume the desired conclusion.

2. **Exactly one exponent 1 in an essential irreducible factor.** Select independent normal forms as coordinates. A degree-one logarithmic derivation is diagonal because it is tangent to every coordinate hyperplane. Every further normal is an eigenvector, so a nonscalar diagonal derivation places normals in a partition of coordinate eigenspaces and produces a nontrivial direct-sum/product decomposition. Irreducibility therefore leaves only the scalar Euler derivation. Essentiality excludes nonzero constant tangent derivations. Consequently the degree-one dimension equals the multiplicity of exponent 1 in a free homogeneous basis. This argument works over the specified field without an algebraic-closure assumption.

3. **Rank-two multiplicity, divisibility and numerical budgets.** For a rank-two pencil choose forms `x,y,x+c_i y`, with distinct nonzero slopes. Tangency to the latter factors and coprimality with `y` imply each `x+c_i y` divides `P-Q` when the first two derivation coefficients are `xP,yQ`. If the pencil has more than `d+1` forms, the product of its `m-2` factors has degree greater than any basis column's `P-Q`, so `P=Q`. The first two rows of a free-basis matrix would then be proportional over the fraction field. A logarithmic module has full fraction-field rank, so this is impossible. Thus all rich lines have size three, or possibly four in the cubic case. Each pair belongs to a unique complete rank-two flat; its contribution to `b2` is `m-1`, giving the stated defect identity. Direct arithmetic gives:
   - `(1,2^(r-1))`: `n=2r-1`, `b2=2(r-1)^2`, `u=r-1`, `v=0`.
   - `(1,2^(r-2),3)`: `n=2r`, `b2=2r^2-2r-1`, `u+3v=r+1`.
   Formality gives `r <= u+2v` in the second case, hence `v<=1`.

4. **Connectedness and cycle dimensions.** Different rich lines meet in at most one normal because distinct hyperplanes give nonproportional normals. If the bipartite incidence graph were disconnected, all local relation spaces would be supported in individual components. Formality then decomposes the entire relation space by components; a cross-component relation must restrict to a relation in each component, proving their normal spans are in direct sum. This contradicts irreducibility and also excludes an isolated normal. For connected graphs the computed cycle dimensions are zero in the all-2 case, zero with one quadruple line, and three in the all-triple cubic case. No unproved matroid connectedness substitute is used.

5. **Minimal dependency and forced core.** In the last case `r+1` triple relation vectors span a space of dimension `r`. An inclusion-minimal dependent subfamily has all dependence coefficients nonzero; each triple's supported relation has three nonzero coordinates because every pair of normals is independent. Hence every support element occurs at least twice. The support incidence graph is connected, since disconnected components would split off smaller dependences. If it has `t` triples and `s` elements, then `s<=3t/2` and `2t-s+1<=3`; these imply `t<=4`. Every triple needs three distinct other triples to cover its three support elements, so `t>=4`. The inequalities then force `t=4,s=6`. All element degrees are two and every pair of triples meets at a distinct element. This is exactly the subdivided `K4` core. Its three cycles exhaust the full graph's cycle dimension. Contracting the connected core therefore yields a connected acyclic multigraph; all outside components are trees attached once, with attachments only at core element vertices. Loops, parallel connections back to the core, and extra core-to-core rich lines would increase the cycle dimension and are excluded.

6. **Terminal-pencil deletion and modular chain lifting.** With `L={a,p_1,...,p_(m-1)}` and all `p_i` in no other rich line, all remaining local relations lie in `E'`. The sum `R=R_L+S` is direct because a relation from `R_L` supported in `E'` would be supported on the nonzero single normal `a`. Restricting to `E'` proves `R'=S`, so deletion is formal; it removes `m-1` elements and `m-2` relation dimensions, dropping rank by exactly one. For `U=span L`, `W=span E'`, dimension counting gives `U intersect W=Ka`; removed normals are outside `W`, so `E'` is exactly a represented coatom. For every represented flat, zero/one outside generators cause no extra intersection vector, while two distinct outside normals span `U` and produce precisely the represented direction `a`. Thus its intersection with `W` is represented and `W` is modular. A modular flat within `W` meets any full flat by first meeting its represented intersection with `W`, proving global modularity and chain lifting. Freeness of deletions is never assumed. This is the point where omitting formality would fail; the packaged exact negative control confirms that limitation.

7. **Termination and rank-three core.** In each tree choose a deepest line away from the root pencil. Its outward elements are leaves, so the pruning lemma applies; finite repetition ends in a rank-two pencil. In the cyclic case the same procedure removes every attached tree. The four remaining triple relations have rank exactly three by minimal dependence, and deletion formality makes the remaining normal configuration rank `6-3=3`. Other stars meet it in a represented element; every ordinary pair consists of disjoint edges of the indexing `K4`, and exactly one of those edges is incident to the fixed star. Consequently every rank-two flat meets the star's normal plane in a represented direction. Rank zero, one and three are immediate, so the star is modular. An atom on it completes a maximal modular chain. Reversing pruning, forming products and undoing essentialization completes the asserted universal theorem.
