## Independent reconstruction and attempted falsifications

### 1. Reductions do not hide an extra hypothesis

After quotienting by the common intersection, zero exponents disappear and the intersection lattices are isomorphic. An empty arrangement is the rank-zero trivial case. Essential rank one is also immediate. The arrangement is a set, so no two defining normals are proportional.

For an essential arrangement choose independent defining normals as coordinate forms. A homogeneous degree-one logarithmic derivation acts diagonally on these coordinates. Every further defining normal must be an eigenvector. If its diagonal entries have more than one value, the normals split among the disjoint coordinate eigenspaces, giving a direct product. Thus an essential irreducible free factor has exactly one exponent `1`. Conversely, degree-zero derivations vanish for an essential arrangement, and the free homogeneous basis counts the dimension of the degree-one derivation space. This deals with all additional `1` exponents by a genuine product reduction; it does not assume irreducibility from mere exponent notation.

Products preserve freeness, unite exponent multisets, and preserve supersolvability. The last assertion can also be checked directly from the additive rank formula on the product lattice: concatenate modular chains of the factors. Nonessentiality does not change that conclusion. My independent fixture suite includes empty, rank-one, Boolean rank-two, reducible, and nonessential cases.

### 2. Multiplicity bound and arithmetic

For a rank-two pencil of `m` distinct hyperplanes take normals `x`, `y`, and `x+c_i y` for distinct nonzero `c_i`. For a degree-`d_j` logarithmic derivation write its first two coefficients `xP_j`, `yQ_j`. Tangency implies `(x+c_i y) | (P_j-Q_j)` since `(x+c_i y)` is coprime to `x`. If `m>d+1`, where every basis degree is at most `d`, the `m-2` distinct factors force `P_j=Q_j` for every free-basis member. The first two rows of the derivation coefficient matrix would then be proportional over the rational function field, contradicting basis independence. This is a valid direct proof, with no appeal to the withdrawn manuscript's pencil lemma or to localization exponent containment.

Let `b2` be the positive rank-two characteristic coefficient. Counting all unordered pairs gives

`C(n,2)-b2 = sum_{rank-two flats X} C(m_X-1,2)`.

Double points contribute zero. For the irreducible all-quadratic case, independent expansion gives

`n=2r-1`, `b2=2(r-1)^2`, and the number of triples `u=r-1`.

For the one-cubic case the expansion gives

`n=2r`, `b2=2r^2-2r-1`, and `u+3v=r+1`,

where `v` counts four-point pencils. A four-point pencil has local relation dimension two. Formality gives

`r=dim R <= u+2v = r+1-v`,

so `v<=1`. No equality of local relation sums is silently assumed in this inequality.

### 3. Why the incidence graph is connected

Formality means that the relation kernel is generated by local rank-two kernels. Each such generator is supported within one incidence component. Hence the global relation kernel splits by those components. If the spans of normals from separate components had a nonzero intersection, write an equality between two such spans as a relation; restricting that relation to the components would make each component contribution zero, a contradiction. Thus the normal spans form a direct sum. More than one component, including an isolated element, contradicts irreducibility. This confirms that the `+1` in all Euler-characteristic counts is warranted only after the irreducible reduction.

The connected graph has one node per element and one per rich rank-two flat. In the all-quadratic case its cycle dimension is zero. In the one-cubic/four-point case (`v=1`, `u=r-2`) it is again zero. In the remaining case (`v=0`, `u=r+1`) its cycle dimension is exactly three. These statements are valid at their smallest allowed ranks: triple and four-point pencils finish at rank two; the cyclic case necessarily starts at rank three.

### 4. The cyclic core is forced, including coefficient cancellation

In the `v=0` case there are `r+1` one-dimensional triple relation generators spanning an `r`-dimensional relation space. Take an inclusion-minimal dependent subset of `t` of them. Minimality ensures every dependence coefficient is nonzero and the support graph is connected. Every coordinate of every triple relation is nonzero: otherwise two distinct normals would be proportional. Consequently each represented element of the support occurs in at least two of its triples; this is a cancellation argument, not a graph-degree assumption.

Writing `s` for the number of represented elements gives `s<=3t/2`. The support graph has `3t` edges and `s+t` nodes, so its cycle dimension is `2t-s+1`. Cycle spaces of subgraphs embed in that of the complete incidence graph. Therefore `2t-s+1<=3`, or `s>=2t-2`; together these imply `t<=4`.

A distinct rich line meets a given rich line in at most one normal. Each of the three elements of each support triple must therefore be covered by a different other support triple, forcing `t>=4`. Hence `t=4` and `s=6`. Every represented element has support degree two, and every pair of the four triples intersects in a different element. This is exactly the subdivision/incidence graph of `K4`, with triple nodes corresponding to its vertices. No alternative minimal support, geometric parameter family, or larger-rank core escapes these inequalities.

The support exhausts all three cycles of the complete graph. Contract this connected core and delete its internal edges; the resulting connected multigraph has cycle dimension zero. It is a tree, so each outside component is a tree attached at exactly one core vertex. Multiple attachments, an extra outside cycle, or two parallel attachment edges would produce a further cycle. Every core triple already has all three of its incidences inside the core, so attachments can meet only represented-element nodes. This checks both asserted forest structure and the attachment location.

I independently enumerated all `C(20,4)=4,845` four-triple families on six labeled points. Exactly 30 linear families with every point degree at least two survive, and every one has this `K4` incidence. This finite enumeration corroborates the equality case; the inequalities above prove the universal classification.

### 5. Terminal deletion really preserves the needed structure

This is the most likely place for a false general deletion or localization claim; neither is needed. Let `L={a,p1,...,p_(m-1)}` be a terminal rich line whose outward normals occur in no other rich line. Let `E'` retain `a` and remove its outward normals.

All local kernels apart from `R_L` are supported on `E'`, and `R_L` intersects that coordinate subspace trivially because a remaining relation would be supported only on the nonzero normal `a`. Formality therefore gives the genuine direct decomposition

`R=R_L direct-sum R(E')`.

In particular `E'` remains formal, its relation dimension drops by `m-2`, and its rank drops by exactly one. The rich-line incidence graph of the smaller configuration is precisely the pruned graph: all other rich lines avoid the removed normals, while the terminal line leaves only `a`.

Write `W=span(E')` and `U=span(L)`. If one removed normal belonged to `W`, the resulting relation using it and normals in `E'`, decomposed by the displayed direct sum, would yield a relation supported on that normal and `a`. Their independence forbids this. Thus `W` is a represented flat of codimension one, `U∩W=K a`, and every normal outside `W` belongs to `U`.

For any represented flat `F`, choose a generating subset of normals. If it contains no more than one outside `W`, its intersection with `W` is generated by the inside normals. If it contains at least two outside `W`, those distinct normals span `U`, and its intersection with `W` is generated by the inside normals together with `a`. Both intersections are represented flats. This proves `W` modular by the linear-representation criterion, not only by a numerical rank check in selected examples.

If `C` is modular in the restricted configuration on `W`, then for any global flat `F`, first `W∩F` is a represented flat, and then `C∩F=C∩(W∩F)` is a represented flat. Thus every modular-chain member lifts, and adjoining the full normal space gives the chain for the original configuration. This confirms the candidate's terminal-pencil lemma for triple and four-point pencils. It makes no claim about an arbitrary deletion being free or formal.

### 6. The core and termination

A rich-line node farthest from a chosen root has all outward element nodes as leaves, so terminal pruning exists at every non-base step. The two tree cases finish at a rank-two pencil.

In the cyclic case pruning all attachments leaves exactly the four original core triples on six normals. Their relation vectors form a minimally dependent four-set, hence have rank three. Preserved formality makes the remaining normal rank `6-3=3`; there are no unaccounted higher-support relations.

Fix a core triple `L_i`. Every other triple meets it in its designated shared normal. Every remaining rank-two flat is a two-point flat indexed by disjoint edges of the indexing `K4`; those edges partition the four vertices, so exactly one belongs to the star representing `L_i`. Thus all distinct rank-two flats meet `span(L_i)` in a represented point. Rank-one intersections are zero or the represented point itself, and the full-space and self intersections are immediate. Therefore `L_i` is modular. Any represented point of `L_i` is modular as well, completing its rank-three modular chain. Reattaching terminal pencils lifts this chain in every rank.
