# Routine scaling of the frozen transcendental family

2026-10-15 UTC. Root-authored deduction for independent challenge; it changes none of the 28 frozen native inputs and makes no historical novelty claim.

For every integer k≥3, every prescribed b∈C\{0}, and every z0∈C, choose a nonzero λ with

    λ^(k−1) = −1/(2^(k−1)−2),

and set A=−2b/λ and f(z)=A(exp(λ(z−z0))−1)^2. With t=exp(λ(z−z0)),

    f′=2Aλt(t−1),
    f′−b=2Aλ(t−1/2)^2,
    f^(k)=Aλ^k(2^k t^2−2t).

The common zero set of f and f′ is t=1, since A,λ,t are nonzero. Multiplicities are two and one. The b-fiber of f′ is exactly t=1/2, and contains the explicit points z=z0+(−log2+2πin)/λ for all integers n. At every such point,

    f^(k)=Aλ^k(2^(k−2)−1)=−Aλ/2=b.

The infinitely many zeros z=z0+2πin/λ prove transcendence and exclude the proposed nonzero exponential at a=0. Thus the frozen normalized family extends by ordinary scaling and translation to arbitrary prescribed b. This is a corollary of the existing construction, not an additional resolution of a new problem. No assertion is made for a≠0 or for transcendental functions at k=2.
