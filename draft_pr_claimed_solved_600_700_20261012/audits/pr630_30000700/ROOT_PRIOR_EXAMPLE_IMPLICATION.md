## Direct complete implication, without transferring the neighboring theorem

Fix any target b≠0 and instantiate the earlier explicit example with B=2b. Then

    g(z)=bz²/2,       g′(z)=bz,       g″(z)=b.

For the literal printed 2007 IM replacement set a=0 and k=2. The following checks are global over C:

1. g is a nonconstant entire function, since b≠0.
2. The zero sets of g and g′ are both {0}. Their multiplicities are two and one, so this is IM and not CM.
3. The required implication g′=b ⇒ g″=b holds because g″ is identically b. Its antecedent is attained at z=1, so it is nonvacuous. Note that the earlier source's separate B-fiber condition is not used.
4. The proposed conclusion at a=0 is d exp(cz), with c,d≠0 and c^(k−1)=1. At k=2, c=1; every such exponential is zero-free, whereas g(0)=0. Thus the conclusion fails.
5. The catalogue's additional b≠a also holds, and the additive-constant transcription discrepancy vanishes at a=0.

This is exactly the PR's smallest counterexample, with b=1 as a special case. It is therefore a positive explicit prior-example implication to the negative answer to the **universal** IM statement. It does not rest on calling an elementary function old merely because it is familiar, on an absence-of-search result, on a nonzero-a theorem, or on importing the earlier source's different b-condition. One additional direct differentiation checks the target condition for the already published zero-sharing counterexample.

The original question asks whether the theorem remains universally valid after the replacement, and k=2 is within its quantified range. A counterexample at k=2 refutes that universal assertion. The source is not claimed to give all fixed-order families or the packet's polynomial classification verbatim.
