# Turn 1: scalar extension can erase the entire graded distinction

Problem30006345. First substantive author turn. **Original arbitrary-field modular-isomorphism question remains unresolved.** This turn proves an explicit obstruction to recovering the prime-field group merely from the scalar-extended associated graded algebra. It does not construct isomorphic full group algebras.

## Theorem

Let p be any odd prime, let d be a nonsquare in F_p, and write K=F_p(s), s²=d. Define H(R) for a finite commutative ring R by triples (a,b,c)∈R³ with

(a,b,c)(a′,b′,c′)=(a+a′,b+b′,c+c′+ab′).

Set S=H(F_p)×H(F_p) and N=H(K), viewing N as a finite p-group. Both have order p⁶, exponent p, nilpotency class exactly two, center and derived subgroup of order p², and minimal generator number four. For every characteristic-p field k containing a root r of X²−d,

    gr_J(kS) ≅ gr_J(kN)

as graded k-algebras, where J is the augmentation/Jacobson radical. Nevertheless, for **every** characteristic-p field k, including these extension fields,

    kS ≄ kN.

Indeed their center dimensions are respectively (p²+p−1)² and p⁴+p²−1, whose difference is 2(p−1)²(p+1)>0. Thus neither a full graded-algebra identification over k nor its low-degree alternating tensor alone can justify prime-field descent. Higher filtered multiplication can retain distinguishing information lost in passage to gr_J.

## 1. Groups and conjugacy counts

Associativity of the displayed law follows by expanding the central coordinate: both bracketings add c+c′+c″+ab′+ab″+a′b″. The inverse is (−a,−b,−c+ab). The commutator convention here is ghg⁻¹h⁻¹; it gives (0,0,ab′−a′b). Also (a,b,c)^m=(ma,mb,mc+binom(m,2)ab). For odd p every element has pth power1 and there are elements of order p. Over any field F_q, q>1, the center and derived subgroup are both {(0,0,c)}; the group is nonabelian and class two.

For noncentral (a,b,c), the map (a′,b′)↦ab′−a′b is a nonzero F_q-linear functional and is onto. Thus the conjugacy class has size q. There are q central singleton classes, and (q³−q)/q=q²−1 noncentral classes. Therefore

    k(H(F_q))=q²+q−1.

Conjugacy classes of a direct product are products of classes. This proves the two counts above. The center of any group algebra over **any** field has a basis of conjugacy-class sums: commuting with each group element says exactly that coefficients are constant on conjugacy classes. Disjoint supports prove independence even when the characteristic divides class sizes. Consequently its dimension equals the number of conjugacy classes, proving the full-algebra separation without a characteristic-zero character argument.

## 2. Explicit presentation of gr_J(kN)

Use the F_p-basis1,s of K. Let x_i=(s^i,0,0), y_i=(0,s^i,0), z_i=(0,0,s^i), i=0,1. Lowercase letters are group elements; uppercase denote their augmentation leading terms. Give X_i,Y_i degree1 and Z_i degree2. The graded algebra has the presentation

- Z_0,Z_1 are central;
- [X_0,X_1]=[Y_0,Y_1]=0;
- [X_0,Y_0]=Z_0, [X_0,Y_1]=[X_1,Y_0]=Z_1, [X_1,Y_1]=d Z_0;
- X_i^p=Y_i^p=Z_i^p=0.

Here [u,v]=uv−vu. For completeness, this special case of the credited Jennings–Quillen description can be verified directly. The central z_i−1 lie in J² because they arise from group commutators. Since gh−hg=(ghg⁻¹h⁻¹−1)hg, the commutator formulas pass to degree2. For an integer m representing λ∈F_p, z_i^m−1≡λ(z_i−1) modulo J³, as z_i−1∈J². All other displayed commutators vanish exactly, and (g−1)^p=g^p−1=0. Thus the displayed presented algebra U maps onto gr_J(kN). The latter is generated in degree1, since J is generated by x_i−1,y_i−1: these four group elements generate N, including z_0,z_1 through their commutators.

Every word in U reduces to an ordered monomial X_0^a X_1^b Y_0^c Y_1^e Z_0^f Z_1^g with exponents0,…,p−1. To see termination, swap out-of-order degree-one letters; the correction is central and has two fewer degree-one letters, while the swapped term has fewer inversions. Move Z letters to the end. The power relations then bound every exponent. Hence dim_k U≤p⁶. The augmentation ideal of a finite p-group algebra is nilpotent, so dim_k gr_J(kN)=dim_k kN=p⁶. The surjection is therefore an isomorphism and the ordered monomials are a basis. This argument does not presuppose that kN is isomorphic to its graded algebra.

The elementary nilpotence fact used here has a short induction proof. Choose a central element z of order p. The ideal (z−1)kN is central-generated with pth power0, and the quotient is k[N/⟨z⟩]. By induction the image of J has some power0, so J^m lies in (z−1)kN for some m and J^(mp)=0. The quotient by J is k, hence J is the Jacobson radical. The same argument applies to S.

## 3. Splitting over the quadratic extension

Assume r²=d in k. Since d≠0 and p is odd, r and2 are invertible. In U put

    X_±=(X_0±r⁻¹X_1)/2,
    Y_±=(Y_0±r⁻¹Y_1)/2,
    Z_±=(Z_0±r⁻¹Z_1)/2.

Direct expansion gives [X_+,Y_+]=Z_+, [X_−,Y_−]=Z_−, and [X_+,Y_−]=[X_−,Y_+]=0. X pairs commute, Y pairs commute, and all Z are central. Every new generator has pth power0: each is a linear combination of two **commuting** p-nilpotent generators, so the ordinary characteristic-p binomial theorem applies. No unjustified pth-power linearization of noncommuting elements is used.

The change of variables is invertible: X_0=X_++X_− and X_1=r(X_+−X_−), with the analogous Y,Z formulas. These are precisely two commuting copies of the three-generator graded presentation for H(F_p). That presentation follows by the same ordered-monomial argument with p³ monomials. Equivalently, apply the six-generator argument directly to S, whose only nonzero basic brackets are [X_+,Y_+]=Z_+ and [X_−,Y_−]=Z_−. The inverse linear formulas verify the original relations as well. This gives the claimed graded isomorphism.

One can also view this as the familiar splitting K⊗_(F_p)k≅k×k. The explicit calculation is included to control grading, pth-power relations and the direction of scalar extension. It says nothing about an algebra isomorphism before passing to gr_J.

## 4. Interpretation and limits

The scalar-extended alternating commutator tensor and the **entire** graded radical algebra are insufficient to recover the original finite group. The example lies within the precise class-two/exponent-p family, unlike the characteristic-two truncated D_8/Q_8 example in Margolis–Sakurai. It nevertheless fails the original hypothesis kS≅kN, as the center calculation proves. It is therefore an obstruction to one proof route, not a negative solution of the source problem.

The construction uses standard Heisenberg groups, restriction of scalars, conjugacy-class sums and the Jennings–Quillen mechanism. Historical novelty is not asserted. The explicit proof is supplied as campaign work, not as a replacement for the credited known prime-field theorem. Subsequent turns must retain filtered information or another all-field invariant rather than identify gr_J with kG.
