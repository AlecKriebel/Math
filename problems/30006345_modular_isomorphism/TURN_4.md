# Turn 4: a precise connected-stabilizer descent criterion

Problem 30006345. Fourth substantive author turn. **Original unresolved, 4/5.** This turn supplies a conditional positive theorem using the classical Lang theorem. Geometric connectedness is a genuine hypothesis; it is not proved for arbitrary groups and fails in the split-product example of turn 1.

## 1. Statement

Let p be odd and let G be a finite group of exponent p and nilpotency class two. Put V=G/G′ and W=G′, as F_p-vector spaces, and let beta:Λ²V→W be the commutator map. Let S_beta be the algebraic stabilizer over F_p with points

    S_beta(E) = {(A,C)∈GL(V_E)×GL(W_E):
                 beta(Ax,Ay)=C beta(x,y) for all x,y},          (1)

for field extensions E/F_p. Interpret this as the matrix stabilizer scheme, and let K_beta=(S_beta)_red be its reduced algebraic subgroup over F_p.

**Conditional theorem.** If K_beta is geometrically connected, then for every characteristic-p field k and every class-two exponent-p group H,

    kG≅kH as k-algebras  ⇒  G≅H.                              (2)

Equivalently, a counterexample in the source's exact family must have a geometrically disconnected reduced commutator-tensor stabilizer. The condition is on the algebraic group over an algebraic closure, not on connectedness of a finite group of F_p-rational points.

This is a deduction from the credited Lang theorem and the explicit graded reconstruction below. It is not a new proof of Lang's theorem and carries no historical novelty assertion.

## 2. The intrinsic commutator tensor in the associated graded algebra

Here is the general form of the ordered-monomial argument used in earlier turns. Choose lifts g_1,…,g_d of a basis of G/G′ and a basis z_1,…,z_w of G′. Since G′ is central and G/G′ is generated by those lifts, all commutators of G are generated by the [g_i,g_j]; hence the g_i generate G itself. Each generator has order p. Write the commutators using structure constants b_(ij,l)∈F_p.

Over any characteristic-p field E, define a graded algebra U with degree-one X_i and central degree-two Z_l, and relations

    [X_i,X_j]=Σ_l b_(ij,l)Z_l,       X_i^p=Z_l^p=0.           (3)

Collecting words gives at most p^(d+w) ordered monomials. Sending X_i to g_i−1 modulo J² and Z_l to z_l−1 modulo J³ gives a graded surjection U→gr_J(EG): all relations follow from group commutators and orders. The target is generated in degree one and has total dimension|G|=p^(d+w), since the augmentation ideal J is nilpotent. Thus the surjection is an isomorphism.

In particular, degree one identifies with V_E, while the linear span of brackets inside degree two identifies with W_E; the Z_l are independent by the dimension equality. The induced bilinear bracket is beta_E. This proves that any E-algebra isomorphism EG≅EH gives equivalent commutator tensors over E, by first passing to the intrinsic radical filtration. We do not assume that the isomorphism respects a group basis or Hopf structure, or that EG is isomorphic to its graded algebra.

For completeness, an F_p-equivalence of the tensors gives a group isomorphism. Both groups have the presentation with the above generators, central z_l, pth powers 1 and prescribed commutators. Collection bounds its order by p^(d+w), and its surjection to either group is an isomorphism by order. Equivalently, it is the standard Baer model on V⊕W with law(x,z)(y,t)=(x+y,z+t+beta(x,y)/2). This model has the same order, powers and commutators, so the presentation identifies it with G. A pair(A,C) intertwining tensors acts on the model by (x,z)↦(Ax,Cz).

## 3. Reduction to the algebraic closure of the prime field

Suppose kG≅kH. The general reduction theorem of García-Lucas–del Río, Theorem A and Corollary B in https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09355 , implies that F_(p^m)G≅F_(p^m)H for some m. This theorem is credited and does not say m=1. Extending scalars gives a tensor equivalence over Fbar_p by Section 2. Alternatively, the nonempty tensor-isomorphism equations over F_p have a point over its algebraic closure by the weak Nullstellensatz; we use the cited finite-field reduction here.

Identify the source and target vector-space dimensions and write their tensors as beta and gamma. The group L=GL_d×GL_w acts by

    (A,C)·beta(x,y)=C beta(A^−1x,A^−1y).

Choose g∈L(Fbar_p) with gamma=g·beta. If F is entrywise pth power, both tensors are F-fixed. Therefore

    a=g^−1 F(g) ∈ S_beta(Fbar_p)=K_beta(Fbar_p).               (4)

Passing to the reduction changes no points over fields. Over the perfect field F_p the reduction is an algebraic subgroup and is smooth. These standard facts are stated in Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2022 online edition), Proposition 1.26 and Corollary 1.39, https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf . The geometric connectedness hypothesis therefore makes K_beta a connected group variety to which Lang's theorem applies.

## 4. Frobenius correction and descent

The credited Lang theorem states that for a connected group variety over a finite field, h↦h^−1F(h) is onto its points over the algebraic closure. See Milne, Corollaries 17.97–17.98, printed 383–384 (PDF pages394–395 in one-based counting), at the link above. The source states the equivalent inverse-variable form hF(h^−1); replacing h by h^−1 gives the form used here.

Thus choose h∈K_beta(Fbar_p) with h^−1F(h)=a. Set r=g h^−1. Then

    F(r)=F(g)F(h)^−1=(g a)(h a)^−1=g h^−1=r.                (5)

All entries of r are in F_p, and r is invertible. Since h stabilizes beta, r·beta=g·beta=gamma. Hence the tensors are F_p-equivalent, and Section 2 yields G≅H. This proves (2). In particular, no unsupported descent of individual coefficients has been inserted: the entire matrix equivalence is corrected by an actual stabilizer element.

## 5. Nonvacuous checks and a real limitation

If beta:Λ²V→W is an isomorphism, each A∈GL(V) uniquely determines C=beta∘Λ²A∘beta^−1. The stabilizer is the graph of that morphism and is isomorphic to GL(V), which is geometrically connected (the nonvanishing-determinant open subset of affine matrix space is irreducible). Thus the criterion applies to the free class-two exponent-p model. In this particular case the same conclusion also follows directly from maximal commutator dimension; it is a sanity check, not a new resolution of an unknown subclass.

For the smallest Heisenberg group with dimV=2 and dimW=1, the stabilizer is likewise GL_2 with C=det A. Again the criterion is consistent with the already-known all-field result. No novelty credit is claimed for these examples.

By contrast, take G=H(F_p)×H(F_p), the split group in turn 1. Its two-dimensional commutator pencil is diag(uJ_2,vJ_2). Over the algebraic closure its rank-two locus in the projective parameter line consists of exactly the two coordinate points. Any stabilizer element permutes those two points. Swapping the two group factors induces a nontrivial permutation, so the reduced stabilizer has a surjective morphism to the constant two-element permutation group and is disconnected. More concretely, the alternatives of fixing or swapping the two distinguished lines are disjoint closed subsets which are also open in their union, and both occur. Therefore the hypothesis cannot be assumed throughout the source family. The quadratic-field Heisenberg group of turn 1 becomes equivalent to this tensor after scalar extension, exactly where the connected-stabilizer argument is unavailable.

This does not prove that disconnected stabilizers cause full group-algebra collisions. Turn1 proves that its two tensor forms have distinct full centers. The conditional theorem is useful precisely because it identifies an explicit descent obstruction without replacing a full-algebra isomorphism by a weaker hypothesis.
