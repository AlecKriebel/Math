# Primary sources and exact dependency scope

SOURCE_MANIFEST.json binds four primary files. They remain local and are
not redistributed. The imported records were read at dataset revision
37e53eabe540fb458758e198be61634bd02ee008. No further research report is used
as a substitute for a primary theorem.

1. David Aldous, *Scale-invariant random spatial networks*,
   arXiv:1204.0817v1 (3 April 2012, manuscript dated April 5), author-hosted
   56-page version:
   https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/206-SNET/Papers/aldous_SIRSN_long.pdf .
   Open Problem34 is in §8.4.3, printed p.50. This identifies the imported
   number. The source itself does not assert maximal integrability.
2. Published Aldous paper, Electronic Journal of Probability 19 (2014),
   paper15, pp.1–41, DOI10.1214/EJP.v19-2920:
   https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/EJP-ECP/article/download/2920/2920-16115-1-PB.pdf .
   The PDF has an extra final journal-information page in the supplied file; the
   journal's printed labels remain 1–41. The target is Open Problem8,
   §8.4.3, printed p.38, rendered and visually inspected. Relevant complete
   sections read: §§2.1–2.3 (feasible routes, compatibility, measurable FDDs,
   independent Poisson sampling, first length moment and major-road axiom);
   Proposition3.1 and §3.7 (bounded stretch and continuum/randomization);
   Proposition5.1 (no initial straight segment at sampled endpoints);
   §6, especially (6.2), Proposition6.1, Proposition6.3 and (6.4) (major-road
   intensity, crossings, sampling independence and planted origin).
   Turn4 uses these as credited primary inputs, without claiming to
   re-prove the source's general random-set construction.
3. Aldous's maintained SIRSN page:
   https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/sirsn.html . It points to
   the general problems and distinguishes subsequent model constructions.
   Its continued listing is a bounded status observation, not an exhaustive
   proof that no later result exists.
4. Jonas Kahn, *Improper Poisson line process as SIRSN in any dimension*,
   arXiv:1503.03976v3 (29 September 2016), published Annals of Probability
   44(4) (2016), 2694–2725, DOI10.1214/15-AOP1032:
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.03976v3 . Full Theorem3.1 and its construction
   and tail bound, Theorem5.1 and its proof, and Remark5.1 were read. Printed
   p.25 was rendered and visually inspected. The common travel-time bound
   and speed-record mechanism are credited, including the uniform geometric
   interpretation in Remark5.1. The stronger moments conjectured there are
   not used. The packet writes the speed bound for the intersection event,
   so it needs no unproved independence after conditioning on travel time.
