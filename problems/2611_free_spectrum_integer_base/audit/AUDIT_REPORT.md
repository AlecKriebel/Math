## 2. Attempt 1: joins, nilpotence, and minimality

**PASS.** In the absolutely free group on n letters, the identity subgroup of a join is the intersection of the individual identity subgroups. Consequently the relatively free group embeds in the product and maps onto every factor. The inequalities

`max_i a_i(n) <= a_G(n) <= sum_i a_i(n)`

hold pointwise, including trivial factors. For finitely many factors with existing root limits, the factor t between maximum and sum has nth root tending to one, giving the maximum rule. The packet does not incorrectly assert a max-of-liminfs formula for several arbitrary oscillating factors.

For a class-c, exponent-e group, the relatively free group inherits both identities. The quotient of consecutive lower-central terms in weight j is generated by at most n^j commutators in the free generators, has exponent dividing e, and therefore has order at most e^(n^j). Multiplying those finite factors gives the stated bound. A cyclic prime-order subgroup of a nontrivial G supplies the opposite n log p estimate. Thus the nilpotent root limit is 1; for the trivial group it is 0.

The quotient-domination squeeze is valid: a quotient's variety is contained in the original variety, hence the original relatively free group maps onto the quotient variety's free group. The lower bound from the nonabelian-monolith theorem and upper bound from the abelian-normal estimate have the same exponential base.

A finite nonmonolithic group has two distinct minimal normal subgroups with trivial intersection. Its embedding in the product of the corresponding proper quotients gives precisely the equality of varieties used in the least-order argument. A finite abelian minimal normal subgroup is elementary abelian. Each claimed restriction on a smallest counterexample follows, and none disposes of the remaining case.

## 3. Attempt 2: the modular lower bound and nonsplit construction

**PASS. This is the main delicate argument, and it survives adversarial checking.**

### Cyclic-vector lemma

A direct sum of simple modules is a semisimple module even over a nonsemisimple group algebra. Every submodule therefore decomposes into simples. A simple constituent mapping nontrivially to V_i is isomorphic to V_i; its projections into all nonisomorphic V_j are zero. Since each isomorphism class occurs once, it is the whole coordinate V_i. Thus a submodule projecting nontrivially into every V_i equals their direct sum. No appeal to Maschke or coprimality occurs.

### Counting and inequivalence

The action of Aut(Q) on ordered generating r-tuples is free. Two epimorphisms from the same absolutely free group onto Q have the same kernel exactly when they differ by an automorphism of Q. The diagonal image B therefore has distinct coordinate-projection kernels: pulling them back through the surjection from the free group preserves and detects equality.

Inflating a faithful irreducible Q-module along a surjection B -> Q gives a simple B-module whose action kernel is exactly that projection kernel. Isomorphic B-modules necessarily have the same action kernel. Thus the chosen distinct kernels really do force pairwise nonisomorphic modules. The argument does not make the false converse that equal action kernels force module isomorphism. Discarding all but one tuple per Aut(Q)-orbit is conservative and sufficient.

The subgroup generated by r lifted B-generators and the one vector v contains all conjugates of v. The additive subgroup they generate is exactly the F_p B-submodule generated by v, hence is the whole M^t. It is an at-most-(r+1)-generated subgroup of A^t, so it is a quotient of F_A(r+1). This justifies the order lower bound, with the correct direction of the free-object map.

Every nongenerating tuple lies in a proper subgroup, each of size at most |Q|/2. A union bound gives the stated generating-tuple estimate, irrespective of overlaps. For r sufficiently large its parenthetical factor is at least a positive constant. Substituting n=r+1 loses one fixed factor |Q|, not an n-dependent factor. The resulting lower bound holds for every sufficiently large n, rather than on a subsequence.

### Split section and self-centralizing monolith

Inside G x G, D normalizes M x 1. The diagonal copy of M is normalized by D and centralized by M x 1 because M is abelian, so it is normal in their product. The image of D is G/M; the image of M x 1 is M. Their intersection is trivial and their conjugation action is the original quotient action. Consequently their quotient really is the desired semidirect product, a section of a direct square and hence in var(G), whether or not G splits.

For C_G(M)=M, the quotient action is faithful. Minimal normality gives irreducibility. The variety inclusion transfers the affine lower bound to G, and Proposition 1 supplies the matching upper bound. The separate Q=1 case is correctly handled. S_4 has the specified faithful irreducible GL(2,2) action on its normal V_4, so the conclusion of base 6 is legitimate in modular characteristic.

## 5. Attempt 4: central kernels and the quadratic loss

**PASS.** A word function on G descends modulo Z to a word function on Q, independently of the chosen lifts of a quotient tuple. Identity inclusion makes the map well defined, and the free generators make it surjective. Its kernel consists precisely of word functions taking every value in Z, and these commute coordinatewise with every word function. Therefore the kernel is central and the relevant index is exactly |F_Q(n)|.

With t equal to that index, the primary Schur–Wiegold statement applies. Replacing log_q t by the larger log_2 t weakens its bound in the correct direction. The n-generated abelianization has exponent dividing exp(G), so its order is at most e^n. Multiplication of the derived-subgroup and abelianization bounds, followed by natural logarithms, gives exactly equation (9), including the negative linear term.

When Q is nontrivial, t>=2 for all n>=1; when Q=1, the group under consideration is abelian and the separate elementary estimate applies. Dropping the negative term and taking nth roots gives max(1,u_Q^2). Quotient monotonicity and the independent abelian-normal upper bound then yield [L,min(|Q|,L^2)] whenever the nontrivial quotient has root limit L. Such a quotient has L>=1, so eliminating the outer maximum with 1 is justified.

The iteration along h central extensions also has the stated exponent 2^h. No inference is made that the central kernel is n-generated. The bound has exponential-base loss when L>1 and genuinely does not prove central-extension invariance.

## 6. Attempt 5: chief actions and SL(2,3)

**PASS.** For an abelian chief factor A/B of a section T, one has A<=C_T(A/B), so the quotient action kernel C_T(A/B)/A is well defined in T/A. The split-section construction is applied to T/B, with abelian normal subgroup A/B and quotient T/A. The action-kernel complement is normal in the resulting semidirect product: it is normal in the acting group and centralizes the entire module. Quotienting it out gives the faithful affine group asserted in (12).
