# Attempt 4 — central extensions and a quantitative obstruction

Fourth substantive proof attempt. The main unresolved family after attempts 1–3 includes groups with nonfaithful action on their abelian monolith. Central extensions are a basic case. This attempt obtains a quantitative bound but does not establish the desired invariance.

## 1. The free-group map has central kernel

Let Z be a central subgroup of finite G and put Q=G/Z. Realize S=F_G(n) as the subgroup of G^(G^n) consisting of word functions on G^n. Quotienting each value modulo Z induces a surjective homomorphism

S -> R=F_Q(n).

The map is well defined, because a word function on G that is identically trivial induces an identity on Q; its image consists of all word functions on Q, because every tuple of Q lifts to G. Its kernel K consists exactly of word functions whose values all lie in Z. Every such function commutes coordinatewise with every element of S. Hence K is central in S and |S/K|=|R|.

This identifies the central-extension obstacle inside the free spectrum, without claiming that K has small rank.

## 2. Schur–Wiegold bound

We use the classical bound discussed and reproved in B. A. F. Wehrfritz, *Schur's theorem and Wiegold's bound*, Journal of Algebra 504 (2018), 440–444, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2018.02.023 . The publisher's primary article introduction explicitly states: if a group X has a central subgroup of finite index t>1, and q is the least prime divisor of t, then

|X'| <= t^((log_q t - 1)/2).

For our application the weaker consequence with log_2 t is sufficient. The theorem is credited, not reproved or claimed as a new result here.

Let e be the exponent of G, so S has exponent dividing e. The abelianization S/S' is generated by at most n elements and has exponent dividing e. Therefore |S/S'|<=e^n. With t=|R|, the bound gives

a_G(n) <= n log e + a_Q(n)^2/(2 log 2) - a_Q(n)/2.          (9)

When Q=1, S is abelian and the simpler inequality a_G(n)<=n log e applies. If Q is nontrivial, t>=2 for all n>=1 and (9) is valid throughout.

Writing u_Q=limsup a_Q(n)^(1/n), and retaining the convention that the trivial variety has root value zero, (9) proves

limsup a_G(n)^(1/n) <= max(1,u_Q^2).                        (10)

The quotient map also gives a_G(n)>=a_Q(n). Independently Olshanskii's abelian-normal bound gives limsup a_G(n)^(1/n)<=|Q|. If Q has a nontrivial root limit L, then

L <= liminf a_G(n)^(1/n) <= limsup a_G(n)^(1/n)
                                      <= min(|Q|,L^2).    (11)

For L=1 the interval collapses to 1, recovering central-extension invariance in that case. For L>1 it generally does not collapse.

## 3. Why this estimate is insufficient

For example, if Q=A_4, the known root limit is 3 (also covered by the affine theorem in attempt 2). A central extension G of Q is bounded by

3 <= liminf <= limsup <= 9,

which is stronger than the general upper bound 12 but proves neither equality of the limits nor integrality of either limit. The statement is a bound on any such G; it is not a claim that a group attaining 9 exists. A central extension with quotient a nonabelian-monolithic group is already settled more sharply by attempt 1.

Repeated application along h successive central extensions produces an upper bound max(1,u_Q^(2^h)). This makes clear that the Schur–Wiegold estimate does not supply the polynomial-in-n multiplicative loss that would be needed to preserve an arbitrary exponential base.

A tempting but invalid shortcut would be to treat the central kernel K as generated by n elements because S is. Subgroups of an n-generated finite group can require many more generators; centrality alone does not give that rank bound. The present argument avoids it by bounding S' and S/S' separately.

## Outcome

A rigorous free-spectrum central-extension inequality is obtained. It improves some coarse upper bounds and precisely shows the remaining exponential loss. The original target is unresolved. In particular this attempt does not answer Olshanskii's separate central-extension invariance question.
