# Attempt 2 — faithful irreducible actions give matching exponential growth

Second substantive proof attempt. This attempt proves a class of convergence statements; the original universal question is still unresolved. The proof uses the split-section construction explicitly present in section 5 of Olshanskii's v3. No historical-novelty claim is made for the resulting class theorem.

## 1. A cyclic vector across distinct simple modules

Let B be any group and let V_1,...,V_t be finite simple F_p B-modules which are pairwise nonisomorphic. If v=(v_1,...,v_t) has v_i nonzero for every i, the submodule F_p B v of the direct sum V=direct_sum_i V_i is all of V.

Proof. A direct sum of simple modules is semisimple, so its submodule U=F_p B v is a direct sum of simple modules. Every simple submodule of V is supported on its unique isomorphism class: projection into a nonisomorphic simple factor is zero, while a nonzero projection between simple modules is an isomorphism. Each factor occurs only once. Since the projection of U onto each V_i contains v_i and is therefore nonzero, it follows that U contains every V_i. Thus U=V. This argument does not assume p is coprime to |B| and does not invoke Maschke's theorem.

## 2. Semidirect-product lower bound

Let Q be a finite nontrivial group and let M be a finite faithful irreducible F_p Q-module, regarded as an elementary abelian group. Put A=M semidirect Q. Write e_Q(r) for the number of ordered generating r-tuples of Q. The automorphism group of Q acts freely on these tuples. Choose one tuple in every orbit and write

t=e_Q(r)/|Aut(Q)|.

In Q^t take r diagonal coordinate generators x_j, whose i-th coordinate is the j-th entry in the i-th chosen tuple. Let B=<x_1,...,x_r>, so each coordinate projection pi_i:B->Q is onto. The kernels of these projections are pairwise distinct: their pullbacks to the absolutely free group on r letters are exactly the kernels of the chosen epimorphisms to Q, and two such epimorphisms with the same kernel differ by an automorphism of Q.

Make M_i into a B-module through pi_i. It is simple since pi_i is onto. Its action kernel is ker(pi_i), because the original Q-action is faithful. Hence the M_i are pairwise nonisomorphic B-modules. Fix a nonzero m in M and set v=(m,...,m) in M^t. Inside A^t the subgroup

L=< (0,x_1),...,(0,x_r), v >

contains the B-submodule generated by v, namely all of M^t by section 1. Therefore L has r+1 generators and order at least |M|^t. It belongs to var(A), and so

a_A(r+1) >= (e_Q(r)/|Aut(Q)|) log|M|.                     (3)

For q=|Q|, there are at most s(q/2)^r nongenerating r-tuples, where s is the fixed number of proper subgroups of Q. Thus e_Q(r)>=q^r(1-s 2^(-r)). For all sufficiently large r, (3) is at least c q^r with c>0 independent of r. Equivalently,

a_A(n) >= c' q^n for all sufficiently large n.             (4)

Olshanskii's Proposition 1, applied to the abelian normal subgroup M of A, bounds a_A(n)<=C n q^n. Combining with (4) proves

lim_n a_A(n)^(1/n)=|Q|.                                   (5)

## 3. Nonsplit groups with a self-centralizing abelian minimal normal subgroup

Let G have an abelian minimal normal subgroup M satisfying C_G(M)=M. Then M is an elementary abelian p-group, irreducible under Q=G/M, and the Q-action is faithful. A split group A=M semidirect Q belongs to var(G), even if M has no complement in G:

Inside G x G let D={(g,g):g in G}, N_1=M x 1, and K=D N_1. The group L={(m,m):m in M} is normal in K: it is invariant under D by normality of M and centralized by N_1 because M is abelian. In K/L, the image of D is isomorphic to G/M and meets the image of N_1 trivially. The latter image is isomorphic to M, is normal, and the two images generate K/L. Their conjugation action is the original Q-action on M. Hence K/L is A, a section of G x G. This is the credited construction from Olshanskii, section 5, written out here to bind the lower bound to G.

Now a_G(n)>=a_A(n) by variety inclusion, while Proposition 1 gives a_G(n)<=C n |Q|^n. Equation (4) therefore proves the full integer limit |G/M| for G.

If Q=1, then G=M=C_p and the elementary abelian calculation already gives limit 1=|Q|. This handles the omitted boundary case.

## 4. Concrete modular test case

The normal Klein four subgroup M of S_4 is a 2-dimensional vector space over F_2. Its conjugation image is GL_2(F_2), of order 6, and the action is faithful and irreducible; C_{S_4}(M)=M. Thus the theorem proves that the root limit for var(S_4) is 6. This is a modular example (2 divides 6); imposing a coprimality restriction would needlessly lose it. The forthcoming exact check constructs the generating-pair modules for GL_2(F_2) and verifies the cyclic-vector rank, supplementing but not replacing the all-r proof.

## Outcome and remaining gap

The least-order-counterexample reduction from attempt 1 sharpens: if such a counterexample G has abelian monolith M, then C_G(M) must strictly contain M. The argument does not prove convergence or integrality when the action of G/M on M has a nontrivial kernel. It also does not prove that the potential is invariant under replacing a general extension by its split section.
