# Turn 3: complete lattice-translate certificates for two nonsymmetric trapezoids

Third substantive turn. Original unresolved. For each of the two polygons

    K_m=conv{(0,0),(m,0),(1,1),(0,1)},    m=2 or3,

we prove the stronger two-color statement for ALL finite families of INTEGER translates: if every translate from the first family meets every translate from the second, then one family can be pierced by three points. The proof is a finite exact certificate with a justified reduction from the infinite lattice, not merely a bounded-window experiment. No conclusion for arbitrary real translates or for general convex bodies follows. Both polygons are nonsymmetric quadrilaterals, so this is not just a rebranding of the credited triangle/centrally-symmetric cases.

## 1. Why finitely many normalized center types suffice

Let D_m=K_m-K_m. Pick one translate from the second nonempty family, and shift every translation vector by its integer center so that this center becomes0. Every first-family center then lies in

    U_m=D_m∩Z².

Repetitions do not affect intersection or piercing and may be discarded. The sets U_2 and U_3 have13 and17 points, respectively. Hence the entire first family, regardless of its original cardinality or location, corresponds to a subset S⊂U_m.

If S is non-three-pierceable, it contains an inclusion-minimal non-three-pierceable subset T. Each such T has piercing number exactly4: deleting one center leaves a three-pierceable family, and one additional point covers the deleted translate. The arrangement-mask algorithm in turn1 computes every subset's exact piercing number and identifies precisely these critical T.

Every possible second-family center belongs to

    C(T)={v∈Z² : t-v∈D_m for every t∈T}.

Since T is nonempty and T⊂D_m, central symmetry and convexity imply C(T)⊂(2D_m)∩Z²=:V_m. Indeed choose t∈T and write v=t-d with d∈D_m. Thus the common-neighbor search also has a proved finite bound. V_2 and V_3 have39 and53 points. It is not permissible to intersect C(T) with U_m and ignore possible exterior points; the certificate tests the whole V_m.

## 2. Exact certificate and theorem

LATTICE_CERTIFICATE.json records the integer lists U_m,V_m, every critical mask T in the lexicographically ordered U_m, and its entire common-neighbor list. The complete exact enumeration gives:

- m=2:8192 subsets,576 non-three-pierceable subsets,3 minimal critical subsets
- m=3:131072 subsets,31052 non-three-pierceable subsets,113 minimal critical subsets

For every one of the116 critical subsets, C(T) is the singleton{(0,0)}. This is verified by rational point-in-polygon tests against every element of V_m; there is no numerical tolerance.

Therefore, if the first family is not three-pierceable, every second-family center equals0. All its sets coincide and have a one-point transversal. This proves the stated two-color theorem for all integer translates of K_2 and K_3. The original three-color lattice-restricted conclusion follows by applying the theorem to any two nonempty colors; empty colors are trivial.

The conclusion is invariant under a common invertible affine change of coordinates: it also holds for translates of A(K_m) whose centers lie in a translate of the lattice A(Z²), with the same A. It does not cover arbitrary fine grids while keeping the original body fixed; that changes the body's coordinates relative to the lattice.

## 3. Reproducibility and limits

The checker reconstructs the difference polygons and all integer points in their exact bounding boxes, generates rational arrangement coverage masks, and uses the recurrence

    τ(S)=1+min{τ(S\M): M is a coverage mask containing a fixed pivot of S}

with τ(∅)=0. This recurrence is exhaustive because any optimal cover must cover the pivot with some candidate point, and turn1 proves candidate completeness. Dominated masks may be removed because replacing one by a superset never worsens a cover. Memoization changes runtime, not the set of states considered.

It tests all one-element deletions to identify critical subsets, verifies that each non-three-pierceable subset contains a recorded critical one, and reconstructs every common-neighbor set from the larger V_m. The JSON certificate is regenerated byte-for-byte, with its digest reported in TURN_3_CHECKS.json. There is no search cap. Counts and small integer coordinates are exact, and boundary touching is included.

This is a complete theorem for the declared discrete translation classes. The lattice restriction is essential to what has been proved; the source quantifies over all real translations.
