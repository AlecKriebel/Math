# Final result: original unresolved after five turns

The source asks for a finitely presented group with a cyclic subgroup that is unbounded but distorted in its finite-normal-generator word norm, or unbounded without detection by an ordinary homogeneous real quasimorphism. No such counterexample is constructed here, and no universal theorem for all finitely presented groups is proved.

Scoped results:
1. Every d-generated metabelian group has derived subgroup of normal-word diameter at most d(d+1), with no assumption that the derived subgroup is finitely generated. Its normal metric differs by a bounded additive error from abelianization.
2. Every finitely generated virtually metabelian group has bounded derived subgroup and both dichotomies. All finite-index arguments use the ambient norm.
3. Both dichotomies pass from Q to any finitely generated split extension M semidirect Q with M abelian. The kernel [G,M] has norm bound2d and the quotient is the direct product of coinvariants and Q. No corresponding claim for every nonsplit extension.
4. An exact terminating rational linear program computes stable norms and decides cyclic boundedness for a finite presentation supplied with a valid bounded-derived-subgroup hypothesis. It does not recognize that hypothesis or apply to arbitrary presentations.
5. Every finite-range braid-shift presentation G_K admits a finite braid quotient whose credited homogeneous signature detects the proposed word w. In the infinite-distance-relation version, powers of the same word are bounded by42, by the credited fixed-displacer inequality. Neither supplies the original counterexample, and no assertion about other elements of G_K or abstract finite presentability of the limit is made.

Classical commutator/module/transfer/linear-programming tools, BGKM positive classes, Brandenbursky–Kędra/Gambaudo–Ghys signature calculations and Burago–Ivanov–Polterovich displacement are credited. No novelty certification. Author search stops at five turns. Independent full review is required before publication or final disposition.
