# Final result: original finite-cardinality question unresolved, 5/5

The assigned OWR record combines two questions. The first must be read in its original context: it asks about odd finite numbers beyond the already known 3,19,219,..., not merely whether any odd number other than 1 occurs. No new such cardinality is realized or excluded in this packet. The second, many-one/one-one antichain question has a credited affirmative resolution in Richter–Stephan–Zhang (2026), arXiv:2607.06218, whose relevant construction and proof were read. This is prior work, not a new campaign solution. The adjacent OWR question about infinitely many minimal one-one degrees is outside this assigned record.

## Five completed partials

1. Necessary complemented join-semilattice structure; if the fiber has five elements, only the four-atom or paired-two-chain order can occur. Abstract models of every odd size satisfy these axioms, so this does not decide realizability.
2. For a generic finite-alphabet sequence, injective finite-block encodings compare positively exactly by reverse containment of their induced finite partial orders. This embeds all labelled partial orders on a fixed alphabet, preserving joins and conjugation, but does not count the complete fiber.
3. Exact signed-mask comparison modulo finite disagreement for generic oracles, also for generic finite alphabets with chain codes. This constructs an infinite antichain and proves the finite-code families in Turn 2 do not exhaust their fibers.
4. Effective monotone extension on a Cantor cylinder: order compatibility of two arbitrary total truth-table images is equivalent to a positive functional on that cylinder. At a generic point, positive reducibility is exactly existence of such a cylinder. Compactness proves a concrete finite-search algorithm terminates; the semantic hypothesis is not claimed decidable.
5. A quantitative Sperner estimate for reversed-coordinate equations, upgraded to an effective null test using partial-program enumeration. Every Martin-Löf random oracle also has an infinite signed-mask antichain in its truth-table degree. This is a class restriction, not a theorem for every nonrecursive degree.

All five author scripts replay byte-exactly and total 1,449,000 finite assertions. No finite control decides infinite reducibility, genericity or randomness. The written proofs supply those arguments. All scripts use Python's standard library.
