# Turn 4: the joint spectral obstruction and why base expansion is insufficient

**2594 / KOU-21.85. Substantive author turn 4/5. Original implication remains unresolved.**

The transitive-action reduction suggests spectral expansion as an obstruction to repairing a puncture. This turn proves the exact spectral hypothesis needed and gives a self-contained expander-family diagnostic showing why a weaker hypothesis fails. The spectral/intertwiner instability mechanism is classical; no novelty is claimed for it.

## 1. A graph of a partial identification is far from invariant

Let G be generated by a nonempty finite symmetric set S. Let ρ be a transitive action on a finite set Y of size M, let τ be any action on X of size n<M, and let i:X→Y be an injection. Consider the diagonal action on Y×X and its subset

    A={(i(x),x):x∈X},     |A|=n.

Equip functions on Y×X with unnormalized counting ℓ² norm. Let P be the orthogonal projection onto the invariant functions, i.e. averaging separately on each diagonal orbit. Then

    ||1_A−P1_A||² ≥ n/2.                                   (1)

**Proof.** Let O be a diagonal orbit, and let Z⊂X be its projection to X. Then Z is one τ-orbit, of size d≤n. The projection O→Y is surjective because ρ is transitive, hence |O|≥M>d. The fibers of the equivariant map O→Z have a common positive integer cardinality b=|O|/d, so b≥2. Since A has at most one point over each x∈X,

    a_O:=|A∩O|≤d,    a_O/|O|≤1/b≤1/2.

Thus

    ||1_A−P1_A||² = Σ_O (a_O−a_O²/|O|)
                  ≥ (1/2)Σ_O a_O=n/2.

The argument accommodates nontransitive τ and zero intersections. ∎

For s∈S put

    E_s=|{x∈X : ρ(s)i(x)≠i(τ(s)x)}|.

By comparing sA with A under their X-coordinate bijection,

    ||s·1_A−1_A||² = |sA△A| = 2E_s.                       (2)

## 2. The required Poincaré gap is a joint-action gap

Suppose the diagonal action ρ×τ satisfies the Poincaré inequality

    λ ||F−PF||² ≤ (1/|S|)Σ_{s∈S} ||s·F−F||²              (3)

for all functions F. By (1)–(2),

    (1/|S|)Σ_s E_s ≥ λ n/4.                               (4)

Now take X⊂Y, with k=M−n, and use the inclusion as i. If c(g)=c_X(ρ(g)) denotes canonical compression and

    ε=max_{s∈S} d_X(c(s),τ(s)),

then E_s≤εn+k by the compression discrepancy bound of Turn 1. Hence

    ε ≥ λ/4−k/n.                                           (5)

Thus a lower bound λ≥λ₀>0, **uniform over all candidate actions τ on the smaller sets**, supplies a fixed-gap strict-instability witness when k/n→0.

This recovers the familiar shape of the property-(τ)/(T) obstruction when a group has the corresponding uniform finite-action spectral gap and finite transitive actions of unbounded degree. It does not prove that such a group is flexibly stable. That missing global hypothesis is exactly why a non-P-stability witness for a property-(τ) group does not automatically refute KOU-21.85.

More importantly, (3) is a hypothesis about the diagonal action on Y×X, not just the given action on Y. Replacing it by expansion of ρ alone is invalid. The following full construction demonstrates the distinction with one fixed free group.

## 3. A self-contained bounded-degree expander family from four permutations

For every integer M≥2 there exist four permutations σ₁,…,σ₄ of Y=[M] such that every set B⊂Y with 1≤a=|B|≤M/2 satisfies

    max_i |σ_i(B)\B| ≥ a/100.                              (6)

**Probabilistic proof.** Choose the four permutations independently and uniformly. Fix B of size a and let ℓ=ceil(99a/100). If |σ_i(B)\B|<a/100, at least ℓ elements of B are mapped into B. Union-bounding over those ℓ elements gives

    Pr(|σ_i(B)\B|<a/100)
       ≤ binom(a,ℓ) (a)_ℓ/(M)_ℓ
       ≤ exp(a H(1/100)) (a/M)^(99a/100),                  (7)

where H(t)=−t log t−(1−t)log(1−t). The falling-factorial bound follows termwise from (a−j)/(M−j)≤a/M. For the binomial bound, write r=a−ℓ≤a/100; the usual estimate binom(a,r)≤exp(aH(r/a)) follows by bounding one term of the binomial distribution by 1, and H is increasing on [0,1/2]. When r=0 the same bound is immediate.

There are at most (eM/a)^a choices of B. Independence and another union bound show that the probability (6) fails for some B is at most

    Σ_{a=1}^{floor(M/2)}
       [ exp(1+4H(1/100)) (a/M)^(74/25) ]^a
       ≤ Σ_{a≥1} β^a,

    β=exp(1+4H(1/100)) 2^(−74/25) < 1/2.                 (8)

Here is an explicit verification of the strict numerical bound. Since log 100<5 and −(1−t)log(1−t)≤t, H(1/100)<3/50. Therefore exp(1+4H)<exp(31/25)<exp(5/4)<7/2. Also 74/25>17/6 and 2^(17/6)>7 because 2^17=131072>117649=7^6. Thus β<(7/2)/7=1/2. The elementary exponential bounds can be certified by rational Taylor partial sums and a geometric bound for the positive tail; the accompanying verifier does so.

The geometric sum in (8) is <1. Thus a tuple satisfying (6) exists for every M. If an explicit selection is desired, choose the lexicographically first successful tuple in the finite set Sym(M)^4; the existence proof ensures this finite search terminates. No efficiency claim is made.

Let F₄ be the free group on four generators and map them to σ₁,…,σ₄, obtaining a genuine action ρ_M. The symmetric generating set S contains these four formal generators and their inverses, so |S|=8 even if some image permutations happen to coincide. Condition (6) implies transitivity: otherwise an orbit or its invariant complement supplies a nonempty invariant set of size ≤M/2.

For every B with 0<|B|≤M/2,

    (1/8)Σ_{s∈S}|ρ_M(s)B\B| ≥ |B|/400.                   (9)

Indeed, each permutation and its inverse have equal outgoing-boundary cardinality, so (6) contributes at least 2|B|/100 to the sum.

## 4. The base actions have a uniform spectral gap

For completeness, (9) implies

    (1/8)Σ_{s∈S} ||s·f−f||²
         ≥ (1/160000) ||f−mean(f)||².                     (10)

Here is a short discrete Cheeger proof in the exact normalization used here. Put h=1/400 and d=8. For a nonnegative real function u supported on at most half the vertices, level-set integration of (9) gives

    Σ_{s,x}|u(x)²−u(sx)²| ≥ 2dh Σ_x u(x)².

Cauchy–Schwarz and (a+b)²≤2(a²+b²) bound the square of the left side above by

    [Σ_{s,x}(u(x)−u(sx))²] [4d Σ_x u(x)²].

Therefore the first bracket is at least dh²||u||². For a real function f, subtract a median m and split f−m into its nonnegative positive and negative parts, both supported on at most half the vertices. Their squared gradient energies sum to at most that of f, so the preceding bound gives energy ≥dh²||f−m||²≥dh²||f−mean(f)||². Apply the real result separately to real and imaginary parts if needed. Divide by d to obtain (10).

This proof establishes the infinite family and its uniform base-action gap without extrapolating a finite numerical experiment or appealing to an unverified expander construction.

## 5. Nevertheless every puncture has an exact generator repair

Delete one point from Y, so |X|=n=M−1, and define τ_M on the four free generators by

    τ_M(s_i)=c_X(ρ_M(s_i)).

The universal property of the free group extends this generator assignment to a genuine action of F₄ on X. Compression preserves inverses, so it agrees with the compressed generator permutations on all of S. Its maximum generator-error ε is **zero**, for every M.

For the diagonal action ρ_M×τ_M, (2) has E_s≤1 for every s. By (1), the nonzero vector 1_A−P1_A has squared norm at least n/2, and its average energy is at most 2. Consequently the best Poincaré constant of the diagonal action satisfies

    λ(ρ_M×τ_M) ≤ 4/n → 0.                                 (11)

This occurs despite the base-action gap (10), which remains at least 1/160000. Therefore base expansion alone cannot replace the joint gap in (5).

The whole compressed maps c_X(ρ_M(g)) are asymptotic homomorphisms by Turn 1. They are pointwise close to τ_M: for a fixed word of length ℓ, successive use of their 2/n multiplicativity bound and exact agreement on generators gives error at most 2(ℓ−1)/n (identity and length-one cases are exact). This example is therefore consistent with strict permutation stability of free groups. In fact, for any source challenge of a free group, simply prescribing its generator values gives exact actions pointwise close to that challenge by the same fixed-word argument.

No group constructed in this section separates flexible and strict stability. Its purpose is to refute a proposed shortcut to such a separation using only individual expanding finite actions.

## 6. Credit, scope, and exact remaining issue

The projection estimate (1) is the permutation form of Becker–Lubotzky Proposition 2.4(ii). Their Theorem 4.1 and its following warning already explain why a spectral gap only along one chosen family of finite-index subgroups is insufficient, and give a free-group congruence example. The four-permutation argument above is an independently checkable alternative diagnostic of that known distinction. The deletion/intertwiner spectral mechanism belongs to this established literature: Becker–Lubotzky, *Group stability and Property (T)*, JFA 278 (2020), 108298, [arXiv:1809.00632](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00632), and the one-point compression discussion in Becker–Chapman's 2023 paper. The counting projection proof above and the elementary random-permutation diagnostic are supplied in full for checkability, without a novelty claim.

The finite verifier checks the projection occupancy bound and exact energy identity for small diagonal permutation actions, the rational constants in (8), and explicit bounded-size examples. It does not infer a uniform spectral gap from floating-point eigenvalues, nor claim the full group has property (τ).

**Outcome:** an exact finite-instance spectral obstruction and a complete reason why ordinary expansion cannot supply it. **Remaining gap:** produce a globally flexibly stable group with a uniform joint-gap obstruction, or prove that flexible stability always allows the required low-error transitive repairs. Neither is established. One substantive author turn remains.
