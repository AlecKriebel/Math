## 1. The first dyadically split leading coefficients

For a=2 or a=−2, all even b are already handled by Turn 1. If b is odd, v_2(a)=1>2v_2(b)=0, so the quadratic discriminant element is a square in the dyadic algebra. A new global argument is needed.

Every such integer quadratic ax²+bx+c is equivalent, by an integer translation and possibly x -> −x, to

    a x²+x+(1−N)/(4a),       N=b²−4ac == 1 (mod 8).       (1)

Indeed choose epsilon=±1 with epsilon==b mod 4 and k=(epsilon−b)/(2a) in Z. Substitution x=epsilon y+k makes the linear coefficient epsilon(2ak+b)=1, preserves the leading coefficient a and the discriminant N, and hence gives (1). Conversely every integer N congruent to one modulo eight gives an integer constant in (1). Thus this parameterization is exact, with no missed b residue class.

The norm from Turn 3 becomes

    D_+(N)=N^5+8192N−32768       for a=2,
    D_−(N)=N^5+8192N+32768       for a=−2.                 (2)

A negative or nonsquare integer value in (2) proves global irreducibility. A square value would only be a necessary condition for reducibility, not a sufficient one.

## 2. Complete infinite-band theorem

**Theorem.** Let a be 2 or −2, let b be odd, and let c be an integer. If

    b²−4ac=n²+8r,    n a positive odd integer,
    r in {−1,0,1},                                         (3)

then f(ax²+bx+c) is irreducible over Q.

These are infinite families of integer substitutions inside the complementary class of Turn 1. The statement includes all square discriminants N in this class and the two adjacent offsets by eight. It is not a claim about every nonsquare N congruent to one modulo eight.

### 2.1 The exact approximate square

For n positive and r in {−1,0,1}, put

    P_r(n)=n^5+20r n³+120r² n.

For either sign in (2), direct expansion gives

    D_±(n²+8r)=P_r(n)²+E_±(n,r),                          (4)

where

    E_±(n,r)=320r³ n^4+(6080r^4+8192)n²
              +32768r^5+65536r ∓32768.                   (5)

For n>=200 and these three r, the following bounds are elementary and uniform:

    P_r(n)>0,
    2P_r(n)−1 > 399n^4,
    |E_±(n,r)| < 321n^4.                                 (6)

For example P_r(n)>=n^5−20n³, so

    (2P_r(n)−1)/n^4 >= 2n−40/n−1/n^4 >399.

For the residual, its leading absolute coefficient is at most 320, its n² coefficient at most 14272, and the constant absolute value at most 131072. Thus

    |E_±(n,r)|/n^4 <=320+14272/n²+131072/n^4 <321.

The residual is strictly negative when r=−1 and strictly positive when r=0 or 1 in this range. For r=−1, (5) is −320n^4+14272n² minus a positive constant, so it is negative. For r=0 it is 8192n²∓32768, positive for n>=200; r=1 has all coefficients positive.

Consequently (4) lies strictly between (P_r−1)² and P_r² when r=−1, and strictly between P_r² and (P_r+1)² when r is zero or one. It is not a square for any n>=200. These inequalities are an analytic infinite-tail proof, not sampled bounds.

## 3. Further sign scope and the remaining equation

In the a=−2 case, D_−(−7)<0, so all admissible N<=−7 do. These additional sign ranges are stated separately from the three bands.
