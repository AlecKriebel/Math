## A self-contained bounded-cover theorem
Fix N and a finite extension K/Q_3 of degree at most D. Any subgroup Γ of GL_N(K) with relatively compact closure has a torsion-free normal subgroup of index at most 3^(D N^2). This assertion concerns the abstract group Γ, with no finite-generation hypothesis.

Proof. A compact subgroup C preserves an O_K-lattice: start with O_K^N, take the O_K-span of its C-translates, and use compactness to sandwich this span between two scalar multiples of O_K^N. It is finitely generated, full rank and invariant. Choose a lattice basis, so C lies in GL_N(O_K). Let U be the kernel of reduction modulo 3O_K. The quotient has at most |O_K/3O_K|^(N^2)=3^([K:Q_3]N^2) elements.

To prove U torsion-free, suppose A=I+3B is nonidentity of finite order. A power has prime order ℓ. Write A=I+E and let s be the minimum normalized 3-adic valuation of its nonzero matrix entries, with v(3)=1; then s≥1. If ℓ≠3, the first term ℓE in (I+E)^ℓ−I has valuation s and every later term has valuation at least2s>s. If ℓ=3, 3E has valuation1+s, while 3E² and E³ have valuations at least1+2s and3s, both strictly larger than1+s. Choose an entry of E of valuation s: it cannot cancel. Thus A^ℓ≠I, contradiction. Intersect U with Γ.

## Consequence for the source problem
Let Γ_i be lattices in one fixed higher-rank simple linear real G, with volumes v_i→∞. Suppose each Γ_i admits a faithful abstract representation into GL_N(K_i), with relatively compact image and [K_i:Q_3]≤D, for fixed N,D. Then the source conclusion holds for this family, even when Γ_i has torsion.

Indeed choose torsion-free normal H_i≤Γ_i with m_i≤M=3^(DN²). Its volume is w_i=m_i v_i→∞. The prior FMW theorem gives (d(H_i)−1)/w_i→0. A finite group of order m is generated by at most floor(log_2 m) elements: adjoining a missing generator at least doubles the subgroup order. Lifting such generators of Γ_i/H_i yields
(d(Γ_i)−1)/v_i ≤ m_i (d(H_i)−1)/w_i + floor(log_2 m_i)/v_i →0.
This is a deduction from FMW and an elementary congruence argument, not a novel solution of its torsion-free theorem.

In particular every sequence of finite-index subgroups of one fixed linear lattice Λ is covered: Selberg's lemma gives a fixed torsion-free normal Λ_0, and Γ_i∩Λ_0 has uniformly bounded index in Γ_i. This latter deduction needs no common local representation. It does not cover arbitrary lattices in different commensurability classes.

## Remaining obstacle
Neither compactness at some local place nor a bound depending on the individual lattice provides fixed N,D. As arithmetic defining fields vary their degrees can grow. Quantitative Selberg bounds cited in SOURCE_GATE are precisely nonuniform in this sense. The argument therefore supplies a genuine family result and does not establish the original conjecture.
