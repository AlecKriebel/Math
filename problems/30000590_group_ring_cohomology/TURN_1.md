# Author turn 1: finite-index reduction and the dual-cochain obstruction

**Scoped algebraic results, original unresolved; 1/5 substantive turns.**
The standard finite-index, projective-resolution and coherence machinery is
credited. This turn makes the equivariance and the remaining kernel problem
explicit; it does not claim a new general finite-generation theorem.

Write R_G=ZG. Group cohomology uses the left regular module; the commuting
right action makes H^i(G;R_G) a right R_G-module. Type FP means a finite-length
resolution of the trivial left module by finitely generated projectives.

## 1. Passing to a finite-index subgroup preserves the exact question

**Theorem.** If H≤G has finite index, then there is an isomorphism of right
ZH-modules

    Res_H H^i(G;ZG) ≅ H^i(H;ZH),                        (1)

where the left side is restriction of the natural right action. In particular,
H*(G;ZG) is finitely generated as a right ZG-module if and only if H*(H;ZH)
is finitely generated as a right ZH-module.

The subgroup need not be normal, and no division by its index is used.

### Bimodule and cochain proof

Let pr_H:ZG→ZH discard coefficients outside H. Define

    Phi:ZG→Hom_ZH(ZG,ZH),
    Phi(a)(g)=pr_H(g a).                                (2)

The Hom uses left ZH-linearity on the first argument. Its left G-action is
(k·f)(g)=f(gk), and its right H-action is multiplication on values. Then

    Phi(k a)(g)=Phi(a)(gk),
    Phi(a h)(g)=Phi(a)(g) h,       h in H.               (3)

The second equality uses pr_H(bh)=pr_H(b)h. Thus Phi is a (ZG,ZH)-bimodule
homomorphism. It is an isomorphism when [G:H] is finite: choose representatives
r for left H-cosets H\G. A left-H-linear function is specified by its finitely
many values at r. For basis element a in G, there is exactly one representative
r with r a in H, and the nonzero value of Phi(a) there is r a. This gives a
bijection between the integral bases G and the pairs (r,h), h in H. Equivalently,
one recovers a coefficient h at r by the element r^(−1)h.

Take any projective left ZG-resolution P_*→Z. Restriction to H is a projective
ZH-resolution, since ZG is free as a left ZH-module. Coinduction adjunction
and (2) give a cochain isomorphism, respecting the right H-action,

    Hom_ZG(P_*,ZG)
       ≅ Hom_ZG(P_*,Hom_ZH(ZG,ZH))
       ≅ Hom_ZH(Res_H P_*,ZH).

Explicitly, the second map evaluates an equivariant function at the identity
in G; its inverse sends psi(p) to the function g↦psi(gp). This proves (1).
It is the finite-index Eckmann–Shapiro argument with its commuting action
retained, rather than an unequivariant isomorphism of abelian groups.

For any right ZG-module M, finite generation over ZH implies finite generation
over ZG. Conversely, if M is generated over ZG by m_1,...,m_s and g_1,...,g_d
represent the cosets G/H, then the finitely many m_i g_j generate M over ZH.
Applying this to (1) proves the assertion for the total graded cohomology.

For a virtually-FP G, choose its FP finite-index subgroup H. If its finite
resolution has length n, (1) also proves H^i(G;ZG)=0 for i>n. Thus the original
question is exactly equivalent to the torsion-free type-FP case and has no
extra infinite-degree difficulty hidden in the notation H*.

## 2. What finite resolutions do and do not imply

Let H be type FP with a projective resolution P_* of length n. The dual complex

    C^i=Hom_ZH(P_i,ZH)

consists of finitely generated projective right modules. The natural map
P_i→P_i** is an isomorphism for such projectives, but this alone does not make
the cohomology modules finitely generated over a noncoherent ring.

**Top degree.** H^n(H;ZH)=coker(C^(n−1)→C^n) is finitely presented. A quotient
of a finitely generated projective module by a finitely generated submodule
has a finite presentation. All higher cohomology is zero.

H^0(H;ZH) is also finitely generated: if H is infinite, an invariant element
of the regular module with finite support must be zero; if H is finite, the
invariants are generated by the norm element sum_(h∈H)h. In the type-FP
integral finite-group case H must in fact be trivial, but that restriction
is not needed for this degree-zero observation. In particular, a virtually-FP
group whose FP subgroup has a resolution of length at most one satisfies the
original conclusion.

**Coherent-ring sufficient condition.** If ZH is right coherent, all the
cohomology modules of C^* are finitely presented, hence the original answer
is affirmative for G.

Here right coherence means that every finitely generated right submodule
of a finite free module is finitely presented; the equivalent statement for
finitely generated projective ambient modules follows by adding a complement.
The image B^(i+1) of C^i→C^(i+1) is finitely generated, so is finitely presented.
Consequently the kernel Z^i of that map is finitely generated: a kernel of
an epimorphism from a finitely generated projective onto a finitely presented
module is finitely generated. Coherence then makes Z^i finitely presented.
Finally H^i=Z^i/B^i is a quotient by a finitely generated submodule and is
finitely presented. There are finitely many degrees; (1) gives the required
finite generation for G. Right Noetherian rings are a sufficient special case.
This is a conditional ring-theoretic theorem, not an assertion that arbitrary
integral group rings are coherent or Noetherian.

## 3. A precise two-degree test for the obstruction

For any bounded cochain complex C^0→...→C^n of finitely generated projective
right modules over any ring R, with n≥1,

    H^(n−1)(C) is finitely generated
      iff H^n(C) is of type FP_2 as an R-module.          (4)

For a module, FP_2 means there is a projective resolution with finitely
generated terms in degrees0,1,2. This condition is stronger than a finite
presentation, which is FP_1.

To prove (4), put B=im(C^(n−1)→C^n) and Z=ker(C^(n−1)→C^n). The two exact
sequences

    0→B→C^n→H^n→0,
    0→Z→C^(n−1)→B→0

show that H^n is FP_2 iff B is finitely presented iff Z is finitely generated.
These syzygy characterizations are independent of the chosen finitely
generated projective presentation. One proof is Schanuel's lemma: for two
projective epimorphisms onto the same module, their kernels become isomorphic
after adding the other projective source. Repeating this once gives the
FP_2 statement; finite generation and finite presentation are stable under
finite direct sums and direct summands. The forward implications can also
be obtained by splicing a finite projective presentation of the kernel.

The boundary module im(C^(n−2)→C^(n−1)) is finitely generated (zero when n=1).
The exact sequence from this boundary to Z to H^(n−1) shows that Z is finitely
generated iff H^(n−1) is: the quotient direction is immediate, and in the
other direction lift finitely many quotient generators and adjoin finitely
many boundary generators. This proves (4).

Thus a counterexample in the degree just below a chosen top resolution
degree would be witnessed by a top cohomology module that is finitely
presented but not FP_2. A computation of a quotient presentation alone
would miss precisely this possible failure. No such group has been found
in this turn.

## 4. An explicit finite-complex counter-control, not a group counterexample

It is unsafe to infer finite generation of cohomology from the finite
projective terms alone. Here is a finitely generated ring where that argument
fails. Let

    R=Z<x,y>/(x y^a x : a≥0).

The quotient by this monomial ideal is free as an abelian group on the normal
words

    y^a,                 a≥0,
    y^a x y^b,           a,b≥0.                         (5)

Indeed a word is killed exactly when it contains two occurrences of x;
between two successive x's are only y's. Distinct surviving monomials stay
linearly independent in a quotient by a monomial ideal.

Consider the two-term complex of free right R-modules

    C^0=R  --left multiplication by x-->  C^1=R.         (6)

The map is right R-linear. It sends the distinct y^a to the distinct nonzero
x y^a, and kills every normal word containing x. Hence

    H^0(C)=I=direct sum_(a≥0) y^a x Z[y].                (7)

Right multiplication by a normal word without x changes only the final
power of y, and right multiplication by a normal word with x annihilates
this ideal. It never changes the initial exponent a. Therefore any finite
set of elements of I generates a submodule supported on only finitely many
initial exponents. Choosing an omitted exponent proves that I is not
finitely generated as a right R-module.

Meanwhile H^1(C)=R/xR is cyclic and finitely presented, with its single
relation supplied by x. By (4) it is not FP_2. The complex therefore exhibits
the exact coherent-kernel obstruction in the smallest possible example.

This ring is not asserted to be ZG for any group. Nor is (6) the dual of an
FP-resolution of the trivial module of a group. In particular it is NOT a
counterexample to Davis's question. It shows only that a proof using finite
projective cochains, without additional group-specific input, has a genuine
missing step. A group realization of such a kernel would require a new
mechanism.

## 5. Remaining routes

Finite-index torsion and degree conventions are now removed. The top degree
and coherent-ring cases are controlled, but kernels in intermediate degrees
of the group-specific dual resolution remain. The known Coxeter, RAAG and
acyclic Bestvina–Brady results are credited; their finite equivariant
filtrations do not extend automatically to arbitrary FP groups.

The next substantive route should seek a group-specific source of a bad
kernel, for example in a graph-of-groups or mapping-torus resolution, while
checking the full finite-length FP property and the natural right module
action. Merely inserting an arbitrary noncoherent ring example or confusing
infinite abelian rank with infinite ZG-generation cannot settle the target.

The finite controls check the nonnormal finite-index pairing/equivariance and
the monomial-ring normal forms. They support the written algebra and are not
a finite search over all groups or a resolution of the remaining question.
