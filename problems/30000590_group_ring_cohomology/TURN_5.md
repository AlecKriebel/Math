# Author turn 5: a bad finite matrix over an actual good FP group ring

## 1. What this turn establishes

There is a concrete right-module map

\[
(\mathbb ZG)^3\longrightarrow\mathbb ZG,
\qquad G=F(a,b)\times F(c,d),                                    \tag{1}
\]

whose kernel is not finitely generated. Nevertheless G has finitely generated total regular-coefficient cohomology. Thus the coherence hypothesis of turn 1 is genuinely restrictive even among positive examples for the original question. The matrix in (1) does **not** occur as the indicated differential in the dual of an augmentation resolution of G; using its cohomology as H*(G;ZG) would be invalid.

The construction is a height-kernel example in a product of free groups, the standard Bestvina--Brady/Stallings--Bieri setting. Its elementary homological obstruction is proved below rather than attributed a novelty claim. The ambient positive right-angled-Artin case is also credited in the bound Davis--Okun source. This final substantive turn leaves the original question unresolved after five turns.

## 2. The three-generator height kernel

Put A=F(a,b), B=F(c,d), G=A times B. Let chi:G->Z send all four free generators to 1, and put H=ker(chi). Define

\[
t=ac^{-1},\quad u=ba^{-1},\quad v=dc^{-1}.                       \tag{2}
\]

For the height map on A alone, its kernel K_1 is free on

\[
u_i=a^i b a^{-(i+1)},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Indeed, the infinite cyclic cover of the two-petal rose has vertices indexed by Z and a- and b-edges from i to i+1. Its a-edges form a spanning tree; the remaining b-edges give exactly these free generators. Similarly K_2 is free on

\[
v_j=c^j d c^{-(j+1)},\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The first-factor height map H->Z splits via t. If (w,z) is in H and the height of w is s, then

\[
(w,z)=(w a^{-s},z c^s)t^s.
\]

Consequently

\[
H=(K_1\times K_2)\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
\quad t u_i t^{-1}=u_{i+1},\quad t v_j t^{-1}=v_{j-1}.            \tag{3}
\]

In particular t,u_0,v_0 generate H. The two opposite shifts, rather than two shifts of the same sign, are important below.

## 3. An explicit infinite-rank H_2, hence failure of FP_2

The product of two countable roses is a K(K_1 times K_2,1): its universal cover is the product of trees. Its integral cellular homology is

\[
H_1(K_1\times K_2;\mathbb Z)
 =\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z u_i\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_j\mathbb Z v_j,
\qquad
H_2(K_1\times K_2;\mathbb Z)=\bigoplus_{i,j}\mathbb Z e_{i,j}.
\]

The automorphism (3) is realized by permuting the oriented loops of these roses. Its mapping torus is a K(H,1): the infinite cyclic cover is the product of the original aspherical space with R, and the universal cover is contractible. The mapping-cone chain complex of 1 minus this cellular automorphism gives the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(K_1\times K_2;\mathbb Z)_{\langle t\rangle}
\longrightarrow H_2(H;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(K_1\times K_2;\mathbb Z)^{\langle t\rangle}
\longrightarrow0.                                             \tag{4}
\]

This is equivalently the two-column homological Hochschild--Serre sequence for the infinite cyclic quotient. The mapping-torus derivation just given supplies (4) directly.

Every chain here is a finite linear combination. Thus the H_1 invariant term is zero: a nonzero coefficient invariant under either bilateral shift would have infinite support. On H_2 the action is

\[
e_{i,j}\longmapsto e_{i+1,j-1}.
\]

Its orbits are exactly the sets with fixed i+j. The coinvariants of a free permutation module have one free integral generator per orbit. Explicitly, the sum of coefficients on each orbit gives the quotient map; a finite-support coefficient vector with zero sum on an orbit is a finite sum of adjacent differences along that orbit. Therefore

\[
H_2(H;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z.         \tag{5}
\]

In particular H is not of type FP_2 over Z. If a partial projective resolution through degree 2 had finitely generated terms, tensoring it with the trivial module Z would give finitely generated abelian groups through degree 2. Its H_2 is a quotient of a subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group, hence finitely generated, contrary to (5). This applies equally to right-module and left-module FP_2 by the group-ring inversion anti-isomorphism.

## 4. The explicit matrix and faithful induction

Let S=ZH and R=ZG. Because t,u,v generate H, the augmentation ideal I_H of the right S-module S is generated by t-1,u-1,v-1. This follows from

\[
hk-1=(h-1)k+(k-1),\qquad h^{-1}-1=-(h-1)h^{-1}.
\]

There is an exact right S-module sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow K_H\longrightarrow S^3
 \xrightarrow{\delta_H}S\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0,
\quad
\delta_H(x,y,z)=(t-1)x+(u-1)y+(v-1)z.                            \tag{6}
\]

The kernel K_H cannot be finitely generated: a finite free module surjecting onto it would extend (6) to a finite-type projective resolution through degree 2, contradicting Section 3.

As a left S-module R is free on coset representatives and contains an S summand. Hence it is flat and tensoring with it detects zero right S-modules. Inducing (6) gives

\[
K_R=K_H\otimes_S R
 =\ker\big[R^3\xrightarrow{(t-1,\ u-1,\ v-1)}R\big].            \tag{7}
\]

Moreover K_R is not finitely generated over R. To see the finite-generation descent directly, suppose finitely many tensors generate it. Choose all finitely many K_H-coordinates occurring in expressions for those tensors, and let K_0 be their right S-span. Then (K_H/K_0) tensor_S R=0. Faithfulness of the left free module R implies K_H/K_0=0, a contradiction.

Thus (7) is the promised explicit finite matrix over the group ring of an actual FP group. It also proves that R is not right coherent. The two-term cochain complex R^3->R has non-finitely-generated H^0, while

\[
H^1=R/I_HR\cong\mathbb Z\otimes_S R=\mathbb Z[H\backslash G]
\]

is cyclic and finitely presented but not FP_2. The last assertion is also the top-syzygy equivalence in turn 1; here it follows directly from the finite presentation (7) and Schanuel's lemma.

## 5. The ambient group still satisfies the original conclusion

A two-petal rose is a finite K(A,1), and its regular-coefficient cochain complex is

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb ZA
 \xrightarrow{r\mapsto((a-1)r,(b-1)r)}(\mathbb ZA)^2
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Its H^0 vanishes, since an invariant finite-support group-ring element for the infinite group A is zero. Its sole nonzero cohomology D_A=H^1(A;ZA) is generated by two elements over ZA. It is torsion-free as an abelian group: if a positive integer m times a cochain z equals delta(r), then r mod m is an A-invariant element of (Z/m)A, necessarily zero by finite support. Write r=ms; the free abelian cochain group then gives z=delta(s). The same reasoning applies to B.

The integral product formula of turn 3 consequently has no Tor term, and gives

\[
H^i(G;\mathbb ZG)=0\ (i\ne2),\qquad
H^2(G;\mathbb ZG)\cong D_A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}D_B.
\]

This right ZG-module is generated by four tensors. G has a finite K(G,1), the product of the two finite roses, so it is FP with a finite-length free resolution. Thus the bad kernel (7) occurs over a group ring whose own regular cohomology is a positive example, not an original counterexample.

## 6. Why finite preceding boundaries do not repair this matrix

Keep the differential in (7) fixed. Any preceding finitely generated projective term in a cochain complex has a finitely generated image B contained in K_R. The quotient K_R/B cannot be finitely generated: if it were, lifts of its finite generators together with finite generators of B would generate K_R. Thus adding only finitely generated preceding boundaries cannot eliminate this failure of finite generation while keeping that matrix.

This is a restriction on an attempted realization mechanism. It does not say that every finite matrix can occur in a dual augmentation resolution, or that changing the differential cannot help. The exact original obstruction is still to find a group whose **own** finite projective resolution of Z dualizes to a non-finitely-generated cohomology module, or to prove that the augmentation-resolution constraints always prevent this. Neither has been achieved here.

## 7. Five-turn disposition

All five substantive author turns are used. The packet proves finite-index and syzygy reductions; positive coherent-ring, ascending-HNN, free-product, flat-product and duality-quotient-extension cases; exact Tor/Bockstein criteria; and the actual-group-ring failed-shortcut control above. It gives no counterexample or proof for an arbitrary virtually-FP group. The proposed disposition is **unsolved, 5/5**, subject to independent review. No sixth proof-search turn is taken.
