# Five-turn result: opposite vertices of base polyhedra

**Original unresolved,5/5 author turns complete.** Independent full review pending. No novelty or priority certification; no sixth author search.

The exact Egres question requires both v and−v to be vertices, not merely feasible points. It permits v=0. The archived page's2011 open status is not treated as a current proof of openness. Imported research was only OPEN-TRIAGE and is credited.

Scoped results:
1. A maximal zero-set chain reduces to Cartesian base factors with positive proper canonical ranks. Complete enumeration then proves the conjecture for every ground set of size at most5, and for arbitrary dimensions whose reduced blocks all have size at most5. The five-element search covers all1,048,576 possible reduced tables, of which4,209 are submodular.
2. The conjecture holds for all integral root-direction zonotopes, including zonotopal bases, in every dimension. Cube widths force weighted graph degree at most2, and paths/cycles/doubled edges yield explicit opposite vertices.
3. It holds for bases with a laminar integer upper-constraint representation together with cube bounds and total zero. Nested zero bounds and odd residual atoms are handled; both signs have exact active-constraint vertex certificates.
4. It holds for translated sums of coordinate simplices. Widths and0 membership force a tree/unicyclic dual incidence graph, providing two explicit exposing weight vectors. This includes non-zonotopal coverage bases, but not all submodular bases.
5. Tight partitions give a self-contained bipartite-forest criterion for vertices of intersections of two bases, and an integrality proof. At maximum-support symmetric feasible integer points the zero-coordinate residual graph is a forest. Nontrivial trees can still prevent an endpoint from being a vertex of the original base. An explicit four-coordinate example refutes that shortcut while satisfying the original conjecture by another pair.

The general conjecture, Frank's related2-polymatroid question and the stronger g-polymatroid variant remain unresolved. No reduction to the special decompositions above is asserted. A reduced minimum-size counterexample, if it exists, must have at least6 coordinates and lie outside these classes.
