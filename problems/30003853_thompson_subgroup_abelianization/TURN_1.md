# Turn 1: the complete restricted-wreath obstruction

Completed 2026-10-02 UTC. Original target unresolved, 1/5 substantive turns. Subjective progress toward the original question: 5%, not a probability of correctness.

## 1. A credited splitting input

Use Bieri–Geoghegan–Kochloukova, Theorem 2.7 (arXiv:0807.5138v1, p6), attributed there to Bieri–Strebel Theorem A. If G has type FP_2(R), where R is any commutative ring with 1 != 0, and has no nonabelian free subgroup, any nonzero discrete character gives an ascending HNN decomposition over a finitely generated subgroup K of its kernel. The stable letter maps to a generator of the character image. In particular, if the kernel is nontrivial, K is nontrivial, and one of the two conjugations by the stable letter sends K into K. We use the published input, not a new splitting theorem.

## 2. All FP_2 subgroups of an abelian restricted wreath product

**Theorem.** Let d >= 1 be finite, B = direct-sum over i in Z of Z^d, and W_d = B semidirect Z, with the generator translating lamp indices. For any nonzero commutative ring R, every subgroup H <= W_d of type FP_2(R) is a finitely generated free abelian group. More precisely, H contained in B is free abelian of finite rank; otherwise H is infinite cyclic.

**Proof.** FP_2 implies finite generation. If H <= B, the finitely many generators have finite combined support; hence H is a subgroup of Z^N for finite N, so is free abelian of finite rank.

Otherwise the projection of H to Z is mZ, m >= 1. Normalize this to an epimorphism chi:H -> Z. Write M = H intersect B. If M = 0 then chi is an isomorphism. Suppose M != 0. The group W_d is metabelian, so H has no nonabelian free subgroup. Apply the credited splitting theorem: it gives nontrivial finitely generated K <= M and s in H projecting to +/-m, with s^{-1}Ks <= K after possibly exchanging the direction.

All elements of K have their support in a single finite set S: take the union of supports of a finite generating set, since multiplication within B adds lamp vectors. Conjugation by s on B translates every support by a nonzero displacement +/-m; the base component of s acts trivially because B is abelian. For 0 != k in K, the successive supports of s^{-n}ks^n are nonempty translates by n times this displacement. They cannot all lie in S, contradicting s^{-n}ks^n in K for every n >= 0. Thus M = 0. This proves the theorem. The trivial subgroup is included in the first case. QED.

The same argument works for a torsion-free abelian lamp group A in place of Z^d: a finite support and finitely many lamp entries reduce the first case to a finitely generated torsion-free abelian subgroup. We do not assert the theorem for nonabelian lamps, whose base component can act by conjugation (although support remains a useful invariant).

**Consequence for the exact source route.** No subgroup with nontrivial torsion in its abelianization can acquire finite presentation while staying a subgroup of the same abelian restricted wreath product. This eliminates every such repair of Röver's example, not merely its original three generators. It does not eliminate subgroups of F outside these wreath products.

## 3. The precise torsion in Röver's mechanism

Put L = Z[t,t^{-1}], write epsilon(f)=f(1), and let I_m=(m,t-1) <= L for m >= 1. The group H_m=I_m semidirect <t> is generated by t, the lamp m, and the lamp t-1. At m=2 these are exactly the source's t, s and r, in additive lamp notation. It has index m in Z wr Z because I_m is the kernel of epsilon modulo m.

Since the base is abelian,

    H_m' = (t-1)I_m,
    (H_m)_ab = Z direct-sum I_m/(t-1)I_m.

There is an explicit isomorphism

    I_m/(t-1)I_m -> Z direct-sum Z/m,
    [f] |-> ( f(1)/m, f'(1) modulo m ).

Derivatives are formal Laurent derivatives. This is well-defined: for f=(t-1)g with g in I_m, f(1)=0 and f'(1)=g(1) is divisible by m. It is onto, since m maps to (1,0) and t-1 maps to (0,1). Conversely, if the displayed pair vanishes, f(1)=0, hence f=(t-1)g for a Laurent polynomial g; then g(1)=f'(1) is divisible by m, so g is in I_m. This proves injectivity. The elementary identity ker(epsilon)=(t-1)L follows by multiplying a Laurent polynomial by a power of t and applying ordinary polynomial division; multiplication by a power of t does not affect this ideal.

Consequently

    (H_m)_ab is isomorphic to Z^2 direct-sum Z/m.

All H_m fail FP_2(R) for every nonzero commutative R by Section 2: their projection is nonzero and their base intersection is nontrivial. The case m=1 is W itself and has no abelianization torsion, but still fails FP_2.

## 4. Arbitrary finite abelian torsion, still outside finite presentation

For positive integers m_1,...,m_d, use M = I_{m_1} direct-sum ... direct-sum I_{m_d} <= L^d, and H=M semidirect <t>. The same commutator and derivative calculation gives

    H_ab = Z^{d+1} direct-sum (direct-sum_j Z/m_j).

It is generated by t and the 2d lamps m_j e_j, (t-1)e_j, and has index product_j m_j in W_d. Thus every finite abelian group can occur as precisely the torsion subgroup in a finitely generated example of this form. This is an elementary extension of Röver's credited mechanism, with no novelty claim.

For completeness, W_d embeds in F. Choose a one-bump element t of F and a dyadic interval J in (0,1) whose translates t^i(J) have disjoint interiors. Inside J choose d disjoint dyadic intervals and nontrivial elements a_j of F supported there. All translated lamps commute, and their supports show that any nonzero finite lamp combination is nonidentity. Projection to the translating element supplies an infinite cyclic top: a nonzero power of t cannot have compact support contained in finitely many of these translated intervals. This yields the restricted wreath product. One can take J between a dyadic point c and t(c); the half-open fundamental intervals partition the one-bump orbit interval, with endpoints fixed by every lamp. All constructions use finitely many pieces for each group element.

## 5. Verification and exact gap

verify_turn1.py checks Laurent ideal membership, derivative quotient maps, explicit kernel division, semidirect commutators, and the finite-support translation obstruction on bounded exact integer examples. These are supplementary controls; the arbitrary-ring FP_2 conclusion rests on the stated theorem and proof, not the finite sample.

Nothing here shows that an arbitrary finitely presented subgroup of F lies in an abelian wreath product. Closed/diagram subgroups are a known positive class by Guba–Sapir, but arbitrary subgroups need not be closed or diagram groups. The original question is still open in this packet.
