# Candidate result: common basin boundaries for the exact transfer system

Problem 30001370 / OWR-4132-003. Three substantive author turns used; proof
search is frozen for independent review. Proposed disposition, only if the
full audit passes: claimed_solved 3/5. No historical-priority claim.

For the exact Möbius two-branch system with
G(m)=A tanh(Bm/A), 0<A≤0.4 and 6<B≤16, the central basin of the constant
density is the common relative-L1 boundary of both other basins, on the
space of all probability densities. No regularity or strict-positivity
hypothesis is added to the densities.

## Proof structure

1. TURN_1 proves that the full nonlinear operator is an open surjection,
   with continuous exact sections through every density. The mechanism is
   invertible mean-dependent Möbius transport followed by ordinary doubling.
   Therefore each noncentral basin boundary is completely invariant.
2. TURN_2 identifies the true stable functional of the linearized operator,
   correcting a missing invariant-kernel step in the inspected preprint's
   short hyperbolicity proof. It also proves that stable L1 convergence is
   not uniform. This turn is informative but is NOT required for the final
   nonlinear proof, and does not contradict the source's main theorem.
3. TURN_3 proves that every central-basin density is an L1 limit of finite
   preimages of the constant density. A terminal cumulative-distribution
   correction is lifted backward with its feedback solved at every step.
   Dimension-independent L2 contraction controls the accumulated parameter
   change. Möbius distortion and the bounded source invariant core yield
   a total-variation estimate for the initial law, including densities
   that vanish or are unbounded.
4. The known boundary property at the constant density, credited to
   Bardet–Keller–Zweimüller, now pulls back and passes to the closure.

The uniform inverse bound has a 40-interval rational certificate covering
all the report parameters. The three exact checkers report 60,278, 445 and
211 assertions respectively: total 60,934. These finite controls complement,
but do not replace, the analytic proofs or independent review.
