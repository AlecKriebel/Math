# Turn 3: complete twenty-letter classification

Original classification unresolved3/5. The twenty-letter case is now proved even under strict Hurwitz equivalence. No faithfulness of a representation of G is assumed, and no finite sample is extrapolated to longer words.

## Theorem

Every positive word of length20 that equals c² in G is one of the ten distinct cyclic rotations of h², as a literal word in the five Artin generators. All these factorizations are strictly Hurwitz equivalent to h². Here G,c,h have their exact source definitions from turns1–2.

## 1. A complete finite reduction

Turn2 proves that its pure-braid linking vector is twice a star centered at some labelled strand k. A positive word has no negative crossing cancellations. Thus no two strands other than k can cross; each other strand crosses k exactly four times. Those five noncentral strands keep their relative order throughout the braid diagram.

It follows that the entire word is determined by the position of strand k. Starting at position k, it performs a nearest-neighbor walk on the six positions, returns to k, and crosses each of the five edges of that path exactly four times. Crossing edge i is precisely the generator a_i. Conversely every such walk gives a positive pure braid with the required star-linking vector. At a given edge the noncentral strand encountered is fixed by the order of the five other strands, so edge counts exactly record the five individual crossing counts.

All walks have length20. A depth-first enumeration that tries each of the at most two adjacent positions, rejects an edge only once it has already been used four times, and retains a walk at length20 precisely when it returns to the start, is exhaustive. At length20 every edge count is automatically four because all five counts are at most four. There is no additional cap or pruning assumption.

The counts are81,162,162,162,162,81 for the six starting positions, totaling810 distinct words. These are all the possible positive words of length20 that could represent the target. Necessary linking conditions alone do not declare them equal in G.

## 2. An explicit quotient representation supplies a safe rejection test

Let F4 be freely generated by x1,x2,x3,x4. We use right actions of automorphisms: x acted on by a word uv means first apply u, then v. Define A_i on the ordered generators by the following tuples, with integer j denoting x_j and−j denoting its inverse:

    A1 = (1, 12, 13, 14)
    A2 = (1(-2)1, 1, 3, 4)
    A3 = (1, 2(-3)2, 2, 4)
    A4 = (1, 2, 3(-4)3, 3)
    A5 = (1, 2, 3, 3(-2)14).

For clarity these are words, not numbers or matrix entries. The inverse tuples are

    A1^-1 = (1, (-1)2, (-1)3, (-1)4)
    A2^-1 = (2, 2(-1)2, 3, 4)
    A3^-1 = (1, 3, 3(-2)3, 4)
    A4^-1 = (1, 2, 4, 4(-3)4)
    A5^-1 = (1, 2, 3, (-1)2(-3)4).

Substitution followed by free reduction verifies both inverse compositions. It also verifies all adjacent braid relations, all distant commutations, and A_c A_h^−1=identity, in the right-action convention. These finitely many equalities of finite reduced words prove that a_i↦A_i defines an action of the presented group G. The complete tuples and every relation check appear in the standalone checker. The construction is motivated by the classical lifted braid action on a genus2 surface, but no geometric or injectivity assertion is required: the explicit presentation check is sufficient.

Therefore any word equal to c² in G must have exactly the same four reduced images as c². Failure of this test conclusively rejects a word. Passing it alone would not prove equality, because the action is not asserted faithful.

## 3. Exhaustive test and positive certificates for all survivors

The checker constructs all810 words from §1 and applies the exact F4 test of §2 using only free reduction of integer-letter words. Precisely ten words pass. The retained set is checked for literal equality with

    { cyclic rotations of h² }.

All ten are stored in TURN_3_SURVIVORS.txt, and the full set hash is bound in the receipt. The candidate counts per center and retained counts(1,2,2,2,2,1) are also recorded. This computation is a finite proof obligation, not a probabilistic or bounded-prefix search. The reduction already covers every word in this source length class.

Each survivor has an independent positive group certificate: h²=c²=z in G, where z is central. Moving an initial segment to the end conjugates a word's product, and conjugation fixes z. Consequently every cyclic rotation really does equal c² in G. The necessary representation test has therefore been supplemented by a sufficient certificate for every accepted case.

## 4. Strict Hurwitz equivalence

For a tuple(g1,...,gm) with central product z, successively applying inverse Hurwitz moves that move g1 to the right gives

    (g2,...,gm,(g2...gm)^−1 g1(g2...gm)).

Since g2...gm=g1^−1 z, the last factor equals z^−1 g1 z=g1. This realizes an ordinary cyclic rotation of the tuple by strict Hurwitz moves. Repeating connects all ten rotations of h².

Together with §3, this proves the theorem. In particular, the twenty-letter slice consists of exactly one strict Hurwitz class, and hence one class after adding simultaneous conjugation.

## 5. Verification limits and remaining work

The standard-library checker includes the complete automorphism tables, inverse/relator checks, exhaustive walk recursion, all810 exact image comparisons and the survivor/rotation equality. It is short enough to inspect and independently reimplement. It does not rely on a group solver reporting an undecidable or timed-out word equality as false, and it does not assume the automorphism action faithful.
