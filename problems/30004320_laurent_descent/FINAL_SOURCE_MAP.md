# Exact primary-source dependency map

Raw PDFs are excluded from the public packet. Their exact bytes are bound
by SOURCE_MANIFEST.json and SOURCE_ADDITION_T1/T4/T5.json (11 PDFs total).
SOURCE_ADDITION_T3.md records the additional Stacks pages and carefully
states the limits of their short discussion. All source statements below
refer to the retrieved versions, not to guessed published theorem numbers.

## Original formulation and prior descent

- Philippe Gille, joint with Mathieu Florence, *Residues on Affine
  Grassmannians*, OWR 52/2019, printed 3264–3265, PDF pages 22–23.
  https://ems.press/journals/owr/articles/17295 and
  https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46832 . The complete two-page
  contribution and both rendered pages were read. Theorem 2 gives torsor
  descent; the ensuing question asks for general homogeneous spaces.
  Report year 2019, publisher's publication date 19 November 2020.
- Florence–Gille, full author manuscript of 10 November 2019:
  https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~mathieu.florence/residus11nov.pdf ; and
  arXiv:1910.14509v3, 8 February 2021, PDF dated February 9:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.14509 . Proposition 5.2(3) and Theorem 5.4 of
  the latter imply the constant-torsor descent used in every turn. Relevant
  §§4–5 were read. Publication: J. reine angew. Math. 776 (2021), 119–150,
  DOI 10.1515/crelle-2021-0007. General homogeneous descent is not supplied
  by the torsor statement.
- Florence, *Points rationnels sur les espaces homogènes et leurs
  compactifications*, primary preprint
  https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/lag/man/174.pdf . Its Proposition 4.7
  plus Proposition 5.4 prove homogeneous Laurent descent for every perfect
  field. Lemma 4.8 handles nonreduced geometric stabilizers. The preprint's
  Theorem 5.6 is the subsequent compactification consequence; Lemma 5.5
  alone is a reverse-direction construction and is not our descent citation.
  Published Transformation Groups 11 (2006), 161–176,
  DOI 10.1007/s00031-004-1107-9; published numbering differs.
- Gille, *Wound algebraic groups and their compactifications*,
  https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~gille/prenotes/gille_pisa.pdf , page 30 after
  Theorem 12.4, credits the perfect-field result and identifies the
  imperfect-field case as open. Page 30 was rendered and inspected.
  This is a bounded source-status check, not proof of current global novelty.

## Galois and gerbe foundations

- Temkin, *Tame distillation and desingularization by p-alterations*, Annals
  of Mathematics 186 (2017), 97–126,
  https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v186-n1-p03-p.pdf ,
  §§2.2.3–2.2.6 for tame closure and pro-p wild kernel, including imperfect
  residue fields.
- Brosnan–Reichstein–Vistoli, *Essential dimension of moduli of curves and
  other algebraic stacks*, primary author PDF
  https://math.umd.edu/~pbrosnan/Papers/brv2.pdf . Proposition 5.5, pp.12–13,
  describes finite étale gerbes by profinite group extensions and objects by
  continuous lifts. Proposition 5.6 supplies a section of Gal(k((t)))→Gal(k)
  even for imperfect k. Proposition 5.7 and Remark 5.8 distinguish compatible
  tame sections from arbitrary full sections. Section 4 records abelian
  band/twisting machinery; Theorem 5.10 is tame-gerbe specialization.
- Stacks Project https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/06NY (Lemma 8.11.8)
  supplies the canonical abelian band. The discussion
  https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CJZ is not treated as a full standalone
  proof of the Giraud H2 classification. The standard classification and
  twisting are used with explicit fppf topology and credited accordingly.

## Scheme-theoretic stabilizers and rigidification

- Conrad, *Reductive Group Schemes* (2014),
  https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/luminysga3smf.pdf . Proposition
  2.1.2, pp.56–57: smooth normalizers/transporters for multiplicative-type
  subgroups in smooth groups. Lemma 2.2.4, pp.64–66: smooth centralizer,
  even for a non-smooth multiplicative-type subgroup. Theorem A.1.1, p.267:
  a geometrically maximal torus over the actual field. Definition A.1.5 and
  discussion, pp.268–269, and Theorem 1.1.19(1): connected Cartan centralizers
  and the unipotent quotient used in the envelope argument. Theorem 3.2.6,
  p.94: maximal-torus scheme and étale-local conjugacy for reductive identity;
  Proposition 3.2.8, p.96: finite étale Weyl group. Appendix B supplies
  multiplicative-type character-module facts. Remark 3.1.5, p.83, warns that
  general multiplicative-type subgroups need not be torally contained; no
  such false assertion is used in this packet.
- Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli, *Tame stacks in positive characteristic*,
  Ann. Inst. Fourier 58 (2008), 1057–1091,
  https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2008__58_4_1057_0.pdf . Appendix A,
  Theorem A.1, pp.1087–1089, including its proof: rigidification by a normal
  flat finitely presented inertia subgroup; centrality or finiteness is not
  required. Locally this is BH→B(H/H0).
- AOV corrigendum, Ann. Inst. Fourier 64 (2014), 945–946,
  https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.2869.pdf . Both body pages were
  read. It corrects the Lie-algebra method in Lemma 2.14 and a deformation
  computation in Proposition 3.6; it does not amend Appendix A.

## Wild cyclic algebra control

- Gille–Szamuely, *Central Simple Algebras and Galois Cohomology* (2006),
  https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Gille-Szamuely.pdf , §§2.5–2.6,
  especially Corollary 2.5.5 (printed p.36): cyclic degree-p central simple
  algebras and reduced norm. The generator convention is stated explicitly
  in TURN_5; it may be the opposite convention from the book. The complete
  order, residue field and nonconstant-Brauer-class argument are proved in
  the packet, not attributed to an uninspected source theorem.
