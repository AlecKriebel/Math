## 2. Ambient-full invariant corners: complete recomputation

Let alpha:G -> Aut(A), with G finite abelian and A unital. Let p be invariant and full in A, and assume the restricted action on pAp is strongly approximately inner. Fix g, and write sigma=alpha_g. We prove the full conclusion without fullness in A^alpha.

### Finite frame and Fourier normalization

Fullness supplies an invertible element T=sum_j a_j p b_j: the algebraic ideal generated by p is dense in A, so choose its element within distance less than one of 1. Put Y=(a_j p) and Z=(b_j* p), as finite row matrices, so T=YZ*. Since

    TT* <= ||Z*Z|| YY*

and TT* is positive invertible, YY* is positive invertible. Its normalization yields y_j=(YY*)^(-1/2)a_j p, with sum_j y_j y_j*=1. No invariance of the y_j is asserted.

For each character chi of G define

    P_chi(y) = (1/|G|) sum_h conjugate(chi(h)) alpha_h(y).

Invariance of p implies P_chi(y_j) belongs to Ap. Changing the summation index gives alpha_k(P_chi(y))=chi(k)P_chi(y). Character orthogonality, with exactly |G| characters, gives

    sum_chi P_chi(y)P_chi(y)*
      = (1/|G|) sum_h alpha_h(yy*).

Consequently all the x_i=P_chi(y_j), including harmless zero terms, satisfy

    x_i in Ap,   alpha_h(x_i)=chi_i(h)x_i,   sum_i x_i x_i*=1.

The normalization factor is 1/|G| in each Fourier projection; there is no additional square-root factor in this Fourier-frame construction. For comparison, the orbit-frame construction uses x_(h,j)=|G|^(-1/2)alpha_h(y_j). Both yield the same normalization identity but are different frames.

### Exact fixed membership and both Gram limits

Choose u_n in U((pAp)^alpha) with Ad(u_n)(b) -> sigma(b) for every b in pAp. Set

    V_n = sum_i sigma(x_i) u_n x_i*.

Each term is exactly fixed by alpha_h: its two frame factors receive chi_i(h) and its conjugate, sigma commutes with alpha_h, and u_n is exactly fixed. Thus V_n belongs to A^alpha, with no asymptotic qualification.

For every b in pAp,

    ||u_n* sigma(b) u_n - b|| = ||sigma(b) - u_n b u_n*|| -> 0.

This is the precise inverse relation needed for the second Gram limit. Since all sums below are finite and x_i* a x_j belongs to pAp for arbitrary a in A,

    V_n V_n*
      = sum_ij sigma(x_i) Ad(u_n)(x_i*x_j) sigma(x_j*)
      -> sum_ij sigma(x_i x_i* x_j x_j*) = 1;

    V_n* V_n
      = sum_ij x_i u_n* sigma(x_i*x_j) u_n x_j*
      -> sum_ij x_i x_i* x_j x_j* = 1;

    V_n a V_n*
      = sum_ij sigma(x_i) Ad(u_n)(x_i*a x_j) sigma(x_j*)
      -> sigma(sum_ij x_i x_i* a x_j x_j*) = sigma(a).

No commutation of a frame Gram entry with u_n has been assumed. These are ordinary norm limits for a fixed finite family of corner elements.

Both Gram limits matter in a general unital C*-algebra: the first produces an eventual right inverse and the second an eventual left inverse, so V_n is eventually invertible. Functional calculus then gives

    W_n = V_n (V_n*V_n)^(-1/2) in U(A^alpha),
    ||W_n-V_n|| -> 0.

The last assertion follows from V_n*V_n -> 1 and boundedness of V_n. Therefore Ad(W_n)(a) -> sigma(a) for every a, proving the lemma. Eventual indices can be reindexed; no separability choice or uniformity across all a is silently needed beyond the assumed point-norm sequence.

### Reverse direction and exact inner extension

If instead v_n in U(A^alpha) implements alpha_g and p is invariant, then [v_n,p] -> 0. For z_n=pv_np,

    z_n*z_n -> p,   z_nz_n* -> p

in the unital algebra pAp, and z_n is exactly alpha-fixed. Its polar unitaries w_n satisfy w_n-z_n -> 0. For b=pbp, Ad(z_n)(b)=p v_n b v_n* p -> alpha_g(b). This proves restriction to an arbitrary invariant corner, with no fullness condition.

There is also an exact version for an arbitrary automorphism sigma preserving a full p. If sigma|pAp=Ad(u), take any finite frame y_j in Ap and U=sum_j sigma(y_j)u y_j*. The three displayed Gram/implementation calculations become equalities: UU*=U*U=1 and UaU*=sigma(a). No group, abelianness, or fixedness of u is used. Hence an outer automorphism cannot become inner on an invariant full corner.

## 3. Honest cocycle perturbations: complete recomputation and converse

Let beta be strongly approximately inner, and let d:G -> U(A) satisfy the genuine normalized beta-cocycle identity

    d_1=1,   d_(gh)=d_g beta_g(d_h).

Let alpha_g=Ad(d_g) beta_g. Fix g and v_n in U(A^beta) implementing beta_g, and put t_n=d_g v_n. Then t_n is a unitary and implements alpha_g asymptotically. For each h, exact beta-fixedness of v_n and commutativity of G give

    alpha_h(t_n)
      = d_h beta_h(d_g) v_n d_h*
      = d_g beta_g(d_h) v_n d_h*.

The last equality is precisely the two cocycle expressions for d_(hg)=d_(gh). Moreover

    ||alpha_h(t_n)-t_n||
      = ||beta_g(d_h)-v_n d_h v_n*|| -> 0.

There is no hypothesis that d_h is beta-fixed, that the d_h commute, or that the v_n for different g multiply approximately. The identity uses only the sequence for this fixed g on the finitely many fixed elements d_h of A, not an n-dependent input.

Set z_n=|G|^(-1)sum_h alpha_h(t_n). The finite group average is exactly alpha-fixed and satisfies

    ||z_n-t_n|| <= (1/|G|)sum_h ||alpha_h(t_n)-t_n|| -> 0.

It follows that z_n is eventually invertible, since it is eventually within distance less than one of the unitary t_n. Its polar unitary w_n is exactly alpha-fixed and satisfies ||w_n-t_n|| -> 0, proving strong approximate innerness of alpha.

The converse follows from an **honest inverse cocycle**, not merely an informal symmetry argument. Put e_g=d_g*. Then

    e_g alpha_g(e_h)
      = d_g* d_g beta_g(d_h*) d_g*
      = beta_g(d_h*) d_g* = d_(gh)* = e_(gh),

and beta_g=Ad(e_g)alpha_g. Applying the proved direction to alpha and e gives the converse.

Thus the property is invariant under honest cocycle perturbations, and therefore under the usual exterior equivalence relation using genuine cocycles, for finite abelian groups. A family implementing pointwise inner differences whose multiplication agrees only up to scalars does not meet that definition. Abelianness is used in equating the two expressions for d_(gh); finite averaging is used to turn asymptotic fixedness into exact fixedness. No broader group claim is made.

## 4. Adversarial boundary cases and obstructions

### Period two: full in A, not full in A^alpha

Take the bidirected complete bipartite graph with cyclic classes V0={0,1}, V1={2,3}; there is exactly one edge each way between every cross-part pair. It is strongly connected, has period two, and all cycles have exits. Put p=p0+p1. The order-two gauge action sends each S_e to -S_e and equals Ad(U), U=2p-1. Indeed U multiplies every edge by the source sign times the terminal sign, which is -1. Therefore

    A^alpha = {a:[a,p]=0} = pAp direct-sum (1-p)A(1-p).

Both summands are nonzero, so p is not full in A^alpha. It is full in A: a path mu from V0 to any vertex v gives S_mu* p S_mu=p_v, so the ideal generated by p contains all vertex projections.

A homogeneous frame of Ap consists of p and the edges with source in V1 (whose ranges in the graph sense are in V0). The corner action is the identity, implemented by u=p. The lift gives exactly

    V = p - sum_(s(e) in V1) S_e S_e* = 2p-1.

Thus the stronger lemma handles exactly the boundary that defeats an attempted requirement of fixed-algebra fullness. It does not contradict the original submitted sufficient lemma, whose assumption was stronger.

For the order-four gauge subgroup, the phase i has inner subgroup {0,2} in Z4: length-two closed paths force the square of an inner gauge phase to be one. The return graph has adjacency [[2,2],[2,2]]; the quotient return action is an outer order-two action. This also checks that the period and inner-kernel reductions interact correctly rather than forcing the original action to be outer.

### Pauli projective obstruction

Let X=[[0,1],[1,0]], Z=diag(1,-1). Their conjugations commute and define an action of Z2 x Z2 on M2, although XZ=-ZX. A matrix commuting with Z is diagonal, and commuting also with X forces equal diagonal entries. Hence the fixed algebra is the scalars and its unitaries cannot implement Ad(X). The action is not strongly approximately inner.

It remains a counterexample on M2 tensor O2, which is simple and purely infinite: the fixed algebra is 1 tensor O2, whose unitaries fix M2 tensor 1 pointwise. Thus neither abelianness nor pure infiniteness eliminates a projective obstruction by itself. For beta=id, a genuine cocycle is an actual unitary representation of G, whose unitaries must commute. No change of scalar choices can turn X and Z into commuting unitaries. The Pauli example is consequently not a counterexample to section 3; it shows why honest cocycles cannot be replaced with arbitrary pointwise inner perturbations.

For an inner action Ad(U_g) on a simple unital A, if fixed unitaries implement alpha_g in the limit, their conjugations fix U_h exactly; hence alpha_g(U_h)=U_h. The scalar commutator of U_g and U_h must therefore be 1. Conversely commuting implementers are already fixed by the whole action and implement exactly. This independently pinpoints the obstruction.

### Noncyclic mixed kernel

On the period-two graph above let G=Z2 x Z2, with characters (-1)^a at all sources in V0 and (-1)^b at all sources in V1. This is a faithful action; its inner kernel is {(0,0),(1,1)}, because every length-two closed path has character (-1)^(a+b). The nontrivial inner-kernel element is the order-two gauge action. Normalized vertex twisting yields an outer quotient of order two. Thus ordinary kernel, inner kernel, and whole-group fixedness cannot be interchanged.

The exact rational checks in `CHECKS.py` additionally use a genuinely noncommutative beta-cocycle in M4. With beta_g=Ad(R_g) for a commuting diagonal representation and a rational orthogonal Q, set d_g=Q R_g Q* R_g*. The d_g need not commute or be beta-fixed, but satisfy the honest cocycle law. The test verifies both directions and the transport t_g=d_gR_g=QR_gQ* exactly. It also tests Fourier normalization starting with a nonhomogeneous frame, both unitary products, all 64 group/matrix-unit comparisons, and all 256 source-character labelings of this exact CK graph subclass. These checks support algebraic consistency; the general proofs above carry the infinite-dimensional conclusions.

### Excluded cycle case

For a directed cycle of length d, its algebra is M_d(C(T)). A gauge phase z acts on the central cycle coordinate by z^d. If z^d is not one, the action is not approximately inner, since inner conjugations fix the center. Thus exclusion of directed cycles is substantive and is supplied by the original simple purely infinite assumption.

## 5. Independent reconstruction of the graph-to-matrix deduction

Write S_e*S_e=p_r(e), S_eS_e*<=p_s(e), with alpha_g(S_e)=chi_e(g)S_e and fixed vertices. Assume E finite, strongly connected, and not a directed cycle.

### Normalized inner kernel

Every cycle has an exit: an exitless directed cycle would contain all vertices and all outgoing edges by strong connectivity, making the graph a directed cycle. Thus the path diagonal D is a MASA. If an edge-diagonal sigma(S_e)=z_eS_e is inner, its implementer u commutes with D because sigma fixes D pointwise; therefore u belongs to D=C(E^infinity). For any finite path mu and infinite continuation x, the diagonal cylinder relation yields

    (S_mu* u S_mu)(x)=u(mu x),   u(mu x)=z_mu u(x).

It is checked first on cylinder projections and then by uniform density. For a closed mu use x=mu mu mu ... ; u(x) has modulus one, giving z_mu=1.

Fix a base b and paths theta_v:b->v, with theta_b empty. If every closed-path phase is one, a return path eta:r(e)->b compares the two closed paths theta_s(e) e eta and theta_r(e) eta. This gives

    z_e=z_theta_s(e)^(-1) z_theta_r(e).

Consequently t_v=z_theta_v^(-1) yields z_e=t_s(e) conjugate(t_r(e)), and sigma is implemented by sum_v t_v p_v. Normalization t_b=1 gives uniqueness by connectivity. This establishes the inner criterion in both directions, including all closed walks, not just simple cycles.

Let K be the inner kernel of the action, and define for **every** g in G

    d_g=sum_v chi_theta_v(g)^(-1) p_v.

These form an actual representation, all are alpha-fixed, and alpha_k=Ad(d_k) for k in K by the criterion just proved. Therefore beta_g=Ad(d_g*)alpha_g is an edge-diagonal action, beta_k=id on K, and beta descends to G/K. If beta_g is inner, alpha_g is inner, so g belongs to K: the quotient is pointwise outer. Also beta fixes each d_h, making d an honest beta-cocycle. This avoids any character-extension or projective-splitting assumption. If K=G, beta is trivial and the desired conclusion follows directly with d_g as implementers.

### Period reduction for a pointwise outer action

For such an outer beta, let d be the graph period, V0 one cyclic class, and p=sum_(v in V0)p_v. It is invariant and full in A by the path calculation in section 4; no assertion about fullness in A^beta is needed. Let F have vertex set V0 and one separate edge for each length-d E-path starting in V0. Parallel paths remain distinct.

The return graph is primitive. For completeness, the gcd of lengths of all based closed walks at a vertex b equals d: a loop at another vertex is inserted between paths from and to b, and subtracting the length of the uninserted based walk proves divisibility in the required direction. Finitely many based lengths already have gcd d. After dividing by d, their nonnegative semigroup contains every sufficiently large integer: choose one positive generator q, note that the residues generated by the other generators fill Z/q, select one nonnegative representative of each residue, then add multiples of q. Fixed paths u->b and b->v have combined length divisible by d when u,v are in V0. Inserting based loops gives all sufficiently large lengths divisible by d from u to v. Thus a common sufficiently large power of F's adjacency matrix has all positive entries.

Some vertex of E branches; otherwise strong connectivity makes E a directed cycle. Trace backwards from that vertex fewer than d steps to V0, take either of two branch edges, and extend to length d using no sinks. Hence F has a branching vertex, at least two edges, and the exit condition required for uniqueness.

The projections p_v (v in V0) and S_mu (length d, s(mu) in V0) satisfy the F relations and have nonzero vertices and edges. Graph uniqueness gives an injection C*(F)->pAp. To prove surjectivity, span pAp by monomials S_mu S_nu* whose initial vertices lie in V0 and whose terminal vertices agree. Their lengths are congruent modulo d. Choose k in {0,...,d-1} making both extended lengths divisible by d; then

    S_mu S_nu* = sum_(|tau|=k, s(tau)=r(mu)) S_(mu tau) S_(nu tau)*.

Every right-hand path decomposes into length-d return edges. Length-zero paths are vertex projections, so no boundary monomial is omitted. This proves the equivariant corner isomorphism, with return-edge character chi_mu.

If a nontrivial quotient automorphism became inner on pAp, the exact inner extension in section 2 would make it inner on A. Thus the return action is outer. It meets all hypotheses of the authenticated Barlak-Szabo graph theorem. That theorem makes it strongly approximately inner on pAp. The ambient-full corner lemma lifts it to A. If desired, primitivity in the published edge formulation is seen explicitly from the edge matrix L(e,f)=1 when r(e)=s(f): a sufficiently large length between r(e) and s(f) gives a common positive power of L. This checks the paper's edge-based wording as well as the vertex adjacency formulation.

Pull this quotient conclusion back to G; the fixed algebra is unchanged by pullback under G->G/K. Section 3 applied to the honest beta-cocycle d now gives strong approximate innerness of alpha on A. Thus no fixed-point simplicity theorem, equivariant Morita-invariance assertion, or unsupported cohomology vanishing statement is needed by this alternate argument.

### Exact original matrix formulation

In the OWR definition write alpha_g(s_i)=sum_j c_ji(g)s_j and p_i=s_i s_i*. Fixed p_i and the orthogonality p_i p_j=0 imply p_j alpha_g(s_i)=0 for j!=i. Since p_j s_j=s_j!=0, all off-diagonal c_ji vanish. The group law gives alpha_g(s_i)=chi_i(g)s_i with characters chi_i.

For the graph with edge i->j exactly when M(i,j)=1, set T_ij=s_i p_j in O_M. Direct multiplication gives T_ij*T_ij=p_j, distinct-edge orthogonality, and sum_(j:M(i,j)=1)T_ij T_ij*=p_i. Conversely s_i=sum_j T_ij satisfies s_i*s_i=sum_j M(i,j)p_j and s_i s_i*=p_i; cross terms vanish by the mutually orthogonal terminal projections. These inverse formulas provide the equivariant isomorphism O_M=C*(E), with source character chi_i on edge i->j.

There are no sinks or sources by the no-zero-row/column assumption. Simplicity forces strong connectivity. One can see this without importing an ideal-classification theorem: otherwise choose a terminal strongly connected component T and a distinct source component S in the finite component DAG. Such a choice exists whenever the DAG has more than one vertex. No sources in E implies S contains a directed cycle. Let X be the nonempty set of infinite paths never hitting T, containing a periodic path in S. Prefixing edges on l2(X), with zero when the prefixed path leaves X, gives a graph representation. For each path in X its first edge also stays in X, so all CK vertex sums hold. It kills every p_t for t in T and is nonzero on a vertex of S. Universal vertex projections p_t are themselves nonzero, for example in the full infinite-path representation. This gives a nonzero proper kernel and contradicts simplicity. A strongly connected graph that is a directed cycle has algebra M_d(C(T)), also excluded. Hence the graph theorem applies to exactly the original matrix target.
