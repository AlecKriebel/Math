## Exact target and primary scope

The target is the affirmative strong approximate-innerness assertion for every finite abelian diagonal quasi-free action on an essential finite-matrix Cuntz–Krieger algebra that is simple and purely infinite. Here essential means no zero row or column. For each group element, a separate implementing sequence must lie exactly in the fixed algebra of the entire action. No approximate group law is included.

The root freshly downloaded and inspected the original OWR2017/42 report, printed p.2621 (PDF p.21), including its definition, Question1 and Theorem2. The question has neither an outerness nor an aperiodicity restriction. The theorem below it has both. ## Identities and boundary cases

1. **Matrix presentation.** Put p_i=s_i s_i* and T_ij=s_i p_j when M_ij=1. Orthogonality of the p_j makes the cross terms in the outgoing sum vanish. Then T_ij*T_ij=p_j, and the outgoing ranges sum to p_i. Conversely sum the outgoing T_ij to recover s_i. These formulas are inverse on generators. This is an equivariant isomorphism, not just stable equivalence. Range-fixing and span preservation force the s_i to be eigenvectors; the action law gives characters.

2. **Strong connectivity.** An arbitrary finite simple graph algebra can have sources and need not have a strongly connected presenting graph. Here both the no-sink and no-source conditions are present. If the condensation graph has more than one component, take a terminal component T and a different source component S. Every vertex of S receives an edge inside S, so S contains a cycle disjoint from T. The hereditary closure of T stays disjoint from that cycle. Saturation cannot add the first cycle vertex, since its next cycle edge remains outside. Thus there is a nonempty proper saturated hereditary set and its nonzero proper graph ideal contradicts simplicity. The directed-cycle case gives a matrix algebra over continuous functions on the circle and is excluded. A one-vertex0–1 matrix cannot satisfy the target; a one-vertex multigraph with at least two loops can satisfy the intermediate graph theorem.

3. **Inner diagonal automorphisms.** In the strongly connected noncycle graph every cycle has an exit. The canonical graph diagonal is a MASA. An inner implementer for an edge-phase automorphism commutes with this entire diagonal and therefore belongs to it. The infinite-path model then gives h(mu x)=z_mu h(x). On a periodic path for a closed word mu, h never vanishes, so z_mu=1. This tests all closed directed paths, including nonsimple closed walks. Comparing base-to-source and base-to-range paths followed by the same return path gives z_e=t_s conjugate(t_r), with t_v the inverse base-path phase. The signs in the submitted proof agree with Ad(sum t_v p_v). Normalized uniqueness follows from connectivity. A continuous diagonal implementer cannot hide a nontrivial periodic-orbit obstruction.

4. **Full frame.** Fullness of p in a unital B gives an invertible finite sum YZ*. The operator inequality (YZ*)(YZ*)* <= ||Z*Z|| YY* makes YY* positive invertible. Multiplication by its inverse square root supplies a finite frame x_i in Bp with sum x_i x_i*=1. This does not require p to be equivalent to1.

5. **Corner outerness.** If sigma fixes p and its restriction is Ad(u), take U=sum sigma(x_i)u x_i*. For Gram entries q_ij=x_i*x_j, u q_ij u*=sigma(q_ij). In UU*, substitute this identity; in U*U, use its inverse. The double frame sum becomes1 in both cases. In UaU*, substitute the same identity for x_i*a x_j to obtain sigma(a). Thus innerness of the restriction would contradict outer sigma. Fullness is needed in A, not in the fixed algebra, for this lemma.

6. **Lifting strong approximate innerness.** The submitted different lemma assumes fullness in A^alpha and chooses its frame there. All x_i are exactly fixed. With V_n=sum x_i u_n x_i*, both Gram calculations converge to1, and V_n a V_n* converges to alpha_g(a). From Ad(u_n) to sigma in point norm one gets Ad(u_n*) to sigma inverse by applying the first convergence to sigma inverse(a); no uniform-in-a conclusion is needed. Two-sided near unitarity gives invertibility. The polar correction belongs to the same fixed C*-subalgebra and differs from V_n by a quantity tending to zero. A separate sequence for every g is sufficient, including identity g.

7. **Return corner.** For period d choose one cyclic class V0. Different length-d paths remain different return edges. The V0 block of the d-th adjacency power is primitive. A branching vertex can be reached backwards from V0 within d-1 steps; both outgoing choices extend to length d, giving at least two return edges. Thus the single-loop exception is avoided, even if the return graph has one vertex. Nonzero terminal projections make every return-edge image nonzero, as required by the inspected uniqueness theorem. For a corner spanning monomial S_mu S_nu*, the two lengths agree modulo d. Append the same complementary-length path tau to both and sum using the graph relations. Both words now split into return edges. This proves surjectivity, including zero-length cases. The return corner is equivariant and is full in A by simplicity. Corner outerness follows from item5. The imported theorem applies to the quotient's outer return action.

8. **Fixed-point fullness.** The submitted argument then uses the standard finite-group outer-action simplicity consequence. The freshly read final Echterhoff–Rordam2024 publisher body, printed pp.127 and129, states the required consequence and attributes crossed-product simplicity to Kishimoto. The averaging projection e in A crossed G identifies A^alpha with e(A crossed G)e; the coefficient expectation proves the embedding injective. Thus simplicity passes to the corner. A nonzero cyclic-class p is full there. This is a valid published import. The root did not freshly acquire Kishimoto's1981 original body and does not imply otherwise.

9. **Inner kernel and exact group law.** K is the kernel of G to Out(A). Use the same fixed base paths on every g to define D_g=sum inverse(chi_theta_v(g))p_v. This is already a genuine representation on all G; it needs no extension of subgroup characters or choices of projective implementers. Every D_g is fixed by alpha. For k in K the preceding inner-phase classification gives alpha_k=Ad(D_k). Hence beta_g=Ad(D_g*)alpha_g is an actual action, descends through G/K and is outer on the quotient. The edge-character formula and all signs agree. If the quotient is trivial, the unit is an exact beta implementer.

10. **Restoring the whole fixed algebra.** A beta-fixed implementing sequence v_n need not itself be alpha-fixed. Since beta_g fixes every p_v, the commutators [v_n,p_v] tend to zero. Vertex pinching is norm close, beta-fixed and commutes exactly with all p_v. Its eventual polar unitary retains these memberships. It commutes with every D_h, so alpha_h(w_n)=D_h beta_h(w_n)D_h*=w_n for every h. Then D_g w_n is exactly alpha-fixed and implements alpha_g in the limit. This proves the original target, with no exterior-equivalence shortcut.

A genuine obstruction exists if the diagonal restriction is removed. On M2, conjugation by the Pauli matrices X and Z defines a commuting C2xC2 action although XZ=-ZX. Their common fixed algebra consists of scalars. Tensoring with O2 makes the ambient algebra simple and purely infinite, but its fixed unitaries commute with the M2 factor and cannot approximate the automorphism sending Z to -Z. Thus one cannot justify the target merely by saying that finite abelian inner actions have fixed implementers. The graph-MASA/vertex-potential argument specifically eliminates this obstruction. The program checks exact Pauli anticommutation; the stated fixed-algebra conclusion follows directly by equating entries of a2x2 matrix, and its tensor version by matrix coefficients.
