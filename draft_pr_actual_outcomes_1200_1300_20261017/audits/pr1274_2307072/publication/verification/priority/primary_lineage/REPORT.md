## Earlier exact witness: Iwaniec 1986

The open-access [original article](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1029003418), *The best constant in a BMO-inequality for the Beurling-Ahlfors transform*, Michigan Math. J. 33(3) (1986), 387-394, was downloaded normally from the publisher and all eight pages visually read. Printed p.387 defines the same negative kernel and states \(T(\bar\partial w)=\partial w\). Printed p.388, equations (6)-(7), gives
\[
\rho(z)=\left(\frac z{|z|}\right)^2\chi_D(z),\qquad
T\rho(z)=(1+\log|z|^2)\chi_D(z).
\]
The line immediately below gives \(w(z)=(z\log|z|^2)\chi_D(z)\). Since \((z/|z|)^2=z/\bar z\) away from zero, these are the submission's exact nonpolynomial input, output and compact-support primitive. Printed p.393 reuses the example in the discussion of exponential integrability.

This is positive prior evidence, substantially stronger than the generic fact that \(B:L^\infty\to L^\infty\) fails. Iwaniec's paper does not formulate Hinkkanen's later numbered annihilator question or the submitted polynomial family. Its explicit formula, combined with angular annihilation, yields the literal general negative conclusion as a deduction. The latter connection is recorded here as a deduction, not falsely described as a sentence from 1986.

## Direct general mechanism: Gill 2010

The final openly available [Gill paper](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1281531461), Michigan Math. J. 59(2) (2010), 353-363, provides the decisive broader formula. Printed p.354, Theorem 1, says that for real \(f\in L^p(\mathbb R^+)\), \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
F(z)=\frac z{\bar z}f(|z|^2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
SF(z)=-\Lambda^* f(|z|^2),\qquad
\Lambda^*f(t)=\int_t^\infty\frac{f(s)}s\,ds-f(t).
\]
Its pp.355-356, equation (4) and Section 2, reproduce the exact logarithmic pair and attribute it to Iwaniec. These complete relevant pages were visually inspected. The submitted radial factors are real and compactly supported, so this theorem applies directly.

The final [Bañuelos-Janakiraman weak-type paper](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1250169072), Michigan Math. J. 58(2) (2009), 459-477, was also downloaded and inspected. Remark 2.1(1), printed p.461, quotes the same pair and credits Iwaniec; reference 13, p.477, identifies the 1986 article. Its positive kernel on p.459 differs in sign from that quoted pair under the target convention; the original 1986 paper and Gill's theorem resolve the convention match without silently correcting that display. This 2009 paper is **not** the book's reference 52.

The author manuscript [Bañuelos-Osękowski, adjoint operator paper](https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/~ados/publications/papers/adjoint3.pdf), p.2, is a forward citation to Gill's adjoint formula. ## Checkable reconstruction of the overlap

This paragraph is the present audit's calculation, not a claimed quotation or historical full-target theorem. If \(a\) is bounded on \((0,1)\), the function \(F(re^{i\theta})=e^{2i\theta}a(r^2)\) annihilates every analytic area-\(L^1\) function: the product is absolutely integrable, polar Fubini applies, and the angular integral against every nonnegative Taylor mode vanishes. Thus Iwaniec's \(\rho\) lies in the literal \(N\). Its displayed output is essentially unbounded, proving the general negative answer immediately.

Gill's formula, with \(a_m(t)=1-(1-t)^m\) on \((0,1)\) and zero outside, gives
\[
Bv_m(t)=a_m(t)-\int_t^1\frac{a_m(s)}s\,ds
=1-(1-t)^m-\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{(1-t)^k}{k}.
\]
Here \(v_m=e^{2i\theta}a_m(t)=z^2\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-t)^j\) is a polynomial, has norm one and lies in \(N\). The displayed output increases from \(-H_m\) to \(1\): its derivative is \(m(1-t)^{m-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-t)^j>0\). Its essential norm is \(H_m\), including \(m=1\). The family and exact norm therefore specialize an old full transform theorem through an elementary integral calculation. No source located in this audit states that exact polynomial family and norm; that bounded absence is not evidence of a publishable new theorem.

Even without this exact calculation, \(v_m\to\rho\) in \(L^2\) with \(\|v_m\|_\infty=1\). The established \(L^2\) isometry implies \(Bv_m\to B\rho\) in \(L^2\). A common polynomial \(L^\infty\) bound would pass to an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence and force \(B\rho\) bounded, a contradiction. This qualitative bridge is a current composition of old results, not a located published earlier full-target statement.

The conjugated pairing \(\int_Dv\bar\phi\,dA=0\) is a different hypothesis. The exact witness fails it: with \(\phi=z^2\), the integral is \(\pi/2\). No conclusion about that alternative follows. Nor do finite-p full-plane norm bounds alone establish the endpoint target. A recent arXiv finite-p claim surfaced in the search and its record was saved; its proof was not audited and is outside this priority conclusion.
