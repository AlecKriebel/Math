## The 1964 complex-scalar argument

The full Proposition 1 proof, Proposition 2 proof, homogeneous corollary and small-support remark on de Leeuw–Mirkil pp.113–114 were read together and visually checked. The homogeneous-symbol obstruction is applicable to complex-valued test functions and the complex scalar operators ∂ and dbar. To remove any reliance on an implicit field convention, their measure-and-scaling proof specializes as follows.

Suppose ∥∂u∥∞≤C∥dbar u∥∞ for every complex u∈C_c^∞(R²). The functional

\[
L(\bar\partial u)=\partial u(0)
\]

is well defined, complex-linear and bounded on a subspace of complex C_0(R²). Complex Hahn–Banach and Riesz representation give a finite complex measure μ with ∂u(0)=∫dbar u(x)dμ(x). Apply this identity to u_r(x)=u(rx), divide by r, and let r→∞. Dominated convergence gives

\[
\partial u(0)=\mu(\{0\})\bar\partial u(0).
\]

Take u(z)=zχ(z) with a smooth compactly supported cutoff χ equal to one near zero. Then ∂u(0)=1 and dbar u(0)=0, a contradiction. This also exhibits explicitly why the complex scalar field matters: restricting u to real values would give |∂u|=|dbar u| and would not yield the claimed non-inequality. Treating the two real components as unrelated controlling operators would be a different hypothesis.

Equivalently, the degree-one characteristic symbols of ∂ and dbar are not complex scalar multiples. Dilation puts any compact support inside D and preserves the derivative-norm ratio. ## Closed graph and polynomial transfer

The full target is

\[
N=\{v\in L^\infty(D):\int_D v\phi\,dA=0\text{ for all }\phi\in A^1(D)\}.
\]

Each test functional is continuous on L∞, so N is a closed Banach subspace. Suppose every v∈N had B(v1_D)|_D∈L∞(D). If v_n→v in L∞ and B(v_n1_D)|_D→w in L∞, zero extension gives convergence in full-plane L². L² continuity of B forces w=B(v1_D)|_D. The graph is therefore closed and the closed graph theorem supplies a common bound. The impossible derivative estimate above rules out that bound, since v=dbar u∈N for u∈C_c^∞(D), by interior integration by parts, and Bv=∂u. Thus the all-members mapping assertion fails. The norm is measured on D; ∂u vanishes outside D, so no full-plane/disc norm gap occurs.

For the polynomial clause, fix such u and put h=u/(1−|z|²), extended by zero near and across ∂D. It is smooth on a neighborhood of the closed disc. Complex polynomials q_n(x,y) can approximate h in C¹ there: for example, tensor Bernstein polynomials on a containing square approximate a smooth extension and its first derivatives. Their zero trace makes U_n∈W^{1,2}(C), with no boundary measure in either first derivative, even though the zero extension need not be C¹ across the circle. Hence

\[
v_n=\bar\partial U_n|_D\in\mathbb C[z,\bar z],
