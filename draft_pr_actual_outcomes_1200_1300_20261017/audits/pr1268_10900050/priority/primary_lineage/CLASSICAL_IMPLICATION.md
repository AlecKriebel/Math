# Checkable composition of classical ingredients

This is an audit reconstruction on 17 October 2026, after reading the accepted candidate proof. It is the same ideal/residue mechanism as that proof, not an independent discovery claim or an identified historical theorem of another author.

## General observation

Let O be the ring of integers of a number field and P a nonzero nonprincipal prime ideal. Consider the graph on O in which an edge difference is a unit or an element p generating a nonzero prime ideal. Then reduction modulo P is a proper coloring, so the clique number is at most N(P).

Proof. A proper ideal contains no unit. If p belongs to P and (p) is a nonzero prime ideal, maximality of nonzero prime ideals in O gives (p)=P, contradicting nonprincipality. Consequently no allowed nonzero edge difference belongs to P. Two vertices congruent modulo P cannot be adjacent, and a clique injects into O/P.

The maximality fact can be checked without a class-number theorem. Choose nonzero a in a nonzero prime ideal Q. Multiplication by a on an integral basis has nonzero determinant, so O/(a) is finite; O/Q is a finite integral domain and hence a field. Thus Q is maximal.

For unit differences alone, this is already the injection used by Lenstra, Proposition 2.2, journal p. 242, in [Euclidean Number Fields of Large Degree](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1977b/art.pdf). The exclusion of prime elements from a nonprincipal prime ideal is the additional standard-algebra step in this reconstruction. No inspected source is asserted to have stated that composite graph observation.

## Arithmetic application

For O=Z[sqrt(10)], the map a+b sqrt(10) to a modulo 2 is a surjective ring map to F2 with kernel P=(2,sqrt(10)), hence N(P)=2. If P were generated by a+b sqrt(10), the determinant of multiplication in the basis (1,sqrt(10)) would force |a²−10b²|=2. Modulo 5, this requires a² to be 2 or 3, impossible. Thus P is nonprincipal.

The general observation bounds both target graph clique numbers by 2. The edge {0,1} supplies the lower bound. Every proper ideal quotient has at least two elements and P attains two, so the minimum nonzero prime-ideal norm is 2. This yields the strict comparison 2<2·2. Full-ring identification and total reality are established in the accepted candidate proof, rather than newly attributed here.

The exact ideal and norm obstruction occur in [Evan Dummit's 2021 v1.90 notes](https://dummit.cos.northeastern.edu/teaching_sp21_4527/numthy_9_geometry_of_numbers_v1.90.pdf), PDF p. 13, and are retained in his 2022 v2.00 notes. The arithmetic ingredient is therefore not a plausible new mathematical contribution of the 2026 candidate.

## Convention boundary

The prime-element requirement is essential. One can check P²=(2): its products are generated by 4, 2 sqrt(10), and 10, and 2=10−2·4 lies among their integer combinations; all generators are divisible by 2. Thus the element 2 qualifies in a graph allowing generators of prime-ideal squares, such as Lopez's Definition 52, even though it is not a prime element. In that larger graph {0,1,2} is a triangle. This is a boundary example, not a computation of that larger graph's maximum clique size.

An irreducible-difference graph likewise differs: 2 has absolute norm 4, and a proper nonunit factorization would need factors of norm 2, just excluded. Therefore 2 is irreducible, while (sqrt(10))²=10 belongs to (2) and sqrt(10) does not, so 2 is not prime. This prevents substitution of an irreducible convention.

- 1977: Lenstra's primary paper documents the unit-clique residue injection.
- 2015/2016: Long's question and Long–Thistlethwaite's graph framework appear in the pinned sources; their distinct printed minima must remain distinguished.
- 2018: Lopez's thesis develops prime-power/Lenstra–Hurwitz extensions. It does not state the exact target counterexample in the full extracted text inspected here.
- 2021/2022: Dummit's dated author notes explicitly document the exact arithmetic ingredient. This is evidence of an old ingredient before the present audit, not the earliest provenance of the arithmetic.
- 2026-10-17: this audit writes the above composition. It does not establish when anybody first made the composition or whether it has appeared in an unlocated source.

This proof shows that the candidate conclusion is accessible by a short composition of old facts. It does not turn that logical implication into proof of historical explicit anticipation, nor does failure to locate the composition establish novelty.
