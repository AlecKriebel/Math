## Strongest mathematical attribution challenges

[Lenstra 1977](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1977b/art.pdf), Proposition 2.2, printed p. 242, already proves the unit-clique bound by distinct residues modulo a proper ideal. For the accepted norm-two ideal, the unit-clique conclusion is therefore an immediate old-theorem specialization. B is right to exclude any claim to a new unit-clique theorem.

[Győry 1980](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/3543/download/10_5486_PMD_1980_27_3-4_05.pdf), printed p. 231, uses a complement convention and bounds its complementary clique quantity by an ideal norm; the unit specialization is explicitly credited to Lenstra. Although the displayed graph has an arbitrary finite vertex subset, this does not prevent applying its uniform bound to every finite clique of an infinite ambient unit graph. The finite-vertex wording is not a reason to discard that old unit bound. It does not supply the prime-element augmentation. [Győry 1982](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/3598/download/10_5486_PMD_1982_29_1-2_08.pdf), pp. 79–81, introduces a broad complement Cayley definition, then restricts the main theorem setup to a finite set times a finitely generated multiplicative group. Its general definition alone cannot be substituted for a theorem covering all prime differences.

[Akhtar et al. 2009](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v16i1r117/pdf/), Proposition 6.1, p. 8, explicitly colors finite commutative unitary Cayley graphs by independent ideal cosets. [Defant v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.3054v2), Facts 4.1–4.2, p. 7, concerns finite Dedekind-domain quotients and unit differences. B accurately cites these as established coset-coloring background. Kiani–Molla Haji Aghaei's standing finite-ring assumption and matrix-ring clique formulas were also confirmed. These source scopes do not state an exact prime-graph theorem for O=Z[sqrt(10)].

The distinction can be checked without a literature inference. Reduction modulo an ideal sends unit differences to units in the quotient, yielding an upper-bound coloring when the quotient is colored. Equality or clique constructions in a finite quotient do not generally lift to the infinite ring: F3 has a three-vertex unit clique, while the unit graph on Z has clique number two. For prime-augmented graphs an extra exclusion is necessary: modulo (2) in Z, the adjacent vertices 0 and 2 have the same residue because 2 is a prime element.

For a nonprincipal nonzero prime ideal P in a number ring, that obstacle vanishes. A contained prime element p would give a nonzero prime ideal (p) contained in P. Maximality forces (p)=P, contradicting nonprincipality. No unit lies in P either. Thus P-cosets are independent in the prime graph. With norm two and the edge 0–1, both clique numbers are two. This is the accepted proof's routine classical composition, not evidence that a historical author published the complete graph conclusion. B maintains that distinction correctly.
