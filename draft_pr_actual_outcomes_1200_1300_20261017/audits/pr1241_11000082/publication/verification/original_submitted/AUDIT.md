## 1. Fixed genus, a separating filling pair, and the flat structure

Fix g >= 2 first, choose the separating filling pair a,b on S_g, and put n = i(a,b). This choice is made once and never changes with the later trace parameter m. In minimal position the graph a union b has n vertices and 2n edges; because the pair fills, it has f >= 1 disk faces. Euler characteristic gives n = f + 2g - 2 >= 2g - 1. A closed curve crosses a separating curve an even number of times, so n is even. Thus n >= 4, in particular n > 2. No incorrect assertion about the exact minimum intersection in genus two is needed.

The rectangle construction is especially transparent for a single curve in each family. Its intersection matrix is the one-entry matrix [n], its positive eigenvector may be taken as [1], and the second eigenvector parameter is n^(-1)[n][1] = [1]. Consequently every rectangle is a unit square. Minimal position rules out complementary bigons, so the associated unmarked cone points have angle at least 2pi; genuine singularities are among square vertices. The edge identifications preserve the horizontal and vertical line fields and are translations or half-turns. Choosing square coordinates with integral corners makes every transition z -> +/-z+c have c in Z+iZ.

The horizontal cylinder has circumference n and height 1; its full twist has derivative A = [[1,n],[0,1]]. The vertical full twist has derivative B = [[1,0],[-n,1]] in the stated orientation convention. The signs do not affect the subsequent conjugacy or spectral-radius reasoning.

For n > 2, Leininger's fundamental-domain argument identifies the derivative image as a free rank-two Fuchsian group whose convex core is a twice-punctured disk. Its two cusp groups are precisely the conjugates of powers of A and B. Every other nonidentity element is hyperbolic. The abstract free group on two letters maps onto the twist subgroup and thence onto this free derivative image; the composition is a surjection between free groups of rank two and hence an isomorphism. Thus the derivative is injective on the twist subgroup itself, not merely finite-to-one there. This verifies the faithfulness needed later.

The added genus-two example also passes. Two sphere curves meeting in four points give six disk regions, three on each side of either curve. A double cover branched once in each region has Euler characteristic -2. Each curve encloses three branch points on both sides, so its inverse image is one separating curve bounding genus-one sides. A region with one branch point lifts to a disk, and its boundary has twice as many sides. Thus no upstairs complementary region is a bigon; the two lifted curves fill in geometric intersection eight. This example only illustrates g=2; the general theorem does not derive higher genera by varying this example during the multiplicity argument.

## 2. The full signed-holonomy lattice, including half-translation issues

Let V be the set of holonomy vectors of saddle connections in the unit-square atlas, with both signs included. Developing a segment across square charts preserves the integral grid. Its initial and final singularities develop to integral vertices, so V is a subset of Z^2. The possible sign ambiguity in a half-translation surface is exactly why both signs are included. Global orientability of the measured foliations is neither assumed nor needed.

Let Lambda be the additive subgroup generated by V. It has rank two: the complete horizontal and vertical cylinder decompositions have boundaries made of saddle connections in their respective directions. A closed genus >= 2 flat surface has genuine singularities, as also follows from the cone-angle Euler-characteristic formula; the decompositions therefore supply a nonzero horizontal vector and a nonzero vertical vector. More concretely, following a directional separatrix along the finite square-edge network reaches a singularity again and yields a saddle connection in that direction. Hence Lambda is a rank-two subgroup of Z^2 and therefore a genuine lattice. It need not equal Z^2.

Any affine automorphism preserves the singularity set and carries a saddle connection to a saddle connection. A representative of its projective derivative M carries each vector in V to another in V, with its sign accounted for. Applying the same reasoning to the inverse gives M Lambda = Lambda, not just containment. Its determinant is positive, and change of variables for the finite total flat area gives det(M) = 1. Thus every derivative in the full disk stabilizer belongs to L = SL(Lambda)/{+/-I}. In a positively oriented lattice basis L is PSL_2(Z).

This establishes precisely the ambient arithmetic group needed by the proof. It does not assert that the two-twist group, the subgroup later called H, or even the full disk stabilizer has finite index in any larger group. The full stabilizer may have a finite derivative kernel; that kernel is harmless for the necessary conjugacy implication used below.

## 3. Johnson depth and a fixed purely pseudo-Anosov free subgroup

The commutator filtration property used is [I_g(1),I_g(j)] <= I_g(j+1), not merely the weaker statement that successive quotients are abelian. The FLM proof explicitly uses the resulting lower-central-series containment. In the candidate's indexing L_1(F)=F and L_(j+1)(F)=[F,L_j(F)], induction gives L_j(F) <= I_g(j), since separating twists lie in Torelli. There is no off-by-one error. Separating twists actually lie deeper, but that stronger fact is unnecessary.

One can also understand the centrality input in the standard automorphism filtration. Write gamma_1 = pi_1(S), gamma_(r+1) = [gamma_1,gamma_r]. Representatives of outer classes in I_g(j) can be adjusted by an inner automorphism to act identically modulo gamma_(j+1). For automorphisms with this property, the usual commutator calculus implies that an IA_1 automorphism acts identically on gamma_(j+1)/gamma_(j+2), while an IA_j automorphism acts identically on gamma_2/gamma_(j+2). Comparing their two compositions on a generator then places their commutator in IA_(j+1). Passing to outer automorphisms gives the stated inclusion.

For fixed k >= 3, u_1=B and u_(j+1)=[A,u_j] lie in L_j(F). The first commutator is nontrivial. If a later one vanished, u_j would lie in the cyclic centralizer <A>. For j >= 2 it has zero F-abelianization, so the only possible power of A is the identity, contradicting induction. Hence u_k is nonidentity.

Put v_k=B u_k B^(-1). Both elements lie in the normal subgroup L_k(F). If they commuted, let <r> be the maximal cyclic subgroup containing u_k. The nontrivial intersection of <r> and B<r>B^(-1) would force B into <r> by malnormality. But u_k=r^s, s nonzero, and its zero abelianization forces the abelianization of r to be zero in the torsion-free group Z^2. This would force the abelianization of B to be zero, a contradiction. Thus u_k and v_k do not commute.

Nielsen–Schreier now gives a free subgroup H=<u_k,v_k> of rank two: it has rank at most two by its generating set and is not cyclic; the generating pair is a free basis, by Hopficity of F_2. H is contained in L_k(F) and [F,F]. Every nontrivial element of H therefore has zero abelianization and cannot be F-conjugate to a nonzero power of A or B. By the checked derivative classification, every nontrivial element of H is pseudo-Anosov with hyperbolic derivative. H is fixed before any Horowitz family is selected.

## 4. Why one fixed finite core controls all ambient fusion

Let J be the faithful derivative image of H in L. Because H <= [F,F] and the derivative of F lies in L, J <= K=[L,L]. The abelianization map C_2*C_3 -> C_2 x C_3 has kernel K and index six. This kernel acts freely on the Bass–Serre tree: it meets conjugates of either finite vertex group trivially. The quotient has six edges and 3+2 vertices, so its fundamental group is free of rank 6-5+1=2. Thus K is a fixed free group of finite rank.

Here is a fully based-to-unbased justification of the fusion lemma. Realize K as the fundamental group of a finite rose R, and let C -> R be the connected covering for J with chosen basepoint o. Oriented free homotopy classes of nontrivial loops in C correspond exactly to conjugacy classes in J. Because J is finitely generated, the subgraph Y supporting cyclically immersed closed loops is finite; any hanging tree or basepoint tail contributes no such loops.

Fix a nonempty cyclically reduced K-word w. Any J-conjugacy class lying in its K-conjugacy class has a freely tightened immersed loop in C. Its projection is a cyclic rotation of w. Moving its starting vertex along the loop makes its projected label exactly w. In a covering, a path with specified label and specified initial vertex is unique. Consequently at most |V(Y)| such based lifts, and hence at most |V(Y)| J-conjugacy classes, are possible. This is an upper bound; different lifts can correspond to the same J-class, which only helps it.

Nothing in that argument assumes w is primitive. If w=z^r, a lift still has just one trajectory from each initial vertex, regardless of r. In particular the bound cannot acquire a factor depending on the exponent, word length, or spectral value. As a useful test, J=<b,aba^(-1)> in <a,b> has a two-vertex core with one b-loop at each vertex; b^r and ab^ra^(-1) exhibit two distinct intrinsic classes in one ambient class for every positive r. The same fixed two-vertex bound applies for all r.

Since K is normal of index six in L, a single L-conjugacy class of an element of K splits into at most six K-conjugacy classes: write a conjugating element as k r_i for six fixed coset representatives r_i. Applying the preceding bound to each piece gives C_H = 6|V(Y)|. This is a finite constant depending on the already fixed subgroup H, with no dependence on m or the chosen dilatation.

## 5. Mapping-class conjugators are captured, not discarded

Suppose x,y are nontrivial elements of H and t x t^(-1)=y in Mod_g. The element y stabilizes both D(q) and tD(q), since x stabilizes D(q). Leininger Theorem 3.2 says a pseudo-Anosov stabilizes a unique Teichmüller disk, so tD(q)=D(q). Therefore t has an affine representative and its derivative lies in L by Section 2. The derivatives of x and y are L-conjugate.

This direct use of Theorem 3.2 is a shorter substitute for the earlier draft's equivalent axis argument and was adopted in the final candidate. It avoids any need to introduce a second general statement about uniqueness of a disk containing a geodesic.

Faithfulness on H identifies its conjugacy classes with J-conjugacy classes: if two derivative images are conjugate by an element of J, lift that conjugator to H and use injectivity to recover the same conjugacy upstairs. Thus one Mod_g-conjugacy class meets H in at most C_H intrinsic conjugacy classes. The full stabilizer's derivative need not be faithful, surjective onto L, or finite-index in L. Only the necessary implication to L-conjugacy and faithfulness on H are used. This is the indispensable ambient-conjugacy step.

## 6. Horowitz families and the final quantitative implication

Identify H with F_2 using its checked basis u_k,v_k. Its projective representation has an SL_2(R) lift because this group is free: choose any lift of each basis image and extend by the universal property. Each nonidentity word still projects to a hyperbolic element, so its lifted trace t has |t|>2.

Horowitz supplies 2^m pairwise nonconjugate nonidentity words for every m. The retained proof constructs freely reduced nonempty words and distinguishes their cyclic forms by a unique syllable, so neither nontriviality nor the conjugacy count is inferred from a numerical representation. Its trace identity is universal and therefore applies to the fixed SL_2(R) representation at hand. The elementary rank-two trace-polynomial input is valid even for special or reducible representations; no genericity assumption is hidden here.

For completeness, the source's recursive exponents are 2m and 2m-1, not 2^m and 2^m-1; 2^m is the number of sign choices. The candidate theorem does not depend on spelling out the recurrence. Its common trace t_m yields common spectral radius

lambda_m = (|t_m| + sqrt(t_m^2-4))/2 > 1.

Leininger's hyperbolic-derivative theorem identifies this spectral radius with pseudo-Anosov dilatation, with no missing square or factor of two: hyperbolic translation length is 2 log(lambda_m), while Teichmüller translation length is log(lambda_m).

The 2^m intrinsic H-classes occupy at least ceil(2^m/C_H) ambient Mod_g-classes, all in the same fixed I_g(k). Since C_H was fixed before m varies, this lower bound tends to infinity. This proves the stated negative answer under the source-context ambient convention.
