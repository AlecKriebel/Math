## 1. Whole-stabilizer cover and lattice interface

The decisive distinction is between integral matrices for two generators and an integral overgroup containing derivatives of **all** ambient conjugators. The former is insufficient; the checked deduction establishes the latter.

Let q be the fixed half-translation structure from the filling pair, with square charts and transition linear parts in{+I,-I}. Remove its true singularities. If its sign holonomy is nontrivial, the canonical orientation double cover is the cover corresponding to that sign kernel. If q is already a global square, use the original translation surface rather than a disconnected cover.

For any orientation-preserving affine self-map f ofq with projective derivativeM, the induced action on linear holonomy conjugates each holonomy matrix byM. Since+I and-I are central, the sign of every holonomy element is preserved. Applying the same statement to the inverse gives equality of the sign kernel. The covering lifting criterion therefore supplies lifts of **every** such f. An affine map preserves cone angles and hence the odd-order branching set; the lifts extend over the finitely many filled punctures. In the translation charts the lift has derivativeM or-M, so its projective derivative is exactly the base derivative. This elementary interface is proved here; it is not attributed as a verbatim statement in Hubert–Lelièvre.

The lifted surface is tiled by copies of the same unit squares with translation gluings; its holomorphic1-form has zeros at square vertices. The general relative-period lemmas2.1/2.3 of [Hubert–Lelièvre](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401056), physicalPDF7–8, apply without anH(2) or primitivity restriction. They give one fixed rank-two latticeLambda contained inZ² and invariant under the entire affine derivative group of the translation surface. Every lifted affine map permutes saddle connections between true zeros, and its inverse does too, soLambda is preserved exactly.

There is no rank-one degeneracy: the higher-genus compact translation surface has a true zero; following horizontal and vertical unit-square edges from a zero, continuing through regular vertices, eventually meets a zero in each direction. The finite directed-edge continuation cannot enter a disjoint cycle because it is locally invertible. The corresponding connection vectors supply independent horizontal and vertical periods. This also makes explicit the rank assertion used from lemma2.1.

Compactness and total area give determinant1 for the affine derivative. Consequently every base projective derivative is inL=SL(Lambda)/{±I}, which becomesPSL_2(Z) after choosing a positive lattice basis. Different choices of the two cover lifts only change sign. A coherent group-valued choice of lifts is unnecessary: individual lifting suffices for the common projective derivative containment, and the signs disappear inL. The source'sPDF8 expressly exhibits nonprimitive examples whose derivative groups are not contained inSL_2(Z) in the original unit coordinates; the lattice-basis qualification is therefore essential and is correctly retained.

No two-dimensional absolute-cohomology subspace on the original Torelli surface is assumed. The periods are relative periods on the translation cover, so nonorientability of Torelli pseudo-Anosov foliations supplies no counterexample. Nor is finite index of the two-twist group inL assumed.
