## Findings

### 1. Source scope: passed, with an editorial precision

The official OWR report's final sentence really does state the factorization broadly when isolated. On the preceding page, however, the labeled conjecture explicitly requires absolute irreducibility and a group generated by the support; the surrounding theorems concern indecomposable rack/quandle settings. Vendramin's 2014 notes explicitly place absolute simplicity in the factorization conjecture and attribute it to the same meeting.

It is therefore more precise to say that the standalone extraction **loses the surrounding scope**, rather than to imply that a hypothesis was deleted from the final sentence itself. This distinction is made in the authored proof. It does not invalidate the frozen candidate, which already distinguishes the literal wording from the intended conjecture.

Sources: [OWR report, pp. 2901–2902](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46419); [Vendramin, p. 6, Conjecture 4.4, and p. 7, Definition 5.2](https://leandrovendramin.org/notes/rauischholzhausen2014.pdf).

### 2. Nichols-algebra construction: passed

The matrix gives an invertible diagonal braiding. Its realization over G=(Z/5Z)^3 uses three characters, each well-defined on the group. The action preserves the degree spaces, so the Yetter–Drinfeld compatibility holds. The categorical braiding has exactly the stated matrix. The group is finite and generated by the support.

Every coordinate line is simultaneously invariant under action and coaction. Thus the exhibited module is not simple. The connected A3 diagram does not fix this failure of the intended hypothesis.

### 3. PBW applicability: passed, strengthened with a primary proof source

The frozen candidate cites the 2017 Andruskiewitsch–Angiono survey. Its base-field convention, root-of-unity parameter convention, Cartan diagram, positive roots, and PBW statement were checked directly. In addition, the earlier theorem was independently retrieved and inspected: Andruskiewitsch–Schneider, *Pointed Hopf algebras*, §6.1 and Theorem 6.13.

The earlier theorem requires N>2 and root-of-unity entries with diagonal value q, adjacent products q^-1, and nonadjacent products 1. The candidate satisfies those conditions with N=5. No assumption requiring primes larger than 7 is involved in this type-A theorem. All six root self-braidings have order 5. The two sources use different endpoint/order conventions; both produce root heights 1,1,1,2,2,3 and exponents 0,...,4.

Sources: [2001 primary theorem, pp. 45–53](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110136v1); [2017 explicit PBW description, pp. 40–41](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08387v1).

### 4. Arithmetic and polynomial obstruction: passed

- Ordinary Hilbert series: (5)_t^3 (5)_(t^2)^2 (5)_(t^3)
- Dimension: 5^6 = 15625
- Top degree: 40
- Full factorization: Phi_5^6 Phi_10^2 Phi_15
- Phi_3 multiplicity: 0
- Phi_15 multiplicity: 1
- Exact remainder modulo Phi_3: 5+5t
- Exact remainder modulo Phi_15: 0

The root-of-unity implication for every permitted factor is proved, so the obstruction excludes all finite products of the proposed form. It does not merely exclude bounded searches. Parameters equal to 1 and empty products are harmless and accounted for.

The Hilbert coefficients were independently recomputed in two ways: multiplying the six quantum-integer polynomials, and enumerating all 15625 PBW exponent tuples. They agree exactly. Polynomial division and cyclotomic calculations use integer arithmetic, with no floating-point root evaluation.

### 5. Scope and non-novelty: passed

The example belongs to established diagonal Cartan-type structure theory. This audit establishes neither a new Nichols algebra nor a new PBW theorem. Its value is correcting an overbroad standalone problem formulation. It does not challenge the group-type/absolutely-simple formulation, a general cyclotomic factorization assertion, or a product allowing arbitrary positive grading steps.
