# Primary-source theorem claims and boundaries

This restatement was made from the revised article and supplement before
using stored verifier outcomes.

## Principal-domain classification

For binary standard semi-directed strongly tree-child networks of level at
most two, on the same finite labelled leaf set, with the fixed one-step root
suppression convention, fixed observable `C/G/T` sectors, strict inheritance
probabilities, and every edge in the principal-positive K3P domain, the
source-relative regular containment relation satisfies

`N <= N'  iff  N and N' are ordinary-triangle equivalent  iff  their images
share a regular germ full-dimensional in both`.

Ordinary-triangle equivalence requires equal labelled reduced trees of blobs,
labelled mixed-graph isomorphism of corresponding nontriangle complete
factors, and coherent redirection of remaining ordinary triangles. Hence no
proper one-sided containment occurs for this local relation. It does not mean
equality of complete stochastic images or numerical-parameter
identifiability. Sources:
`manuscript/sections/02_main_theorems.tex:3-29` and
`03_conventions_model.tex:5-169`.

## Strict continuous time

The same classification, generic topology identifiability, and exact-real
reconstruction statements hold after restricting every edge to
`c>gt, g>ct, t>cg`. This is an open full-dimensional subdomain of the
principal-positive domain, and all sufficiency constructions are claimed
strictly within it. No boundary equality is included. Sources:
`02_main_theorems.tex:47-56` and `12_continuous_time.tex:1-46`.

## Genericity and reconstruction

For each fixed topology there is a proper topology-dependent complex
Zariski-closed exceptional set outside which the exact physical tensor
determines the labelled semi-directed topology modulo ordinary triangle
redirection. A finite exact-real procedure terminates using polynomial-sign
decisions and real-closed-field quantifier elimination. No bit-complexity,
conditioning, statistical, finite-sample, or sequence-length guarantee is
claimed. Sources: `02_main_theorems.tex:31-45` and
`11_genericity_reconstruction.tex:1-160`.

## Ordinary triangles and no proper containment

The three labelled orientations have normalized generic rank 14, share the
same irreducible eight-term quartic hypersurface `H14` in the normalized
15-dimensional chart, and share a strict continuous-time smooth rank-14 germ
relative to that hypersurface. They do not contain an ambient-open rank-15
three-leaf germ. Contextualization uses the same labelled contraction.
Sources: `05_three_leaf_geometry.tex:1-212` and
`10_global_classification.tex:1-105`.

## Weak-class sharpness

For every `n >= 3`, two labelled nonisomorphic and nontriangle-equivalent
networks in weak-but-not-strong tree-child level two have strict-CT images
sharing a common full-dimensional regular germ of dimension `6n-3`. At three
leaves both maps have rank 15 throughout a certified rational box, and each
identical labelled cherry adds six observable dimensions. Sources:
`02_main_theorems.tex:58-71` and `13_sharpness.tex:1-203`.

## Scope exclusions

The theorem excludes nonbinary or higher-level networks, arbitrary weakly
tree-child networks, boundary inheritance or edge probabilities, zero or
signed eigenvalues, untransported state-label permutations, nonreversible
models, pointwise identifiability at every parameter, and full numerical
parameter recovery. Source: `16_scope.tex:3-41`.
