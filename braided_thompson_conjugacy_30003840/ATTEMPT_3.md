# Attempt 3 — Exhaustive cabling into finite braid conjugacy

**Aim.** Replace the infinite kernel problem by fixed-strand braid algorithms and see whether this produces a terminating procedure. **Outcome:** an exact existential reduction and a certified positive semidecision. A missing refinement bound prevents a full decision algorithm.

Let K=ker(V_br -> V). Let x=(T,p,T) and y=(U,q,U), with p,q pure. For a refinement S of T, write p_S for the braid obtained by the corresponding sequence of parallel strand clonings. Each p_S is effectively computable. Its geometric meaning and injectivity are standard (Witzel, Lemma 5.9; Zaremsky, Section 1.2).

For n>=1 define two allowed permutation groups:

    A_n^F = {identity},
    A_n^T = <(1 2 ... n)> <= S_n.

Let B_n^A be their inverse images in B_n. For n=1 these are the trivial group. The subgroups B_n^F and B_n^T are precisely the pure and cyclically permuting braid groups.

## 1. The cabling criterion

**Proposition 3.1.** For H=F_br or T_br, x and y are conjugate in H iff there exist refinements S>=T and R>=U with the same number n of leaves, and a braid beta in B_n^A, such that p_S and q_R are conjugate by beta.

Here the orientation of beta is chosen so q_R=beta p_S beta^(-1); replacing beta by its inverse changes nothing about membership in B_n^A.

**Proof, sufficient direction.** Join the two trees S,R by beta to form a braided tree-pair diagram transporting the S-copy to the R-copy. Because beta's permutation is in A, this element lies in H. Diagram multiplication gives the asserted conjugacy of x and y.

**Proof, necessary direction.** Take an H-conjugator h. Refine its endpoint tree on the x-side to a common refinement with T. Strand cloning induces a refinement at h's other endpoint. Refine that other endpoint further to a common refinement with U; this induces only further refinement at the first endpoint and does not undo the earlier one. Thus h is represented between trees S>=T and R>=U with equal leaf counts. Since x and y are kernel elements, refining either of their trees refines both by the same forest; their braids stay pure. Stacking the aligned diagrams reduces the equality h x h^(-1)=y to equality of the corresponding braids at the R-copy. Fixed-tree braid groups inject into V_br, so the equality is an equality in B_n itself. Finally h's membership in H says exactly that beta's permutation is in A. ∎

The argument that two endpoint refinements suffice uses purity of p and q. No analogous bounded-strand statement for arbitrary nonkernel inputs is asserted here.

## 2. Each finite stage is decidable

Fix S,R,n. First solve ordinary conjugacy for p_S,q_R in B_n. If the answer is no, this stage fails. If yes, find beta_0 with q_R=beta_0 p_S beta_0^(-1). Compute a finite generating set for C_(B_n)(p_S), using the credited Franco–González-Meneses algorithm. Let D be the finite permutation group generated by the images of these generators.

By the transporter calculation of Attempt 1, an allowed beta exists iff

    pi_n(beta_0) D intersects A_n^H.                     (3.1)

This is a finite-group calculation. Enumerate the subgroup D by multiplying its finitely many generating permutations. This halts after at most n! distinct permutations. Test the identity (F case) or the n cyclic rotations (T case) for membership in the coset. Store generator words during enumeration; a successful intersection then supplies an actual centralizer word, hence an explicit permitted braid conjugator beta_0 c.

This is stronger than merely enumerating all braid words: every selected pair of refinements receives a conclusive yes or no, even when the centralizer is infinite. The use of a finite permutation image, not an assertion that the centralizer itself is finite, is essential.

## 3. What the unbounded procedure accomplishes

For n=max(number of leaves of T, number of leaves of U), n+1,..., enumerate all n-leaf refinements S of T and R of U. There are finitely many such pairs at each n. Run the terminating test above. If any stage succeeds, output its conjugator.

Every genuine H-conjugacy in K is eventually found by Proposition 3.1. A failure at a given n proves only that **those refinements** do not work. The number of strands cannot simply be frozen at the input size. The existence of an infinite family of stages is not a proof of undecidability either.

An explicit finite check at the start is useful: unequal reduced linking matrices from Attempt 2 reject the pair immediately. Equal matrices still leave the nonabelian commutator example and the general braid comparison to handle.

## 4. An exact formulation of the remaining bound problem

For conjugate pairs x,y in K define w_H(x,y) to be the least n at which Proposition 3.1 has a witness.

**Proposition 3.2.** The conjugacy problem for kernel inputs x,y, with conjugators restricted to H, is decidable iff there is a total computable function B_H(x,y) such that every positive instance has w_H(x,y)<=B_H(x,y).

**Proof.** Given B, check the finitely many stages up to its value, using Section 2. Positive instances succeed by the bound, and negative ones do not. Conversely, given a decision algorithm, first decide the pair. For a negative pair return 0. For a positive pair run Section 3 until a witness appears and return its leaf count. Both branches terminate, defining B. ∎

This equivalence shows why claiming a bound without proving it would assume away the hard part. It also identifies a sharply scoped possible next theorem: an effective common-refinement bound, or another terminating negative certificate. Neither is established here. In particular, solving every fixed-strand braid stage does not itself solve Question 48, even on all kernel inputs.

**Substantive result:** a necessary-and-sufficient finite-refinement witness criterion, an effective constrained-braid test at each stage, and an exact characterization of the missing global termination input.
