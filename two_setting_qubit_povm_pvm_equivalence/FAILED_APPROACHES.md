# Failed approaches and structural information retained

## 1. Universal intrinsic \((7,7)\) Hessian signature

Numerical samples initially suggested that the 14-dimensional residual hypersurface always had a \((7,7)\) second-form signature. This is false. Exact Lorentz coordinates show that projected signatures vary. The useful invariant is instead the ambient inertia \((4,12)\) after the strict dual multiplier is made positive definite.

## 2. One-sided polar-filter projectivization

Deleting one ternary row and polar-decomposing the remaining \(2\times2\) block gives a canonical PVM branch. Coupling deletions on both parties yields a nine-branch identity that reproduces the ternary–ternary probability block exactly.

It does not, in general, reproduce the two binary-setting blocks. A one-sided capacity calculation already fails for an equatorial trine: the maximum branch capacity is \(3/2\) while the required total is \(2\). This showed that any constructive equality proof had to use genuinely two-sided compensation.

The final proof bypasses the missing global branch formula by excluding all strict exposed maxima.

## 3. Broad numerical Bell search

Random residual POVMs, deformed trines, asymmetric Schmidt states, and near-projective ternary POVMs produced apparent finite-sample gaps. Every such gap closed when the PVM support oracle was enlarged or optimized more accurately. This motivated the equality route but was not used as evidence in the theorem.

## 4. Direct elimination of the full 14-parameter amplitude map

Attempting to eliminate \(A,B,C\) from the four modulus-square blocks was too large. The Lorentz metric removes local unitary and phase gauges first and replaces the full elimination by five quadratic null equations in \((P,g)\).

## 5. Global defining hypersurface polynomial

No compact explicit polynomial \(G(P)=0\) for the projected residual image was needed. The incidence lift is smoother and supplies its normal and second form without eliminating the four metric variables.

## 6. Treating singular behavior points as automatically harmless

This is invalid. Rank-zero examples have a semidefinite local second form and therefore cannot be dismissed by generic curvature. Their exact deterministic decomposition is essential. Rank-one points require the separate projective-fiber theorem.

## 7. Independent decomposition of each local POVM

Separately decomposing a ternary POVM into binary/projective pieces does not produce one common shared-randomness decomposition of the complete Bell behavior. The final equality proof avoids this compatibility error. The only explicit decomposition used is the rank-zero joint deterministic decomposition, where all four setting blocks are checked simultaneously.
