## Reconstruction of the clearing proof

1. Reduce the augmentation complex at `t=0` to elementary-divisor bases over `Z_2`. A bounded free complex over this PID splits into scalar disks and its two free cohomology lines. If the disk scalars are `2^{e_i}` times units, the sum of the positive `e_i` is the total torsion length, at most `h_1`. The basis changes are unimodular and constant, so they lift to `R` and do not introduce negative Laurent powers.
2. Cancel those same disk positions formally upstairs. Every rational basis change and every determinant-comparison scalar is obtained from finitely many additions, multiplications, and divisions by the successive pivots. The operation count and matrix dimensions are bounded in terms of `r_0` alone.
3. Represent each pivot as `N_i/D_i`, where `N_i` is polynomial in the transformed original differential entries and `D_i` is a product of earlier pivot numerators. Let `S` be the multiplicative set generated by the `N_i`. At the central specialization the actual rational pivots are precisely the nonzero elementary divisors. Consequently `N_i(0)` is nonzero: its valuation is `e_i + v(D_i(0))`. This recursion bounds the valuations of all selected numerators by a constant depending only on `r_0,h_1`; it cannot import an uncontrolled cancellation valuation from a different numerator.
4. The cancellations are valid in `S^{-1}R`, even if some `N_i` are zero divisors. On every character where all pivot numerators are nonzero, the remaining complex consists of one free node in degree one and one in degree two. If the remaining differential is nonzero, the projected cycle is zero over the field and the stipulated acyclic coordinate is zero. If it is zero, the determinant coordinate is the product of the projected cycle and functional times the canceled-disk factors.
5. All denominators in this finite rational expression are products of the selected pivot numerators. A product `P` of sufficiently high bounded powers clears them and contains every `N_i` as a factor. Thus on the surviving characters the expression has the form `u_x = 2^{-a_0} eta_x Q_x/P_x` with `Q in R`. Since `eta` appears only multiplicatively in the numerator, it does not enter `P`.
6. Take `f = 2^{a_0}P^2` and `d = eta P Q`. On surviving characters, `f_x u_x = d_x`. On every character killed by localization, `P_x=0`, hence both sides of this equality are zero. The augmentation `P_0` lies in `Lambda`, its constant coefficient is nonzero with the preceding valuation bound, and the polynomial degree of `P` is bounded in terms of `r_0`. Hence `v(f_0(0))` is bounded by a constant depending only on `r_0,h_1,a_0`.

For the pole assertion, a polynomial of bounded degree `E` in differential entries whose reductions modulo `2^M` have no powers below `-L_D(M)` has no powers below `-E L_D(M)` (enlarge the bound to be nonnegative if necessary). Integral constant basis changes and powers of 2 do not worsen it. Nothing here requires a bound on `z`, `lambda`, or `eta`.

## Infinite tails and the transfer estimate

Reduction modulo `2^M` identifies `O/2^M O` with `(Z/2^M Z)((t))`. The inverse limit consists of bilateral Laurent series with integral coefficients whose coefficients tend to zero 2-adically as the exponent tends to minus infinity. Their DVR valuation is the minimum coefficient valuation. Products are defined by convergent constant-degree convolutions: in either direction of an infinite convolution, one factor's negative coefficient tends to zero.

At the chosen nonzero address, every negative coefficient of `f_x` is divisible by `2^M`; coefficients below `-L(M)` have this property from the pole hypothesis, and the finitely many remaining negative coefficients are tested. Its constant coefficient has valuation `c`. Therefore `v_O(f_x)<=c`. Since `d_x=f_xu_x` is integral, `v_O(u_x)>=-v_O(f_x)>=-c`. The same bound holds centrally, where `v_O(f_0)<=c`. All coefficients of both `u` series are consequently in `2^{-c}Z_2`, and their negative coefficients vanish by the separate `Lambda[1/2]` hypothesis.

Hence each nonconstant contribution to `[t^0](f_xu_x)` has valuation at least `M-c`, and their sum converges. The tested constant coefficients of `f,d` give

```
f_0(0) (u_x(0)-u_0(0)) = 0 mod 2^{M-c}.
```

Division by a scalar of valuation `c` proves the advertised `M-2c` bound. No test on negative coefficients of `d_x` is involved.

The independent tail check found a sharp local error: take `f=2^c+2^M/(t-2)` and `u=2^{-c}/(1-t)`. Then `v_O(f)=c`, `v_O(u)=-c`, and the negative-tail contribution to `[t^0](fu)` is exactly `-2^{M-c}`. This can be embedded in a valid group-ring family using `f=2^c+2^{M-1}(1-g)/(t-2)` for `M>c`; its augmentation is `2^c`, while the other sign has the displayed tail. The intermediate `M-c` loss is therefore real, not an omitted convergence effect.
