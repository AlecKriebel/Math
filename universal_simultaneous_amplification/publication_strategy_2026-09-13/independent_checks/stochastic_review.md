## Checks and their implications

1. **Exact trace, not an independence approximation.** At zero cut, internal absorption gives an invertible finite fast block. Eliminating that block yields `A + B(I-Q0)^(-1)C0`. Interior macro states have positive paths to an absorbing boundary. At each fixed finite graph, this supports compact-uniform convergence of the scaled boundary-value system. One initial mutant resolves in one module, which gives the finite weighted trace formula.

2. **Early establishment has adequate precision.** With the hub and leaves still resident, both displayed ordinary-count odds equal `r[1+O(1/C)]` uniformly up to `K=ceil(A0 log C)`. The number of stopped ordinary changes is `O(K)` in expectation and the hub-change hazard per ordinary change is `O(1/C)`. Thus an error `O(log C/C)` is adequate: it is smaller than `C^(-3/4)`. Choosing the logarithmic multiplier using the lower endpoint of a fixed fitness compact also makes the geometric failure tails smaller than this scale.

3. **Hidden coordinates are not improperly assumed Markov.** The proof uses intensity-ratio comparisons conditioned on the complete past for the ordinary count and deficit. The displayed generator bounds imply a negative deficit drift above a fixed boundary layer. The exponential excursion estimates, polynomial clock horizon, and expected number of excursion starts supply the claimed confinement error. This is the right mechanism for dependence on moving hub and leaf coordinates.

4. **Bd cleanup accounts for actual fixation.** The two-phase leaf committor has the stated monotonicity in the favorable rates. Its comparison assigns strip exit a favorable terminal value and separately charges that exit as exceptional; this avoids conditioning on future confinement. The stopped leaf trace has `O(m)` expected changes, each with `O(C)` mean wait. Once hub and leaves are mutant, the displayed deficit drift is correct. The integrated deficit controls hub-loss hazard, producing a fixed success probability. Stopping-time regeneration then boosts this to arbitrary polynomial reliability. The distinguished-leaf restart argument gives failure `O(m log C/C)=o(1)`, which is sufficient after multiplying by leaf mass `m/C`.

5. **Reciprocal estimates have the extra factor needed for the sweep.** The subcritical ordinary process has bounded particle-time and exponentially decaying hazard-weighted size tails. A Bd mutant-hub excursion returns quickly because its loss rate is at least `m`. A dB hub excursion can generate leaves, but its ordinary cloud is controlled by subcritical immigration over a finite horizon and its later reactivation probability is `O((m+constant)/C)`. The stated order of limits establishes `y_(C,L)=o(1)` for fixed `L`; combining this with the first-hub-change probability `O(1/C)` gives `x_(C,1)=o(1/C)`. Both reciprocal portal quantities consequently have the strength needed for `q C'/B=o(q/C)`.

6. **Gate rates and error propagation check out.** Summing the cross-module Bd and dB events gives the rate table in the manuscript. The one-pair odds simplify to `sigma(r^2-1)` and `2r(r-1)/sigma`. Failure from an already mutant center requires a reversal, so the union bound over at most `q` forward conversions is valid without treating reversal as irreversible failure. The pair starting contribution only requires an `o(1)` gate approximation because its initial mass is `O(q/C)`; the center contribution requires and receives `o(q/C)` control. The `O(1/C)` difference between the dB baseline and `1-1/r` is also negligible at this scale.

7. **The quantifier argument is genuine.** For each finite `t`, exact real-algebraic decision of the compact inequality and compact-uniform convergence guarantee termination of the least-dyadic search. Every fixed fitness in the open target interval belongs to `I_t` eventually. The chosen perturbation changes the scaled gain by at most `1/t`, so it cannot change the eventual sign of a strictly positive limiting response. This constructs one family independently of the subsequently selected fitness; it does not establish a useful numerical bound on its sizes or coupling weights.

## Clarifications worth making before submission

- **dB cleanup activation at lines 903–937.** The proof first reaches `R<=R0`, then waits for hub activation, and then writes suppressed-path estimates with initial deficit at most `R0`. The deficit can change during the activation wait. The natural repair is brief: from `R<=R0`, the hub activation intensity has a uniform positive lower bound and the total ordinary-change intensity has a uniform finite upper bound. Therefore activation before the next ordinary change has a fixed positive probability, even as leaves change. Use that event to start the displayed successful attempt, and regenerate after the complementary event. This preserves a constant success probability and the subsequent amplification by `O(log C)` attempts. Alternatively explicitly control the deficit at activation. This is a repairable exposition/checkability gap, not a demonstrated false theorem.

- **Weak-cut inverse wording.** In the final paragraph of the weak-cut proof, boundedness of “nearby absorbing inverses” should mean the fast-block inverse and the inverse of the *scaled* interior Schur complement. The inverse of the full positive-cut transient system generally diverges as the coupling tends to zero. The surrounding argument uses the correct scaled system, but a literal unqualified reading would be false.
