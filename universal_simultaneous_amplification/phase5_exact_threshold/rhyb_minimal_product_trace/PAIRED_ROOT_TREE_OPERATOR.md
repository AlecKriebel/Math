# Paired root-tree determinant form of the minimal product

Date: 2026-08-13 (America/Los_Angeles)

No external communication or graph search was used.

## Status

This note gives an exact operator form of the universal minimal stationary
product.  It is not a new strengthening and it does not prove the open sign.
Its purpose is to expose the precise common object left by Boolean duality,
the Bd weighted adjoint, and the dB geometric resolvent.

After passing to Markov-tree cofactors, every stationary normalizer cancels.
The conjecture becomes a product of two root-marked determinant derivatives.
The Bd factor is the same determinant derivative for a reversed-arrow
Schrodinger operator, while the dB factor is generated by a sum of positive
targetwise resolvents of that reversed-arrow dynamics.  The Cauchy
orientation square from the singleton-root reduction survives literally as
a square between paired singleton-root trees.

This is the smallest presently known classical operator target retaining
all of the following simultaneously:

1. the higher-rank stationary equations;
2. the two physical portal laws;
3. the Bd orientation defect;
4. the dB locked-target collision history; and
5. the nonnegative root-assignment orientation square.

## 1. The root-marked determinant functional

Let `Q` be the row generator of a finite irreducible continuous-time Markov
chain on a state space `Omega`.  Put

\[
 \tau_Q(a)=\det(-Q)^{\widehat a,\widehat a}.           \tag{1}
\]

Every `tau_Q(a)` is positive.  For a column mark `h` define

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal T_Q(h)=
 \left.{d\over dt}\det\{-Q+t\operatorname{diag}(h)\}
 \right|_{t=0}.}                                      \tag{2}
\]

Jacobi's formula at the singular matrix `-Q`, or direct cofactor expansion,
gives

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal T_Q(h)=\sum_{a\in\Omega}\tau_Q(a)h(a).}       \tag{3}
\]

Thus `T_Q(h)` is the total weight of directed in-arborescences whose root is
marked by `h`.  If `Z_Q=sum_a tau_Q(a)`, the Markov-chain tree theorem gives

\[
                         \pi_Q(a)={\tau_Q(a)\over Z_Q}.          \tag{4}
\]

There is an exact Poisson gauge invariance:

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal T_Q(h+Q\psi)=\mathcal T_Q(h)}         \tag{5}
\]

for every column `psi`.  Indeed, the cofactor row `tau_Q` is stationary for
`Q`.  Formula (5) is the determinant form of stationary averaging; it will
recover the singleton Schur reward without losing any tree weights.

## 2. Exact cancellation of both tree normalizers

For `U in {Bd,dB}`, let `Q_U` be the exact recurrent OR-dual generator at
fitness `r>1`.  In the loopless dB chain the transient full set is omitted.
Put

\[
 g(A)={|A|\over s}-p,\qquad p=1-{1\over r},            \tag{6}
\]

and, for a portal law `omega`,

\[
 f_\omega(A)=
 \begin{cases}\omega_i,&A=\{i\},\\0,&|A|\ne1.
 \end{cases}                                          \tag{7}
\]

The tree theorem gives, exactly,

\[
 q_U^\omega={\mathcal T_{Q_U}(f_\omega)\over Z_U},
 \qquad
 \rho_U-p={\mathcal T_{Q_U}(g)\over Z_U}.             \tag{8}
\]

Consequently the portal-general minimal stationary product

\[
 q_Bq_D\ge r^3[\rho_{Bd}-p]_+[\rho_{dB}-p]_+          \tag{MP}
\]

is equivalent to the normalizer-free paired root-tree inequality

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal T_{Q_{Bd}}(f_\gamma)
 \mathcal T_{Q_{dB}}(f_\alpha)
 \ge r^3[\mathcal T_{Q_{Bd}}(g)]_+
          [\mathcal T_{Q_{dB}}(g)]_+.}                \tag{PTR}
\]

No limiting argument is used in this cancellation.  `(PTR)` is `(MP)`
multiplied by the two positive Markov-tree partition functions.

## 3. Exact recovery of the singleton-root Schur trace

Partition `Omega=S union R`, where `S` is the singleton sector.  Write

\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{SS}&Q_{SR}\\Q_{RS}&Q_{RR}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad G=(-Q_{RR})^{-1},                              \tag{9}
\]

and define

\[
 T=Q_{SS}+Q_{SR}GQ_{RS},
 \qquad \phi=g_S+Q_{SR}Gg_R.                          \tag{10}
\]

Take `psi_S=0` and `psi_R=Gg_R`.  Then

\[
                         g+Q\psi=(\phi,0)^T.           \tag{11}
\]

The gauge identity (5) therefore proves

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal T_Q(g)=\sum_{i\in V}\tau_Q(\{i\})\phi_i.}   \tag{12}
\]

Block determinant elimination also gives the exact cofactor relation

\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_Q(\{i\})=det(-Q_{RR})\,\tau_T(i).}             \tag{13}
\]

Equations (12)--(13) show that `(PTR)` and the singleton-root repayment
inequality are the same certificate at two resolutions.  The killed Green
reward `phi` is precisely the Poisson gauge which moves the signed
higher-rank tree mark onto singleton roots.  It is not an auxiliary
approximation.

## 4. The orientation square as paired root-tree transport

Set

\[
 A_i=\gamma_i\tau_{Bd}(\{i\}),
 \qquad B_i=\alpha_i\tau_{dB}(\{i\}),
 \qquad \mathcal H_T=\sum_i\sqrt{A_iB_i}.             \tag{14}
\]

The portal side of `(PTR)` has the exact Lagrange decomposition

\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal T_{Q_{Bd}}(f_\gamma)
 \mathcal T_{Q_{dB}}(f_\alpha)
 ={}&\mathcal H_T^2\\
 &+{1\over2}\sum_{i,j}
   \left(\sqrt{A_iB_j}-\sqrt{A_jB_i}\right)^2.
                                                               \tag{15}
\end{split}
\]

The second line is not a disposable Cauchy loss.  It is the exact excess of
the two swapped root assignments `(Bd at i,dB at j)` and
`(Bd at j,dB at i)` over their geometric mean.  A proof of `(PTR)` may spend
this square to absorb orientation or target-locking defects.  Replacing the
left side by `H_T^2` is the stronger root-Hellinger route; it is not known
to be universally necessary.

For physical loads `x_i` and `e_i=1/d_i`, the entrywise strengthening is
the paired-tree inequality

\[
\begin{split}
 &\tau_{Bd}(\{i\})e_j\tau_{dB}(\{j\})
 +\tau_{Bd}(\{j\})e_i\tau_{dB}(\{i\})\\
 &\qquad\ge
 r^3(e_i+e_j)
 [\mathcal T_{Q_{Bd}}(g)]_+
 [\mathcal T_{Q_{dB}}(g)]_+.                          \tag{16}
\end{split}

This is exactly the old entrywise portal target after multiplication by the
two tree normalizers.  It makes clear what a forest proof must transport:
positive rank-marked root trees on the right into the two swapped
singleton-root tree assignments on the left.

## 5. The common reversed-arrow operator

Let `L=Q_Bd`, let `C` be the Bd dual after reversing every underlying base
arrow, and put

\[
 \mu(A)=(r-1)^{|A|},
 \qquad
 V(A)=r\{A_\partial(A)-B_\partial(A)\}.               \tag{17}
\]

The proved weighted-adjoint identity is

\[
 L^{\dagger_\mu}=C+\operatorname{diag}(V).             \tag{18}
\]

Because diagonal marks commute with the diagonal similarity defining the
weighted adjoint, transposition and similarity preserve the whole marked
determinant:

\[
 \boxed{
 \det\{-L+t\operatorname{diag}(h)\}
 =\det\{-C-\operatorname{diag}(V)
            +t\operatorname{diag}(h)\}.}              \tag{19}
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal T_{Q_{Bd}}(h)
 =\left.{d\over dt}\det\{-C-\operatorname{diag}(V)
                     +t\operatorname{diag}(h)\}
   \right|_{t=0}.}                                    \tag{20}
\]

The Bd side of `(PTR)` is therefore a root-marked cofactor functional of one
reversed-arrow Schrodinger operator.  The signed cut potential `V` cannot be
deleted: it is exactly the orientation defect.

## 6. dB as a positive targetwise resolvent sum

For target `v`, let `S_v` be one selective row-`P` sample, `N_v` one neutral
replacement, and `G_v` the geometric row-`P` burst, all as row operators on
nonempty sets.  Put

\[
 a={r-1\over r},
 \qquad
 C_v=(N_v-I)+(r-1)(S_v-I).                            \tag{21}
\]

The exact batching identity is

\[
 (I-aS_v)(G_v-I)={1\over r}C_v.                       \tag{22}
\]

Since `S_v` is stochastic and `0<a<1`,

\[
 R_v=(I-aS_v)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}a^kS_v^k              \tag{23}
\]

is positivity preserving.  Summing (22) over targets gives the exact dB
operator

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_{dB}={1\over r}\sum_vR_vC_v.}                     \tag{24}
\]

Equations (20) and (24) place both factors of `(PTR)` over the same local
reversed-arrow pieces `C_v`:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{Bd:}&C+\operatorname{diag}(V),\\[1mm]
 \text{dB:}&r^{-1}\sum_v(I-aS_v)^{-1}C_v.
 \end{array}}                                        \tag{25}
\]

The difference is now exact and irreducible.  Bd carries a signed global
cut potential under weighted adjunction.  dB applies a positive geometric
resolvent separately before summing the target blocks.  Moving a common
inverse outside the sum would erase target locking and is invalid.

## 7. A two-copy cut envelope which preserves root swapping

There is one useful statewise inequality compatible with the orientation
square.  For a nonabsorbing mutant set `A`, let

\[
 a(A)=\sum_{i\in A,j\notin A}P_{ij},
 \qquad
 b(A)=\sum_{i\in A,j\notin A}P_{ji},
 \qquad \kappa(A)={a(A)\over b(A)}.                  \tag{26}
\]

The exact effective up/down odds satisfy

\[
 R_{Bd}(A)=r\kappa(A),
 \qquad
 R_{dB}(A)\le {r^2\over\kappa(A)}.                   \tag{27}
\]

Hence for arbitrary, possibly different, sets `A,B`,

\[
 R_{Bd}(A)R_{dB}(B)
 \le r^3{\kappa(A)\over\kappa(B)}.                  \tag{28}
\]

The orientation factor cancels only after retaining the swapped assignment:

\[
 \boxed{
 \{R_{Bd}(A)R_{dB}(B)\}
 \{R_{Bd}(B)R_{dB}(A)\}\le r^6.}                   \tag{29}
\]

Thus the geometric mean of the two root assignments has the sharp factor
`r^3`, exactly as suggested by (15).  By contrast, either unsymmetrized
cross assignment can exceed `r^3`; on the unweighted three-path at
`r=3/2`, the product for `A={L}`, `B={L,C}` is `3*(5/2)>r^3`.

Equation (29) is a genuine two-copy structural lemma.  It explains why a
proof-first path reversal must keep both swapped histories and why the
orientation square is potentially the required arithmetic-over-geometric
slack.  It does not by itself prove `(PTR)`: one still has to show that this
swapped local bound survives the killed Green/forest composition in
(20)--(24).  Scalar rank bias cannot do that; sharp weak cuts can sustain
two favorable macro biases while losing the entrance mass recorded by the
singleton-root cofactors.

## 8. Exact obstruction to canonical synchronized composition

There is a precise obstruction to lifting (29) by the most direct positive
path construction.  Expand a dB macroedge with retained target `v` into its
labelled source history.  The reversed-arrow `C` source law and the
Bd-oriented source law are

\[
 p_v^C(u)={w_{uv}\over d_v},\qquad
 p_v^L(u)={{w_{uv}/d_u}\over t_v},\qquad
 t_v=\sum_z{w_{zv}\over d_z}.                         \tag{30}
\]

Writing `c_v=d_v/t_v`, their exact likelihood ratio is

\[
                         {p_v^L(u)\over p_v^C(u)}={c_v\over d_u}.
                                                               \tag{31}
\]

Suppose a locked history has `k` selective samples and one final neutral
sample, with sources `u_0,...,u_k`.  The common geometric factor cancels,
so

\[
 {W_L(u_0,\ldots,u_k)\over W_C(u_0,\ldots,u_k)}
 ={c_v^{k+1}\over\prod_{j=0}^k d_{u_j}}.              \tag{32}
\]

Let `B` be the set of distinct sampled sources, let `n_i` be the
multiplicity of source `i`, and put

\[
                         D(A)=\prod_{i\in A}d_i.
\]

The singleton neutral step forces the degree factor in this canonical
arrow-reversal lift.  After removing its endpoint ratio, (32) leaves

\[
 \boxed{
 {W_L/W_C\over D(\{v\})/D(B)}
 ={1\over t_v}c_v^k
   \prod_{i\in B}d_i^{1-n_i}.}                      \tag{33}
\]

The last factor records repeated sources.  It is neither a target clock nor
an endpoint-set coboundary, and therefore does not telescope under projected
set-path rerooting.

This failure is exact at the first possible collision.  At `r=3/2`, take
the weighted path

\[
                    0\mathbin{-}^{1}2\mathbin{-}^{17}1
\]

and retain target `v=2`.  Here

\[
 p_2^C=(1/18,17/18),\qquad p_2^L=(1/2,1/2),
 \qquad t_2=2,\quad c_2=9.                            \tag{34}
\]

For one selective sample followed by the neutral sample, the normalized
endpoint clocks in (33), for endpoints `{0}`, `{1}`, and `{0,1}`, are

\[
                         {9\over2},\qquad {9\over34},\qquad {9\over2}.
                                                               \tag{35}
\]

Thus no clock depending only on the initial set, retained target, and
history length makes the labelled histories endpoint-diagonally conjugate.
After summing the entire geometric burst, the three normalized clocks are

\[
                         {53\over90},\qquad {37\over90},
                         \qquad {1961\over450}.        \tag{36}
\]

Projection to a macroedge therefore does not repair the defect.

There is a second, separate synchronization loss.  The factors in (29) are
ratios of *total* upward and downward cut rates.  A tree selects individual
state edges, while a dB macroedge expands into histories of variable length.
Applying (29) step by step would therefore require a noncanonical matching
of both the tree edges and all hidden source draws.  Formula (33) shows that
even the natural equal-length labelled matching fails to descend to mutant
sets.

This refutes only the canonical positive transport consisting of

1. expansion into target-locked histories;
2. step-synchronous labelled arrow reversal; and
3. telescoping through the multiplicative endpoint degree potential.

It does **not** refute `(PTR)`.  A global grouping of multiplicity-labelled
histories, or a forest identity which averages the collision factor in
(33) together with the orientation square, remains logically possible.
No search over forest matchings was made.

## 9. Exact remaining operator theorem

Combining the preceding identities leaves one explicit statement.  For the
common reversed-arrow blocks in (25), prove

\[
\begin{split}
 &\mathcal T_{C+V}(f_\gamma)
 \mathcal T_{r^{-1}\sum_vR_vC_v}(f_\alpha)\\
 &\qquad\ge r^3
 [\mathcal T_{C+V}(g)]_+
 [\mathcal T_{r^{-1}\sum_vR_vC_v}(g)]_+,             \tag{37}
\end{split}

where `T_(C+V)` means the determinant derivative in (20), not the stationary
functional of a conservative generator.  The Bd operator is singular and
similar to `L^T`, so its diagonal cofactors are positive despite the signed
potential.

A valid proof may use either of two mechanisms:

1. a paired-tree transport which keeps both root assignments and spends the
   square in (15); or
2. a Picone/determinant comparison proving the stronger root-Hellinger term.

The first route is strictly weaker and is the current proof-first target.
A targetwise Loewner comparison, a common trace-edge adjoint, and an
unsymmetrized path-odds multiplication have already been ruled out.
