# The minimal stationary product as a two-copy rank-three trace

Date: 2026-08-13 (America/Los_Angeles)

No external communication or graph search was used.

## Status

This note proves an exact reduction of the minimal one-module gate
inequality

\[
 q_Bq_D\ge r^3[\rho_{Bd}-p]_+[\rho_{dB}-p]_+,
 \qquad p=1-\frac1r,                                  \tag{1}
\]

to a two-copy rank-three excursion inequality and to an equivalent
singleton/doubleton Schur trace.  It also proves an exact dB hole identity
which makes (1) automatic in one explicit structural regime.

The sign in (1) remains open.  The value of the reduction is that it removes
two unnecessary obligations from the proof search:

1. one does not need the separate density assertion
   `rho_dB <= r-1`; and
2. one does not need the three-copy forcing used for the stronger Hellinger
   form BDM.

The resulting open statement is a product of two *signed excess occupation
rewards* generated by the same conductance matrix.  All ranks at least three
enter only through the killed Green corrections and the triple-to-doubleton
return current.  Thus this is the smallest honest higher-rank target presently
available for the minimal gate theorem.

## 1. Exact dB hole deficit

Let `P` be the row-stochastic kernel of a connected loopless weighted graph
on `s` vertices, and let `pi_D` be the stationary law of the geometric-OR dB
dual at fitness `r>1`.  Put

\[
 H_{vi}=h_r(P_{vi})=\frac{rP_{vi}}{1+(r-1)P_{vi}},
 \qquad T_i=\sum_vH_{vi},\qquad
 \sigma=\sum_i\frac1{1+T_i}.                          \tag{2}
\]

For the random hole set `C=V\setminus A`, define

\[
 W(C)=\sum_{i,v\in C}\frac{H_{vi}}{1+T_i}.             \tag{3}
\]

Stationarity of the coordinate `1_{i in A}` gives

\[
 \Pr(i\notin A)
 =\frac{1+\sum_vH_{vi}\Pr(i,v\notin A)}{1+T_i}.       \tag{4}
\]

Summing (4) yields the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{
 E_{\pi_D}|V\setminus A|=\sigma+E_{\pi_D}W(V\setminus A).}
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]

Since `rho_dB=E|A|/s` and `1-p=1/r`, (5) is equivalently

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_{dB}-p
 =\frac1s\left\{\frac sr-\sigma-EW(V\setminus A)\right\}.}
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]

This gives a proof-first branch pruning rule:

\[
 \boxed{\sigma\ge \frac sr\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \rho_{dB}\le p,}                              \tag{7}
\]

so the right side of (1) vanishes.  The exact suppression gap in this regime
is

\[
 p-\rho_{dB}
 =\frac1s\left\{\sigma-\frac sr+EW(V\setminus A)\right\}.           \tag{8}
\]

Equality in (7) can occur only if both `sigma=s/r` and `W(C)=0`
`pi_D`-almost surely.  In particular, whenever the recurrent support gives
positive mass to a hole set containing the endpoints of a positive edge,
the second term is strictly positive.  No complementary-level conjecture is
used.

## 2. Alternating-excursion coordinates

Use the exact low/high split from the rank-three renewal reduction:

\[
 \mathcal L=\{A:1\le |A|\le2\},\qquad
 \mathcal H=\{A:|A|\ge3\}.                            \tag{9}
\]

In this section assume that both blocks are nonempty for both rules, as they
are for every module of order at least four.  The Schur trace in Section 3
also covers the order-two and mixed order-three boundary cases by taking the
empty high-block correction to be zero.

For `U in {Bd,dB}`, let

\[
 T_U=T_U^-+T_U^+,qquad M_U=M_U^-+M_U^+,qquad
 S_U^\omega                                             \tag{10}
\]

be respectively the cycle time, rank reward, and portal-weighted singleton
reward of one low/high renewal cycle.  Here `omega=gamma` for Bd and
`omega=alpha` for dB.  The exact renewal identities are

\[
 q_U^\omega=\frac{S_U^\omega}{T_U},\qquad
 \rho_U=\frac{M_U}{sT_U}.                              \tag{11}
\]

Define the signed excess reward

\[
                         X_U=M_U-spT_U.                 \tag{12}
\]

Then `rho_U-p=X_U/(sT_U)`.  Consequently, on the only nontrivial branch
`X_B>0` and `X_D>0`, (1) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 S_{Bd}^{\gamma}S_{dB}^{\alpha}
 \ge \frac{r^3}{s^2}X_{Bd}X_{dB}.}                    \tag{13}
\]

Call (13) the **minimal product excursion repayment inequality** (MPER).
Unlike the Hellinger RTER, it has no density side condition and no square
roots.  It is equivalent to the minimal gate theorem, not a strengthening
of it.

Writing the rewards out shows exactly where a proof must act:

\[
 X_U=\nu_U^-G_U^-(k-sp\mathbf1)
     +\nu_U^+G_U^+(k-sp\mathbf1).                     \tag{14}
\]

The second term contains every rank at least three.  The boundary law of
that term is not free: it is generated by the explicit triple-to-doubleton
current

\[
 \eta_{Bd}(C)=\sum_{j\notin C}\pi_{Bd}(Cj)
                    \sum_{u\in C}P_{uj},              \tag{15}
\]

\[
 \eta_{dB}(C)=\sum_{j\notin C}\pi_{dB}(Cj)
                    \frac{P_{jC}}{r-(r-1)P_{jC}}.     \tag{16}
\]

Thus MPER is a comparison of two honest excursion systems induced by the
same undirected conductances.  Searching over arbitrary rank profiles would
discard precisely the constraint that remains to be proved.

## 3. Two-copy Schur trace

The same reduction has an even smaller Poisson-certificate form.  Let

\[
 \widehat Q_U=Q_U^{--}+Q_U^{-+}G_U^+Q_U^{+-}           \tag{17}
\]

be the stochastic Schur trace generator on singleton and doubleton states,
and define its Green-corrected time and rank rewards

\[
 \tau_U=\mathbf1+Q_U^{-+}G_U^+\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 \kappa_U=k^-+Q_U^{-+}G_U^+k^+.                      \tag{18}
\]

If `pi_U^-` is the unnormalized stationary low-sector measure, then

\[
 \pi_U^-\tau_U=1,\qquad
 \pi_U^-\kappa_U=s\rho_U.                             \tag{19}
\]

Let `f_gamma` and `f_alpha` be the portal rewards supported on singleton
states, and put

\[
 \chi_U=\frac{\kappa_U}{s}-p\tau_U.                   \tag{20}
\]

Equations (19)--(20) give

\[
 \pi_U^-\chi_U=\rho_U-p,
 \qquad
 \pi_{Bd}^-f_\gamma=q_B,
 \qquad
 \pi_{dB}^-f_\alpha=q_D.                              \tag{21}
\]

Therefore the raw product gap is the stationary mean of the following
forcing on only two low states `ell,d`:

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat F(\ell,d)
 =f_\gamma(\ell)f_\alpha(d)
   -r^3\chi_{Bd}(\ell)\chi_{dB}(d).}                  \tag{22}
\]

As a matrix on the two-copy space, this forcing is the difference of two
rank-one tensors and hence has rank at most two.  The complexity is in the
Green-corrected vectors `chi_U`, not in a large product-state polynomial.

Indeed,

\[
 (\pi_{Bd}^-\otimes\pi_{dB}^-)\widehat F
 =q_Bq_D-r^3(\rho_{Bd}-p)(\rho_{dB}-p).               \tag{23}
\]

On the active branch, a certificate for (1) is consequently a potential on
the two-copy low space satisfying

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat F+
 (\widehat Q_{Bd}^{\ell}+\widehat Q_{dB}^{d})\Phi\ge0.}             \tag{24}
\]

For a fixed module this certificate is also necessary.  On the recurrent
low product class, average (24) to obtain (23).  Conversely, if the mean in
(23) is nonnegative, the finite Poisson equation sets the left side of (24)
equal to that nonnegative constant.  Thus (24) is an exact finite LP-dual
form of the active product inequality, not an additional strengthening.

This is strictly smaller than the three-copy BDM certificate.  Higher ranks
have not been deleted: their exact effects are the positive Green terms in
`tau,kappa` and the return term in `widehat Q`.  Because every return to the
low sector crosses rank three to rank two, (15)--(16) identify the first
indispensable higher-rank datum.

## 4. Exact obstruction to a first-level proof

The singleton stationarity equations are homogeneous in all rank-one and
rank-two masses.  Start with any positive low-sector solutions of those
equations, multiply every rank-one/rank-two mass in both laws by
`lambda>0`, and put the residual mass of each law on a rank-three state.
The singleton equations remain exact, while

\[
 q_Bq_D=O(\lambda^2),\qquad
 \rho_{Bd},\rho_{dB}\longrightarrow\frac3s.           \tag{25}
\]

At `s=8` and throughout

\[
                         \frac32<r<\frac{151}{100},
\]

one has

\[
 \frac38-p=\frac{8-5r}{8r}>0.                         \tag{26}
\]

Hence the right side of (1) tends to the strictly positive value

\[
 r^3\left(\frac38-p\right)^2,
\]

while its left side tends to zero.  Thus even the *minimal* product theorem
cannot follow from singleton equations, positivity, and normalization.
The missing constraint is exactly the Schur repayment from rank three in
(15)--(18), not a sharper manipulation of first-level balance.

This pseudo-law is not stationary for the full chains and is not a graph
counterexample.  Its role is logical: it rules out the relaxed proof space
that would otherwise invite an exhaustive coefficient search.
