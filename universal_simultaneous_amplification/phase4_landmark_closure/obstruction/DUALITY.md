# Exact branching--coalescing duals for the two update rules

## Status and purpose

The dualities in this note are **PROVED** directly from the update rules.
They concern the actual finite-population chains and do not use the retracted
independent-genealogy comparison.  In particular, the dual particles coalesce
and their births and deaths remain coupled exactly as dictated by replacement
events.

For a connected undirected weighted graph, put

\[
 d_u=\sum_v w_{uv},\qquad P_{uv}=\frac{w_{uv}}{d_u}.
\]

The principal conclusion is

\[
 \rho_U(G,r)=\frac1n\,\mathbb E_{\pi_U}|A|,
 \qquad U\in\{\mathrm{Bd},\mathrm{dB}\},                 \tag{1}
\]

where `pi_U` is the limiting law of an explicit branching--coalescing set
process started from all vertices.  Thus the proposed cross-rule fixation-sum
inequality is equivalently a stationary-density tradeoff between two exact
duals.  No such tradeoff is claimed here.

## 1. Boolean duality convention

Identify a mutant configuration with its indicator vector `x` and a dual
particle configuration with a nonempty set `A`.  Write

\[
 H(x,A)=\boldsymbol 1\{\text{some }a\in A\text{ has }x_a=1\}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Every graphical map below is additive: it preserves coordinatewise Boolean
OR.  Its transpose map on subsets is defined so that

\[
 H(\Phi x,A)=H(x,\Phi^* A).                              \tag{3}
\]

Using the same graphical marks forward and backward gives the usual finite
time duality

\[
 \Pr_x\{X_t\cap A\ne\varnothing\}
 =\Pr_A\{x\cap A_t\ne\varnothing\}.                     \tag{4}
\]

Everything needed for (4) follows by composing (3); no independence between
different ancestral lineages is introduced.

## 2. Birth--death dual

Multiplying every transition rate out of a state `S` by
`n+(r-1)|S|` is a state-dependent time change and leaves fixation
probabilities unchanged.  The resulting continuous-time chain has, for every
ordered edge `u -> v`, replacement rate

\[
 \bigl[1+(r-1)x_u\bigr]P_{uv}.                           \tag{5}
\]

It has the following exact graphical construction.

* At rate `P_uv`, a neutral arrow sets `x_v <- x_u`.
* At rate `(r-1)P_uv`, a selective arrow sets
  `x_v <- x_v OR x_u`.

The second arrow changes the configuration precisely when `u` is mutant and
`v` is resident, so (5) is exactly the Bd chain after the harmless time
change.  Transposing the two Boolean maps gives the Bd dual:

* at a neutral arrow into an occupied `v`, replace `v` by `u`;
* at a selective arrow into an occupied `v`, retain `v` and add `u`.

Repeated particles coalesce because the state is a set.  In formulas,

\[
 A\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 (A\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u\},&v\in A \quad\text{(neutral)},\\
 A\cup\{u\},&v\in A \quad\text{(selective)},\\
 A,&v\notin A.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{6}
\]

## 3. Death--birth dual

Suppose `v` dies and let

\[
 x=\sum_u P_{vu}x_u
\]

be the weight fraction of its mutant neighbors.  Directly from dB updating,
the probability that the replacement parent is mutant is

\[
 \frac{rx}{1+(r-1)x}.                                    \tag{7}
\]

Let `K` have the geometric law on `{1,2,...}`

\[
 \Pr(K=k)=\frac1r\left(1-\frac1r\right)^{k-1},           \tag{8}
\]

and, conditional on `K`, sample `U_1,...,U_K` independently from the row
`P_v*`.  Its probability generating function gives

\[
 \mathbb E(1-x)^K
 =\frac{1-x}{1+(r-1)x}.                                  \tag{9}
\]

Therefore the exact dB update can be generated by

\[
 x_v\longleftarrow x_{U_1}\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}\cdots
 \mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}x_{U_K}.                          \tag{10}
\]

The transpose map is the dB dual update

\[
 A\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 (A\setminus\{v\})\cup\{U_1,\ldots,U_K\},&v\in A,\\
 A,&v\notin A.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{11}
\]

One vertex `v` is selected at rate one (equivalently, uniformly at discrete
update times).  Since `K>=1`, the dual never reaches the empty set.

## 4. Fixation equals stationary dual density

Start either dual from `A_0=V`, and start the forward process from a uniformly
chosen singleton `I`, independently of the graphical marks.  By (4),

\[
 \Pr_I\{X_t\ne\varnothing\}
 =\mathbb E\,\boldsymbol 1\{I\in A_t\}
 =\frac1n\mathbb E|A_t|.                                \tag{12}
\]

The finite forward chain absorbs almost surely, and its nonempty indicator
converges to the fixation indicator.  Hence

\[
 \rho_U(G,r)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1n\mathbb E|A_t|.    \tag{13}
\]

This proves (1), with `pi_U` interpreted as the limiting distribution from
`V` (or its unique recurrent-class stationary law).  The argument itself
does not require a separate irreducibility assertion.

## 5. Exact first-moment balances

Write

\[
 t_v=\sum_uP_{uv},\qquad
 C_{\rm in}(A)=\sum_{u\notin A,\,v\in A}P_{uv},
\]

and

\[
 h_r(p)=\frac{rp}{1+(r-1)p}.                             \tag{14}
\]

For the Bd dual, applying its generator to `|A|` gives exactly

\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm Bd}|A|
 =rC_{\rm in}(A)-\sum_{v\in A}t_v.                      \tag{15}
\]

Indeed, neutral arrows coalesce at total rate
`sum_{u,v in A}P_uv`, while selective arrows add a particle at rate
`(r-1)C_in(A)`.

For the dB dual, a sampled neighbor `u` appears at least once with probability
`h_r(P_vu)`.  Consequently

\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm dB}|A|
 =\sum_{v\in A}\left[-1+
   \sum_{u\notin A}h_r(P_{vu})\right].                  \tag{16}
\]

Taking stationary expectations yields

\[
 r\,\mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm Bd}}C_{\rm in}(A)
 =\mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm Bd}}\sum_{v\in A}t_v,            \tag{17}
\]

and

\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm dB}}|A|
 =\mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm dB}}
   \sum_{v\in A,u\notin A}h_r(P_{vu}).                 \tag{18}
\]

These identities are exact but involve two different stationary laws.  A
valid universal obstruction must bridge that difference; adding the two
statewise drifts at a common set does not do so.

## 6. Weighted adjoint and geometric resolvent

Let `L` be the Bd dual generator above.  Reverse every base arrow and call
the resulting set generator `C`.  Thus an occupied target `v` samples
`u` from the row `P_v*`; a neutral event replaces `v` by `u`, while a
selective event retains `v` and adds `u`.  Give a nonempty set reference
mass

\[
 \mu(A)=(r-1)^{|A|}.
\]

Every neutral coalescence of `L` is paired with a reverse selective birth of
`C`, and every same-level swap is paired with the reversed swap.  Only the
diagonal exit rates differ.  If

\[
 A_\partial(A)=\sum_{i\in A,j\notin A}P_{ij},\qquad
 B_\partial(A)=\sum_{i\in A,j\notin A}P_{ji},
\]

then entrywise

\[
 L^{\dagger_\mu}
 =C+r\{A_\partial(A)-B_\partial(A)\}I.                 \tag{19}
\]

There is also an exact targetwise batching identity.  Let `S_v` be one
selective row-`P` sample, `N_v` one neutral row-`P` replacement, and `G_v`
the geometric burst.  As row transition operators acting on column test
functions,

\[
 G_v=\frac1r\sum_{m\ge0}
 \left(\frac{r-1}{r}S_v\right)^mN_v,
\]

so

\[
 \left(I-\frac{r-1}{r}S_v\right)(G_v-I)
 =\frac1r\{(N_v-I)+(r-1)(S_v-I)\}.                    \tag{20}
\]

The inverse resolvent is positivity preserving.  Thus a targetwise sign
certificate for `C` would pass to the dB burst.  The required certificate is
not presently known: the cut potential in (19) and the differently indexed
resolvents in (20) both have to be retained.

## 7. Independent verification

`verify_duality.py` uses rational arithmetic to build both original absorbing
chains and both nonempty dual chains on several weighted graphs.  It checks:

1. every one-step Boolean duality identity;
2. exact equality of uniform-singleton fixation and stationary dual density;
3. the two generator balances (17)--(18).

`verify_exact_duals.py` additionally solves the limiting stationary laws,
checks the reverse-fitness singleton identities and level-one/two balances,
and verifies (19)--(20) entry by entry over exact rationals.
