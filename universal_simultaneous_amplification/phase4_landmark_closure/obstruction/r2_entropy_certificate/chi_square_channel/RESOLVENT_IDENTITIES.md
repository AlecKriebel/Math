# The fair-geometric channel: an exact resolvent and two failed contractions

Date: 2026-08-02 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Status

The resolvent identity below is **PROVED** for every loopless row-stochastic
kernel.  It is genuinely special to fitness two.  Two tempting consequences
are **EXACTLY FALSE**, already for connected undirected weighted graphs on
three vertices.  The target inequality

\[
 I_2(V;B)\le 2
\]

is neither proved nor refuted here.

## 1. Effective, deleted-source, and one-sample measures

Let `Pi` be stationary for the uniform-target geometric-union chain at
fitness two.  For a fixed target `v`, write

\[
 R_v(B)=\Pi(B)1_{\{v\notin B\}},\qquad
 \mu_v=\Pi K_v,\qquad \nu_v=\mu_v-R_v.                 \tag{1}
\]

Thus `nu_v` is precisely the subprobability measure of outputs produced by
an effective update at `v`.  Delete `v` from an occupied source and put

\[
 \sigma_v(C)=\Pi(C\cup\{v\}),\qquad v\notin C.          \tag{2}
\]

Finally, let `A_v` be the one-sample union kernel

\[
 A_v(C,C\cup\{i\})=P_{vi}.                             \tag{3}
\]

Empty `C` is permitted in (2)--(3), although the stationary dual itself is
supported on nonempty proper sets.

## 2. Exact midpoint resolvent

Let `Q_v` be the geometric-union kernel acting after deletion of `v`.  If
`N` is geometric on `{1,2,...}` with `Pr(N=j)=2^{-j}`, then its first-sample
decomposition gives

\[
 Q_v={1\over2}A_v+{1\over2}A_vQ_v.
\]

All one-sample union operators commute, so equivalently

\[
 \boxed{Q_v=A_v(2I-A_v)^{-1}.}                         \tag{4}
\]

Since `nu_v=sigma_v Q_v`, (4) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{2\nu_v=(\sigma_v+\nu_v)A_v.}                  \tag{5}
\]

The Cayley form is

\[
 \boxed{\sigma_v-\nu_v=(\sigma_v+\nu_v)(I-A_v).}      \tag{6}
\]

Thus an effective output can be generated by taking an equal mixture of a
deleted occupied source and another effective output, then adjoining one
fresh `P_v` sample.  At general fitness `r`, the coefficients in (5) become
`r` and `1,r-1`; the exact midpoint occurs only at `r=2`.

## 3. Posterior effective-event identity

Write

\[
 e_v(B)={\nu_v(B)\over\Pi(B)}\quad(v\notin B),
 \qquad k=|B|.
\]

Stationarity and the fact that every `v`-output omits `v` give

\[
 \sum_{v\notin B}e_v(B)=k.                            \tag{7}
\]

Consequently, in the stationary experiment with uniform target `V`,

\[
 \boxed{\Pr(\text{effective}\mid B)=k/n,\qquad
 \Pr(V=v\mid B,\text{effective})=e_v(B)/k.}           \tag{8}
\]

This separates the target posterior into a null part, uniform over the
holes, and an effective part.  In particular

\[
 I_2(V;B)=1+{E_\Pi k\over n}
 +{1\over n}E_\Pi\sum_{v\notin B}e_v(B)^2.             \tag{9}
\]

## 4. The fixed-reference resolvent contraction is false

A natural attempt is to use (5) together with contraction of `A_v` in the
quadratic norm

\[
 \|\eta\|_{\Pi^{-1}}^2
 =\sum_v\sum_{B:\,v\notin B}{\eta_v(B)^2\over\Pi(B)}. \tag{10}
\]

But `A_v` does not preserve `Pi`, and the required contraction is false.
For the unweighted path `0-1-2`, exact calculation gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\nu\|_{\Pi^{-1}}^2={13\over9},\qquad
 \|(\nu_vA_v)_v\|_{\Pi^{-1}}^2={19\over9}.}           \tag{11}
\]

For the same graph,

\[
 E_\Pi|B|={11\over9},\qquad
 I_2(V;B)={17\over9}<2.                               \tag{12}
\]

So the target inequality survives even though the most direct resolvent
contraction fails by the exact gap `2/3`.

## 5. Separating null and effective information also fails

Reveal the binary flag `J` saying whether the update was effective.  Directly
from (8),

\[
 I_2(V;B,J)
 =1+E_\Pi\left[{1\over |B|}
   \sum_{v\notin B}e_v(B)^2\right].                   \tag{13}
\]

It would be convenient if the expectation in (13) were at most one.  It is
not.  On the triangle with symmetric edge weights

\[
 w_{01}=7,\qquad w_{02}=w_{12}=1,                     \tag{14}
\]

the exact verifier obtains a strictly positive rational value for

\[
 E_\Pi\left[{1\over |B|}\sum_{v\notin B}e_v(B)^2\right]-1.
\]

Thus data processing after revealing the effective/null flag gives an upper
bound strictly larger than two.  Any proof of (9) must retain the negative
cross-cancellation between the null and effective histories.

## 6. Consequence for the proof search

The useful content of the resolvent is therefore structural rather than a
ready-made Hilbert contraction.  A successful entropy argument must use a
reference measure transported along with `A_v`, or exploit the aggregate
stationarity relation (7) before taking squares.  Treating targets
individually, freezing `Pi` as the reference measure, or revealing the
effective-event flag all lose an essential cancellation.
