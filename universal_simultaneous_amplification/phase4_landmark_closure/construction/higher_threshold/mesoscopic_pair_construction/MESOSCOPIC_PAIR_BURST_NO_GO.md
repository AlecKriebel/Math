# A cross-rule obstruction for separated mesoscopic pair modules

Date: 2026-08-02 (America/Los_Angeles)

No literature search or external contact was used.  Every transition below
is derived directly from the Bd and dB rules.

## 1. Outcome

Consider a growing strong-pair windmill: a portal `o` and `s` disjoint
blades `(x_i,y_i)`, with

\[
 w(o,x_i)=w(o,y_i)=a_i,
 \qquad w(x_i,y_i)=b_i.                         \tag{1}
\]

Put

\[
 p_i={a_i\over\sum_j a_j},\qquad
 \lambda_i={a_i\over b_i},\qquad
 x_i={\lambda_i\over p_i},\qquad
 m=\sum_i\lambda_i.                            \tag{2}
\]

The balanced mesoscopic regime is

\[
 s\longrightarrow\infty,qquad
 \max_i p_i\longrightarrow0,qquad
 \max_i\lambda_i\longrightarrow0,             \tag{3}
\]

with `m` bounded away from zero and infinity along the sequence.  The last
compactness assumption is only used to make the graph-to-burst convergence
uniform; the algebraic burst obstruction itself holds for every `m>0`.

**PROVED (burst tradeoff).**  In the optimistic rare-mutant burst processes
generated by (1)--(3), let `E_B` and `E_D` be the uniformly averaged
probabilities that one initially monomorphic mutant blade has a lineage that
never dies out.  If the original mutant is a uniformly selected blade
vertex, its maximal establishment probabilities are

\[
 \alpha_B={r\over r+1}E_B,
 \qquad
 \alpha_D={1\over2}E_D.                         \tag{4}
\]

For every

\[
 \boxed{r>{1+\sqrt3\over2}=1.3660254037\ldots}  \tag{5}
\]

the two quantities in (4) cannot both reach

\[
 p=1-{1\over r}.                                \tag{6}
\]

The separation is strict for every fixed `r` in (5) with `r<2`.

**PROVED (graph consequence).**  In addition suppose that the balanced
ratios are uniformly nondegenerate,

\[
 0<x_-\le x_i\le x_+<\infty,                   \tag{6a}
\]

with constants independent of population size.  Take any number of copies
of (1), let both the blade number and copy number diverge, and join their
portals by an arbitrary connected undirected weighted macrograph on a still
rarer scale.  Assume that there is a cutoff `K_s -> infinity`, slow enough
that the stopped local chain through level `K_s` converges to the burst
process and that an intermodule event before local extinction or level
`K_s` has probability `o(1)`.  Then for every fixed

\[
 {1+\sqrt3\over2}<r<2,                          \tag{7}
\]

the resulting graph sequence cannot eventually amplify both Bd and dB.
Indeed, grant immediate global fixation when `K_s` mutant blades are first
reached.  The stopped-chain coupling and the uniform branching extinction
tail then give the upper bounds (4), so all later module conversion and
macrograph dynamics have already been treated in the most favorable possible
way.  For `r>2`, the dB entrance factor `1/2` itself is strictly below (6).

Thus separated mesoscopic pair modules do not extend the simultaneous
interval beyond `3/2`; in fact this route is obstructed already above the
smaller algebraic value (5).  This is a broad class no-go, **not** a
universal obstruction for arbitrary graph families.  The endpoint `r=2`,
where a vanishing finite-size comparison could remain, is not claimed.

### 1.1 Homogeneous audit

For the literal homogeneous balanced windmill

\[
 p_i={1\over s},\qquad \lambda_i={c\over s},    \tag{6b}
\]

the Jensen bounds below are equalities.  The compound-branching extinction
probabilities reduce to

\[
 q_B={2c(r+1)+r^2\over
 r^2\{1+2rc(r+1)\}},\qquad
 q_D={1+rc\over r(c+r^2)}.                     \tag{6c}
\]

The exact limiting establishment, and hence fixation, probabilities are

\[
 \boxed{\rho_B^\infty=
 {2c(r^3-1)\over r\{1+2r(r+1)c\}},\qquad
 \rho_D^\infty={r^3-1\over2r(r^2+c)}.}         \tag{6d}
\]

Here survival of the one-type supercritical burst process reaches a positive
blade density with probability tending one.  To audit the post-establishment
path, let `y` be the mutant-blade fraction.  Under Bd the portal telegraph
has rates `2rcy` and `2c(1-y)`; under dB its two rates are
`ry/[ry+1-y]` and `(1-y)/[ry+1-y]`.  Both have stationary mutant probability

\[
 \pi(y)={ry\over ry+1-y}.                       \tag{6f}
\]

After averaging the `O(1)` portal chain against the `O(1/s)` density jumps,
the Bd blade gain and loss rates are

\[
 \pi(y){r^2\over r+1}(1-y),\qquad
 \{1-\pi(y)\}{y\over r+1},                     \tag{6g}
\]

and the dB rates are

\[
 \pi(y)rc(1-y),\qquad
 \{1-\pi(y)\}{c y\over r}.                    \tag{6h}
\]

The forward/backward ratio is exactly `r^3` in both (6g) and (6h).  Hence
the density drift is strictly toward one on every compact subinterval of
`(0,1)`, and the reverse sweep probability after establishment tends zero by
the direct biased birth--death comparison.  Equivalently, stop at
`K_s -> infinity` with
`K_s^2/s -> 0`: the branching error is `o(1)`, while extinction after level
`K_s` is at most `q_U^{K_s}`.  Thus (6d) audits the full windmill rather than
only its first handoff.

Comparison with (6) is transparent:

\[
 \rho_B^\infty>p\iff c>{1\over2},\qquad
 \rho_D^\infty>p\iff c<{1+r-r^2\over2}.        \tag{6e}
\]

The dB upper endpoint in (6e) is strictly below `1/2` for every `r>1`.
Therefore the homogeneous balanced windmill is never a simultaneous
amplifier at any beneficial fitness.  Heterogeneity requires the stronger
odds-product argument below.

## 2. Exact early Bd burst process

Use independent continuous-time reproduction clocks; multiplying every
clock by the same state-dependent factor does not change absorption
probabilities.  Suppose the portal is resident and only `O(1)` blades are
mutant and monomorphic.

For a mutant blade `i`:

- its two mutant vertices seed the portal at rate
  `2r lambda_i+o(lambda_i)`;
- resident-portal reproduction enters the blade at rate `p_i`, after which
  the resident fixes the strong pair with probability `1/(r+1)`.

Thus the blade seeds a mutant-portal episode at rate `2r lambda_i` and is
erased at rate `p_i/(r+1)` in the limiting burst process.

While the portal is mutant, resident blade vertices replace it at total rate

\[
 \delta_B=2m.                                   \tag{8}
\]

The mutant portal targets blade `j` with mass `p_j`; the introduced mutant
fixes that strong pair with probability `r/(r+1)`.  Successful mutant-blade
children therefore arrive at rates

\[
 \beta_{B,j}={r^2\over r+1}p_j,qquad
 \beta_B=\sum_j\beta_{B,j}={r^2\over r+1}.      \tag{9}
\]

Consequently one portal episode produces a geometric number (supported on
`0,1,2,...`) of children, with type law `(p_j)` and probability generating
function

\[
 G_B(z)={2m\over2m+\beta_B(1-z)}.               \tag{10}
\]

Let `q_i^B` be extinction from a mutant blade of type `i`, and put

\[
 Q_B=\sum_i p_iq_i^B,qquad
 h_B=1-G_B(Q_B)
 ={\beta_B(1-Q_B)\over2m+\beta_B(1-Q_B)}.       \tag{11}
\]

First-event conditioning on death versus a portal episode gives exactly

\[
 q_i^B={1\over1+2r(r+1)x_i h_B}.                \tag{12}
\]

The mean progeny matrix is rank one.  A type `i` parent has mean total
offspring `r^3x_i/m`, with child law `(p_j)`, so its Perron value is

\[
 \sum_i p_i{r^3x_i\over m}=r^3.                 \tag{13}
\]

Equation (13) explains why a mean-rate calculation sees the same apparent
fitness under both rules.  Extinction depends on the entire geometric burst,
not only this Perron value.

## 3. Exact early dB burst process

With a resident portal, a portal death copies mutant blade `i` at rate
`r p_i+o(p_i)`.  Resident-portal invasion of a mutant blade occurs at total
rate `2 lambda_i/r`; the resulting heterotypic strong pair resolves toward
the resident with probability `1/2`.  Hence a type `i` blade starts portal
episodes at rate `r p_i` and dies at rate `lambda_i/r`.

During a mutant-portal episode, the portal reverts at rate one.  Resident
blade `j` becomes mutant at successful rate `r lambda_j`: either blade vertex
may die, and the resulting singleton fixes internally with probability
`1/2`.  Therefore

\[
 \delta_D=1,qquad \beta_D=rm,\qquad
 G_D(z)={1\over1+rm(1-z)},                      \tag{14}
\]

where a child has type law `(lambda_j/m)`.  Define

\[
 Q_D={1\over m}\sum_i\lambda_iq_i^D,qquad
 h_D=1-G_D(Q_D)
 ={rm(1-Q_D)\over1+rm(1-Q_D)}.                 \tag{15}
\]

First-event conditioning now gives

\[
 q_i^D={x_i\over x_i+r^2h_D}.                  \tag{16}
\]

A type `i` parent has mean total offspring `r^3m/x_i`, with child law
`lambda_j/m`; again the rank-one Perron value is exactly `r^3`.

## 4. Sharp common-environment bounds

The two child laws in Sections 2--3 are reciprocal size biases.  This gives
the key exact tradeoff.

For Bd, `(p_i)` is a probability law and

\[
 \sum_i p_ix_i=m.                               \tag{17}
\]

The function `x -> 1/(1+cx)` is convex.  Jensen's inequality in (11)--(12),
followed by direct rearrangement, gives

\[
 \boxed{
 h_B\le H_B(m):={r^3-1\over
 r\{r^2+2m(r+1)\}}.}                           \tag{18}
\]

For completeness, if `h_B>0`, write `c=2r(r+1)h_B`.  Jensen gives
`1-Q_B<=cm/(1+cm)`.  Substitution in (11), division by `h_B`, and collection
of terms gives (18).  The case `h_B=0` is immediate.

For dB, use the child law `lambda_i/m`.  Under that law,

\[
 {1\over m}\sum_i\lambda_i{x_i}^{-1}={1\over m}.             \tag{19}
\]

Writing `y=1/x`, the survival function in (16) is
`r^2h_D y/(1+r^2h_D y)`, which is concave in `y`.  Reciprocal Jensen in
(15)--(16) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 h_D\le H_D(m):={m(r^3-1)\over r^2+mr^3}.}     \tag{20}
\]

Both bounds are sharp exactly when all child-relevant ratios `x_i` are
equal.  They also cover arbitrarily heterogeneous initial types: a
vanishing population class may carry nearly all portal mass, but it cannot
make either environment parameter exceed (18) or (20).

## 5. The odds-product obstruction

Let

\[
 K=2r(r+1)H_B(m),\qquad A=r^2H_D(m),\qquad L=KA.               \tag{21}
\]

By (12), (16), (18), and (20), the two typewise lineage-survival
probabilities are bounded above by

\[
 b(x)={Kx\over1+Kx},\qquad
 d(x)={A\over x+A}.                             \tag{22}
\]

Their survival odds have constant product `L`.  Eliminating `x` gives

\[
 d=f_L(b):={L(1-b)\over b+L(1-b)}.              \tag{23}
\]

If `L<=1`, then `b(x)+d(x)<=1` pointwise.  If `L>1`, the function `f_L` is
decreasing and concave because

\[
 f_L''(b)={2L(1-L)\over\{L+(1-L)b\}^3}<0.       \tag{24}
\]

Let `B,D` be the uniform averages of (22).  To have both values in (4) at
least (6), it is necessary that

\[
 B\ge B_0:=1-r^{-2},\qquad
 D\ge D_0:={2(r-1)\over r}.                     \tag{25}
\]

When `L<=1`, equations (22) and (25) are incompatible as soon as

\[
 B_0+D_0-1={2r^2-2r-1\over r^2}>0,             \tag{26}
\]

which is exactly condition (5).

When `L>1`, Jensen's inequality in (23) gives

\[
 D\le f_L(B)\le f_L(B_0)={L\over r^2-1+L}.     \tag{27}
\]

It remains to bound `L` uniformly over the total pair load `m`.  Directly
from (18)--(21),

\[
 L(m)=
 {2r^2m(r+1)(r^3-1)^2\over
  \{r^2+2m(r+1)\}\{r^2+mr^3\}}.                \tag{28}
\]

The denominator divided by `m` is minimized at

\[
 m_* = \sqrt{r\over2(r+1)},                    \tag{29}
\]

and therefore

\[
 L(m)\le L_{\max}(r)
 ={2(r+1)(r^3-1)^2\over
   \{r^{3/2}+\sqrt{2(r+1)}\}^2}.                \tag{30}
\]

For `1<r<2`, the condition that the right side of (27) reach `D_0` is

\[
 L\ge L_{\rm req}(r)
 ={2(r-1)^2(r+1)\over2-r}.                     \tag{31}
\]

In fact (30) is strictly smaller than (31) for every `1<r<2`.  After taking
positive square roots and canceling `r-1`, this is equivalent to

\[
 (r^2+r+1)\sqrt{2-r}
 < r^{3/2}+\sqrt{2(r+1)}.                       \tag{32}
\]

Set the right side minus the left side equal to `g(r)`.  Then `g(1)=0` and

\[
 g'(r)={3\over2}\sqrt r+{1\over\sqrt{2(r+1)}}
 +{5r^2-5r-3\over2\sqrt{2-r}}.                 \tag{33}
\]

The sum of the first two terms is increasing on `(1,2)`.  The derivative of
the last term is

\[
 {-15r^2+45r-23\over4(2-r)^{3/2}}>0,           \tag{34}
\]

because its numerator is a concave quadratic equal to `7` at both endpoints
`1` and `2`.  Finally `g'(1)=1/2`; hence `g(r)>0` on `(1,2)`, proving (32).
Equations (27), (30), and (31) now contradict (25).

This proves the burst tradeoff (5).  Notice that it allows arbitrary
heterogeneity in the ratios `lambda_i/p_i` and arbitrary vanishing
population classes carrying the portal load.  Replacing the geometric
episode by its mean would miss precisely the extinction effect used here.

## 6. From the burst process to separated mesoscopic graphs

Fix `K`.  Before there are `K` mutant blades, at most `O(K)` blade
excursions are active.  Under (3), the chance of two introductions colliding
in one blade, or of an additional portal event changing a resolving strong
pair, is `o(1)`.  The exact Bd and dB clocks listed in Sections 2--3 therefore
converge, on this stopped state space, to the corresponding finite-type burst
process.  This is a finite-state rare-event argument: delete self-loops,
divide by the total changing rate, and use entrywise convergence of the
resulting stopped transition matrix.  Compact bounds on `m` make the
convergence uniform over all states with at most fixed `K`.

Under (6a), all particle extinction probabilities are bounded above by one
constant `q_*<1`, uniformly in module size.  Indeed, the burst parameters
range over a compact set and their rank-one Perron value is the fixed number
`r^3>1`.  More explicitly, the scalar survival fixed-point map has derivative
`r^3` at zero and uniformly bounded second derivative on this compact class,
so its positive fixed point is bounded away from zero.  Choose
`K_s -> infinity` sufficiently slowly that the stopped
transition error still tends to zero; for example, in the homogeneous case
any additional restriction `K_s^2/s -> 0` controls repeated-target
collisions.  At the first entrance to level `K_s`, the infinite branching
process has at least `K_s` contemporaneous particles, whose descendant trees
are independent.  Its subsequent extinction probability is therefore at
most `q_*^{K_s}`.  The finite graph is coupled to this branching process only
up to the hitting time; after that time level `K_s` is simply declared
successful.
Hence the probability of reaching level `K_s` differs from nonextinction by
`o(1)`.  This supplies the uniform-tail step that a merely fixed-`K`
approximation would not provide.

For a singleton mutant inside a blade, all external events before internal
resolution have probability `o(1)`.  Directly from the two update rules, the
probability that the pair first becomes all mutant is

\[
 {r\over r+1}+o(1)\quad\hbox{under Bd},
 \qquad {1\over2}+o(1)\quad\hbox{under dB}.     \tag{35}
\]

The portals form only `1/(2s+1)` of the vertices, so portal starts vanish
under uniform initialization.  By the assumed outer scale, an intermodule
event before local extinction or level `K_s` also has probability `o(1)`.
Global fixation requires reaching level `K_s`; declaring that event
immediate success gives

\[
 \limsup \rho_B\le {r\over r+1}\Pr_B(\tau_{K_s}<\tau_0),
 \qquad
 \limsup \rho_D\le {1\over2}\Pr_D(\tau_{K_s}<\tau_0).        \tag{36}
\]

The uniform extinction-tail bound converts these hitting probabilities to
the branching nonextinction probabilities, giving (4).  Since the
complete-graph baselines converge to (6), the strict separation proved in
Section 5 rules out eventual simultaneous amplification for every fixed `r`
in (7).

## 7. Extension to arbitrary two-state protected blades

The Jensen argument is not special to literal two-vertex pairs.  It extends
verbatim to any protected module whose rare trace has two monomorphic states
and the following common scale form.  Write `p_i` for its portal parent mass,
`lambda_i` for its reverse portal load, and again `x_i=lambda_i/p_i`,
`m=sum_i lambda_i`.

Suppose the Bd trace has

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{event}&\text{rate}\\
 \hline
 \text{mutant blade seeds portal}&s_B\lambda_i\\
 \text{mutant blade is erased}&d_Bp_i\\
 \text{successful children during mutant-portal episode}
      &\beta_Bp_j\\
 \text{mutant portal is lost}&\ell_Bm,
 \end{array}                                                    \tag{37}
\]

and the dB trace has

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{event}&\text{rate}\\
 \hline
 \text{mutant blade seeds portal}&s_Dp_i\\
 \text{mutant blade is erased}&d_D\lambda_i\\
 \text{successful children during mutant-portal episode}
      &\beta_D\lambda_j\\
 \text{mutant portal is lost}&\ell_D.
 \end{array}                                                    \tag{38}
\]

All eight constants are positive and may depend on `r` and on the protected
module shape, but not on `i`.  This is exactly the trace produced by scaled
copies of one internally resolving two-state module; its isolated forward
and reverse establishment probabilities are absorbed into the constants.

Put

\[
 C_B={s_B\over d_B},\quad C_D={s_D\over d_D},\quad
 R_B={C_B\beta_B\over\ell_B},\quad
 R_D={C_D\beta_D\over\ell_D}.                  \tag{39}
\]

When `R_B,R_D>1`, the same extinction calculation gives

\[
 q_i^B={1\over1+C_Bx_ih_B},\qquad
 q_i^D={x_i\over x_i+C_Dh_D}.                  \tag{40}
\]

Jensen and reciprocal Jensen give the sharp bounds

\[
 C_Bh_B\le {R_B-1\over m+u},\qquad
 C_Dh_D\le {m(R_D-1)\over1+vm},                \tag{41}
\]

where

\[
 u={\beta_B\over\ell_B},\qquad
 v={\beta_D\over\ell_D}.                      \tag{42}
\]

Thus the maximal typewise survival-odds product is

\[
 \boxed{
 L_{\max}^{\rm two\mbox{-}state}
 ={(R_B-1)(R_D-1)\over(1+\sqrt{uv})^2},}       \tag{43}
\]

attained at `m=sqrt(u/v)` in the homogeneous-ratio environment.  If `e_B`
and `e_D` are the two uniform singleton-to-mutant-blade entrance
probabilities, set

\[
 B_0={p\over e_B},\qquad D_0={p\over e_D}.      \tag{44}
\]

The template is obstructed whenever `B_0+D_0>1` and

\[
 D_0>
 {L_{\max}^{\rm two\mbox{-}state}(1-B_0)
  \over B_0+L_{\max}^{\rm two\mbox{-}state}(1-B_0)}.          \tag{45}
\]

The first inequality handles environments with odds product at most one;
the second handles products above one by concavity.  Formula (45) is an exact
test, not a universal assertion about all protected modules: a module with
different trace topology need not have (37)--(38).

For literal pairs,

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C_B=2r(r+1),\quad \beta_B={r^2\over r+1},\quad \ell_B=2,\\
 C_D=r^2,\quad \beta_D=r,\quad \ell_D=1,
 \end{gathered}                                                    \tag{46}
\]

so `R_B=R_D=r^3`, `u=r^2/[2(r+1)]`, and `v=r`.  Substitution in (43)
recovers (30), while `e_B=r/(r+1)` and `e_D=1/2` recover the threshold (5).
Therefore the odds-product reduction is robust throughout the whole
two-state trace class; whether it yields a no-go for another protected module
is decided by the module-specific scalar certificate (45).

## 8. Scope and status

**PROVED:** the exact rank-one geometric-burst equations (10)--(16), the
sharp environment bounds (18) and (20), the odds-product obstruction, and
the threshold (5).

**PROVED CLASS NO-GO:** separated repeated mesoscopic strong-pair windmills
with uniformly balanced ratios (6a), arbitrary rarer connected portal
macrographs, and immediate success granted after local establishment.  The
algebraic burst tradeoff itself allows completely arbitrary positive ratios;
(6a) is the clean sufficient hypothesis for the graph-to-branching uniform
tail.

**NOT CLAIMED:** nonseparated portal coupling, multiple simultaneously active
portals inside one module, protected modules not reducible to strong pairs,
or a universal upper bound on `R_sim`.
