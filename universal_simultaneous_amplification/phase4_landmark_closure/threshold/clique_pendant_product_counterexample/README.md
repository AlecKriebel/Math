# Exact endpoint product counterexample

## Result

Let `G` be the unweighted graph formed from a clique on 32 vertices by
distinguishing one clique vertex as a hub and adjoining four degree-one leaves
to that hub.  Thus `n=36`.  At mutant fitness `r=3/2`, exact rational
calculation proves

```text
rho_Bd(G,r) / rho_Bd(K_36,r) > 1,
rho_dB(G,r) / rho_dB(K_36,r) < 1,
```

but the product of the two displayed ratios is strictly greater than one.
Consequently this graph is an **exact counterexample to the proposed universal
product inequality** at the endpoint.  It is **not** a simultaneous amplifier
and does not refute the weaker disjunctive endpoint conjecture or prove that
`R_sim>3/2`.

It also exactly refutes the balanced arithmetic separator: if `x` and `y` are
the displayed normalized Bd and dB values, then `(x+y)/2>1`.  More generally,

```text
lambda x + (1-lambda)y > 1
```

holds exactly when

```text
lambda > lambda_0 := (1-y)/(x-y).
```

The certificate gives `lambda_0` as an exact rational number (approximately
0.4699202), so in particular `lambda=1/2` lies strictly above the crossing.

The exact integer numerator of the normalized product gap and the two complete
rational fixation calculations are generated by `certify_counterexample.py`.

## Exact lumping

Write `c=31` for the ordinary clique vertices and `m=4` for the leaves.  A
configuration is represented by `(h,i,j)`, where `h` is the mutant indicator of
the hub, `i` is the number of mutant ordinary clique vertices, and `j` is the
number of mutant leaves.  The group `S_c x S_m` acts transitively on every such
fiber.  Both update rules are invariant under this group, so the total
transition probability from one fiber to another is independent of the chosen
configuration.  This proves strong lumpability.  There are

```text
2(c+1)(m+1) = 320
```

lumps and 318 transient lumps.

For `F=n+(r-1)(h+i+j)` and `d=c+m`, the non-self Bd rates are

```text
(0,i,j) -> (1,i,j): r(i/c+j)/F,
(1,i,j) -> (0,i,j): ((c-i)/c+m-j)/F,
(h,i,j) -> (h,i+1,j): r(c-i)(h/d+i/c)/F,
(h,i,j) -> (h,i-1,j): i((1-h)/d+(c-i)/c)/F,
(h,i,j) -> (h,i,j+1): rh(m-j)/(dF),
(h,i,j) -> (h,i,j-1): (1-h)j/(dF).
```

Terms with an out-of-range target are zero.  For dB they are

```text
(0,i,j) -> (1,i,j): (1/n) r(i+j)/(r(i+j)+c+m-i-j),
(1,i,j) -> (0,i,j): (1/n) (c+m-i-j)/(r(i+j)+c+m-i-j),
(h,i,j) -> (h,i+1,j): ((c-i)/n) r(h+i)/(r(h+i)+c-h-i),
(h,i,j) -> (h,i-1,j): (i/n)(c-h-i+1)/(r(h+i-1)+c-h-i+1),
(h,i,j) -> (h,i,j+1): h(m-j)/n,
(h,i,j) -> (h,i,j-1): (1-h)j/n.
```

These follow directly by choosing the reproducer and then its target (Bd), or
the death site and then its competing neighbor (dB).  `verify_lumping.py`
independently enumerates every microscopic replacement event and compares its
fiber sum with every displayed rate.

## Uniform singleton initialization and baselines

If `H_U(h,i,j)` is the exact harmonic solution, then

```text
rho_U(G,r) = [H_U(1,0,0) + c H_U(0,1,0) + m H_U(0,0,1)] / n.
```

This is the required uniform average over the one hub, `c` ordinary, and `m`
leaf singleton states.  Direct one-dimensional first-step calculation on the
complete graph gives

```text
rho_Bd(K_n,r) = (1-r^-1)/(1-r^-n),
rho_dB(K_n,r) = ((n-1)/n)(1-r^-1)/(1-r^(-(n-1))).
```

The solver works over `QQ` and checks all 636 transient harmonic equations with
Python's independent rational arithmetic.  It also asserts the direction of
both individual comparisons, the strict positive product gap, and the strict
positive balanced-arithmetic gap.  `verify_independent.py` separately rebuilds
the system from labelled microscopic replacement events and solves it with
SymPy's exact `DomainMatrix` interface; its two fixation fractions must match
the committed certificate exactly.
