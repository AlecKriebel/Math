# Author Summary

Population structure can change whether a new advantageous type spreads or
disappears. Two standard evolutionary update rules perform reproduction and
replacement in opposite orders, and structures that help under one rule often
hurt under the other. We asked whether one sequence of population networks,
chosen without knowing the mutant's exact advantage, can improve fixation
under both rules as populations grow. We construct such a sequence by
combining three simple ingredients: a large, densely connected core, a small
number of leaves attached to one core vertex, and a small number of strongly
connected vertex pairs linked only weakly to the core. The leaves and pairs
have opposite effects, but their proportions can be balanced so that both net
effects are positive. We prove that the same sequence works for every fixed
fitness below approximately 1.5028569, which is strictly beyond the previously
proved boundary of 1.5. Exact symbolic programs independently check every
finite algebraic component. This result shows that 1.5 is not the universal
phase boundary, while leaving the ultimate largest possible interval as an
open problem.
