                                                Abstract
         The expected root degree of the free uniform spanning forest need not be invariant under
     squaring an inﬁnite connected locally ﬁnite simple vertex-transitive graph. For the binary grand-
     parent graph G, of degree 8, two explicit free-exhaustion reductions give E degFUSF(G) (o) =
           √
     (24 − 2)/7 > 3.22. For its simple square G2 , of degree 40, a ﬁnite rational-current certiﬁ-
     cate proves E degFUSF(G2 ) (o) ≤ 3208526010913748780/1018 < 3.21. All currents, divergences
     and energies are veriﬁed with integer arithmetic; the passage to the inﬁnite free forest is jus-
     tiﬁed by ﬁnite-support ﬂows and free exhaustion. With the explicitly adopted normalization
     bF (X) = 21 E degFUSF(X) (o) − 1, this gives bF (G) − bF (G2 ) > 1/200, a negative answer to the
     unrestricted vertex-transitive assertion in Abért’s Question 24. The graph and electrical/forest
     principles are established background; no new general forest mechanism or exhaustive priority
     claim is made.


1    The question and the precise convention
For a simple graph X, its simple square X 2 has the same vertices, with distinct vertices adjacent
when their X-distance is at most two. Every edge below has unit conductance. The free uniform
spanning forest, abbreviated FUSF, is the weak limit of uniform spanning trees on ﬁnite connected
induced subgraphs exhausting X, with no boundary vertices identiﬁed [2, §5]. For vertex-transitive
X, deﬁne
                                bF (X) = 21 E degFUSF(X) (o) − 1.                              (1)
The choice of o does not matter.
     Abért’s Some questions, dated 2 November 2010, printed p. 4, Question 24 [1], asks square
invariance for the ﬁrst L2 Betti number obtained from expected free-forest degree for an arbitrary
vertex-transitive graph. The original does not print a normalization formula. We state (1) explicitly:

it is the usual forest-derived normalization matching E degFUSF = 2 + 2β1 on Cayley graphs [4,
p. 1]. Our directly proved assertion is unequal expected degrees, and the answer about bF uses (1).
We do not identify a diﬀerent nonunimodular L2 invariant with it. No unimodularity hypothesis is
present in the inspected question; its adjacent comment already aﬃrms the Cayley case.

2    The graph and the electrical facts used
The end stabilizer in Aut(T ) acts transitively and preserves G, proving transitivity; G contains
T . At any vertex there are three incident tree edges and ﬁve incident grandparent edges, with no
repetitions.
    We also verify nonunimodularity for the full group. A tree edge x p(x) lies in exactly three
triangles of G, through p2 (x) and the two children of x. A grandparent edge x p2 (x) lies in exactly
one, through p(x). Thus G intrinsically determines the tree edges. On the three recovered tree-
neighbors of x, the induced G-graph is K1,2 , whose unique center is p(x). Every automorphism
therefore preserves p and the distinguished end. Conversely an end-preserving tree automorphism
preserves G. The stabilizer of x ﬁxes p(x), while the stabilizer of p(x) has an orbit of size two on
its children, including x. The paired suborbit sizes 1, 2 violate the usual unimodularity criterion
[5, §2.3]. A nonunimodular transitive subgroup alone would not have suﬃced for this full-group
conclusion.

Let W be the radius-10 ball about o in T . Write K = G[W ], J = K 2 . Then J ⊆ H. Every
H-neighbor of o has a witness using at most two G-edges, hence a tree walk of at most four edges,
entirely within the radius-4 tree ball. Therefore J contains every H-edge at o.
    For clarity, the complete neighbor count is as follows: four strict ancestors; 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 30
strict descendants in depths one through four; the sibling; the uncle (the parent’s sibling); the
sibling’s two children; and the uncle’s two children. These disjoint possibilities exhaust two ancestor-
or-descendant G steps and total 40. The end stabilizer preserves H, so its degree is 40 everywhere.

6    Predecessors, scope, and reproducibility
The grandparent graph, ﬁnite electrical principles and free-exhaustion framework are inherited,
with explicit references above. Tang’s inspected arXiv version proves connectivity and branching
properties for this family, not the comparison proved here. Pete–Timár [4, Theorems 1.1–1.2]
already show that FUSF component counts can depend on the generating set even for a ﬁxed
virtually free group; they explicitly distinguish this from expected degree, which is group-invariant
for Cayley graphs. Those results are not claimed as new here. Gaboriau’s harmonic-projection
invariant is formulated with a transitive unimodular action [3, §2.4]. No such tracial hypothesis is
used in our negative calculation. No claim concerning failure in the unimodular subclass is made.
    The original question was checked in the actual seven-page 2010 source, including its unrestricted
scope and Cayley comment. Our bounded primary-source investigation through 22 October 2026
authenticated no earlier publication of this complete expected-degree square comparison. That
does not certify historical ﬁrstness or continued worldwide openness. Tang’s ﬁnal journal PDF
and Pete–Timár’s ﬁnal journal PDF returned access-challenge pages; their actual arXiv versions
identiﬁed below were read instead. The full Lyons–Peres book and all unindexed sources were not
inspected. Source-edition and exact read-scope limits are recorded in the supplement. None of
these dependency DOIs is presented as a prior publication of this full counterexample.
    The supplement contains the entire rational-current data, the independent indexed-tree veri-
ﬁer, exact per-target outcomes, an independent binary-word/global-rational-elimination check of
the ﬁnite reductions, editable manuscript, dependencies, priority notes, licenses and oﬄine packag-
ing instructions. Every current certiﬁcate entry is checked exactly; no external solver, numerical
tolerance, original claimant program or installed third-party package is needed for replay. The cer-
tiﬁcate proves a ﬁnite statement; Lemma 2 is the separate universal argument making it an inﬁnite
free-forest bound. The ﬁnite circuit diagnostics do not prove the inﬁnite limits; Sections 3–4 do.
No formal proof-assistant certiﬁcation is claimed.
