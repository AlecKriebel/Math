# Setwise convergence: a rigorous recoupling theorem and its scope limits

Research date: 2026-10-20. Target: Thorisson Problem 3.2; see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). This file proves a separable-measure-algebra recoupling theorem, not the arbitrary-space theorem of [Attempt 3](ATTEMPT_3.md). The final scope and partial/unresolved named-problem disposition are recorded in [SCOPE.md](SCOPE.md). No novelty claim is made.

## 1. What is proved

Let `(S,Σ)` be a measurable space and let `μ_n,ν` be probability measures. Put

`λ = ν + Σ_{n≥1} 2^{-n} μ_n`.

Assume that the measure algebra of `(S,Σ,λ)` is separable for the pseudometric `d_λ(A,B)=λ(A△B)`. This holds whenever `Σ` is countably generated, including standard Borel spaces and countable products of standard Borel state spaces.

Then these two conditions are equivalent:

1. `μ_n(A) → ν(A)` for every `A∈Σ`.
2. On ONE probability space there exist random elements `Y,Y_1,Y_2,…`, with laws `ν,μ_1,μ_2,…`, such that, for EVERY fixed `A∈Σ`,
   `P(1_A(Y_n) ≠ 1_A(Y)) → 0`.

The probability space and all the random elements in (2) are chosen once, not separately for each test event. No topology, Skorohod representation, or regular conditional distribution is required in this result. Conversely, (2) implies (1) on every measurable space without the separability assumption.

Equivalent in (2) is `E|f(Y_n)-f(Y)|→0` for every bounded measurable real function `f`: approximate such a function uniformly by a finite-valued measurable function.

## 2. Proof

### Uniform absolute continuity

Each `μ_n` is absolutely continuous with respect to the finite measure `λ`. Setwise convergence and the Vitali–Hahn–Saks theorem give

`lim_{δ↓0} sup_n sup_{λ(B)<δ} μ_n(B) = 0`.

The same absolute-continuity bound includes `ν` (indeed `ν≤λ`). This is the essential ingredient that upgrades countably many partition tests to every measurable test. Countable generation alone does not justify that upgrade without an appropriate approximation bound.

### Increasing finite partitions

Choose a countable `λ`-dense family of measurable sets `D_1,D_2,…`. Let `P_k` be the finite measurable partition generated by the first `k` sets. For every fixed `k`, setwise convergence gives

`d_{n,k} := (1/2) Σ_{C∈P_k} |μ_n(C)-ν(C)| → 0`.

A deterministic diagonal choice yields integers `k(n)→∞` with `d_{n,k(n)}→0`: for example, choose increasing `N_k≥k` such that `n≥N_k` implies `d_{n,k}≤1/k`, and use the largest `k` with `N_k≤n` (handling finitely many initial indices arbitrarily).

### A common target and all couplings at once

For each `n`, enumerate the cells of `P_{k(n)}` as `C_1,…,C_r`. Let `q_{ij}` be a maximal coupling matrix of their probability vectors: its row sums are `μ_n(C_i)`, its column sums are `ν(C_j)`, and `Σ_i q_{ii}=1-d_{n,k(n)}`.

For `y∈C_j` with `ν(C_j)>0`, define the probability kernel

`K_n(y,B)= Σ_{i:μ_n(C_i)>0} [q_{ij}/ν(C_j)] [μ_n(B∩C_i)/μ_n(C_i)]`.

On cells of zero `ν`-measure choose `K_n(y,·)=μ_n`. These are genuine kernels on arbitrary measurable spaces: their dependence on `y` is constant on finitely many measurable cells. They require no disintegration theorem.

First sample `Y` with law `ν`. Conditional on `Y`, use the kernels `K_n` to sample all `Y_n`, conditionally independently. The countable kernel product construction gives a single joint probability measure on `S×S^N`; it is valid on arbitrary measurable spaces. By summing the matrix rows, `Law(Y_n)=μ_n`. Moreover,

`P(Y_n and Y lie in different cells of P_{k(n)}) = d_{n,k(n)} → 0`.

This equality concerns measurable partition labels, not a potentially nonmeasurable diagonal of `S×S`.

### Every measurable event

Fix `A∈Σ` and `ε>0`. By uniform absolute continuity choose `δ>0` such that `λ(B)<δ` implies `sup_n μ_n(B)<ε` and `ν(B)<ε`. Choose some `D_j` with `λ(A△D_j)<δ`. For all sufficiently large `n`, `k(n)≥j`, so `D_j` is a union of cells of `P_{k(n)}`. Thus

`P(1_A(Y_n)≠1_A(Y))`
`≤ μ_n(A△D_j) + ν(A△D_j) + P(1_{D_j}(Y_n)≠1_{D_j}(Y))`
`≤ 2ε + d_{n,k(n)}`.

Taking the limit superior and then `ε↓0` proves (2). Finally,

`|μ_n(A)-ν(A)| ≤ P(1_A(Y_n)≠1_A(Y))`

proves the converse.

## 3. What survives on an entirely arbitrary measurable space

For each prescribed COUNTABLE family of events `D`, setwise convergence always gives a single recoupled sequence satisfying the mismatch limit for every event in `σ(D)`. Repeat the proof using the increasing finite partitions generated by `D`; finite-measure approximation shows their algebra is `λ`-dense in `σ(D)`.

However, that coupling may depend on `D`. No argument above obtains one coupling for all of `Σ` when its `λ`-measure algebra is nonseparable. This is a proof gap in that generality, not a proved impossibility theorem. The fact that countably many Radon–Nikodym densities generate a countably generated sub-σ-field does NOT remove the gap: an arbitrary test event may lie outside that sub-σ-field, and a coupling preserving its relevant additional randomness has not been constructed.

For an arbitrary finite partition, maximal coupling of its labels is always possible. The assertion that its mismatch tends to zero is just total variation convergence on a finite set, hence a direct restatement of finitely many setwise tests. A partition-dependent family alone should not be advertised as a substantive full solution to Thorisson's problem.

## 4. Why this theorem does not solve the process-coupling problem

For `S=E^{N_0}`, write `μ_n=Law(θ_nX)` and assume `ν` stationary. The construction above supplies `Y_n~Law(θ_nX)`, but imposes no consistency relation `Y_{n+1}=θ_1Y_n`. It therefore does NOT supply a coupling of the whole original processes `X,X′`.

In particular it does not prove the candidate single aligned-orbit condition

`∃ coupling (X,X′), ∀A∈Σ: P(1_A(θ_nX)≠1_A(θ_nX′))→0`.

That candidate implies setwise convergence immediately, using stationarity of `X′`. This recoupling argument does not establish necessity; Attempt 2 proves necessity false for that aligned-orbit candidate. Replacing the independent recoupled sequence by the original shift orbit is a substantive extra constraint, not a change of notation.

Similarly, the theorem makes no assertion of eventual equality of whole paths, almost-sure convergence for every event, finite coupling times, distributional exact coupling, or distributional shift-coupling. Its limits are in probability separately for each fixed measurable event.

## 5. A genuinely false stronger formulation: comparing shifted X to fixed X′

Even a stationary iid fair Bernoulli process is a counterexample to the proposal

`∃ coupling (X,X′), ∀A: P(1_A(θ_nX)≠1_A(X′))→0`.

Take `A={x:x_0=1}`. This would force `P(X_n≠X′_0)→0`. The union bound then gives

`P(X_n≠X_{n+1}) ≤ P(X_n≠X′_0)+P(X_{n+1}≠X′_0)→0`,

whereas the fixed law of the iid process gives `P(X_n≠X_{n+1})=1/2` for every `n`. No coupling can change that. Nevertheless its shifted laws equal its stationary law exactly. A fixed recoupled target `Y` is legitimate only because the `Y_n` are NOT required to retain the original orbit's joint law.

## 6. Source and acceptance posture

The original target and general-measurable-state scope were checked against Thorisson's public preprint, *Some Open Probability Problems*, Section 3: https://cms.dm.uba.ar/depto/public/Some%20Open%20Problems-preprint.pdf .

The uniform-absolute-continuity input is the classical Vitali–Hahn–Saks theorem, not a new assertion. An openly accessible university exposition with the applicable theorem and proof is Robert Gardner's measure-theory lecture material, Section 18.5: https://faculty.etsu.edu/gardnerr/5210/Beamer-Proofs/Proofs-18-5.pdf . The finite-kernel construction and event approximation above supply the remaining argument explicitly.

Acceptable claim: a proved separable-measure-algebra characterization of setwise convergence by a single freely recoupled sequence, together with an elementary obstruction to a fixed-target orbit characterization.

Unacceptable claim: resolution of the original arbitrary-measurable-space coupling problem, proof of the aligned-orbit candidate, novelty over the coupling literature, or validation of any uninspected contemporary manuscript.

## 7. Subsequent independent audit of the aligned-orbit obstruction

After completing the proof above, I independently checked `ATTEMPT_2.md`, which proves an obstruction using a mixing rank-one automorphism. Its central extension from graph couplings to arbitrary bistochastic couplings appears sound. In particular:

- The averaged restricted coupling and the mixture of normalized tower-diagonal surrogate measures agree exactly on tower-measurable rectangles.
- The required uniform marginal bounds hold for the aggregate restricted coupling and for the surrogate mixture. They need not hold for each normalized cell restriction of the original arbitrary coupling; the proof correctly avoids assuming that.
- Surrogate probabilities are asymptotically invariant and dominated by a common constant times an off-diagonal graph coupling. For offsets tending to infinity, mixing and invariance force a product-measure limit. For a fixed nonzero offset, ergodicity forces that graph-coupling limit.
- A single finite partition uniformly separates these possible limits from the diagonal. The tower estimate and then `a↑1` force the original bistochastic coupling to be diagonal.
- Symmetrization plus a nonnegative diagonal remainder converts the alleged coupling of `fρ` and `ρ` into a bistochastic coupling, without losing the required mismatch limits.

Attempt 2 supplies explicit staircase parameters and checks the hypotheses of the cited Creutz–Silva statement of Adams' mixing theorem. Mixing remains an imported theorem, not independently reproved here. With that dependency, the combined record DISPROVES necessity of the aligned-orbit condition stated in Section 4, by the separate rank-one argument rather than by this freely recoupled-sequence theorem.

This strengthens the scope warning: a single universally tested freely recoupled sequence can exist while no universally tested aligned pair preserving both process orbits exists. This freely recoupled-sequence theorem is still restricted to separable measure algebras and does not retain the original orbit.
