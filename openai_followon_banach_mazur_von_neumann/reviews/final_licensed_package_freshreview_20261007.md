## Exact mathematical claim and cohomological input

The paper's quantifiers match the original target: for each fixed complex
von Neumann algebra M there exists one positive epsilon_M such that every
complex von Neumann algebra N satisfying either strict ordinary
Banach–Mazur inequality is Jordan *-isomorphic to M. The threshold depends
on M. The comparison objects are von Neumann algebras, the maps are bounded
bijective complex-linear maps with bounded inverses, and the preduals are
the canonical preduals. No universal numerical threshold, arbitrary-Banach-
space target, complete-bounded reinterpretation, or global associative
*-isomorphism conclusion is claimed.

I reconstructed the needed family-295 result from all five actual source
sections, and then from the entire actual archived PDF. Its cochains are
all bounded complex multilinear maps into the same algebra, with the
ordinary multilinear norm and the actual image of the standard Hochschild
differential. The final assembly gives an actual bounded primitive. It
does not merely show a reduced group vanishes or that separately normal or
completely bounded cohomology vanishes.

## Arbitrary scope, full carriers and two fixed sources

For a fixed halving algebra V, Roydor's block map F is a self-adjoint
isomorphism with inverse norm tending to one. The target product
`y diamond z=qyz+(1-q)zy` is the direct sum of the original product and its
opposite; centrality of q makes it associative, involutive and unital with
the original C*-norm. Pulling it back to **the whole fixed V** makes the
multiplication defect tend to zero. The deformation lemma applies once to
V, and F Phi inverse is a *-isomorphism into the modified target and hence
a Jordan *-isomorphism to the original target. The two block defects take
a maximum. This avoids assigning uniform deformation constants to moving
central corners and retains opposite orientation correctly.

The full-carrier lemma also has the correct inverse-order and norm factors.
For source projection h of carrier one, rounded target projection k,
w=c(k), and z=theta inverse(w), approximate order for T inverse gives

`h-z <= [2(1+t) r(t)+gamma(t)] 1`,

where r(t)=140 sqrt(t) and gamma(t)=2(1+t)sqrt(2t+t^2).
Both inverse approximation errors carry the factor 1+t. Compression by the
central 1-z makes h(1-z) a projection bounded by a scalar less than one;
it is zero. Hence h<=z and full carrier forces z=w=1. Unitality gives the
same test for the complement. No fiber or separability assertion enters.

The fixed decomposition is M=A plus P plus O: abelian A; halving P
containing even finite type-I, every infinite-cardinal type-I, type-II and
type-III pieces; and finite odd degrees at least three in O. The rank-(n-1)
projection e on each degree-n piece and its rank-one complement both have
full carrier. The single fixed E=eOe halves. Universal vanishing is applied
directly to the two von Neumann algebras **P and E**. It is not deduced by
restricting M's cohomology to a noncentral corner.

For the odd target D, compression and carrier preservation give q and 1-q
of full carrier. The complementary corner is abelian by the commutative
geometric result; therefore D is type I. Exact rigidity of E gives a
Jordan *-isomorphism E to qDq. Full-corner center identification transports
the original degree supports through an exact center isomorphism beta.
Unital Jordan *-isomorphisms are positive order isomorphisms, so beta
preserves all projection joins; the degree supports exhaust the target.

On the image of degree n, q splits into n-1 equivalent abelian projections
with full central support. The complementary abelian projection has the
same support and is equivalent to each. They fill the entire unit of that
piece with exactly n equivalent abelian projections, establishing type I_n
and the prescribed center. Finite homogeneous classification then gives
the isomorphism on each piece, and bounded central products assemble them.
I checked the exact full-corner center and equal-carrier abelian projection
statements, and finite homogeneous classification, in the primary
[Nelson lecture notes](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/banelson/teaching/209/209_notes.pdf),
Lemma 5.3.11, Corollary 5.3.12, Lemma 5.3.13 and Theorem 5.3.17.

This reconstruction neither cancels infinite cardinal ranks nor enumerates
infinite degrees. All infinite cardinalities are already in P. It works
with arbitrary centers, unbounded odd finite degrees, and nonseparable
preduals. Only three fixed central restrictions, two fixed deformation
thresholds, and finitely many errors tending to zero must be controlled.
Thus the quantifier order is for each fixed M, one positive epsilon_M
working for all N, not a target-dependent or degree-dependent sequence of
thresholds whose infimum might be zero.

For an ordinary predual isomorphism S:M_* to N_*, its adjoint maps N to M,
has exactly the same norm, and has inverse (S inverse)* with the same
inverse norm. Symmetry of algebra-space distance yields
`d_BM(M,N)<=d_BM(M_*,N_*)`. The same algebra threshold therefore works.
Jordan *-isomorphisms are normal by preservation of bounded increasing
positive suprema and induce canonical-predual isometries. The converse
predual-isometry implication adjoints to an algebra isometry and uses
Kadison's theorem after removing the unitary multiplier. The primary
[Sherman article](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0309365v2) covers all algebras
at p=1 as well as the p=infinity Kadison case; its 1<p<infinity theorem
is not substituted at the excluded endpoints.

Boundary checks pass. The literal formula gives d(0,0)=0 and mixed zero
distances infinity, so either hypothesis for M=0 forces N=0. Nonzero
distortions are at least one. Exact isometries meet either positive strict
threshold. The proof requires no attainment of the infimum, and makes no
claim at equality. Zero summands are omitted. Algebra/opposite pairs give
the intended Jordan conclusion rather than an unjustified associative
conclusion.
