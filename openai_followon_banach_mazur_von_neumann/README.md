For each complex von Neumann algebra M, the candidate theorem gives a positive threshold epsilon_M such that every complex von Neumann algebra N satisfying either d_BM(M,N)<1+epsilon_M or d_BM(M_*,N_*)<1+epsilon_M is Jordan *-isomorphic to M. Distance uses bounded complex-linear Banach-space isomorphisms and ordinary operator norms. The same threshold works for the algebra and canonical-predual conclusions. It may depend on M; the comparison object N is also a von Neumann algebra, and the conclusion retains the possible reversal of multiplication on central summands.

The proof relies on OpenAI family 295's ordinary bounded self-coefficient Hochschild vanishing theorem, pinned at `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`, and Roydor's established geometric near-isometry estimates. The upstream manuscript received full independent mathematical audits with no substantive gap found. Finite exact checks and static Lean authentication supplement those audits; no fresh Lean kernel build, import or axiom probe succeeded, and this follow-on theorem is not formalized here.

The complete user-supplied 26-page Roydor publisher-formatted source has now been read. Its printed Theorem 1.2 assumes **separable predual**, unlike the unrestricted conditional formulation already announced in Roydor's October 2020 slides. Thus a bare composition of that printed theorem with family 295 proves only the separable case. The current manuscript supplies a separate fixed-source extension: it partitions M into its abelian, halving and finite odd type-I parts, corrects multiplication on at most two fixed algebras, and reconstructs odd degrees from full abelian projections. This treats all infinite type-I cardinal dimensions inside the halving part and allows arbitrary centers. It avoids using Roydor's Theorem 1.1 or Lemma 4.3 and explicitly corrects the source's printed differential and off-diagonal index errors.

The cohomological correction mechanism is inherited from Johnson, Raeburn–Taylor and the symmetry-preserving Ricard–Roydor iteration.
