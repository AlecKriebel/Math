## Main theorem: independent adversarial assessment

The central bound is correct. In a connected locally finite one-ended spanning tree, deleting a tree edge has exactly one nonempty finite side. From each v, its successive ray sides S_n strictly increase and exhaust the graph. Strict increase makes their sizes distinct positive integers; coalescence makes the exhaustion valid even for vertices initially off that ray. The argument uses a global degree bound Delta, as the proposition states.

Every ambient non-tree edge crossing S_n has a connecting tree path containing the parent edge t_n. The maximum rule therefore bounds its insertion probability by 2^(-s_n). There are at most Delta*s_n crossing edges. The union bound gives

    sum_n P(t_n deleted or a non-tree bypass of S_n inserted | T)
      <= sum_m>=1 (1+Delta*m) 2^(-m) = 1+2*Delta < infinity.

One inserted edge may affect several cuts, and different bad-cut events may be dependent. First Borel–Cantelli needs no such independence. Conditional on any good deterministic T it gives eventual **actual singleton X-boundaries**, not merely surviving tree bridges. Countability of v yields a single simultaneous good event, and integration over the tree law is legitimate.

The retained-tree tails percolate and all meet a common retained infinite component C_R. Any infinite retained-tree component contains a ray and hence meets that component. An infinite X-component from v must leave each late finite S_n across its only boundary edge t_n; both endpoints of that edge lie on the retained tail. Thus it meets C_R. This excludes an extra infinite component assembled out of added edges and finite retained-tree pieces. It proves uniqueness without relying on an invariant-percolation uniqueness theorem.

After fixing a good pair (T,X), every infinite bond-thinned path must retain arbitrarily many specified distinct late cut edges. Its probability is at most p^k for each k. Site thinning must retain arbitrarily many distinct endpoints along the same ray. Both bounds vanish for every p<1, while retained tails give percolation at p=1. Restriction to C preserves the boundary identity because the late cut endpoints lie in C_R. The argument proves all four quenched thresholds. A single structural witness works for every real p<1; no uncountable intersection of null-set statements is used.

Forgetting T causes no conditional-energy gap. All other X-coordinates are functions of T and uniforms other than U_e. Therefore the open and closed conditional probabilities are E[r_T(e) | other X] and E[1-r_T(e) | other X]. Both integrands are strictly positive. Integrating over a putative positive-measure zero set of either conditional expectation gives a contradiction. Countably many edges and standard Borel conditional versions handle the common null set and harmless modifications there. Small hidden-tree rates neither prove nor disprove a common marginal energy bound; the note correctly claims neither.

The finite-side/path functions and good-tree domain have countable Borel descriptions. Setting fallback rates to 1/2 on the invariant complement yields a global measurable equivariant kernel. Interpreting them as coarse bonds retains all coarse vertices and is not the induced graph on final open sites. There is no parity mixture in this coarse law.

For a finite primal tree side, the dual ambient boundary is finite and even. Only the parent edge's dual is outside the complementary dual tree. Therefore that boundary is exactly the simple fundamental cycle of that added dual edge: a further nonempty finite even component cannot lie in a tree. Along the remaining finite dual-tree path, finite-side sizes occur at most twice by decomposition into two strictly increasing ancestor arms. The conditional expected open coarse cut size is consequently at most

    1 + 2 sum_m>=1 4^(-m) = 5/3.
