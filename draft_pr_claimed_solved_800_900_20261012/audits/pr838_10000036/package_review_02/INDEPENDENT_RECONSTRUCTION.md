## Exact target and mechanism

The target is a bond law on every nearest-neighbor Z^d, d>=2, with strict-positive conditional probabilities for both states after conditioning on all *other output* edges, all graph-automorphism invariance, translation mixing, a unique infinite component, and quenched bond/site thresholds one for both the whole output graph and its infinite component. The graph may have finite components. A uniform marginal energy bound is neither needed nor established.

For a fixed locally finite connected one-ended spanning tree, orient each edge toward the unique end. The finite descendant side at successive ray edges from v strictly grows and contains any fixed w once the v-ray passes its coalescence point with the w-ray. Thus these sides exhaust the graph, their cardinalities are distinct positive integers, and ambient boundary sizes are at most Delta times their cardinalities. A non-tree edge crosses such a side exactly when its finite tree path uses that side's parent edge. Consequently the max-size insertion rule bounds every bypass probability by 2^(-s), and a union bound gives a summable sequence of bad cuts. First Borel–Cantelli, conditional on the entire tree, yields singleton output boundaries eventually on each ray; countability gives the property for all starting vertices on one joint full-measure event.

Retained tree ray tails exist, coalesce, and belong to a single infinite component of X intersect T. Any other infinite component of X would have to cross a late singleton cut whose edge already belongs to that retained infinite tree component. This proves uniqueness without importing an invariant-percolation uniqueness theorem. Every infinite bond-thinned path must preserve arbitrarily many prescribed distinct late cut edges; its probability is at most p^k. Site thinning must preserve arbitrarily many distinct prescribed ray endpoints, giving the same bound. A single good realized pair (T,X) gives these estimates for every p<1, rather than a problematic uncountable intersection of events. Rational-p connection probabilities and countable finite-ball limits also show that the graph-only threshold property is Borel; no selection of a hidden tree from X is used.

Conditional energy survives mixing over hidden trees: X_e uses an independent uniform U_e, while all other outputs are measurable in the tree and uniforms other than U_e. The conditional success probability is E[r_T(e) | other X], and both r_T(e) and 1-r_T(e) are strictly positive. A nonnegative conditional expectation cannot vanish on a positive-measure conditioning event when its integrand is strictly positive. Ordinary a.s. versions can be set to 1/2 on null exceptional conditioning configurations.

Finite sides, finite tree paths and the good-tree domain have countable Borel descriptions. The kernel commutes with graph automorphisms because the sizes and connecting paths do. Timár's inspected 2018 v1 Theorem 1 supplies a connected one-ended FIID spanning tree on the deterministic rooted lattice: bounded degree, amenability, unimodularity, ergodicity of the deterministic rooted class, and one-endedness all hold for d>=2. Under the coherent isomorphism-equivariant FIID convention, the tree has full lattice-automorphism invariance. Joint iid vertex and edge labels are translation mixing, and a measurable translation factor inherits mixing. A disconnected high-dimensional UST cannot replace this connected input because some connecting paths would be undefined.

## Independently reconstructed planar deduction

The exact fixed-phase HM midpoint marginal retains every coarse vertex by interpretation; it is not the induced post-flip open-site graph. A midpoint separating s coarse vertices has s coarse vertex sites and s-1 subdivided finite-tree edge sites behind it, hence b=2s-1 and flip rate 2^(-b-1)=4^(-s). Conditional independence of the midpoint marginal follows by retaining a fixed subset of independent conditional flip coordinates. This rate is different from the current all-dimensional max rule.

For a finite primal past, only its parent tree edge crosses the cut. Complementary dual edges of the remaining boundary are in the dual tree. The whole finite boundary is dual-even; adding the missing dual parent edge to the dual tree therefore produces exactly its fundamental cycle, since an additional nonempty finite even component cannot lie in a tree. The dual path has at most two edges of any finite-past size: split the simple path at its common ancestor toward the unique end, giving two strictly increasing chains. The conditional expected number of open coarse boundary edges is consequently at most 1+2 sum_(m>=1) 4^(-m)=5/3. Fatou forces finite liminf almost surely. Exhaustion makes boundaries escape each finite edge set, allowing a subsequence of disjoint boundaries of random finite bounded size. Independent thinning deletes each selected whole cut with a uniform positive lower probability (1-p)^K, excluding infinite escape. Summable original tree deletions guarantee percolation before thinning, and positivity/automorphism invariance descend as above.

For the actual HM fine-site graph the same cycle is subdivided on the fine lattice. Apart from the single primal-tree midpoint, its sites are initially closed and lie along a simple path in the subdivided one-ended dual tree. Their finite descendant sizes occur at most twice, now allowing zero, so their expected flips to open total at most 2 sum_(m>=0) 2^(-m-1)=2. Thus expected open sites on the surrounding fine circuit are at most 3. The circuits escape finite sets, and Fatou plus vertex-disjoint circuit subsequences proves internal *site* threshold one. HM Lemma 3.1 then yields the internal bond threshold for the bounded-degree induced open graph. That lemma alone would not provide an external coarse bond law with ordinary finite energy. This argument is supplied here, not read as an assertion of an earlier printed exact solution.
