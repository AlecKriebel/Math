## Why the HM threshold cannot be dismissed as unstated

The relevant new audit observation is that singleton cut boundaries are unnecessary. For a finite primal tree past, its planar dual boundary is the complementary-dual-tree path plus one missing edge. On a finite path of a one-ended tree, each finite-past size occurs at most twice. Summing HM flip probabilities along that path bounds the conditional expected number of finally open sites on the surrounding fine-grid cycle by three, independently of the past. Fatou gives a finite liminf almost surely. Escaping cycles then supply infinitely many disjoint finally-open cutsets of bounded size. For a fixed good output and any p<1, independent thinning deletes a whole cutset with probability bounded away from zero, so no infinite path survives. The same structure works simultaneously for all p after the graph is fixed.

The actual HM post-flip site graph therefore has the threshold-one property by this derivation. HM Lemma 3.1 transfers its internal site/bond thresholds, but does not create external bond finite energy. The report includes a precise counterexample to that shortcut and an exact fixed-phase midpoint projection that avoids it. The projection uses HM’s unchanged flip rates 4^{-s}, not the initially considered changed-rate 2^{-s} adaptation. Its expected bond-cut size is at most 5/3. These distinctions prevent both under-crediting the old construction and falsely citing it as a stated bond theorem.
