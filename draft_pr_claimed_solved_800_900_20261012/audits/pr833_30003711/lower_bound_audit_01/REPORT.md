# PR833 / problem 30003711: independent lower-bound dependency audit

**Audit conclusion:** the proposed Poincare-duality challenge is correct. For the exact ordinary mod-3 map stated in Chen--Wan arXiv:2306.17303v2, Proposition 4.2(1), one has

\[
c^*:H^7(BK;\mathbf F_3)\longrightarrow H^7(PU(3)/K;\mathbf F_3),\qquad c^*=0.
\]

The target has dimension two. This contradicts that preprint proposition. It also supplies a specific extra element in the edge-map kernel beyond the Chern-class ideal used on preprint pages 6--7.

The audit does **not** disprove the genus lower bound 8. A different detector, possibly with a nontrivial coefficient module, could prove it. The published 2025 article has later revision dates and a 13-page pagination, whereas the inspected preprint has 10 pages. No published proof body or corrected alternative proof was recovered here. Consequently the candidate's lower-bound dependency is **not cleared**, and its equality claim cannot be independently certified from the inspected material. This is a focused dependency finding; the candidate's new upper bound is outside the verdict of this audit.

Completed 2026-10-15 UTC. No prior acceptance verdict was used. No outreach, Git/index/branch mutation, publication, queue action, or service write was performed.

## Exact claims and maps

The original queue asks for the ordinary Schwarz genus of the nine-flex cover on the smooth cubic coefficient space \(X=\mathbf {CP}^9\setminus\Delta\). It does not replace this base by the quotient under projective coordinate changes. The author's proof claims genus 8 using a new upper bound and Chen--Wan Theorems 1.2/4.1 for the lower bound. The original queue JSON and the complete author proof were read.

Use \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), and the **actual determinant-one matrices**

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\operatorname {diag}(1,\omega,\omega^2).
\]

Let \(K\subset PU(3)\) be the group generated by their projective images. Put

\[
G=\langle A,B,\omega I\rangle\subset SU(3),\quad
q:G\twoheadrightarrow K,\quad M=PU(3)/K.
\]

The map \(c:M\to BK\) classifies the **nine-sheet regular cover** \(PU(3)\to M\). The map \(u:M\to BG\) classifies the universal cover. Up to the conventional possible opposite-group identification for right deck transformations,

\[
c\simeq Bq\circ u.
\]

Changing that convention changes coordinates or signs, not the vanishing assertions below.

The preprint's other group is the topological group

\[
\widetilde P=\{\lambda A^aB^b:\lambda\in U(1),\ a,b\in\mathbf F_3\}\subset U(3).
\]

It has nine circle components, rather than 27 discrete elements. If \(p:\widetilde P\to K\), and \(j:M=U(3)/\widetilde P\to B\widetilde P\) classifies the principal \(\widetilde P\)-bundle, the preprint diagram is

\[
c^*=j^*p^*.
\]

Neither \(p^*:H^*(BK)\to H^*(B\widetilde P)\) nor the universal-cover map \(u\) is being substituted for \(c\). All coefficients in the contradiction are **constant \(\mathbf F_3\)**.

## Independent proof of the vanishing map

### 1. The finite lift and fundamental group

Direct multiplication gives \(BA=\omega AB\). Every element of \(G\) has a unique expression

\[
(a,b,t):=\omega^t A^aB^b,\qquad a,b,t\in\mathbf F_3,
\]

with multiplication

\[
(a,b,t)(d,e,s)=(a+d,b+e,t+s+bd).
\tag{1}
\]

Uniqueness follows either from the nonzero position and phase of each matrix column or by comparing the three entries after the shift is fixed. These 27 matrices are unitary with determinant one. Every element has order dividing three. Their center and derived subgroup both equal

\[
Z=[G,G]=\{(0,0,t):t\in\mathbf F_3\}\cong C_3.
\]

Indeed (1) gives commutator central coordinate \(bd-ea\), and these values exhaust \(\mathbf F_3\). Commuting with both coordinate generators forces \(a=b=0\). Thus

\[
G/[G,G]\cong K\cong C_3^2.
\tag{2}
\]

The kernel of \(SU(3)\to PU(3)\) is \(\mu_3 I\); hence the entire inverse image of \(K\) is exactly \(G\). Also \(SU(3)\) is simply connected: the standard fibration \(SU(2)\to SU(3)\to S^5\), with \(SU(2)\cong S^3\), proves this from its homotopy sequence. Therefore

\[
M\cong SU(3)/G,\qquad \pi_1(M)\cong G
\]

with the harmless deck convention already noted. In particular \(\pi_1(M)\) is **not** \(K\).

### 2. The critical product is explicitly a coboundary

Write \(y,y'\in H^1(BK;\mathbf F_3)\) for the coordinate characters \(y(a,b)=a\), \(y'(a,b)=b\). Define the normalized inhomogeneous one-cochain on \(G\)

\[
f(a,b,t)=t-ab\pmod 3.
\]

For trivial coefficients the differential is

\[
(\delta f)(g,h)=f(h)-f(gh)+f(g).
\]

Substituting \(g=(a,b,t),h=(d,e,s)\) and (1) gives

\[
\delta f(g,h)
=(s-de)-[t+s+bd-(a+d)(b+e)]+(t-ab)
=ae
=(q^*y\smile q^*y')(g,h).
\tag{3}
\]

Thus \(q^*(yy')=0\) in \(H^2(BG;\mathbf F_3)\), and

\[
c^*(yy')=0\quad\hbox{in }H^2(M;\mathbf F_3).
\tag{4}
\]

This proof does not infer the extension class from a group name. The section \((a,b)\mapsto(a,b,0)\) has factor set \(b d\); this represents \(y' y=-yy'\). It has no Bockstein \(x\) or \(x'\) summand: its restrictions to the two coordinate cyclic subgroups split, and its alternating part is nonzero. The sign is immaterial to (4). Equation (3) is the stronger, directly checkable certificate.

### 3. All degree-one classes are pulled back

For any connected space, \(H^1(-;\mathbf F_3)=\operatorname {Hom}(\pi_1(-),\mathbf F_3)\). By (2), precomposition by \(q\) is an isomorphism. Therefore

\[
c^*:H^1(BK;\mathbf F_3)\xrightarrow\sim H^1(M;\mathbf F_3).
\tag{5}
\]

Both sides have dimension two. No assumption that the nine-sheet cover is universal was made.

### 4. Poincare duality rules out the degree-seven detector

The space \(M\) is a connected compact smooth manifold of real dimension eight without boundary. A Lie-group orientation of \(SU(3)\) descends: every right translation by an element of \(G\) is isotopic to the identity through right translations, since \(SU(3)\) is connected. Hence \(M\) is oriented, also over \(\mathbf F_3\).

The constant-coefficient ring of \(K=C_3^2\) is

\[
H^*(BK;\mathbf F_3)=\Lambda(y,y')\otimes\mathbf F_3[x,x'],\quad
|y|=|y'|=1,\quad |x|=|x'|=2.
\]

Every \(\alpha\in H^7(BK;\mathbf F_3)\) is \(yP(x,x')+y'Q(x,x')\), where \(P,Q\) have polynomial degree three. For \(\beta=r y+s y'\),

\[
\alpha\beta=yy'(sP-rQ).
\]

Equation (4) implies \(c^*\alpha\smile c^*\beta=0\). Equation (5) says that \(c^*\beta\) runs over **all** \(H^1(M;\mathbf F_3)\). Poincare duality makes the pairing

\[
H^7(M;\mathbf F_3)\otimes H^1(M;\mathbf F_3)
\longrightarrow H^8(M;\mathbf F_3)\cong\mathbf F_3
\]

nondegenerate. Consequently \(c^*\alpha=0\) for every \(\alpha\), as claimed. Duality also gives \(\dim H^7(M;\mathbf F_3)=2\), so this is not a vanishing-target argument.

None of the possible escape routes in the challenge applies: the fundamental group, abelianization, extension factor set, coefficient ring, degree count, compactness, absence of boundary, orientability, identity of the classifying map, and perfect pairing have each been checked. At characteristic two the exterior-sign discussion would need changing; the exact claim uses characteristic three.

## Diagnosis of the preprint proof

The inspected [Chen--Wan v2 source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17303v2) states the nonzero constant-coefficient degree-seven map on page 5, invokes it for genus at least eight, and ends its proof on page 7 with the proposed class \(yxx'^2\). Equations (3)--(5) and duality show that this proposed class maps to zero.

The relevant primary comparison is [Leary's arXiv copy of his 1992 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.4831), Theorem 2 and proof on its pages 2--3. Leary defines \(c_2\) as the reduction of a transfer-defined integral class, \(z\) as a top Chern class, and identifies the nonzero degree-two class \(y'y\). For \(p=3\), use \(z=c_3\). His relations include

\[
xy'=x'y,\quad x^3y'=x'^3y,\quad x^3x'=x'^3x,
\]
\[
c_2y=-x^2y,\quad c_2y'=-x'^2y',\quad
c_2x=-x^3,\quad c_2x'=-x'^3.
\]

The preprint's displayed Theorem 4.3 omits the degree-eight polynomial relation and prints \(-x^2y'\) in one place where Leary has \(-x'^2y'\); its later calculation uses the latter. These transcription differences do not repair the degree-seven claim.

Here is a concrete reason the kernel assertion cannot hold. Let \(R=H^*(B\widetilde P;\mathbf F_3)\), with the correctly transcribed Leary relations. In degree seven a basis is

\[
u_0=yx^3,\ u_1=yx^2x'=yx'^3,\ u_2=yxx'^2,\
v_3=y'x'^3,\ zy,\ zy'.
\tag{6}
\]

Leary's stated monomial basis in his proof independently verifies independence. The Chern ideal \(I=(c_1,c_2,c_3)\), with \(c_1\) a nonzero multiple of \(yy'\), has degree-seven part spanned by

\[
u_0,u_1,v_3,zy,zy'.
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed \(c_1 R^5=0\), \(R^3\) is spanned by \(yx,yx'=y'x,y'x'\), and multiplication by \(c_2\) gives \(-u_0,-u_1,-v_3\). The remaining two generators come from \(c_3R^1\). Thus \(u_2\notin I\), but the preceding independent proof gives

\[
j^*(u_2)=j^*p^*(\widetilde y\,\widetilde x\,\widetilde x'^2)=0.
\]

Therefore \(I\subsetneq\ker j^*\) already in degree seven. This is an actual failure of the kernel equality, beyond a general concern about spectral sequences.

For completeness, the Chern identifications can be checked without accepting the preprint's assertion on faith. The inclusion representation of \(\widetilde P\) is induced from the character \(\lambda B^b\mapsto\lambda\) on its index-three subgroup. The determinant of the induced trivial representation is trivial (a three-cycle has positive sign), so the first Chern class is the transfer of that character's first Chern class. It is Leary's \(\chi_1\), reducing to \(y'y\) in his convention. The sign difference from \(yy'\) changes no ideal. His \(z\) is explicitly the top Chern class of this irreducible representation.

One can also check the second Chern class in degree four. For each of the four projective lines \(\ell\subset K\), its inverse image has the form \(T_\ell=U(1)\times C_3\). A noncentral order-three matrix in \(G\) has eigenvalues \(1,\omega,\omega^2\): for a nonzero shift it is a weighted three-cycle of determinant one, and for shift zero this follows from the diagonal entries. With degree-two generators \(t\) from the circle and \(w\) from \(C_3\), its three weights are \(t,t+w,t-w\). Its mod-3 second Chern class is \(-w^2\). The relations \(c_2x=-x^3,c_2x'=-x'^3\) show that Leary's \(c_2\) restricts to the same class; \(w\) is a non-zero-divisor in \(H^*(BT_\ell;\mathbf F_3)\).

These restrictions detect \(R^4\). In its basis \((c_2,x^2,xx',x'^2)\), evaluate at \((a,b)=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2)\). The resulting four rows are \((-1,a^2,ab,b^2)\); their determinant is nonzero modulo three. Thus the two degree-four classes agree. Accordingly changing the Chern labels does not remove the extra kernel class.

The logical error on preprint page 6 is inferring a complete edge-map kernel from the transgressions of the fibre generators. Those transgressions give elements of the kernel. They do not in general exclude additional differentials supported by classes involving zero divisors after earlier differentials. Here \(yy'\) is nonzero in \(R\), but annihilates \(y,y',x,x',c_2\); many fibre combinations can survive the first differential. The explicit extra kernel element (6)--(7) proves that additional effects must be accounted for. This audit does not assert a particular secondary differential formula without a full filtered calculation; the direct vanishing proof and the strict kernel inclusion already settle the challenge.

In particular, there is no contradiction in \(p^*(yy')\ne0\) on \(B\widetilde P\) but \(c^*(yy')=0\) on \(M\). Only the latter space is the closed eight-manifold to which the duality proof applies.
