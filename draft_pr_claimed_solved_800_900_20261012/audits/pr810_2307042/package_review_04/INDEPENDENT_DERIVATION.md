## Claim and source interpretation

The claim is a local strict envelope inequality for the normalized minimal kernel at the inner antipode of the specific annulus 1<|z|<e, base a=sqrt(e). It rules out any equality set that has non-base points accumulating at a. It does not rule out distant isolated equality and makes no global envelope formula claim. The source's disc-radius equality and its subsequent Green-line question support equality along a line, rather than the automatic normalized equality at a. Printed pages 173–174 are confirmed by the fetched v2 PDF; the formula there has typographic inconsistencies, but the disc example fixes the tested property. The attribution is to Arsove and Johnson. ## Independent analytic reconstruction

Take cylinder coordinates (x,y)=(log|z|,arg z), x in (0,1), y modulo 2pi. In w=y+ix, exp(pi w) maps the strip to the upper half-plane. The standard upper-half-plane Poisson density, multiplied by the derivative of the boundary map, yields p(x,t)=sin(pi x)/(2(cosh(pi t)-cos(pi x))). It is positive and harmonic. On an interior compact set the denominator grows as exp(pi|t|), so any fixed number of derivatives is bounded by a compact-dependent constant times exp(-pi|t|). Periodization over t=y-pi+2pi k therefore converges with all fixed finite derivatives. This makes P a single-valued positive harmonic function on the annulus.

The upper-half-plane Green logarithmic ratio pulls back to g_S=1/2 log[(cosh(pi(y-eta))-cos(pi(x+s)))/(cosh(pi(y-eta))-cos(pi(x-s)))]. The difference of numerator and denominator is 2 sin(pi x) sin(pi s)>0. Periodization produces one logarithmic pole on the quotient, continuous zero boundary data, and no added harmonic term by the maximum principle on the compact annular closure. Differentiating the formula in s at zero gives 2pi p. A uniform second-variable expansion on interior first-variable compact sets and eta near pi gives g_C=2pi s P_eta+O(s²). The denominator at A=(1/2,0) has a uniform strictly positive coefficient as eta approaches pi. After division, the error is O(s); P_eta tends locally uniformly to P_pi. This proves the Martin ratio limit for arbitrarily tangential as well as normal approaches. No fixed eta/s relation is assumed.

For minimality, 0<=v<=P forces continuous zero values at all regular boundary points except (0,pi). In local coordinates (x,t) near that point, the singular summand has denominator pi²(x²+t²)/2+O((x²+t²)²), numerator pi x+O(x³), hence p=x/(pi r²)+O(x), uniformly over the half-disc including approaches with x much smaller than |t|. Other summands are smooth and O(x). Odd reflection of v is harmonic on the punctured full disc and O(1/r). Its circle Fourier coefficients, bounded by C/r, exclude every r^{-n} mode with n>=2. Single-valuedness leaves only a possible log term, two dipoles, and a regular harmonic part. Reflection oddness removes log r and t/r²; v=B x/r²+v_0. Along t=0, 0<=B<=1/pi. The singularity of v-pi B P cancels; its regular odd part and O(x) remainder tend to zero. It is harmonic inside and continuous zero on the entire compact annular boundary, so the maximum principle forces it to vanish. Thus P is minimal, independently of whether every boundary point is identified globally with the Martin boundary.

At A, a summand has value q_k/2 with q_k=sech((2k-1)pi²), x derivative -pi q_k²/2, and the y derivatives cancel in k,1-k pairs. Therefore grad K=(-c,0), c=pi sum(q_k²)/sum(q_k)>0. The quotient is a weighted mean bounded by sech(pi²). The elementary inequalities 3<pi<4 and cosh(pi²)>1+pi⁴/2>83/2 prove c<8/83<1/10 without truncation.

The four proposed functions have zero Laplacian, are periodic, normalized at A, and strictly positive everywhere. In particular |b sin y cosh(x-1/2)|<=1/2 and |x-1/2|<1/2. b=1/(2cosh(1/2))>1/4 follows from the geometric majorant cosh(1/2)<=4/3. Their gradient convex hull is the diamond with vertices (±1,0),(0,±b); (-c,0) lies strictly inside it. More explicitly, support in d=(u,v) is max(|u|,b|v|)+cu >=(1-c)max(|u|,b|v|)>75|d|/664>|d|/10. This is an analytic bound in every direction. A common bounded Hessian on a small closed disc for the finite differences h_j-K gives |R_j(d)|<=C_0|d|². Selecting the maximal first derivative yields max(h_j-K)>|d|/10-C_0|d|²>0 for a single punctured disc. Thus H>=max h_j>K with no regularity hypothesis on H. Logarithmic coordinates are a local diffeomorphism at a, so this disc maps to a neighborhood in the annulus.

## Fresh punctured-disc deduction, before supplied addendum

On 0<|z|<1, let a=1/2 and normalize P_zeta(z)=(1-|z|²)/|z-zeta|² by its value at a. Direct differentiation gives the gradients at a:

- pole -1: (-8/3,0);
- poles +i and -i: (-32/15,+8/5), (-32/15,-8/5);
- normalized -log|z|: (-2/log 2,0).

The target (-8/3,0) lies strictly inside the triangle of the last three gradients because -2/log 2<-8/3<-32/15. The needed exact inequality log 2<3/4 follows, for example, from log 2=2 sum_{n>=0} (1/3)^{2n+1}/(2n+1)<2/3+(2/3)sum_{n>=1}(1/9)^n=3/4. Its barycentric weight at the logarithmic vertex is lambda=8 log 2/(30-32 log 2), strictly between zero and one; both other weights are (1-lambda)/2. The three competitors are positive harmonic and normalized. The finite-support-function minimum on the unit circle is positive and the common Taylor argument gives the same local strict isolation for the pole -1, provided it is a minimal punctured-disc Martin kernel. That last classification is classical and will be checked directly in the cited primary sources in the next phase. This deduction is mathematical derivability, not evidence that an earlier author recognized the Green-line consequence or proved this smooth-annulus statement.

## Boundary and falsification checks

At a equality is retained. Small parameter changes may preserve the convex-interior condition but no such generalization is claimed. If c approached one the x-negative support margin would disappear; the present exact c bound avoids that failure. Deleting the y competitors loses the first-order argument in vertical directions. Substituting a kernel near its pole could place its gradient outside the competitor diamond and the argument would fail. These limitations keep the claim local and specific. Numerical direction samples cannot establish the uniform inequality or certify a radius. The exact arguments above do.
