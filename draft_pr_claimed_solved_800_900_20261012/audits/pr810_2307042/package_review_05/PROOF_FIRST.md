# Proof-first independent derivation (before any supplied review verdicts)

Exact hypotheses: cylinder C=(0,1)×R/(2πZ), A=(1/2,0), pole ξ=(0,π); annulus identified by z=exp(x+iy). H is supremum over strictly positive harmonic functions with value ≤1 at A. Success is Kξ(A)=H(A)=1 and Kξ<H on some deleted neighborhood, forbidding any curve of equality with nonbase points tending to A. No statement about remote isolated equality is required or proved.

Derivation: upper-half-plane transform of strip gives p(x,t)=sin(πx)/[2(cosh(πt)-cos(πx))]. The positive periodization at π is harmonic: for interior compact x and bounded y, derivatives of each fixed order are bounded by constants times exp(-π|t|), so all differentiation is normal. Strip Green function is half log of (cosh π(y-η)-cos π(x+s))/(cosh π(y-η)-cos π(x-s)); its singularity is -log distance plus a regular constant. Both boundary sides vanish; periodization has one pole on quotient. The finite cylinder has compact closure, so the Green uniqueness maximum principle is available. Boundary derivative is π sin(πx)/(cosh π(y-η)-cos πx)=2πp. Uniform C² bounds in source s down to 0 and η near π give g=2πsPη+O(s²), uniformly on any target compact. The denominator at A is bounded below by a constant times s. Ratio error O(s) therefore remains valid even for arbitrary tangential η(s)→π.

Minimality: near ξ write r²=x²+t². Taylor expansion shows p=x/(πr²)+O(x) uniformly, since numerator correction O(x³) and denominator correction O(r⁴) yield error O(x). Other images are analytic, vanish at x=0, and contribute O(x). A dominated positive harmonic v has zero continuous boundary data away from ξ, so odd reflection is harmonic across every nonzero boundary point; |v_reflected|≤C/r. Fourier/Laurent coefficients exclude all poles order ≥2, while oddness excludes log and tangential 1/r terms. Thus v=B x/r²+v0, with v0 regular and odd. Along t=0, 0≤B≤1/π. Difference v-πBP is regular at ξ and vanishes there and at all other compact boundary points; maximum principle yields v=πBP. No boundary representation theorem is being assumed in this argument.
