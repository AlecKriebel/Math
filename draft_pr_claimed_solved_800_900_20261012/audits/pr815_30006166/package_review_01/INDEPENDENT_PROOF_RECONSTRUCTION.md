## Claim reconstructed

For the full closed Polish space of continuous actions of the restricted countable wreath product W=(direct sum over Z of Z) semidirect Z on Cantor space, a dense G-delta of actions has an orbit relation that is an increasing union of finite Borel equivalence relations on the **entire** space. The auxiliary claim is every Borel W-action on a standard Borel space carrying an equivariant Borel map into the factorial profinite completion of Z. It permits stabilizers and does not assume a measure, minimality, or a surjective height.

## Generic height: reconstruction and attacks

For each continuous finite residue map c, generator equations determine a clopen constraint D(c,m). Countably many c exist because clopens in Cantor space are finite unions from a countable basis. The conditional lifting obligations U(c;m,n), m dividing n, are open. Given an action satisfying c and any finite action-neighborhood constraint, the exact finite fiber-product extension is conjugated back to Cantor space by a homeomorphism chosen independently on the atoms of a fine finite partition. Each atom and its preimage are nonempty Cantor spaces; the latter is exactly n/m disjoint copies of that atom. The action relations survive by conjugation, with no approximate homomorphism or relation stability assumption.

The partition must refine both the old residue fibers and inverse images of a fine output partition under the finitely many tested group elements. Then the first coordinate of h(x) remains in x's atom, the conjugated output stays in the atom of the original extension output, and the two action outputs remain in the same fine output atom. Enlarging the finite test set symmetrically controls inverse maps in Homeo(C). This checks the full product topology, not only generator evaluations. The resulting residue d reduces to the **prescribed** c, so compatibility survives recursion. Outside D(c,m), the conditional obligation is automatic. Intersecting all countable obligations and beginning at the unique residue mod 1 gives compatible factorial residues for each action. The inverse-limit map is continuous and equivariant. No uniform Borel selection as a function of the action is needed. Compact image and invariance under +/-1 imply surjectivity, though the later Borel theorem does not require it.

Attacks considered: missing fibers, m=n or m=1, lack of compatibility of independently chosen factors, false density in the full group-action space, and accidentally proving a result only for free/minimal actions. None survives this construction.

## Finite slabs: reconstruction and attacks

Normalize x by its unique finite residue r: z=t^(-r)x in B_m. The inverse is t^r z, so this is a Borel bijection even when lamps stabilize points. The lamp move becomes a_(-r) at layer r and an admitted shift retains the base coordinate. Thus a slab uses the fixed subgroup K_m generated by m lamps, abstractly Z^m, acting Borelly on B_m. Degree at most four ensures all metric balls are finite. Finite-word partial-move descriptions make the graph and its extended path metric Borel.

CJMST's Corollary 5.5 explicitly covers **any** Borel action of uniform local polynomial growth; applying it to fixed Z^m is legitimate. Its finite-move characterization is supplied by Lemma 2.2 plus Lemma 3.1 and the paragraph following Definition 3.2. Disjointizing a finite Borel cover gives a fixed finite palette. For each threshold R, the finite set of K_m words of length at most ceil(R) has uniformly finite monochromatic components. Lift the base coloring to every layer. Every length-R slab connection changes its normalized base by at most ceil(R) lamp letters. A monochromatic threshold chain therefore projects into one monochromatic base component. Its connecting paths need not be monochromatic; only threshold-edge endpoints matter. Fibers have m points, so a lifted threshold component has at most m*C_R points. A simple threshold path gives diameter at most R*(m*C_R-1). This proves finite Borel asymptotic dimension for each m, with no uniform bound asserted as m grows.

Attacks considered: nonfree quotient actions, a mistaken positive instead of negative lamp conjugacy, confusing whole metric balls with same-colored threshold components, using uncolored intermediate points incorrectly, and transferring a uniform dimension bound to the infinite-rank lamp group. No gap found. Uniform cardinality is warranted by the finite-move characterization; no uncontrolled orbit-fiber pullback is used here.

## Regular exhaustion and persistent edge

When m divides M, forbidding residue M-1 can only remove a shift already forbidden at width m. Thus G_m is contained in G_M and new distances do not exceed old finite distances. Factorial widths satisfy CJMST 7.3's exact compatibility inequality. The privately inspected manuscript states separate finite dimensions per stage, not a single common bound.

The forward shift at a point is omitted at every factorial width precisely when its height is -1 in the inverse limit. All other forward shifts are eventually admitted, permanently. On the invariant preimage of profinite nonintegers every vertex visited by a finite generator path is again noninteger. A maximum of the finitely many admission stages therefore places the **whole** path into one slab. Both inclusions of the metric-relation union and the W-orbit relation follow. Restriction to this invariant set does not delete an intermediate path point. A countable invariant split is not being substituted for full-space hyperfiniteness, and no general hyperfinite increasing-union principle is used.

## Full exceptional preimage

Embedded integers form a countable Borel subset of the profinite space, but their preimage need not be countable or negligible. The inverse integer height h is Borel on that preimage. Normalization q=t^(-h)x maps to B=p^(-1)(0). Orbit comparison is exact: t^(-h(y))*g*t^(h(x)) has zero shift character and lies in N, while an N-move of normalized points conjugates back to a W-move. Each finitely generated subgroup of N lies in a finite-rank free abelian subgroup and has polynomial growth. CJMST Corollary 7.5 explicitly permits any Borel action of local polynomial growth, so its increasing finite exhaustion F_j on B applies.

Pulling F_j back alone would have infinite classes, but the note restricts heights to [-j,j] and isolates everything outside. These relations are Borel equivalence relations, their classes have at most (2j+1)*|[q(x)]_(F_j)| points, and they increase. Exact uniqueness of the point with normalized base z and integer height k follows from x=t^k z, independent of lamp stabilizers; a nonzero shift cannot fix a point because of the height equation. Every orbit pair eventually lies in a height band and one F_j-class. Disjointly joining the two invariant finite exhaustions completes the entire relation.

Attacks considered: merely treating the fiber p^(-1)(-1), discarding the exceptional set as small, forgetting heights in the class-size bound, transitivity failures at the band boundary, and a false N-invariance of B. None applies.
