## Independent algebra and slab construction

Let σ_k(f)(i)=f(i-k) on finitely supported integer sequences. Associativity of (f,k)(g,l)=(f+σ_k g,k+l) follows from σ_kσ_l=σ_{k+l}. The inverse is (-σ_{-k}f,-k). Put t=(0,1), a_i=(δ_i,0). Then t^k a_i t^{-k}=a_{i+k}. In particular t^{-r}a_0 t^r=a_{-r}; the author uses the correct sign.

For m≥1 set r(x) to the standard representative of p(x) modulo m and B_m={r=0}. N leaves r fixed and t increases it by one modulo m. The maps Φ_m(x)=(t^{-r(x)}x,r(x)) and Ψ_m(z,r)=t^r z are Borel inverse bijections between X and B_m×{0,…,m−1}. Indeed the residue of t^r z is r, giving Φ_mΨ_m=id, while Ψ_mΦ_m=id follows from the action law. This argument uses the prescribed representative, not uniqueness of group words or a free action. It survives any N stabilizer. Full profinite height additionally forbids stabilizers of nonzero shift height, although that stronger fact is unnecessary for this coordinate bijection.

Define the undirected lamp edges using a_0 and the forward t-edge at x precisely when r(x)<m−1. Its neighbors lie among a_0x,a_0^{-1}x,tx,t^{-1}x; the reverse t-edge is present precisely at r(x)>0. Removing loops gives degree≤4. Its graph metric is a genuine extended metric: distance zero means equality, disconnected points have infinite distance, and each finite radius ball has at most the number of generator words of length at most floor(R), a finite bound uniform in x. It is Borel because a distance≤k pair is witnessed by one of finitely many words of length≤k in partial Borel moves with Borel domains. Countable finite-distance classes follow as a union of finite balls.

Under Φ_m, a lamp edge is (z,r)↔(a_{-r}z,r), an interior shift edge is (z,r)↔(z,r+1), and no wrap edge occurs. Coinciding lamp endpoints yield omitted loops and do not invalidate these identities. The subgroup K_m generated by a_0,…,a_{-(m−1)} is abstractly Z^m because these independent finitely supported lamp vectors have no relation. Its action on B_m may nevertheless be nonfaithful or have nontrivial stabilizers. At m=1, there are no shift edges and K_1=⟨a_0⟩; nothing degenerates.

## Exact published-input check and transfer

CJMST Corollary 5.5, printed p.21, applies to every Borel action of a countable group whose finitely generated subgroups have polynomial volume growth bounded by a fixed degree. It does not assume freeness. K_m=Z^m has degree bound m; thus its action on B_m has some finite Borel dimension D_m. The bound may depend on m.

Lemma 3.1 and Definition 3.2, printed pp.13–15, characterize that dimension by Borel covers with uniformly bounded radius-chain components. The final paragraph of Section 3 explicitly states the Borel counterpart of Lemma 2.2: one may equivalently require uniformly finite components for each finite set of group moves. For completeness, choose the word metric in the displayed finite generating set of K_m. Each metric ball of fixed radius has uniformly finite cardinality, even for a nonfree action, because it is the image of a finite group ball. Uniform diameter of a component therefore implies uniform cardinality. Conversely, an S-chain component with cardinality C and S inside a word ball of radius R has diameter at most R(C−1). Thus the finite-move characterization here does not silently assume free or uniformly bounded orbit size.

Fix a real slab radius R>0 and let S consist of the K_m group elements with word length≤ceil(R). Choose a Borel partition of B_m into D_m+1 colors such that each monochromatic S-chain component has at most C_R points. Covers supplied by Lemma 3.1 can be made a partition by removing earlier sets; this only shrinks their chain components. Color Φ_m(x)=(z,r) by its base color. A pair at slab distance≤R is joined by a path of length≤floor(R). Every vertical step preserves the base; each lamp step applies a_{-r}^{±1}. Its base endpoints therefore differ by an element of S. A same-color radius-chain in the slab projects to a same-color S-chain in B_m, irrespective of the colors of intermediate path vertices. Its total component has at most m C_R points. A simple path in the radius-adjacency graph then gives slab-metric diameter≤R(m C_R−1). This proves finite Borel asymptotic dimension of the slab metric by Lemma 3.1. The case 0<R<1 has only singleton radius-chain components and is also covered.

CJMST Theorem 7.3, printed pp.31–32, requires proper Borel extended metrics on one standard Borel space, finite Borel asymptotic dimension separately for each metric, and a finite bound on next-metric distance for pairs at a fixed previous-metric distance bound. It has no freeness assumption and no uniform bound on all the dimensions D_m. Its proof explicitly indexes dimension by stage, confirming this quantifier. This is the applicable compatible-metric union theorem, not a general union statement for hyperfinite relations.

## Increasing factorial slabs and exact regular exhaustion

If m divides n and r_m(x)<m−1 then r_n(x) cannot equal n−1, since the latter reduces to m−1. All lamp edges are unchanged. Therefore G_m⊆G_n and ρ_n≤ρ_m, including on every pair at finite previous distance. For any real R>0, the supremum appearing in Theorem 7.3 is≤R; the compatibility condition holds.

The embedded integer subset Z of Zhat is countable, hence Borel. Its preimage X_exc is Borel and W-invariant because p(gx)=p(x)+χ(g). The complement X_reg is also invariant. Restricting every slab graph to X_reg preserves all graph paths between its points because each edge is a W move; it also permits the same K_m cover argument on B_m∩X_reg.

A forward shift edge x↔tx fails to appear in every factorial slab exactly when p(x) is congruent to −1 modulo every factorial, i.e. p(x)=−1. The factorial inverse limit separates points, so there is no other profinite value with this failure. In particular every forward shift edge on X_reg occurs at a finite stage. All lamp edges occur already at m=1. Any orbit pair is joined by a finite word in a_0^{±1},t^{±1}. The finitely many involved edges occur jointly at a sufficiently late stage by monotonicity. Conversely each slab path is an orbit path. Thus the union of the slab finite-distance relations on X_reg equals E_W restricted to X_reg. Theorem 7.3 makes that relation hyperfinite. Omitting just p^{-1}({−1}) would fail invariance; excluding the entire integer preimage is correct.

## Full exceptional preimage with an actual finite exhaustion

On X_exc let h(x) be the unique integer equal to p(x), B=p^{-1}({0}), and q(x)=t^{-h(x)}x. Countably many Borel level sets make h and q Borel. The maps x↔(q(x),h(x)) give a Borel bijection X_exc↔B×Z; h(t^k z)=k and q(t^k z)=z prove the inverse statement without any free-lamp assumption.

For y=gx, put k=h(x), l=h(y)=k+χ(g). Then t^{-l}g t^k has zero shift height and hence lies in N. Therefore q(y) belongs to the N-orbit of q(x). Conversely q(y)=f q(x) for f∈N gives y=t^l f t^{-k}x. It follows that E_W on X_exc is precisely the pullback through q of E_N on B.

CJMST Corollary 7.5, printed p.33, applies to any Borel action of a countable group of local polynomial growth. Each finitely generated subgroup of N is free abelian of finite rank, so N meets that assumption. Obtain increasing finite Borel relations F_j exhausting E_N on B. An infinite-to-one pullback of F_j through q need not be finite: for instance a singleton base F_j class pulls back to {t^k z:k∈Z}. The height cutoff is therefore essential.

For integer j≥0 define H_j to be equality outside the band |h|≤j and inside the band the q-pullback of F_j. This is a Borel equivalence relation: its classes in the band are exactly {(t^k z):|k|≤j, z∈C} for an F_j class C, with all remaining points singleton classes. Their cardinalities equal (2j+1)|C| and are finite. Since bands and F_j increase, H_j⊆H_{j+1}. Any E_W pair has finite heights and an F_j stage joining its two normalized points, so it enters H_j at some sufficiently large j. Thus union H_j=E_W restricted to X_exc. Negative heights are included by the absolute value; no exceptional fiber is discarded.

Choose increasing finite exhaustions on the two invariant Borel pieces and unite them stagewise. A class lies wholly in one piece, so the result is finite and increasing, with union E_W on X. This completes the audited Borel theorem.
