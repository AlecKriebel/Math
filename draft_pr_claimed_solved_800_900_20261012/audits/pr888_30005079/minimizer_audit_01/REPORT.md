## Normalization and valuation-domain attack

For a degree-m section in first-factor E-order k and second-factor toric character (i,j),

raw v-weight = ak+b(j+m),
A_X(v)=a+b,
canonical weight = ak+b(j+m)-m(a+b)=a(k-m)+bj.

The coordinate D has inward primitive normal (0,1), hence ord_D(z^i w^j)=j; its anticanonical section shift is +m. The local monomial valuation has positive weights on the transverse divisors E×S and V×D, center E×D and finite discrepancy a+b. It is a valuation on K(X), rather than just a weight on K(Z×S).

With s=k/m-1, the first-factor probability density is rho(s)/5, with rho=(2+s)^2 on [-1,0] and (2-s)^2 on [0,2]. The second marginal is (2+y)/4 on [-1,1]. Therefore on the displayed candidate,

H(v)=log[(1/5) integral exp(-as)rho(s)ds]+log[(1/4) integral exp(-by)(2+y)dy].

At a=b=0 this is0. The directional derivatives there are -1/12 and -1/6, consistent with the positive optimal scales. This equation is an independent sign/normalization check. It proves only optimization in the shown family unless the global theorem is applied; the candidate does apply that theorem.

The toric polygon's x-slice has midpoint y/2, so the y-moment equation also kills its x moment. Full-dimensional covariance makes the critical point unique in the entire toric Lie algebra. This determines the parameter needed by the existence theorem, but remains logically separate from the full valuation-domain conclusion.

## Product weak-soliton verification

Let omega_i=ddc phi_i, and use MA_n(phi)=omega^n/n!. In the Han–Li convention the factor equations are

exp(-theta_Z)MA_3(phi_Z)=exp(-phi_Z),
exp(-theta_S)MA_2(phi_S)=exp(-phi_S).

For phi=phi_Z+phi_S and theta=theta_Z+theta_S on the product,

MA_5(phi)=MA_3(phi_Z) tensor MA_2(phi_S),
exp(-theta)MA_5(phi)=exp(-phi_Z-phi_S).

Only the mixed top-degree term survives the binomial expansion, and the factorials cancel its coefficient exactly. The product anticanonical bundle identifies the right side with its canonical volume. The metric potentials lift boundedly to a resolution. Bedford–Taylor/nonpluripolar Monge–Ampere measures do not charge the analytic singular set, nor does the klt canonical measure, so the equality on the regular locus extends globally. Product invariance implies invariance under the induced smaller torus.

Thus the central pair admits the soliton for precisely the candidate's canonical parameter. BLXZ makes it weighted K-polystable, hence semistable. Han–Li Theorem5.3 and Theorem1.5 then give global minimization, including competitors mixing factors. BLXZ Theorem1.2 gives absolute uniqueness and canonical first-step identification. This avoids using an unproved product formula for minimizers or a circular assumption of global optimality.

## Adversarial boundaries and bounded clarifications

- A nonzero vector field on an unchanged variety would not prove the target. Here nontriviality is conditional on the independently checked singular Z×S versus smooth X.
- Rational rank2 requires irrational a/b. An irrational scalar times a divisorial valuation would not suffice; this report does not replace that separate proof by finite numerical evidence.
- Specialness, klt anticanonical central ring, and its actual induced vector are indispensable. Finite generation alone for an unrelated filtration would not permit the criterion. The candidate explicitly states these obligations.
- The correct convention is Ric(omega)-omega=-ddc theta_phi(xi) for Han–Li's xi and exponential weight exp(-theta). Wang–Zhu's analytic field has the opposite sign. The actual candidate displays only the correct Ricci product identity, so no wrong-sign equation was found. Adding this explicit convention is an optional clarification, not a mathematical repair to v.
- Finite generation should mean finitely many weighted section generators/geometric real degeneration, not that every positive element of a dense ordered rank2 group is generated as a semigroup. Miao–Wang Definition2.1 uses finite generation of the associated graded algebra; Han–Li's geometric formulation accommodates finite real weights. Lifting finitely many homogeneous graded generators terminates in each degree because there are finitely many jumps. No dense-semigroup finiteness assertion is needed.
