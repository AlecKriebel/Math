## 1. Exact comparison claim

The official OWR 17/2022 body, printed pp.888–889 [S01](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46955), identifies the first step as the unique real degeneration minimizing the global H-functional. Its center is Proj(gr_v R). The request combines:

1. an explicitly described klt Fano X;
2. its canonical **first** central variety Z, with X not isomorphic to Z;
3. an explicit globally minimizing real valuation v;
4. a proof that v is not c ord_E for any positive c and prime divisor E.

No dimension cap or exclusion of products occurs in the passage. A nonzero soliton, moduli, a different final KRF limit without identifying the first center, or an irrational scalar multiplying a divisor does not settle this conjunction.

Its group aZ+bZ has rational rank two but is an ordered subgroup of R and has ordered rank one. “Rank two valuation” must therefore be qualified as **rational rank two**.

## 4. Toric, complexity-one and irregular examples

The toric route fails the nontrivial-center condition directly: BLXZ Theorem5.11 gives unchanged first center. Hisamoto [S09](https://www.math.nagoya-u.ac.jp/~hisamoto/papers/Destabilizer3.pdf), introduction p.3, also distinguishes the toric entropy optimizer's product family from the Ricci–Calabi optimizer, whose behavior concerns another functional. Hisamoto's allowance of irrational actions supplies no named example satisfying all four tests.

Ilten–Süß [S11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.04442v3), inspected v3 (18 May2022, matching their2017 journal article), Theorem6.2 p.19 and Example5.3, constructs solitons on the original complexity-one threefolds. Cable–Süß [S12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02920v2), v2 (10 March2018), Theorem1.8 p.3, Remark4.3 and Table3 p.15, extends such original-variety existence and gives numerical vector estimates. Those are useful soliton factors; they do not by themselves change the canonical first variety. Decimal approximations also cannot prove rational independence.

Delcroix's author-hosted EAG lecture [S13](https://delcroix.perso.math.cnrs.fr/lecture2-EAG-20220106.pdf), numbered slide26/34 (PDFp.29), lists existence of a Fano manifold with an irrational soliton vector as a question. Its URL identifies January2022 and the official KIAS schedule confirms the lecture date of6January2022; the downloaded body's creation date is not independently established. This is historical context only; it does not establish a2026 open status or define “irrational” precisely enough to replace a valuation calculation.

The classical irregular cone mechanism is genuinely different. Gauntlett–Martelli–Sparks–Waldram [S16](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0403002), hep-th/0403002v3, §4 printed p.13 (PDFp.14), describes rank-two irregular Sasaki–Einstein Reeb orbits and the absence of an orbifold quotient for those orbits. Li–Xu [S15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05561v2), arXiv:1707.05561v2 (1 March2019), Theorems1.1,1.3 and Lemma2.16, studies normalized volume on a **local affine klt singularity**. Its real-weight rank statement is relevant background, but neither theorem is global H-minimization on the projective Fano in this task.

A cone's grading contributes an additional direction. A higher-rank cone valuation need not restrict to a higher-rank projective function-field valuation; the radial grading can disappear on degree-zero quotients. Moreover, the Han–Li cone correspondence uses a weighted normalized-volume setup (§7 of [S02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01010v1)), not an identification with every ordinary normalized-volume minimizer. An argument transferring the target to an unsupported equality between these optimization problems is blocked.

Delcroix [S17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07864v1), arXiv:2411.07864v1 (12 November2024), Theorem1.2 p.4, gives nonisomorphic **weighted** optimal centers for quadric examples that are already Kähler–Einstein for the ordinary problem. Its introduction distinguishes the generalized weight problem. Changing g does not demonstrate a nontrivial canonical first center for ordinary global H; no irrational-ratio assertion was located.

## 5. The product-theorem attack, stated precisely

Here is the strongest old-input recipe; it is an inference, not a located named theorem or example.

Assume a smooth Fano A has an explicit special real degeneration to (A0,ξ), with a weak KRS, and a toric Fano B has a KRS (B,η). If the tensor/Segre filtration on R(A×B) is a special real filtration whose canonical vector is (ξ,η), then the product soliton and Han–Li criterion make it a global H-minimizer. If the two weight directions generate a Q-space of dimension at least two, its valuation is nondivisorial. If A0 is singular while A is smooth, A0×B is visibly not isomorphic to A×B.

Every italicized verification matters in applying the recipe to a named pair: existence of the factors is old; compatibility of the actual graded ring and canonical shifts, global semistability, and rank are separate checks. Distinct factors or distinct irrational numbers do not imply an irrational ratio. If their weights are equal, or rationally proportional, the product valuation can remain divisorial.

Taking Li–Li's case(1) and an arbitrary toric soliton, for example, does not automatically supply the required rank. Taking a toric factor with an independently verified non-rational-ray soliton direction would suffice, but this audit did not locate such an earlier primary verified factor. Taking two copies of the same example explicitly gives no new rank. Products of purely toric varieties leave the first center unchanged.

The candidate supplies one concrete instance of the recipe with a proved a/b irrational. Thus earlier general theorem coverage is strong and should be prominently credited, but the old recipe alone is not a prior full resolution.
