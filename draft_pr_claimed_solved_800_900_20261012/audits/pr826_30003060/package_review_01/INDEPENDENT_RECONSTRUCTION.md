The exact target says: for a quadratic algebra A, is Koszulness equivalent to vanishing of higher Koszul homology in positive homological degrees? The full cached record adds no field restriction; its prior_research object is empty. Independently derive the free-algebra case as follows. A = T_k(V), R = 0, hence W_0 = k, W_1 = V, W_i = 0 for i >= 2. The left free resolution 0 -> A tensor V -> A -> k -> 0, with prefix/final-letter multiplication, is exact and generated linearly (every nonempty word has exactly one final letter). Thus A is Koszul. For coefficient A the distinct differential is b(a tensor v) = av - va. There are no ordinary degree-one boundaries. BLS final (28) for p=q=1 gives left cap e_A(a tensor v) = va, with positive sign. On b-cycles, sum va = sum av; after quotienting by commutators, the higher map is the natural map ker(1-rho) -> coker(1-rho) in each positive word weight.

For each distinct cyclic orbit O of size d, invariants are spanned by s_O = sum of its distinct words and coinvariants by [w]. The coefficient-sum functional proves [w] nonzero without division. The induced map is s_O -> d[w]. Therefore, in char p, its kernel and cokernel each have one dimension iff p divides d. In char zero neither survives at positive weight. There is no higher incoming boundary in degree one, since ordinary HK_2 = 0. At weight zero only k in homological degree zero occurs. Empty alphabet yields A=k and no positive-weight orbits; one-letter alphabet gives d=1 always, also when the word is x^p. Arbitrary fields of a given characteristic and field extensions have the same answer: only the integer scalar d is tested.

For r=2 and prime p, w=x^(p-1)y has p distinct rotations; unique y positions ensure d=p. Splitting each rotation into prefix and last letter gives nonzero sum z_p, b z_p=0 by telescoping and delta[z_p]=p[w]=0. It is nonzero in ordinary and higher degree one. For p=2 this is x tensor y + y tensor x. Repetition length n/d does not replace d in this calculation.
