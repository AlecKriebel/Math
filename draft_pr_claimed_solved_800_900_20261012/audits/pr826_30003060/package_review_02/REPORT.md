For A = T(V) with R = 0, W0 = k, W1 = V and Wj = 0 for j ≥ 2. Splitting off the final letter identifies the augmentation ideal with A ⊗ V, yielding an exact linear free left resolution of k. Independently, for each fixed word of length n, the bimodule small complex is the incidence sequence of n adjacent edges between n + 1 cuts of that word. Its edge map is injective over every field and its cokernel is one copy of the word. This also establishes the bimodule definition of Koszulness used in BLS. The zero relation space is explicitly permitted by BLS §2. These arguments do not need a positive relation or finite-dimensional algebra.

With coefficients A, the ordinary differential is b(a ⊗ v) = av − va. The displayed multiplication map av in the left resolution is distinct from this commutator map. The fundamental left cap in degree one is va, with positive sign. On a cycle sum the two summed concatenations coincide. These are ordinary associative Hochschild signs with an internal weight grading, without extra super signs. The paper explicitly defines total weight, avoiding the alternative coefficient-weight convention in BLS.

At total weight n > 0, concatenation turns b into 1 − ρ, where ρ rotates the final letter to the front. Ordinary H1 is its kernel and ordinary H0 its cokernel. Because W2 = 0, no ordinary degree-one boundary or higher incoming degree-one boundary is present. Higher cap is the natural map from rotation invariants to coinvariants.

On an orbit of d **distinct** rotations, invariants are spanned by the orbit sum sO and coinvariants by one word class [w]. The coefficient-sum functional proves [w] is nonzero over every field, including modular characteristic. The map sends sO to d[w]. It therefore has one-dimensional kernel and cokernel exactly when the prime characteristic p divides d. No averaging, semisimplicity, division by d, or norm/inclusion identification is used. This proves the full higher H0 and H1 formula; higher degrees ≥ 2 vanish because their ordinary homology groups vanish.

The word x^(p−1)y has exactly p distinct rotations because its unique y changes position. Splitting all these words into prefix and final letter gives a nonzero sum of distinct basis chains, an integral telescoping commutator cycle, and higher image p[w] = 0. No incoming degree-one boundary can kill it. At p = 2 this is x ⊗ y + y ⊗ x. The same orbit contributes a positive-weight higher H0 class, so adding the degree-zero conjunct does not rescue the unrestricted forward implication.

Boundary cases were checked by deduction: at total weight zero only higher H0 = k occurs; an empty alphabet has no positive-weight words; one generator has only singleton orbits, so its positive-weight higher groups vanish in every characteristic. The word x^p has d = 1 and does not give a witness. Repeated words of larger length use their least period d, not their length. Characteristic zero makes every positive d invertible. Every field extension preserves the integer scalar and orbit bases, so the result holds over arbitrary fields of characteristic p, not only prime fields.
