The target is the unrestricted implication: for a nonzero diffuse locally finite random measure on a locally compact second countable Abelian group, with an observed jointly measurable acted-on pair (X,xi), does distributional invariance under every measurable self-preserving allocation imply mass-stationarity, without adding independent stationary background? A counterexample on R² with constant X suffices to negate this universal question. Diffuse means atomless, not absolutely continuous. Neither freeness, full support, absence of invariant directions, one dimension, nor positive density is stipulated.

The allocation convention is pointwise global covariance: tau(theta_t omega,s−t)=tau(omega,s)−t for every state and every s,t. Equivalently tau(omega,s)=s+pi(theta_s omega), with measurable pi. The source permits arbitrary noninjective allocations; no bijection is imposed. xi preservation means pushforward equality, distinct from the law-invariance test theta_pi(omega) omega =d omega. Preservation only Q-a.e. is also a legitimate stronger stress test, while retaining pointwise covariance. The observed sigma-field may not be enlarged by a hidden seed.

Mass-stationarity is the full joint identity (theta_V omega,U+V)=d(omega,U), for every relatively compact Borel C with positive Haar measure and Haar-null boundary, U uniform on C independently of omega, V conditionally distributed according to xi restricted and normalized on C−U. We will require a window of positive finite mass. Failure of the first marginal proves failure of the full identity; first-marginal invariance alone would not establish mass-stationarity.
