# A periodic diffuse counterexample to allocation-only mass-stationarity

Problem: 9900008 / AMR-098-0008. Date: 2026-10-06.

## 1. Result and conventions

The background-free implication in the stated problem is false. There is a locally finite, nonzero, diffuse random measure on the locally compact second countable Abelian group R² whose law is invariant under every measurable equivariant preserving allocation, but is not mass-stationary. A constant random element X suffices.

This is a counterexample under the pointwise-covariant allocation convention actually used in Thorisson's Problem 4.1 and Last–Thorisson's Example 3.4 and Problem 7.5. It does not impose, or assume, injectivity of allocations. It does not cover a modified problem requiring a free action or forbidding invariant directions, or one allowing added independent stationary randomness. No novelty or priority claim is made.

Write θ_t μ(B)=μ(B+t). An allocation generated by a measurable displacement π has

    τ_ω(s) = s + π(θ_s ω).

It satisfies τ_(θ_t ω)(s−t)=τ_ω(s)−t for all ω,s,t. Conversely, every pointwise-covariant allocation has this representation, with π(ω)=τ_ω(0). Preserving means (τ_ω)_*ξ_ω=ξ_ω. The tested distributional invariance concerns θ_(τ_ω(0))ω, not the pushforward measure alone.

For a probability law Q and locally finite ξ with the origin in its support, mass-stationarity requires the following joint identity. For every relatively compact Borel C with positive Haar measure and Haar-null boundary, independently draw U uniformly in C and then draw V with conditional law ξ(·∩(C−U))/ξ(C−U). One requires

    (θ_V ξ, U+V)  =_d  (ξ,U).

We will violate even its first marginal for C=[0,1)². Thus no marginal-only replacement of the definition is used.

## 2. The measure, state space, and measurable action

Let λ₁ be one-dimensional Lebesgue measure and define

    ν = Σ_(n∈Z) δ_n + 2 Σ_(n∈Z) δ_(n+1/2),
    μ = λ₁ ⊗ ν.

The first coordinate is horizontal. For u∈T=R/Z let μ_u=θ_(0,u)μ. Its horizontal lines are at heights n−u, with density 1, and n+1/2−u, with density 2. Set

    Ω=T,  F=B(T),  θ_(a,b)u=u+b mod 1,
    ξ(u)=μ_u,  Q=δ_0.

Take X constant in a one-element measurable space with trivial action. Thus under Q the random measure is the deterministic measure μ.

All the hypotheses here are genuine measure-theoretic ones:

* μ_u is a Borel locally finite Radon measure. A compact rectangle meets finitely many supporting lines, each in a bounded segment.
* μ_u is diffuse: each singleton has λ₁-mass zero, although an entire horizontal segment may have positive mass. Diffuse does not mean absolutely continuous with respect to two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
* μ is nonzero and 0 belongs to its support. It has infinite total mass, which is allowed; every compact set has finite mass.
* The action (t,u)↦θ_tu is jointly Borel, and ξ is flow-adapted: ξ(θ_tu)=θ_tξ(u).
* The map u↦μ_u is continuous for the vague topology and injective. Indeed, the positions of density-1 lines distinguish the phase modulo 1. Since T is compact and the space of Radon measures is Hausdorff, this is a homeomorphism onto its image. In particular, σ(ξ)=B(T). There is no hidden larger sigma-algebra supplying randomness.
* The group R² is Abelian and amenable. No additional amenability assumption is needed by the proof. The subgroup R×Z acts trivially on every state; freeness is deliberately absent.

The support under Q is R×(Z/2), which is not all of R². Neither full support nor an absolutely continuous density is assumed in the source problem.

## 3. Every preserving allocation leaves the rooted state unchanged

Fix an arbitrary measurable equivariant allocation τ on this state space. Let π(u)=τ_u(0) and write

    π(0)=(a₀,b₀),     π(1/2)=(a₁,b₁).

The representation in Section 1 gives, for every x∈R and n∈Z,

    τ_0(x,n)       = (x+a₀, n+b₀),
    τ_0(x,n+1/2)   = (x+a₁, n+1/2+b₁).

This is forced by covariance and the stabilizers, not by an assumed bijectivity or regularity of τ. In particular, each complete source line is translated horizontally and sent to one complete target line. The lines of a given source family have distinct images: consecutive input heights give consecutive output heights. Two different source families can initially be allowed to collide.

Horizontal translations preserve λ₁. Consequently, on the measure μ,

    (τ_0)_*μ
      = λ₁ ⊗ [Σ_n δ_(n+b₀) + 2 Σ_n δ_(n+1/2+b₁)].                 (1)

Preservation at the single Q-supported state 0 already forces equality of the bracketed one-dimensional measures with ν. A useful way to read that equality is to restrict to one half-open interval of length 1 and identify heights modulo Z. This yields the finite measure identity on T

    δ_[b₀] + 2 δ_[1/2+b₁] = δ_0 + 2 δ_[1/2].                  (2)

This does not push the entire infinite measure directly to the quotient, which would give infinite atoms. It compares its finite per-period measure. Equivalently, for each residue r the exact mass equation is

    1{[b₀]=r} + 2·1{[1/2+b₁]=r}
      = 1{r=0} + 2·1{r=1/2}.                                  (3)

Both source atoms in (2) must lie in {0,1/2}, since otherwise the left side has mass outside the right-hand support. If they coincide, their mass is 3, impossible at either target. If they swap targets, the mass at 0 is 2 instead of 1. Therefore the only solution is

    [b₀]=0,       [1/2+b₁]=1/2,

so b₀,b₁ are integers. The mass-2 target cannot be supplied by two distinct mass-1 source families: there is only one such family modulo the forced unit-period covariance, and each member of it has exactly one preimage in that family. This explicitly handles the possible noninjectivity instead of assuming it away.

In particular, τ_0(0)=π(0)=(a₀,b₀) belongs to the stabilizer R×Z. Hence

    θ_(τ_0(0))μ = μ,
    Q ◦ (ω↦θ_(τ_ω(0))ω)⁻¹ = Q.

Because τ was arbitrary, the required allocation-invariance holds for every measurable equivariant preserving allocation. It holds whether preservation is demanded everywhere on Ω or only Q-almost surely; only preservation at u=0 was used. Since σ(ξ)=F, the result also holds for the explicitly σ(ξ)⊗B(R²)-measurable class in the older formulation.

The class is not empty: the identity allocation is preserving, as are every fixed horizontal translation and every fixed integer vertical translation.

### Why the origin is not an exceptional-point loophole

Although the origin is a μ-null singleton, its image is not free to be independently reassigned. Pointwise covariance makes τ_0(x,0)=(x,0)+π(0) for every horizontal x. Changing π(0) changes the images of the entire density-1 line and, by vertical period 1, all density-1 lines. Each unit segment on these lines has positive μ-mass. Thus (1) constrains the image of the root itself. We do not claim a result for a different definition that identifies allocation maps only modulo spatial null sets without specifying a covariant representative at the origin.

## 4. Direct failure of the defining mass-stationarity identity

Choose C=[0,1)² and draw U=(U₁,U₂) uniformly in C. Haar measure of C is 1 and its boundary has Haar measure zero. For every u₂∈(0,1) apart from the irrelevant boundary value 1/2, the vertical interval [−u₂,1−u₂) contains exactly:

* the density-1 line at height 0;
* one density-2 line, at height 1/2 if u₂<1/2 and at height −1/2 if u₂>1/2.

The horizontal interval has λ₁-length 1. Therefore μ(C−U)=3 almost surely. If V is sampled from the required normalized restriction of μ to C−U, then

    P(V₂=0 | U)=1/3,
    P(V₂∈{−1/2,1/2} | U)=2/3.                                 (4)

Let

    H(η)=η([0,1)×{0}).

This is measurable for the evaluation sigma-algebra on random measures. Under the original law, H(ξ)=H(μ)=1 with probability 1. Under the mass-chosen shift, horizontal displacement is immaterial, and

    H(θ_V μ)=1 with probability 1/3,
    H(θ_V μ)=2 with probability 2/3.

Thus θ_V ξ does not have the law of ξ. The joint identity defining mass-stationarity is consequently false. This is a direct positive-probability witness, with discrepancy 2/3 for the event {H=2}.

## 5. Optional Palm consistency check

This check is not needed to establish the counterexample. Let P be uniform phase measure on T, with the same action and random measure. P is stationary; it is ergodic for this R² action because vertical translations act transitively on T. The intensity is 3. For B=[0,1)² and any nonnegative measurable f on T, Campbell's formula gives

    ∫_T ∫_B f(θ_tu) μ_u(dt) du = f(0) + 2 f(1/2).

Indeed, each phase has one line of each family in B, and shifting the origin to a density-1 line produces phase 0, while shifting to a density-2 line produces phase 1/2. Hence the normalized Palm law of this stationary model is

    P_ξ^0 = (1/3)δ_0 + (2/3)δ_(1/2),

in agreement with the mass-sampling computation. Our Q=δ_0 is the incorrectly biased rooted law. We do not infer non-mass-stationarity merely from its differing from one Palm law; Section 4 proves the failure independently.

## 6. Exact conclusion and limits

The general implication for diffuse random measures is refuted already on R². The example retains a continuous translation stabilizer, so it does not refute results under the exclusion of invariant directions or free-action hypotheses. It is singular relative to planar Lebesgue measure and therefore does not touch the established positive-density theorem. It is not a one-dimensional counterexample. Adding independent stationary spatial information can destroy the stabilizer obstruction; that is the additional hypothesis deliberately excluded by the question.

The neighboring Markovian-kernel problem is outside this result. Fractional transport can split a line family's mass, so the deterministic two-atom calculation is not a proof about arbitrary Markovian kernels.
