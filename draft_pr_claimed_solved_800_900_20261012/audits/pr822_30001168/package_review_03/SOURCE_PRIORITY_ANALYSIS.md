The mechanism of long Pinocchio cylinders and its earlier ancestry is
already explicit in A--J. Their first-positive-eigenvalue supremum and
fixed-index convergence results do not by themselves establish a heat
trace comparison. A trace needs a growing number of longitudinal modes,
which the present paper obtains by a direct k-dimensional trial-space
argument for every finite k. Nor does A--J's bare cap inequality
F_L(r)<=1/r prove uniformly bounded cap volume: integrating its nth power
against r^(n-1)dr gives a logarithmically divergent majorant at r=0.
Choosing a fixed cap at the rescaled radius removes that missing uniform
bound. On the conformally flat sphere this can be made an exact round
product cylinder. After this choice, ordinary Dirichlet min--max, linear
volume growth, and a strict elementary round bound assemble a qualitative
counterexample. The note's explicit finite smoothing, L=10000, time and
rational gap quantify this classical application. Its attribution does
not claim that A--J states Morpurgo's exact refutation or that the
spectral/cylindrical mechanism is new.

## Functional, normalization, and dimension traps

The retained Morpurgo 2002 material is **tool-captured selected author-text
sections**, not a downloaded complete PDF or authenticated final Duke
version. Its SHA-256 is
`875f53e5141c560e5cf28e7c929b015883ec4206e7eb6d4bd3f42782c5a9cecb`.
I inspected the saved Proposition 2.3, transformation (16), Lemma 2.4,
Theorem 4.1/Corollary 4.2 and Theorem 5.10/Corollary 5.12 passages. The
nonregularized comparison is for a continuous convex Psi with a
small-argument integrability requirement, through

    L_Psi(u) = integral_0^infinity exp(-v/u) Psi(v) dv/v.

A fixed heat time would select a delta at v=t, not an allowed continuous
convex Psi. Equivalently exp(-t/u) has second derivative
t exp(-t/u)(t-2u)/u^4, which changes sign. The stated inverse-moment,
transformed-trace, or regularized zeta inequalities therefore do not
directly compare individual heat times or their scaled maxima. Recovering
that claim from them would require an additional order-preserving
inversion/localization argument; it is not supplied. I did not infer it.
The regularized zeta restrictions on dimension, d, and s likewise do not
disappear by calling the operator conformal. The source's final version
and several older full texts remain unavailable in the retained evidence.

Branson's 2007 pp.26-27 discussion explicitly assumes even dimension and
describes an inconclusive zeta/determinant route. Its displayed sign
description differs from the retained 2002 author excerpt. I preserved
that unresolved version/sign issue; neither statement is used to refute
or prove the n=3 heat maximum. Møller--Ørsted v1 concerns stationary-point
Hessians and local determinant/conformal-functional behavior, not the
required global scaled trace maximum. A local extremum of another
functional would not exclude the present large conformal deformation.

The Sire--Xu v3 main theorem fixes the mean of the conformal metric factor
(integral W), rather than the target integral W^(n/2). Its fixed-index
upper estimate and Pérez-Ayala's corresponding non-volume extremal theory
do not transfer to the target just by rescaling the weight. The recent
Humbert--Petrides--Premoselli v2 framework optimizes negative or fixed
positive eigenvalues of GJMS operators, including singular factors; it
does not state a simultaneous all-index heat comparison on the smooth
normalized sphere. The actual paper avoids these transfers.

Zhao's retained arXiv:2012.08757v2 PDF has SHA-256
`23c8bda4acd068d111d43bc1398eaf121f3247c6117c7d1bb2724ca31ac7d8e8`.
Its Introduction explicitly distinguishes h^-1 Delta from the geometric

perturbation heat estimates do not supply the Yamabe potential, sharp

The more recent Morpurgo/Qin and Fontana/Morpurgo/Qin retained main-result

## Counting and weak-ideal traps

The actual Frank 2014 and Hundertmark--Kunstmann--Ried--Vugalter 2022
texts give counting/weak-ideal or diagonal heat estimates. Their upper
constants do not themselves yield a lower normalized heat-trace example
or identify the sharp round time maximum. Corso--Ried 2025 solves a
method-specific variational problem for improved upper CLR/LT bounds;
that is not an equality of the distinct optimal heat and counting
functionals.

In Frank's 2020 review, Section 4.6 (Lemma 15 and the following
stereographic calculation), the Glaser--Grosse--Martin count examples
correspond to constant sphere potentials. Under the target weight's
volume normalization a constant positive weight reduces to W=1; constant
metric scaling also leaves the maximum of t^(n/2)Tr(exp(-tY)) invariant
after changing time. The stated counterexamples to a different
semiclassical/counting conjecture occur for dimension d>=7 and do not
provide a nonround S3 example. The original GGM full paper was not
available in the retained corpus. I did not promote the later primary
reconstruction into a claim of direct original-paper inspection.
