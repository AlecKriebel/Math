## Row 44 — K3 moduli Hodge theorem

**Lay claim (two sentences):** For a broad family of spaces parametrizing sheaves or stable objects on K3 surfaces, including their Hilbert schemes and arbitrary finite products built from different K3 surfaces, every rational cohomology class of the kind predicted by the Hodge conjecture comes from an algebraic cycle. This would settle the rational Hodge conjecture in every codimension throughout that natural family.

**Exact final claim:** Theorem 1 applies to complex projective K3 surfaces with Brauer classes and to actual smooth projective Gieseker-stable twisted-sheaf moduli, or actual smooth projective moduli of stable objects for generic Bridgeland stability, precisely within Bülles Theorem 0.1. It covers finite mixed products with different or repeated K3 bases and includes all finite products of Hilbert schemes S_i^[n_i], n_i >= 0. Smoothness and projectivity are hypotheses on the actual moduli space; genericity does not remove them. Empty and zero-dimensional factors are explicitly handled.

**Importance if genuinely new:** This is a substantial broad family of a central algebraic-cycle conjecture, spanning arbitrarily large dimensions and codimensions in hyperkähler geometry. The result includes the universal mixed-K3 Hodge theorem because S^[1] = S. It merits 8 under the supplied rubric: a major result in algebraic geometry, but it does not solve rational Hodge for arbitrary smooth projective varieties. It should not receive 10 merely because its name contains a Millennium conjecture.

**What is actually new in this paper:** Its own abstract calls it an “assembled corollary.” Bülles already supplies the rational Chow-motive splitting into finite sums of Tate twists of powers of each base K3; Arapura had earlier conditional transfers for Hilbert schemes and sheaf moduli. Tensoring those split maps transfers the already supplied mixed-K3 Hodge theorem to the moduli product. The different-base quantifier is useful and correctly distinguishes the conclusion from separate self-power results, but its difficult mathematical content comes entirely from the upstream mixed-K3 theorem. Section 3 explicitly says that, with Bülles' established reduction, the universal mixed-moduli and mixed-K3 assertions are equivalent. The paper claims no new motivic reduction, base proof, firstness, or substantial independent novelty.

The concrete contribution is a carefully delimited corollary and an explicit pull-push proof with codimensions, non-fine/twisted normalization, and degenerate cases checked. That is **4/10, a technical consequence note**, rather than a newly solved conjecture credited independently to this paper.

**Limits:** No arbitrary K3^[n] deformation theorem, singular or semistable moduli result, general abelian-surface moduli theorem, integral/generalized Hodge conclusion, or finite-dimensional-motive theorem. It depends on the unrefereed upstream mixed-K3 geometric proof. The source explicitly says the Lean Clifford module and small finite checks do not formalize the Hodge theorem; this note does not independently decide their correctness.

## Row 52 — rational-point undecidability and height

**Lay claim (two sentences):** No algorithm can always decide whether an arbitrary smooth projective geometrically integral variety over the rational numbers has a rational point. Consequently, no computable rule based on the size of its defining equations can guarantee how far one must search to find a point whenever one exists.

**Exact final claim:** Inputs are finite homogeneous integer equation presentations of X_e in projective N-space, promised smooth and geometrically integral over Q. The algorithm need only terminate on valid inputs. Ambient dimension, dimension, degrees, equation count, and coefficient sizes all vary. Theorem 3 gives an effective finite nonadaptive disjunctive oracle equivalence with H10(Q); its undecidability corollary imports the repaired OpenAI Family004 input. The height corollary rules out a total computable B(s(e)) containing at least one rational point, if any exists, for primitive integral coordinates with height max |z_i| and a specified finite equation encoding. The proof also excludes an appropriate computable bound depending on the presentation itself.

**Importance if genuinely new:** This establishes a fundamental impossibility for the general rational-point decision problem and for universal effective search bounds. Its decision strength is that of rational Hilbert's tenth problem, a longstanding central problem in number theory and logic. **9/10** is appropriate under the supplied rubric; the variable dimension and other unrestricted parameters remain essential, and this is not a fixed-dimensional arithmetic breakthrough.

**What is actually new in this paper:** Poonen's published effective transfer already makes rational-point existence on smooth projective geometrically integral varieties equivalent to existence on arbitrary varieties, and credits a prior Q route to R. Robinson. The finite-query/promise discipline is a useful explicit reconstruction of that old mechanism. The least-height corollary is finite enumeration after a hypothetical computable bound, and the general least-height/H10 connection is explicitly in older literature. Hence neither conclusion constitutes an independent new route to H10(Q), a new geometric reduction, or a substantial new height method.

The package does more than print the corollary: it documents two technical substitutions in the arithmetic inputs. One replaces unrestricted new 2-adic modularity in the non-CM five-point argument with Pan's published theorem at 5 plus prime-independent traces; the other reconciles direct/inverse Euler conventions through an integral universal group-ring correction with controlled central valuations. The final supplement correctly labels the coefficient-prime derivation conditional on retained geometry, confines it to the fixed discriminant-210 five-point configuration, and disclaims proving all of H10(Q). These additions justify **5/10, a credible narrow consequence-and-correction contribution**, if correct. Without credit for those repairs the main corollary alone is a **4/10** technical note. Their correctness/independent novelty was not re-established by this impact reading, so 5 is credit for the actual documented contribution, not proof of a new central arithmetic theorem.

**Limits:** No curves, surfaces, fixed dimension, Fano, general-type, finite-point promise, many-one completeness, dimension preservation, quantitative hardness, or separate Turing-degree theorem. The height result bounds the search for one point, not every point. The decisive unconditional input is still the upstream arithmetic proof, with local corrections and retained assumptions; package audits and finite checks are not complete formal verification or human refereeing.
