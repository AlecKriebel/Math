## Row 35 — Binary consequences of relation-liveness automata lower bounds

**Exact actual theorem.** Encode each relation on h points by its h² adjacency bits, and let K_h be exactly the concatenations whose relation product is nonempty; reject every incomplete binary block. The paper gives an endmarked one-way NFA with N_h=(3h³−h)/2+2 states and an ordinary epsilon-free one-way NFA with N_h−1 states. Every ordinary one-way NFA for this precise strict-block language requires at least h³ states. An s-state binary two-way target pulls back to an sh²-state relation-alphabet target. Hence every binary 2DFA for K_h and every read-only binary 2NFA for its full complement in {0,1}* requires 2^Ω(h)=2^Ω(N_h^(1/3)) states. The source counts, full-complement conventions, empty inputs, partial transitions, and finite-run acceptance are explicit.

**Lay brief.** Some tiny machines can guess their way through binary strings, but any deterministic machine that scans back and forth needs vastly more memory states. Even a guessing machine needs vastly more states to recognize all strings the original machine rejects.

**Importance.** As theorem content this is very important: a fixed alphabet still exhibits a superpolynomial obstruction to deterministic simulation, and to same-model complementation. It bears directly on the longstanding Sakoda–Sipser program and read-only two-way automata questions. It is stronger in asymptotic kind than the quadratic lower-bound literature, although its stated binary exponent is a cube root, not linear in source states.

**New versus inherited.** Both exponential engines are the OpenAI family-129 inputs. Binary adjacency coding is already credited to Kapoutsis. This paper supplies the complete compiler/pullback with exact conventions and counts, and the h³ fooling-set observation establishing the order-optimal source cost for this chosen strict-block encoding. These are useful, checkable refinements, but do not constitute a new exponential lower-bound method. Consequently B=5 fits a credible narrow technical contribution attached to a major theorem. A reader seeking the main automata breakthrough would cite the upstream theorem alongside this explicit binary account.

**Boundaries.** No 2^Ω(n) binary lower bound, no unary conclusion, no L≠NL conclusion, and no short uniform transition-table claim. The complement is over every binary word, including malformed lengths. The actual paper explicitly says the transfer is inherited and not an independent resolution of the base conjecture.

**Primary comparison refreshed.** [Adeogun–Kapoutsis, arXiv:2602.24279v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24279v2) states the Ω(h²) unrestricted 2DFA one-way-liveness bound. Its current abstract confirms the contrast with the new exponential inputs. The final manuscript also credits [Kapoutsis 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2012.11.001) for prior binary adjacency coding and distinguishes the rewriting model of [Guillon–Prigioniero–Taheri, STACS 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.48).

## Row 46 — Exact semidefinite complexity of the symmetric traveling-salesman polytope

**Exact actual theorem.** For the convex hull of undirected Hamiltonian cycles on N cities, measured by the order of one real PSD matrix or the sum of PSD block orders, there are c>0 and N₀ such that for every N≥N₀,

    2^(cN) ≤ xc_psd,R(TSP(N))
           ≤ 2(N−1)+(N−1)(N−2)2^(N−3).

Thus the exact unrestricted real PSD extension complexity is 2^Θ(N). The lower-bound chain is OpenAI's exponential PSD rank of shifted matching matrices, a one-dimensional block shift to the actual matching slack matrix, exact slack factors from arbitrary affine lifts, and a linear-size matching-to-TSP face projection. The note makes Yannakakis's 3n-city construction explicit, gives a checked 2n-city contraction, pads to every sufficiently large N, and counts the subset-state flow upper bound.

**Lay brief.** No exact description of all traveling-salesman tours using positive-semidefinite matrices can be compact: its matrix size must grow exponentially with the number of cities, even when auxiliary variables are freely allowed.

**Importance.** This closes the gap between stretched-exponential known exact SDP lower bounds and the exponential formulation upper bound for an iconic polytope. That is a major result in extension complexity, and its sharp order merits A≈8–9. It does not rule out every algorithm for TSP and says nothing about the useful accuracy/size tradeoff of approximate SDPs.

**New versus inherited.** The difficult PSD lower bound is entirely OpenAI family 126. Yannakakis's linear-size face projection is established; cone-factorization transfer and subset dynamic-programming upper bounds are established too. The contraction, padding, exact matrix-order accounting, and bridge details make a careful consequence note. The theorem is newly available through the new upstream premise, but the intellectual lower-bound breakthrough is upstream. B=5 recognizes the explicit checked refinement without awarding the matching proof to this paper.

**Boundaries.** Exact real lifts, unrestricted matrix order. Counting only the largest block produces a different question. No approximate-relaxation lower bound, no hierarchy-specific result, no coefficient-bit bound, no P-versus-NP result. The accompanying upstream Lean source states a superpolynomial result, not the exponential input; the final paper explicitly preserves that distinction.

**Primary comparison.** [Lee–Raghavendra–Steurer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6317) supplied stretched-exponential exact SDP lower bounds. The final paper correctly cites the later quantitative correction [Lee–Prakash–de Wolf–Yuen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02311), namely 2^tildeΩ(N^(1/11)), rather than presenting 1/13 as the latest baseline. [Yannakakis 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(91)90024-Y) is the original transfer credit. These primary records were refreshed; the precise corrected exponent and original printed construction are also documented in the local final-package priority audit.

## Row 50 — Output-sensitive sparse factorization over finite fields from dense factorization

**Exact actual new theorem.** Let K=F_p[T]/(h) be explicitly represented, deg h=m, L=ceil(log₂p). Let a nonzero sparse f have t terms, binary exponents at most N, b=max(1,ceil(log₂(N+1))), and D the sum of degrees of its distinct monic irreducible factors, returned densely. Complete factorization with arbitrary binary multiplicities reduces deterministically to at most 2b dense-factorization calls, each of degree at most (b+1)D, with nonoracle bit cost polynomial in t,b,D,m,L. Constants and monomials bypass the oracle. The direct prime-Frobenius reduction gives at most 2b(b+1)D prime-field calls with the same degree ceiling.

The substantive additional lemma is the repeated sparse/dense representation. Maintain F=U/V with U sparse, V dense, V|U, and nonzero constant terms after stripping X. Characteristic-p residue extraction followed by the constant Cartier section gives a new numerator with at most t terms and degree divided by p, while

    deg V_new ≤ deg V+(1−1/p)radical_degree(F) ≤ deg V+D.

After k descents deg V≤kD. Visible-residue products have degree at most (t−1)(radical_degree(F)+deg V), avoiding numeric-p loops. Signed multiplicity residues telescope; temporary denominator-induced factors cancel. This is what allows all p-divisible multiplicities while retaining polynomial dependence on log N rather than N.

**Other actual results.** Established Berlekamp/Shoup/Rai reductions, together with OpenAI's prime-field deterministic factorization theorem, yield uniform complete dense factorization and prescribed-degree irreducible/extension construction over every supplied finite-field representation. Construction degree d is a numeric dense-output parameter. The upstream analytic dependency includes the general cyclotomic Hecke zero-free theorem; a Dirichlet-only premise is insufficient.

**Lay brief.** A polynomial may use only a few written terms while its degree is enormous. This method can still find all its distinct factors efficiently when those factors have a modest total degree, even if each occurs an enormous number of times. It avoids expanding the huge intermediate polynomial.

**Importance and novelty.** This is the strongest incremental computational contribution among the four. Dense deterministic finite-field factoring is a famous central problem, hence A=9 for that theorem content, but its resolution and the classical extension/construction reductions are inherited. The additional sparse theorem has its own mechanism and deserves independent credit: classical rational reconstruction and residue descent do not themselves state the repeated compressed invariant and its radical-degree-sensitive full factorization bound. This supports B=7, a strong specialist algorithmic contribution, rather than scoring the whole paper as merely an elementary dense-factorization corollary. For the sparse theorem alone A≈7.5 is appropriate; its audience and input/output regime are narrower than the unrestricted dense derandomization breakthrough.

**Priority limits.** The final paper carefully credits Padé reconstruction to Kaltofen; partial radicals/logarithmic derivatives to Dutta–Saxena–Sinhababu; residue multiplicities to Mattarei; Cartier pull-out to Rowland–Yassawi; and characteristic-p descent to Lecerf. The local independent priority audits read the actual preceding algorithms and distinguish their numeric-degree dependence. None of those inspected statements supplies this combined all-multiplicity output-sensitive invariant. The complete Gianni–Trager paper was inaccessible, so a historical firstness guarantee is unsupported. That uncertainty supports the B range 6–8, rather than either claiming a universally first algorithm or erasing the actual new lemma.

**Boundaries.** Dense distinct-factor output, with size controlled by D; not polynomial in sparse output term count alone, not a sparse-output GCD solution, and not an arbitrary-circuit factorization theorem. No general poly(log d) construction or poly(log r) root extraction. The dense theorem's gigantic fixed exponent supplies theoretical polynomiality, not practical performance. This does not change the sparse reduction's independent mathematical content.

**Primary comparisons.** The local actual-source audit includes [Kaltofen 1988 §8](https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/88/Ka88_jacm.pdf), [Dutta–Saxena–Sinhababu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03214) and the author JACM §6.3, [Rowland–Yassawi Proposition 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/1310.8635), [Lecerf's dense algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00200-008-0062-4), and [Demin–van der Hoeven](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17380). ## Row 53 — Nonnegative binary rational hafnians

**Exact actual theorem.** For every symmetric even-order matrix A with arbitrary nonnegative binary rational off-diagonal entries, construct a simple unweighted graph H and integer D≥1 with #PM(H)=D^m haf(A), where A has order 2m. A positive integer weight W uses a two-terminal gadget with signature (W,1,0,0), 4bits(W)−2 vertices, and at most 6bits(W)−5 edges. Clearing denominators yields O(L²) graph size and polynomial binary scale. Each weighted matching has exactly D^m times its weight in unweighted lifts.

Substituting OpenAI's general-graph FPRAS yields relative ε approximation with failure at most δ in polynomial bit time in input length, ε⁻¹, and log δ⁻¹, and exact zero/nonzero output on every execution through independent matching feasibility. A bounded-bit counting self-reduction always outputs a feasible matching and approximates the weighted product law within TV η in time polynomial in input length and η⁻¹. Empty inputs, disconnected supports, arbitrary denominators, small positive weights, and arbitrarily small positive hafnians are covered.

**Lay brief.** It gives a theoretically efficient randomized way to estimate the total weight of all pairings and draw a pairing according to its weight, even when some weights are extremely small. Clever small graph gadgets turn the weights into ordinary counting.

**Importance.** An unrestricted relative FPRAS for nonnegative rational hafnians is major approximate-counting theorem content: general unweighted perfect-matching counting is a central longstanding problem and is contained as a special case. A=9 is justified if this were an independently novel resolution. It offers no signed/complex hafnian or general Gaussian-boson-sampling result.

**New versus inherited, including late priority discovery.** The final paper explicitly reconciles the earlier independent gadget discovery with prior literature. Dell and collaborators had logarithmic weight-removal machinery; McQuillan already treated arbitrary binary rational weights and exact scaling to a simple unweighted perfect-matching count. Jerrum–Valiant–Vazirani supplies classical count-to-sample self-reduction. The FPRAS breakthrough is OpenAI family113. The Horner realization, quantitative bit bounds, feasible fallback, rounding budget, and reproducible checks are the paper's presentation/implementation contribution. They do not establish a new weighted equivalence or an additional complexity classification after the unweighted theorem. B=4, a useful technical consequence note, is therefore the appropriate incremental score. Independent rediscovery does not remove the prior-art credit, and the final manuscript is candid about it.

**Strong primary confirmation refreshed.** [McQuillan §7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.2880v1) explicitly defines weights as ratios of nonnegative binary integers, Lemma 25 gives polynomial-time binary weight gadgets, and Lemma 27 constructs a simple graph with a computable positive exact factor. I freshly read those actual statements and proofs. This is positive prior disclosure of the central equivalence, not a mere similarity of abstracts. The final priority audit also credits [Dell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775), and records the classical sampler history.
