## Row 36 — Hypersingular Riesz-energy constants on surfaces from triangular-lattice universal optimality

**Actual strongest claim.** Theorem 1 gives, for every real s>2, the exact ordered-pair planar constant C_(s,2)=zeta_Lambda(s), where Lambda has covolume one and is triangular. It then gives E_s(M,N) ~ zeta_Lambda(s) A^(-s/2) N^(1+s/2) on every smooth compact embedded surface of positive area A, including smooth boundary and using ambient Euclidean distances. The unit-sphere coefficient is zeta_Lambda(s)/(4 pi)^(s/2). The explicit finite lower bound also records an error 8 epsilon^(-s) zeta_R(s-1) N^(1-s/2) before the epsilon limit.

**Lay summary.** It determines the exact leading cost of spreading many strongly repelling points over a smooth surface. The familiar triangular pattern fixes this cost universally; the surface enters through its area.

**Importance.** A=8, a major niche advance if the conclusion were independently new: it settles the two-dimensional hypersingular part of a longstanding lattice-constant conjecture and a continuum of exponents, rather than a single numerical case. Its reach is real within discrete potential theory, optimal point configurations and energy asymptotics, but it does not change all of analysis or prove crystallization.

**New versus inherited.** The decisive input is OpenAI's triangular-lattice universal-optimality theorem. The finite/periodic reduction is credited to Hardin–Saff–Simanek, the thermodynamic identification to Hardin–Leblé–Saff–Serfaty, and manifold universality to Hardin–Saff. This paper supplies a self-contained gap-periodization proof in the exact disk/density/ordered-pair conventions, an explicit finite bound and careful boundary/addendum handling. The final manuscript explicitly disclaims invention of those reductions, an independent optimality breakthrough, and first public priority. Hence B=4, a useful technical consequence note; the famous upstream theorem should not be attributed to this paper.

**Material limitations.** Leading minimum value only; neither microscopic triangular crystallization nor uniqueness of finite minimizers. No s<=2 assertion, subleading expansion or other-dimensional constant. The external upstream theorem is essential. The paper is a preprint; full pinned Lean rebuilding and follow-on formalization are not claimed.

## Row 37 — Even Minkowski uniqueness as a consequence of logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski

**Actual strongest claim.** In every ambient dimension n>=2 and every 0<=p<1, every smooth strictly positive even density f has exactly one smooth positive even support solution to h^(1-p) det(nabla^2 h+hI)=f with positive-definite curvature-radius matrix. For 0<p<1, equality of Lp surface-area measures determines arbitrary full-dimensional origin-symmetric bodies, including nonsmooth ones. At p=0 the smooth-density statement is expressly restricted; arbitrary singular measures are not uniquely determining.

**Lay summary.** It says that specified weighted curvature data determine a centrally symmetric convex shape uniquely, including the delicate logarithmic case for smooth data. This closes an important shape-reconstruction question across all dimensions and the stated parameter range.

**Importance.** A=8: the smooth even log-Minkowski/Lp uniqueness family is an important longstanding target at the intersection of convex geometry and nonlinear PDE, and the nonsmooth positive-p result is broad. This is more consequential than a minor regularity lemma. The broader logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski breakthrough would itself warrant a higher headline rating; this paper does not prove that inequality.

**New versus inherited.** OpenAI supplies the all-dimensional origin-symmetric logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality. Differentiation to mixed-volume inequalities, Jensen strictness and equal-measure comparison are established machinery. He–Liu's Theorem 4 already provides the smooth uniqueness transfer from the inequality; existence and regularity come from Bianchi–Böröczky–Colesanti–Yang, Chou–Wang and related work. The paper writes the nonsmooth first variation and strictness carefully and gives a local-path version of the known endpoint argument, avoiding a global smoothness assumption. Its mathematical novelty is mainly the explicit checked consequence and clarified scope, so B=4. It is not a new independent solution mechanism.

**Material limitations.** Origin symmetry, positivity and the admissible support-function class matter. There is no p<0 or nonsymmetric general uniqueness theorem. At p=0 distinct equal-volume boxes have identical cone-volume/L0 measures, so removing smooth positive data would make the headline false. No first announcement is claimed; the final paper also records Stancu's 2018 announcement with deferred details. The external inequality remains essential. Preprint status and incomplete upstream Lean rebuild are expressly disclosed.

## Row 40 — An explicit complex polynomial retract in five variables

**Actual strongest claim.** A smooth integral complex algebra A of transcendence degree four is a retract of C^[5] and is not a polynomial algebra. The paper supplies explicit polynomial inclusion/retraction maps and a five-component idempotent endomorphism of affine five-space whose image is Spec A, with denominator-free formulas and a direct check on the p=0 fiber. Nonpolynomiality is an imported theorem about the explicit cancellation fourfold.

**Lay summary.** It gives a concrete polynomial projection of five-dimensional complex affine space onto a smooth four-dimensional object that is not itself affine space. Thus a long-conjectured principle that polynomial retracts must be polynomial spaces fails in this dimension.

**Importance.** A=8: a characteristic-zero counterexample to Costa's question is a substantial niche result in affine algebraic geometry, connected to the central cancellation landscape and a question dating to 1977. This is not the full cancellation breakthrough, and the remaining four-variable threshold matters.

**New versus inherited.** Nagamine's Proposition 1.4 already makes the cylinder-to-retract implication explicit. The nonpolynomial fourfold, stabilization and its concrete cylinder coordinates are OpenAI's. The paper's new work transports inclusion/evaluation through those coordinates, spells out the five-component idempotent, and provides checkable symbolic identities and the limiting-fiber computation. Those are useful explicit artifacts, but the existence and obstruction mechanism are inherited. B=4 is the best calibrated technical-note rating. B=5 is defensible if one gives substantial value to the usable explicit formulas, but it would still be far from the A-level significance.

**Material limitations.** The ambient four-variable retract question is untouched. Polynomial identities demonstrate the maps; they do not establish the crucial nonpolynomiality, which remains external. No new cancellation theorem or first-priority claim is made. Full upstream kernel rebuilding was not reproduced; the paper is an unrefereed preprint.

## Row 49 — Metric rigidity at the boundary of cubic compactifications

**Actual strongest additional claim.** An irreducible projective klt weak KE Fano with bounded potentials and an actual ample Cartier root -K_X=rL, r>dim(X)/2+1, has stable tangent sheaf on every finite quasi-etale cover, admits no nontrivial product cover, and has only the two parallel orthogonal complex structures +/-J. The threshold is sharp for the general class, via P^(r-1) x P^(r-1). For cubic n-folds n>=5 this gives holomorphic/antiholomorphic classification of all KE boundary isometries and ordinary metric GH compactification equal to cubic GIT modulo conjugation. The paper also states the complex GH/GIT comparison, but expressly credits its antecedents and stronger already public KSZZ result.

**Lay summary.** For these singular positively curved varieties, the real metric still determines the complex geometry up to complex conjugation. It makes the metric compactification of higher-dimensional cubic shapes precise even at singular boundary points.

**Importance.** A=7 for the distinctive metric theorem/application: a strong specialist result with a clear sharp criterion and a complete boundary interpretation. The broad complex cubic K/GIT comparison considered in isolation could merit A=8, but that is already public prior work and should not be the novelty headline for this paper. There is no evidence here for a field-changing result or resolution of a famous longstanding central metric-rigidity conjecture.

**New versus inherited.** The actual extra contribution is the singular high-index exclusion of product covers, descent/all-cover stability, and regular-locus-to-boundary metric-fiber argument. DGP supplies the deep splitting theorem; the Hilbert-polynomial index bound and positive-Ricci tensor mechanism are established ingredients. Smooth cubic tangent stability is old (Peternell–Wisniewski), smooth sign rigidity is old (Spotti), and conjugation quotients for singular del Pezzo surfaces are old (Odaka–Spotti–Sun). OpenAI's ODP upper gap, Li–Liu and Spotti–Sun supply the complex comparison route. KSZZ Theorem 1.1 already gives the stronger all-dimensional complex algebraic comparison, including stacks and good moduli spaces. The final note accurately narrows its novelty. B=6: a solid, above-average specialist extension assembled from established deep machinery, with more real incremental content than the three pure consequence notes, but not a major new K-moduli breakthrough.

**Material limitations.** Actual Cartier root is essential; arbitrary Q-Cartier numerical index is not sufficient for the stated argument. General rigidity assumes weak KE klt Fano hypotheses. Cubic application begins at n=5 and gives topology/closed points and metric fibers, not schemes/stacks or effective GH distances. Sharpness for general Fanos is not a cubic-fourfold counterexample. The complex comparison uses an external upper-gap theorem. No first-disclosure or formal-verification claim is made, and the paper is an unrefereed preprint.
