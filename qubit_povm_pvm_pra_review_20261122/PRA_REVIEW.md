## What the main theorem buys

With two settings per party and complex local dimension at most two, a finite shared random variable can select complete projective strategies that reproduce **all entries of the original POVM behavior at once**. Each branch can have its own two-qubit state. The output sets can be arbitrary finite sets and can differ by input. One-input scenarios are local. The separating example uses three settings on Alice’s side, two on Bob’s, and active output counts $(2,2,3)$ and $(2,2)$. Thus the minimum counts the settings needed to exhibit a separation with suitable output alphabets.

I would put the following application immediately after the theorem. For a two-input Bell functional, enumerate the at most two displayed labels used by each projective setting, including singleton supports and both assignments of the two projectors. In suitable local bases, each party’s first Bloch axis is along $Z$ and its second is in the $XZ$ plane. Scalar settings impose no restriction on the basis. For each label assignment the bound is the largest eigenvalue of a real symmetric $4\times4$ Bell operator, maximized over at most two angles. Optimizing over states is exactly the eigenvalue problem. The theorem makes this finite collection of binary-measurement optimizations sufficient even when all original measurements allow many outcomes.

A value above this bound also certifies **Schmidt number at least three** in any finite-dimensional tensor-product realization. Compress the POVMs to the local Schmidt supports of each pure component in a hypothetical Schmidt-number-two decomposition and apply the theorem to each component. Conversely, a finite mixture of qubit PVM strategies can be realized using local orthogonal classical flags and a state of Schmidt number at most two. These are short consequences proposed for the manuscript. Neither is a separate endpoint in the reviewed Lean package.

**The trine counterexample.** Measure a singlet with identical effects $E_i=(I+n_i\cdot\sigma)/3$, where the three unit vectors $n_i$ form a planar trine. The joint probabilities are $(1-n_i\cdot n_j)/9$. The resulting table $(J_3-I_3)/6$ has zero diagonal, off-diagonal entries $1/6$, and determinant $1/108$. Every single pair of qubit PVMs, even with independent stochastic output channels, gives a table $K_AQK_B^T$ with $Q$ at most $2\times2$, hence rank at most two. Reusing the measurements on the second inputs gives a two-input counterexample. ## A short proof that can replace Appendix G

The projective-fiber proposition at `main.tex` 1592–1607 treats four exceptional divisors in `appendices.tex` 321–551. The following argument proves both the base-locus and injectivity assertions over the entire real null quadric. The existing Lean proof verifies the same proposition independently.

Write $R=[r_1\,\cdots\,r_5]$. The first four columns are the standard basis and $r_5=(1,1,-1,-1)^T$, so any four columns are independent. In the ten-dimensional space of real symmetric $4\times4$ matrices with trace pairing, the five matrices $r_i r_i^T$ are independent. The first four span the diagonal, and the fifth has nonzero off-diagonal entries. The space of quadratic forms vanishing on the five rays therefore has dimension five. A basis is $g,H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4$, where $H_k$ are the four metric derivatives in the paper. All five forms vanish on the frame rays. The four $H_k$ are independent by their binary-to-ternary cross entries. The binary–binary off-diagonal entry of $g$ is $1/2$ and vanishes in every $H_k$. Also $x^TH_kx=2\Phi_k(x)$.

For $D=\sum_i c_i r_i r_i^T$ let $s$ count the nonzero coefficients. If $s\le4$, the columns of $R$ on that support are independent, so $R_S\operatorname{diag}(c_i)R_S^T$ has rank exactly $s$, for either sign of the coefficients. If $s=5$, Sylvester’s rank inequality gives rank at least $4+4-5=3$.

If $x\ne0$ is null and $\Phi(x)=0$, then $xx^T$ is orthogonal to all five basis forms and is such a linear combination. Rank one forces $s=1$, so $x$ is a frame ray. The converse follows by evaluation. Now let two distinct null rays $x,y$ have proportional nonzero images, with $\Phi(x)=t\Phi(y)$. Then $D=xx^T-t\,yy^T$ is orthogonal to the five forms and has rank two, since $t\ne0$ and $x,y$ are independent. Its support has size two. Its image is both the span of $x,y$ and the span of two frame rays $r_i,r_j$. On the latter plane,

$$
(a r_i+b r_j)^Tg(a r_i+b r_j)=2ab\,r_i^Tg r_j.
$$

Strictness gives $r_i^Tg r_j>0$. The only null directions in the plane are its two frame rays. Both are base points, contradicting the nonzero images. This proves injectivity wherever the map is defined. Two independent adversarial readers checked the argument, including the five-term rank bound and projective factors of either sign.
