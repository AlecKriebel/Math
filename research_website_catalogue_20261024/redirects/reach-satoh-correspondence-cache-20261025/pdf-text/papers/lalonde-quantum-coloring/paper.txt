                                                    Abstract
          In Section 4.2 of Quantum colorings of spheres, Lalonde proposed the family of finite graphs
      Gn = G19 ∨ Kn−3 as candidate witnesses for his complex-sphere obstruction. To avoid overload-
                                                                           (1)
      ing the fixed base graph G19 , write Jn for his Gn . Lalonde proved χq (Jn ) = n + 1 for rank-one
      color projectors [2, Theorem 1.5] and conjectured the same value without a rank restriction. We
      prove that conjecture in the finite-dimensional projector model:

                                          χq (Jn ) = n + 1      (n ≥ 3).

      Consequently ξ(Jn ) = n < χq (Jn ) = n + 1, confirming the candidate finite witnesses and giving
      an alternative finitary proof of Lalonde’s complex-sphere lower bound. In fact, Lalonde’s fixed-
                                                          [d]         (s)
      dimension and fixed-rank parameters also satisfy χq (Jn ) = χq (Jn ) = n + 1 for every d, s ≥ 1.
      In particular, adjoining one apex to G19 gives a 20-vertex graph H with χq (H) = 5.
          The proof first symmetrizes an arbitrary coloring, so that every projector has a common
      rank r. For a fixed color, a rational sum-of-squares identity rigidifies vertices 1, . . . , 13 into a
      rank-r sign-vector configuration. The remaining six subspaces are classified, including all non-
      transverse branches, by an r-plane invariant under a canonical complex structure. Orthogonality
      between two colors then preserves the two complex-structure sectors while reversing membership
      in the invariant planes. The resulting sector subspaces are pairwise orthogonal across colors,
      and their dimensions give the impossible inequality 3nr ≤ 2nr. The core sum-of-squares and all
      formal block identities have an exact, standard-library verifier.

Corollary 4.2 (Finitary complex-sphere obstruction). For every n ≥ 3, the complex unit sphere
with orthogonality adjacency satisfies

                                             χq (SCn−1 ) ≥ n + 1.

In particular, Theorem 1.1 gives an alternative finitary proof of [2, Theorem 1.2].

Proof. The equality ξ(Jn ) = n gives a graph homomorphism Jn → SCn−1 , and the quantum chro-

therefore gives
                                   n + 1 = χq (Jn ) ≤ χq (SCn−1 ).
This finite witness does not follow merely from the infinitary lower bound by a general de Bruijn–
Erdős principle: χq does not have that property [2, Proposition 3.3].


5   Relation to the rank-one theorem and exact verification
The three higher-rank obstacles. The closing discussion of [2, Section 4.2] identifies three bar-
riers to the unrestricted problem: rank-one propagation no longer classifies Grassmannian-valued
representations; no evident analogue of Lemma 4.6 supports the rank-one proof; and even a few
values of n appear to require very large, n-specific SOS programs. Lemmas 3.1– 3.3 address the first
barrier by classifying every fixed-color higher-rank representation through one rational core SOS
and a J-invariant tail plane. Lemma 4.1, together with the subsequent sector relations, replaces
the role of Lemma 4.6 by the structure needed across colors. These statements are independent of
n, so one exact rational identity and the final packing argument treat the entire family without an
SDP.
    Remark 3.4 shows that the higher-rank classification recovers Lalonde’s scalar normal form [2,
Theorem 4.5], but the proof does not assume that classification or equal original ranks. Symmetriza-
tion makes fixed-color ranks uniform, the SOS classifies the fusion-frame core, and Lemma 3.3
treats every tail, including non-graph and noncommuting branches. The obstruction therefore cov-
ers arbitrary finite dimension, zero projectors, and reducible or irreducible representations. On the


                                                        8
induced Mančinska–Roberson graph G13 , operator propagation proving χq (G13 ) = 4 [4] is an earlier
higher-rank rigidity precedent; the present argument uses the extra six-vertex tail. For context,
the computer-assisted method behind Lalonde’s Lemma 4.4 is developed in [3]; neither that lemma
nor its algorithm is used in the present classification.
    Two standard-library obstruction verifiers replay the SOS, signs, six tail compressions, J in-
tertwining, final symbolic dimension arithmetic, and an independent trace-SOS formulation with
clique and cross-color checks. A separate graph checker verifies graph6, no four-clique, Lalonde’s
four-coloring, and exhaustive non-three-colorability. All arithmetic is exact; no numerical SDP en-
ters. The programs do not formalize the unitary gauge step in Lemma 3.2, the projection/subspace
semantics and complement equalities in Lemma 3.3, or the final orthogonal-subspace packing; those
remain part of the human proof.
