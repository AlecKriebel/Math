                                                Abstract
         Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acín, and Augusiak introduced two cyclic Bell families
     designed for eﬀicient certification of√maximal global randomness. For the first reduced operator
     they proved the general bound d 2, supplied an attaining strategy of value 2 csc(π/(2d)),
     and conjectured that this sharper value is exact. We prove that formula in every dimension;
     the polar-decomposition argument also applies to commuting-operator realizations. Its scalar
     equality phases yield two complementary structural results. Every attained finite-dimensional
     tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family has, on the support of Alice’s
     reduced state, all d equality roots with equal multiplicity, so d divides the supported dimension.
     At the same time, conditional permutations of those phases generate a large orbit of exact
     maximizers invisible to all first-harmonic correlators in the score. For every d ≥ 4, a final-
     two swap maximizes both augmented cyclic families while producing a nonuniform designated
     joint-output distribution. Thus maximal value of the displayed scalar functional alone does not
     certify the proposed uniform 2 log2 d bits or uniquely determine the canonical behavior. This
     does not challenge certification conditioned on the complete canonical behavior, nor determine
     the worst-case guessing probability. An exact d = 4 certificate over Q(ζ16 ) accompanies the
     all-dimensional proofs.


1   Introduction
Bell inequalities can certify randomness without a model of the devices, but the conditioning data
matter. A complete behavior p(a, b | x, y) contains far more information than one scalar Bell value.
If a Bell maximum exposes a unique behavior, symmetries of the score may force uniform outputs
and, with an operator-level argument, privacy against a purifying adversary. If the maximizing
face contains several behaviors, the same symmetry argument need not apply to each of them
[2, 3, 4].
    Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acín, and Augusiak recently introduced two families built
around cyclic Fourier correlators [1]. They defined the families,
                                                         √        found explicit maximally-entangled
strategies, and proved the general first-family bound d 2. Their strategy has value 2 csc(π/(2d));
they conjectured equality and confirmed it numerically through d = 6 with the NPA hierarchy [5].
They also proved the exact value of the second reduced family by a sum-of-squares identity.
Their symmetry analysis motivated maximal-global-randomness statements; for selected dimen-
sions they evaluated Eve’s guessing probability numerically after fixing the complete canonical
behavior. These are logically different inputs to a randomness problem.

     The surrounding literature includes high-dimensional and modular Bell inequalities [6, 7, 8], self-
testing from few measurements [9, 12], and analytic or algorithmic SOS constructions [11, 13]. Two
contemporaneous works of the originating collaboration pursue all-dimensional global randomness
and three-outcome noise robustness by different constructions [14, 15]. We use these works to
set the attribution boundary; the family-specific polar bound and the phase-permuted maximizers
below are not imported from them. To our knowledge, no prior work establishes either the first-
family upper bound in this normalization or the nonuniform exact maximizers constructed here;
the accompanying priority audit records the qualified search basis for that statement.
     This paper unifies the exact-value and randomness questions through the equality phases of the
first operator. The polar proof reduces its upper bound to a scalar roots-of-unity extremum. At
equality the relative unitary may carry the full set of maximizing phases in different cyclic orders.
Those orders leave the Bell-visible first harmonics unchanged, but they alter higher Fourier data
and can change a complete output table. The same Bob observables also annihilate the sum-of-
squares factors of Ref. [1], Eqs. (22)–(23), for the second family.
     Our main conclusions are as follows.
1. The finite-dimensional tensor-product, approximate, and commuting-operator values of the
   first reduced cyclic operator are all 2 csc(π/(2d)).
2. Every attained finite-dimensional tensor-product exact maximizer of the first augmented family

    has all equality roots with equal multiplicity on Alice’s support; in particular, d divides that
    supported dimension.

3. A polar-linear permutation theorem gives explicit suﬀicient conditions under which phase per-
   mutations remain admissible maximizers and preserve the entire first-harmonic correlator ma-
   trix.

4. For each d ≥ 4, one such maximizer has uniform local marginals but a nonuniform designated
   joint table. With trivial Eve this already gives a guessing probability strictly larger than 1/d2 .

5. The same construction maximizes the second augmented cyclic family and has the same target
   table.

6. The result separates scalar Bell-value certification from certification conditioned on a fixed
   full behavior. It is not a claim about the exact worst-case guessing probability, the complete
   maximizing face, or the randomness of the canonical realization.

    The exact value and support rigidity are proved in Sections 3–4; the explicit equality orbit is
developed in Section 5; the two biased families are treated in Sections 6 and 7; and the randomness
interpretation is isolated in Section 8. Section 9 collects the binary benchmark and sharply scoped
low-setting design conditions.

4       Finite-dimensional support-level equality rigidity
The equality certificate yields additional information at the precise scope below once the first
cyclic operator is augmented by its aligned term. The following result concerns every attained
finite-dimensional tensor- product exact maximizer of the first augmented family. It does not
extend silently to qa or qc.

     The proof, given in Appendix B, starts from the individual vanishing of every positive residual
 in (6) and of the augmented stabilizer. A Schmidt- support cancellation lemma moves those vector
 relations to K. Kernel- safe polar cancellation then makes each polar partial isometry unitary on
 K, adjacent half-angle phases produce one reflection for each equality root, and a rank inequality
 forces all root multiplicities to agree.
     Equal supported multiplicities are necessary but do not imply a Weyl representation, unique-
 ness, or self-testing. In particular, every explicit permutation maximizer below has K = Cd and
 is compatible with the theorem.

Corollary 8.1 (Behavior-level nonuniqueness). For either augmented family and every d ≥ 4, the

10    Discussion and open problems
The exact value theorem and the biased maximizers expose a structural distinction between opti-
mality and rigidity. The first operator controls a sum of moduli of first-order linear functions of
the relative unitary. Its score is sharp enough to determine the allowable scalar phases but not
their cyclic order. That missing order is invisible to the Bell score and visible to the target output
distribution.
    Several questions are now sharply posed.

1. Determine whether the support-level rigidity conditions, admissible phase permutations, di-
   rect sums, and irrelevant ancillary systems generate all attained finite-dimensional tensor-
   product exact maximizers of the first augmented family. First classify the minimal-support
   case dim K = d. Theorem 4.1 then forces the equality-root spectrum to be simple; determine
   whether every such maximizer is equivalent, under the explicitly allowed symmetries, to a
   member of the permutation orbit of Theorem 6.1. Then separately characterize the qa and qc
   maximizing faces.

2. Determine the exact worst-case guessing probability at the Bell maximum; the bound (26) need
   not be tight.

3. Resolve the d = 2, 3 maximizing faces for the first augmented family. The permutation orbit
   studied here is uniform in those dimensions.

4. Classify which phase permutations are related by local isometries and output relabelings, and
   which produce genuinely different full behaviors.

5. Find a low-setting modification whose Bell score controls the missing higher Fourier data, and
   determine whether it is rigid or robustly self-testing.

6. Develop robust bounds conditioned on a minimal set of extra correlators, rather than either
   one scalar score or the entire behavior.

The positive private-MUB criterion and the negative permutation-blindness mechanism point in the
same direction: a successful low-setting score must control operator-valued adversarial information,
not merely a scalar maximum or an unordered first-harmonic spectrum. These problems are
natural opportunities for collaboration with the originating authors and the broader Bell-inequality
community.

G     Source conventions and claim boundary
Our first-family definition (1) follows the displayed main-text operator definition in Ref. [1]. A
nearby appendix writes one Bob adjoint differently while its subsequent calculation follows the main
convention. Replacing every Bob observable by its adjoint transports between the two notations,
but mixing them within one formula does not. We therefore state every adjoint explicitly and test
the Fourier orientation.
    For the second family, (20) matches the Hermitian content of the source definition and the
SOS of Ref. [1], Eqs. (22)–(23), with prefactor 1/(2d). The source text adjacent to the canonical
construction briefly omits Alice’s entrywise conjugation, while its theorem includes it. Our choice
Aℓ = Dℓ is the one that annihilates the displayed SOS on |Φd i.
    If instead every Bob observable in the source appendix is written with an adjoint, set By′ = By†
and Bd′ = Bd† . This is Bob’s outcome inversion: it sends p(a, b) to p(a, −b) while preserving
the maximum, nonuniformity, and guessing probability. It cannot be mixed termwise with the
no-adjoint convention used in (20).
    The source’s numerical guessing-probability table explicitly fixes the full canonical distribution.
Our scalar-value conclusion should therefore not be read as a criticism of that calculation. The
literal maximal-violation conjecture and the fixed-behavior numerical experiment have different
logical hypotheses; the present construction separates them.
