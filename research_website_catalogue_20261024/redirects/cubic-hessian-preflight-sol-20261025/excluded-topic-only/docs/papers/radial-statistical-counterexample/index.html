Statistical geometry · Research note 1.0.1

Radial orthogonality does not imply dual 1-conformal flatness.

A negative answer to Kurose’s printed Question 3(e), with a counterexample on the round cylinder.

Alec Kriebel · ORCID · 26 September 2026

The counterexample

S²(1) × ℝ

Product metric. Levi–Civita connection.

Every radial distribution
is integrable.

The dual structure
is nowhere locally 1-conformally flat.

01

The proof in three steps

Metric duality

The connection is self-dual.

The Levi–Civita connection preserves the product metric. Its statistical cubic tensor vanishes, and its dual is the same connection. The original question permits this case.

First variation

The radial covector is exact.

For any center, differentiating geodesic energy gives the metric pairing with terminal velocity. The orthogonal distribution is the kernel of this derivative, so energy level surfaces integrate it.

Curvature obstruction

One vector, incompatible values.

A 1-conformal flattening would require an endomorphism A. The unit sphere plane forces A(e₁) = e₁. A mixed sphere–line plane forces A(e₁) = 0. This is impossible at every point.

The failure survives a nonzero cubic tensor.

A conformal metric change and a projective connection change preserve the radial distributions and give a nowhere zero cubic tensor. The paper proves that the dual still cannot be flattened. Adding Euclidean factors also gives examples in every dimension at least three.

02

Easy to inspect. Exact to reproduce.

The paper contains the complete analytic proof. The package adds two independently implemented algebra checks.

  • A Python standard-library verifier solves the curvature equations with exact rational arithmetic.
  • A SymPy verifier derives the tensors from the coordinate metric and passes 403 exact checks.
  • Compatible controls pass; a deliberately incorrect deformation fails.

The scripts check tensor identities. The written proof establishes integrability for every center and admissible neighborhood.

python3 verification/verify_exact.py

No dependencies for the quick check. Instructions for the independent symbolic check are in the README.

03

Attribution and publication status

The result answers Question 3(e) in Furuhata, Matsuzoe & Urakawa (1998), p.126, attributed there to Takashi Kurose and indexed as AMR-059-0011. The definition is the statistical one in Kurose (1994), not ordinary conformal flatness of a Riemannian metric.

The ingredients are classical. The exact deformation is already printed as a statistical (−1)-conformal change in Matsuzoe (1999), p.178.
