                           A Lipschitz-nonincreasing deformation
                               of small unitary-valued maps
                                                    Alec Kriebel∗

                                      September 23, 2026          (version 1.1)


                                                        Abstract
              For the unit-round sphere and either the unnormalized Hilbert–Schmidt or operator-norm
          length metric on U (N ), we give an explicit strong deformation retraction of the maps with
          Lipschitz constant at most 1/2 onto the constant maps. At time t ∈ [0, 1], the Lipschitz constant
          is at most (1 − t2 )/(1 + t2 ) times its original value. The formula depends continuously on the
          entire map and stays in the original U (N ). In particular, it answers the literal question [?24](i)
          in Gromov’s 2017 scalar-curvature problem list, with the stronger requirement that the Lipschitz
          bound be preserved throughout. The proof uses a classical matrix fractional transformation and
          an elementary resolvent identity.

2    Proof
For either norm we use unitary invariance and the ideal inequality

                                    ∥BXC∥ν ≤ ∥B∥op ∥X∥ν ∥C∥op .                                   (3)

Lemma 1. If W ∈ U (N ) and dν (I, W ) ≤ π/2, then Re W = (W + W ∗ )/2 ≥ 0.

Proof. Let W v = eiθ v with ∥v∥ = 1 and principal angle |θ| ≤ π. Every piecewise smooth unitary
path U from I to W yields a path U v on the real unit sphere underlying CN . Its length is at
least the spherical distance arccos(cos θ) = |θ|, and its speed is at most ∥U ′ ∥op ≤ ∥U ′ ∥ν . Thus
|θ| ≤ dν (I, W ). The spectral theorem now gives the conclusion.

    Write S = {W ∈ U (N ) : Re W ≥ 0}. For W ∈ S set

                                   Ft (W ) = (W + tI)(I + tW )−1 .

The identity
                     (I + tW )∗ (I + tW ) = (1 + t2 )I + t(W + W ∗ ) ≥ (1 + t2 )I                 (4)
proves invertibility, even at t = 1, and ∥(I + tW )−1 ∥op ≤ (1 + t2 )−1/2 . Also (W + tI)∗ (W + tI) =
(I + tW )∗ (I + tW ), so Ft (W ) is unitary. Direct substitution gives

                            F0 (W ) = W,       F1 (W ) = I,          Ft (I) = I.                  (5)

    For U, V ∈ S the noncommutative resolvent identity is

                      Ft (U ) − Ft (V ) = (1 − t2 )(I + tU )−1 (U − V )(I + tV )−1 .              (6)

Indeed, multiplying on the left by I + tU and on the right by I + tV reduces it to

                     (U + tI)(I + tV ) − (I + tU )(V + tI) = (1 − t2 )(U − V ).

Here U commutes with its own resolvent; U and V need not commute. Equations (3)–(6) imply

                                  ∥Ft (U ) − Ft (V )∥ν ≤ qt ∥U − V ∥ν .                           (7)

Diﬀerentiating the same rational formula on its open domain gives

          DFt (W )[E] = (1 − t2 )(I + tW )−1 E(I + tW )−1 ,           ∥DFt (W )[E]∥ν ≤ qt ∥E∥ν    (8)

when W ∈ S. Hence every absolutely continuous curve W (s) in S satisﬁes

                                        ℓν (Ft ◦ W ) ≤ qt ℓν (W ).                                (9)

This uses the ordinary ﬁnite-dimensional chain rule almost everywhere. For the induced length
metric, the length of an absolutely continuous unitary curve is the integral of its matrix-norm
speed. In particular, (9) is an intrinsic length statement, not an inference from a chordal distance
bound on arbitrary pairs.

    Let L = Lipdν (Φ) ≤ 1/2. Bi-invariance and the diameter π of the sphere yield

                           dν (I, WΦ (x)) = dν (Φ(p), Φ(x)) ≤ Ld(p, x) ≤ π/2.

Thus WΦ (x) ∈ S by Lemma 1. For x, y choose a minimizing spherical geodesic γ between them.
The restriction Φ ◦ γ is a Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous, curve, of length at most Lℓ(γ).
Its matrix-norm absolute continuity follows also from ∥U − V ∥ν ≤ dν (U, V ). Its normalized image
stays in S, so (9) and left invariance give

                   dν (Ht (Φ)(x), Ht (Φ)(y)) ≤ ℓν (Ht (Φ) ◦ γ) ≤ qt Lℓ(γ) = qt Ld(x, y).

This proves (2), including for nonsmooth input maps.

The rational formula is continuous on the compact set [0, 1] × S, by (4), so its dependence on t is
uniform in W . Combined with (10), this proves joint continuity. Matrix-norm and intrinsic uniform
topologies agree because the corresponding metrics induce the same topology on compact U (N ).
Finally, (5) proves all retraction properties. Since 0 ≤ qt ≤ 1, the strict sublevel is also preserved.
This proves Theorem 1.


3    Boundary cases and interpretation
The two-point sphere. If S 0 is assigned angular distance π, the same result holds. One value
is ﬁxed. For the other, the principal eigenangles satisfy |θ| ≤ π/2, and

                                                                 1−t     θ

The derivative of the resulting angle with respect to θ is (1 − t2 )/(1 + t2 + 2t cos θ) ≤ qt ; at t = 1 the
angle is identically zero. Thus each new absolute angle is at most qt times the original. Distances
to I are the Euclidean norm of principal angles for dHS and their maximum for dop , proving the
asserted Lipschitz estimate. We do not use a path-length metric on disconnected S 0 .

Families and scope. For any topological parameter space Y and continuous Φ : Y × S n−1 →
U (N ) whose slices obey the bound, substitution into (1) gives a continuous family of deformations.
The formula is also equivariant under constant changes of frames: Ht (QΦR) = QHt (Φ)R for
Q, R ∈ U (N ). No claim about nontrivial sphere ﬁbrations is needed or made. The bound 1/2
answers the printed question; the preceding discussion of maps with Lip < 1 in [1] is a diﬀerent
bound.

Even the weaker assertion without preservation of a Lipschitz sublevel follows from the principal
logarithm: for L < 1, the normalized image avoids −1, and A exp((1 − s) log WΦ ) deforms the
map to A continuously. The point of Theorem 1 is its elementary, explicit quantitative estimate
throughout the deformation for both stated length metrics. No novelty is claimed for the Cayley
transform, logarithm chart, or local contractibility. A documented literature search accompanying
this note found no earlier explicit statement of this quantitative retraction as an answer to [?24](i);
this is not a guarantee of ﬁrst priority.

Veriﬁcation and provenance. The proof above is self-contained apart from standard ﬁnite-
dimensional spectral and curve-length facts. Accompanying exact algebra checks and numerical
stress tests are optional diagnostics, not a formal proof or a replacement for the analytic argument.
The candidate proof, independent adversarial checks, literature search, and manuscript preparation
used generative-AI assistance. The accompanying reports are AI-assisted internal reviews, not
external peer review. This note is an unrefereed preprint. The project page provides the source,
