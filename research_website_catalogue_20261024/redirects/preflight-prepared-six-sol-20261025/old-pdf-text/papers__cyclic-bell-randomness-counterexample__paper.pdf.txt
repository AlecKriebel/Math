                                                 Abstract
          Perito, D’Avino, Jung, Mironowicz, Acin, and Augusiak conjectured that maximal violation
      of an augmented cyclic Bell functional certifies 2 log2 d bits of global randomness. We disprove
      this Bell-value-based assertion for every d ≥ 4. For each such d, a sparse weighted-shift strategy
      on Cd ⊗ Cd , with no adversarial side information, attains the exact Bell maximum
                                                      π
                                                2 csc      +1
                                                       2d
      while the designated joint output distribution is nonuniform. Quantitatively,

                                                1     2 sin(π/d) sin(3π/d)   1
                            G(AB | 1, d, E) ≥       +                      > 2.
                                                d 2         d (d − 1)
                                                             2              d

      Both one-party marginals remain uniform, so the obstruction concerns global joint randomness
      rather than a local-output bias. At d = 4, a compact certificate over Q(ζ16 ) gives probabilities
      1/32 and 3/32, hence G = 3/32 > 1/16. We reproduce the exact Bell upper certificate, prove
      the infinite counterexample family from first principles, and provide an exact-arithmetic verifier
      for the finite witness. The construction does not challenge the separate numerical certificate for
      the fixed canonical full behavior, and every root ordering in this family is uniform at d = 2, 3.

6    Consequences, verification, and scope
1. After correcting the normalization typo described in Remark 1.1, the Bell-value reading of
   Conjecture 2 of Ref. [1] is false for every d ≥ 4.

2. The obstruction needs neither a direct sum nor an Eve-held flag. It occurs on one d × d block,
   on a maximally entangled state, while both local marginals remain uniform.

3. The Bell maximum does not determine a unique target behavior in these dimensions. Hence
   the uniqueness premise of the paper’s symmetry argument fails, and the first augmented family
   cannot self-test a unique maximizing correlation.

                                                            8
Cyclic Bell maximizers                                                                       A. Kriebel


4. No upper bound based only on the Bell value or Bell deficit can converge to 1/d2 as the deficit
   tends to zero. Indeed, if Gval (d, ε) is the supremum of the guessing probability over strategies
   with hI d i ≥ Md + 1 − ε, the exact witness is feasible for every ε ≥ 0, so the lower bound in (7)
   holds for every ε. Bounds using additional observed statistics are not excluded.
5. The complete maximizing face, its worst guessing probability, and the maximal-violation ran-
   domness question for d = 2, 3 remain unresolved here. The Appendix proves only a multiplicity
   restriction, not a classification.

    The general SDP discussion in the source preprint permits either a full target distribution or
a Bell constraint, but its Appendix B.1 calculation fixes the full canonical probability distribution.
That calculation can support randomness of the fixed canonical behavior without excluding another
behavior at the same Bell maximum. The strategy above supplies exactly such a behavior, and
its own full behavior is nonuniform. This note does not dispute the separate protocols of Farkas,
Mironowicz, and Augusiak [3], nor the distinct three-outcome robustness study of D’Avino et al. [4].
