                                                Abstract
         We study how many measurement settings are needed for one Bell score to certify 2 log2 d
     private random bits from projective d-outcome measurements. We do not determine the all-
     dimensional minimum, but prove four exact structural results. First, if either party has only
     one input, the complete behavior is local and has a compatible pure realization in which Eve
     guesses every joint output perfectly; thus both parties need at least two inputs. Second, we give
     a positive-factor theorem for a broad first-harmonic polar-linear class. Under explicit phase-
     product hypotheses, every permutation of the scalar maximizing phases produces an admissible
     order-d maximizer with the same Bell-visible correlators, although its full target distribution
     can change. Third, the phase-permutation counterexamples previously found for the first cyclic
     family of Perito et al. also annihilate the sum-of-squares certificate of their second family. For
     both augmented families and every d ≥ 4, an exact maximizer with trivial Eve obeys
                                           1    2 sin(π/d) sin(3π/d)   1
                              G(AB|E) ≥       +                      > 2.
                                           d2         d2 (d − 1)      d
     At d = 4, an exact Q(ζ16 ) certificate gives G = 3/32 > 1/16. Finally, we compute all four ideal
     tables of the standard two-input qudit self-test and prove that a natural separately bounded
     third-setting repair cannot expose its common computational mutually unbiased basis. These
     results explain why several eﬀicient designs fail, but they neither rule out nor construct an
     all-dimensional 2 × 2 or 2 × 3 protocol.

• independent, dependency-free checks of the binary SOS and standard qudit probability tables;

• discovery code for the second-family Fourier construction through d = 12;

mutation theorem for the second cyclic family. This is not proof of worldwide priority. Unindexed,
