 The odd-part ratio under slow variation A note on the printed Hilberdink problem 
 Alec Kriebel ·  ORCID  · Version 1.0.1 · 23 September 2026 
 The limit exists under the hypothesis printed in the original report. Its value follows from a short monotonicity argument. 
 Read the 3-page paper Source & verification Zenodo DOI & files 
 
 
 The result 
 Let  f  be a nonzero nonnegative multiplicative function. Let  F ( x ) sum its values over all positive integers up to  x , and let  F 2 ( x ) sum only over odd integers. 
 If  F (2 x )/ F ( x ) → 1, then 
 lim x→∞  F 2 (x)/F(x) = 1 / ∑ k≥0  f(2 k ). 
 The reciprocal is zero when the series diverges. Bounded summatory functions are included. 
 Writing every integer uniquely as 2 k  times an odd integer gives  F(x) = ∑ k≥0  f(2 k ) F 2 (x/2 k ) . If A K  is the first K + 1 terms of the local sum, monotonicity gives A K F 2 (x) ≤ F(2 K x). For fixed K, division by F(x) and slow variation give a limiting upper bound of 1/A K . If the full sum A is finite, F(x) ≤ AF 2 (x) supplies the matching lower bound. 
 
 Why the wording matters 
 OWR-15956-012  and  Hilberdink’s original Problem 3  ask for existence under slow variation: F(λx)/F(x) → 1. The original report then predicts a different, weighted constant. A simple example exposes the conflict: 
 For f(n) = 1/n Value Actual odd-part limit 1/2 Report’s weighted prediction 3/4 
 Exact scope.  This proves the printed limit-existence assertion and corrects its constant. The surrounding formulas suggest a possible intended hypothesis F(λx)/F(x) → λ. This paper does not resolve that different formulation or establish the proposer’s intent. 
 
 Easy to check 
 The analytic proof is the mathematical certificate. The accompanying script checks the finite identities and bounds with exact rational arithmetic, including examples that are multiplicative but not completely multiplicative. 
 python3 verification/verify.py 
 Run from the extracted source package with Python 3.9 or later. No extra packages or network access. Normal and optimized runs passed. These finite checks do not constitute a formal proof of the limiting theorem.
