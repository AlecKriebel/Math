 The complete equality class 
 Let  K  be a compact convex body with nonempty interior in ℝ n , with  n  ≥ 1 and  r  > 0. Suppose every extreme point has Euclidean norm at least  r . Write  g K  for its volume centroid and  C 2 ( K ) for the average squared Euclidean norm of a point chosen uniformly from  K . 
 C 2 (K) ≥ [r² + (n + 1) ‖g K ‖²] / (n + 2) Equality holds if and only if   K  is a nondegenerate simplex whose vertices all have norm  r . 
 The inequality and the polytope equality case are due to  Fradelizi, Paouris, and Schütt . This note proves the conjecture following their Theorem 1.1 for general convex bodies, also recorded in the  2009 Oberwolfach report, p. 2908  and catalogued as  OWR-4136-011 . 
 
 
 Why equality forces one simplex 
 Partition the body.  Add a dense finite or countable collection of extreme points one at a time. Triangulate each visible facet of the previous hull, then cone its simplices to the new point. The pieces have disjoint interiors, use only extreme-point vertices, and cover the body up to a set of volume zero. Keep the deficit exactly.  It splits into two nonnegative quantities: the excess of the vertices' squared norms over  r ², and the variance of the simplices' centroids. Use disjoint interiors.  Equality makes every simplex centroid equal to  g K . A simplex contains its centroid in its interior, so two distinct pieces cannot share that centroid. Only one simplex remains. 
 
 Two simplex pieces have distinct interior centroids A square is divided into two triangles by a diagonal. Each triangle's centroid is in its own interior, and the centroids are separated. This creates a positive variance contribution.

 The square illustrates strictness. For a convex body satisfying the theorem’s hypotheses, two fixed pieces retained in the partition give a positive lower bound that survives approximation. 
 
 Regularity is unnecessary. Even the nonregular triangle with vertices (1, 0), (0, 1), (−1, 0) attains the bound: its centroid is (0, ⅓) and its second moment is ⅓. For the weaker bound  C 2 ( K ) ≥  r ²/( n  + 2), equality additionally requires centroid zero. 
 
 
 A short proof, with checkable support 
 The universal result follows from the written proof. The accompanying script independently integrates simplex polynomials and polygon boundary moments, then checks the finite deficit and shell identities using exact rational arithmetic. 
 python3 verification/verify.py 
 Extract the proof package and run this from its project folder. Python 3.9+ is sufficient; no packages or network are needed.  298 checks across 18 cases pass , including dimensions 1–6 and an auxiliary coplanar-facet test. These finite checks do not prove the infinite geometric argument.
