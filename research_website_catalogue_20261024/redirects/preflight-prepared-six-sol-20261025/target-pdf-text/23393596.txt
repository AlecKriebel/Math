Lemma 2. The restriction q|P3 is not a homomorphism.
Proof. Put x = σ1 , y = σ2 , and

                     a = x2 ,      b = y2,    h = yx2 y −1 ,   z = (xy)3 = ∆23 .

These four braids are pure. The Artin relation xyx = yxy gives

By [2, Remark 3, p.545], the two-strand closure of xℓ has signature 1 − ℓ for ℓ ≥ 1. Adding a split

    The next input is stated with its hypotheses because we use the pointwise signature at −1,
including at Alexander roots. It is a standard signature obstruction; we supply the local-coeﬃcient
argument and cite the precise surgery formula.
Lemma 3. If an algebraically split oriented link L admits an integral smooth 1-solution in the
sense of [5, Deﬁnition 2.3], then σL (−1) = 0.
Proof. Let ML = ∂W be zero surgery and let {Li , Di }ri=1 be the framed embedded hyperbolic basis
of H2 (W ; Z) supplied by the solution. Both sets of surface groups lie in π1 (W )(1) , and inclusion
induces H1 (ML ; Z) =∼ H1 (W ; Z) =∼ Zm . The character taking every meridian to −1 therefore
extends over W ; denote its rank-one rational local system by Qα .
    In a ﬁnite cellular structure, each twisted boundary matrix has integral entries obtained by
evaluating group elements at ±1. Modulo 2 it is the ordinary cellular boundary matrix. A minor
nonzero over F2 has odd determinant, so rankQ dαj ≥ rankF2 dj for each j. The chain dimensions
are equal, and hence
                             dimQ H2 (W ; Qα ) ≤ dimF2 H2 (W ; F2 ) = 2r.
The ﬁnal equality follows from integral universal coeﬃcients: H2 (W ; Z) is free of rank 2r and
H1 (W ; Z) has no 2-torsion.
    The local system restricts trivially to each basis surface because its character kills the commu-
tator subgroup. Each surface therefore has a local-coeﬃcient fundamental class after a choice of
trivialization. Their twisted pairings are zero except for the prescribed dual pairs, whose single in-
tersections have coeﬃcients +1 or −1. Framings make the self-pairings zero. Rescaling one class in
each pair gives r hyperbolic blocks. These 2r classes are independent; the dimension bound makes
them a full basis. Thus the twisted form, including any possible ambient radical, has signature
zero. The ordinary form is also hyperbolic and has signature zero. Extension to real or complex
coeﬃcients preserves these signatures.
    Toﬀoli’s bounding formula [6, (1.1)] gives ρα (ML ) = sign(W ) − signα (W ) = 0. His single-colour
surgery formula [6, Remark 4.25, (4.21)] gives ρα (ML ) = −σL (−1): the entire framed linking
matrix is zero for this algebraically split, zero-framed surgery, so all correction terms vanish. This
pointwise formula applies at every unit value other than 1; no Alexander-root exclusion or average
of signatures is used. Therefore σL (−1) = 0.

3    Persistence through the derived series
We recall a consequence of established quasimorphism theory, giving a proof to make the application
self-contained apart from amenable rigidity. Kawasaki–Kimura–Maruyama–Matsushita–Mimura [4,
Lemma 7.12, p.1207] prove the stronger identity DG (f ) = DN (f |N ) whenever G/N is solvable. In
particular, the following group-theoretic fact is not claimed new.

Lemma 4. If a homogeneous real quasimorphism f on G is not a homomorphism, then f |G(k) is
nonzero for every ﬁnite k ≥ 1.

Proof. Suppose f vanishes on N = G(k) . This subgroup is normal. For g ∈ G, u ∈ N and positive
r, normality gives (gu)r = g r ur with ur ∈ N . Applying the defect bound once yields

                                         |rf (gu) − rf (g)| ≤ D(f ).

After division by r and passage to inﬁnity, f (gu) = f (g). Thus f descends to a homogeneous
quasimorphism on G/N . This quotient is solvable, hence amenable. A homogeneous real quasimor-
phism on an amenable group is a homomorphism [1, Proposition 2.65, p.33; p.27]. Pulling it back
contradicts the hypothesis.
