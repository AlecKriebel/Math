Intermediate-area cylinders in the principal quadratic stratum
Alec Kriebel
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
15 October 2026
Unrefereed preprint, version 1.0
Abstract
Delecroix asked for the area spectrum of cylinders on large-genus square-tiled surfaces, high-
lighting the normalized-area window [g−1/2, 2g−1/2]. For the principal holomorphic quadratic
stratum, with the number of squares tending to inﬁnity before the genus, we derive a Poisson
limit at every intermediate scale sg → 0, gsg → ∞ . The rescaled cylinder areas converge to a
Poisson point process of intensity dx/ (2x); compact-window counts converge with every ﬁxed
joint moment. The highlighted count tends to Poisson((log 2)/ 2), and its mean and variance
have the same limit. The proof extracts a uniform Gamma/Dirichlet approximation from the
published formulas of Delecroix–Liu and Delecroix–Goujard–Zograf–Zorich, then applies classi-
cal assembly identities and elementary tail estimates. The weighted permutation model and its
unmarked Poisson approximation are prior results. We explicitly credit the earlier microscopic
announcements and retain their unresolved normalization discrepancy with the short problem
report. AI tools were used extensively; no external human peer review is asserted.
1 Question, ensemble and conclusion
Fix g ≥ 2. Sample an isomorphism class of square-tiled half-translation surfaces in the princi-
pal holomorphic quadratic stratum Q(14g−4), uniformly among those made from at most N unit
squares. Count each maximal horizontal cylinder once, and divide its area by the entire surface area.
Cylinder heights are unbounded; in the multicurve notation of [ 6] this is m = ∞ . The normalized
areas sum to one. The ﬁxed-genus N → ∞ law of their unordered multiset exists by [ 6, Theorem
1.4]; denote it by νg. The usual automorphism-weighted sampling has the same ﬁxed-genus limiting
law, as in those counting formulas. All subsequent limits refer to νg, hence to the iterated limit
N → ∞ followed by g → ∞ .
Problem 2 in Delecroix’s report [ 4, p. 2387] asks for the cylinder-area spectrum and, speciﬁcally,
intermediate areas such as [g−1/ 2, 2g−1/ 2]. The surrounding law is the principal quadratic law just
speciﬁed. Arbitrary-stratum extensions are conjectural there, and diagonal sampling N = N (g) is
its separate Problem 3.

4 Transfer and the sampling order
Proposition 2 transfers every bounded point-process observation to the geometric law, even when
sg depends on g: total variation controls any measurable observation at each genus. For a ﬁxed
nonnegative count moment, the bad-event contribution is o(1) by ( 8). On the good event, ( 9)
applies uniformly to that statistic. Its model expectation is bounded by the preceding argument,
and removing the model cutoﬀ changes it by o(1), again by ( 8). This proves convergence of the
geometric moments. In particular, total variation alone has not been used to infer convergence of
unbounded expectations.
For each ﬁxed suﬃciently large g, the endpoints of the relevant normalized-area windows have
no atoms under νg. Mixture components with at least two cylinders have simplex densities; the one-
cylinder atom is at area one, outside these windows. The same holds for ﬁnite unions of windows.
Since the cylinder count is at most 3g − 3 at ﬁxed genus, the ﬁxed-genus N → ∞ law also gives
convergence of these counts and their ﬁxed moments. Taking g → ∞ afterward proves Theorem 1
in the stipulated order.
No result at the endpoints gsg = O(1) or sg ≍ 1 is inferred from this proof. Proposition 2 is
a whole-multiset approximation for the stated ensemble, rather than a relative-error assertion for
arbitrarily rare events.
