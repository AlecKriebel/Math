rooted binary network whose sd0 reduction is exactly the given mixed graph. Thus a root site may
be an undirected edge or a compatible edge entering a reticulation: inserting the root on u → r

map. In particular, it is not the broader cleanup operation sometimes used after taking induced
subnetworks: such a cleanup may merge parallel edges or erase reticulation vertices and is not used
to deﬁne the rooting universe here.
We distinguish

              RTC = {rooted tree-child presentations},
              WTC = {semi-directed networks with at least one tree-child rooting},
                 STC = {semi-directed networks for which every rooting is tree-child}.

Thus STC ⊆ WTC , while RTC is a property of a chosen rooted presentation.
A cut edge is an edge whose deletion disconnects the underlying graph. A blob is a maximal
connected subgraph containing no cut edge. (For the simple binary mixed graphs used here, this
agrees with the nontrivial biconnected-block convention.) A network is level two if each blob
contains at most two reticulations.

Deﬁnition 2.1 (Ordinary triangle redirection). Suppose a semi-directed network has a triangle ∆.
An ordinary triangle redirection T changes the reticulation vertex and the two incoming arrowheads
within ∆ while leaving the labelled underlying graph and every arrowhead outside ∆ unchanged.

In particular, T cannot move a labelled pendant component from one vertex of the underlying
graph to another.
