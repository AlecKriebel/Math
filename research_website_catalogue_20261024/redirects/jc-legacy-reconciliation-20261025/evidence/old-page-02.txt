The pair’s location in the tree-child hierarchy is important for the scope of the result. Following
the semi-directed terminology of Holtgrefe et al. [6], a semi-directed topology is weakly tree-child
if it has at least one tree-child rooting and strongly tree-child if every admissible rooting is tree-
child. The pair below is weakly but not strongly tree-child. It therefore does not contradict the
triangle-free strongly tree-child identiﬁability theorem of Englander et al. [4]. It proves failure over
the full weakly tree-child class even modulo ordinary triangle redirection, but because the pair also
contains a triangle, it does not settle the triangle-free weakly tree-child subclass.
Ardiyansyah [1] classiﬁed several four-leaf simple level-2 comparisons algebraically and identiﬁed
same-type comparisons that were not resolved by those calculations. The construction below sup-
plies an exact negative instance for one labelled pair of the triangle-containing type. It is distinct
from the three-leaf semialgebraic triangle-orientation overlap studied by Currie et al. [3], and from
the stacked-reticulation and two-blob local modiﬁcations studied by Sullivant [8]. Brits et al. [2]
prove full identiﬁability for level-1 networks modulo triangle redirection and analyze tree–network
separation in the presence of speciﬁed two-sub-blob phenomena; the construction here is a level-2
comparison and uses no two-sub-blob suppression.
The main result is deliberately local in scope.

Theorem 1.1 (All-taxa full-dimensional ambiguity). For every n ≥ 4, there are two leaf-labelled
binary standard semi-directed level-2 networks Nn , Nn0 ∈ WTC \ STC such that:
  (i) Nn and Nn0 are nonisomorphic and are not related by ordinary triangle redirection T;
 (ii) their open JC model images have dimension 2n; and
(iii) their images share a regular relatively open subset of dimension 2n.
In particular, the nonidentiﬁable parameter set contains a nonempty Euclidean-open subset of the
full parameter space and cannot be conﬁned to a proper algebraic exceptional set.

The proof starts from an exact four-leaf certiﬁcate and then uses a leaf-substitution map with an
analytic inverse. Equality of complex model closures alone is never used as evidence of stochastic
overlap.


2     Networks and the JC model
2.1   Rooted and semi-directed conventions

Let X be a ﬁnite set of taxon labels. A rooted binary phylogenetic network on X is a ﬁnite directed
acyclic graph without parallel arcs in which the root has bidegree (0, 2), a tree vertex has bidegree
(1, 2), a reticulation has bidegree (2, 1), and each leaf has bidegree (1, 0) and a unique label in X.
We additionally require the root to be the lowest stable ancestor of X. A vertex is a stable ancestor
of X if it lies on every directed path from the root to each labelled leaf; thus no proper descendant
of the root may have this property. A rooted network is tree-child if every internal vertex has a
child that is a tree vertex or a leaf.

its two incident edges by one edge joining its two children, retaining at either endpoint exactly the
arrowhead that was present there before deletion. A rooted presentation is admitted only if this
operation produces a simple binary mixed graph: it creates neither a loop nor a parallel edge,
