    - heading "Why equality forces one simplex" [level=2]
    - list:
      - strong: Partition the body.
      - text: Add a dense finite or countable collection of extreme points one at a time. Triangulate each visible facet of the previous hull, then cone its simplices to the new point. The pieces have disjoint interiors, use only extreme-point vertices, and cover the body up to a set of volume zero.
      - strong: Keep the deficit exactly.
      - text: "It splits into two nonnegative quantities: the excess of the vertices' squared norms over r², and the variance of the simplices' centroids."
      - strong: Use disjoint interiors.
      - text: Equality makes every simplex centroid equal to g
      - subscript: K
      - text: . A simplex contains its centroid in its interior, so two distinct pieces cannot share that centroid. Only one simplex remains.
