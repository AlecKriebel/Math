		32 container abstract
			33 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				34 text Abstract
			35 text Reticulate evolutionary histories encode hybridization, recombination, and lateral transfer, but such histories are scientifically recoverable from sequence data only when distinct networks induce distinguishable site-pattern distributions. We determine this information limit for binary, LSA-rootable, already-simple semi-directed strongly tree-child level-2 networks under the fixed one-step reticulation-preserving semi-directed convention used here and the open four-state Jukes–Cantor model. A source-relative full-dimensional regular stochastic containment occurs if and only if the labelled reduced bridge trees agree and every pair of corresponding blobs is labelled-isomorphic or differs by ordinary triangle redirection. Hence no proper one-sided generic containment exists, and an exact generic distribution determines a unique topology modulo that redirection. Fourier cut ranks recover the bridge tree, while positive rank-one factorizations recover only projective local tensors modulo the full incidence-scaling group; physical bridge multipliers are not identified. A graph-to-algebra classification of cycle and theta factors, together with marginal submersions and coherent bounded probes, reconstructs arbitrary subdivisions.
			36 text The boundary is sharp even without triangles. For every n ≥ 4 we construct two triangle-free networks that are weakly but not strongly tree-child, nonisomorphic, and not triangle-related, yet whose open JC images share a regular relatively open subset of full dimension 2n + 1. A second triangle-containing family exhibits a distinct pendant-transfer mechanism. All finite-atlas, class-membership, Fourier-equality, sign, and Jacobian claims are supported by exact primary and separately implemented replay certificates.
		37 container plain-language
			38 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				39 text The idea in plain English
			40 text An evolutionary network allows two ancestral lineages to merge, as they can during hybridization. Even with unlimited sequence data, two different networks might predict exactly the same distribution of DNA patterns. This paper studies when that ambiguity remains under the Jukes–Cantor substitution model.
			41 text The distinction is between a network that has a tree-child rooting and one whose every admissible rooting is tree-child. In a tree-child rooting, each internal vertex has at least one child that is not a merger. Under the paper’s precise graph convention, the stronger condition permits generic recovery of level-2 networks, apart from changing the orientation inside an ordinary triangle.
			42 text The surprising part is the boundary: allowing just the weaker rooting condition admits triangle-free examples that remain ambiguous on a full-dimensional open region of distributions. These are not isolated coincidences or cases where a parameter is forced to zero. The construction works for every number of leaves from four onward.
			43 text Earlier work already establishes generic recovery for triangle-free networks in the strong class and explains local triangle ambiguity. This paper extends the proposed classification to triangles at level two and supplies explicit weak-class counterexamples. It addresses an exact mathematical model; it is not a test of a particular biological history.
		44 container result
			45 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				46 text The precise result
			47 text The positive theorem concerns binary, LSA-rootable, already-simple semi-directed networks of level at most two. Reticulation arrowheads are preserved under the specified one-step root suppression, and every admissible rooting must be tree-child. The model uses a uniform root distribution and a displayed-tree mixture. Each edge has a Jukes–Cantor Fourier multiplier strictly between zero and one, and every inheritance probability is also strictly between zero and one.
			48 text Within that class, a regular neighborhood of a source model’s full dimension lies in another network’s stochastic image exactly when their labelled reduced bridge trees agree and corresponding blobs are labelled-isomorphic or differ by ordinary triangle redirection. Thus the directed full-dimensional containment relation has no proper one-sided cases.
			49 text Outside a proper algebraic exceptional set, the exact site-pattern distribution determines the semi-directed topology within this class, modulo ordinary triangle redirection. The bridge cuts themselves have a pointwise criterion throughout the strictly open parameter domain: their Fourier flattenings have rank at most four, while non-bridge splits have larger rank.
			50 text For every n ≥ 4, the paper constructs two labelled triangle-free level-2 networks that are weakly but not strongly tree-child, are nonisomorphic and not triangle-related, and share a regular relatively open region of JC distributions of dimension 2n + 1. A separate triangle-containing family shares such a region of dimension 2n.
			51 text The manuscript gives written reductions and a computer-assisted finite classification. The reader supplement explains the proof dependencies and explicit witnesses. The small source and entrypoint ZIPs accompany the paper; the full exact certificate and replay package is archived separately at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22064121.
		52 container proof
			53 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				54 text Why the result holds
			55 text First, Fourier rank conditions identify the bridges that connect the network’s reticulate components. Positive rank-one factorizations then separate the local component tensors, while keeping the full freedom to rescale their incidences. Those rescalings must be tracked because the physical bridge multipliers are not uniquely determined.
			56 text Each level-2 component reduces to a cycle or a theta shape with labelled attachment ports. The finite exact classification records the graph, all physical port arms, incoming roles and relevant marginal completions. Bounded probes recover the order of arbitrary subdivisions and test local containment modulo the allowed positive scaling.
			57 text The ordinary triangle ambiguity is realized on a regular physical open region. Compatible positive scalings then join local matches across the recovered bridge tree. Conversely, nonmatching local structures must be separated by the finite classification. This is the computational part on which the global theorem depends.
			58 text For the negative examples, explicit rational or algebraic parameter choices produce identical complete Fourier tensors on two different labelled networks. Rank witnesses and local parameter arguments extend those equalities to regular open regions. Attaching the same cherry at corresponding leaves extends the construction to larger numbers of taxa.
		59 container limits
			60 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				61 text Scope and limitations
			62 text Generic topology recovery allows a proper algebraic exceptional set. The pointwise bridge-cut theorem does not turn the entire topology theorem into an everywhere-identifiable result.
			63 text The comparison class matters. The theorem does not classify all level-2 networks, arbitrary rooted refinements, or alternative semi-directed cleanup conventions. The weak-class counterexamples show that the strong hypothesis cannot simply be replaced by the whole weak class; they do not show that every weakly tree-child network is ambiguous.
			64 text Triangle redirection is a specific change of the reticulation placement and its arrowheads within a triangle, with the labelled underlying graph and outside attachments fixed. The theorem concerns full-dimensional regular overlap, not equality of entire stochastic model images. A particular distribution can distinguish triangle orientations even when the models overlap elsewhere.
			65 text Root location and all numerical parameters are not recovered. Positive factorization determines local tensors only up to the full incidence-scaling action, and physical bridge multipliers remain unidentifiable by that factorization.
			66 text The strictly open four-state JC model excludes zero or infinite edge lengths and inheritance probabilities zero or one. The result does not transfer automatically to K2P, K3P, coalescent models, rate mixtures, or sampled data with estimation error.
			67 text The constructive discussion assumes an exact full distribution with 4^n entries. Its bounded local probes do not provide a polynomial-time-in-n method for finite DNA samples. The bounded primary-literature comparison also does not establish worldwide priority.
