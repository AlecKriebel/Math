                                                        Technical summary

Resolution. The high-level K3P trinet question has a negative answer: the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet
inequality fails. A fixed binary semi-directed strict level-two theta trinet with a genuine nontrivial 3-blob produces exactly
the same three-leaf distribution as an admissible three-star tree. The witness lies in the quartic number field Q(h), where
h = 5−1/4 is isolated by 2/3 < h < 7/10; exact calculations use the basis 1, h, h2 , h3 . Both inheritance probabilities are
1/2, every nontrivial edge eigenvalue lies in (0, 1), and every transition probability is strictly positive. This answers
negatively the K3P high-level trinet question raised by Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, and Martin after their level-one K3P
result and arbitrary-level JC/K2P results.
Topology and root suppression. Use the rooted calculation network

                                 ρ → 1, ρ → u, u → p, q, p, q → r2 , p, q → r3 , r2 → 2, r3 → 3.

After suppressing ρ, the two root edges combine into the semi-directed pendant edge 1 − u. The remaining blob is the
theta graph with paths
                                       p − u − q,    p − r2 − q,    p − r3 − q.
Its three incident cut edges lead to leaves 1, 2, 3, and it contains exactly the two reticulations r2 , r3 . Thus it is a strict
level-two nontrivial 3-blob. No no-2-sub-blob convention is needed.

What the result does and does not show.
• It shows that the K3P analogue of the high-level trinet inequality fails and blocks the direct K3P extension of the
  JC/K2P global blob-detection corollary.
• It shows that a nontrivial 3-blob is not universally identifiable from three-leaf K3P distributions on Θ0 .
• It does not settle K3P quartet distinguishability, classify all tree/theta intersections, or imply generic tree-equivalence
  of theta-network parameters.
