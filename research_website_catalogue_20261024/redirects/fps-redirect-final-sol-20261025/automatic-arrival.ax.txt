		30 container abstract
			31 heading Original abstract, Value: 2
				32 text Original abstract
			33 text Fradelizi, Paouris, and Schütt proved a sharp lower bound for the second moment of a convex body whose extreme points lie outside an open Euclidean ball. They characterized equality for polytopes and conjectured the same characterization for arbitrary convex bodies. We prove this conjecture: equality holds precisely for nondegenerate simplices whose vertices lie on the bounding sphere. The proof extends their simplex argument by an almost-everywhere partition into countably many simplices with extreme-point vertices. An exact nonnegative deficit identity retains the equality information for this partition.
		34 container plain-language
			35 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				36 text The idea in plain English
			37 text The second moment measures the average squared distance of a body from a fixed origin. Fradelizi, Paouris and Schütt proved a sharp lower bound when every extreme point stays on or outside a specified sphere. They also found exactly which polytopes attain equality.
			38 text This note extends that equality characterization to arbitrary convex bodies in every dimension n≥1. Equality holds precisely for nondegenerate simplices whose vertices lie on the sphere. The simplex need not be regular, its centroid need not be the origin, and it need not contain the origin.
			39 text The proof keeps a finite or countable collection of simplices whose vertices are actual extreme points of the original body. An exact sum of nonnegative deficits preserves the information needed to recognize equality. A second argument retains a fixed pair of simplices to show that a body which is not a simplex has a strictly positive deficit.
			40 text The inequality, simplex moments and polytope equality mechanism are established FPS results. The contribution presented here is the extension retaining equality for general bodies; a bounded literature comparison does not certify first priority.
		41 container result
			42 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				43 text The precise result
			44 text Let n≥1 and r>0, and let K⊂ℝⁿ be compact and convex with nonempty interior. Assume ‖v‖₂≥r for every actual extreme point v∈ext K. Write |K| for n-dimensional Lebesgue volume, g_K=|K|⁻¹∫_K x dx for its centroid, and C₂(K)=|K|⁻¹∫_K ‖x‖₂² dx for its normalized second moment.
			45 text The full inequality is C₂(K)≥[r²+(n+1)‖g_K‖₂²]/(n+2). Equality holds if and only if K is a nondegenerate n-simplex and all its vertices have norm r. The radius hypothesis is non-strict and anchored at the specified origin. If every extreme-point norm is strictly greater than r, equality is impossible.
			46 text The weaker inequality C₂(K)≥r²/(n+2) has equality exactly for the same inscribed simplices with centroid zero. In dimension one the equality interval is [−r,r]. Regularity is not required for the strengthened inequality: the triangle with vertices (1,0),(0,1),(−1,0) has centroid (0,1/3), C₂=1/3 and equality at r=1.
			47 text If an initial extreme-vertex simplex S differs from K, retain the next visible-facet simplex T formed from an outside extreme point u. With a=|S|>0, b=|T|>0 and v_i the opposite vertex, the deficit has the positive lower bound ab‖u−v_i‖₂²/[(n+1)(n+2)|K|(a+b)]. This is a finite body-dependent strictness witness.
		48 container proof
			49 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				50 text Why the result holds
			51 text Classical finite-dimensional extreme-point representation and separability give a countable dense choice of actual extreme points. Insert them successively into finite hulls. Strictly visible facet shells split into simplices using only their existing vertices; retaining all earlier cells produces disjoint interiors and covers the body up to its null boundary. No closedness of the extreme set or compatible face subdivisions is required.
			52 text For each cell S_j, put w_j=|S_j|/|K|, c_j its centroid and q_j the mean squared norm of its vertices. FPS’s established simplex moment formula and weighted variance give the exact identity Δ_r(K)=[Σ_j w_j(q_j−r²)]/(n+2)+[(n+1)/(n+2)]Σ_j w_j‖c_j−g_K‖₂². Compactness makes all the moment series absolutely convergent, and every displayed term is nonnegative.
			53 text If the deficit vanishes, every positive-volume cell has all vertices on the sphere and centroid g_K. Two nondegenerate simplices with that same centroid would share an interior point, contradicting their disjoint interiors. Hence the body is one closed simplex. Conversely, the simplex moment formula proves equality for every inscribed nondegenerate simplex.
			54 text The separate finite route retains the same two positive-volume simplices S and T through every larger finite hull. Completing the square in their centroid terms gives the fixed positive witness before taking limits. Bounded moment convergence then preserves it; strictness of unrelated approximating polytopes alone would not suffice.
		55 container limits
			56 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				57 text Scope and limitations
			58 text The theorem uses the Euclidean norm about the specified origin, ordinary Lebesgue volume, compactness and nonempty n-dimensional interior. Degenerate or unbounded bodies, other norms and other probability weights are outside its stated scope.
			59 text No centroid-zero, origin-containment, regular-simplex, smooth-boundary, finite-extreme-set or exposed-extreme-point assumption is made. Only the weaker bound without the centroid term requires centroid zero for equality. The countable construction is an analytic existence proof, not a finite algorithm for arbitrary bodies.
			60 text The retained finite witness depends on the particular body and chosen simplices. It is not a uniform quantitative stability modulus in a shape distance. Finite exact integration checks corroborate formulas and examples rather than proving the universal theorem.
			61 text FPS already proved the inequality, simplex moments, finite vertex decomposition and common-centroid rigidity for polytopes. The original FPS and Oberwolfach formulations identify the historical question; no claim about the current status or wording of an online catalogue is needed. Bounded bibliographic coverage does not certify absence of later equivalent work, first disclosure or current worldwide openness.
