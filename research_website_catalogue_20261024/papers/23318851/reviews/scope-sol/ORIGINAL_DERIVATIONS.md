## 1. Hypothesis and exact domain

Let V_n=(R^2)^{tensor n}, with its ordinary Euclidean/Frobenius norm. For each mode, M_i(T) is a 2 by 2^{n-1} real matrix and A_i=M_i M_i^T. Write d_i=det A_i and

G_n={d(T): ||T||_F<=1}, C_n=[0,1/4]^n,
H_n={d in C_n: d_i<=sum_{j!=i}d_j for every i}.

The target is the universal nonexistence statement: for each integer n>=3, there is no P in R[d_1,...,d_n] with G_n={d in C_n:P(d)>=0}; likewise inside H_n. Coefficients are arbitrary real numbers and the degree is unbounded. The norm bound is closed, the coordinates are determinants rather than singular values or eigenvalues, and zero coordinates are included. The claim is about one additional weak polynomial sign condition in a specified domain. It does not preclude systems, Boolean combinations, radicals, or auxiliary tensor variables. Replacing >= by <= is equivalent after changing P's sign. A stronger all-positive-domain statement for n>3 is not established by the closed-face argument below.

Success requires a universal argument for all possible P, not failure of a particular low-degree candidate. A finite computation cannot establish this quantifier. The second, logically distinct task is to check the displayed rational point against the particular formulas Q_1,Q_2 defined in the manuscript; matching those formulas and inequality directions to the actual cited Seigal edition is a subsequent primary-source interface check.

Each A_i is positive semidefinite with trace s=||T||_F^2<=1. Its determinant lies between 0 and s^2/4<=1/4. Thus the cube bounds need no imported theorem. The ball is compact and the determinant map is continuous, so G_n is compact and closed. Its existential description in the original tensor entries consists of polynomial equations for d_i and the polynomial norm inequality; this makes clear why one condition in determinant coordinates is a separate issue from introducing variables.

## 2. Real normalized marginal necessity, derived directly

Take ||T||=1. Let a<=1/2 be the smaller first-mode eigenvalue. If a=0, its polygon inequality is immediate. If a>0, a real singular-value decomposition gives

T=sqrt(a) e_0 tensor u + sqrt(1-a) e_1 tensor v,
with u,v orthonormal real vectors in the remaining tensor product.

For j>=2 choose a unit smaller-eigenvalue eigenvector in mode j, and let P_j be its rank-one projector tensored with identities on the other remaining factors. These projectors commute. In the product eigenbasis, N=sum P_j has eigenvalues equal to the number of selected smaller eigenvectors. Its kernel is exactly the one-dimensional line generated by w, the product of complementary eigenvectors. Therefore N>=I-ww^T. This remains true when a marginal has repeated eigenvalues: any orthonormal eigenbasis is available, and the product-basis assertion remains exact.

Writing p=|<w,u>|^2 and q=|<w,v>|^2, orthonormality implies p+q<=1. If t_j denotes the smaller j-mode eigenvalue, expectation of N in the remaining reduced state is sum t_j. Hence

sum_{j>=2} t_j >= a(1-p)+(1-a)(1-q)
                  >= a(2-p-q) >= a.

The middle inequality uses 1-a>=a and 1-q>=0. Permuting the distinguished factor proves all t_i<=sum_{j!=i}t_j. The argument covers eigenvalue zero and one-half and uses real vectors throughout. It checks the precise necessity interface as printed in the original; no assertion about the wording or proof of the unread HSS paper is inferred from its citation.

## 3. An independent geometric control of the closed ball

Here is a direct concavity derivation of the normalization step, instead of depending on the manuscript's ratio differentiation. Put q(t)=t(1-t) on [0,1/2] and lambda(d)=(1-sqrt(1-4d))/2, its increasing inverse.

For a normalized tensor U with smaller eigenvalues t_i, the tensor T=sqrt(s) U, 0<s<=1, has determinants d_i=s^2 q(t_i). Determinants scale by the fourth power of tensor amplitude; they do not scale merely by s. Define

h_s(t)=lambda(s^2 q(t)).

For 0<s<1, with R=sqrt(1-4s^2 t(1-t)), differentiation by hand gives

h_s'(t)=s^2(1-2t)/R >=0,
h_s''(t)=-2s^2(1-s^2)/R^3 <=0.

Thus h_s is increasing and concave, with h_s(0)=0. At s=1 it is exactly t, so the endpoint requires no singular derivative formula. At s=0 it is zero.

Elementary scalar lemma: if h is increasing and concave on [0,c], h(0)=0, and x_i in [0,c] obey x_i<=sum_{j!=i}x_j, then the h(x_i) obey the same polygon inequalities. Let S=sum_{j!=i}x_j. If 0<S<=c, concavity gives h(x_j)>=x_j h(S)/S, so sum h(x_j)>=h(S)>=h(x_i). If S>=c, concavity gives h(x_j)>=x_j h(c)/c, so sum h(x_j)>=h(c)>=h(x_i). If S=0 then x_i=0. No assumption that all x_i are positive is needed.

Apply this with c=1/2 and h=h_s. It proves lambda(d_i)<=sum_{j!=i}lambda(d_j) for every tensor in the closed ball, including all boundary traces. The zero tensor is separate and immediate. In particular, lowering the norm cannot repair a violation of the trace-one polygon inequalities.

For comparison, the manuscript's a_s(d) ratio formula is consistent: log a_s(d) has derivative -1/sqrt(s^2-4d), so the derivative of log(a_s(d_j)/a_s(d_i)) is the printed positive difference when 0<d_j<d_i. Zero d_j and a repeated smaller maximum do not create a hole: under a strict failed polygon inequality the distinguished d_i is strictly larger than every other d_j. The trace endpoint is handled by continuity or the direct concavity argument above.

## 4. Exact three-factor real realization

For t_i in [0,1/2] satisfying the three triangle inequalities, define

w_0=1-(t_1+t_2+t_3)/2,
w_1=(t_2+t_3-t_1)/2,
w_2=(t_1+t_3-t_2)/2,
w_3=(t_1+t_2-t_3)/2.

The last three are nonnegative by the triangle inequalities, w_0>=1/4 because the sum of the t_i is at most 3/2, and the four weights sum to one. In the real tensor

sqrt(w_0)e_000 + sqrt(w_1)e_011 + sqrt(w_2)e_101 + sqrt(w_3)e_110,

the two rows of each flattening have disjoint support. Consequently every off-diagonal Gram entry is zero. Its first-mode diagonal entry for bit 1 is w_2+w_3=t_1; the corresponding entries in the other modes are w_1+w_3=t_2 and w_1+w_2=t_3. The complementary entries are 1-t_i. Thus the determinants are q(t_i), and the norm is one, even when one or more weights vanish.

Together with Sections 2-3 this establishes the complete equality

G_3 = {d in C_3: lambda(d_i)<=sum_{j!=i}lambda(d_j), i=1,2,3}.

The original only requires higher-dimensional necessity, not an unproved higher-dimensional sufficiency imported from quantum marginal theory.

## 5. Independent check of the geometric ambient hull

Apply the scalar concavity lemma to h=q. It gives d_i=q(lambda(d_i))<=sum q(lambda(d_j)) for every feasible tuple, so G_n is contained in H_n without invoking the cited convex-hull theorem.

One can also check the hull statement itself directly. Scale to x_i=4d_i. The polytope is 0<=x_i<=1 and 2x_i<=sum x_j. Its vertices are precisely 0 and the zero-one vectors with support of size at least two. Here is a finite-dimensional active-facet argument valid for every n. Support size one is infeasible. With support size two, the two positive coordinates must be equal, and the only vertices on that segment are its zero and one endpoints. With at least three positive coordinates, at most one polygon inequality can be tight, because two tight ones force all the other coordinates to vanish. If two or more coordinates are strictly between zero and one, their bound constraints are inactive and at most one remaining polygon equality cannot isolate them; there is a nonzero feasible small line perturbation. If exactly one coordinate is fractional, call it r and let m be the number of coordinates equal to one. A tight polygon equality would require either r=m or r=2-m, both integers. With no tight polygon equality, r can vary. Thus no fractional vertex exists.

For a zero-one vector with support I of size at least two, the real unit GHZ tensor on those factors, (e_{0...0}+e_{1...1})/sqrt(2), tensored with unit e_0 on the other factors, has determinant 1/4 precisely on I. The origin is realized by zero. Therefore every vertex of H_n is in G_n and conv(G_n)=H_n. This is a hand check of the geometric interface, not a new historical attribution claim.

## 6. Local semialgebraic geometry and the one-condition obstruction

The map q^3 from D=(0,1/2)^3 to (0,1/4)^3 is a diffeomorphism: its diagonal Jacobian entries 1-2t_i are strictly positive. In this chart G_3 is exactly the closed triangle region relative to D.

On the boundary plane L=t_1-t_2-t_3=0 take t=(b+c,b,c) with b,c>0 and b+c<1/2. Here the other two triangle inequalities are strict. A small transverse motion across L=0 crosses from feasible L<0 to infeasible L>0. This is a genuine internal boundary of the determinant locus, away from the cube boundary.

Suppose P provided the claimed description, and put F(t)=P(q(t_1),q(t_2),q(t_3)). Along this boundary F is zero by continuity, because F>=0 on the feasible side and F<0 on the infeasible side. Expanding F in the affine coordinates (L,t_2,t_3), the constant coefficient vanishes on a two-dimensional open set, hence is the zero polynomial. L divides F. F is not the zero polynomial, since there is an open set of infeasible points in D. Its exact multiplicity m along L is finite. After writing F=L^m A, the restriction of A to L=0 is not identically zero, so it is nonzero somewhere in the chosen open boundary patch. At that point F changes sign transversely. Hence m is odd. This uses the representation's strict negativity on infeasible points; weak nonnegativity on feasible points alone would not suffice.

Now use that F is a polynomial in the q(t_i), so it is globally invariant under t_1 -> 1-t_1. The transformed affine factor is -L', where L'=t_1+t_2+t_3-1. This polynomial automorphism preserves exact divisibility and multiplicity, so L' also occurs with exact multiplicity m.

The plane L'=0 has a patch around (1/3,1/3,1/3) wholly inside D and strictly inside the triangle region. On a full neighborhood there F>=0. At a generic point of that patch the residual factor after removing (L')^m is nonzero; otherwise it would vanish identically on the plane, contrary to exact multiplicity. An odd-order zero would give opposite signs on the two sides, so m is even. This contradicts odd m.

The geometric issue is the incompatible behavior of an algebraic component at a true boundary and inside the feasible region. The reflection is not assumed to preserve D or feasibility. In fact it carries the first boundary patch outside D. Global polynomial divisibility is what propagates the factor to the full second plane, which has another patch inside D. Omitting this global step would be an invalid purely local argument. A prospective defining polynomial is allowed to vanish in the feasible interior; the even-order argument explicitly accommodates that possibility.

For H_3 the crossings remain in the ambient domain. On the first patch the three determinant slacks are 2bc, 2c(1-b-c), 2b(1-b-c), all strictly positive. At the second patch, sum t_i=1, the slack indexed by i is 2t_j t_k>0. Strict positivity persists in small full neighborhoods. For example b=c=1/8 gives the first patch point d=(12,7,7)/64; at the second center the determinants are all 2/9. There is also a strictly H_3-internal infeasible point t=(5/16,1/8,1/8), giving d=(55,28,28)/256 and determinant slacks (1,55,55)/256. Thus the polynomial cannot be identically zero in the H_3 version either.

This reconstructs the universal obstruction from local feasibility geometry and exact polynomial consequences. The mechanism is disclosed in the original manuscript; no claim is made that this is a different new impossibility theorem. The distinct additional controls in this scope family are the concavity normalization, direct convex-hull derivation, explicit local domain margins, and counterexample arithmetic.

## 7. Exact boundary slices and all higher dimensions

If d_i=0, A_i is positive semidefinite and has determinant zero, so rank M_i<=1. If T is nonzero, the flattening has rank exactly one and T factors as S tensor u in that mode. Choose u unit by absorbing its length into S. Then ||S||=||T||, and for every remaining mode the flattening contraction over u supplies the factor sum u_k^2=1. Its entire Gram matrix, not just its determinant, is unchanged. Repeating this argument gives

G_n intersect {d_4=...=d_n=0}=G_3 times {0}^{n-3}.

If T=0 the inclusion is immediate. Conversely append unit real one-factor vectors to any three-factor tensor in the ball. The new factors have zero determinant and the old Gram matrices and norm are unchanged. This proves equality of the slice, not merely an inclusion.

The analogous cube slice is C_3 and the analogous hull slice is H_3: inequalities for zero coordinates are automatic, and the first three become exactly their three-factor inequalities. Restricting any candidate P_n to that face would produce a three-variable polynomial description of G_3, contradicting Section 6. It is immaterial if the restriction were zero; that would describe the entire three-dimensional ambient domain, which contains the explicit infeasible point above.

For n=2 the flattenings are a matrix and its transpose; both Gram determinants equal (det T)^2. Every d in [0,1/4] is obtained from diag(sqrt(lambda(d)),sqrt(1-lambda(d))). Thus G_2 is the diagonal in C_2, described by -(d_1-d_2)^2>=0. Inside H_2 it is already the full domain. For n=1 the flattening has one column, so G_1={0}, described inside C_1 by -d_1^2>=0. These are genuine exceptions to n>=3. They do not contradict the higher-dimensional slice argument.
