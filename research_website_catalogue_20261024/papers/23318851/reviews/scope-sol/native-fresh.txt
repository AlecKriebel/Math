                                                  Abstract
          For a real tensor with n binary factors, consider the determinants of its one-mode Gram
      matrices as the tensor ranges over the closed Frobenius unit ball. For every n ≥ 3, this locus
      cannot be deﬁned by one polynomial nonnegativity condition in the determinant coordinates,
      even after imposing the cube bounds or the known convex-hull inequalities. This answers Sei-
      gal’s single-polynomial question negatively for n ≥ 4. The proof combines established qubit
      marginal polygon inequalities, an explicit real three-factor realization, a multiplicity obstruc-
      tion, and exact zero-determinant slices. A strictly positive rational counterexample also refutes
      the proposed polynomial. Our theorem was recorded in a September 30, 2026 GitHub submis-
      sion. Ferudun’s October 2, 2026 preprint gives an overlapping stronger theorem; we credit it and
      record the chronology without claiming exclusive priority or certifying mutual independence.

Theorem 1. For every n ≥ 3, no polynomial P ∈ R[d1 , . . . , dn ] satisﬁes

                                       Gn = {d ∈ Cn : P (d) ≥ 0}.

The same conclusion holds with Cn replaced by Hn .
    The variables are precisely the determinants. This concerns one additional polynomial sign
condition in the indicated domain; it makes no assertion about auxiliary variables or Boolean
combinations of polynomial conditions. For n = 2, the two determinants coincide and every value
in [0, 1/4] is attained, so P = −(d1 − d2 )2 deﬁnes G2 inside C2 . For n = 1, the locus is {0}.
    The marginal polygon criterion is prior work: Higuchi, Sudbery and Szulc [3] prove necessity
and real realizability; Bravyi [4, Section 2] gives an even-parity construction. Domanov, Stegeman

3    Multiplicities and exact slices
Proof of Theorem 1. First suppose n = 3 and such a polynomial exists in C3 . Deﬁne

                                F (t) = P (t1 (1 − t1 ), t2 (1 − t2 ), t3 (1 − t3 )).

The coordinate map is an analytic diﬀeomorphism from D = (0, 1/2)3 onto the open cube. By
Lemma 1, feasibility in D is exactly the triangle region. The polynomial F is invariant under each
reﬂection ti 7→ 1 − ti .
     Let L = t1 − t2 − t3 . Its plane has the relatively open patch t2 , t3 > 0, t2 + t3 < 1/2. Near
this patch the other two triangle inequalities are strict; L < 0 is feasible and L > 0 is infeasible.
Thus F ≥ 0 on the ﬁrst side and F < 0 on the second. Continuity forces F = 0 on the patch.
In coordinates (L, t2 , t3 ), the coeﬃcient of L0 vanishes on an open set and is identically zero, so L
divides F .
     The polynomial F is nonzero, since the open cube contains infeasible points. Write F = Lm A,
with exact multiplicity m ≥ 1. The restriction of A to the plane is nonzero, so there is a point of
the boundary patch with A 6= 0. Across that point F changes sign; therefore m is odd.
     The reﬂection τ (t) = (1 − t1 , t2 , t3 ) ﬁxes F and sends L to −L0 , where L0 = t1 + t2 + t3 − 1.
Thus L0 has the same exact multiplicity m. A relatively open patch of L0 = 0 about (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)
lies strictly inside D and the triangle region. At some point there, the residual factor after division
by (L0 )m is nonzero. Since F ≥ 0 on a full neighborhood, its multiplicity is even. This contradicts
odd m.
     For H3 , both patches lie strictly inside its determinant inequalities. On the ﬁrst patch write
t = (b + c, b, c); the determinant slacks are

        d2 + d3 − d1 = 2bc,       d1 + d3 − d2 = 2c(1 − b − c),          d1 + d2 − d3 = 2b(1 − b − c),
                                           ∑
all positive. On the second patch ti = 1, each slack is 2tj tk > 0. Both local crossings therefore
remain inside H3 . Moreover t = (5/16, 1/8, 1/8) is infeasible and gives the strict-H3 point d =
(55, 28, 28)/256; hence F cannot vanish identically in this version either.
    For n > 3, the exact slice is

                                Gn ∩ {d4 = · · · = dn = 0} = G3 × {0}n−3 .

A zero Gram determinant means that ﬂattening has rank at most one. A nonzero real tensor
therefore factors as S ⊗ u in that mode. Make u unit by absorbing its length into S. This preserves
the norm and every other one-mode Gram matrix. Repeat to peel oﬀ all zero-determinant factors.
Conversely, adjoining unit vectors to a three-factor tensor realizes the reverse inclusion. The zero
tensor causes no exception.
    Restricting Pn to that face would deﬁne G3 in C3 , a contradiction. Since the same face of Hn
is H3 × {0}n−3 , the other assertion follows.
