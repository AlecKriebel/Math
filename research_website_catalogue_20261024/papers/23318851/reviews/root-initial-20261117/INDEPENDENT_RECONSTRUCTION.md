The exact claim concerns real n-binary tensors in the closed Frobenius unit ball, with d_i the determinants of their one-mode 2x2 Gram matrices. For n>=3 no single real polynomial condition P(d)>=0 defines their determinant locus relative to C=[0,1/4]^n or its specified linear polygon convex hull H. Auxiliary variables and Boolean combinations are outside the claim. n=2 is an essential boundary: P=-(d1-d2)^2 works. n=1 has only zero determinant.

I reconstructed the unit-trace necessity directly: Schmidt split the first factor as sqrt(a)u and sqrt(1-a)v, a<=1/2, with orthonormal u,v. Select the least marginal eigenvectors on other factors and their complementary product vector w. The sum N of least-eigenvector projections satisfies N>=I-ww*. Orthogonality gives p+q=|<u,w>|^2+|<v,w>|^2<=1. Hence sum(lambda_j)>=a(1-p)+(1-a)(1-q)>=a. This is independent of source-support controls.

For trace s=norm^2<=1, the unnormalized least eigenvalue is a_s(d)=(s-sqrt(s^2-4d))/2. If the trace-one polygon fails at i then d_i>d_j for all other j. The derivative in s of log[a_s(d_j)/a_s(d_i)] equals 1/sqrt(s^2-4d_i)-1/sqrt(s^2-4d_j)>0. Therefore the ratios are smaller at smaller trace; a failure at s=1 cannot be rescued at any admissible s. Zero d_j are handled without logarithms by continuity; the d_i=1/4 endpoint permits only s=1. The zero tensor is separate.

For three factors, polygon feasibility for least eigenvalues t_i in [0,1/2] has an explicit real unit realization supported on four even-parity vertices. Squared coefficients are w0=1-(t1+t2+t3)/2 and w_i=(sum_{j!=i}t_j-t_i)/2. They are nonnegative, sum1, and each Gram diagonal has eigenvalues t_i,1-t_i. The t_i<=1/2 constraint gives w0>=1/4. Thus the determinant pullback to the eigenvalue box is exactly the triangle-inequality region.
