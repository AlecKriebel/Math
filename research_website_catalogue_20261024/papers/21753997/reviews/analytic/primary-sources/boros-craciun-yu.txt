                        Weakly reversible mass-action systems
                       with infinitely many positive steady states
                       Balázs Boros1 , Gheorghe Craciun1,2 , and Polly Y. Yu1
                          1
                            Department of Mathematics, University of Wisconsin-Madison
                   2
                       Department of Biomolecular Chemistry, University of Wisconsin-Madison


                                                    Abstract
         We show that weakly reversible mass-action systems can have a continuum of positive steady states,
     coming from the zeroes of a multivariate polynomial. Moreover, the same is true of systems whose
     underlying reaction network is reversible and has a single connected component. In our construction, we
     relate operations on the reaction network to the multivariate polynomial occurring as a common factor
     in the system of differential equations.


1    Introduction
It was widely believed that every weakly reversible mass-action system has a nonzero, finite number of
positive steady states in each invariant polyhedron. One of the fundamental questions about dynamical
systems is the description of the set of steady states. Many mathematical analyses implicitly assume that
steady states form a discrete set. The authors of [9] proposed a proof of both existence and finiteness of
positive steady states of weakly reversible mass-action systems. Their proof concerning existence was made
complete in [4]; however, their argument on finiteness is insufficient, and as shown in this paper, is not true
in general. We construct weakly reversible (even reversible) mass-action systems with continua of positive
steady states within some invariant polyhedra.
Some mass-action systems are known to have at most one positive steady state within each invariant poly-
hedron. For example, detailed-balanced and complex-balanced systems [15], injective systems [7], and those
satisfying the hypotheses in the Deficiency-One Theorem [12].
For a weakly reversible system, we expect the set of positive steady states in each invariant polyhedron to be
bounded. This follows from the Permanence Conjecture [8]: every invariant polyhedron of a weakly reversible
mass-action system admits a compact global attractor. The conjecture is proven for some special cases,
including in 2-dimension [8,19], or when the network has a single connected component [1,5,13]. Furthermore,
since any system of polynomial equations has finitely many nondegenerate positive roots [3, 17, 18, 21],
necessarily, the positive steady states in our constructions lie in a bounded set (of an invariant polyhedron),
and infinitely many of them are degenerate.
For example, the reversible mass-action system in Figure 1 admits infinitely many positive steady states,
including all positive zeroes of the common factor in its system of differential equations

                                ẋ = (x2 y 2 + x2 + y 2 + 1 − 5xy)[1 − xy + y − x],
                                ẏ = (x2 y 2 + x2 + y 2 + 1 − 5xy)[1 − xy − y + x].

The four positive monomials in the common factor correspond to four translated copies of a base unit, while
the negative monomial corresponds to a flipped copy of that (Figure 1). More details of these operations on
networks are given in Section 3. The examples presented in Section 4 are 2-dimensional, but the construction
works in higher dimensions as well.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. After a short section on mass-action systems (Section 2), we
present the basic operations used in our construction (Section 3). Then we construct a number of examples
with infinitely many positive steady states (Section 4). Finally, we list a couple of open questions and
conclude with some remarks (Section 5).

2    Mass-action systems
In this section we briefly introduce mass-action systems and related notions that are necessary for our
exposition. For more details about mass-action systems, see [2, 11, 14, 23]. The symbol R+ denotes the set
of positive real numbers.

Definition 2.1. A Euclidean embedded graph (or a reaction network ) is a directed graph (V, E),
where V is a finite subset of Rn .
Vertices are points in Rn , so an edge (y i , y j ) ∈ E can be regarded as a bona fide vector in Rn . We associate
a reaction vector y j − y i ∈ Rn to each edge (y i , y j ).

 (b) weakly reversible whenever every edge is part of a directed cycle.

3     Three operations on mass-action systems
In this section, we introduce via examples three operations on mass-action systems, namely, translation,
scalar multiplication, and addition. In Section 4, we will combine these to construct examples of mass-action
systems with infinitely many positive steady states.

3.1    Translation
Consider translation of a mass-action system in the plane by a fixed vector (α, β)> . The effect of this
operation on the graph corresponds to multiplying the right-hand side of the dynamical system by the
monomial xα y β . For example, in Figure 2(a) we have a mass-action system whose dynamical system is
                                                  ẋ = κ1 − κ3 xy,
                                                  ẏ = κ2 x − κ4 y.
We translated this system by (2, 1)> to obtain the one in Figure 2(b), whose dynamical system is
                                                ẋ = x2 y[κ1 − κ3 xy],
                                                ẏ = x2 y[κ2 x − κ4 y].

4    Examples
We now apply a combination of the above operations to create a general procedure that gives rise to weakly
reversible mass-action systems with infinitely many positive steady states. We start by picking a weakly
reversible mass-action system, which we call the base unit. Take a negative scalar multiple of the base
unit. Each reaction in this (necessarily not weakly reversible) system came from a reaction in the base unit
by reflection. Therefore, as in Figure 4, we can add an appropriate translated copy of the base unit, so the
two reactions form a reversible pair. By adding the negative scalar multiple and all the translated copies,
we arrive at a weakly reversible mass-action system. Its associated dynamical system is obtained from that
of the base unit by multiplying each equation by the same scalar polynomial. With the monomials in the
common factor represented by their exponent vectors as points in Rn , by construction the negative term is
in the interior of the convex hull of the positive terms, an object called the Newton polytope [22]. Therefore,
the value of the polynomial near the boundary of Rn+ is positive (see [20] for a detailed study of monomial
domination). If the coefficient of the negative term is sufficiently negative, the value of the polynomial at
(1, 1, . . . , 1)> is negative. Consequently, this polynomial has uncountably many roots in Rn+ , resulting in
infinitely many positive steady states of the constructed mass-action system.

Example 4.1. The square in Figure 2(a) with rate constants κi = 1 is our base unit, and Figure 5 shows
five transformed copies of it. The full system obtained by adding these five together (Figure 6) is a weakly
reversible mass-action system whose associated dynamical system is
                                         ẋ = (x2 y + xy 2 + x + y − 5xy)[1 − xy],
                                         ẏ = (x2 y + xy 2 + x + y − 5xy)[x − y].

The set of positive steady states is {(x, y)> ∈ R2+ : x2 y + xy 2 + x + y − 5xy = 0} ∪ {(1, 1)> }. The first piece
is a closed curve in R2+ , and the point (1, 1)> is the unique positive steady state of the base unit. The phase
portraits of the base unit and the full system are shown in Figure 7.
An analogous example exists in any dimension n ≥ 2, resulting in a hypersurface of positive steady states.
There the base unit is a weakly reversible n-cube, and the common factor has 2n terms with coefficient
1 and the monomial −αx1 x2 · · · xn with α > 2n. For example, in the n = 3 case the common factor is
x2 yz + xy 2 z + xyz 2 + yz + xz + xy − αxyz with α > 6.

5     Open questions and discussion
In this section we list a couple of open questions. Below, we are asking the questions in the context of
constructing a (weakly) reversible mass-action system with infinitely many positive steady states.
    • What is the minimal degree of the polynomials in the right-hand side of the dynamical system?
    • What is the minimum number of vertices?
    • What is the minimum number of reactions?
    • What is the minimum deficiency?
The deficiency of a reaction network is the nonnegative integer m − ` − dim S, where m is the number of
vertices, ` is the number of connected components, and S is the stoichiometric subspace [10]. Pertaining to
the above questions, note that Example 4.3 has as the right-hand side of its dynamical system, a degree-4
polynomial system. It has 9 vertices, 12 reactions, and its deficiency is 4.

The rate constants in the examples of Section 4 admit some flexibility, though they cannot be set arbitrarily.
On one hand, we can always choose rate constants for a weakly reversible network such that the resulting
mass-action system is complex-balanced. In particular, it has a unique positive steady state in each invariant
polyhedron. On the other hand, for the system in Figure 10, if

                              κ12  κ2   κ3   κ4                                 κ13  κ2   κ3   κ4
                                1 = 22 = 23 = 24                and              1  = 32 = 33 = 34 ,
                              κ1   κ1   κ1   κ1                                 κ2   κ2   κ2   κ2

then there is a common factor κ11 x2 + κ21 xy 2 + κ31 y − κ41 xy, resulting in a continuum of positive steady states
provided κ41 is sufficiently large. In light of this, we pose the following question:
    • Is it possible for a weakly reversible network to have infinitely many positive steady states for each
      choice of rate constants in an open set of the parameter space RE +?

Unlike all of our examples, a common factor is in general not necessary for a continuum of positive roots in
3 dimensions or higher. So the interesting question is:
    • Can a weakly reversible mass-action system have infinitely many positive steady states without having
      a common factor on the right-hand side of its differential equations?
The analysis and characterization of the set of steady states is one of the most fundamental questions about
mathematical models of reaction or interaction networks. In particular, in order to best interpret data from
experimental observations, and in order to design software that finds the steady states of a specific model,
it is important to know if we should expect the set of fixed points to be finite, or discrete, or a continuum
[14, 16].
Mass-action models of reversible and weakly reversible reaction networks are known or conjectured to enjoy
several remarkable properties (such as existence of positive equilibria for any parameter values [4], persistence


                                                                     8
[8,19], and permanence [5,6,13]). It has also been widely believed that they can have at most a finite number
of positive steady states within any invariant polyhedron, but here we have shown that this is actually not
true. Hence, it becomes important to identify minimal additional assumptions (e.g. low degree, sparsity, or
low deficiency of the network) that, in addition to reversibility, do actually imply that the set of positive
steady states is finite. While low degree may be a good candidate for such an assumption, note that
high degree models do appear naturally in applications, for example via elimination of denominators for
Michaelis-Menten or Hill kinetics [6]; therefore, a better option may be some version of sparsity or low
deficiency [14, 23].
