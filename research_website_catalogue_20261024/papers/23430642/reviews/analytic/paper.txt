                                               Abstract
         For explicitly listed rational bimatrix games of variable positive dimensions and exact rank
     rank(A + B) = 1, deciding whether there is exactly one mixed-strategy equilibrium pair is coNP-
     complete. Its nonuniqueness counterpart is NP-complete. Arbitrary degeneracy is allowed. A
     polynomial reduction from nonempty positive-integer Partition produces a known strict pure
     equilibrium and an additional equilibrium exactly when the source instance admits a partition.
     The construction applies the signed ﬂow counter of Disser and Skutella and the auxiliary zero-
     sum representation of Adsul and coauthors. A comparison-preserving rational perturbation
     isolates every real-demand ﬂow optimum, an injective simplex encoding transfers diagnostic arc
     ﬂow to a scalar consistency equation, and a uniform polynomial-bit exact penalty controls every
     auxiliary minimizer. Rational support-polytope certiﬁcates establish membership, including
     positive-dimensional equilibrium sets. A bounded source audit found no matching proof among
     the inspected bodies; worldwide priority remains unknown.


1    Statement, scope, and lineage
A rational bimatrix game is given by explicitly listed matrices A, B ∈ Qm×N , with m, N ≥ 1.
Both players maximize their own expected payoﬀs. Its equilibrium set E(A, B) consists of pairs
(x, y) ∈ ∆m × ∆N satisfying the two best-response conditions. The word unique refers to the entire
strategy pair. Deﬁne the ordinary languages

                      Uniqueness1 = {(A, B) : rank(A + B) = 1, |E(A, B)| = 1},
                 Nonuniqueness1 = {(A, B) : rank(A + B) = 1, |E(A, B)| > 1}.

Malformed encodings are rejected; rank-invalid games belong to neither language. Dimensions vary
with the input, whose size includes all rational entries in binary.
Theorem 1. Uniqueness1 is coNP-complete and Nonuniqueness1 is NP-complete under polynomial-
time many-one reductions. Hardness holds for games supplied with a known strict pure equilibrium.
The construction permits degeneracy and imposes no nondegeneracy promise.
   We prove this by reducing Partition on nonempty lists of positive integers w1 , . . . , wn , n ≥ 1.
This restriction retains its usual NP-completeness. Every output has one speciﬁed strict pure
equilibrium; it is the sole equilibrium on no instances, and has a distinct companion on yes instances.
No claim is made about strong hardness, ﬁxed dimensions, nondegenerate-promise hardness, or
uniqueness of equilibrium payoﬀs.

    Successive shortest paths (SSP) starts from zero and augments along a shortest simple residual
s–t path by its bottleneck. An original arc of cost ci contributes a reverse residual arc of cost −ci .
The initial zero ﬂow is value-zero optimal because the original graph is acyclic, even though some
cheap arc costs are negative.

Lemma 2 (Disser–Skutella source event). Every legitimate SSP execution on this unperturbed
network augments in units and uses e if and only if Partition is a yes instance. Any forward use
raises fe from zero to one and is immediately followed by a reverse use lowering it to zero. At a
forward midpoint the ﬂow value is q = 2j + 21 ≤ F − 32 and fe = 12 .

Proof. This is the state/two-step argument in the complete journal Lemmas 3.1–3.2 of [4]. We spell
out the comparisons needed for its universal SSP quantiﬁer. In a gadget, the cheap connectors ﬁrst
run the lower gadget forward; once they are exhausted, the crossed connectors run it in reverse.
The recursive state j has marginal path cost j + v [j] + (−1)j δ for signed weights v = σα, and the
base arc is empty at even j and full at odd j. The inductive phases are strictly separated: after
the base adjustment their cost envelope is [j − T − δ, j + T + δ], and 2(T + δ) < 1. Thus shortest
ties do not permit a departure from this recursive state sequence.
    When the two gadgets are synchronized at state j, their whole-gadget paths and the bridge
path have the following costs, where z = zj :

       State                  + path         − path       Forward bridge       Next reverse if z = 0
       Even j, base empty     j+z+δ          j−z+δ        j                    j + 2δ
       Odd j, base full       j+z−δ          j−z−δ        j − 2δ               j

If z ̸= 0, one whole-gadget path is strictly cheaper than the bridge because |z| ≥ 5δ. Its compan-
ion augmentation restores synchronization, and the same comparisons exclude bridge use in that
companion step. If z = 0, the forward bridge wins strictly; the next shortest path reverses it and
restores synchronization at the next state. The recursive arcs and base/bridge capacities make
each bottleneck one. This state induction applies to any chosen shortest path and has no special
tie-breaking hypothesis. The forward use starts at value 2j, giving the claimed midpoint, with
j ≤ 2n − 1.

6    Encoding and membership
The preceding reduction is a polynomial construction. Each capacity has O(n) bits and F has
n + 2 bits. Original cost denominators divide 240S, so D has O(log S) bits; a generic denominator-
product bound would also suﬃce. The ternary denominators add O(p) bits per arc. Costs of order
2n , the topological potential, L, K, ε, and all R, r, s, c coeﬃcients have polynomial encoding length.

Lemma 5 computes an extremely large numerical M , but of polynomial binary length, by integer
arithmetic. Explicitly listing the m × N matrices is polynomial. The exponential SSP run is used
only to prove correctness; it is never executed by the reduction algorithm. This proves NP-hardness
of Nonuniqueness1 and, by complementing the source decision, coNP-hardness of Uniqueness1 .
    We include the certiﬁcate proof because exact pair uniqueness with arbitrary degeneracy re-
quires rational witnesses even when initially observed equilibria are irrational. This membership
conclusion is established prior art [7, §3.13]; the explicit bounds below also clarify our encoding
convention.

    Let ℓ be the total binary input length. Multiply payoﬀs by the product of their denominators,
producing integer entries of magnitude at most 2ℓ , and comparison coeﬃcients of magnitude at most
H1 = 2ℓ+1 . Remaining coeﬃcients and right sides are 0, ±1. At a vertex in ambient dimension d,
active normals span Rd , even for a lower-dimensional cell: otherwise a small movement in both signs
along an annihilating direction preserves active constraints and the ﬁnite strict inactive inequalities,
contradicting extremality. Select d independent active equations. Hadamard’s bound and Cramer’s
rule bound each coordinate’s numerator and denominator by
                                                dd/2 H1d .
Each needs at most ⌈d(ℓ + 1) + (d/2) log2 d⌉ + 2 bits, including sign. Use d = m and d = N for the
two factors.
    The vertices of a product are products of factor vertices. If the union of all nonempty cell vertices
had only one pair, every nonempty cell would be that singleton, as would its union. Thus whenever
the equilibrium set is nonsingleton, it contains two distinct rational vertex pairs of polynomial size,
even if the initial witnesses were irrational or in a continuum. A veriﬁer checks nonnegative unit
vectors, distinctness, and the exact inequalities
                   (Ay)i ≤ xT Ay     for every i,       (B T x)j ≤ xT By   for every j
for each pair. These are suﬃcient as well as necessary because expected payoﬀs are averages of the
pure payoﬀs.
    Finite-game equilibrium existence [8] makes the complement of nonuniqueness on valid rank-one
inputs exactly uniqueness. Rank is polynomially checkable by exact arithmetic: for C = A + B ̸= 0
choose a nonzero pivot Ci0 j0 , and rank is one exactly when Cij Ci0 j0 = Cij0 Ci0 j for all i, j. The
ordinary complement of Uniqueness1 has a polynomial-time rank-invalid (or malformed) branch
and the two-pair NP certiﬁcate branch. Therefore Uniqueness1 ∈ coNP and Nonuniqueness1 ∈
NP, completing Theorem 1. The supplied-default version uses the same reduction and certiﬁcates.

accompany the supplement: the ﬂow family checks 37 weight vectors, 660 augmentations with
half-ﬂow certiﬁcates, 864 residual cycles in small instances, six original-choice searches, and four

constructs six complete games and checks 204 auxiliary pairs, strict defaults, dual certiﬁcates, and
deliberate penalty/sign/certiﬁcate failures. These ﬁnite checks supplement the proof; they do not
enumerate every equilibrium or prove the all-instance claims. They are separate from the omitted
historical six-game/39-network script references. The original eleven-ﬁle theoretical edition contains
no executable, ﬁxtures, or check logs, and those historical computations were not replayed.
