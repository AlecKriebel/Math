              A supercritical nonuniqueness example on a graph
                   roughly isometric to the square lattice
                                                Alec Kriebel
                                          Independent researcher
                                        ORCID 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                   Abstract
         We give an explicit deterministic graph of maximum degree four, roughly isometric to the
     square lattice, on which iid bond percolation has at least two inﬁnite clusters with probability
     greater than one third at parameter 19/20, while its critical probability is at most 9/10. Edges
     internal to two opposite quadrants are retained; every other lattice edge is replaced by a path of
     length 100. Elementary dual parity and all-distance path bounds prove the assertions without a
     lattice threshold formula or a transitivity assumption. The example refutes the explicitly declared
     all-supercritical interpretation of Benjamini’s 2013 Problem 9.6. The original question omits its
     probability quantiﬁer; no unique author intent, exclusive priority, or current open status is asserted.


1    Statement and the source question
All graphs here are countable, simple, undirected and connected, with graph distance. In Bernoulli
bond percolation every edge is independently retained with the same probability p. Write N∞ for the
number of inﬁnite open components and

                                    pc (G, o) = inf{p : Pp (o ↔ ∞) > 0}.

For a connected graph, positive survival at one vertex implies positive survival at every vertex at the
same p > 0: force open a ﬁxed ﬁnite connecting path. Indeed, conditioning those ﬁnitely many bonds
to be open can only increase any increasing event, by coupling the other bonds identically. Hence
Pp (o ↔ ∞) ≥ p|F | Pp (v ↔ ∞) for such a path F from o to v. We may therefore write pc (G).
    We use precisely the multiplicative-plus-additive rough-isometry convention of [1, Deﬁnition 1.25,
manuscript p. 9]: a map f : V (G) → V (H) and a constant C > 0 satisfy

                      C −1 dH (f (x), f (y)) − C ≤ dG (x, y) ≤ CdH (f (x), f (y)) + C,

and every vertex of H is at distance strictly less than C from the range.
    Problem 9.6 of the 30 October 2013 author manuscript [1, p. 64] asks for uniqueness on bounded-
degree graphs roughly isometric to Zd . Its sentence supplies no p-quantiﬁer. The chapter’s preceding
page explicitly discusses p > pc , while transitivity is a separate hypothesis of nearby theorems. We
address the following declared interpretation: for every such graph and every ﬁxed pc (G) < p ≤ 1,
Pp (N∞ = 1) = 1. A negative example in dimension two refutes this interpretation. It does not refute
a weaker assertion of uniqueness only suﬃciently near one, determine the author’s unexpressed intent,
or require a simultaneous assertion for all p in one coupling.

2    The graph and the exact coarse model
In the nearest-neighbor square lattice let

                   A = {(x, y) : x ≥ 1, y ≥ 1},           B = {(x, y) : x ≤ −1, y ≤ −1}.

Call edges internal to A or internal to B strong; all others are weak. Keep each strong edge, and
replace every weak edge by a path of exactly 100 edges with 99 new internal vertices private to that
path. There are no other edges. Original vertices have degree four; new vertices have degree two. The
resulting graph is inﬁnite, countable, connected, simple and deterministic. The subdivision bound is
global, not a length growing with location.
    Fix original vertices under f : G → Z2 , and send an internal path vertex to its nearer orig-
inal endpoint, choosing ties consistently. Adjacent vertices have equal or neighboring images, so
dZ2 (f (x), f (y)) ≤ dG (x, y). Each vertex is within 50 actual edges of its image, and a lattice geodesic
between original vertices expands to a path at most 100 times as long. Thus, for all vertices,

                        dZ2 (f (x), f (y)) ≤ dG (x, y) ≤ 100dZ2 (f (x), f (y)) + 100.

Surjectivity of f supplies the strict 100-net condition. These inequalities imply the displayed source
convention with the single constant 100, including for chain midpoints.
     At actual bond parameter p, declare a coarse strong edge open when its single bond is open,
and a coarse weak edge open when all its 100 bonds are open. Coarse states are independent with
probabilities p and q = p100 , respectively: their actual bond sets are disjoint. Sharing endpoints creates
no dependence in a bond model. This familiar series reduction is used, for example, in [7, Section 2];
it is not a new probabilistic mechanism.
     Actual and coarse connectivity of original vertices agree. A simple actual path between originals
must traverse every chain it enters completely, since its internal vertices have degree two. Conversely,
expand open coarse edges. An actual component containing no original lies inside one ﬁnite chain. A
component containing ﬁnitely many originals lies in those vertices and their ﬁnitely many incident ﬁnite
chains, and is ﬁnite. Hence every inﬁnite component contains inﬁnitely many originals. Restriction
to originals and expansion therefore give a bijection between actual and coarse inﬁnite components,
even when partial chain branches are present. The inhomogeneous coarse description is exactly the
requested homogeneous iid model on G.

5    Related work, attribution and limits
Bounded subdivision, series reduction, dual parity, path counting and product independence are es-
tablished elementary tools. Benjamini and Schramm [2], Example 1 and Proposition 1, already give
a planar example with a supercritical nonuniqueness regime. It is an enhanced binary tree roughly
isometric to a hyperbolic sector, rather than a Euclidean lattice; its stated planar model is site per-
colation. Their Lemma 1 concerns uniqueness suﬃciently near one. Benjamini–Kozma [3, Theorem
1] give critical nonuniqueness on a subexponential-growth product graph, without the required lattice
rough-isometry assertion.
    Teixeira [4, Theorem 1.4] and Candellero–Teixeira [5, Theorem 1.8] prove high-density unique-
ness under their respective local-isoperimetric/volume or rough-transitivity, growth, isoperimetric and
decoupling assumptions, with site-model distinctions. Hutchcroft–Tointon [6, Theorem 1.8] give a
high-density bond result under a uniform ﬁnite-exhaustion isoperimetric hypothesis, and their Theo-
rem 1.10 has a separate uniform-connection premise. A threshold below one in these theorems is not
an identiﬁcation with pc . The present example is compatible with a weaker near-one question.
    Hilário–Sá–Sanchis–Teixeira [7, Section 2 and Remark 5] explicitly use independent coarse probabil-
ities that are powers of p. Their full-supercritical uniqueness statement concerns an annealed random
stretched-lattice law, using its translation invariance, ergodicity and ﬁnite energy. Those properties
of the averaged law do not assert uniqueness for every deterministic angular stretching pattern. No
comparison theorem is imported into the proof above.
