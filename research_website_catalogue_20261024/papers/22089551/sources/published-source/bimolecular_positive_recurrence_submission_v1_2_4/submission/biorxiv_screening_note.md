# Screening note for a possible bioRxiv submission

**Proposed category:** Systems Biology

This manuscript presents a new theorem about stochastic biochemical reaction
network models. Continuous-time Markov chains with stochastic mass-action
rates are used to represent reaction noise at low molecule counts. Weak
reversibility implies that the population set reachable from every initial
state is a closed communicating class. The paper proves that, for every
positive rate vector, every nonabsorbing such class is positive recurrent when
the finite reaction network also has one linkage class and molecularity at
most two; an absorbing singleton has its point-mass law.

The direct systems-biology relevance is the resulting stationary
molecule-count distribution and the associated long-run state frequencies and
bounded-observable statistics. Finite moments of unbounded molecule counts
require additional integrability and are not claimed. The paper does not
claim that all biochemical networks satisfy its structural assumptions. Its
reaction cycles are abstract network motifs, not experimentally validated
mechanisms, and it makes no organism-specific, clinical, or empirical claim.

The result is analytic. A deterministic verification package checks exact
identities, boundary and closure cases, finite calibration chains, and
reproducibility. These computations are falsification aids; no finite atlas or
random test proves the universal recurrence theorem, enumerates the Foster set,
or certifies a useful bound on it. No empirical biological dataset was
collected or analyzed.

This note states the direct model relevance and limitations for screening. It
does not presume or guarantee that bioRxiv will determine the mathematical
work to be in scope. Nothing has been uploaded or submitted.
