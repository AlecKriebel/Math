                                                  Abstract
         We give a negative answer to the comparison question of Gishboliner and Shapira in Ober-
     wolfach Report 19/2019, Problem 5, and their later Problem 5.6. The fixed property consisting
     of K4 -free graphs that become 3-colorable after deleting at most one vertex has a polynomial
     classical canonical tester, for every input size. In the vertex-distribution-free model, every adap-
     tive two-sided tester has superpolynomial total sample-plus-adjacency cost as the proximity
     parameter tends to zero. The lower bound follows by an elementary tripartite-cover and heavy-
     apex reduction from the established two-sided triangle-testing lower bound. All distances allow
     arbitrary edge additions and deletions. The reduction remains valid when the tester is given the
     vertex set, its size, the full positive rational distribution, and the heavy vertex. A self-contained
     classical sampling argument and exact finite verification materials accompany the note.


1   The question and the fixed property
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Sampling inputs have a nonempty vertex set. For a
probability distribution D on that set, use the unordered-pair normalization

Thus uniform distance is the number of changed edges divided by n2 . We call distance at least ε
ε-far. Strict-threshold conventions give the same conclusions after a fixed proximity rescaling.
    A tester accepts members and rejects ε-far inputs with probability at least 2/3. The VDF
oracle supplies independent samples from the unknown vertex distribution and adjacency answers.
We count their total number, as in the original workshop model [1, p. 1138]. The comparison
question asks whether an extendable hereditary property has the same, or nearly the same, testing
complexity in the VDF and uniform dense models [1, Problem 5], [2, Problem 5.6]. Hereditary
means closed under induced subgraphs; extendable means that every member has a one-vertex
extension which is also a member.
    Let C3 be 3-colorability, let A3 mean that deletion of at most one vertex leaves a 3-colorable
graph, and fix
                                    P = {K4 -free graphs} ∩ A3 .
This property is hereditary, closed under deleting edges, and extendable by adding an isolated
vertex. Moreover, C3 ⊆ P.

Theorem 1. For this single fixed property P:
    1. There is a one-sided classical canonical tester, without knowing n, using O(ε−6 log(1/ε))
       independent uniform samples and O(ε−12 log2 (1/ε)) adjacency queries as ε → 0.

    2. Along a sequence of positive rational parameters ε → 0, every two-sided adaptive VDF tester
       has worst-case total oracle cost exp(Ω(log2 (1/ε))). This holds even with the vertex set, its
       size, the complete positive rational weight vector and the distinguished heavy vertex supplied.
Consequently VDF complexity admits no fixed polynomial bound in the classical canonical complexity
and 1/ε, even after any fixed positive constant rescaling of classical proximity.
    The lower-bound ingredients are established results: polynomial colorability sampling [4, 5], the
hard-triangle constructions and their two-sided extensions [6, 7, 8], and the VDF framework [3, 2].
The reduction below transfers triangle hardness to a property with an easy classical canonical
tester. It supplies the full negative comparison, rather than a new triangle lower bound. Fei’s
recent edge-distribution work [9] also uses classical parity constructions; its default sample oracle
and arbitrary pair weights differ from the present adjacency oracle and vertex-product distance. The
current priority audit, including primary theorem bodies and version histories, identified no earlier
full comparison answer. This is a bounded literature finding: the 2021 journal version-of-record
body of [2] was unavailable, although its arXiv v5, ECCC revision 2 and author manuscripts were
inspected; forward-citation retrieval was incomplete. Detailed coverage and limitations accompany
the package.

3    Transferring triangle hardness
Write T for triangle-freeness.

Lemma 3 (Heavy apex). If H is 3-colorable on n ≥ 1 vertices, add a universal vertex a to form
J(H) and set D(a) = 1/2, D(v) = 1/(2n) on the tail. Then

                                  distD (J(H), P) = 41 distunif (H, T ).

Proof. Delete a minimum triangle-hitting set of tail edges. The resulting tail remains 3-colorable
and triangle-free, so its join with a is K4 -free and belongs to P. Each tail edge costs 1/(4n2 ), giving
the upper bound.
    For any repair F ∈ P, let S be the tail vertices whose edge to a was deleted. Its apex-edge cost
is δa = |S|/(4n); denote its tail-edit cost by δt , including additions. The restriction F [V (H) \ S] is
triangle-free, since a triangle together with a would give a K4 . Use that restriction and isolate S
to repair H. This changes at most 4n2 δt + n|S| edges. Therefore

                              distunif (H, T ) ≤ 4δt + |S|/n = 4(δt + δa ).

Minimizing over all repairs proves the reverse inequality.

Lemma 4 (Tripartite cover). For any graph H on n ≥ 1 vertices, let L(H) have vertex set
{1, 2, 3} × V (H), with (i, u)(j, v) an edge exactly when i ̸= j and uv ∈ E(H). Then L(H) is
3-colorable, H ∈ T implies L(H) ∈ T , and

                               distunif (L(H), T ) ≥ 91 distunif (H, T ).

Proof. The three parts color L(H). A triangle projects to three distinct pairwise adjacent vertices
of H. Triangle-free repairs can be restricted to deletions: intersecting a repair’s edge set with the
original graph preserves triangle-freeness and never increases cost. Take a minimum deletion repair
of L(H), with t deleted edges. Delete uv from H if any of its six cross-copy edges was deleted. This
deletes at most t original edges. Every surviving triangle would lift, one vertex in each part, to a
surviving triangle in the cover, a contradiction. Dividing the two deletion counts by n2 and (3n)2
gives the claim.

    To use a VDF tester at proximity ε, simulate it on (J(L(H)), D). A sample is generated locally
from this explicit distribution. Self-queries return zero, distinct apex–tail pairs are adjacent, and
same-part pairs or copies of the same original vertex are nonadjacent. Every remaining adjacency
uses at most one query to H. All promised extra information is supplied explicitly. Consequently a
VDF tester with total budget Q gives a two-sided adaptive tester for triangle-freeness at proximity
36ε, using at most Q adjacency queries. Samples require no graph queries. If the VDF execution
uses s samples and e adjacency calls, the sampled original indices and query endpoints involve at
most s + 2e ≤ 2Q source vertices. This simulation also works for randomized, history-dependent
choices of queries and for early stopping.
    The established two-sided triangle-testing lower bound is exp(Ω(log2 (1/δ))) in the uniform dense
graph model: see [7, Theorem 4(ii) and §7], including its explicit undirected consequence. That
work gives the vertex count and permits adaptive adjacency queries; its inspected-vertex convention,
with all induced pairs available, changes the present query count by at most a constant factor.
Fixed changes in the unordered/ordered distance normalization also leave this bound unchanged.
Applying it at δ = 36ε to the simulation proves part 2. It suﬀices to retain a rational sequence
of parameters; the weights above are already strictly positive and rational. Combining this lower
bound with the polynomial classical bound proves the final assertion of Theorem 1.


4    Scope and verification
The separation concerns total VDF oracle cost. A sample-only lower bound with unlimited free
arbitrary-name adjacency queries is not asserted. The original proof preserved in the supporting
package also gives an explicit rational hard family and a direct parity/exposure proof, with all its
combinatorial and probabilistic estimates. The shorter proof here imports the established triangle
lower bound and gives the complete reduction instead.
    Independent programs test arbitrary weighted repairs on small graphs, the cover reduction,
coloring lists and finite adaptive queries. These finite checks test the arguments; the universal
claims rest on the proofs and the cited triangle theorem. The original programs and their hashes
accompany the reports. The public checkers raise errors explicitly, and run their checks with Python
optimization enabled as well as in ordinary mode.
