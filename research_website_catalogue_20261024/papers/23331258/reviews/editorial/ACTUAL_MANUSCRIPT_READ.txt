Abstract
We prove that, in a ﬁnite simple undirected graph with unit edge lengths, ﬁnding a minimum
edge set whose deletion increases all three distances between three speciﬁed terminals by at least
one is NP-hard. The budget decision problem is NP-complete even when all deletion costs are one
and all three original terminal distances are three. A direct reduction from Vertex Cover passes
through a tripartite graph and identiﬁes its vertex-cover number with the minimum shortest-
path blocker size. The construction also gives NP-completeness when the terminal distances
must remain ﬁnite, or must increase exactly from three to four. This provides a classiﬁcation of
the undirected triangle question posed by Krause in 2005, without asserting bibliographic ﬁrst
priority.
1 The question and its model
Krause asked for the complexity of Blocking Shortest Paths (BSP) when the demand pairs are the
three pairs among terminals r, s, t [1, pp. 2856–2858]. The same undirected triangle case appears
in his dissertation [ 2, Section 4.3]; directed triangle hardness was already known [ 2, Theorem 9.3].
We classify the undirected problem by a self-contained reduction, using only the classical NP-
completeness of Vertex Cover [ 3] as an external complexity result.
An earlier related construction attaches the three parts of a tripartite Vertex Cover instance to
three vertices on a terminal path [ 4, Section 6]. That construction concerns protected vertices, ver-
tex deletion, one terminal pair and zero/one traversal lengths. We retain the tripartite attachment
mechanism but use three geodesic demands, unit traversal lengths, and unrestricted unit-cost edge
deletion. The no-larger-cover extraction below explicitly accounts for deletion of the middle edges.
All graphs below are ﬁnite, simple and undirected, and every edge has traversal length one.
Write dG(x, y ) for shortest-path length, with dG(x, y ) = ∞ if there is no path. The three terminals
are distinct and pairwise connected initially. A blocker is an edge set F satisfying
dG− F (x, y ) ≥ dG(x, y ) + 1 for {x, y } ∈ {{r, s }, {r, t }, {s, t }}. (1)
Terminal disconnection is allowed: inﬁnity exceeds every ﬁnite integer. Deleting edges cannot
shorten a path, so ( 1) is equivalent to meeting every original shortest path for each demand pair.
All edges, including terminal edges, may be deleted. Deletion costs and traversal lengths are
diﬀerent input attributes.

The unit-cost decision problem supplies a nonnegative, binary-encoded integer budget B and
asks whether |F | ≤ B. An edge-list certiﬁcate and three breadth-ﬁrst searches before and after
deletion verify feasibility in polynomial time. With positive binary-encoded rational deletion costs
and a rational budget, exact addition and comparison have polynomial bit complexity as well. Thus
both decision versions belong to NP.
2 Two cover identities
For a graph J, let τ(J) be its minimum vertex-cover cardinality. The ﬁrst identity is the vertex-cover
form of Poljak’s classical two-subdivision stable-set identity [ 5, p. 308]; we include its proof.
Lemma 1. Let Q = (V, E ) have n vertices and m edges. Replace each edge e with ordered endpoints
(ue, v e) by the path
ue − be − ce − ve,
using two new vertices unique to that edge. The resulting graph H is tripartite with parts A = V ,
B = {be : e ∈ E} and C = {ce : e ∈ E}, and
τ(H) = m + τ(Q).
If m ≥ 1, there is an edge between each pair of parts.
Proof. Given a cover S of Q, add one new vertex per edge: choose ce if only ue is in S, choose be
if only ve is in S, and choose either if both are. The resulting set covers H and has size m + |S|.
Conversely, let X cover H, and put S = X ∩V . Every middle edge requires at least one of be, c e
in X. If neither original endpoint is in S, both new vertices are required. Writing q for the number
of such uncovered Q-edges gives |X| ≥ |S|+ m + q. Adding one endpoint of each uncovered edge
to S produces a cover S′ of Q with
τ(Q) ≤ |S′| ≤ |S|+ q ≤ |X| −m.
These inequalities prove the identity. Each edge gadget contributes one edge of each cross-part
type, establishing the ﬁnal assertion.
Lemma 2. Let H = ( W, J ) be tripartite with a supplied partition W = A ⊔ B ⊔ C, and with at
least one edge of each cross-part type. Add new terminals r, s, t , adjacent respectively to all vertices
in A, B, C , and no other new edges. Call the resulting graph GH . Its three terminal distances are
three, and its minimum unit-cost blocker size satisﬁes
opt(GH ) = τ(H).
Proof. For w ∈ W , let ew be its unique terminal spoke. Terminals are nonadjacent and have disjoint
neighborhoods, ruling out terminal paths of length one or two. A cross-part edge supplies a path
of length three for each terminal pair. Moreover, for the terminals a, b of parts U, V , the complete
family of shortest paths is
a − u − v − b (u ∈ U, v ∈ V, uv ∈ J).
Indeed, any three-edge path must use a spoke at each end and one H-edge between its two interior
vertices.

If X covers H, deleting the spokes ew for w ∈ X meets every such geodesic. Therefore
opt(GH ) ≤ τ(H).
For the reverse inequality, let F be any blocker, allowing deletions of H-edges as well as spokes.
Include w in a set X for every deleted spoke ew, and include one arbitrary endpoint of every
deleted H-edge. Then |X| ≤ |F |. For each uv ∈ J, the corresponding terminal geodesic has edges
eu, uv, e v. At least one is in F , and its contribution to X covers uv. Thus X is a vertex cover and
τ(H) ≤ |F |.
3 The complexity classiﬁcation
Theorem 3. Undirected, unit-length three-terminal BSP is NP-complete in its budget decision
form, even with unit deletion costs and all three original terminal distances equal to three. Exact
optimization is NP-hard. The positive binary-encoded rational-cost decision version is also NP-
complete.
Proof. Take a Vertex Cover instance (Q, k ) with k ≥ 0 and m = |E(Q)| ≥ 1. Construct H and
then GH by the two lemmas, and set B = m + k. They give
τ(Q) ≤ k ⇐ ⇒ τ(H) ≤ m + k ⇐ ⇒ opt(GH ) ≤ B.
The graph has n + 2m + 3 vertices and n + 5m edges. Construction is polynomial in the input
length, and computing m + k takes polynomial bit time. The graph size does not depend on the
magnitude of the binary budget. The hypotheses of Lemma 2 hold because m ≥ 1. In particular
the terminals are pairwise nonadjacent, initially connected, and at distance three.
Edgeless source instances are always yes-instances and can be sent to a ﬁxed yes-instance satis-
fying the restrictions: use a triangle as H, attach one spoke per vertex, and set B = 2 . Thus the
reduction is total. Vertex Cover NP-completeness and the NP-membership argument above prove
the unit-cost claim. Unit costs are permitted positive rational costs, giving hardness of that version
too. An exact optimizer decides the budget problem by comparing its optimum with the budget,
proving optimization hardness.
Corollary 4. The unit-cost decision problem remains NP-complete if all terminal distances must
remain ﬁnite, or must increase exactly from three to four.
Proof. Add to GH a fresh internally vertex-disjoint four-edge path between each terminal pair; the
nine new internal vertices have no other incident edges. A simple terminal path using a new internal
vertex must traverse a whole added path. Thus no new terminal path of length at most three is
created, and the original terminal geodesics are unchanged.
Any blocker in the augmented graph must still hit each original geodesic. Ignoring backup-path
deletions, the extraction in Lemma 2 produces an H-cover of no greater size. Conversely, spoke
deletions for an H-cover leave all three backup paths intact and block every length-three geodesic,
so all terminal distances become exactly four. Both strengthened optima are therefore τ(H), and
the same budget reduction applies. Their additional distance constraints are veriﬁable by BFS,
proving NP membership. This asserts terminal connectivity, not connectivity of every remaining
vertex.

The standard-library Python veriﬁcation checks include arbitrary edge-deletion subsets, cover ex-
traction, tripartite optimum equality, all nonempty labelled source graphs through ﬁve vertices,
binary budgets, and backup-path controls. Integrity mutations are rejected. Finite checks sup-

enumeration or a proof assistant.
The result answers the mathematical question recorded as open in the historical sources. A
bounded current-literature audit is supplied separately. We make no claim that the problem re-
mained open until this work, that our argument is the ﬁrst proof, or that the literature search is
exhaustive. General length-bounded cuts, directed demands and full-disconnection multicuts re-
quire separate model comparisons; their hardness alone does not establish this restricted theorem.
AI tools were used extensively in constructing, verifying, adversarially auditing and preparing
this work. Independent AI-agent review is disclosed as such. This is an unrefereed preprint: it
has not undergone independent human peer review or journal review. No planar, bounded-degree,
weighted-traversal-length or globally connected residual-graph classiﬁcation is claimed.
