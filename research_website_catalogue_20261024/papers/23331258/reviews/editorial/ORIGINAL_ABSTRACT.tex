
We prove that, in a finite simple undirected graph with unit edge lengths,
finding a minimum edge set whose deletion increases all three distances between
three specified terminals by at least one is NP-hard. The budget decision problem
is NP-complete even when all deletion costs are one and all three original
terminal distances are three. A direct reduction from Vertex Cover passes through
a tripartite graph and identifies its vertex-cover number with the minimum
shortest-path blocker size. The construction also gives NP-completeness when
the terminal distances must remain finite, or must increase exactly from three
to four. This provides a classification of the undirected triangle question
posed by Krause in 2005, without asserting bibliographic first priority.
