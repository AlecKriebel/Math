## Exact claim reconstructed from the original

For complete binary physical trees with H>=2, k=2^(H-1) leaves on each side, N=4k-1, there are exactly k^2 cross-root commodities and no others. Unit initial demands traverse their unique physical paths. Every edge e carries k*k_e initially. Strictly positive linear increment prices on fixed topology force the unique cheapest doubled-demand capacities C_e=2*k*k_e. The topology, dense traffic pattern, growing capacities and random law are design choices, not universal-network hypotheses.

Quenched independent Exp(1) slopes X_ij give d_ij(t)=1+(t-1)X_ij for t>=1. The offered slope S_k=sum X_ij is positive almost surely. F_k(t) maximizes the total admitted matrix under entry caps and physical edge capacities. r_k(t)=F'_{k,+}(t)/S_k. The spare-edge graph has all physical vertices and edges whose loads are strictly below capacity for a chosen optimizer; M_k is its largest component. The graph can depend on optimizer inside the window. The outer event quantifies simultaneously over all t in two ranges and every optimizer, whereas the convergence statement uses any measurable optimizer selection.

The theorem states delta_k=8*sqrt(log(k)/k), epsilon_k=2*k^(-31)+2*k^(-2)/(1-k^(-2))^2+(4/k^2)^k. For binary k with delta_k<1, one event of probability at least 1-epsilon_k gives (a) every 1<=t<=2-delta_k: full admission, r=1, whole-tree spare graph and M=N; and (b) every t>=2+delta_k: F=2k^2, r=0, edgeless spare graph and M=1. Hence the total window width is 2 delta_k, and it is a sufficient upper bound. Fixed t<2 and t>2 give the claimed marginal/component limits; throughput divided by k^2 tends to min(t,2), including t=2. No critical r_k(2), critical profile, matching lower width bound, fresh-time resampling, linear time variance, adaptive expansion, cyclic-network theorem or global priority certification is asserted.

## Own deterministic and probability controls

1. **Physical-to-terminal reduction.** A physical edge load is the sum of terminal row loads or terminal column loads below it. Row/column bounds 2k therefore imply all edge bounds 2k*k_e, and terminal edges imply the row/column bounds. There is no overlooked root-throughput inequality once terminal bounds hold.
2. **Capacity optimizer uniqueness.** Unique physical paths force each designed edge lower bound separately; all doubled unit demands attain those bounds simultaneously. Positive prices make any strict excess more expensive. This concerns the capacity design, separately from admitted-flow optimizer multiplicity.
3. **Cut and right derivative.** A source-side cut containing A of left leaves and B of right leaves costs 2k(k-|A|+|B|)+sum_(A x (R\B))d_ij. A finite minimum of these affine functions is concave and nondecreasing. At a tie its right derivative is the minimum active slope. Each slope is a subset sum of the positive X's, so r is in [0,1].
4. **Every-optimizer lower phase.** Entrywise upper bounds force y=d for any optimizer reaching the offered total. Strict offered row/column inequalities force all physical edges strictly below capacity. Endpoint strictness gives a right neighborhood, which is necessary for r(2-delta)=1. Monotonicity in t extends the same event to the entire lower interval.
5. **Every-optimizer upper phase.** Row sums and column sums are each at most 2k. If their total equals 2k^2, every row and column must equal 2k for every optimizer. Every physical edge is a sum of the requisite saturated terminal loads. Monotone entry caps retain a feasible maximizing matrix at every later t, so the throughput and its right derivative remain constant. This does not depend on selecting one particular flow algorithm's output.
6. **Lower probability bound.** At t=2-delta, each offered row/column reaches 2k only if a Gamma(k,1) sum reaches k/(1-delta). Chernoff with I(a)=a-1-log(a) gives exponent >=k*delta^2/2; the 2k union factor gives 2k^-31 at delta_k. Independence between row and column events is not needed.
7. **All-cut upper event.** Deficient cuts need a+d>k with a=|A|, d=|R\B|. Identity ad-k(a+d-k)=(k-a)(k-d)>=0 makes a deficiency imply a Gamma(ad,1) lower tail below ad/(1+delta). Its exponent c*ad has c>=delta^2/8. For q=min(a,d), ell=k-max(a,d), 0<=ell<q. When q<=floor(k/2), ad=q(k-ell)>=qk/2 and counting by 2 binomial(k,q)binomial(k,ell) and summing ell is bounded by 2q*k^(2q). When q>k/2, ad>k^2/4 and at most 4^k cuts suffice. Substituting c>=8 log(k)/k gives the stated geometric-series bound and large-cut remainder. No cut independence is used. Empty/full and a=d ties can be overcounted, which is harmless for this upper bound.
8. **Rate constants.** d/d(delta) I((1-delta)^-1)=delta/(1-delta)^2>=delta; d/d(delta) I((1+delta)^-1)=delta/(1+delta)^2>=delta/4 for 0<delta<1. Integration gives delta^2/2 and delta^2/8. Constants and the total window factor 16 are internally consistent.
9. **Limits.** S_k/k^2 has mean 1 and variance k^-2. On the common event, lower-end throughput is k^2+(1-delta_k)S_k. Monotonicity sandwiches F_k(2) below 2k^2. The proof does not exchange derivatives with limits. Binary-k summability supports the stated eventual almost-sure outer events under any common coupling. For high phase M/N=1/(4k-1), not identically zero at finite k.
