2. Solve and differentiate the scalar comparison. For p=1/2, let w(t)=[K+(sqrt(w0)-K)e^(-t/2)]^2. Set B=K+(sqrt(w0)-K)e^(-t/2)>0. Then w'=2B*(-1/2)(B-K)=K B-B^2=K sqrt(w)-w, and w(0)=w0. Thus the native bound is the exact equality solution, with no missing factor2 or sign. If w0<K^2 it increases toward K^2 from BELOW, proving that replacing min(w0,K^2) by K^2 for all t would already fail at t0. For w0>K^2 it decreases toward K^2; persistence does not assert monotone growth of actual shares. Scaling w0 arbitrarily small shows why an initial-independent positive floor cannot hold for all t>=0 under unrestricted positive initial states.

3. Threshold/control of false extension. At p=1 the comparison equality is w'=(K-1)w and, for0<K<1, w=w0 e^{-(1-K)t}->0. At p>1, w'=w(K w^{p-1}-1)<0 for 0<w<K^{-1/(p-1)}, allowing decay. These are counterexamples to deriving positive persistence from this scalar lower inequality outside p<1, not counterexamples to the actual city equation there. The paper makes no such extension. For p<1, w=0 followed after an arbitrary delay tau by [K(1-e^{-(1-p)(t-tau)})]^{1/(1-p)} is a C1 equality solution with initial0: the boundary scalar equation is not locally Lipschitz. This warns against transferring positive-orthant uniqueness to closed-boundary data; the paper does not do so.

5. Excluded coincidences/zero data. If two sites coincide and positive equal weights are used, their >= winning cells both contain the entire domain up to other competitors; for n=2 both areas equal1, so sum A_i=2 and the asserted simplex argument fails. Pairwise distinct sites prevent this positive-area tie. With z_i=0 and at least one other positive share, i has zero influence almost everywhere and area0 under the natural extension, making its zero face invariant; no positive lower bound can start at that zero. Both are explicitly excluded. A boundary site cannot admit the required full centered positive square inside D; no claim for it follows from this construction.

6. Simplex/area consistency. The Q_i proof uses BOTH z_i<=1 (square inclusion) and every competitor z_j<=1 (dominance). On the arbitrary positive orthant a formula A_i>=K_i z_i^p need not be true: if z_i is so large that K_i z_i^p>1 it contradicts A_i<=1. This does not undermine local regularity on that orthant; it limits the persistence estimate to normalized states, exactly as stated. Positive initial data with mass1 and sum A_i=1 make that domain invariant. Also the disjoint fixed squares imply sum K_i<=1, so summed asymptotic floors are consistent with normalized total mass.

7. Ratio, time, convergence and empirical controls. The dominance test is dist_i/z_i^q<=dist_j/z_j^q; (alpha,beta)=(2,8) and(1/2,2) both give q=1/4, hence identical A and identical A-z at the SAME time, not merely reparametrized curves. This establishes ratio invariance only; deciding which literal source-conjecture clause fails requires the persistence theorem, not an assumption of another conjectured clause. Positive lower/upper bounds alone do not imply convergence (a positive bounded sinusoidal function illustrates that logical implication's failure but is not asserted to solve this ODE). In this model shares and unit-square area fractions are dimensionless, and time is normalized to the relaxation coefficient1; the proof does not calibrate physical area/population units or establish stochastic founding/empirical demographic law. An unnormalized physical domain would require corresponding area/time/share normalization before making dimensional claims.
