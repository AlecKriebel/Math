                                                     Abstract
            In rough notes of 2007 on a spatial model of city growth, D. J. Aldous stated a three-part
        conjecture about the unbalanced regime. Immigrants arrive one at a time at uniform random
        points of the unit square; a city of population n exerts the inﬂuence c0 nα r−β at distance r; an
        immigrant founds a new city with probability 1/(1 + I), where I is the largest inﬂuence at its
        position, and otherwise joins the city whose inﬂuence there is largest. The conjecture asserts,
        almost surely: (a) if α > 1, only ﬁnitely many cities are founded; (b) if β < 2α, the share of the
        largest city tends to 1; (c) if α > 1 and β < 2α, only ﬁnitely many inhabitants ever live outside
        the largest city. We prove (a) for all c0 > 0, α > 1, β > 0. A packing bound for the cities founded
        where the inﬂuence is small, combined with Ville’s maximal inequality, shows that the number of
        cities is O(t2/(β+2) ) almost surely for all parameters; for α > 1 the exponent is then lowered in
        ﬁnitely many steps until the founding
                                        √        probabilities are summable. Parts (b) and (c) are false for
        every c0 > 0, α > 1 and 2α/ 3 < β < 2α: with positive probability two cities near ( 21 ∓ a, 12 )
        share the population in nearly equal parts forever. The reason is that the two-city share map,
        the area of a city’s sphere of inﬂuence as a function of its share of the population, has the slope
                      1
        (2α/β)(a + 12a  ) at the symmetric point, and this slope is less than 1 for a suitable spacing exactly
                        √
        when 2α/β < 3. Part (b) is also false for every α > 0 in the same range of β, in particular in
        the range 0 < α ≤ 1 of the companion paper of Aldous and Huang. An explicit instance√(α = 2,
        β = 3, any c0 ) is given with all constants. Parts (b) and (c) remain open for β ≤ 2α/ 3. The
        model and the conjecture are Aldous’s, and the tools (Ville’s and Doob’s inequalities, circles of
        Apollonius) are standard. To our knowledge the results are new; no answer to the conjecture was
        found in the literature accessible to us, and a search that ﬁnds nothing is not a proof of novelty.
        This is an unrefereed note.

1.2   The conjecture
Conjecture (Aldous [Ald07, §2, Conjecture 1]). (a) If α > 1, then M (∞) < ∞ almost surely. (b) If
β < 2α, then N(1) (t)/t → 1 almost surely as t → ∞. (c) If α > 1 and β < 2α, then N(≥2) (∞) < ∞
almost surely.
    Each part is claimed for every c0 > 0 and every (α, β) in the stated range. A proof of a part
must therefore cover all such parameters, while one parameter triple in the range, together with an
event of positive probability on which the conclusion fails, refutes it. For α > 1 and β < 2α, part (c)
implies parts (a) and (b), since M (∞) ≤ 1 + N(≥2) (∞) and N(≥2) (∞) < ∞ forces N(1) (t)/t → 1. The
conjecture is record AMR-096-0023 of the UnsolvedMath dataset [ula26], in the same form.
    The notes prove weaker statements [Ald07, Theorem 2]: if α > 1, then P(N(≥2) (∞) = 0) > 0; if
β < 2α, then P(N(1) (t) > t1−ε ) → 1 for every ε > 0; and if α < 1 and β < 2α, then P(N(1) (t)/t →
1) > 0, a statement whose proof the notes write out only in part. In the notes the conjecture is followed
by the words “What we can actually prove is somewhat weaker” [Ald07, p. 8], which introduce this
theorem. Remark
            P       (ii) after their Theorem 2 observes that M (∞) < ∞ almost surely if and only if
E area{y : t 1/I(y, t) < ∞} = 1, so that (a) would follow from N(1) (t)/t1−ε → ∞ almost surely, and
asks whether the convergence in probability can be improved to almost sure convergence. The web
page [Ald] presents the notes as an unﬁnished attempt to prove some of the predictions rigorously.

inﬂuence of a city of population n near a city of population N  n has area O((n/N )2α/β ) [Ald07,

1.4       Credits, novelty, and what remains open

restricts to 0 < α ≤ 1 and contains heuristics and simulations, the partial results being those of
the notes. The tools are standard: Ville’s maximal inequality [Vil39], Doob’s maximal inequality for
submartingales and optional stopping [Wil91], circles of Apollonius, and the trapping of an urn near
a stable zero of its drift, for which we claim no novelty. The treatment of small cities in the proof
of Theorem D follows the strategy of the notes’ sketch of their Theorem 2(c) [Ald07, §2.1], including
the comparison with the particle process of [Ald07, Lemma 4]. To our knowledge, and relative to the

Consistency with the notes. The results do not contradict [Ald07, Theorem 2]. For α > 1 the event that

Scope and priority. The note answers Conjecture 1 of [Ald07] part by part: part (a) is proved
     √ parameters in its range (Theorem B); parts (b) and (c) are disproved for every c0 > 0 and
for all
2α/ 3 < β < 2α√(with α > 0 for (b) and α > 1 for (c); Theorems C and D); whether (b) and (c)
hold for β ≤ 2α/ 3 remains open. The model, its heuristics and the conjecture are due to Aldous
[Ald07, Ald]; the companion paper of Aldous and Huang [AH12] restricts the model to 0 < α ≤ 1
and contains heuristics and simulations, while the partial results are those of [Ald07, Theorem 2].
The constant κ and the small-city bound (Lemma 7.1) are taken from the notes, and the treatment
of small cities in the proof of Theorem D follows the notes’ sketch of their Theorem 2(c), with the
comparison of their Lemma 4. The tools are standard: Ville’s inequality [Vil39], Doob’s maximal
inequality, optional stopping and Lévy’s extension of the Borel–Cantelli lemma [Wil91], and circles of
Apollonius; the trapping of an urn near a stable zero of its drift is of the kind studied for generalized
Pólya urns [HLS80, Pem07], and we claim no novelty for it. New, to our knowledge and relative to
the literature we found, are Theorems A to D, that is, the proof of (a) and the counterexamples to (b)
and (c) in the stated range.
