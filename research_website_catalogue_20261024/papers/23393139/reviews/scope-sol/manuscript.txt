             Quantitative persistence in a ﬁxed-city area equation
                                                 Alec Kriebel∗

                                     15 October 2026 (version 1.0.0)



                                                    Abstract
            For ﬁnitely many ﬁxed distinct interior sites in the unit square, let each site’s positive
        population share grow at the rate of the area where its inﬂuence is maximal, minus its current
        share. Inﬂuence is proportional to population to the power α times distance to the power
        −β. We prove that every positive initial probability vector gives a global trajectory and, when
        β > 2α, every share has an explicit positive lower bound for all time. An asymmetric three-site
        instance with α = 2 and β = 8 has all shares between 1/4096 and 2047/2048. This disproves
        the unrestricted α > 1 collapse alternative in the literal 2007 ﬁxed-city conjecture. The result
        is a quantitative, all-site persistence statement; it does not establish convergence or resolve the
        stochastic city-founding model. We credit the original local geometric mechanism and a directly
        overlapping 2026 two-city counterexample analysis. No ﬁrstness claim is made.


1       The equation and the literal conjecture
Let D = [0, 1]2 , let n ≥ 2, and ﬁx distinct x1 , . . . , xn ∈ (0, 1)2 . For α, β > 0 and z ∈ (0, ∞)n , deﬁne

                          Si (z) = {y ∈ D : ziα |y − xi |−β ≥ zjα |y − xj |−β for all j},                      (1)
                         Ai (z) = area Si (z),      żi = Ai (z) − zi .                                        (2)

Conventions at the ﬁnitely many sites and at tied boundaries do not aﬀect the areas. Initial data
lie in the positive probability simplex

These are exactly the ﬁxed-city equations displayed in the 2007 rough notes of Aldous [2, §6.4, p. 40].
The global parameter declaration allows all positive α, β [2, p. 2]. The literal Conjecture 32(a)
predicts that some zi (t) tends to one if α > 1 or 0 < β < 2α, for positions and initial values in
an unspeciﬁed general position. We refute its unrestricted α > 1 alternative, on the open region
α > 1, β > 2α.
   This is a statement about the displayed ﬁnite-dimensional equation. The later Aldous–Huang
paper declares 0 < α ≤ 1, permits stochastic founding of new cities, and does not state this
ﬁxed-city conjecture [3, §2]. Our result does not refute that paper’s model.

Corollary 3. The unrestricted α > 1 collapse alternative in the literal Conjecture 32(a) of [2] is
false. Its counterexamples include every positive initial probability vector and every conﬁguration
of distinct interior sites whenever n ≥ 2, α > 1, and β > 2α.

    This is more than an exceptional symmetric equilibrium: it covers an open class of conﬁgura-
tions, initial conditions in the relative simplex topology, and parameter pairs. The source does not
deﬁne general position. An ordinary genericity condition excluding lower-dimensional or measure-
zero exceptional data cannot eliminate this class.

4    Antecedents, overlap, and limits
The model, conjecture, and local geometric growth mechanism belong to the preceding literature.
In particular, Aldous’s Lemma 3 uses the area exponent 2α/β, and the unﬁnished discussion around
Proposition 30 places a protected disk inside a city’s inﬂuence region and obtains a lower growth
estimate with that exponent [2, pp. 9, 36–37]. That discussion has stochastic and environmental
conditions and explicitly omitted details. We do not present either the exponent or the protected-
neighborhood idea as new. The proof above applies the idea uniformly on the normalized ﬁnite-city
simplex and integrates it to obtain (5).
    The unrefereed note of Ferudun, posted 14 October 2026, directly addresses overlapping ques-
tions [4]. Its Corollary 4.4 and §8 explicitly
                                          √ refute Conjecture 32(a) using open families of two-city
invariant intervals for 1 ≤ p = 2α/β < 3 (on p = 1 with α > 1). Its proved Lemma 4.1(iii) also
gives, for every p > 0, the derivative

Diﬀerentiability gives inward drift at two nearby endpoints, and its Lemma 4.3 preserves those signs

change. We therefore claim no ﬁrst negative answer to the literal conjecture. The present theorem
supplies all-site, all-positive-initial-state quantitative persistence, rather than a local two-city basin
statement.
    Ferudun also records the ratio invariance of the equation. For example, (α, β) = (2, 8) and
(1/2, 2) give the same vector ﬁeld, while the two clauses of the 2007 conjecture predict incompatible
behavior. This diagnoses an inconsistency in their conjunction; by itself it does not identify which
clause fails. Theorem 1 directly rules out collapse without assuming the conjectured convergence
clause.
