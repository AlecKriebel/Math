                                                  Abstract
         The closed set of complex monic quadratics with roots in the closed left half-plane is not prox-
      regular at z 2 . For every ﬁxed positive-deﬁnite real inner product on coeﬃcient space, unstable
      quadratics with multiple attained global nearest stable quadratics occur arbitrarily close to z 2 .
      The conclusion also holds in the full exact-degree quadratic coeﬃcient space. A direct proof
      exhibits a limiting normal whose cubic support dominates a quartic squared displacement. The
      same obstruction disproves set prox-regularity of the whole abscissa epigraph. In contrast, the
      monic quadratic abscissa is function prox-regular at the origin relative to each ﬁnite limiting
      subgradient. Thus the historical question about local unique stable-polynomial projection has a
      negative answer for complex coeﬃcients, while this example does not refute the separate function
      condition.


1    Question, conventions, and result
Lewis [2, §8, pp. 166–168] asks whether the stable monic set is prox-regular, after specifying complex
coeﬃcients and their real Euclidean coeﬃcient metric. The later Oberwolfach question [3, p. 106,
Question 1] asks whether every degree-k polynomial near z k has a unique nearest stable polynomial.
That short question does not specify its coeﬃcient ﬁeld, normalization, or metric. We address
its complex coeﬃcient convention, which is explicit in the earlier set question and in the cited
variational theory [4, 5]. No conclusion for the real-only general question is asserted.
    For pa,b (z) = z 2 + az + b, identify (a, b) ∈ C2 with R4 , in the order (Re a, Im a, Re b, Im b). Let
α(p) be the maximum real part of a root, and set

                                S = {(a, b) : α(pa,b ) ≤ 0},      c0 = (0, 0).

Imaginary-axis roots, including multiple roots, are allowed. For a ﬁxed real symmetric positive-
deﬁnite matrix G, write hu, viG = uT Gv and kuk2G = uT Gu. In the full coeﬃcient space put

               Sfull = {(ℓ, a, b) ∈ C3 : ℓ 6= 0, α(ℓz 2 + az + b) ≤ 0},       cfull = (1, 0, 0).

A nearest point always refers to a global minimum over the stated stable set.

Theorem 1. For every ﬁxed positive-deﬁnite real coeﬃcient inner product, S is not prox-regular at
c0 , and every neighborhood of c0 contains an unstable quadratic with at least two distinct attained
global nearest points in S. The corresponding statements hold for Sfull at cfull . The abscissa epigraph
is not prox-regular as a set at height zero over either base point, for every ﬁxed positive-deﬁnite real
inner product on the respective coeﬃcient/height space.

    The ﬁrst-order polynomial root and normal calculus and the connection between prox-regularity
and projection are classical [4, 2, 6]. The underlying stability-boundary cusp is a positive Whitney-
umbrella geometry; the related real-quartic boundary was studied by Bottema [1] and is reviewed
as a Whitney umbrella by Kirillov–Overton [7]. We give the coeﬃcient normal, metric obstruction,
and global attainment arguments directly. This note makes no assertion of ﬁrstness or of the
question’s present-day openness. The accompanying priority memorandum records the primary
sources checked and the remaining inaccessible bodies. Nearest-stable coeﬃcient approximation
was already studied by Moses–Liu [10] for real disk-stable polynomials, including recognition of
possible global nonuniqueness, and by Orban de Xivry [11, Chapter 4] for complex half-plane
coeﬃcients. Our assertion concerns attained ties in every neighborhood of the speciﬁed quadratic
base, with all ﬁxed coeﬃcient inner products; generic nonconvexity or competing local algorithmic
minima alone do not establish that assertion.


2    A limiting normal that is not proximal
For a locally closed set C in a ﬁnite-dimensional inner product space, a vector v is a proximal normal
at x ∈ C if, for some τ > 0, x is a nearest point to x + τ v; equivalently, a local quadratic upper
support can be used and the normal step made suﬃciently small. Limits of proximal normals at
convergent points are limiting normals. Set prox-regularity at x0 requires a uniform local projection
property near each limiting normal at x0 [6, Deﬁnition 1.1 and Proposition 1.2]. In particular, every
limiting normal at x0 must itself be proximal there. For closed sets in ﬁnite dimension, set prox-
regularity at x0 is equivalent to single-valued nearest projection on some neighborhood of x0 [6,
Theorem 1.3(a),(k)]. These facts apply in any ﬁxed inner product, by a linear isometry to Euclidean
space. Locally closed sets may be clipped inside a closed ball, with the base point in its interior.

    The exact-degree stable set is not globally closed, so attainment requires localization. On
ℓ 6= 0 the abscissa equals α(a/ℓ, b/ℓ) and is continuous. Choose a closed metric ball B of radius
R about cfull with ℓ 6= 0 throughout. Then C = Sfull ∩ B is nonempty and compact. Its local
normals along the path are unchanged, so C fails prox-regularity at the base. For an input q with
kq − cfull kG < R/3, every stable point outside B is more than 2R/3 from q, while the stable base is
less than R/3 away. Boundary points of B cannot minimize either. Thus the global nearest points
in C and Sfull agree. Applying the closed-set projection characterization to C proves the full-degree
assertion of Theorem 1, including global attainment and unstable inputs.

4    Why the function condition is diﬀerent
Function prox-regularity at a point relative to a ﬁnite limiting subgradient v0 localizes the subgra-
dients near v0 as well as the base points and function values. Its epigraph correspondence is at the
normal (v0 , −1) [8, Deﬁnition 1.1 and p. 205]. The preceding horizontal normal does not test that
condition: the unscaled gradients along (1) diverge. The distinction is substantive, as the next
proposition shows.

     Cross [9, Chapter 5] studies the ﬁnite-subgradient function condition and gives a negative
example for a nonlinear two-real-parameter matrix family (Example 5.38). Its coeﬃcient map is
(x1 , x2 ) 7→ (a = 0, b = −x21 +x22 ), whose derivative at the origin vanishes. That example concerns the
parameter metric and does not identify distinct nearest complex coeﬃcient polynomials. His real-
quadratic pointwise proximal-subgradient calculation on pp. 131–133 is also a diﬀerent statement.
Neither such parameter results nor partial smoothness replace the ambient support and attainment
arguments above.


Reproducibility and disclosure
The veriﬁcation package contains this source, a standard-library exact rational veriﬁer for the root,
normal, and support identities, deterministic high-precision boundary controls for Proposition 3,

universal-metric, and global projection conclusions follow from the proofs. No explicit unstable
input together with its two minimizers is claimed, and no all-degree or real-only classiﬁcation is
