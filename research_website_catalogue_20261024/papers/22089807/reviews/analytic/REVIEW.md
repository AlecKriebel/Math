## Claim, assumptions, success criteria, and boundaries

The model is finite, connected, loopless, undirected and weighted, with nonnegative symmetric edge weights and positive weighted degrees. Mutants have fixed fitness `r>1`; residents have fitness one. The initial mutant is uniformly selected over vertices. Bd selects a parent proportionally to fitness and then a replacement target proportionally to its incident edge weight. dB first selects a death target uniformly and then a neighboring parent proportionally to fitness times incoming edge weight. The two comparisons concern these pure processes separately.

The benchmark is the unit complete graph of the **same order**, using the correct distinct finite Bd and dB fixation probabilities. The claim has order

`exists {G_t}, for all fixed r in (1,R_hyb), exists t_0(r), for all t>=t_0(r)`.

Graphs and weights are selected before fitness is quantified. The theorem does not require a graph for every integer population size, eventual amplification at the endpoint, or one size threshold uniform over the entire open interval.

The graph uses `C=t^4`, `q=t`, `m=floor(lambda_* t)`, a unit clique with one hub and `m` unit hub pendants, `q` internally heavy two-vertex pairs of weight `C/sigma_*`, and a common positive dyadic cut from every pair vertex to every clique vertex. Thus `eta=q/C=t^{-3}` is the correction scale. The constants `sigma_*` and `lambda_*` are fixed positive algebraic numbers. Floors change the scaled response by `O(1/t)`, which vanishes. Empty compact selection intervals occur only for finitely many small `t` and are explicitly assigned a positive cut. Every resulting graph is connected. All weights and update probabilities are dimensionless; multiplying all edge weights by the same positive constant leaves either update process unchanged.

Success requires both normalized first-order responses to be positive and all discarded terms to be `o(eta)`, including failure after a mutant center establishes. The following cases are excluded or left unresolved by the statement: neutrality, fitness approaching either endpoint with size, strict amplification exactly at `R_hyb`, singular or size-varying model parameters outside the stated analysis, useful finite-size bounds, nonseparated dynamics, arbitrary alternative modules, and the unrestricted optimum.

3. **Gain-scale center estimates (pp.6–16; Proposition 6, Lemmas 7–11).** This is the most analytically demanding component and was checked separately from response algebra.
