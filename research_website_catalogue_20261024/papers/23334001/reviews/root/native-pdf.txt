                                 in elliptic billiards

                                                      Abstract
          We prove invariant k403,a observed by Reznik, Garcia and Koiller: for every primitive odd-period
      elliptic billiard family with a strict confocal elliptical caustic, the signed areas of the original orbit’s
      origin pedal and origin antipedal have constant product. All admitted coprime star windings are
      included, and every real antipedal intersection is ﬁnite. A complete pole analysis distinguishes
      coincident from opposite complex endpoints. Odd-period arithmetic then places the antipedal area
      and the credited pedal trace in complementary one-dimensional elliptic-function spaces. A direct
      argument covers circles; an exact four-periodic example rules out dropping the odd restriction.

    Reznik, Garcia and Koiller recorded the conjectural product AO A∗O for odd N in their 2020 table;
the same unproved row k403,a appears in the published Table 5 [1, 2]. Both constructions concern the
original orbit. We give a complete proof of that observation in the strict elliptical-caustic setting.

Theorem 1. Fix a noncircular ellipse with semiaxes a > b > 0 and a strictly nested, nondegenerate
confocal elliptical caustic. In any billiard family of least odd period N ≥ 3, the product AO A∗O is phase
independent. Every admitted coprime star winding is included. All consecutive antipedal intersections
are ﬁnite.

    No division by a derived signed area is involved. Reversal negates both areas and preserves their
product. Repeating an admissible cycle r times multiplies the product by r2 ; the hypothesis concerns
the least period, rather than parity manufactured by a repeated list. Hyperbolic or collapsed caustics
are outside the theorem.

Lemma 2. S is even, ℓ-periodic and 2p-antiperiodic. On X it has exactly two simple poles, at p, 3p,
and exactly two simple zeros, at p + ℓ/2, 3p + ℓ/2. Consequently S(w)S(w + ℓ/2) is constant. Every
meromorphic function on X with only possible simple poles at p, 3p and antiperiod 2p is a scalar multiple
of S.

Proof. Since N is odd and gcd(N, τ ) = 1, multiplication by δ/ℓ = 2τ permutes the real subgroup of
order N modulo 2K. Reindexing gives period ℓ and evenness; (4) gives the antiperiod. The simple
poles of dn are 2rK + (2t + 1)p. At w = p only one summand is singular modulo 2K, with residue −i
by (5); it cannot cancel. The antiperiod gives the opposite residue at 3p. These exhaust the quotient
pole classes.
    Negation followed by translation by ℓ + 2p ﬁxes p + ℓ/2, while changing the function’s sign. This
forces a zero; the same argument gives the other displayed zero. They are distinct nonpoles. The trace
is not identically zero since it is positive on the real axis. Equality of total zero and pole orders on a

The odd restriction is substantive. For a2 = 5, b2 = 3, the strict four-periodic
