An eﬀective criterion for prescribed block-code extensions
Alec Kriebel
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
16 October 2026 Version 1.0
Abstract
Given an onto block self-map of a nonempty two-sided irreducible soﬁc shift, we give a ﬁnite,
computable criterion for extending that same map from a containing shift of ﬁnite type into the
original shift. This answers the speciﬁed-map question in Boyle’s Problem 16.3, including its
mixing case. The criterion is locally testable separation of the allowed language from a regular
bad-word ideal. An irreducibility argument converts the published Place–van Rooijen–Zeitoun
separator bound into a single canonical ﬁnite-type approximation test. The result covers every
choice of extension radius and local-rule completion. It uses that published theorem; it does
not assert a new general separation theorem, historical ﬁrstness, or a solution of the separate
existence-over-all-maps and cellular-automaton stability questions.
1 The question and ﬁnite criterion
A shift is a closed, shift-invariant subset of AZ for a ﬁnite alphabet A. Write L(T ) for its ﬁnite-word
language, including ε. A shift of ﬁnite type (SFT) is speciﬁed by ﬁnitely many forbidden words; a
soﬁc shift has a ﬁnite labelled-graph presentation. Irreducibility means that any two allowed words
can be joined by an allowed word. All shifts below are nonempty and two-sided.
Boyle’s Problem 16.3 [1, Section 16] asks when a speciﬁed surjective block code f : T → T on a
mixing soﬁc shift has an extension G : S → T from some SFT S ⊇ T . Containment is non-strict,
and the extension must agree with f on T . This property is called steadiness. Earlier terminology
and a broader soﬁc-homomorphism question occur in Dykstra’s thesis [ 2, Section 3.6, especially
Problem 3.6.8]; Ballier credits Barth–Dykstra [ 3] and develops substantial closing/continuing and
cover-based results [ 4, Sections 2–4]. We retain these attributions.
Present f with memory a, anticipation b and window length ℓ = a + b + 1. Complete its local
rule arbitrarily on the ℓ-words absent from T , obtaining a block map F : AZ → AZ. For a ﬁnite
word w, let F∗(w) be its consecutive local-rule outputs, or ε if |w|< ℓ. Set
L = L(T ), B = {w ∈ A∗ : F∗(w) /∈ L}.
The language B is regular: a ﬁnite automaton stores the most recent ℓ− 1 input letters and feeds
each new output letter into a complete automaton for L. Moreover B is a two-sided ideal. Indeed,
F∗(w) is a factor of F∗(uwv) for a bad word w, and L is factorial. Since F extends f , L ∩ B = ∅.
Choose a ﬁnite monoid M and morphism α : A∗ → M recognizing both L and B. Eﬀectively
one may enumerate the transition monoid of the disjoint union of their complete deterministic
automata. For n ≥ 2 deﬁne
T [n] = {x ∈ AZ : every length- n block of x belongs to Ln(T )}.

This is a canonical SFT containing T .
Theorem 1. Let T be an irreducible soﬁc shift and f : T → T an onto block code. With the data
above, put
k = 4(|M |+ 1), N = max{2, ℓ, k }.
The following conditions are equivalent:
1. f extends to a block map G : S → T for some SFT S ⊇ T ;
2. some locally testable language C satisﬁes L ⊆ C and C ∩ B = ∅;
3. L(T [N ]) ∩ B = ∅;
4. F (T [N ]) = T .
Consequently one ﬁnite-automaton reachability test decides the extension question. On success,
T [N ] and the chosen F are a witness; on failure no diﬀerent completion, radius or containing
alphabet can provide an extension.
Here a language is locally testable if membership is determined by bounded-length factors and
bounded preﬁxes and suﬃxes. The precise bound in the theorem is imported from Place–van
Rooijen–Zeitoun [5, Theorem 4.2]: two regular languages recognized by a common ﬁnite monoid M
are LT-separable if and only if they are LT[k]-separable for k = 4(|M |+ 1). Their LT[k] equivalence
records the set of k-proﬁles, with no multiplicities. A proﬁle consists of at most ⌊k/ 2⌋letters before
a position and at most ⌈k/ 2⌉ letters beginning there, truncated at endpoints [ 5, Sections 2–4]. We
use the published theorem, without reproving its general algebraic argument.
2 Proof
Normalization. Suppose G : S → T is an extension. If its alphabet is larger than A, replace S
by S ∩ AZ. This remains an SFT containing T . For all suﬃciently large n, T [n] ⊆ S: choose n at
least the lengths of a ﬁnite forbidden list for S. Every such forbidden word is absent from T .
Write F and G on one common ﬁnite interval. Their rules agree on every word of that interval
appearing in T . Increasing n beyond its length makes F = G on T [n]. Hence an extension exists
if and only if F (T [n]) ⊆ T for some n ≥ 2. Canonical approximation normalization is also used in
[4, Lemma 2.1]. For every n,
F (T [n]) ⊆ T ⇐ ⇒ L (T [n]) ∩ B = ∅. (1)
A bad input word produces a forbidden output block; conversely any forbidden output block has
a ﬁnite bad input interval. Since T ⊆ T [n] and f (T ) = T , image inclusion in (1) implies equality.
This is the only use of surjectivity.
A witness gives a separator. The n-block overlap graph, with vertices Ln−1(T ) and edges
Ln(T ), is strongly connected by irreducibility. Thus any word of length at least n whose n-factors
are allowed extends in both directions in T [n]. The allowed shorter words are exactly the words
of L of those lengths. Therefore L(T [n]) is locally testable: for long words check the ﬁnite list of
forbidden n-factors, and handle the ﬁnitely many short words separately. A successful canonical
witness supplies condition 2.

Lemma 1 (Saturation). If an LT[k] language contains L and avoids the ideal B, then L(T [n])∩B =
∅ for every n ≥ max{2, k }.
Proof. Suppose a bad word w occurs in T [n]. Irreducibility provides a word U ∈ L, of length at
least n + k, containing every word of Ln(T ): concatenate that ﬁnite list using allowed connecting
words, and extend if necessary. Strong connectivity of the overlap graph supplies connecting words
c, d with
W = U cwdU ∈ L(T [n]).
Since B is an ideal, W ∈ B. Independently, irreducibility of T gives e with V = U eU ∈ L.
Both V and W contain precisely all allowed n-words. They therefore have the same set of
length-k factors, namely Lk(T ): use k ≤ n and extend each allowed k-word to an allowed n-word.
Their preﬁxes and suﬃxes of length k − 1 agree because they both begin and end with U .
Their proﬁle sets agree under the published convention. Full proﬁles are precisely the length- k
factors split after ⌊k/ 2⌋ letters. Proﬁles truncated at either end are determined by the common
preﬁxes and suﬃxes. There is no proﬁle truncated at both ends, since both words have length at
least 2k. An LT[k] separator containing V must consequently contain W , contradicting W ∈ B.
Apply the published LT-separation bound to condition 2 and then Lemma 1 with n = N to
obtain condition 3. Equation (1) and surjectivity give condition 4. Its restriction is the extension
in condition 1. This proves all equivalences.
Eﬀectivity . All constructions use ﬁnite data. Build the overlap graph of T [N ], label each edge
by its appended letter, and take every vertex as both initial and ﬁnal. Its ﬁnite-path language is
exactly L(T [N ]): strong connectivity extends every path bi-inﬁnitely, and every occurring word can
be read after a preceding (N − 1)-block. Product with the automaton for B reduces condition 3 to
reachability of an accepting product state. No empirical word-length or radius cutoﬀ is used. The
monoid and graph can be enormous; no useful complexity improvement or implemented arbitrary-
input solver is claimed.
3 Controls, scope and veriﬁcation
If T is SFT, take S = T . Conversely an automorphism of T is steady only if T is SFT. Its inverse g is
a block map, and the local identity gF = id on T holds on T [n] for large enough n. If F (T [n]) ⊆ T ,
then x = gF (x) ∈ T for every x ∈ T [n], so T = T [n].
For example, the binary even shift (between successive zeros there are evenly many ones) is
mixing soﬁc and not SFT. For any n, choose odd m with m + 2 > n . The periodic point (01m)∞
belongs to T [n] but not T , so its identity has no extension. Onto also matters for the image equality:
a constant code to a ﬁxed point can extend from the full shift while having only a singleton image.
The theorem answers the given-map question 16.3, with irreducibility suﬃcing in place of mix-
ing. It does not decide whether some suitable self-code exists as in Problem 16.2, classify stable
cellular-automaton limit sets, or solve Dykstra’s broader question for arbitrary reducible soﬁc shifts.
