NP-completeness of the initial odd-ear preﬁx
Alec Kriebel
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
22 October 2026
Preprint, version 1.0.0
Abstract
For a ﬁnite 2-vertex-connected graph G, let β(G) be the largest number of consecutive odd
ears at the beginning of an open ear decomposition, counting its initial circuit and its one-edge
ears. We prove that deciding β(G) ≥ t, with t part of the input, is NP-complete even for
simple graphs with a universal vertex whose deletion leaves a connected bipartite graph. This
answers the variable-threshold complexity question posed by Sebő and by Benchetrit–Sebő. The
reduction is from explicitly encoded Set Cover. It uses the classical factor-critical formulation of
β, a cone identity, and a block-loss inequality that permits arbitrary partial blocks. In particular,
it does not assume an optimal subgraph is a union of whole blocks.
1 Question and conventions
All graphs are ﬁnite and undirected. The general input graph is loopless, may have parallel edges,
and is 2-vertex-connected: it has at least three vertices and remains connected after deleting any
one vertex. A circuit is a connected 2-regular subgraph. An open ear decomposition consists of an
initial circuit followed by paths with distinct old endpoints and new internal vertices. Each edge
occurs in exactly one ear, and the ears partition the edge set. Paths of one edge are allowed. An
ear is odd if its number of edges is odd. The initial circuit is also counted as an ear.
For such a graph, β(G) is the maximum length of the initial consecutive odd preﬁx. Once the
ﬁrst even ear occurs, later odd ears do not count. If no odd initial circuit is possible, β(G) = 0 .
Sebő’s Question 4 in the 2014 Oberwolfach report [ 1, printed p. 2914] asks for the complexity of
computing this parameter. Benchetrit–Sebő give these precise open-ear conventions, the factor-
critical interpretation, and the polynomial-computability question [ 2, §§1.1,3.1, Question 3.3].
Theorem 1. The language of pairs (G, t) with G as above, t an integer in binary, and β(G) ≥ t,
is NP-complete. NP-hardness holds on simple 2-vertex-connected graphs with a speciﬁed universal
vertex z such that G − z is connected and bipartite.
Thus an exact polynomial-time algorithm for β would imply P = NP. The threshold varies
with the input. This does not assert hardness at any ﬁxed threshold, and is consistent with the
ﬁxed-parameter result in [ 2]. It concerns neither closed ears nor the total number of odd ears
throughout a decomposition.

2 The cone identity
A graph H is factor-critical if H − v has a perfect matching for every v ∈ V (H). We use the
classical theorem of Lovász: a 2-vertex-connected graph is factor-critical if and only if it has an
all-odd open ear decomposition [ 3, 2]. This is an established structural input, not a new result of
this note.
Lemma 2. For a 2-vertex-connected graph G,
β(G) = max
H
(
|E(H)| − |V (H)| + 1
)
, (1)
where H ranges over 2-vertex-connected factor-critical subgraphs of G, with value zero if the family
is empty. The maximum can be taken over induced subgraphs.
Proof. The union of an all-odd preﬁx is 2-vertex-connected and factor-critical by the classical
theorem. Each ear contributes one to |E| − |V | + 1, including the initial circuit. This proves one
inequality.
Conversely, an eligible H has an all-odd open decomposition by the same theorem. It extends
to G: if a component of G − V (H) had only one distinct neighbor in H, that neighbor would be
a cut vertex of G. Hence any such component has two distinct old neighbors; a path through the
component between them supplies an open ear. Repeat until all vertices are present, then add
all unused edges as one-edge ears. This also proves extension starting from any later intermediate
union. The resulting decomposition of G begins with all the ears of H.
For a ﬁxed vertex set, adding its missing edges preserves both factor-criticality and 2-vertex-
connectivity and can only increase the quantity in (1). Thus induced H suﬃce.
For a graph B, its cone z ∗ B adds one new vertex z and one edge from z to every vertex of B.
Lemma 3. If B is connected and bipartite with at least two vertices, then G = z ∗ B is 2-vertex-
connected, and
β(G) = max { |E(B[S])| : S ̸= ∅, B [S] is connected and has a perfect matching }. (2)
Proof. Deleting z leaves connected B; deleting any other vertex leaves connectivity through z. A
2-vertex-connected factor-critical graph cannot be bipartite. Indeed its order is odd, its bipartition
classes are nonempty, and deleting a vertex from the smaller class leaves unequal class sizes, which
precludes a perfect matching. Thus every eligible H in (1) contains z. Make it induced and write
H = z ∗ B[S].
Deleting z shows that 2-vertex-connectivity of H requires B[S] connected, and factor-criticality
requires a perfect matching of B[S]. Conversely, if B[S] is connected with a perfect matching M ,
then |S| ≥ 2, and the cone is 2-vertex-connected. Deleting z uses M . After deleting u ∈ S, remove
its matching edge uv and match v to z instead. This proves factor-criticality. Finally,
|E(H)| − |V (H)| + 1 =
(
|E(B[S])| + |S|
)
− (|S| + 1) + 1 = |E(B[S])|.
Both directions give (2). Since B contains an edge, the feasible family is nonempty.
3 The reduction
We reduce from Set Cover, whose NP-completeness is classical [ 4]. An instance explicitly lists a
universe U = {1, . . . , m}, sets A1, . . . , An, and a budget s. It asks whether at most s sets cover U .

Preprocessing. Budgets below zero give a NO instance. With a nonnegative budget an empty

Proposition 4. The Set Cover instance is YES if and only if β(G) ≥ F .
Proof. Suppose ﬁrst that a cover of size at most s exists. Delete redundant sets to obtain an

an element of Aj belonging to no other selected set. Otherwise Aj could be deleted. These private

Encoding size. Let L be the length of the explicit Set Cover input. After the union test,
m, n, T = O(L), and hence k = O(L). The construction lists O(L2) vertices and O(L3) edges;
the binary threshold F has polynomial bit length. Renumbering the elements is polynomial. No
succinct multiplicities, weights, implicit graph, or parallel edges are used. Proposition 4 proves
NP-hardness.
Membership in NP . For t > 0, a certiﬁcate supplies a subgraph H ⊆ G and a perfect matching
of H − v for each v ∈ V (H). Check that |V (H)| ≥ 3, that H and every H − v are connected,
that all matching edges belong to the required deletion graph and cover every remaining vertex
exactly once, and that |E(H)| − |V (H)| + 1 ≥ t. These are polynomial checks with a polynomial
certiﬁcate. Lemma 2 proves the required equivalence. For t ≤ 0 the answer is immediate on valid
inputs. Invalid input graphs may ﬁrst be rejected in polynomial time. This completes the proof of
Theorem 1.

4 Relation to earlier work and veriﬁcation
The cone identity optimizes the number of all induced edges on a connected perfectly matchable
vertex set. These two qualiﬁcations matter. Sharifov’s polynomial algorithm [ 5] permits discon-
nected perfectly matchable selections. For example, take three K3,3 blocks and two left connectors,
one joining the distinguished root right vertex to each of the other blocks. All block vertices alone
give 27 induced edges and a perfect matching, but three components. The connected optimum is
25: retaining all three blocks requires both connectors, whose matching balance loses at least six
block edges and adds at most four connector edges. The bound is attained by omitting one left
vertex in each nonroot block and matching the connectors there. If a block retains no internal edge,
at most 27 − 9 + 4 = 22 edges remain; with fewer than two connectors one cannot connect all three
retained blocks. Thus connectivity cannot simply be dropped.
Weighted Connected Matching [ 6] instead maximizes the weight of the matching edges ; it does
not maximize the number of all edges induced by the endpoints. Its related hardness theorem is
therefore credited without identifying the two objectives. Constrained ear decompositions [ 7] and
joins in matroids [ 8] likewise concern diﬀerent constraints or total-parity parameters. The new
ingredient here is the explicit Set Cover reduction for (2), including the partial-block inequality
(6).
