## Plain-language explanation

Some groups can be understood through the geometry of spaces assembled from squares and cubes. The group acts by moving the pieces while preserving distances. A particularly useful arrangement has nonpositive curvature and repeats in a controlled way, so a finite piece describes the entire infinite space.

This paper gives an explicit construction for one group in the AIM problem list. Its finite model has one vertex, seven edges, eight squares and two three-dimensional cubes. The paper specifies how their faces are joined, checks the geometry near the vertex and identifies the resulting fundamental group. Taking the universal cover produces the infinite CAT(0) cube complex on which the group acts freely, properly and cocompactly.

The compact description is the interesting feature: the group’s infinite geometry is encoded by this small set of cells and their face identifications. “Two cubes” counts the three-dimensional cells; it does not mean two ordinary solid cubes with every boundary point kept distinct. The construction allows boundary identifications in the finite quotient.

## Exact result and context

The automorphism is `a↦a`, `b↦ab`, `c↦bcb` on the free group with generators `a,b,c`, and the mapping-torus convention is `tut⁻¹=φ(u)`. In particular, the final `b` in `bcb` is positive. This is the formulation in the archived AIM Question 5.2, attributed to Rylee Lyman. The official workshop summary discusses a related formula with `b↦aba`; the paper keeps the two formulas distinct.

The contribution is an explicit small finite model with all attaching maps and group identifications supplied. The paper also explains how earlier WPD, Morse and cubical-completion results provide another route to qualitative existence. It makes no historical-firstness claim, no claim that the dimension or cell counts are minimal, and no virtual-specialness claim.
