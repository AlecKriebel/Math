## Exact claim and success criterion

For a **fixed finite** collection of species, fixed positive masses `m_a` and real charges `e_a`, put

`f_a(t,x,v) = m_a^3 F_a(t,x,m_a v)`, `q(v)=sqrt(1+|v|^2)`, `u=v/q`, and `lambda_a=e_a/m_a`.

The system is Vlasov transport with force `lambda_a(E+u cross B)`, Maxwell current `sum_a e_a integral u f_a dv`, and charge density `sum_a e_a integral f_a dv`. The initial densities are nonnegative smooth compact phase-space data. The fields are smooth with every spatial derivative bounded, belong to `L^2`, and obey the Gauss constraints. The claimed conclusion is a unique global classical solution, smooth on every finite time interval, with compact particle phase support on each such interval and fields in `C_t L^2`.

There is no smallness, neutrality or same-sign charge hypothesis. The theorem does not cover zero masses, infinitely many species, noncompact momentum tails, or arbitrary finite-energy fields without the stated smoothness and bounded-derivative assumptions. Its constants may depend on the fixed masses, charges, species count, data and finite horizon. Uniformity as a mass tends to zero is not claimed.

A successful complete verification would require both (i) the species-dependent modifications and continuation argument and (ii) all analytic premises of the imported signed-impulse/dyadic chain. Passing finite algebra tests is sufficient for neither the analytic estimates nor global existence.

## Directly reconstructed ingredients

### Normalization, signs and positive budgets

The Jacobian `dp=m_a^3 dv` gives the stated number-density normalization. A receiver of species `a` interacting with a field generated by species `b` carries the coefficient

`c_ab = e_a e_b / m_a`,

not `lambda_a lambda_b`. The source-acceleration coefficient is `c_ab lambda_b = e_a e_b^2/(m_a m_b)`. Distinguishing the field's source coefficient from the receiver's acceleration is necessary when masses differ. These coefficients were independently tested, including a negative control that detects the incorrect extra source inverse mass.

The phase transport is divergence-free because `u=grad_v q` and `div_v(u cross B)=0`. It preserves positivity, particle number and each species' supremum norm. The speed is strictly below one, so the particle spatial support lies in `|x| <= R_0+t`.

The conserved positive energy is

`H = integral (|E|^2+|B|^2)/2 dx + sum_a m_a integral q(v) f_a dx dv`.

The particle work factor is `m_a lambda_a=e_a`, which cancels Maxwell work irrespective of charge signs. The outgoing cone flux has electromagnetic part

`(|E+n cross B|^2 + |n dot B|^2)/2`

and matter part `sum_a m_a integral q(1-n dot u) f_a dv`, both nonnegative. Thus opposite charges do not destroy the budgets. Conversely, the signed charge density must not be treated as a positive budget. For fixed positive masses, `f_* = sum_b f_b` inherits a positive energy bound, but generally **does not satisfy one common Vlasov equation**. The transfer uses labelled characteristics and finite sums. A negative control detects the false common-force interpretation.

### Retarded measures and pair identity

Write `a=u(V_a(t))`, `u=u(V_b(s))`, `t=s+r`, `r n=X_a(t)-Y_b(s)`, and

`d=1-n dot u`, `D=1-n dot a`, `eta=1-a dot u`.

On a compact classical interval these divisors are positive. Differentiation gives `dt/ds=d/D` and `r'=d/D-1`. The source-label-to-cone Jacobian is `d`; the resulting combined measure is `D dt f_b dy dv`. An exact six-dimensional rank-one determinant control verified this Jacobian independently.

Let `Q=D I+n tensor a`, `H=Q/D`, `g=(u-n)/d`, and `k=u-eta n/D`. Then `Hg=k/d`. The converted electromagnetic kernel decomposes as

`-(k/(r d))' + eta/(r^2 D^2) [n/(q_X^2 D)-a] + n(a_t dot k)/(r D^2)`.

Here `a_t=(I-a tensor a) K_a/q_X`; `K_a` is already the receiver momentum force, so another factor `lambda_a` must not be inserted. The source-acceleration terms cancel before taking absolute values. This is a same-pair identity, not cancellation between positive and negative species.

The identity was checked by independently differentiating the primitive with exact forward-mode differentiation, inserting arbitrary rational cone directions and physically correct source and receiver accelerations. It was compared against the assembled Liénard–Wiechert force, including conversion by `d/D` and the magnetic contribution. This is materially different from accepting the supplied fixed-cone polynomial certificates. A zero-velocity control detects omission of the transport term.

The null set of source labels colliding with a receiver can be removed on compact prefixes: a time mesh of order `zeta^-1` and spatial collision volumes of order `zeta^3`, together with bounded momentum support and density, give an `O(zeta^2)` label bound. Compact-prefix constants used for this justification must not subsequently be called uniform a priori constants; the inspected proof separates these roles.

### Conditional analytic chain and the circularity attack

The manuscript explicitly imports the analytic structure of pinned family 362, Sections 4–8, rather than treating the one-species global conclusion as if it automatically implied the genuinely different multispecies case. The following reconstruction checks the transfer and logical ordering; it is not an independent complete proof of every imported analytic estimate.

On a compact prefix and fixed horizon take a common dyadic momentum ceiling `P`, `L=log(2+P)`, and `S(w)=P^2 L/w`. The simultaneous signed bootstrap is

`|Delta V_a| <= M P sqrt(I) + A I S(w)`.

The baseline scale is

`B(I,w)=P sqrt(I)+(M+sqrt(A)) I S(w)`.

The square root on `A` matters. Baseline absolute force control has an additional `sqrt(w)` loss; the final signed estimate does not follow by simply replacing signed integrals with absolute integrals.

Source labels of all species can be placed in a disjoint union. The same geometric streaming law then permits common partitions and crossing estimates, while species-dependent force and charge factors remain fixed constants. The stability time depends on `M`, `A`, the relative angular scale and `min(p,w)`. A first-exit argument bounds velocity changes before that time. Transverse monotonicity then controls the length of a label's visit to a tube. Energy allocation is performed in jointly disjoint source-momentum/angular bins; using an independent full energy budget for each bin would be invalid. No such repeated full-budget argument was identified in the inspected transfer.

The suspected dependency loop is addressed in the stated order: baseline occupation and signed bootstrap first give direction-change counts; enhanced occupation is then deduced from those counts. It is not necessary to assume enhanced occupation to prove the counts. Direction changes are tested by an endpoint-zero signed chord weight, not by a priori bounded absolute total variation. Derivatives of its direction projector retain the full force contribution. Receiver error terms are absorbed using the baseline absolute estimate and the angular gain.

Source and receiver direction partitions are combined through their common refinement, whose number of intervals is bounded by the sum of boundary counts. A product of counts would lose the estimate. Retarded hits that straddle a temporal partition boundary are confined to an `O(h)` source-time interval; the argument does not presume the time Jacobian `d/D` is uniformly comparable to one. On good pieces transverse speed and separation give the sharper crossing bound.

The resulting enhanced-occupation constant initially depends on `M,A`. A positive power of `P` absorbs that constant and the logarithmic loss into the threshold `P_0(M,A)` before the final closure constants are chosen. The explicit absorption exponent in the checked arithmetic is `56/3125`. It would be circular to use a hidden `M,A`-dependent final coefficient in the later choice of `M,A`; the inspected parameter order avoids that particular problem.

The selection test uses the minimum of positive occupation majorants, and the same selected pair multiplier is retained throughout the integration by parts. The exact exponent implication checked in the supplied and independent controls has incompatible bounds

`H/z > 62/125`, `H/z < 37487/93500`,

with positive difference `8889/93500`. This verifies the numerical contradiction **given its analytic and exponent premises**; it does not establish those premises by computation. The selected receiver multiplier sum is bounded by `C/sqrt(w)`, which compensates the baseline `sqrt(w)` absolute-force loss. Cutoff derivatives, neighboring bins and endpoint weights remain essential terms. Partition cancellation applies to the same pair and physical multiplier, not across different species kernels.

### Endpoint restoration, removal of the bootstrap and doubling

The final weighted signed estimate has coefficient `C_eta` multiplying `B(I,w)`. Removing endpoint cutoffs uses signed increments and gives

`|Delta V| <= (C_eta+2 M sqrt(eta)) P sqrt(I)`

`             + [C_eta(M+sqrt(A))+2 A eta] I S(w)`.

Choose `eta` first with `2 sqrt(eta),2 eta <=1/8`, then `M >= max(32,8 C_eta)`, then `A >= max(1,16 C_eta M,256 C_eta^2)`, and only then the necessary momentum threshold. These inequalities imply the strict half-bootstrap improvement. The independent controls check these algebraic choices over several scales of `C_eta`.

On each compact prefix the actual maximum force is finite. This fact gives short-interval bootstrap validity and openness after a strict improvement; it is not used as a uniform global force bound. Closedness follows from continuity and truncating intervals. Long intervals whose right-hand side already dominates the bounded endpoint momenta are trivial. Thus, conditional on the uniform analytic estimate, this topological removal is not itself a circular global-existence argument.

Let `Q(t)` be the maximum normalized particle energy over all species and initial-support labels. At its first hit of `2^n`, choose a maximizing species and label and its last preceding hit of `2^(n-1)`. This remains valid when the species realizing the global maximum changes. Since `q` is one-Lipschitz, the signed estimate yields a crossing-time lower bound `c/log(2+1024*2^n)`. The harmonic divergence excludes infinitely many doublings in finite time. The argument also treats the empty-particle case separately as vacuum Maxwell evolution.

The extension from Sobolev fields to the stated smooth bounded-derivative `L^2` fields is substantive and survived inspection. For compact smooth signed density, its Coulomb field lies in every `H^k`: at low Fourier frequency the multiplier is `O(|xi|^-1)`, square integrable in dimension three even with nonzero total charge. Subtracting it leaves a divergence-free electric field. The divergence-free potential `(integral_0^1 t G(tx) dt) cross x` has curl `G`; cutting it off preserves constraints and the fields on a prescribed ball. Vacuum Maxwell evolution of the field difference vanishes in the particle region by finite propagation if the ball radius exceeds `R_0+2T`. Adding or subtracting that vacuum field transfers the solution and momentum criterion on the chosen finite horizon. No global charge-neutrality assumption is needed for this localization.

Uniqueness in the stated classical class follows from particle transport difference energy plus Maxwell difference energy, with a common compact momentum radius and bounded density gradients on the interval. Spatial cutoffs justify the field energy calculation without silently assuming global `L^2` field derivatives.
