## 1. Fixed-algebra involutive deformation

Let C^n be the Banach space of all bounded complex n-linear A-valued maps, with the standard Hochschild differential d and Z^n=ker d. Actual-image H^3=0 makes d:C^2/Z^2→Z^3 a bounded Banach-space bijection. Actual-image H^2=0 makes d:C^1→Z^2 onto. Open mapping therefore gives K,L≥1 with distance(f,Z^2)≤K||df|| and a primitive h of any z∈Z^2 satisfying ||h||≤L||z||. Strictly increasing the quotient constants permits representative choices without attainment; zero right sides are solved exactly. No linear bounded right inverse is asserted.

Put R_nf(a_1,...,a_n)=f(a_n^*,...,a_1^*)^*. Term reversal gives dR_n=(-1)^(n-1)R_(n+1)d: left and right terms exchange, and the merge numbered j corresponds to the merge numbered n+1-j. Multiplying R_n by (-1)^((n-1)(n-2)/2) gives S_n and dS_n=S_(n+1)d. These are conjugate-linear isometric involutions on the complex cochain space. For symmetric Δ, average a cocycle approximation z with S_2z; then average its chosen primitive h with S_1h. Both norm bounds persist and h is complex-linear and star preserving. Conjugate-linearity of S does not obstruct real averaging by 1/2.

Associativity of μ=m+Δ gives dΔ=Δ(Δ(x,y),z)-Δ(x,Δ(y,z)), hence ||dΔ||≤2||Δ||². With dh=z, g=id+h and b=g^-1, direct expansion using gb=id gives

The ordered products g_(n-1)...g_0 converge in norm, lie within exp(1/4)-1<1/2 of id, and conjugate the original product to m.
