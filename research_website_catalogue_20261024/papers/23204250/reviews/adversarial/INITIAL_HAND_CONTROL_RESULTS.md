1. **A tight cut at n4.** For U={1}, A={{1,2},{3,4}} has one crossing; slack0 and shifted slack1/2 atρ1/2. For U={1,2,3} the same A again has one crossing. At n6,U={1,2,3}, M={{1,4},{2,5},{3,6}} has three crossings and slack2; M={{1,4},{2,3},{5,6}} has one crossing and slack0. All odd cuts cross oddly because |U|=2·(internal matched edges)+crossings.

2. **Shift factors and endpoint.** For any real PSD F,G, tr(diag(F,1/2)diag(G,1))=tr(FG)+1/2; both blocks remain PSD and order isr+1. Atρ1 take diagonal factors indexed by unordered edges: F_U(e,e)=1 ife crossesU and0 otherwise, G_M(e,e)=1 ife∈M. Their trace is|M∩δ(U)|=A_n(1), orderbinom(n,2). Atρ>1 an extra scalar blockρ−1 gives polynomial order as well. The0<ρ<1 restriction is material; no endpoint extrapolation is valid.

3. **Absorbing the subtraction.** If x=2^(γn/2)≥2 then2^(γn)−1=x²−1≥x. Thus γn≥2 is a sufficient threshold for the original last inequality. It does not hold for arbitrary very small positive γn without increasing the threshold.

4. **Why common-support compression matters.** With B₁=diag(1,0), B₂=diag(2,0), J=diag(3,0), the matrices A_k=diag(1,k) have evaluations(1,2) while traceA_k→∞. Uncompressed trace boundedness is false. Compression to W=span e₁ gives A_k|_W=1, compact. Separately, the PSD projection M↦(M₁₁,M₁₂) is not closed: [[ε,1],[1,1/ε]] is PSD and maps to(ε,1)→(0,1), but a PSD matrix with zero first diagonal must have zero first row. The proof must use the special nonnegative finite evaluation cone, not a blanket closed-image assertion.
