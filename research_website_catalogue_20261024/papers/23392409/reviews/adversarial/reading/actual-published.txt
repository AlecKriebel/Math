Five outgoing centers in a 16-realization cube move
Alec Kriebel ∗
15 October 2026
Unrefereed research note
Abstract
We specify six algebraic boundary points, an incoming center, and at least ﬁve distinct outgo-
ing centers for a cube move of 16-realizations. Existence follows from strict rational sign bounds
for two continuous one-variable residuals. The boundary parameters are uniquely deﬁned real
roots of monotone polynomials. This contradicts the at-most-three expectation for construc-
tion curves sharing a focus in Remark 4.8 of Melotti–Ramassamy–Thévenin, in the realization
setting allowing crossings. The ﬁxed boundary is crossed, so the example concerns no proper
embedding. Neither the exact number of centers for this boundary nor a universal maximum is
determined.
1 Statement and setting
A quadrilateral P QRS is an α-quad when
|P − Q|α + |R − S|α = |P − S|α + |Q − R|α.
We use the realization setting of [ 1, Deﬁnitions 2.1, 2.4–2.5]: crossed faces are permitted. For ﬁxed
distinct A1, . . . , A6, an incoming center Z must make
A1A2A3Z, A 3A4A5Z, A 5A6A1Z
α-quads; an outgoing center X must make
A2A3A4X, A 4A5A6X, A 6A1A2X
α-quads. Both alternating boundary triples must be noncollinear.
Theorem 1. There exist six ﬁxed distinct boundary points, with both alternating triples non-
collinear, that admit an incoming 16-realization and at least ﬁve distinct outgoing 16-realization
centers. Each selected center is distinct from every boundary point.
The construction also gives at least ﬁve intersections of two construction curves with a common
focus. Thus it contradicts the α > 1 expectation in [ 1, Remark 4.8, p. 811], and the maximum-
three conjecture in the crossing-permitted setting of [ 2, p. 1785]. The boundary itself has a strict
crossing, as veriﬁed below. The result makes no claim about proper embeddings, the exact total
count, a universal maximum, or α < 1.

5 Exact veriﬁcation and scope
The support ﬁle verify_five_centers.py uses only Python’s standard library. All validity de-
cisions use integers and Fraction; no ﬂoating-point value decides a sign. For q ≥ 0, it bounds
q1/8 between consecutive points k/1036 and (k + 1)/1036, found by binary search and checked by
integer eighth powers. Ordinary outward rational interval operations then establish the displayed
bounds. The three boundary roots use 128 exact bisections from [−8, 8], with strict endpoint signs.
Dependence lost in repeated interval occurrences only enlarges an enclosure; the signs remain strict
for the same ﬁxed algebraic boundary.
The ﬁle exact_certificate.json records the full rational enclosures. A default veriﬁer run
recomputes and compares it without writing. Each of ﬁve sign brackets is reﬁned by 100 sign-
preserving bisections, retaining ﬁve disjoint zero brackets without assuming uniqueness. The re-
sulting center boxes and the whole incoming box avoid every line through a boundary pair. Internal
check counts are implementation bookkeeping, not counts of independent mathematical proposi-
tions.
To see concretely why this is not an embedding example, let or(P, Q, R) = det( Q − P, R − P ).
The exact certiﬁcate proves the sign pattern
or(A1, A2, A3) or( A1, A2, A4) or( A3, A4, A1) or( A3, A4, A2)
sign − + + −
so the ﬁxed boundary segments [A1, A2] and [A3, A4] cross in their interiors. No central-point choice
can make that ﬁxed drawing a proper embedding. Exact total root counts and a global maximum
remain undetermined, and no conclusion for α < 1 is drawn.
Earlier weighted-distance families and priority . The curves themselves belong to earlier
weighted-distance families. Ash–Bolker [ 3] study costs φ(|x − P |) + w(P ), including φ(t) = t4;
their separate oscillatory example φ(t) = t + k sin t is not a pure power and does not itself settle
the present count. Bourne–Peletier–Roper [ 4, Deﬁnition 4.12 in the author manuscript] explicitly
deﬁne p-power cells by comparison of |x − xi|p − wi; Bourne–Roper [ 5, Section 5, p. 2568] repeat
this family. With p = 16 and weights wA = 0 , wB = λ, wC = 1 , equality of the three costs is
exactly ( 2). No new family of curves is claimed here.
A bounded search of primary texts and public records completed on 15 October 2026 located
no earlier pure-power shared-focus counterexample with at least four intersections in the checked
corpus. That bounded negative ﬁnding does not establish historical priority. No ﬁrstness, indepen-
dence from unknown work, or contemporaneous public-awareness claim is made.
