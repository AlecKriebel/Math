A seven-hyperplane counterexample to a generic-initial degree
bound
Alec Kriebel
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
Research note, 15 October 2026
Abstract
For the characteristic-zero arrangement deﬁned by Q = xy(x − y)(x − 2y)zw(x + z + w),
the degree-reverse-lexicographic generic initial ideal of the ambient Jacobian ideal has pure-
second-variable cutoﬀ 8 and a degree- 7 minimal generator whose largest variable is the third.
This explicitly contradicts the second-variable bound of Bigatti–Palezzato–Torielli, also stated
in OWR Report 5/2021. We prove the generic sectional dimensions on a nonempty open set of
compatible ﬂags, using scalar polar syzygies and Hilbert–Burch, and give exact rational checks.
The arrangement is a classical parallel connection; the methods and the valid three-variable
arrangement theorem are prior work. Historical novelty and independent discovery are not
established. This unrefereed note was developed with extensive AI assistance and has not
undergone conventional human peer review.
1 Statement and conventions
Let K be any ﬁeld of characteristic zero. Use the order x1 > x 2 > x 3 > x 4 for degree reverse
lexicographic generic initial ideals. For an arrangement A with Jacobian ideal J, put
B = gin revlex(J), p = min{d ≥ 0 : xd
2 ∈ B}.
Conjecture 5.7 of Bigatti–Palezzato–Torielli [ 1] asks that a minimal generator of B involving x3
have degree at least p. Their notation is d0 = min {d : xd+1
2 ∈ B}, so p = d0 + 1. The same
second-variable statement appears as Conjecture 7 on printed page 234 of OWR Report 5/2021
[2], and as Conjecture 7.9 in [ 3, 4]. A ﬁrst-variable exponent in an imported problem record was
a transcription error; correcting it is source normalization, not a mathematical contribution.
Theorem 1. In S = K[x, y, z, w], let
Q = xy(x − y)(x − 2y)zw(x + z + w), J = (Qx, Qy, Qz, Qw).
For B = gin revlex(J) ⊂ K[x1, x2, x3, x4], one has p = 8 , and B has a degree- 7 minimal generator
in K[x1, x2, x3] divisible by x3. In particular its largest variable is x3 and its degree is strictly less
than p.
The seven factors are distinct, and the coordinate factors make the central arrangement essen-
tial. Euler’s identity xQx + yQy + zQz + wQw = 7 Q shows that adjoining Q does not change J.
The four polars have degree 6.

The arrangement itself is a classical construction: it is the parallel connection along the shared
factor x of the rank-two four-element pencil {x, y, x−y, x−2y} and the rank-three four-element cir-
cuit {x, z, w, x+z +w}. Their matroids are U2,4 and U3,4. See Falk–Proudfoot [ 6, §4, Theorem 4.1]
for the realization of parallel connections by identifying shared coordinates. No new-arrangement
claim is made here.
Marchesi–Palezzato–Torielli prove the conjecture for arrangements in K3 [5, Theorem 4.7]. Our
ternary section retains four ambient polars . It is not the three-generated Jacobian ideal obtained
by ﬁrst restricting Q and then diﬀerentiating in three variables. Theorem 1 therefore respects that
result.

This dimension is 21 − 24 + 3 = 0 at k = 6 and 24 − 24 + 1 = 1 at k = 7. There are no elements of B
in degrees below 6, since taking an initial ideal preserves the graded Hilbert function. A degree- 6
minimal generator of B3 divisible by x3 would, after division by x3, give a standard monomial in
the ﬁrst kernel. Thus no generator of B3 involving x3 has degree at most 6.
The second kernel is nonzero and is spanned by killed standard monomials. Choose a degree- 6
standard monomial m with x3m ∈ B3, and let v be a minimal generator dividing x3m. If x3 ∤ v,
then v | m, which contradicts standardness. Since v involves x3 and has degree at most 7, the
preceding paragraph forces deg v = 7. It is also minimal in B, because a divisor of a monomial in
the ﬁrst three variables cannot involve x4. This proves Theorem 1.
Equation ( 8) is the classical sectional-matrix triangle calculation; compare [ 1, Theorem 6.6].
We use it inside B3 and then prove minimality in B by divisibility. A row for the third truncation
does not detect arbitrary full-ring generators involving later variables. No full generic initial ideal
or particular exponent vector for v is needed.

6 Prior overlap, priority limits, and disclosure
An applicable prior family theorem deserves explicit credit. For this arrangement there is one
codimension-two ﬂat of multiplicity four, with three hyperplanes outside the pencil. Migliore–
Nagel [ 12, §8.2, Theorem 8.11 and Corollary 8.12] give
0 − →S(−7)3 ⊕ S(−9) − →S(−6)5 − →J top − →0,
where J top is the intersection of the codimension-two primary components of J. This is substantive
prior information about this family. It concerns a ﬁve-generated ideal, whereas J has four minimal
ambient polars. Their §3 also stresses the distinction between restricting an ambient Jacobian
ideal and taking the Jacobian ideal of a restricted arrangement. The proof above concerns the
successive sections of J.
Historical priority remains unresolved. A bounded literature audit completed on 15 October
2026 did not identify an earlier complete predecessor in its inspected corpus. In particular, the
full text of Ilardi’s 2018 article [ 13] was not obtained: publisher retrieval failed and institutional
access was restricted. Its abstract does not exclude a relevant theorem or family. No assertion of
ﬁrstness, independent discovery, or present openness follows from the accessible literature. The
audit did not exhaust parameter families, related nine-factor generic initial ideals, or historical
code, and it did not authenticate the candidate’s ﬁrst public disclosure timestamp. The supported
outcome here is an explicit counterexample with a checkable proof. We make no claim of smallest
hyperplane count, a full generic initial ideal, or a positive-characteristic extension.
Extensive AI tools were used in solving, drafting, literature inspection, exact-veriﬁcation de-
velopment, and adversarial checking. The artifact has received multiple AI-assisted mathematical
checks but no conventional human peer review and is unrefereed. Those checks are not evidence of
historical priority or a substitute for such review. All cited construction and method credits apply
regardless of the eventual priority determination.
