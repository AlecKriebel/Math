# Independent proof and adversarial boundary derivations

This is a derivation of the literal original theorem, separate from the finite rational certificates. Classical graded Hilbert–Burch and generic-initial/strong-stability/revlex-section facts are used in their standard stated settings. Their critical interfaces were checked in actual primary texts after the early independence seal; no original general theorem is claimed newly proved here.

## 1. Arrangement and target ideal

Use the ordered ambient rows

`e_x, e_y, e_x-e_y, e_x-2e_y, e_z, e_w, e_x+e_z+e_w`.

Over every characteristic-zero field these are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional. The coordinate rows have rank4, so the seven central hyperplanes form an essential arrangement. The defining polynomial Q has degree7, and Euler gives sum_j x_j Q_j=7Q. Since7 is invertible, adjoining Q to the four derivative generators does not change J. This is the unsaturated ambient Jacobian used by the target conjecture, not its saturation or codimension-two part.

For an injective section C of dimension r=2 or3, denote coordinate restrictions by X,Y,Z,W and the seven factor restrictions by L_i. Retain all four polars f_j=Q_j composed with C, obtained by differentiating before restriction. The derivatives of Q composed with C instead consist of only r chain-rule linear combinations of these four polars, so those ideals cannot be silently identified.

## 2. All degree-zero and degree-one syzygies on the stated open set

Assume X,Y independent, all L_i nonzero and pairwise nonproportional; for r=3 additionally assume X,Z,W independent. Product differentiation gives

`sum_j u_j f_j = sum_i (a_i.u) product_{h!=i} L_h`.

Suppose the left side vanishes. Modulo any L_i, the ring is a polynomial ring in r-1 variables over K, hence a domain. Every other L_h has a nonzero image. Their product is nonzero, so L_i divides a_i.u. For degree-one coefficients u_j, this is exactly a_i.u=lambda_i L_i for some scalar lambda_i. Substitution back into the identity gives `(sum_i lambda_i) product_i L_i=0`, so sum_i lambda_i=0. Conversely these scalar equations give a syzygy. There are no missing nonconstant quotients, because both relevant forms have degree one.

For degree-zero u, divisibility forces a_i.u=0 for all i. The coordinate rows then give u=0. Thus the four f_j are linearly independent and minimally generate in degree6.

The first three factors X,Y,X-Y force the same scalar on the first two coefficient coordinates; X-2Y has that scalar too. Write it a and the remaining scalars b,c,d. The complete system becomes

`u=(aX,aY,bZ,cW)`,

`(a-d)X+(b-d)Z+(c-d)W=0`,

`4a+b+c+d=0`.

For r=3, independence of X,Z,W forces a=b=c=d, and then7a=0 forces all zero. Hence no linear syzygy exists. Degree6 has dimension28-4=24 and degree7 has dimension36-12=24; degree5 has no ideal element and dimension21.

For r=2, pairwise nonproportionality gives rank2 for X,Z,W. Its relation space has dimension1; choose nonzero rho with rho_x X+rho_z Z+rho_w W=0. Every solution of the vector equation is

`a=d+t rho_x, b=d+t rho_z, c=d+t rho_w`,

`7d+t(4rho_x+rho_z+rho_w)=0`.

This single equation has rank one even if its t coefficient vanishes. The parameter line maps injectively to u: if u=0 then a=b=c=0 since X,Y,Z,W are nonzero; the vector equation then gives d(X+Z+W)=0. The last factor is nonzero, so d=0, and nonzero rho then gives t=0. Therefore the binary linear syzygy space has exactly dimension1 throughout the open set.

## 3. Binary height and Hilbert–Burch degrees

After extension to an algebraic closure, suppose the four binary polars vanish at a point of the projective line. Restricted Euler forces product L_i=0. At a projective point at most one of the distinct binary factors can vanish. If L_i is that factor, the ambient gradient becomes the nonzero row a_i times product_{h!=i} L_h, which is nonzero. Contradiction. Thus the homogeneous binary ideal has no projective zero, is height2, and its quotient is Artinian. This conclusion descends to the original field by extension of scalars.

A height-two ideal in K[s,t] minimally generated by four degree6 elements has a graded Hilbert–Burch resolution with three syzygy degrees6+e_j, e_j>=1. The numerator of the quotient Hilbert series is

`N(t)=1-4t^6+sum_{j=1}^3 t^(6+e_j)`.

Since the quotient has finite length, N is divisible by (1-t)^2. Its derivative at1 gives `-24+18+sum e_j=0`, so sum e_j=6. The unique linear syzygy means exactly one e_j=1; the other two are at least2 and sum5. Hence their multiset is(1,2,3), with

`HS=(1-4t^6+t^7+t^8+t^9)/(1-t)^2`,

`H2(6)=3, H2(7)=1, H2(8)=0`.

This is a structural conclusion throughout the open set. It is not inferred just from ranks at a chosen flag.

## 4. Compatible flags and genericity over every field in scope

The ternary section `(X,Y,Z,W)=(s,t,u,s+2t+3u)` and binary restriction u=2s+3t have binary factor rows

`(1,0),(0,1),(1,-1),(1,-2),(2,3),(7,11),(10,14)`.

All21 pair determinants are nonzero integers, and X,Z,W in the ternary section are independent. The given C0 is invertible with determinant1; its first two columns give the binary section, while its first three span the ternary subspace W=X+2Y+3Z. Its parametrization of that plane is a different basis from the displayed raw ternary coordinates, which does not change the section Hilbert function.

Independence, nonvanishing and nonproportionality are rank/minor conditions, so they define a nonempty Zariski-open subset O of GL4. The preceding scalar and Artinian arguments prove the same stated dimensions for *every* point of O. Characteristic-zero generic-initial theory supplies a nonempty Zariski-open subset U on which the initial ideal is the strongly stable gin B. GL4 is irreducible: its coordinate ring is the domain K[c_ij,1/det]. Thus O intersects U in a nonempty open set.

No algebraic closure is silently required here. The data and opens are defined over K. An infinite field's rational points are Zariski dense in affine space: a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on all K^m, proved by induction on m using the univariate root bound. Therefore any nonempty open subset of GL4 defined over K has a K-rational point. Characteristic-zero fields are infinite. If the coordinate action is expressed by an inverse/transposed matrix convention, inversion/transposition is an automorphism of GL4 and merely pulls back these nonempty opens. The compatible-flag argument remains valid.

In degree reverse lexicographic order with x1>x2>x3>x4, the classical section identity identifies these generic Hilbert functions with those of K[x1,...,xr]/B_r, B_r=B intersect K[x1,...,xr]. The identity is order-specific. It also follows from the homogeneous revlex fact `in(I+(last variables))=in(I)+(last variables)` in generic coordinates; modding out the last variables of a monomial ideal leaves precisely its first-variable truncation. We do not assert this identity for arbitrary term orders.

## 5. Cutoff8 and a minimal full-ring degree7 generator

Strong stability implies that if x2^k belongs to B, every binary monomial of degree k belongs to B, by repeatedly replacing x2 by x1. Since H2(7)=1, x2^7 is absent. Since H2(8)=0, every degree8 binary monomial, including x2^8, is present. Any earlier pure power would multiply to x2^7 and contradict its absence. Thus p=8 exactly.

Set A3=K[x1,x2,x3]/B3. The cokernel of multiplication by x3 in degree k is the degree-k part of K[x1,x2]/B2. Rank-nullity therefore gives

`dim ker[x3:(A3)_(k-1)->(A3)_k]=H3(k-1)-H3(k)+H2(k)`.

At k=6 this is21-24+3=0; at k=7 it is24-24+1=1. There are no ideal elements in degrees below6 because the original homogeneous J is generated in degree6 and taking an initial ideal preserves degree-by-degree dimensions.

A degree6 minimal generator of B3 involving x3, divided by x3, would be a standard degree5 monomial killed by x3, contrary to the zero first kernel. No such generator exists below degree7.

For a monomial quotient, multiplication by x3 sends each standard monomial either to a distinct standard monomial or to zero. Thus the nonzero second kernel contains a standard degree6 monomial m with x3m in B3. Choose a minimal generator v of B3 dividing x3m. If v did not involve x3, it would divide m, contradiction. Since v involves x3 and has degree at most7, the previous paragraph forces degree7. It is minimal in the full ideal B as well: every divisor of a monomial in the first three variables still lies in those variables and cannot acquire x4. This completes the literal counterexample theorem.

This last argument is deliberately inside B3. It does not claim that a third-row sectional calculation detects arbitrary full-ring generators containing later variables. No full gin, particular exponent vector, smallest count or positive-characteristic extension is needed.

## 7. Adversarial controls and field limits

The four restricted ambient sextics have degree-six span rank4, while differentiating the restricted ternary product gives rank3. Their quotient dimensions already differ in degree6, so the valid three-variable arrangement theorem cannot be applied to this four-generator section as if it were the ternary Jacobian.

The raw displayed ternary coordinate section has no s^6 coefficient in any of its four sextics, and hence no s^6 in its ordinary initial ideal at degree6, despite its favorable generic sectional Hilbert values. A nonzero strongly stable gin with degree-six elements must contain its first-variable sixth power. This is an exact negative control to inferring gin genericity from favorable section ranks alone.

With the excluded ternary section W=2s+3u, X,Z,W become dependent and the degree7 rank drops from12 to11. At the separate binary edge section W=(11/2)s+9t, Z=2s+3t, every factor remains distinct and the relation rho=(1,-6,2) has4rho_x+rho_z+rho_w=0. The trace equation still has rank1, and an actual nonzero linear syzygy exists, matching the proof's edge case.

In characteristic7, Euler gives a nonzero linear polar syzygy because7Q=0. Exact modular elimination at the original ternary flag gives rank11 in degree7, confirming failure of the characteristic-zero syzygy conclusion there. In characteristic2, x-2y=x, so even the seven-distinct-hyperplane premise fails. These are excluded-case controls, not counterexamples to the literal theorem.

## 8. Construction and top-dimensional-part predecessor

Exact ambient-row flat closure gives16 codimension-two flats: one on the four pencil factors and15 double flats. Exact circuit enumeration agrees with the parallel-connection formula: four three-element circuits of U2,4, the four-element U3,4 circuit, and the three unions of circuits through the shared x after removing x.
