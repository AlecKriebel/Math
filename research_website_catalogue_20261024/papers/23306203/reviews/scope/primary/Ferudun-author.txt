                                    Elliptic Billiard Polygons

                                                       Abstract
              For a billiard with a ﬁxed nondegenerate confocal elliptic caustic, form the outer polygon by
          intersecting consecutive tangents to the boundary ellipse at its orbit vertices. If the billiard has
          least period divisible by four, the product of distances from either original focus to the outer
          vertices is independent of phase. We give an explicit positive formula valid for every admissible
          coprime star winding and for repeated traversals of such primitive cycles. The proof factors the
          squared focal distance, uses a Jacobi quarter-period product, and cancels the divisor of a cyclic
          elliptic-function denominator with multiplicities. An exact repeated triangle shows that a listed
          length divisible by four alone does not suﬃce. The result addresses the genuine-period interpre-
          tation of the source-listed invariant k115 ; no unqualiﬁed resolution of an ambiguous repetition
          convention or absolute-priority claim is made.

                                               T (u) = (−A sn u, B cn u).

6    Attribution and reproducible checks
The canonical parameter and quarter-period method are established background [3], and the Jacobi
identities and divisor data are standard [4]. None is claimed as new. For n = 4, the focal product
4a2 b2 is already implicit in the author’s retained four-periodic outer-rectangle calculation for the
related invariant k806,b (AMR-050-0048). The present argument treats every admitted least period
divisible by four. A bounded review of relevant sources did not identify a direct all-period proof of
k115 , but does not establish absolute novelty or priority.
     The accompanying standard-library Python checker veriﬁes nine exact polynomial identities, a
primitive eight-periodic geometric ﬁxture with a2 = 40, b2 = 15, λ = 24/7 and focal product
2000000/3, and the repeated-triangle product arithmetic. The triangle’s reﬂection and tangency
proof is given above. A separate optional 85-digit numerical calculation checks direct tangent in-
tersections, reﬂections and products in 39 families at four phases per family, with admitted periods
4, 8, 12, 16, 20. These checks are regression evidence, not a substitute for the analytic proof.
     AI assistance was used in developing the argument, checking algebra, reviewing sources and
preparing this manuscript. The author is responsible for its claims and presentation. This is an
unrefereed preprint; no independent human review or formal proof-assistant veriﬁcation is claimed.
