## Claims assessed and necessary boundaries

Theorem 1.1 classifies a source-relative full-dimensional regular stochastic containment for **binary, LSA-rootable, already-simple fixed mixed graphs of level at most two, strongly tree-child under every admissible rooting of that same one-step reticulation-preserving semi-deorientation**. The observation is the exact four-state Jukes–Cantor independent displayed-switching site-pattern distribution, with uniform root and every physical Fourier multiplier and inheritance probability strictly in `(0,1)`. This is not the multispecies-coalescent model.

Within that class, containment holds precisely when the labelled reduced bridge trees agree and corresponding nontrivial blobs are isomorphic or related by ordinary triangle redirection. The symmetric relation follows from the same condition. Corollary 1.2 identifies the topology modulo that move outside a proper algebraic exceptional set. Neither theorem claims identifiability of the original physical bridge multipliers, nor equality or density of complete stochastic images under triangle redirection.

Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 9.2 give triangle-free weakly-but-not-strongly tree-child pairs for every `n ≥ 4`, with distinct labelled topologies and a common regular relatively open germ of full dimension `2n+1`. Theorem 9.3 gives a different triangle-containing family of dimension `2n`. These show that extending the theorem to the whole weak class fails. They do not say every non-strong topology is ambiguous, or that strong tree-childness is necessary for every identifiable individual topology.

Boundary multipliers 0 or 1, zero or unit inheritance, unrestricted cleanup/refinement fibers, arbitrary substitution models, unlabelled comparisons, singular exceptional distributions and finite-sample statistical recovery are outside the asserted classification. The algorithm counts algebraic operations on an explicit `4^n` table; it is not a polynomial-in-`n` or uniform bit-complexity claim.
