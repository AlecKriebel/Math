                  A simple brace outside asymmetric products
                              of two trivial braces
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                   17 October 2026         Version 1.0.0

                                                   Abstract
          We give a negative answer to Cedó’s Problem 9 in Oberwolfach Report 51/2019. An explicit
      left brace of order 20 000 is nontrivial and simple, has metabelian multiplicative group with
      abelian Sylow subgroups, and is not an asymmetric product of two trivial braces. The obstruc-
      tion is intrinsic: every such two-factor product has zero triple star products within each additive
      Sylow subbrace, whereas the constructed brace has (x∗x)∗x = x3 6= 0 in its 5-primary subbrace.
      The obstruction covers the full admissible symmetric-cocycle deﬁnition, not only a chosen bi-
      linear construction family. All operations and the simplicity argument are given directly; exact
      veriﬁcation programs and independent adversarial audits accompany the note.


1    The question
A left brace (B, +, ◦) has an abelian additive group, a multiplicative group, and the compatibility
                                      a ◦ (b + c) = a ◦ b − a + a ◦ c.
Write λa (b) = −a + a ◦ b and a ∗ b = λa (b) − b. The maps λa are additive automorphisms and
satisfy λa◦b = λa λb . The brace is trivial when a ∗ b = 0 for all a, b. An ideal is an additive subgroup
invariant under all λa and normal in the multiplicative group; a nonzero brace is simple if its only
ideals are 0 and itself.
    Cedó asks whether every ﬁnite nontrivial simple left brace with a metabelian multiplicative
group and abelian multiplicative Sylow subgroups is an asymmetric product of two trivial braces
[2, p. 3228, Problem 9]. We retain all these hypotheses.
Theorem 1. There is a nontrivial simple left brace B of order 20 000 with
                       ∼ C4 × C5 ,
                (B, +) =                (B, ◦) =∼ (C3 ⋊τ C2 ) × (C4 ⋊M C5 ),
                                  5      2                   5                 2

where τ negates the ﬁrst and third coordinates of F35 and M is the ﬁve-cycle on the even-weight
subspace of F52 . Its multiplicative group is metabelian with every Sylow subgroup abelian. No brace
isomorphic to B is an asymmetric product of two trivial braces.


2    An invariant of every two-trivial-factor product
We use the full asymmetric-product deﬁnition of Catino–Colazzo–Stefanelli, in its left-brace formu-
lation [3, 4, 6]. With trivial factors T, S, an admissible presentation has operations
                                 (t, s) + (u, v) = (t + u, s + v + b(t, u)),                                (1)
                                  (t, s) ◦ (u, v) = (t + αs (u), s + v),                                    (2)


                                                      1
where α : S → Aut(T, +) is a group homomorphism. Initially b : T × T → S is only a normalized
symmetric 2-cocycle: b(t, 0) = 0, b(t, u) = b(u, t), and

                             b(t + u, w) + b(t, u) = b(t, u + w) + b(u, w).

Admissibility means that these operations deﬁne a left brace, as in the cited general deﬁnition.
Lemma 2. If a ﬁnite brace has a presentation (1)–(2) with T, S trivial and all its multiplicative
Sylow subgroups abelian, then
                                       (a ∗ b) ∗ c = 0
for all a, b, c in any one additive Sylow subbrace.
Proof. The cocycle identity gives

                             (t, s) − (u, v) = (t − u, s − v − b(t − u, u)),

and therefore, before assuming biadditivity,

                                 λ(t,s) (u, v) = (αs (u), v − b(αs (u), t)).

Since (t, 0) ◦ (u, 0) = (t + u, 0), lambda composition evaluated on (w, 0) forces b(w, t + u) = b(w, t) +
b(w, u). Symmetry makes b biadditive.
    For a prime p, write Tp , Sp for the primary subgroups of the ﬁnite abelian factor groups. Mixed-
primary values of b vanish, and b(Tp , Tp ) ⊆ Sp : each value is killed by the relevant prime powers.
Consequently the set P = Tp ×Sp is an additive subgroup of the full p-part of the order. Its additive
law need not split, especially for p = 2; its order suﬃces. The action preserves Tp , so the same set
is also a multiplicative Sylow subgroup.
    Commuting (t, 0) and (0, s) inside this abelian multiplicative Sylow subgroup gives αs (t) = t
for s ∈ Sp , t ∈ Tp . For a = (t, s) and b0 = (u, v) in P , twisted additive subtraction now gives

                           a ∗ b0 = (u, v − b(u, t)) − (u, v) = (0, −b(u, t)).

Every (0, w) with w ∈ Sp has identity lambda action on P . Thus (a ∗ b0 ) ∗ c = 0 for every c ∈ P .

   The lemma does not require simplicity or metabelianity. Brace isomorphisms preserve additive
Sylow subgroups and both star operations, so a nonzero primary triple star excludes every possible
presentation, including one with diﬀerent factors or coordinates.

Proposition 3. These operations make B a left brace with the two groups stated in Theorem 1.

Proof. Write L(r) = l1 x + l2 x2 + l3 x3 + sx4 and k = (l1 , l2 , l3 ). The map

                                      Ψ(r, v, z) = ((k, z + Q(v)), (v, s))

is a bijection onto (F35 ⋊τ C2 ) × (V ⋊M C5 ): recover r with E, and then z. For a second input with
corresponding coordinates h, f, w, t, (3) gives the output logarithm L(r) + σ e(a) L(u). Its ﬁrst three
coeﬃcients are k + τ e(a) h and its fourth is s + t. Equation (4) gives

              z + Z + β(v, M s w) + Q(v + M s w) = z + Q(v) + Z + Q(w) = e(a) + f.

Thus the complete transformed product is exactly ((k + τ e(a) h, e(a) + f ), (v + M s w, s + t)). This
proves the group law, including associativity and inverses.

4    Simplicity and exclusion
Proposition 4. Every nonzero ideal of B is B.

Proof. For an ideal I, i ∈ I and g ∈ B, both λg (i) − i and i ∗ g lie in I. The second inference
follows in the quotient brace B/I, where i becomes zero; it is not an assumption about arbitrary
left ideals.
    If a nonzero element (r, v, z) ∈ I has r 6= 0, its additive sixfold multiple is (r, 0, 0) 6= 0. Let cxj
be the lowest nonzero term of r. For j < 4, formula (6) gives

                                 (λ(x4−j ,0,0) − id)(r, 0, 0) = (cx4 , 0, 0).

For j = 4 this already holds without applying a lambda diﬀerence. Additive rescaling gives a =
(x4 , 0, 0) ∈ I.
     Otherwise I has a nonzero element (0, v, z). If v = 0 it is b = (0, 0, 1). If v 6= 0, choose w with
β(w, v) = 1. Then (λ(0,w,0) − id)(0, v, z) = b. Thus every nonzero ideal contains a or b.
     From b one obtains b ∗ (x, 0, 0) = (3x, 0, 0) and hence a nonzero ring element, which gives a.
Conversely, η(x4 ) = 1 implies

                                    a ∗ (0, w, 0) = (0, (M − id)w, 0).

Surjectivity of M − id puts every pure V vector into I, and a binary lambda diﬀerence gives b.
Thus either starting point gives all of P2 = {0} × V × F2 and also (x, 0, 0). For j = 1, 2, 3,

                                  (λ(x,0,0) − id)(xj , 0, 0) = (xj+1 , 0, 0).

These monomials generate P5 = R × {0} × {0} additively. Hence I contains both primary parts
and equals B.

   On P5 , star multiplication is ordinary ring multiplication. In particular x ∗ x = x2 6= 0, so the
brace is nontrivial, and
                                        (x ∗ x) ∗ x = x3 6= 0.
Lemma 2 rules out every asymmetric product of two trivial braces. Together with Propositions 3
and 4, this proves Theorem 1 and gives the full negative answer to the original question.


5    Prior work, veriﬁcation, and review status
Asymmetric and matched products, radical-ring braces, truncated logarithm/exponential identities
and cyclic orthogonal actions are established methods [3, 4, 5, 6]. General matched-product formu-
las [1] recover (5), but their existence statement does not replace the all-ideal simplicity proof. No
new general construction formalism is claimed. Recent work also uses local commutative algebras
and invariant homomorphisms [7]; those ingredients themselves are not a novelty claim.
    A literature audit on 17 October 2026 found no prior full answer or veriﬁed published full
implication for this target. This is a bounded ﬁnding, not historical-priority certiﬁcation. It
included the current arXiv version of [8] and the recent constructions [9, 10]. The full bodies
of [11, 12] and several ﬁnal journal versions, including that of [8], were unavailable; their broader
consequences are not excluded by abstracts. The companion audit records exact versions, inspection
boundaries, access failures and citation-coverage limits.
