                                                 Abstract
          We compute the intersection of the closed Nash sets of the complete family of prime divisors
      obtained by n successive free point blowups over (C2 , 0), after ﬁxing a preﬁx of k centers, where
      2 ≤ k < n. In suitable coordinates the intersection is {ordt x ≥ dn/ke, ordt y ≥ n}. For ﬁve
      blowups, ﬁxing two or three centers gives the thresholds (3, 5) or (2, 5). Neither is q(1, j) for
      positive integers q, j. These examples disprove the speciﬁed-reference-multiple assertion in Con-
      jecture 1 of Fernández de Bobadilla, Pe Pereira and Popescu-Pampu, under each contact and
      indexing convention explicitly treated here. The source’s bare equivalence symbol is undeﬁned
      and its indexing is inconsistent; no unique author-intended correction is asserted. The intersec-
      tions are themselves maximal divisorial sets for other toric divisors. Thus the result does not
      disprove an assertion allowing an arbitrary divisor, or solve the generalized Nash problem.

2    The entire free family
The source explicitly allows free as well as satellite prime divisors; the free/satellite sequence with
proximity data encodes combinatorial type [1, pp. 5–7]. A point is free if it lies on the smooth
locus of the exceptional divisor. No restriction to ﬁnal divisors of minimal embedded resolutions
of singular branches is imposed. Smooth curvettas and the extra free blowups after resolving them
are included.

Lemma 2. Every member of Fn,k , and no additional divisor, is described by

         P (x) = ak xk + · · · + an−1 xn−1 ,         y = P (x) + xn w,     (ak , . . . , an−1 ) ∈ Cn−k .   (3)

Thus this is the full family, not a selected subfamily.

Proof. After j blowups along y = 0, the ﬁnite chart is y = xj uj . For j ≥ 2, the newest exceptional
divisor has equation x = 0; its sole intersection with older exceptional components is the point
uj = ∞. Blowing up a ﬁnite point uj = a gives the two charts

                                uj = a + xuj+1 ,         x = vh,   uj = a + h.                             (4)

In the second chart the new component is h = 0, the old component is v = 0, and on the over-
lap uj+1 = 1/v. The one point omitted from the ﬁrst chart is therefore precisely the satellite
intersection, not an omitted free choice.
    Starting with the ﬁxed preﬁx, repeated substitution of the ﬁrst formula in (4) gives exactly (3).
Conversely every ﬁnite coeﬃcient tuple chooses only free points. Diﬀerent tuples ﬁrst diﬀering in
one coeﬃcient choose a diﬀerent center before creation of the ﬁnal divisor, and hence a diﬀerent
ﬁnal divisor.
    For completeness, the minimal sequence for one ﬁnal prime divisor is its chain of predecessors.
A center on an older component away from the newest component would make the intervening

proximity data, and therefore the speciﬁed all-free type. This excludes other apparent completions.
Its weighted graph is the chain with n − 1 weights −2 and ﬁnal weight −1. The initial ﬁxed tangent
allows x to be the parameter of every smooth curvetta under discussion.

    For a nonzero preﬁx jet one ﬁrst makes the polynomial coordinate change subtracting that jet
from y; this gives the same statement in regular coordinates. The counterexample needs only the
displayed zero reference. In the conditional exact-contact reading, exactly k common centers means
ak 6= 0 in (3).


3    Closures, including satellite limits
Lemma 3. For the divisor EP in (3), put z = y − P (x). Then

                              N EP = SP := {ordt x ≥ 1, ordt z ≥ n}.                              (5)

This includes limiting arcs with x = 0, the constant arc, and lifts meeting a satellite point.

Proof. The coordinate change (x, y) 7→ (x, z) is polynomial and invertible. The equations of SP are
just the vanishing of the ﬁrst n − 1 coeﬃcients of z. Its coeﬃcient ring is a polynomial ring in the
remaining variables, so SP is a reduced irreducible cylinder. The nonempty open subset ordt x = 1
is dense. Every arc there has w = z/xn ∈ C[[t]], so lifts in (3), meeting the newest component
transversely away from its older intersection. Hence SP is contained in the closure of the transverse
locus.
    For the reverse inclusion, the two charts covering the ﬁnal component have maps

                   (x, w) 7−→ (x, z = xn w),     (v, h) 7−→ (x = vh, z = v n−1 hn ).              (6)

An arc centered on the new component has respectively ordt x ≥ 1, or ordt h ≥ 1; in either case
ordt x ≥ 1, ordt z ≥ n. This proves containment even at its satellite intersection and hence (5).
   One can check density directly without omitting degenerate arcs. For any (x0 , y0 ) ∈ SP , set

                            xϵ = x0 + ϵt,      yϵ = P (xϵ ) + y0 − P (x0 ).

The coeﬃcients are polynomial in ϵ; the arc at zero is the given one, and away from at most one
value of ϵ it lies in the transverse locus. This handles x0 = 0 as well. A separate specialization for
each P suﬃces for membership in each closure.

Proof of (2). For Dj the charts are (6) with P = 0 and n = j. The contact order in the ﬁrst
chart is ordt x; in the second it is ordt h. Exact contact q, or contact at least q, therefore implies
ordt x ≥ q, ordt y ≥ jq. Conversely the cylinder with those two inequalities is irreducible, and its
nonempty dense open subset ordt x = q lifts in the ﬁrst chart with exact contact q. Thus the
closures for both conventions are that cylinder. This argument works also for j = 1, whose two
charts are the usual ﬁrst-blowup charts.


4    Intersections and uniform separation
Proof of (1) and the exact-contact variant. The members P = 0 and P = xk force ordt y ≥ n
and ordt xk ≥ n. Since orders of nonzero formal series are integers, the latter is ordt x ≥ dn/ke.

Conversely, these inequalities imply ordt P (x) ≥ n for every polynomial in (3), because all its
exponents are at least k. This proves (1) for the entire family.
   For the exact-contact family, ak 6= 0. Its two members P = xk and P = 2xk force ordt xk ≥ n
by subtraction, and then ordt y ≥ n. The converse is unchanged. This calculation treats a possible
reading; it does not assert that the source deﬁned its bare relation that way.

   Order cylinders with ﬁnite independent thresholds (a, b) and (a0 , b0 ) are equal only if a = a0 and
b = b0 . For example, give one coordinate exactly its lower threshold and the other arbitrarily high
order, and interchange their roles. Taking n = 5 gives

                   I5,2 = {ordt x ≥ 3, ordt y ≥ 5},            I5,3 = {ordt x ≥ 2, ordt y ≥ 5}.               (7)

Equality with (2) would require (q, jq) = (3, 5) or (2, 5). Neither 3j = 5 nor 2j = 5 has an integer
solution. This proves the remaining conclusion of Theorem 1 for every q, j, without a bounded
search.

5    Scope, prior framework and reproducibility
The result concerns the speciﬁed reference multiple. The cylinders in (7) are themselves maximal
divisorial sets for the primitive toric valuations with weights (3, 5) and (2, 5). This follows from the
established toric description [3, Example 2.10]; adjacent unimodular charts are respectively

                               (x, y) = (u3 v, u5 v 2 ),       (x, y) = (u2 v, u5 v 3 ).

Their exponent matrices have determinant 1. The inverse monomials are

                            (u, v) = (x2 /y, y 3 /x5 ),        (u, v) = (x3 /y, y 2 /x5 ).

The dense exact-order loci have ordt u = 1 and v a unit. Thus allowing a diﬀerent arbitrary prime
divisor removes this particular obstruction. No negative solution of the generalized Nash problem,
no statement about Conjecture 2, and no new general theory of maximal divisorial sets is claimed.
    We also distinguish closed sets from nonreduced intersections. For n = 5, k = 2, the two-member
coeﬃcient ideal after eliminating the ﬁrst four coeﬃcients of y is

                                           (x21 , 2x1 x2 , x22 + 2x1 x3 ),

whose radical is (x1 , x2 ), although x1 is not in that ideal. For k = 3 the corresponding ideal is
(x31 , 3x21 x2 ), with radical (x1 ). Equation (7) states the reduced supports, not equality of these
nonreduced schemes. Divisible cases n = 6, k = 2, 3 satisfy the reference equality, and ﬁxing
the ﬁnal center gives the singleton Dn . Varying all tangents for an all-free length-n type gives
                                 n
{ordt x, ordt y ≥ n} = Cont (D1 ), consistent with the ﬁrst-exceptional specialization rather than
refuting it.
      The standard maximal-divisorial/contact framework is due to prior work, including [3]; com-
binatorial invariance and ﬁnite cylindrical intersections are proved in [1]. The contribution here
is the explicit complete free-family calculation and its reference-ray obstruction. The source’s au-
thors still discuss their two conjectures as conjectures in [4, Section 8]. An extensive but bounded
primary-source investigation is documented in the accompanying SOURCE_AUDIT.md. It located
no authenticated earlier publication of this exact negative calculation. This is not a certiﬁcate of
worldwide ﬁrstness, continuous openness, or completeness of the literature search. The exact claim
is tied to the inspected arXiv1501.06898v3 and workshop2016 bodies. The publisher-typeset full
text was not accessed; the original paper’s DOI is a reference, not this counterexample’s publication
DOI. No author-approved erratum, intended new hypothesis, or unique resolution of its notation
is asserted.
