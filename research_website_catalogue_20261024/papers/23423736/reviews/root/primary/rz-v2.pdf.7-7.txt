                    3. Twisted rings over non-σ-ample L
   Throughout this section let k be an algebraically closed field, X be a projective
k-scheme, σ an automorphism of X, and L an invertible sheaf over X. We will
show some preliminary results about twisted homogeneous coordinate rings (which
we call twisted rings for brevity) for non-σ-ample invertible sheaves L.

    Keeler showed that σ-ampleness depends on a property of the automorphism σ
called quasi-unipotence [Ke1]. To define this, let Num X be the quotient of the
Picard group of X by the subgroup of divisors numerically equivalent to 0; then
σ induces an action of the finitely generated free abelian group Num X, and σ is
called quasi-unipotent if the eigenvalues of this action are roots of unity. We will
not need the explicit definition of quasi-unipotence below, but only the criterion
that Keeler proved: if σ is quasi-unipotent, then an invertible sheaf L is σ-ample
if and only if Ln is ample for some n, while if σ is not quasi-unipotent then no
invertible sheaf on X is σ-ample [Ke1, Theorem 1.3]. When L is σ-ample then
B(X, L, σ) is a noetherian ring with well-understood properties. Comparatively
little is known about twisted rings B(X, L, σ) where σ is not quasi-unipotent. The
existing knowledge about this case is again due to Keeler, as follows.
Lemma 3.1. [Ke1, Proposition 3.7] Suppose σ is non-quasi-unipotent. Let L be
an ample invertible sheaf. Then there is an integer m0 with the property that for
any given m ≥ m0 , one can find constants C > 0 and r > 1 such that the graded
pieces of B := B(X, L⊗m , σ) satisfy dimk Bn ≥ Crn . In particular, it follows that
for any m ≥ m0 the ring B is not noetherian.
