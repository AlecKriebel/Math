## 2. Two-sided quotient, with no hidden relations

Let F=k<x1,x2,x3,x4> and J=(x3−x1,x4−x2). The mutually inverse free-algebra maps show F/J ≅ k<X,Y>. Direct substitution into the six original ordered noncommutative polynomials gives

    f1 ↦ X(alpha X−X)+X(X−alpha X) = 0,
    f2 ↦ X(alpha Y−Y)+X(Y−alpha Y) = 0,
    f3 ↦ Y(alpha X−X)+Y(X−alpha X) = 0,
    f4 ↦ Y(alpha Y−Y)+Y(Y−alpha Y) = 0,
    f5 ↦ X(beta X−Y)+Y(X−beta Y) = Q,
    f6 ↦ X(beta X−Y)+Y(X−beta Y) = Q.

Therefore F/(J+(f1,...,f6)) is exactly k<X,Y>/(Q). Surjectivity here is the universal property of a two-sided quotient, not an assumption that the six images exhaust a computationally sampled kernel. Alpha disappears identically before making any specialization. The two-sided interpretation matters; a right ideal by the same two expressions would not supply this quotient.

## 3. Universal graded twist and conventions

Let S=k[s,t], and let theta be the graded automorphism theta(s)=s+beta t, theta(t)=beta s+t. Its determinant is D=1−beta² ≠ 0. On S as a graded vector space define, for f in S_m and g in S_n,

    f * g = f theta^m(g).

For h in S_p both (f*g)*h and f*(g*h) equal

    f theta^m(g) theta^(m+n)(h).

The unit is 1. This establishes associativity in every characteristic and every degree, rather than checking integer exponent addition in finitely many triples. Theta^m maps S_1 onto S_1, and ordinary polynomial multiplication S_n S_1 spans S_(n+1); induction proves that degree one generates the twisted algebra B. Its degree-n vector space remains S_n, of dimension n+1.

For nonzero homogeneous f,g, f theta^m(g) is nonzero because S is a domain and theta is injective. For arbitrary nonzero sums, their highest nonzero homogeneous terms multiply to the highest homogeneous term of the product and cannot cancel with lower terms. Thus B is a domain in the ordinary, ungraded sense.

The linear lift M=[[1,beta],[beta,1]] of sigma identifies positive pullback on H⁰(P¹,O(1)) with theta, not theta inverse. The lift supplies compatible identifications of sigma^i*O(1) with O(1), hence L_n with O(n), and the section multiplication becomes f theta^m(g). This is exactly the positive-pullback convention displayed in actual RZ v2 physical p.7. P¹ and this construction exist over arbitrary k; the algebraically closed standing convention in that paper is not needed to perform this direct construction over k.

For every coherent sheaf F on P¹, F tensor L_n is isomorphic to F(n). Serre vanishing therefore gives sigma-ampleness. No bound uniform in F is claimed or needed. A scalar alternative lift would produce an isomorphic graded twist after degree-dependent rescaling; the fixed lift above specifies the exact generator normalization used here.

## 4. Relation and injectivity without an unproved normal-form theorem

The four degree-two twisted products are

    s*s=s²+beta st,     s*t=beta s²+st,
    t*s=st+beta t²,     t*t=beta st+t².

Consequently beta(s*s)−s*t+t*s−beta(t*t)=0 coefficient by coefficient. Degree-one generation gives a graded surjection A→B.

Set U=X−Y, V=Y. This triangular change has determinant one, including in characteristic two. Expanding in the FREE algebra gives

    Q=beta U²+(beta−1)UV+(beta+1)VU,
    VU=q UV+r U²,
    q=(1−beta)/(1+beta),  r=−beta/(1+beta).

Since beta² ≠ 1, both beta+1 and q are nonzero. In words of fixed length n, order lexicographically with V>U. Replacing an occurrence of VU by either UV or UU strictly decreases that finite order; the prefix before the occurrence is unchanged. Repeated expansion thus terminates. A word with no VU is U^i V^(n−i), so those n+1 words SPAN A_n. Uniqueness of a reduction outcome is unnecessary. The graded surjection to B_n of dimension n+1 then forces dim A_n=n+1 and injectivity degree by degree. Each element is a finite homogeneous sum, so the entire graded kernel is zero.

## 5. Independent tensor-kernel mechanism

There is a second all-degree proof that does not use the spanning estimate. Identify E=kX+kY with ks+kt. On E^(tensor n) put

    C_n = id tensor theta tensor theta² tensor ... tensor theta^(n−1).

The map sending a word to its degree-n twisted product is ordinary symmetric multiplication composed with C_n. Each C_n is invertible. A direct degree-two calculation gives

    C_2(Q) = −D (X tensor Y−Y tensor X).

For an insertion of Q in adjacent positions i,i+1 (positions numbered from 1), the central action is (theta^(i−1) tensor theta^i)(Q). Applying theta^(i−1) to both slots after the C_2 calculation multiplies the alternating tensor by det(theta)^(i−1). Thus the central result is −D^i times the standard commutator tensor. The maps on all other slots are invertible. Therefore C_n maps the full degree-n two-sided ideal generated by Q ONTO the full degree-n commutator ideal. This remains true in characteristic two: the alternating expression becomes a sum, and the determinant identity still holds; D stays invertible.

The quotient of T(E) by its adjacent commutator ideal is the polynomial algebra S(E), in all characteristics: letters can be sorted by commuting them, and the sorted monomials are independent in the ordinary polynomial algebra. Hence the kernel of symmetric multiplication is exactly that commutator ideal. Pulling it back by C_n proves that the twisted multiplication has kernel exactly (Q)_n, for every n. This independently establishes A≅B without inferring an infinite assertion from the deposited bounded tensor identities.

## 6. Secondary Ore interpretation and characteristic checks

    delta(U^n)=r(1+q+...+q^(n−1))U^(n+1),  delta(1)=0.

The identity [m+n]_q=[m]_q+q^m[n]_q verifies the tau-Leibniz rule on monomials. The relation VU=tau(U)V+delta(U) is precisely the relation above. This explains the familiar Ore-plane form.
