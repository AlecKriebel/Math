                                                  Abstract
         For the six-relation presentation R(α, β) in Rogalski and Sierra’s 2010 report and 2012
     article, the linear ideal (x3 − x1 , x4 − x2 ) has a projective-line twisted homogeneous coordinate
     ring as its exact quotient. It is a nonzero homogeneous completely prime ideal strictly below the
     augmentation ideal. This gives negative answers to both printed assertions: uniqueness of the
     nontrivial graded prime and absence of nontrivial maps to twisted homogeneous coordinate rings.
     The calculation works over every ﬁeld whenever β 2 6= 1, hence for all the prescribed algebraically
     independent parameters. The classical twist machinery is credited; historical priority of this
     explicit quotient remains unresolved.


1    The displayed problem
Let k be a ﬁeld, let α, β ∈ k, and give the generators degree one in

                             R = R(α, β) = khx1 , x2 , x3 , x4 i/(f1 , . . . , f6 ),

where

                                  f1 = x1 (αx1 − x3 ) + x3 (x1 − αx3 ),
                                  f2 = x1 (αx2 − x4 ) + x3 (x2 − αx4 ),
                                  f3 = x2 (αx1 − x3 ) + x4 (x1 − αx3 ),
                                  f4 = x2 (αx2 − x4 ) + x4 (x2 − αx4 ),
                                  f5 = x1 (βx1 − x2 ) + x4 (x1 − βx2 ),
                                  f6 = x1 (βx3 − x4 ) + x4 (x3 − βx4 ).

In particular, the left factor in the second summand of both f5 and f6 is x4 .
    The oﬃcial report [1, pp. 1361–1362] gives these relations and asks whether, for algebraically
independent α, β over the prime subﬁeld of an uncountable algebraically closed k, the augmentation
              ⊕
ideal R+ = n>0 Rn is the only nontrivial graded prime ideal, and whether R has no nontrivial
homomorphism to any twisted homogeneous coordinate ring. Both assertions recur in the published
article [2, p. 1198], whose Theorem 3.5(2) on p. 1202 gives this presentation with parameters c, d
in place of α, β. The printed assertions impose no injectivity, birationality or lower bound on the
dimension of the image. We exhibit a unital graded surjection with inﬁnite-dimensional image of
Gelfand–Kirillov dimension two.

Proof. Imposing x3 = x1 = X and x4 = x2 = Y sends the six relations to 0, 0, 0, 0, Q, Q, where

                                      Q = βX 2 − XY + Y X − βY 2 .

The universal property of free-algebra quotients therefore gives the ﬁrst displayed isomorphism,
with exactly this deﬁning ideal.
   Let S = k[s, t] and let θ be the graded algebra automorphism

                                     θ(s) = s + βt,        θ(t) = βs + t.

It is invertible since its determinant is 1−β 2 . On the graded vector space S, deﬁne f ⋆g = f θm (g) for
homogeneous f ∈ Sm , and extend bilinearly. The automorphism law proves associativity and the
unit property. A product of two nonzero homogeneous elements is nonzero; taking highest nonzero
homogeneous components proves that this algebra B is a domain for arbitrary elements as well. Its
degree n piece has dimension n + 1. It is generated by degree one: inductively, Sn θn (S1 ) = Sn+1 .
     The linear lift of σ above identiﬁes σ ∗ on H 0 (O(1)) with θ, and identiﬁes Ln with O(n). Under
these compatible identiﬁcations the section multiplication is exactly ⋆. Thus B = B(P1 , O(1), σ).

Every replacement strictly decreases ﬁxed-length lexicographic order with V > U . Reduction
therefore terminates and shows that U i V n−i , 0 ≤ i ≤ n, span An . Consequently dim An ≤ n + 1 =
dim Bn . The surjection forces equality in each degree, hence is an isomorphism and the kernel of
R → B is exactly I.
    The relations of R are quadratic, so its four degree-one generators are linearly independent. In
particular x3 − x1 6= 0. The ideal I is homogeneous, two-sided and contained in R+ . Since x1 maps
to the nonzero element s, it is not in I; thus 0 6= I ⊊ R+ . The domain quotient proves complete
primality, and hence both ordinary and graded primality. Finally dim Bn = n + 1, so the Hilbert
series is (1 − z)−2 ; the cumulative dimension is (n + 1)(n + 2)/2, proving GKdim B = 2.

    Algebraic independence of α, β implies β 2 6= 1. Thus the ideal and surjection refute both
assertions in the stated parameter regime. The image comes from a positive-dimensional projective
curve; it is neither an augmentation to k nor a ﬁnite-dimensional image.


3    Scope and historical context
No classiﬁcation of all primes is claimed. Nor does a quotient contradict the separate obstruction to
sharing a graded quotient ring with a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring discussed in [2, p. 1198].
The source’s surface algebra and this curve quotient have diﬀerent Gelfand–Kirillov dimensions.
    The theorem concerns the displayed presentation in all characteristics. The journal’s geometric
identiﬁcation uses c = (ϑ−1)/(ϑ+1) and d = (ρ−1)/(ρ+1) [2, Theorem 3.5]. These preserve inde-
pendent parameters in characteristic diﬀerent from two; in characteristic two the fractions collapse
to one when deﬁned. The theorem does not identify the independent-parameter characteristic-two
presentation with that geometric family or transfer the latter’s noetherian or dimension results. At
the excluded values β = ±1, the corresponding Q factors as (X + Y )(X − Y ) or −(X − Y )(X + Y );
the quotient then has nonzero linear zero divisors. At β = 0 it is the commutative polynomial
plane, consistent with the stronger theorem.
    The two-generator algebras, degree-reduction argument and polynomial graded twists are clas-
sical. The source-speciﬁc point is the substitution (x1 , x2 , x3 , x4 ) 7→ (X, Y, X, Y ) and its exact
projective-line quotient. Later Nevins–Sierra preprints [5, v1, p. 25; v2, pp. 28–29] repeat the
contrary no-map assertion for the Rogalski–Sierra algebra. Their Theorem 1.2 requires additional
point-tail moduli and geometric hypotheses, including dimension at least two; a universal factor in
Theorem 4.10 need not itself be a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring. Likewise the canonical-
map theorem in [4] requires strong noetherianity, which the generic source family lacks [2, p. 1236].
These theorems alone do not supply an earlier resolution. Campbell’s thesis [6, Chapter 6 and §8.2]
treats inﬁnitesimal deformations at a baseline algebra and function skew ﬁelds of another deforma-
tion family; those statements are not this exact generic algebra’s homomorphic curve quotient.
    Two independently positioned priority audits veriﬁed no full matching predecessor in the in-
spected primary passages. Historical priority remains unresolved. Unread journal versions, older
papers and books, unindexed work and incomplete citation coverage remain gaps. In particular,
the repeated assertion above is attributed to the inspected arXiv versions, not to an unread journal
body. No ﬁrstness, exhaustive-absence or current worldwide-open claim is made.
