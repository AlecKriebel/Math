                                                      Abstract
              The classical Robinson sextic is nonnegative, but adding any nonnegative constant does not
          make it a sum of polynomial squares modulo the ideal of polynomials vanishing on its real
          tangency variety. This rules out both the exact radical certiﬁcate and the positive-constant
          certiﬁcate in the proposed unrestricted replacement of the gradient ideal. The same obstruction
          applies to the separate-cross-product ideal printed in the original report. Ten zero lines force the
          low-degree homogeneous parts of all square summands to vanish; an additional coordinate axis
          then makes the certiﬁcate impossible. The proof allows arbitrarily many summands of arbitrary
          degree and is entirely elementary. We credit the classical form and zero-set method, and make
          no claim of ﬁrst bibliographic priority.

2    The elementary zero-set obstruction
Put a = x2 , b = y 2 , c = z 2 and write R = F (a, b, c). Direct expansion gives

                                    F = (a − b)2 (a + b − c) + c(a − c)(b − c).

The symmetric polynomial F may be evaluated after ordering a ≥ b ≥ c ≥ 0; both displayed terms
are then nonnegative. Hence R ≥ 0, and its inﬁmum is zero, attained at the origin.
    Let Z consist of the ten vectors

                       (1, s, t),     (1, s, 0),   (1, 0, t),      (0, 1, t),        s, t ∈ {−1, 1},

where each applicable sign varies independently. Substitution gives R(v) = 0 for every v ∈ Z, and
homogeneity gives R(uv) = 0 for every real u.

Lemma 2. A homogeneous cubic vanishing on Z is zero. The same holds in degrees zero, one and
two.

Proof. The six vectors with a zero coordinate force the cubic to have the form

               p = Ax(x2 − y 2 − z 2 ) + By(y 2 − x2 − z 2 ) + Cz(z 2 − x2 − y 2 ) + Dxyz.

At (1, s, t) its value is −A − Bs − Ct + Dst. Summing these four values against the sign characters
1, s, t, st gives −4A, −4B, −4C, 4D, respectively. Thus all four coeﬃcients vanish. If a homogeneous
degree-d polynomial pd , with 0 ≤ d < 3, vanishes on Z, then x3−d pd is such a cubic. Since S is an
integral domain, pd = 0.

   The ten-zero cubic obstruction is classical; Reznick gives the same ten directions and cubic
argument in [2, Section 4(b), p. 259]. We include the full argument, then use whole zero lines and
an additional axis to address the ideal remainder and positive constant perturbations.
   Every point of the lines Rv, v ∈ Z, is a global minimizer of R. Its gradient is therefore zero,
and each line lies in both VR (JR ) and VR (KR ). Both varieties also contain the axis (u, 0, 0): there
∇R = (6u5 , 0, 0), all cross-products vanish, and R = u6 .

3    Formulation boundaries
The following standard sphere-minimization argument also underlies the tangency-set inﬁmum iden-
tity in Laurent’s updated manuscript of 6 February 2010 [5, Section 7.2.3, Lemma 7.21, manuscript
pp. 117–118]. For any polynomial f in n variables, nonnegativity on all of Rn is equivalent to
nonnegativity on the real variety of its tangency minors. If f (w) < 0 and w = 0, the origin already
belongs to that variety. Otherwise minimize f on the sphere of radius ∥w∥. At a minimizer v,
Lagrange multipliers give ∇f (v) = λv and f (v) ≤ f (w) < 0, so all tangency minors vanish. This
proves the reverse implication without any assumption about the global inﬁmum. For the Robinson
example, the ﬁrst two proposed conditions therefore hold and both certiﬁcates fail.
    The literal product reading has a further, separate defect. We use a constant shift of the
standard nonattaining example in [3, Section 1.6, equation (6)] and [1, p. 810]:

                       Q(x, y) = (1 − xy)2 + y 2 − 12 ,                KQ = (yQx , xQy ).

Here yQx = 2y 2 (xy − 1) and xQy = 2x(x(xy − 1) + y). The ﬁrst equation forces y = 0 or xy = 1.
The second then forces x = 0 in the former case, and equals 2, a contradiction, in the latter.
Thus VR (KQ ) = {(0, 0)}. Nevertheless Q(0, 0) = 1/2 and Q(2, 1/2) = −1/4. Its inﬁmum −1/2
is not attained: equality would require both y = 0 and xy = 1, whereas Q(1/u, u) = u2 − 1/2
approaches the inﬁmum. This diagnostic applies only to the product reading; it does not negate
the tangency-minor equivalence just proved.
    For the actual gradient ideal GR = (Rx , Ry , Rz ), Euler’s identity gives 6R = xRx + yRy + zRz ,
hence R ∈ GR . There is no contradiction with the gradient-ideal theorem. Likewise the gradient-
tentacle inequalities [3] and truncated tangency certiﬁcates [4, 6] use additional inequalities and
SOS-multiplier terms; they are diﬀerent certiﬁcates, which this note does not refute.
