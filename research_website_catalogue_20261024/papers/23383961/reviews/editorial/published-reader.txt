    Nonmonotone Argument and Radial Angle of a Support Slit
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                        ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                             14 October 2026

                                                  Abstract
         We give negative answers to the two slit-monotonicity questions of P. L. Duren recorded as
     Problem 6.72 in Hayman–Lingham. Every global maximizer of the real part of a2 +i10−24 (a4 −3a3 ) in
     the normalized schlicht class omits an arc with nonmonotone continuous argument and nonmonotone
     radial angle, under both signed and ordinary unsigned conventions. The proof uses classical support-
     point geometry, a quantitative area-theorem estimate for the actual slit tip, and controlled inversion
     of the Schiﬀer diﬀerential. It establishes actual extremizers by compactness and does not assume that
     an arbitrary trajectory is an extremal slit. No formula for the maximizing function or uniqueness
     is required. This is an unrefereed AI-assisted research note.

A support point maximizes Re L for a continuous complex-linear functional on H(D) whose real part
is nonconstant on S. Duren’s questions ask whether the omitted analytic arc of a support point must

    For a continuous argument θ of W ,

                                    θ′ (t) = Im(W ′ /W ) = −2 Im Z(t)/t.

Its derivative is negative at 1 and positive at T , both regular points of the actual slit. It cannot be
monotone. The signed outward radial angle is

                                     β(t) = arg((−W ′ )/W ) = arg Z(t).

The branch is ﬁxed by (11); it tends to zero as t ↓ 0, since V (t) = a−1/2 t + O(t3 ). It is positive
at 1 and negative at T . A nondecreasing function with initial limit zero cannot take the negative
value, and a nonincreasing one cannot take the positive value. Thus the signed angle is nonmonotone.
Continuity gives a zero between these two points, whereas |β| is positive at both endpoints. The ordinary
unsigned angle is therefore nonmonotone even on that compact subarc. An inward tangent gives its
supplement, and the acute tangent-line interpretation gives |β|; neither restores monotonicity. This
proves Theorem 1.


5     Attribution, scope and veriﬁcation
The support-slit geometry and Schiﬀer method are classical, as are coeﬃcient-extremal and Loewner–
Schiﬀer control methods [4]. Duren and Brown established positive monotonicity results for particular
evaluation and lower-degree coeﬃcient functionals [2, 6, 7]. Zemyan’s tangent-waver construction [8] uses
Brown’s actual point-evaluation extremizers, whose polar argument and radial angle remain monotone.
Tangent direction arg w′ is a diﬀerent observable from arg w and arg w′ − arg w. The complete later
papers [9, 10] investigate ﬁniteness and bounds for hypothetical polar reversals; their examples do not
supply an actual support point with either target failure. Our proof does not use their count bounds.
    Duren–Leung–Schiﬀer [11] establish strict tip-angle inequality for ﬁnite coeﬃcient functionals. The
classiﬁcation studied in Zhang–Ma [12] concerns saturation of the tip-angle bound; it does not imply
monotonicity in the interior. Rationality of a functional and rationality of its maximizer must also be
distinguished [13, 14]. These prior results do not give the fourth-degree reversal mechanism proved
here. Goh’s coeﬃcient two-functional theorems [15] require a second independent supporting or Schiﬀer-
critical functional; maximization of our single L does not supply that hypothesis. The complete paper
