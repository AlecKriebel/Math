1    The question and the result
Write Dr = {z ∈ C : |z| < r} and use unnormalized planar Lebesgue area dA = dx dy. Baern-
stein’s question in [1, Problem 7.37, printed pp. 171–172] asks for an absolute constant C and, for
each positive normalized squared-pole sum, an area-π set independent of R, with integral at most
2π log R + C. Here is an explicit answer.
                                                       ∑                              ∑
Theorem 1. Let n ≥ 1, zj ∈ C, λj > 0, and nj=1 λj = 1, and set g(z) = nj=1 λj /(z − zj )2 .
There exists a compact S = S(g), of area exactly π, such that, simultaneously for every real R > 1,
                              ∫
                                       |g| dA ≤ 2π log R + π(log 2 + e−1 ).                             (1)
                               DR \S

These are ordinary ﬁnite nonnegative Lebesgue integrals. Poles may coincide. No smaller logarith-
mic coeﬃcient works with a ﬁnite absolute additive constant.

5    Attribution, priority limits and veriﬁcation
This note supplies the displayed target-speciﬁc deduction from established results. It claims no
new Beurling estimate, quadrature construction, or general balayage/operator mechanism. Related

recent work includes Lin’s partial-balayage principles [5] and Guerra’s real-input Beurling weak-type
estimates [6]; those inspected operator conclusions do not themselves state the ﬁxed-set, all-radius
assertion proved here. The generic ingredients used above were available by 2009. A bounded
priority audit identiﬁed no veriﬁed earlier full-target answer; worldwide ﬁrst discovery/publication
is not certiﬁed. In particular, Leung preliminary reports titled On a ﬁxed area problem, AMS
Abstracts 11 (1990), p. 181, and 13 (1992), p. 440, were located through primary indexes, but
their mathematical texts were inaccessible. Their relevance remains unresolved. Other unavailable
full/ﬁnal texts and exact inspection scopes are listed in the supporting audit. Neither historical
open labels nor repository timestamps establish ﬁrst priority.
    The source archive preserves the original proof, source identities, three distinct mathematical
reviews, priority reviews, the correction check and Leung supplement. Supplementary standard-
library programs test kernel normalization, quadrature ﬁelds (including holes), scaling, scalar
bounds and deliberate wrong alternatives. Floating calculations are diagnostics, not validated
quadrature or proof certiﬁcates; the theorem rests on the universal argument above and its explic-
itly stated established inputs. AI tools were used extensively in proof development, computation,
adversarial checking, literature research and writing. This paper and its supplements are unrefereed
and have not undergone external human peer review, journal acceptance or formal proof-assistant
certiﬁcation.
