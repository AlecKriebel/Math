Fact 3. For every Boolean function f , rdeg(f ) = max(ndeg(f ), ndeg(¬f )).
Proof. Let p/q be a rational representation of f with max(deg(p), deg(q)) = rdeg(f ). Then, p and
q − p are nondeterministic representations of f and ¬f respectively. Thus,

max(ndeg(f ), ndeg(¬f )) ≤ max(deg(p), deg(q − p)) ≤ max(deg(p), max(deg(p), deg(q))) = rdeg(f ).

   Conversely, let p and q be nondeterministic representations of f and ¬f respectively, with
deg(p) = ndeg(f ) and deg(q) = ndeg(¬f ). Then p/(p + q) is a rational representation of f . Thus,

rdeg(f ) ≤ max(deg(p), deg(p + q)) ≤ max(deg(p), max(deg(p), deg(q))) = max(ndeg(f ), ndeg(¬f )).

      Taken together, we obtain rdeg(f ) = max(ndeg(f ), ndeg(¬f )), as required.

Definition 5 (Block sensitivity). Let f : {0, 1}n → {0, 1}. Let x ∈ {0, 1}n . We say that a subset

    The next key lemma relates sensitive blocks of f at x with maximal monomials of a nondeter-
ministic representation of f . It generalizes [BdW02, Lemma 5] (attributed to Nisan and Smolensky)
to involve x, nondeterministic representation, and maximal monomials. The proof is the same.
Lemma 1 (Nisan-Smolensky). Let f : {0, 1}n → {0, 1} be nonconstant and x ∈ f −1 (0). Let p be a
nondeterministic representation of f and M be a maximal monomial of p. Then there exists B ⊆ M
such that f (xB ) = 1.
Proof. Let q be the restriction of p obtained by fixing all variables outside M according to x. Now M
remains a maximal monomial of q. Therefore, q is nonconstant (as a formal polynomial). Therefore,
there exists y ∈ {0, 1}M with q(y) ̸= 0 by uniqueness of multilinear representation. Let x′ ∈ {0, 1}n
be equal to y on M and be equal to x outside M . Then p(x′ ) = q(y) ̸= 0, which implies f (x′ ) = 1
as p is a nondeterministic representation of f . By definition, x′ and x can only differ on M , so x′
can be written as xB for some B ⊆ M .
