               Polynomial compatibility on the Boolean cube
                     from disjoint-support separators
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           17 October 2026


                                               Abstract
         We give an explicit aﬃrmative answer to the two-polynomial question posed by Austrin
     in Oberwolfach Report 9/2025. Two nonzero real multilinear polynomials of degree at most
     d on the uniform Boolean cube have a common nonzero vertex whenever every relative coor-
     dinate inﬂuence is at most 1/(4096d8 ). In fact, disjoint supports force a relative inﬂuence at
     least 1/(1024d8 ). The result is a deduction from the disjoint-support separator of Li–Li–Li–Liu
     and the covariance inequality of O’Donnell–Saks–Schramm–Servedio, combined with a square-
     density estimate. We supply the complete deduction and an explicit separator constant. No
     novelty or ﬁrst-priority claim is made for this consequence.

2    A quantitative total separator
We reconstruct the prior separator to keep both its constant and its behavior on common zeros
explicit. Here alone we use {0, 1} coordinates; the aﬃne change between the two cubes preserves
degrees and query depths.

Lemma 1 (Li–Li–Li–Liu separator, with an extracted constant). If real multilinear p, q of degree
at most d ≥ 1 on {0, 1}n satisfy pq = 0 everywhere, there is a total deterministic decision tree of
depth at most 16d4 , with output h ∈ {0, 1}, such that h = 1 on supp p and h = 0 on supp q. No
coordinate repeats along a branch.

Proof. We ﬁrst recall the block bound used in [3, 4]. Suppose R : {0, 1}b → R has degree at most m,
satisﬁes |R| ≤ 1, R(0) = 1, and R(ej ) ≤ 0 for all j. Its expectation P (t) at independent Bernoulli(t)
bits is a polynomial of degree at most m, bounded by one throughout [0, 1]. Moreover,

The classical Markov inequality, rescaled to [0, 1], gives |P ′ (0)| ≤ 2m2 . Thus b ≤ 2m2 .
    At a node, restrict p, q by the coordinates already queried. If one restriction is zero, label for the
other side, with an arbitrary label if both are zero. Otherwise neither restriction can be a nonzero
constant, since the other has disjoint support. Put D = max(deg p, deg q) ≥ 1. The multilinear
representative of s = p2 − q 2 has degree at most 2D. Choose a vertex z maximizing |s|, whose
maximum is positive. Suppose p(z) ̸= 0, so s(z) > 0 and q(z) = 0.
    Choose an inclusion-maximal family of pairwise disjoint supports M1 , . . . , Mb of maximum-
degree monomials of q. For each Mj , ﬁxing all variables outside it to their values at z preserves its
top coeﬃcient: a further contribution would require a strictly containing monomial. This restriction
is therefore nonzero, although it vanishes at z. Some nonempty Ej ⊆ Mj can be ﬂipped from z to
make q ̸= 0. There p = 0 and s < 0. The blocks Ej are disjoint.
    Encode these block ﬂips by b Boolean variables and divide s by |s(z)|. The resulting function R
is bounded by one, has R(0) = 1 and R(ej ) < 0, and has degree at most 2D: each original coordinate
becomes an aﬃne function of one block bit, and multilinearization cannot increase degree. The
block bound gives b ≤ 8D2 .

    The union H = j Mj contains at most 8d3 coordinates and hits every maximum-degree mono-
mial of q, by maximality. Query all coordinates in H. On every answer branch, q becomes zero
or strictly loses degree; the degree of p cannot increase. The case q(z) ̸= 0 is symmetric. Each
nonterminal stage decreases deg p + deg q by at least one, from an initial value at most 2d. Thus at
most 2d stages, each costing at most 8d3 queries, suﬃce. Only free coordinates are queried. Zero
restrictions terminate before their degree is needed. These rules give a total tree, including on the
common-zero set.

    A nonzero constant has full support, so constants cause no exception. The proof also covers
n < d. The earlier exponential suﬃcient bound uses δ < 2−d /d, as in [2, §5.2]. Its non-strict
endpoint printed in [1] fails already for d = 1, f = 1 + x1 , g = 1 − x1 , both of relative inﬂuence 1/2.
The smaller inclusive threshold in Theorem 1 has slack. No optimality of its exponent or constants
is asserted.

4    Attribution, veriﬁcation, and review status
The contribution of this note is the explicit application above to the stated two-polynomial ques-
tion. The separator, the real covariance inequality, and the Markov/Bernoulli method are credited
prior inputs, available before this note. Reconstructing their proofs does not establish novelty. A
bounded primary-source audit found no earlier recorded complete target-speciﬁc deduction in the
bodies inspected; it does not certify historical priority. In particular, the full ePrint version of the
2022 paper and the full 2023 non-interactive-commitment followup were inaccessible to that audit.
Detailed source/version records, inspection limits, and independent priority reviews accompany the
note. No ﬁrstness or current global literature-status claim is made.
    The accompanying source and veriﬁcation archive includes the original proof record, new in-
dependent mathematical audits, exact rational diagnostics, and a portable oﬄine runner. Finite
enumerations check adaptive trees, support geometry, Fourier identities, boundary cases, and de-
liberate incorrect variants in ordinary and optimized execution. They supplement the universal
written proof; they are not a formal proof certiﬁcate or an exhaustive check in arbitrary dimension.
Third-party source bodies are not redistributed.
    AI tools were used extensively in developing, writing, reproducing and adversarially reviewing
this work. The reviews are internal AI-assisted checks. This preprint is unrefereed; no external
human peer review, journal acceptance, or proof-assistant certiﬁcation is claimed.
