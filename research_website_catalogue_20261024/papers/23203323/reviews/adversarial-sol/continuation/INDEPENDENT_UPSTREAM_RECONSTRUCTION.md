Fix n≥3 and p,q>1, put A=B=0, and suppose u,v are strictly positive C² entire solutions. Let f=u^q, g=v^p and K=c_n|x|^(2−n), c_n=1/((n−2)|S^(n−1)|). Every source is locally bounded; the removable-origin part of the broader weighted argument introduces no extra hypothesis in this specialization.

1. Potentials, mass and no hidden energy input. Spherical integration of a positive superharmonic solution (or smoothing first as the source does) gives K*g≤u and K*f≤v. The harmonic nonnegative remainders are constants by the positive harmonic Liouville argument using ratios of ball volumes as radii tend to infinity. Thus u=c_u+K*g, v=c_v+K*f. On B₁, K(x−y) has a positive dimensional lower bound for x,y∈B₁. With M_f=∫B₁f, M_g=∫B₁g, this gives M_f≥c_f M_g^q and M_g≥c_g M_f^p. Hence 1≥c_f c_g^q M_f^(pq−1), and both masses have universal upper bounds. This direction of inequality is essential; it is not an upper bound on u or an assumed Lane–Emden result.

2. Scaling gives ∫B_R f≤C R^(n−2−β), ∫B_R g≤C R^(n−2−α), for every R>0 with α=2(p+1)/(pq−1), β=2(q+1)/(pq−1). If c_u>0 then f≥c_u^q, whose centered mass grows as R^n, contradicting the first bound since β+2>0. Thus both harmonic constants vanish. The positive exponents α,β make dyadic potential tails summable (C2^(−jα) and C2^(−jβ)). Tonelli and local integrability of K then give ∫B₄(f+g+u+v)≤C and the truncated potential bound ∫|x−y|≥ℓK(x−y)(f+g)(y)dy≤Cℓ^(2−n), for x∈B₂, 0<ℓ≤1. These use source masses and exact potentials only; no uniform local energy, pointwise solution bound, finite total energy, symmetry or infinity condition has entered. Local energy finiteness for each individual solution follows from local boundedness; it is separate from a universal bound.

3. Interaction and virial signs. Define dπ=f(x)g(y)K(x−y)dxdy. Its two marginals are f(x)u(x)dx and g(y)v(y)dy. With the actual compact radial cutoff η and X=xη, w=−x·∇η≥0, the kernel gradient has sign ∇K=−(n−2)(x−y)K/|x−y|². Integration by parts therefore yields exactly
 a n E_f+b n E_g−a W_f−b W_g=(n−2)∫Φdπ,
where a=1/(q+1), b=1/(p+1), Φ=(X(x)−X(y))·(x−y)/|x−y|². Absolute convergence of the two unsymmetrized gradients is justified before Fubini. The unweighted origin is smooth and X=O(|x|), so there is no singular boundary defect. There is no global energy subtraction.

4. Localization is energy relative without circularity. The actual exponents are m=n+3, k=n(m+1), N=k+n+2, η comparable to d^N and w≤C d^(N−1), with d=min(1,(2−|x|)_+). On close pairs of radius δd^m, Taylor's formula gives |Φ−η(x)+w(x)D|≤Cδd^(N−2+m) and |η(x)−η(y)|≤Cδd^(N−1+m). The powers are at least N, so interaction marginals bound these by CδE, E=E_f+E_g. On far pairs, the two required exponents after the truncated-potential bound are N−1−m(n−2)=4n+7 and N−m(n−1)=3n+5, both positive. The remainder is therefore a finite C_δ times source mass, not an unknown energy. It follows that |E_f−E_g|+|Q|≤εE+C_ε for Q=∫(Φ−η+wD)dπ. This is the needed localization assertion with arbitrarily small relative error.

5. Interval inequality and sign control. For distinct parallel lines, λ>0 and h=s−s′, let K_λ(h)=c_n(λ²+h²)^((2−n)/2), D=h²/(λ²+h²). Integration by parts over bounded intervals I,J gives
 ∫(2K−K_I−K_J)=(n−2)∫[D−d₀h/(λ²+h²)]K,
where d₀ is the difference of interval midpoints. The difference-coordinate density is ρ(h)=F(|h−d₀|), F(r)=max(0,min(|I|,|J|,(|I|+|J|)/2−r)). For d₀≥0, ρ(h)≥ρ(−h) at h>0; the reverse holds for d₀≤0. Thus d₀∫hK/(λ²+h²)ρ(h)dh≥0. This proves the sign for unequal lengths, disjoint intervals, and either midpoint orientation. Multiplying by min(c_I,c_J) and bounding the remaining endpoint defects by ∫K proves the nonnegative weighted formulation. At most one positive coefficient is pointwise trivial. This is why unbounded zero-weight intervals, including the logarithmic n=3 tail, introduce no illegal infinity-minus-infinity operation. The coincident-line set is null for the eventual n-dimensional integration; λ→0 need not have uniform bounded interval integrals.

6. The central layer-loss estimate. The normalized cap weights satisfy E_e h_e=w, sup h_e≤C_κd^(N−n), |∇h_e|≤C_κd^(N−n−1), and |D_e−D|≤2κd on their support. Exact angular normalization, rather than independence from layer geometry, is crucial. A source-layer interval gets χ_I=inf_I h_e only if bounded and |I|≤2δ inf_I d, otherwise zero. Joint measurability follows from measurable forward/backward exit distances and rational-offset infima, so the multiple layer/line integrals are legitimate.

For fixed x, split component lengths at ℓ=δd^m. If |I|≤ℓ, it meets the retention rule and h_e(x)−χ_I≤C_κδd^N. If |I|>ℓ, one forward or backward segment of length ℓ/2 lies in {f>t}. Polar integration gives probability≤C/(tℓ^n) using only the centered mass of B₄; there is no translated weighted estimate or unproved source bound. For levels t≥P d^(−k), sup h times this probability is ≤C_(κ,δ)P^(−1)d^N, since k=n(m+1). Integrating in t up to f(x), then against u, produces a small multiple of E_f. 7. Endpoint and recovery checks. A positive retained component lies in the annulus where h>0, and its endpoints satisfy f=t. In this unweighted specialization, u(x_−)=u(x_+)=t^(1/q) exactly. The weighted endpoint error in the full primary proof therefore vanishes here; no weakened new hypothesis is being substituted.  aW_f+bW_g−(n−2)∫wD dπ≤[Cκ+C_κδ+C_(κ,δ)/P]E+C_(κ,δ,P).
The order is first κ small, then δ small with κ fixed, then P large with κ,δ fixed. All additive constants remain finite and independent of the solution. This closes the actual central pressure mechanism, rather than assuming it.

8. Energy absorption and full hyperbola. Put c₁=an−(n−2), c₂=bn and γ=c₁+c₂=n(a+b)−(n−2)>0. The virial equation becomes c₁E_f+c₂E_g=aW_f+bW_g−(n−2)T+(n−2)Q. 9. Final contradiction. Under centered dilation, the local energy on B₁ equals R^(α+β+2−n) times the original local energy on B_R.
