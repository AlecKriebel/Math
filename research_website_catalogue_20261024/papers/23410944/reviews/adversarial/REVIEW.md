## Adversarial proof audit

1. **Null-volume connection versus connected open geometry (published pp. 2–3).** With the next cube touching the old final cube at the prescribed face, every connector point away from its middle face already lies in one of those cubes: δ < aₙ < a ensures the transverse and axial inequalities. The new set is nevertheless open and connected because the full connector cube is included. Only a face patch is added, so the L² identification is valid even though H₀¹ changes. An actual positive-length passage adds positive measure and would require a different argument; the note expressly excludes it. No slit-boundary regularity is silently used.

2. **Finite-window limit, including d = 2 (pp. 2–3).** Embed all finite form domains in H₀¹ of a fixed containing box. Their decreasing intersection is H₀¹(D₀). For a bounded function in the intersection, choose δ so small that the connector is inside the region where the point cutoff vanishes. Multiplying compact smooth approximants then produces functions in H₀¹(D₀). In d ≥ 3 the cutoff gradient energy is O(r^(d−2)); in d = 2 the logarithmic profile has energy (2π/log(1/r))∫|θ′|². Dominated convergence controls the other terms. Componentwise truncation supplies the unbounded case. The real/imaginary truncations preserve each form space. This avoids the false planar claim that a fixed-shape radial cutoff has vanishing gradient energy.

   For an arbitrary δⱼ → 0 (not necessarily monotone), weak H¹ limits belong to each fixed Vₛ because eventually δⱼ < s, and hence to V₀. Constant recovery sequences lie in every Vδ. To upgrade to norm-resolvent convergence, unit input sequences have weakly convergent subsequences; solution outputs are uniformly bounded in H₀¹ of the box and therefore precompact in L². Testing against V₀ identifies the limit as R₀f, while compactness also gives R₀fⱼ → R₀f. This contradicts any fixed positive norm discrepancy. Positivity and compact resolvent follow from the same box Poincaré/Rellich argument. The proof does not assert norm convergence for the final unbounded exhaustion.

3. **Nonresonance is a genuine finite choice (pp. 3–4).** Only finitely many old eigenvalues are protected at a given stage. On the bounded next-width interval (aₙ+1,aₙ+2), an equality π²Σmᵢ²/(4a²) = λ bounds every positive integer mᵢ. Each such index/value pair forbids at most one width. Thus a width remains available, chosen before the aperture; all previous geometry stays fixed. The absence of an algorithm for computing an admissible width or aperture is a limitation explicitly acknowledged, not a gap in the existential choice. Arbitrary prescribed widths would remove the justification for avoiding contamination by a new-cube eigenspace.

4. **Multiplicity and nested-cluster transport (pp. 3–4).** The isolated old eigenvalue has precisely its old full eigenspace in the disconnected direct sum because of the nonresonance choice. Small Riesz contours for the nonzero eigenvalues of the bounded resolvents give norm convergence of whole spectral projections. The theorem does not follow individual eigenvectors through a multiple eigenvalue. Contours/intervals are disjoint, all cluster members are retained, and sufficiently small δ simultaneously satisfies finitely many projection bounds. Norm distance < 1 yields equality of ranks by injectivity of each projection on the other's range. Complete-eigenspace status is preserved even when an old multiple eigenvalue splits. A new sufficiently high finite spectral cutoff contains all transported clusters and approximates the finite current probe set. Older projections need not stay initial spectral cutoffs; no uniform gap over infinitely many stages is needed.

5. **Correct unbounded-domain limit and fixed-k quantifier (pp. 4–5).** The increasing open exhaustion has form-dense union: every compactly supported smooth test is contained in one finite stage. The finite-stage solution is the form-inner-product projection of the final solution, proving strong resolvent convergence after zero extension. These extended finite-stage operators have kernels on future cubes and are not treated as genuine resolvents on all of H. For each fixed k, summable errors yield a norm limit Pᵏ with tail bound 2^(−n−1), preserving its finite rank. Passing nesting and commutation to the limit requires only norm convergence of Pₙᵏ, strong convergence of Rₙ, and uniform boundedness of Rₙ. All are present. Taking a diagonal sequence of cutoffs instead would not preserve finite rank; the proof does not make that mistake.

6. **No escape to infinite energy; actual completeness (p. 5).** The final R = (A+1)⁻¹ is positive and injective. Its restriction to each finite-dimensional reducing Ran Pᵏ is therefore positive definite. Every resulting eigenvector has a positive resolvent eigenvalue μ and lies in dom A, with eigenvalue μ⁻¹−1. Thus Ran Pᵏ is in the closed pure-point subspace. A fixed cube sine-basis vector is tested at *every* n ≥ max(j,ℓ); birth error plus future transport error is at most 2^(−n)+2^(−n−1) → 0. Therefore every basis vector belongs to that subspace. The countable face patches are null, so the cube basis is genuinely complete in L²(Ω). This establishes the exclusion of both continuous components, independently of the easier density-of-eigenvalues claim. Injectivity and repeated vanishing-error probes are each essential to this reasoning.

7. **Spectrum, essential spectrum and zero (pp. 5–6).** The modulated compact bump functions fit inside mutually disjoint growing cubes. Their normalization, supports and product-rule error O(rₙ⁻²)+O(|ξ|rₙ⁻¹) are correct, including λ = 0. Their disjoint supports make them orthonormal and weakly null, so Weyl's singular-sequence criterion yields every λ ≥ 0 in the essential spectrum. Nonnegativity excludes negative spectrum. Completeness identifies the spectrum with the closure of the eigenvalues. A zero eigenfunction has zero weak gradient, is constant on the connected open domain, and must vanish because the domain has infinite measure. No contradiction exists between a full interval as essential spectrum and pure-point spectral type; the interval is the closure, not the countable eigenvalue set itself.

- **Fixed probe error is insufficient.** P = I − J/N has rank N−1, misses the all-ones direction, and yet approximates every coordinate vector with squared residual 1/N. Checked for N = 16, 25, 36. This falsifies replacing the paper's repeated vanishing-error schedule by a fixed small tolerance.
- **Injectivity is needed.** Rₙ = diag(1/(n+1),1/2) are genuine resolvents of Aₙ = diag(n,1), commute with the same rank-one P, and converge in norm to a matrix whose kernel contains Ran P. The protected energy diverges. This countermodel verifies why the final resolvent's injectivity is used.
- **A growing diagonal cutoff is not a fixed-rank norm limit.** Finite sections of coordinate projections Pₙ → I strongly retain norm distance one from I while ranks grow. The published proof correctly fixes k first.
- **The dimensional boundary is real.** In one dimension, the tent u(x) = 1−|x| lies in H₀¹(−1,1), but any zero-midpoint-trace competitor on the disconnected intervals has gradient-distance squared at least two by Cauchy–Schwarz on each half. This defeats the point-removal step in d = 1. For d = 2, a different explicit polynomial logarithmic profile gives exact energy 20π/(7 log(1/r)), confirming the vanishing-capacity mechanism. This C² diagnostic profile is not substituted for the smooth profile in the proof.
- **Touching faces matter.** A positive-length gap of length g and transverse halfwidth h contributes new volume g(2h)^(d−1)>0, checked exactly for d = 2,…,8. The common-L² identification cannot simply be reused for passages.
- **Dense eigenvalues are insufficient.** Let D on ℓ² have a diagonal enumeration of the positive rationals and let Mₓ act on L²(0,∞). D ⊕ Mₓ has dense eigenvalues and spectrum [0,∞), but the vector 1_[1/4,3/4] in the second summand has a nonzero Lebesgue spectral measure. The explicit spectral-measure argument, not its small rational-grid diagnostic, proves this counterexample. The reviewed note avoids the invalid inference by proving Hpp = H.
