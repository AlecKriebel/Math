## Exact claims and boundaries

- The fixed model is the displayed-tree mixture with a uniform root and K3P transition matrices; there is no network multispecies coalescent process in this paper. The three nonzero Fourier state labels remain fixed.
- Networks are finite, binary, standard semi-directed, level at most two, and strongly tree-child. Standard means one-step suppression of the binary root without subsequent cleanup, retaining the relevant arrowheads. Strong means every admissible rooting is tree-child; weak means at least one.
- The principal domain requires 0<c,g,t<1 and the three positive inverse-Fourier inequalities, with inheritance strictly in (0,1). The strict continuous-time domain adds c>gt, g>ct, t>cg. Zero rates/eigenvalues, identity or signed boundaries, and boundary inheritance are outside the theorem.
- The main relation is a source-open regular analytic family with a physical analytic target section. It is directional and can involve a nonregular target. It is stronger than a shared point and different from complex variety containment or equality of full stochastic images.
- Theorem 2.1 identifies this relation with labelled blob-tree agreement and isomorphic/ordinary-triangle-related complete factors with coherent transports. The common germ is full-dimensional relative to each image. Thus there is no *proper* one-sided regular containment in this class.
- Theorem 2.2 is generic topology identification modulo ordinary triangle redirection, outside a proper Zariski-closed exceptional set for each fixed source topology. It is not pointwise topology identification at every parameter.
- Theorem 2.3 is termination with an exact-real representation supporting polynomial-sign decisions and real-closed-field quantifier elimination. It gives no bit-complexity, conditioning, finite-sample or sequence-length guarantee, and no universal numerical parameter identification.
- Theorem 5.2 says the ordinary three-leaf triangle maps have normalized rank 14, one common irreducible eight-term quartic closure H14 in A15, and a common strict continuous-time smooth relative germ. Their physical images are not claimed equal and do not fill an ambient-open 15-dimensional germ.
- Theorem 2.5 supplies, for every n≥3, pairs in WTC minus STC with a common regular germ of dimension 6n−3. This shows that replacing strong by weak makes the class theorem false. It does not classify all weak networks or say every weak network is ambiguous. Uniqueness of a Krawczyk root concerns one selected equality slice, not global parameter identification.
- Proposition 2.6 is a separate outer-class tree–double-theta collision, outside even WTC. It must not be conflated with the sharp weak-versus-strong example.
- The bridge claim is deliberately asymmetric: true cuts have rank≤4 pointwise, noncuts are detected generically, and equality of cut sets is proved under strong-class containment with 204 bounded pointwise obstructions. No universal pointwise converse is promoted.

## Contribution and primary literature

The bounded search found relevant earlier work that supports the manuscript’s qualified attribution. It does not establish exhaustive novelty or priority.

1. Gross et al., *Distinguishing level-1 phylogenetic networks on the basis of data generated by Markov processes* (2021), [doi:10.1007/s00285-021-01653-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-021-01653-8). Theorem 2 covers triangle-free level-1 networks with a fixed reticulation count under JC/K2P/K3P. The discussion before Lemma 1 already observes identical ideals for all three 3-leaf triangle orientations. The new paper therefore cannot claim discovery of their algebraic indistinguishability.
2. Gross, Krone, and Martin, *Dimensions of Level-1 Group-Based Phylogenetic Networks* (2024), [doi:10.1007/s11538-024-01314-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-024-01314-z). Section 7 and Table 1 record the K3P three-sunlet deficiency computationally. The present claim is an exact rank certificate, explicit irreducible quartic, and physical germ, embedded in a global classification; it is not the first numerical observation of the deficiency.
3. Cox, Gross, and Martin, *Group-based phylogenetic models on 3-sunlet networks* (2025), [doi:10.1007/s11538-025-01506-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01506-1). Its main dimension theorem concerns odd-order groups (with stated small exceptions); K3P uses Z2×Z2. It is relevant context, not a competing general K3P level-2 theorem.
4. Englander et al., *Identifiability of Phylogenetic Level-2 Networks under the Jukes–Cantor Model* (2025; revised 2026), [doi:10.1101/2025.04.18.649493](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493). The primary abstract establishes generic JC identification for triangle-free strongly tree-child binary semi-directed level-2 networks, plus broader quartet tools for JC/K2P. The current source cites v4, July 2026; that full v4 endpoint was inaccessible in the browsing tool, so the comparison here uses the accessible primary abstract and does not certify all revised details. K3P, triangle inclusion, and physical directed containment distinguish the present claim.
5. Brits et al., *On Tree-Network Distinguishability and Full Identifiability of Phylogenetic Networks*, [arXiv:2607.12919v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12919v3), revised 2026-08-25. Its level-1 topology theorem is pointwise modulo triangle redirection on a restricted domain. The present level-2 theorem remains generic; it must not be advertised as the first pointwise network-identification theorem. The manuscript explicitly discusses translation between cleanup conventions. The current v3 abstract limits its broad tree-versus-network statement to JC; an older search snippet also mentioned K2P, so that stale snippet was not used.
6. Currie et al., *Semialgebraic Conditions for Identifying Triangles in Phylogenetic Networks*, [arXiv:2606.26673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26673). It gives complete JC triangle images with full-dimensional intersections and differences and says K2P/K3P are not immediate consequences. The K3P common relative germ is an exact local refinement in a different model, not a complete semialgebraic image classification.
7. The JC, K2P and tree–theta companion Zenodo papers are by the same author. They supply graph-framework provenance and separate results; they are not independent corroboration. The K3P paper correctly states that it rebuilds the three-sector algebra rather than transferring two-sector identities unchanged.

The paper’s most interesting content for a general page is the combination of the rank-14 triangle hypersurface and the sharp strong-versus-weak class boundary. The global result is a classification of robust local stochastic ambiguity, not a claim that all network parameters can be reconstructed from practical DNA data.

A possible apparent bibliography discrepancy was checked and dismissed: Sturmfels–Sullivant’s *Toric Ideals of Phylogenetic Invariants* appears in primary publisher records at both 12(2):204–228 (doi:10.1089/cmb.2005.12.204) and 12(4):457–481 (doi:10.1089/cmb.2005.12.457). The manuscript’s first citation is therefore not flagged merely because another paper cites the second.
