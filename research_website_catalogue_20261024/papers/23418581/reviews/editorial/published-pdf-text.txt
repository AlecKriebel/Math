                        Simple Young modules with nonprojective
                                polynomial injective hulls
                                                    Alec Kriebel∗

                                        16 October 2026 (version 1.0)



                                                      Abstract
              For every odd prime p, the Young module labelled by λ = (2p − 2, 1) in characteristic p is
          simple, whereas its corresponding polynomial injective hull In (λ) is nonprojective for every n ≥ 2.
          At parameter q = 1, this gives a negative answer to the unrestricted formulation of Miyachi’s
          Conjecture 7 in the 2009 Oberwolfach report. The proof combines classical representation theory
          with a labelled rank-truncation argument. We include explicit proofs of the local calculations
          and reproducible supporting checks. Historical priority remains unresolved; no ﬁrstness claim
          is made.


1        The printed question
Let k be algebraically closed. In degree r, write In (ν) for the injective hull of the simple polynomial
GLn (k)-module Ln (ν), equivalently its injective hull for the Schur algebra S(n, r). At stable rank
N ≥ r, the Schur functor F deﬁnes Y ν = FIN (ν), the Young module in the convention of [1,
pp. 945–946]. Here the parameter is q = 1, so the Hecke algebra is kSr ; in characteristic p, its
quantum characteristic is p.
    The ﬁrst proposal in [1, Conjecture 7, p. 946] asks whether simplicity of top(Y ν ) is equivalent to
projectivity of In (ν) for some n ≥ ℓ(ν). The index-set criterion involving Λin is presented there as
a stronger proposal. The characteristic-zero assumption in the subsequent subsection 0.5 concerns
a diﬀerent method for simple Specht modules; it is not attached to the displayed Conjecture 7.
Theorem 1. Let p be any odd prime and let k be algebraically closed of characteristic p. Put
r = 2p − 1 and λ = (2p − 2, 1). Then Y λ is simple of dimension 2p − 2, but In (λ) is not projective
for any integer n ≥ 2.
   Thus p = 3, λ = (4, 1) already refutes the broad printed proposal. The theorem does not
address a characteristic-zero variant with a nontrivial root-of-unity parameter, nor the separate
De Visscher–Donkin classiﬁcation of projective-injective polynomial modules. In particular, being
row p-regular is not being p-restricted (column p-regular), and no projectivity inference from row
regularity is used.

4    The rank-two obstruction
Write E = k 2 , with basis x, y. In a ﬁxed symmetric power, the monomials vi = xm−i y i have
distinct algebraic-torus weights. An invariant subspace contains individual weight components.
It also contains every coeﬃcient in t of a unipotent translate: Vandermonde inversion over the
inﬁnite ﬁeld k extracts those coeﬃcients from evaluations. This is a statement about rational
GL2 (k)-modules, not just the ﬁnite group GL2 (Fp ).

Lemma 1. The module Sym2p−1 E is simple. The module B = Sym2p−3 E is a nonsplit extension
of two distinct simple modules: its socle has highest weight (2p − 3, 0) and dimension 2p − 4; its top
has highest weight (p − 1, p − 2) and dimension 2.
Proof. From any monomial, the substitution y 7→ y +tx extracts the highest monomial. Translating
that highest monomial in the opposite direction extracts the binomial-coeﬃcient multiples of all
monomials. For degree 2p − 1, every coeﬃcient is nonzero, since in characteristic p

                                  (1 + t)2p−1 = (1 + tp )(1 + t)p−1 ,

and the coeﬃcients of (1 + t)p−1 are alternating units. This proves the ﬁrst assertion.
   For m = 2p − 3, let

                       W = span{vi : 0 ≤ i ≤ p − 3 or p ≤ i ≤ 2p − 3} ⊂ B.

It is the image of the injective equivariant multiplication E (1) ⊗ Symp−3 E → B, where E (1) denotes
Frobenius twist, via x(1) ⊗ f 7→ xp f and y (1) ⊗ f 7→ y p f . The two monomial ranges are disjoint.
The same highest-monomial argument, now using

                                  (1 + t)2p−3 = (1 + tp )(1 + t)p−3 ,

shows that every nonzero submodule of W contains all its displayed monomials. Hence W is simple
with the stated highest weight and dimension.
    The quotient has the two weight lines represented by vp−2 , vp−1 . The opposite unipotents
connect them with coeﬃcient p−1 6= 0, so the quotient is simple of highest weight (p−1, p−2). Any
invariant complement to W must be a sum of torus weight spaces, hence must equal span(vp−2 , vp−1 ).
But the translate of vp−2 under y 7→ y + tx has the nonzero coeﬃcient x2p−3 ∈ W at tp−2 . There
is no complement. A nonsplit extension of two distinct simples has precisely the asserted socle and
top.

   These rank-two structures are established representation theory; for published formulas yielding
them by duality and determinant twist, see [4, Proposition 3.1, classical analogue, and Theorem 3.3].
The explicit proof makes their use here checkable.
   Only (r) strictly dominates λ = (r − 1, 1). By Lemma 1, ∇2 (r) = Symr E is simple and does
not contain L2 (λ). Equation (1) therefore gives

                                      I2 (λ) = ∇2 (λ) = det ⊗B.

Its socle and top are L2 (2p − 2, 1) and L2 (p, p − 1), respectively. Their highest weights diﬀer,
including when p = 3 and both dimensions are 2. Thus I2 (λ) is not contravariantly self-dual and
is not projective.

Corollary 1. The stronger simple-top/Λin criterion in the same printed Conjecture 7 is false in

Proof. Membership in the constructed Λin family is already a suﬃcient condition for In (λ) to be

6    Veriﬁcation, attribution and status
The companion archive preserves the frozen candidate, independent mathematical and priority
reviews, source identities and reproducible exact checks. Formal polynomial-coeﬃcient calculations
in characteristics 3 and 5 verify the local constituents and nonsplitting. A separate tensor-orbit
computation constructs all 56 basis elements of S(2, 5), checks its 3136 products and independently
ﬁnds dim P2 (3, 2) = 6, whereas dim I2 (4, 1) = 4. That optional computation uses NumPy; the core
checks use Python’s standard library. Finite computations support the argument, and do not prove
its all-prime or all-rank assertions.
    The Young/augmentation calculation, rank-two structure and Schur-algebra inputs are classical.
The note’s purpose is their explicit assembly into a counterexample to the broad printed 2009
proposal. An extensive but bounded literature audit on 16 October 2026 did not verify an earlier
complete bridge refutation. Priority remains unresolved: some older two-row papers, tables and
later endomorphism-algebra papers were not available in full, and citation coverage is incomplete.
No claim of ﬁrstness, certiﬁed novelty, or current worldwide open status is made. The source archive
records exact read scopes and gaps.
    AI tools were used extensively in developing and checking the argument, literature investigation,
computations, drafting, reproduction and adversarial review. This is an unrefereed preprint without
external human peer review or proof-assistant certiﬁcation. The note and authored companion
material are licensed under CC BY 4.0.
