                                                                                     Abstract
                                                       The problem of classifying all unitary R-matrices of arbitrary ﬁnite dimen-
                                                   sion that have precisely two distinct eigenvalues is described, working up to a
                                                   natural equivalence relation given by the characters of their braid group repre-
                                                   sentations. Up to one class that might or might not exist in even dimension
                                                   larger than two, a full classiﬁcation theorem is obtained.


                                           1       Introduction
                                           The (quantum) Yang-Baxter Equation (YBE) is an algebraic nonlinear equation for a
                                           linear map R : V ⊗ V → V ⊗ V on the tensor square of a vector space V , namely

                                                                (R ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ R)(R ⊗ 1) = (1 ⊗ R)(R ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ R)                         (1.1)

                                           as an equation in End(V ⊗ V ⊗ V ), where 1 denotes idV . This equation plays a
                                           role in remarkably many diﬀerent ﬁelds in mathematics and mathematical physics,
                                           such as braid group representations [Jon87], knot theory [Tur88], subfactors [Wen90],
                                           quantum groups [CP94], Hopf algebras [AG03], statistical mechanics [Bax82], quantum
                                           ﬁeld theory [AAR01], and quantum computing [RW17]. It also has several interesting
                                           variants, such as the classical Yang-Baxter equation [BK12] or the set-theoretic Yang-
                                           Baxter equation [CV26].
                                               This widespread appearance of the YBE provides of course ample interest in un-
                                           derstanding the solutions of (1.1), and has triggered approaches using various diﬀerent
                                           tools ranging from algebraic and categorical methods over combinatorics to operator-
                                           algebraic ideas. Naive direct attempts to solve it run into the problem that (1.1)
                                           amounts to a system of (dim V )6 cubic equations in (dim V )4 variables, which is not
                                           manageable even for low dim V > 2. From a more general point of view, the structure
                                           of the solution set of the YBE – either for ﬁxed V or for a class of vector spaces – is
                                           largely unknown.
                                               One therefore typically restricts attention to subfamilies of solutions, such as so-
                                           lutions of dimension two [Hie92], solutions representing the Temperley-Lieb algebra
                                           [Byt15], charge-conserving solutions [HMR24], set-theoretic solutions [CV26], or uni-
                                           tary and involutive solutions [LPW19].

    The challenging general problem of classifying R/∼ is currently open. It is however
possible to restrict to subfamilies of R that are compatible with ∼, for instance by
restricting the dimension or the spectrum of R. The family of all unitary R-matrics of
dimension dim R = 2 can be completely classiﬁed up to equivalence, as will be demon-
strated at the end of this section. Already for dim V = 3, no comparable classiﬁcation
result is known (see, however, [HMR24], for a classiﬁcation of the charge-conserving
R-matrices in dimension three). Part of the problem is, as seen in Proposition 1.2,
R-matrices with various diﬀerent spectra and diﬀerent cardinalities of spectra appear,
which leads to many equivalence classes.
    This observation suggests that instead of prescribing the dimension, one might
consider R-matrices with prescribed spectrum. Apart from the trivial case σ(R) = {q}
of R-matrices R = q·1 that are multiples of the identity, the classiﬁcation is understood
in the family of all unitary R-matrices with spectrum σ(R) ⊂ {1, −1}, i.e. the unitary
                                                                                       (n)
involutive R-matrices (of arbitrary dimension). In this case, the representations ρR
factor through the symmetric group Sn . The involutive unitary R-matrices have been
classiﬁed in [LPW19] by using extremal characters of the inﬁnite symmetric group.
One ﬁnds an explicit bijection with the set of pairs of Young diagrams, and for each
equivalence class, a representative can be speciﬁed.

    In this paper, we go one step further and describe the more general classiﬁcation
problem for unitary R-matrices R with two eigenvalues, i.e. |σ(R)| = 2. This family
includes all unitary Yang-Baxter representations of the Temperley-Lieb and Hecke
algebras, underlying the Jones polynomial. As we will see, it contains non-involutive
elements which can be seen as “non-classical deformations” of symmetries (involutive
R-matrices).
    Without the restriction to unitary R-matrices, this family has been considered
by Gurevich [Gur93] and Ram [Ram91]. The restriction to unitary R-matrices has
however a quite eﬀect. As described by Rowell and Wang [RW12] (see also [DRW17]),
most R-matrices are not unitary, and unitary local (in the sense of tensor products)
braid group representations are rare. As unitary R-matrices with two eigenvalues can
also be described in terms of Hecke or Temperley-Lieb algebras, one may equivalently
study the classiﬁcation question in terms of certain representations of these algebras.
In a concrete matrix setting, this question has been addressed by Bytsko [Byt15;
Byt22].
    In contrast, we will here use the subfactor framework developed with Conti in
[CL21]. We will recall the relevant notions in Section 2, focussing in particular on
Markov traces. There we also recall a family of unitary R-matrices known as Gaussian
R-matrices, going back to work of Goldschmidt and Jones [GJ89; Jon89a; Jon89b] and
later generalized by Galindo and Rowell [GR14].
    Whereas Section 2 still applies to arbitrary unitary R-matrices, we specialize to
R-matrices with two eigenvalues in Section 3, ﬁxing without loss of generality the
spectrum to be
                          σ(R) = {−1, q},       q ∈ T \ {−1}.
As the involutive family, corresponding to q = 1, has already been classiﬁed and
behaves very diﬀerently from q 6= 1, we restrict to the non-involutive case q 6= ±1.
    Applying the results from the general framework, we show that the equivalence
classes of unitary non-involutive R-matrices with two eigenvalues are labelled by three

data: The eigenvalue q, the normalized trace η ∈ (0, 1) of the spectral projection
corresponding to eigenvalue −1, and the dimension d ∈ N. These three data q, η, d are
not independent, and the task is to understand which triples (q, η, d) are realized by
R-matrices.
    In this context, a main result is Theorem 3.4, which builds on Wenzl’s work on
Hecke algebra subfactors [Wen88] and a rationality property of braid group characters
(Proposition 2.1), and establishes strong constraints on the possible triples (q, η, d):
Only eight families of equivalence classes, each with ﬁxed q and η, but varying dimen-
sion d, remain. In particular, these classes are empty unless q ∈ {i, −i, eiπ/3 , e−iπ/3 }.
When restricting to the Temperley-Lieb subfamily, this recovers a theorem of Bytsko
[Byt19].
    By exploiting Temperley-Lieb structure and symmetries, these eight families can
be described by just three families. We then show in Proposition 3.6 that two of these
three families can be exhausted by Gaussian R-matrices and tensor products with
identities. The last family of classes, corresponding to q = eiπ/3 , η = 12 , and arbitrary
even dimension d, is not yet understood. We show that it is empty for d = 2, but do
not have a proof that this remains true for d > 2. R-matrices of this type would need
to represent the Hecke algebra but not the Temperley-Lieb algebra, and correspond
to an unkown localization of a speciﬁc braid group representation [GHR12].
    Some of our results were already presented in the Oberwolfach proceedings [Lec25].

3    Unitary Hecke R-matrices
As motivated in the introduction, we now restrict attention to unitary R-matrices
with prescribed spectrum. ∏    That is, we ﬁx a ﬁnite subset S ⊂ T of the unit circle and

    If S = {λ}, the only unitary R-matrix with spectrum S is trivially R = λ1. In the
case of two distinct eigenvalues, S = {λ1 , λ2 } with λ1 6= λ2 , the situation becomes much
more interesting. We ﬁrst note that since the Yang-Baxter equation is homogeneous,
multiples of R-matrices are again R-matrices, and two R-matrices R, S with spectrum
{λ1 , λ2 } are equivalent if and only if −λ−1         −1

with spectrum σ(R) = {−1, q} and base space V or arbitrary (ﬁnite-dimensional) base

because the algebra H∞ (q) := (CB∞ ){−1,q} is known as the (inﬁnite) Hecke algebra
(of type A).
