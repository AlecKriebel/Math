## Reconstructed mathematical argument

1. **Exact local witness.** In the real Pauli basis with (J=XZ=-iY), the four words (A,B,C,D) are commuting Hermitian involutions, (D=ABC), and (E) anticommutes with each. Thus (M=(-A-B-C+D)/2) satisfies (M^2=E^2=I), (ME=-EM). The displayed (H=\sqrt{2/3}M-E/\sqrt3) is a real symmetric reflection with trace zero. With (q=e^{i\pi/3}), (R=(q-1)I/2+(q+1)H/2) has eigenvalues (-1,q), each eightfold, and is unitary. Its negative spectral projector has rank eight and unnormalized partial trace (2I_4) on either site.

2. **The exact scope of the four-point statement.** The 18-word residual is a polynomial identity in two real parameters, before restriction to the unit circle. Setting its coefficients and (alpha^2+eta^2-1) to zero gives precisely (alpha^2=2/3,eta^2=1/3), hence four signed points. Both coordinate-axis cases fail, as does the rational circle point ((3/5,4/5)). This exhausts this particular reflection circle. It does not classify all 16-by-16 Yang–Baxter operators or all exceptional solutions. Inserting (a/b=i/\sqrt3) into the reflection cubic establishes the actual braid equation.

3. **Faithfulness for every strand number.** The scalar partial trace of (P) makes normalized tensor trace a consistent Markov trace with parameter (1/2). Wenzl's uniqueness gives the target trace. If (x) belongs to its annihilator, apply it to (x^*x); faithful matrix trace forces (
ho_n(x)=0). The converse follows by testing all (yx). This proves equality of the kernel and trace annihilator, not just containment, and proves injectivity of the quotient representation. Tensoring with (I_4) proves compatibility and injectivity of the quotient inclusions. The GHR identification of the braid-generated algebra with (mathrm{End}_{\mathcal C}(X^{\otimes n})=H_n(3,6)) makes this an ordinary unitary localization of the whole sequence. It is not a fiber functor.

4. **Minimum local dimension four.** Dimension one cannot support both eigenvalues; dimension two is excluded by the stated GHR lemma. In dimension three, injectivity at two strands forces both eigenprojections to be nonzero. Lechner's positive-Markov-character theorem and the fixed-(q) Wenzl classification give only (eta=1/3,1/2,2/3). Rank excludes (1/2). For (c=1/3), (T=e_1e_2e_1-ce_1) has squared trace norm ((1-c)\eta(\eta-c)); this follows directly from the projection Hecke relation and cyclicity. A parameter-(1/3) representation kills (T), while the target parameter-(1/2) representation has norm (1/18). Complementing (e_i) proves the parameter-(2/3) obstruction. No assumption that an arbitrary proposed localizer initially has trace (1/2) is smuggled in. Identity amplification provides dimensions (4m), with no claim to settle every even dimension.

5. **Active three-qubit operator.** Swapping qubits within each four-dimensional site yields (I_2\otimes K), with the displayed (K_H). Removing the first spectator along the chain yields the ((3,2)) placement on (2n-1) qubits; distant active triples are disjoint. The kernel is unchanged by the spectator. The wrong shift-one residual has squared Frobenius norm 24. The separate comparison with the old GHR operator is the only source-version limitation below.

6. **Quaternionic and local-equivalence statements.** (U_K=(A'+A'B')/2,V_K=(A'-A'B')/2), with (A'=-i\sqrt2M,B'=iE), give quaternionic generators and (U_K+V_K+U_KV_K=-i\sqrt3H). The literal GR2026 §13 matrix uses (P_Z=IZZI,P_X=XIXX). The printed unitary (S) conjugates its *opposite* to (R). Omitting the site swap does not satisfy this displayed conjugacy. The paper correctly leaves open whether another direct common-local-unitary equivalence exists.

7. **All-strand equivalence and finite image.** Site reversal turns opposite local generators into generators with index (n-i). The Garside half twist implements this index reversal internally. Therefore (D_n^\dagger\mathrm{Rev}_nS^{\otimes n}) intertwines the same braid word, for every (n). This is stronger than a bare two-site spectral similarity. The GR operator is a scalar root of unity times ordered Pauli quarter turns, so its projective Clifford image is finite. Finite local order and determinant constrain the central scalar subgroup to finitely many roots of unity; the ordinary braid image is therefore finite. The five-word operator is Clifford in the transported Pauli frame, not necessarily the computational frame. The full quaternionic tower has dimension (4^{n-1}); its braid-generated subalgebra, not the full tower, is (H_n(3,6)).

8. **Enhancement and topology.** The partial traces of (R^{\pm1}) are (2\kappa^{\pm1}I), where (kappa=q-1=q^2). Turaev's actual conventions therefore give (\mathcal J_R(\widehat\xi)=\kappa^{-w(\xi)}2^{-n}\mathrm{Tr}\rho_n(\xi)), with unknot value 2. The Hecke skein and (q\kappa^{-2}=-1) give (J_++J_-=J_0), hence (J=2P_H(i,i)), mirror invariance and unlink value (2^c). (R^3=-I) gives the claimed three-twist sign, not a full braid-group identification. The oriented triple cover is precisely the cover sending each positive meridian to 1; Lickorish–Millett's component-sign conversion gives (2(-1)^{c-1}(-2)^{d_2/2}). Its even-parity input follows from the source's dimension-change proof.

9. **Exact polynomial-time evaluation.** The import fixes the metric group (A=\mathbb Z/2) and its phases; only strand number and braid word vary. The coefficient trace is a constrained quadratic finite-group sum. Character orthogonality removes the linear constraint, yielding a polynomial-size quadratic sum over a fixed modulus, covered by the cited exact algorithm. This is not a claim that dense matrices of dimension (4^n) can be manipulated in polynomial time.
