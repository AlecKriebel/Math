## Claim and necessary qualifications

For sufficiently large natural dimension n, an actual strong involution t in Sp, O, SO, or Omega over an odd finite field, and exactly uniform independent conjugators g, each specified t-eigenspace separately has probability at least c/log(n) of a strong restriction of inv(tt^g). The manuscript gives c=1/40000 for Sp and c=1/5000000 for O/SO/Omega, with explicit marginal thresholds. A further theorem gives O(log(n)(1+log(1/delta))) samples whose outputs generate a subgroup containing C_G(t)''. Its dimension threshold is existential because the imported maximal-subgroup constant is existential.

The main claim is about the number of uniform samples, not efficient implementation, recognition of completion, all dimensions, characteristic two, or arbitrary projective involutions/order-four lifts. Separate strength is essential: simultaneous strength can fail in arbitrarily large dimensions.

## 1. Chart and unconditional masses

For the graph W={(u,Fu)}, the projection along W-perp is determined by (1+F*F)^{-1}(u+F*v). Multiplying 1-2P_W by t gives the displayed matrix (3), with eigenvalue relation lambda+lambda^{-1}=2(1-kappa)/(1+kappa). The excluded values kappa=0,-1 are precisely where the needed invertibility or eigenvalue distinction breaks. With squarefree K, the product is semisimple; a unique maximal nontrivial order-2-part selects exactly that primary block. The selected negative dimensions are equal on A and B.

The orthogonal class-law transfer is valid: both large nondegenerate eigenspaces contain a hyperbolic plane, whose quadratic form represents every nonzero field element. Its reflections give determinant -1 with either spinor character, so O=Omega C_O(t). This also covers conjugation of -t for relabelling even when -t is outside the chosen special group.

In the symplectic case, self-adjointness makes each irreducible primary block a two-dimensional vector space over its extension field with a nondegenerate alternating trace form. The trace pairing and odd characteristic justify the alternating lift. The isometry centralizer is Sp_2(q^d), not a general linear group. Witt extension gives the embedding fibre |Sp(B)|/|Sp(B-A)|. Dividing the count of actual distinct graphs by the complete conjugacy-class size yields equation (5), without a missing chart-conditioning factor. The q exponent is -a. The bound follows from (7/8)^4>1/2, with Sp_0 of order one.

In the orthogonal case, each primary trace form has two coefficient square classes and centralizer order two. Exactly half of all coefficient assignments realize A's discriminant; their equally sized orbits yield |O(A)|/2 operators per polynomial. Determinant characters depend only on the polynomial. For positive complement dimension a quadratic space of either determinant exists; for zero complement the imposed determinant equality is necessary and retained. ## 2. Polynomial counting, including q=3

Lemma 4.1's degree assertion is valid: lambda is quadratic over F_q(kappa), so full degree k can fall only to k/2. Such a fall forces lambda^{q^{k/2}}=lambda^{-1}, contradicting maximal 2-part because k/2 is even. The map has fibres {lambda,lambda^{-1}}; dividing by 2k is therefore correct. The proper-subfield bound leaves at least q^k/4 full-degree maximal-2-part elements at the smallest allowed q=3,k=4 and improves thereafter. LTE gives the strict 2-part distinction for all other factor degrees not divisible by d. The determinant signs +1,-1 follow from k divisible by four, lambda nonsquare, and preservation of quadratic character under the field norm.

The q=3 squarefree generating function was independently reconstructed as (1-u^2/3)/[(1-u)(1+u/3)^2], with coefficient 3/8+(-1)^r(10-8r)/(16*3^r). Its minimum relevant low-degree values are 1/3. The three nonprincipal evaluation characters have ordinary series 1+su, |s|=1, because the two evaluations are independent from degree two onward. Their squarefree series is (1+su)(1-qu^2)/(1-u^2)^2. Fourier inversion consequently gives the stated lower bound for every one of the four sign sectors, including q=3. The harmonic upper bound on factors whose degrees are divisible by d is sufficient even without restricting to squarefree polynomials.

Both target intervals have width at least a/6. The count of odd multiples of d loses at most one endpoint; a>=24d absorbs this loss. The remainder has positive degree at least a/6, contains no factor of degree divisible by d, and cannot duplicate the distinguished factor. Thus the products are squarefree and counted once. Threshold inequalities hold at a=8192 (Sp) and a=32768 (O), and their derivatives remain positive. For the Sp numerical constant one can use a<=n/4, so 1+log(a)<log(n), and 1536*24=36864<40000. This relation is implicit in the ordered half-dimensions and supplies the claimed strict bound.

Lemma 4.3 is a genuine equal-fibre refinement. The nonzero remainder supplies another primary block. Toggling its coefficient type along with the distinguished block preserves total discriminant while toggling the distinguished discriminant. Since k is divisible by four and det(K) there is square, both selected blocks have determinant one and equal spinor character. Exactly half of the chart mass is therefore in Omega(A) x Omega(B); no unproved independence of spinor and determinant is used.

## 3. The 192-class theorem

The full-isometry nondegenerate orbit bounds follow from the displayed order factors. A fixed invariant nondegenerate subspace splits nondegenerately across the two involution eigenspaces. Summing over dimensions and orthogonal types gives 16(k+1) times the largest numerator power. The exponent loss ib+ja-2ij is at least ek/3-k^2/2>=ek/12 for k<=e/2.

For totally singular subspaces, the ordered-basis enumeration gives the displayed product for T(e,k). Its exponent is ke-(3k^2+sigma*k)/2. For even split orthogonal maximal singular subspaces the product includes both families; the full O action permits using that combined orbit. In each feasible orthogonal type the normalized negative factors have product at least 1/2 and positive factors have product below four; the denominator has product above 1/2. Independently, 6,600 exact product calculations for q in {3,5,7,9,11}, dimension up to 40, and every feasible k/type were integral and within the manuscript bounds.

The invariant singular-subspace exponent loss ib+ja-3ij is at least (ib+ja)/4>=ek/12, because i<=a/2 and j<=b/2. Full O-class equality with the Omega class makes the fixed-subspace probability applicable even if an Omega subspace orbit splits. Every reducible natural subgroup is captured either by a radical or by a nondegenerate space/complement. The 192 independent prescribed classes therefore give the stated reducibility union bound; their classes need not coincide.

For non-subspace maximal subgroups, the strong projective class has size at least (21/256)q^{2e^2/9}>=q^{e^2/5}. The projective fibre contains at most the two scalar multiples of a lift. Burness's exponent is at most -1/3 for e>=16. Thus the probability per maximal subgroup is at most q^{-192e^2/15}; multiplying by the LPS upper bound at most C q^{e^2} gives C q^{-59e^2/5}. The displayed e0 absorbs C uniformly even at q=3. Combining both estimates gives failure at most q^{-e}.

Finally KZ(J)=J really implies K=J here: the natural group J is perfect and Z(J) central, so J=J'=(KZ(J))'=K'<=K. The proof does not incorrectly identify generation of the simple quotient with generation of an arbitrary central extension.

## 4. Centralizer and 385-block bridge

For the relevant natural centralizer C, the product L of Sp or Omega factors is perfect and C/L is abelian; consequently C'=C''=L. For a centralizing involution z, its four joint eigenspaces are nondegenerate and one has dimension at least n/4. Reflections in that large joint space map onto every determinant/spinor quotient condition defining D/C. Hence D=C C_D(z) and a C-class of z is exactly a product of component D-classes. Conditional independence of the component values follows from uniformity on that product, rather than being assumed unconditionally.

The kernel lemma is valid. An automorphism acting trivially on the simple quotient induces a central homomorphism on a perfect natural group and thus centralizes that natural group. Symplectic transvections force scalar action. In the orthogonal case the common fixed space of the Siegel transformations based at a singular line is precisely that line. Preserving all singular lines forces a common scalar on a Witt basis and on its anisotropic complement, by the explicit pairs of singular vectors in the proof. An isometry scalar is +/-1.

The Goursat argument covers every abstract simple-quotient graph, not only equal-size or equal-type factors. Once the first samples fix a graph, an extra output failing to break it must satisfy the stated conjugation-action equation. Given its A-component this leaves at most two B-components. There is correctly no union over graph isomorphisms after conditioning on the already generated subgroup. Perfectness lifts full quotient-product generation back to L.

First-success selection from each of 385 disjoint blocks has exactly the event-conditioned law, and conditioning on all block successes preserves block independence. The first 192 target A and the next 192 target B; the final B-strong admissible sample breaks a remaining graph. The failure bound eta+q^{-a}+q^{-b}+13q^{-2b^2/9} follows. Fixed eta=1/20 gives a constant-success trial; retaining the union from independent trials gives the desired dependence on delta without requiring recognition of successful samples or completion.

## 5. Imported theorem hypotheses checked directly

- Burness, *Fixed point ratios in actions of finite classical groups, I*: https://seis.bristol.ac.uk/~tb13602/docs/fpr1.pdf. Read Definitions 1–2, Theorem 1, Table 1 and Corollary 1. Applied to G=S simple and the maximal-coset action, the action is faithful and the image of a strong natural involution has prime order two. In large Sp/O dimension the relevant table corrections are 1/e, 1/(e+2), or 1/(e-2), all bounded by 1/(e-2); the special low-dimensional and characteristic-two cases are excluded. The minimal natural-module convention agrees in these large dimensions. This review verifies the import's scope, not the full classification-dependent proof series.
- Liebeck–Pyber–Shalev, *On a conjecture of G. E. Wall*: https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~mwl/WALL.PDF. Read introduction and Theorem 1.1: the theorem counts actual maximal subgroups, not just conjugacy classes, and has an absolute constant for almost simple groups of Lie type. The simple Sp/O quotients are covered. No effective numeric constant is supplied or assumed here.
- Taylor, *The Geometry of the Classical Groups*, corrected author edition: https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/don/papers/gcg.pdf. Read relevant statement and proof surroundings for 8.7–8.8, 11.24, 11.45–11.48, and 11.50–11.51. They provide the required large-dimension perfectness, simple projective quotients, Siegel membership, and determinant/spinor kernel characterization. All exceptions are far below the manuscript's threshold. Standard finite-field form classification, nondegenerate trace pairings and Witt extension are accepted as the explicitly identified background theory.

## 6. Boundary checks and limits

The Sp_4 counterexample is correct: on a symplectic plane only +/-1 have order dividing two, so neither restriction is strong. The equal-negative-dimension identity for inv(ts) remains valid for nonsemisimple products: separate the commuting odd and 2-parts, use reciprocal eigenvalue pairing over the algebraic closure when the 2-part has order at least four, and use the odd-order square root conjugation on its two eigenspaces when the 2-part has order two. Eigenspace dimensions, not modular traces, justify the integer identity. At the 2:1 eigenspace boundary simultaneous strength forces the common negative dimension to be 4a/3 and fails when 3 does not divide a.
