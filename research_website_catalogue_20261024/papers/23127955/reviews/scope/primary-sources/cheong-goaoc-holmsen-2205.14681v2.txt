                                                 THE TOPOLOGY OF THE SET OF LINE TRANSVERSALS

                                                    OTFRIED CHEONG, XAVIER GOAOC, AND ANDREAS F. HOLMSEN




                                                 Abstract. We prove that for any set F of n ≥ 2 pairwise disjoint open convex sets
                                                 in R3 , the connected components of the set of lines intersecting every member of
                                                 F are contractible. The same result holds for directed lines.




arXiv:2205.14681v2 [math.MG] 5 Sep 2024
                                                                               1. Introduction
                                             Let F be a family of convex sets in Rd . A line transversal to F is a line that
                                          intersects every member of F. Here is the main result that we prove in this note:
                                          Theorem 1. For any family F of n ≥ 2 pairwise disjoint open convex sets in R3 ,
                                          every connected component of line transversals to F is acyclic.
                                          Here, the space of lines in R3 is endowed with its natural topology (see Section 2).
                                          Recall that a set is acyclic if it has trivial homology in dimension 1 and higher. The
                                          statement of Theorem 1 remains true when the lines are directed. A natural question
                                          is whether the assumption that the sets be open can be replaced by a compactness
                                          condition; We do not know if this is possible, but offer some comments in the final
                                          section.

                                             Our proof goes through the analysis of the cone of directions T (F) of the family
                                          F of convex sets, defined as the set of vectors x ∈ S2 for which there exists a
                                          line transversal to F in the direction x. We first establish that S2 \ T (F) is path-
                                          connected (Lemma 5, and the cornerstone of our proof), and it is a well-known fact
                                          that this implies that the connected components of T (F) are contractible. With the
                                          Vietoris-Begle mapping theorem, this implies Theorem 1.
                                          1.1. Context and motivation. Our motivation to study the topology of sets of
                                          line transversals to convex sets originates in questions raised in combinatorial ge-
                                          ometry, more precisely in geometric transversal theory. We refer to the chapters
                                          by Goodman, Pollack and Wenger [15] and by Goodman and Pollack [14] or the
                                          survey of Holmsen and Wenger [21] for an overview.

                                             Helly’s theorem asserts that for any finite family F of convex sets in Rd , if every
                                          d + 1 members of F have a point in common, then all members of F have a point
                                          in common. A natural question is whether a similar phenomenon holds for higher-
                                          dimensional transversals, for instance:

                                                  Is there a constant Hd such that for any finite family of pairwise
                                                  disjoint convex sets in Rd , if every Hd members of F have a line
                                                  transversals then F has a line transversal.

The answer is negative in general, already for d = 2, but positive under further
restrictions on F. In particular, the influence of the geometry of the members of
F on the existence of Helly-type theorems was extensively investigated, leading to
many specialized results and techniques on, for instance, parallelotopes [26, 12],
disjoint translates of a convex set in the plane [16, 22, 27] or in R3 [20], disjoint
planar convex sets with an ordering condition [18], disjoint balls [9, 17, 7, 5], etc.

   A more unified picture emerges when one considers the above question through
a topological lens. Helly’s theorem enjoys several topological generalizations, see
e.g. [19, 10, 23], and some of the recent ones relate the Helly-type properties of a
family of sets to some measure of its topological complexity [8, 11]. These results
allow to retrieve in a single stroke several of the special cases once the influences
of the geometry of F on the homology of the set of line transversals to F has been
elucidated. In the plane, any connected component of line transversals to disjoint
convex sets is contractible, so the geometry only limits the number of connected
components of line transversals; See the discussion in [8, § 7]. Theorem 1 estab-
lishes that the same happens in R3 for open sets.1 We conjecture that the same
holds in higher dimension:
Conjecture 2. For any family F of n ≥ 2 pairwise disjoint open convex sets in Rd ,
every connected component of line transversals to F is acyclic.

   As a final remark it should be pointed out that the study of geometric transversals
is not limited to line transversals. More generally, a k-transversal to a family of
convex sets in Rd is a k-dimensional affine flat that intersects every member of
the family. Most studied is the case of hyperplane transversals, that is, when k =
d − 1, and some notable results are; Necessary and sufficient conditions for the
existence of hyperplane transversals [13, 25, 28, 3], a (p, q)-theorem for hyperplane
transversals [2], and results on the topology of the space of hyperplane transversals
[4]. For 1 < k < d − 1, much less is known, but there are some intriguing results
concerning the topology of the space of k-transversals for small families of convex
sets [6, 24].

                                        2. Background
  We denote by L the space of lines in R3 equipped with its usual topology; this
topology can be defined for instance via a parameterization of that space (e.g. by
Plücker coordinates) or by recasting it as a quotient space of an open subset of
R3 × R3 , via the map that sends a pair of distinct points to the line that they span.
We denote by L+ the the space of directed lines in R3 , equipped with its usual
topology (which can be defined similarly).

  The Vietoris-Begle mapping theorem asserts that in certain conditions, some of
the topological and homological properties of a topological space X are preserved

non-transversal direction for F.

                                   4. Wrapping up
   We now use our path-building Lemma 5 to analyze sets of directions and sets of
line transversals.
Lemma 6. Let F be a finite family of at least two pairwise disjoint open convex
sets in R3 . The set N(F) = S2 \ T (F) of non-transversal directions for F is a
path-connected subset of S2 .
Proof. Let x be a vector in N(F). Starting at x, we move along an arbitrary geo-
desic of S2 until we either meet the boundary of N(F) or we meet Y, stopping at
whichever happens first. If we meet Y first, then we are done since Y is a path-
connected subset of N(F). If we meet the boundary of N(F) first, then we change
direction and move along the path given by Lemma 5, and again we reach Y by a
path contained within N(F).                                                   □
Our main result now easily follows from Lemma 6 by arguments spelled out in [8,
Lemma 24], which we summarize here for completeness.
Proof of Theorem 1. Let F be a finite family of at least two pairwise disjoint convex
sets in R3 . We first prove that the conected components of line transversals to F
are acyclic when (i) members of F are open, and (ii) lines are directed.

   First, let us remark that Lemmas 6 and 4 imply that the connected components
of T (F) are contractible and therefore acyclic.

   Now, let π : L+ → S2 denote the map that sends every directed line to its
direction vector. Let L+ (F) denote the set of directed line transversals to F. For
any direction v ∈ T (F), let L+v (F) denote the set of directedTline transversals to
F with direction v. The fact that L+v is homeomorphic to K∈F pv (K) has two
consequences: (i) for every v ∈ T (F), the fiber π−1 (v) ∩ L+ (F) is contractible, and
(ii) for every connected component U of L+ (F), π(U) is a connected component
of T (F). With Lemma 3, the acyclicity of the connected components of T (F)
therefore implies the acyclicity of the connected components of L+ .

    To go from directed to non-directed lines, let us fix a plane Π separating two
 members of F (which ones does not matter) and a vector ⃗n normal to Π. Let L′ (F)
 denote the set of directed line transversals to F that make a positive dot product with
⃗n. The map that associates each line in L(F) to its orientation making a positive
 dot-product with ⃗n induces a homeomorphism between L(F) and L′ (F). Since no
 line in L+ (F) has a direction parallel to Π, every connected component of L′ (F) is
 a connected components of L+ (F). Hence, every connected component of L(F) is
 homeomorphic to a connected component of L+ (F) and is also acyclic.                  □

                                 5. Concluding remarks
   Let us conclude with a construction that shows that for every compact subset C ⊂
R, there exists a family F of four pairwise disjoint compact convex sets in R3 with
T (F) homeomorphic to C. This highlights some of the issues that arise when trying
to replace the openness condition by a compactness assumption in Theorem 1.

   Let Σ denote the hyperbolic paraboloid defined by the equation z = xy. This
surface is doubly ruled: every point (α, β, αβ) is on two lines contained in Σ, namely
λα = Σ ∩ {x = α} and ℓβ = Σ ∩ {y = β}. For i = 1, 2, 3, let S i denote the closed line
segment supported by λi and bounded by the planes y = 1 and y = 2. The set of
lines transversals to {S 1 , S 2 , S 3 } is exactly {ℓb : b ∈ [1, 2]}.
