           POST-LIE NONEXISTENCE WITH A SEMISIMPLE TARGET

                                               ALEC KRIEBEL


       Abstract. Let g be a finite-dimensional complex unimodular Lie algebra and n a complex semisim-
       ple Lie algebra of the same dimension. We prove that if two Lie algebra homomorphisms j1 , j2 : g →
       n have invertible difference, then g is semisimple. The integrated left-right action has a holomor-
       phic infinitesimal determinant. Unimodularity makes this determinant invariant, and its constancy
       on an open orbit extends to the whole target group. Transitivity and simple connectedness then
       identify the source and target manifolds; their compact-form homology rules out a nonzero source
       radical. Consequently a perfect nonsemisimple complex source and a semisimple target admit no
       post-Lie algebra structure.




                                             1. Introduction
   All Lie algebras in this note are finite-dimensional over C. For two brackets on a common
vector space, a post-Lie product relates the source Lie algebra g to the target Lie algebra n.
Burde, reporting joint work with Dekimpe, formulated the nonexistence conjecture for a perfect
nonsemisimple source and an arbitrary semisimple target in Oberwolfach Report 46/2023, printed
p. 2689 [5]. Burde–Dekimpe–Monadjem proved the target sl3 (C) case and further special cases [4,
Theorem 3.10 and Propositions 3.11–3.12]. An earlier result of Burde–Dekimpe excludes a solvable
unimodular source with a nonzero semisimple target [2, Theorem 3.3].
   We prove the conjectured nonexistence statement as a consequence of a stronger obstruction for
unimodular sources. The passage from post-Lie products to transverse pairs of homomorphisms
is established in the literature [3, Proposition 2.16], [4, Proposition 2.6]; we include the reduction
with explicit bracket conventions.
   The determinant method has a classical aﬀine-space predecessor. Baues associates a determinant
relative invariant to an étale aﬀine representation and proves that triviality of its character forces
global transitivity [1, §5.4.1, equation (5.3), and Theorem 5.19]; see also his Corollary 5.20 for the
unimodular, volume-preserving case. We use that method on a complex group: the determinant is
globally holomorphic, and the identity theorem replaces polynomial continuation on aﬀine space.
The subsequent obstruction uses classical integration, Levi decomposition, compact real forms and
ordinary singular homology. In particular, we do not invoke an aﬀine-space completeness theorem
for the group target.
   The literature comparison underlying this note is bounded: no prior full theorem under the
matching hypotheses was located in the primary passages inspected. This is not a claim of firstness,
exhaustive coverage, or current openness. In particular, the original body of Onishchik’s 1969 work
on reductive-group decompositions was inaccessible in that comparison; this is an access limitation,
not an identified competing theorem.

Theorem 1. Let g and n be complex Lie algebras of the same finite dimension, with g unimodular
and n semisimple. Suppose j1 , j2 : g → n are Lie algebra homomorphisms and j1 − j2 is a vector-
space isomorphism. Then g is semisimple.
  We first recall the solvable-group fact needed in the proof [6, Theorem 49.1], including its short
induction.
Lemma 2. The connected simply connected complex Lie group with a solvable Lie algebra r is
diffeomorphic to CdimC r , and is therefore contractible.
Proof. Induct on dimC r. For r 6= 0, solvability gives a nonzero functional vanishing on [r, r]. Its
kernel r′ is a codimension-one ideal. A complementary line Cx is a subalgebra, so r = r′ ⋊ Cx. Let
R′ be the simply connected integration of r′ . The automorphisms exp(t ad x)|r′ , for t ∈ C, integrate
uniquely to automorphisms of R′ . They give a holomorphic action of the additive group C. Thus
R′ ⋊ C is simply connected and integrates r. Uniqueness of simply connected integration identifies
it with the required group, whose manifold is R′ × C. Induction proves the assertion. We use
integration and uniqueness in their complex, finite-dimensional form [6, Theorems 9.12–9.13 and
Corollary 9.14].                                                                                    □
Proof of Theorem 1. Put d = dimC g = dimC n. If d = 0, both algebras are zero and the conclusion
holds. Assume d > 0. Choose connected simply connected complex Lie groups G, N integrating
g, n. The maps ji integrate to holomorphic homomorphisms Ji : G → N [6, Theorems 9.12–9.13
and Corollary 9.14]. They define a global holomorphic action
(1)                         Ah (p) = J1 (h) p J2 (h)−1     (h ∈ G, p ∈ N ).
No closedness or injectivity of the images Ji (G) is required.
  Under left trivialization of T N , the differential at the identity of h 7→ Ah (p) is
(2)                         Bp : g −→ n,       Bp x = AdN (p−1 )j1 x − j2 x.
Fix bases of g, n and define the globally holomorphic function f (p) = det Bp . Since Be = j1 − j2 is
invertible, the holomorphic inverse function theorem makes the orbit of e open.
   For q = Ah (p), equivariance of the integrated homomorphisms gives
(3)                                Bq = AdN (J2 (h)) Bp AdG (h−1 ).
Indeed, the first term on the right is
                               AdN (J2 (h)) AdN (p−1 ) AdN (J1 (h)−1 )j1 ,
and the second is − AdN (J2 (h))j2 AdG (h−1 ) = −j2 . These are the two terms of Bq , since q −1 =
J2 (h)p−1 J1 (h)−1 .
   For a connected complex Lie group L, the character det AdL : L → C× has differential x 7→
tr(adLie(L) x). If this differential is zero, the character is locally constant, hence identically one.
This applies to G by unimodularity, and to N because a semisimple Lie algebra is perfect and the
trace of a commutator is zero. Taking determinants in (3) yields
                                           f (Ah (p)) = f (p).
Consequently f = f (e) 6= 0 on the open identity orbit. The identity theorem on the connected
complex manifold N gives f ≡ f (e) on all of N . Every Bp is therefore invertible, so every orbit is
open. Connectedness of N implies that there is only one orbit: the action (1) is transitive.
   Let H = {h ∈ G : Ah (e) = e}. It is a closed subgroup with Lie algebra ker Be = 0, hence
is discrete. The orbit map identifies G/H with N as a manifold [6, Proposition 4.12 and Corol-
lary 4.16]. For completeness, G → G/H is a covering: choose an identity neighborhood U with
U −1 U ∩ H = {e}; the sets U h, h ∈ H, are disjoint sheets over the image of U , and left translations

give such neighborhoods everywhere. Because G is connected and N is simply connected, this
covering has one sheet [7, Proposition 1.32]. Thus H = {e}, and
(4)                                   G is diffeomorphic to N.
The orbit map need not be a group homomorphism; no group or Lie algebra isomorphism is inferred
from (4).
   Take a Levi decomposition g = r ⋊ s, where r is the solvable radical and s is semisimple. Let
R, S be their simply connected integrations. The Levi action integrates on R; the simply connected
group R ⋊ S has Lie algebra g. By uniqueness of integration, its manifold R × S is the manifold
of G [6, Theorem 48.9 and Corollary 9.14]. This construction imposes no closed-image assumption
on a previously chosen Levi subgroup. Lemma 2 makes the R factor contractible.
   Polar decomposition retracts a simply connected complex semisimple group onto a compact real
form [6, Corollary 43.5, Theorem 43.7, Corollary 43.8 and the following covering discussion]. Let
these compact groups be KN for N and KS for S. Their real dimensions are respectively d and
s = dimC s, rather than 2d and 2s. The compact connected Lie group KN is orientable: left
translation of a basis at its identity gives a global frame. Its fundamental class therefore gives, in
ordinary singular homology [7, Theorem 3.26(a)],
                                     Hd (N ; Z) ∼
                                                = Hd (KN ; Z) ∼
                                                              = Z.
If r 6= 0, then s < d. The product description of G, contractibility of R, and polar decomposition
for S give
                                   Hd (G; Z) ∼
                                             = Hd (KS ; Z) = 0,
since KS is a compact s-manifold [7, Theorem 3.26(c)]. This contradicts (4). Hence r = 0, proving
that g is semisimple.                                                                            □

                                 3. The post-Lie consequence
  Let g = (V, [ , ]) and n = (V, { , }). Our convention for a post-Lie product x · y is
(5)                             x · y − y · x = [x, y] − {x, y},
(6)                                 [x, y] · z = x · (y · z) − y · (x · z),
(7)                                x · {y, z} = {x · y, z} + {y, x · z}.
Thus L(x)y = x · y defines a homomorphism L : g → Der(n).
Corollary 3. If a finite-dimensional complex pair (g, n) admits a post-Lie product, g is unimodular
and n is semisimple, then g is semisimple. In particular, a perfect nonsemisimple complex source
and a semisimple target admit no post-Lie product.
Proof. For semisimple n, every derivation is inner and its center is zero. To recall the innerness
argument, let κ be its nondegenerate Killing form and D ∈ Der(n). Choose a ∈ n such that
κ(a, u) = tr(D adn u). Using [D, adn u] = adn (Du), cyclicity of trace and invariance of κ, we obtain
                                  (              )
                    κ(Du, v) = tr D[adn u, adn v] = κ(a, {u, v}) = κ({a, u}, v).
Hence D = adn a. The map adn : n → Der(n) is therefore a linear isomorphism. In particular there
is a unique linear map
                           R = ad−1n L : V −→ V,    x · y = {Rx, y}.
Equations (6)–(7) and injectivity of adn give
                                           R[x, y] = {Rx, Ry}.
Equation (5) gives the signs explicitly:
                                [x, y] = {Rx, y} + {x, Ry} + {x, y}.

Expanding {(R + I)x, (R + I)y} now shows that
                                           j1 = R + I,        j2 = R
are homomorphisms g → n, with difference I. Theorem 1 applies. Finally the functional x 7→
tr(adg x) vanishes on [g, g], since adg [x, y] = [adg x, adg y]. A perfect source is consequently uni-
modular.                                                                                            □
Remark 4. The zero-dimensional algebra is semisimple by convention, so it gives no exception to
the nonsemisimple-source assertion. Unimodularity is essential to Theorem 1. For example, let g
have basis H, E, Z, with [H, E] = 2E and Z central. In n = sl2 (C), use the standard basis H, E, F .
The maps
             j1 (H) = H,     j1 (E) = E,     j1 (Z) = 0,      j2 (H) = j2 (E) = 0,     j2 (Z) = F
are homomorphisms with invertible difference, but tr(adg H) = 2. The proof makes no claim for
a general real form, positive characteristic, infinite dimension, or a merely reductive target. It
assumes neither algebraicity nor completeness of a locally defined action, and no bijectivity of a
solvable exponential map.
