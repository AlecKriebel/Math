                                                   tropical abelian varieties. We prove that every H-regular subvariety, lo-
                                                   cally modeled on tropicalizations of sufficiently well-behaved very affine
                                                   varieties, has nonnegative signed Euler characteristic. This gives a trop-
                                                   ical analogue of a theorem of Green–Lazarsfeld for subvarieties of com-
                                                   plex abelian varieties. The main input is a local vanishing theorem for

                                                   affine H-regular tropical varieties. We further show that the signed Euler

                   2. H-regular tropical varieties
  The following condition was introduced by Hacking [Hac08].
Definition 2.1. Let U ⊂ (C∗ )n be a closed irreducible subvariety. We say
that U is H-regular if there exists a smooth projective toric variety X∆ such
that the closure U of U in X∆ is smooth and intersects every torus orbit
O ⊂ X∆ transversely. Moreover, for every torus orbit O, the intersection
U ∩ O is either empty or connected.
  If such an X∆ is fixed, we say that U is H-regular with respect to X∆ .
Remark 2.2. Condition (∗) in [Hac08, Theorem 2.5] only requires connect-
edness when the intersection U ∩ O is positive dimensional. We need the
stronger condition because we will pass to new compactifications by per-
forming weighted blowups along toric boundary strata.
Definition 2.3. By a tropical fan, we mean a weighted rational polyhe-
dral fan satisfying the balancing condition. In particular, tropicalizations of
subvarieties of complex affine tori are tropical fans.
   A tropical fan T ⊂ Rn is called H-regular if T = trop(U ) for some H-
regular subvariety U ⊂ (C∗ )n . More generally, a tropical variety X is called
H-regular if, for every vertex P of X , there is an integral-affine isomorphism
from a small neighborhood of P in X to a neighborhood of the origin in
some H-regular tropical fan.
Remark 2.4. If U is an H-regular subvariety of (C∗ )n with respect to a
smooth projective toric variety X∆ , then trop(U ) is the union of all cones
σ ∈ ∆ such that Oσ ∩U ̸= ∅ [MS15, Proposition 6.4.7]. Moreover, if U meets
a torus orbit Oσ ⊂ X∆ of complementary dimension, then H-regularity
implies that U ∩ Oσ consists of a single point and that the intersection is
transverse. It follows that σ is a top-dimensional cone of trop(U ) with weight
one. Therefore, every top-dimensional cone of an H-regular tropical fan has
weight one [MS15, Proposition 6.7.2].
Remark 2.5. Because of the connectedness assumption, H-regularity is strictly
stronger than being Schön. Hacking’s result is sometimes cited as if it im-
plied that, for every Schön subvariety U ,
              H
              e k (L(trop(U )); Q) = 0   for all k ̸= dim U − 1.
However, we give a counterexample to this statement in Section 8.

Definition 2.9. A tropical abelian variety A is a real torus Rn /Zn with a
positive definite quadratic form on Rn called the polarization. A tropical
d-cycle X on A is a weighted, embedded, rational polyhedral d-complex in
A with integral slopes and satisfying the balancing condition.
Remark 2.10. See [MS15, Definition 3.3.1] for the definition of the balancing
condition. In this paper, we will not use balancing conditions explicitly.
Furthermore, our main results concern tropical varieties whose weights are
all one (see Remark 2.4).
   By our main theorem, the sign of the Euler characteristic of an H-regular
tropical cycle on a tropical abelian variety is determined by its dimension.
Following the idea of [BW15], we construct pure-dimensional subvarieties of
a tropical abelian variety that violate the inequality in Theorem 1.1. This
shows that the H-regularity assumption in Theorem 1.1 is necessary.

                3. Transversality in toric varieties
   Let σ ⊂ Rn be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone of codimension
m = n − dim σ. Then Xσ is an affine normal toric variety with dense
torus T = (C∗ )n . The minimal-dimensional T -orbit of Xσ , denoted O, is
isomorphic to (C∗ )m . After choosing a noncanonical splitting of the ambient
lattice, we may write
                             Xσ ∼= (C∗ )m × Xσ′ ,
where σ ′ denotes the same cone σ, viewed as a full-dimensional cone in
span(σ). The minimal torus orbit of Xσ′ is a point, which we denote by P .

Definition 3.1. With the notation above, let Y ⊂ Xσ be a locally closed
analytic subvariety of pure codimension k. We say that Y intersects O
transversely if, for any point x ∈ Y ∩O, there exist an analytic neighborhood
Ux of x in Xσ , an analytic neighborhood UP of P in Xσ′ , and an isomorphism
of analytic varieties
                                 Ux ≃ B m × UP                             (1)
such that:
   (1) B m is a complex ball of dimension m;
   (2) under the isomorphism (1), we have
                            Y ∩ Ux ≃ B m−k × UP
       for a closed (m − k)-dimensional complex ball B m−k ⊂ B m ;
   (3) the product stratification induced by the torus-orbit stratification of
       Xσ′ agrees, via (1), with the restriction of the torus-orbit stratifica-
       tion of Xσ to Ux .
Remark 3.2. By definition, if Y intersects O transversely and Y ∩ O ̸= ∅,
then k ≤ m. Equivalently, if Y ⊂ Xσ is a locally closed analytic subvariety
of codimension larger than m, then Y intersects O transversely if and only
if Y ∩ O = ∅.
Definition 3.3. Let X be a normal toric variety with dense torus T =
(C∗ )n . For every torus orbit O ⊂ X, let X≥O be the unique affine open toric
subvariety of X whose minimal orbit is O. Let Y ⊂ X be a closed analytic
subvariety. We say that Y intersects all torus orbits of X transversely if, for
every torus orbit O ⊂ X, the subvariety Y ∩ X≥O intersects O transversely
in X≥O in the sense of Definition 3.1.
Remark 3.4. When O is the dense torus orbit, the above transversality
condition requires that Y ∩ O is smooth. More generally, if Y intersects all
torus orbits of X transversely, then Y ∩ O is a closed complex submanifold
of O for every torus orbit O ⊂ X.

                  9. Further discussion and questions
Generalizations to arbitrary matroids. A geometric proof of a state-
ment about realizable matroids often suggests a purely combinatorial proof,
which would extend the statement to arbitrary matroids. This leads to the
following question.
      TOPOLOGY AND EULER CHARACTERISTICS OF TROPICAL VARIETIES                27

Question 9.1. Can the conclusion of Corollary 1.3 be proved for arbitrary
matroids using combinatorial Hodge theory?
   In the current proof of Corollary 1.3, we use refinements of (augmented)
Bergman fans and the decomposition theorem. Both of these ingredients
have combinatorial counterparts, namely [ADH23] and [AHL25]. However,
it is not clear to us how to formulate a combinatorial analogue of Artin
vanishing for perverse sheaves on an affine variety.
Euler characteristics of Ωp . As discussed after Theorem 1.1, the Euler
characteristic of a tropical variety X can be expressed in terms of the tropical
homology Hodge numbers introduced in [Ite+19],
                                     X
                           χ(X ) =      (−1)q h0,q (X ).
                                    q

On the other hand, the generic vanishing theorem ([GL87, Theorem 2])
implies that for a d-dimensional smooth subvariety X of an abelian variety,
                                   X
               (−1)d−p χ(X, Ωp ) =   (−1)d−p−q hp,q (X) ≥ 0.
                                        q

This suggests the following tropical analogue.
Question 9.2. Let X be a d-dimensional H-regular subvariety of a tropical
abelian variety. Do the tropical homology Hodge numbers of X satisfy
                                    X
                 (−1)d−p χp (X ) :=  (−1)d−p−q hp,q (X ) ≥ 0
                                        q

for all p?
Fourier–Mukai transformations. Recently, Ghosh and Shokrieh [GS25]
constructed a cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform for tropical abelian
varieties. Since the theorem of Green–Lazarsfeld that
                           (−1)dim X χ(X, OX ) ≥ 0
for smooth closed subvarieties X of complex abelian varieties can be proved
using Fourier–Mukai transformations [PP11], it is natural to ask whether
there is a tropical counterpart of this approach.
Question 9.3. Can the cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform of [GS25]
be used to give an alternative proof of Theorem 1.1? More generally, can
it be used to identify classes of cycles in tropical abelian varieties with
nonnegative signed Euler characteristic?
Non-abelian analogues. Some non-abelian analogues of the Green–Lazarsfeld
theorem, and of its topological counterpart, the Franecki–Kapranov theorem
[FK00], have recently been established (see [DW25; AW25; DW24]). In the
classical setting, non-abelian analogues of subvarieties of abelian varieties
include smooth projective varieties with negative sectional curvature, and
more generally smooth projective varieties with large fundamental group,
28    SCOTT HIATT, CONNOR SIMPSON, BOTONG WANG, AND CHENXI WU

such as compact smooth Shimura varieties. It would be interesting to de-
velop a corresponding theory in tropical geometry.
Question 9.4. Is there a natural class of “negatively curved” or “hy-
perbolic” tropical varieties, or of tropical varieties with large fundamental
group, that is closed under Cartesian products and passage to smooth sub-
varieties? Moreover, does every H-regular tropical variety in such a class
have nonnegative signed Euler characteristic?
Chern classes. For a compact complex manifold, the topological Euler
characteristic is equal to the integral of the top Chern class, or equivalently
to the degree of the zero-dimensional homology Chern class. Thus the other
Chern classes may be viewed as refinements of the Euler characteristic.
   The Chern classes of smooth subvarieties of abelian varieties satisfy signed
positivity properties. For example, if X is a smooth subvariety of a complex
abelian variety, then Ω1X is globally generated. Consequently, the signed
Chern classes of X are effective. More precisely, if ck (X) denotes the k-th
cohomological Chern class of TX , then (−1)k ck (X) is effective.
   The Chern–Schwartz–MacPherson (CSM) classes of tropical manifolds
were introduced in [MRS23]. Here a tropical manifold is a tropical variety lo-
cally isomorphic to Bergman fans of matroids (see [MRS23, Definition 2.3]).
For a d-dimensional smooth subvariety X of a tropical abelian variety, its
tropical CSM classes satisfy the signed positivity property (−1)d−k csmk (X )
is effective.
Question 9.5. Can one define suitable Chern or CSM classes for H-regular
tropical varieties, and do they satisfy the same signed positivity property?
More generally, can the signed Euler characteristic inequality of Theorem 1.1
be refined to a signed positivity statement for tropical Chern classes?
