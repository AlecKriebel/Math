    Free Hyperplane Arrangements with Exponents 1, 2 and at Most
    One 3 Are Supersolvable: A Proof of a Conjecture of Tohăneanu
                                               Alper Ferudun∗

                                      October 12, 2026 (version 1.1)



                                                   Abstract
          Ş. O. Tohăneanu conjectured (Oberwolfach Report 5/2021, p. 284; Conjecture 4.1 of the preprint
      arXiv:1707.07091v3) that every central free hyperplane arrangement over a ﬁeld of characteristic
      zero whose exponents are 1’s, 2’s and at most one 3 is supersolvable. We prove this conjecture in
      every rank. The preprint was withdrawn by its author on 16 January 2022; no result of it is used
      here, but the strategy of our proof is that of the preprint and of the Oberwolfach abstract. The case
      of exponents 1 and 2 only was ﬁrst proved by Tohăneanu, in the ﬁrst version of the preprint (2017).
      This case, and the case of one exponent 3 together with a codimension-two ﬂat in four hyperplanes,
      also follow from a theorem of DiPasquale (Math. Ann. 385 (2023)); both are proved again here.
      Rank three was also known. New, to our knowledge and relative to the literature we found, is
      the case without such a ﬂat in rank at least 4, and therefore the conjecture in general. With
      two exponents 3 the statement is false: the non-Fano arrangement (exponents (1, 3, 3)) and nine
      hyperplanes of rank four (exponents (1, 2, 3, 3)) are free and not supersolvable. No computation
      enters the proof; the computations reported are tests. The book of Orlik and Terao was not
      accessible to us and was not read. No proof and no counterexample of the conjecture was found
      in the literature accessible to us; a search that ﬁnds nothing is not a proof of novelty. This is an
      unrefereed note.


1     Introduction
1.1   The conjecture
Let K be a ﬁeld, V a K-vector space of dimension k, and S = Sym(V ∗ ) = K[x1 , . . . , xk ]. An
arrangement in V is a ﬁnite set A of hyperplanes of V , that is, of linear subspaces of codimension one;
so all arrangements in∏this note are central. For H ∈ A we ﬁx αH ∈ V ∗∩with H = ker αH , and we put
n = |A| and Q(A) = H∈A αH . The rank r(A) is the codimension of H∈A H, and A is essential if
r(A) = k.

    The intersection lattice L(A) is the set of all intersections of hyperplanes of A, including V , ordered

texts K has characteristic zero and the arrangements are central and essential. The abstract contains
the following conjecture.

      Conjecture (Tohăneanu [Toh21, p. 284]). Every central free hyperplane arrangement
      with “exponents 1’s, 2’s, and at most one 3” is supersolvable.

   In the preprint it is Conjecture 4.1, stated for arrangements with exp(A) = (1, 2k−2 , 3). The
conjecture is record OWR-4990374-015 of the UnsolvedMath collection [ula26], where it is listed as
open.

1.2    Results
Theorem 1.1 (the conjecture). Let K be a ﬁeld of characteristic zero, and let A be a central ar-
rangement in K k which is free and whose exponents are 0’s, 1’s, 2’s and at most one 3. Then A is
supersolvable.
     Exponents 0 occur exactly when A is∩not essential, and supersolvability then refers to the essential
arrangement A/C in V /C, where C = H∈A H (Sections 1.3 and 2.1).
     To state the result on which the proof rests, let L2 (A) be the set of all Z ∈ L(A) of codimension
two, and let mZ be the number of hyperplanes of A containing Z. We call Z multiple if mZ ≥ 3,
a triple point if mZ = 3, and a quadruple point if mZ = 4. An essential arrangement is irreducible
if it is not a product

three non-zero coordinates [BT94, Section 3]. Details are in Section 2.1.

Then A is supersolvable. More precisely, let k ≥ 3, and for k = 3 suppose that A is not the arrangement
of the six lines joining the pairs of four points of the projective plane, no three of them collinear. Then
there is a triple point Z0 , contained in the hyperplanes H0 , H1 , H2 of∩A, such that H1 and H2 contain
no other multiple element of L2 (A). For such Z0 the subspace X = {H ∈ A : H 6= H1 , H2 } is a line

satisﬁes the same hypotheses in rank k − 1.
    In Theorem 1.2 freeness does not occur. An essential free arrangement with exponents (1, 2k−2 , 3)
satisﬁes its hypotheses (Proposition 3.2); the analogous statements for exponents (1, 2k−1 ) are Theo-
rem 5.2(a) and Corollary 5.3(a). Theorem 1.1 follows by a reduction to essential, irreducible arrange-
ments (Section 5). The arrangement excluded for k = 3, which we call the complete quadrilateral, is
supersolvable (Lemma 5.1).

1.3    Conventions that could change the answer
The degree of a derivation is the degree of its coeﬃcients, so the Euler derivation has degree 1 [Toh20,
Section 1]; with the other usual convention all exponents would be smaller by one. “At most one 3”
refers to the multiset of exponents: (1, 1, 2, 3) and (1, 3) are admitted, (1, 2, 3, 3) is not, and with two
exponents 3 the statement is false (Section 7). An essential free arrangement has exactly one exponent
1 if it is irreducible, and at least two otherwise (Lemma 2.3); so the 1’s of the conjecture admit reducible
arrangements, and Conjecture 4.1 of the preprint is the irreducible case. The sources assume that A
is essential; Theorem 1.1 also covers arrangements which are not. It is claimed for characteristic zero,
the setting of the conjecture; see Remark 6.5. Aﬃne arrangements, multi-arrangements and other
exponents are not part of the conjecture and are not treated.

1.4    What was known, what is not new, and what is new
The conjecture and the preprint. The conjecture is due to Ş. O. Tohăneanu. According to the abstract
[Toh21, p. 284] it was veriﬁed in the preprint [Toh20] when the exponents are only 1’s and 2’s, for
inductively free arrangements, and for free arrangements of rank 3, 4 or 5. The case of exponents 1
and 2 only is the theorem of the ﬁrst version of the preprint, of 22 July 2017, and it is Section 3 of
version 3; this is the ﬁrst proof of that case. The preprint was withdrawn by its author on 16 January
2022 (version 4); the withdrawal notice names Sections 4.2.1 and 4.2.2 and case (a) of the proof of
Proposition 4.8 of version 3, and not Section 3. We record these facts only. No result of the preprint
is used in our proofs; in particular the case of exponents 1 and 2 is proved here.
     The strategy of this note is that of the preprint and of the abstract, and we credit it to them: the
formality of free arrangements as the tool; the modular coatom obtained from two hyperplanes which
lie in a single dependent triple ([Toh20, Lemma 2.2], [Toh21, item iii, p. 284]); induction on the rank;
the counts n = 2k and u + 3v = k + 1 [Toh20, Lemma 4.2]; and the pigeonhole count for exponents
(1, 2k−1 ) [Toh20, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2]. The bound mZ ≤ maxi di + 1 is [Toh20, Lemma 2.1] and,
according to [Toh21, p. 284], [Sch03, Lemma 5.2]; Lemma 2.5 generalizes it.
     Not new, and proved again. For exponents (1, 2k−1 ) supersolvability was ﬁrst proved by Tohăneanu
[Toh20, Section 3], in the ﬁrst version of the preprint (2017). For these exponents, and for exponents
(1, 2k−2 , 3) when there is a quadruple point (v = 1), supersolvability also follows from a theorem
of DiPasquale [DiP23, Proposition 6.1 with Corollary 4.10], whose arXiv version is of 2018; the
derivation is in Remark 6.4. The combinatorial analogue of Theorem 5.2(a) and of the induction
step is in [DGT14, Theorems 5.6 and 5.11]. Rank three is covered by the classiﬁcation [Toh16,
Theorem 2.4], as noted in [Toh20], and the question whether free arrangements with exponents 1
and 2 are supersolvable is posed in the introduction of [Toh16]. For arrangements whose underlying
matroid is regular, freeness is equivalent to supersolvability by a recent preprint of Liang and Wang
[LW26], of which we read the abstract and the introduction. This does not give the conjecture, because
the matroid of a free arrangement with these exponents is not known to be regular in advance; when
there is no quadruple point it turns out to be graphic (Remark 6.1), and with a quadruple point it
is not regular. Further attributions, to [Yuz93, BT94, MMR24, BEZ90], are given after Lemmas 4.1
to 4.3. The numbers of [DiP23, DGT14, Toh16, MMR24] are those of their arXiv versions.
     New, to our knowledge and relative to the literature we found, is the case without a quadruple
point (v = 0) in rank k ≥ 4, and therefore the conjecture in general; the other cases are proved again.
The new case is Lemma 4.3(ii): there are k + 1 triple points, their k + 1 relations lie in the relation
space, which has dimension k, and the support of a linear dependency among them leads to a triple
point two of whose three hyperplanes contain no other multiple ﬂat. The same count of dimensions
gives v ≤ 1, and the induction runs inside the classes Ck and Ck′ of Deﬁnition 3.1, so that one proof
covers all cases. We claim no priority; see “Scope and priority” at the end of Section 9.

Lemma 2.2 (rank two). Let Z ∈ L2 (A). Then AZ is free with exponents (0k−2 , 1, mZ − 1).

Remark 6.1 (what the induction produces). Let A ∈ Ck , for instance an essential free arrangement
with exponents (1, 2k−2 , 3) in characteristic zero. Each step of the induction removes two hyperplanes
and one triple point, and keeps the quadruple point if there is one. If v = 1, the induction ends with
four concurrent lines in rank 2; if v = 0, it ends with a complete quadrilateral in rank 3. So the proof
gives a chain of modular elements V = X0 < X1 < · · · < Xk = 0 with |AXl \ AXl−1 | = 2 for l ≥ 4,
and the sizes of the steps |AXl \ AXl−1 |, l = 1, . . . , k, are

                      1, 3, 2, 2, . . . , 2   if v = 1,        1, 2, 3, 2, . . . , 2   if v = 0.

In other words, A arises from a pencil of four lines or from a complete quadrilateral by adding, k −2 or
k − 3 times, one dimension and two hyperplanes which form a triple point with an existing hyperplane
and contain no other multiple ﬂat. For A ∈ Ck′ the sizes are 1, 2, 2, . . . , 2.
    In terms of matroids each step is a parallel connection with a line of three points: as α2 ∈
span(α0 , α1 ) with both coeﬃcients non-zero (Lemma 4.2) and α1 is not in the span of the forms of
AX , a subset of A which contains exactly one of H1 , H2 is independent if and only if its other members
are, and a subset which contains both is independent if and only if its other members do not include
H0 and are independent together with H0 . The forests of a graph to which a triangle is glued along an
edge obey the same rule. Hence the matroid of A is the cycle matroid of the complete graph K4 with
k − 3 triangles glued successively along edges if v = 0, and of a triangle with k − 2 further triangles
glued in this way if A ∈ Ck′ , k ≥ 2 (compare [DGT14, Theorem 5.6]).
Remark 6.2 (the converse). Every A ∈ Ck is free with exponents (1, 2k−2 , 3), and every A ∈ Ck′ is free
with exponents (1, 2k−1 ). Indeed, A is supersolvable by Theorem 5.2, and supersolvable arrangements
are free by the theorem of Jambu and Terao [JT84], which we use as it is cited in [Toh21, p. 284]; we
did not read [JT84]. By Lemma 2.3 exactly one exponent is 1 and the other k − 1 exponents are at
least 2; by (T2) they sum to 2k − 1, respectively 2k − 2. So, for an essential irreducible arrangement
of rank k ≥ 2 in characteristic zero, being free with exponents (1, 2k−2 , 3) is equivalent to membership
in Ck . This remark rests on the cited theorem and is not used elsewhere.

9    Computations and reproducibility
All theorems, propositions and lemmas of this note are proved without computation. The compu-
tations of this section are tests: a search that ﬁnds no counterexample is not a proof. They are
exact (rational arithmetic, or arithmetic in a ﬁnite ﬁeld). The reproducibility package contains
the programs, the command lines and the recorded outputs: the programs with which the result
was ﬁrst tested (original/), those of three independent veriﬁcation runs (verification_run_A/,
verification_run_B/, independent_run_2/), and the program of this note (writing_stage/).
Freeness with prescribed exponents is decided in both directions, by four diﬀerent implementations
which all rest on (T2): an answer “free” comes with a basis, and an answer “not free with these
exponents” is the failure of a necessary condition. Supersolvability is decided from the deﬁnition.
The veriﬁcation report of the package lists every test with its numbers.
