                   A relation-incidence proof of Tohăneanu’s
                   low-exponent supersolvability conjecture
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                       Independent researcher
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                          13 October 2026


                                               Abstract
         We prove that every ﬁnite central free arrangement over a ﬁeld of characteristic zero whose
     nonzero exponents are 1 or 2, with at most one 3, is supersolvable. Factorization and linear-
     relation formality restrict the incidence graph of the multiple rank-two ﬂats to a tree or a
     subdivision of K4 with trees attached. A direct modular-coatom argument then gives a maximal
     modular chain in every rank. This proves the full conjecture stated in Tohăneanu’s Oberwolfach
     contribution. We credit the earlier all-2, tree, rank-three and modular-pruning results and
     acknowledge Ferudun’s later matching full-target preprint. The accompanying package preserves
     the dated candidate, source audit and exact computational diagnostics. AI tools were used
     extensively in developing, writing and checking this work. This preprint is unrefereed; its
     internal AI reviews are not human peer review.


1    Statement, context and attribution
An arrangement is a ﬁnite set of distinct linear hyperplanes, rather than a multiarrangement.
We use the coeﬃcient degrees of a homogeneous basis of logarithmic derivations as its exponents.
Supersolvability means that the intersection lattice, ordered by reverse inclusion, has a maximal
chain of modular elements.

Theorem 1. Let A be a ﬁnite central free hyperplane arrangement over a ﬁeld K of character-
istic zero. If its nonzero exponents belong to {1, 2} except for at most one exponent 3, then A is
supersolvable.

    The source conjecture is on printed p. 284 of Tohăneanu’s Oberwolfach contribution [1], with
characteristic-zero scope set on p. 283. The all-1/2 result is already in his 2017 manuscript [2], and
tree/parallel-connection structure appears in Denham–Garrousian–Tohăneanu [3, Theorem 5.6].
The rank-three quadratic-derivation classiﬁcation is earlier work [4, Theorem 2.4]. The formality,
rank-two counting and terminal-triple modular-pruning strategy also occurs in [2, Lemma 2.2, §3,
Lemma 4.2], whose complete manuscript was withdrawn in 2022: the notice identiﬁes incomplete
hand enumeration in §§4.2.1–4.2.2 and an unjustiﬁed inference in Proposition 4.8(a). We use neither
aﬀected assertion, and prove the pruning and multiplicity steps below directly.
    DiPasquale’s total-formality theorem and TF2 classiﬁcation [5, Corollary 4.10, Proposition 6.1]
give a published predecessor for the cases with independent local relation spaces. Numbering here
refers to arXiv:1806.05295v1. In the two tree cases below the local-sum map is an isomorphism;
independence persists in localizations, so the higher relation kernels vanish and the free arrangement

2    Relations and numerical restrictions

We use two established inputs: Terao factorization [8] gives n = di and b2 = i<j di dj ; Yuzvin-
sky’s theorem [9] says that the linear relations among deﬁning forms of a free arrangement are
generated by rank-two relations. This is linear-relation formality, not topological formality. Di-
Pasquale [5, Corollary 4.10] also supplies the formality implication. Standard lattice and product
conventions may be found in [10].
    Essentialization identiﬁes the intersection lattices and removes only zero exponents. The rank-
zero lattice is trivial, and ranks one and two are supersolvable. Product factors are free, their
exponent multisets unite, and their modular chains give a modular chain in the product. It therefore
suﬃces to consider an essential irreducible arrangement of rank r ≥ 3.
    Such a factor has exactly one exponent 1. Indeed, use independent deﬁning forms x1 , . . . , xr
as coordinates. Every linear logarithmic derivation is diagonal, θ(xi ) = λi xi , and each further
deﬁning form is an eigenvector of its dual map. A nonscalar diagonal derivation partitions all
normals among distinct coordinate eigenspaces and yields a product. Thus only the Euler derivation
remains in degree one. Essentiality excludes constant logarithmic derivations, so this dimension
equals the number of exponent-one basis elements. The exponent lists are consequently (1, 2, . . . , 2)
or (1, 2, . . . , 2, 3).

3    The cyclic case has one forced core
Assume the one-3 case has no four-element line. Choose a nonzero relation ρL on each triple. These
r + 1 vectors span the r-dimensional space R. Choose an inclusion-minimal dependent family T
of size t. Its dependence coeﬃcients and every coordinate of every ρL on its support are nonzero.
Cancellation therefore makes each support element occur in at least two triples.
    Let s be the number of support elements and GT the corresponding incidence subgraph. It is
connected: diﬀerent components would split the dependence into smaller dependences. Thus

Combining the inequalities yields t ≤ 4. Each triple’s three elements must be covered by other
triples, and another rich line meets it in at most one element, so t − 1 ≥ 3. Therefore t = 4, s = 6.
Every element occurs exactly twice and every pair of triples meets at a distinct element. Label
them L1 , . . . , L4 and their intersections eij ; then

The core graph is the incidence subdivision of K4 , with triple vertices corresponding to its vertices
and element vertices to its edges.
    This core has cycle dimension three, exhausting that of G. Contracting the connected core
leaves a connected multigraph of cycle dimension zero, hence a tree. Thus all components outside
it are trees attached at one core vertex. Core triple vertices already have all their incident edges in
the core, so attachments occur only at core element vertices.

4    A terminal-pencil modular extension
We work with the represented normal matroid. A ﬂat is a subspace spanned by normals, together
with all normals in it. A represented ﬂat W is modular exactly when W ∩ F is spanned by
represented normals for every represented ﬂat F : this says that the matroid meet has the full
vector-space intersection dimension and is equivalent to the modular rank equality.

Lemma 1. In a formal normal conﬁguration, let L = {a, p1 , . . . , pm−1 } be a rich line and suppose
every pi occurs in no other rich line. Delete all pi , retaining a, to obtain E 0 . Then E 0 is formal,
its rank drops by one, and its span is a modular coatom. If E 0 is supersolvable, so is E.

Proof. All other local relation spaces are supported on E 0 . Let S be their sum. Formality gives
                             0
R = RL + S. Since RL ∩ K E = 0 (such a relation would be supported only on the nonzero normal
a), this sum is direct. Restricting to relations supported in E 0 gives R0 = S, proving deletion
formality, and

         dim R0 = dim R − (m − 2),        rk(E 0 ) = (n − m + 1) − (n − r − m + 2) = r − 1.

Put W = span(E 0 ) and U = span(L). The full normal span is W + U , so dimension counting gives
U ∩ W = Ka. Every removed normal is nonproportional to a; hence none lies in W , and E 0 is
exactly its represented ﬂat.
    Take a ﬂat F generated by some normals. If at most one generator is outside W , then F ∩ W
is spanned by the inside generators, because a single outside generator has nonzero image modulo
W . If at least two are outside, their distinct directions span U , and F ∩ W is the span of the inside
generators together with a. Thus W is modular.
    For a ﬂat C modular within W and any full ﬂat F , the intersection W ∩ F is represented. Hence
C ∩ F = C ∩ (W ∩ F ) is represented as well, making C globally modular. Lift a maximal modular
chain from W and append the full span.


5    Completion and boundaries
For the all-2 tree, root G at any triple-line vertex. A line vertex farthest from the root has two
outward element leaves. Lemma 1 deletes them; repeat until the root triple remains, a rank-two
supersolvable pencil. In the one-3 tree, root at the unique four-element line and prune the triple
lines in the same way. Again the remaining pencil has rank two. The retained conﬁguration is
formal at every step; no assertion that arbitrary deletions are free or formal is used.
    In the cyclic case, prune the attached trees toward the core. The remaining six-element conﬁg-
uration is formal. Its four triple relations have rank three by minimality of their dependency, so its
normal rank is 6 − 3 = 3. The quotient-tree argument shows that every noncore rich line contains
at most one core element. Its rich lines are therefore exactly the four indicated triples: deleting
normals cannot create any new collinear triple.
    Fix Li . Every other triple line meets it in a represented point. An ordinary rank-two ﬂat is a
pair of disjoint edges of the indexing K4 , since adjacent edges lie together in a star triple. A pair
of disjoint edges covers all four vertices, so precisely one of those edges belongs to Li . Its normal
span therefore also meets span(Li ) in a represented point. All rank-two ﬂats have represented
intersection with span(Li ); the other ranks are immediate. Thus Li is modular. Every atom is
modular, so zero, an atom of Li , Li , and the full rank-three span form a maximal modular chain.
Reverse the pruning using Lemma 1. Finally take products and undo essentialization to prove
Theorem 1.
