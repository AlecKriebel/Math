## Checkable universal argument

1. Essentialization removes only zero exponents and preserves the lattice; product factors are free with union of exponent multisets, and product modular chains combine. Ranks0,1 and2 are immediate. In an essential irreducible factor, choose independent normals as coordinates. A degree-one logarithmic derivation is diagonal and every other normal is its eigenvector. More than one eigenvalue splits the normal spans and the arrangement as a product. Thus its degree-one space is just the Euler line; essentiality excludes degree-zero derivations. The remaining lists are `(1,2^(r-1))` and `(1,2^(r-2),3)`.

2. For a rank-two pencil with forms `x,y,x+c_i y`, the distinct nonzero `c_i` give pairwise distinct linear factors of `P-Q` for any logarithmic derivation with `theta(x)=xP,theta(y)=yQ`. More than `d+1` hyperplanes, where `d` is the largest basis degree, forces `P=Q` in every basis column. The first two coefficient rows would be proportional over the fraction field, contradicting the basis rank. Thus pencils have at most3 or4 members as stated, over the actual characteristic-zero field without an algebraic-closure assumption.

3. Let `R=ker(K^E -> V*)` and let rich lines have at least three normals. Formality gives `R=sum R_L`. If the bipartite normal/rich-line incidence graph were disconnected, this splits the relation space by components. A relation between component normal spans would then restrict to a relation inside every component, so all component contributions vanish; the spans form a direct sum. Irreducibility therefore forces connectedness, including exclusion of isolated normal vertices.

4. Pair partition and factorization give `binom(n,2)-b2=sum binom(m_L-1,2)`. For all2, `n=2r-1,b2=2(r-1)^2,u=r-1,v=0` and incidence cycle rank0. For one3, `n=2r,b2=2r^2-2r-1,u+3v=r+1`, while formality gives `r<=u+2v=r+1-v`, hence `v<=1`. The cycle rank is `3-3v`. These identities agree independently; tree cases and the cyclic case exhaust the possibilities.

5. In the cyclic case all rich lines are triples. Their `r+1` nonzero relation vectors span an `r`-dimensional space. Choose a minimal dependent set of `t` columns on `s` normal coordinates. Minimality makes all dependence coefficients nonzero; each triple relation has all three entries nonzero. Every support coordinate occurs at least twice, so `2s<=3t`. The support graph is connected, otherwise the dependence splits. Its cycle rank `2t-s+1` is at most3. Therefore `2t-2<=s<=3t/2`, giving `t<=4`. A triple needs three distinct other triples to cover its three coordinates, because rich lines meet in at most one normal; hence `t>=4`. It follows that `t=4,s=6`. Every normal occurs twice and every pair of triples meets at a different normal: the incidence subdivision of K4. This is a rank-independent obstruction, not an enumeration cutoff.

6. This connected core has all three ambient cycles. Contracting it leaves a connected cycle-rank-zero multigraph, a tree. Every outside component consequently attaches once. Core triple vertices already have degree3, so attachments occur at element vertices. No second dependency core, extra returning path or extra core-rich-line incidence can remain without exceeding the cycle budget.

7. The terminal-pencil lemma uses **formality**, not an unsupported free-deletion rule. With pencil `L={a,p_i}` whose removed members occur in no other rich line, write `R=R_L+S`, where all other local relation spaces lie on the retained configuration `E'`. A relation of `R_L` on `E'` would be supported only on nonzero `a`, hence vanishes. The sum is direct and `R'=S`. Deleting `m-1` normals and `m-2` relations lowers rank exactly one and preserves formality. With `W=span E'` and `U=span L`, dimension counting gives `W cap U=Ka`; no deleted normal lies in W. For any represented flat F, a generating set with zero/one outside member intersects W in the span of its inside members. Two outside members span U and add precisely a.
