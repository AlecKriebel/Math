## 1. Smooth center, positivity and a scheme-theoretic family control

Over C, the gradients of q0=sum x_i^2 and q1=sum(i+1)x_i^2 on P3 are dependent at a common zero only if (i+1-lambda)x_i=0 for all i. Distinct coefficients force at most one nonzero coordinate, and q0 then forces that one to vanish. Thus C is smooth. It is nonempty: [x0:x1:x2:x3]=[1:i sqrt(2):1:0] satisfies both equations. The smooth quadric H is P1 times P1, and C has class(2,2). Kunneth gives H1(O_H(-2,-2))=0, since both terms have a zero H0(O_P1(-2)) factor. The exact sequence for C gives H0(O_C)=C, hence connectedness. Smooth connected C is irreducible; adjunction gives2g-2=C(C+K_H)=0, so g=1.

The blowup V is smooth. Distinguish carefully the original blowup exceptional divisor F from E, the strict transform of H: E=h-F, and E is H because C is Cartier in H. The codimension-two canonical formula gives -K_V=3h-F. The ideal sheaf I_C is locally generated by u and q1 in Q. Its degree-two twist is generated by q1 and u times all five ambient coordinates. The resulting surjection from a trivial bundle onto I_C(2) embeds the blowup graph into Q times P^N; the two hyperplanes restrict to h and2h-F. Thus3h-F is the pullback of the ample O(1,1) under a closed embedding, and is ample, not merely nef. This is a direct positivity check independent of the cited classification label.

The family t^2u^2+sum x_i^2=0 is flat: each homogeneous coordinate quotient is torsion-free over C[t] (the defining polynomial has a coefficient1 and no t-c factor), and its fibers are nonzero quadrics. Near C times A1 the total space is relatively smooth, and u,q1 form two relative regular normal parameters. The successive quotients of their powers are symmetric powers of a rank-two conormal bundle on C times A1, hence flat over the base. Therefore the Rees algebra commutes with fiber restriction there. Its two ordinary blowup charts are relatively flat; away from the center the map is the original flat family. This verifies both flatness and the central fiber Bl_C Q0, without assuming arbitrary blowups commute with arbitrary base change. For t nonzero, changing u to tu identifies the fiber and center with the original pair.

At o=[1:0:0:0:0], the center condition u=0 fails, so the blowup is an isomorphism. In the u=1 chart the central equation is sum x_i^2=0 in A4: one three-dimensional ordinary double point. The other points of Q0 are smooth, and blowing up the smooth center adds no other singularities. Blowing up the node has discrepancy(4-1)-2=1 by ambient discrepancy and hypersurface multiplicity. ## 5. Universal irrationality, not a finite resultant list

If b were nonzero algebraic, its polynomial equation, with b^2-2 nonzero, would express e^(2b) as an algebraic number. The classical Hermite–Lindemann statement that the exponential of a nonzero algebraic number is transcendental contradicts this. Thus b is transcendental (this classical theorem is an explicit external input, not proved here).

If a/b=p/q>0 with integers p,q, put r=p/q and z=e^(b/q). The two equations produce a common root U=z of the manuscript's A(T,U),B(T,U) at T=b. Their U-degrees are3p and2q. The resultant is homogeneous of degree2q in A's coefficients and3p in B's. Every determinant term therefore has T-degree at most3(2q)+2(3p)=6(p+q). Its coefficient of that top degree is obtained from the top T-degree coefficient lists, namely r^3 U^(3p) and U^(2q)+3. For a monomial, the resultant is

    Res_U(r^3 U^(3p), U^(2q)+3)=r^(6q) 3^(3p),

which is strictly positive. No gcd-dependent cancellation is possible: the first polynomial's only root is0, where the other has value3. Hence this resultant is a nonzero polynomial over Q for EVERY positive p,q, and its vanishing at the transcendental b is impossible. This is the complete universal argument. It does not rely on checking selected rational ratios.

As hand controls, p=q=1 gives top degree12 and leading coefficient27; p=2,q=1 gives degree18 and leading coefficient2^6*3^6=46656.
