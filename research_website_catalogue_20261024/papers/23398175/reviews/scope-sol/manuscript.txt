       The outer focal-pedal area product in odd elliptic billiards
                                             Alec Kriebel
                                        Independent researcher
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                            15 October 2026


                                                 Abstract
          For a fixed odd-periodic billiard family between strictly nested confocal ellipses, project
      each original focus onto the ordered side lines of the outer tangent polygon. We prove that
      the product of the two signed pedal areas is constant, for every coprime winding, including
      stars. This proves the experimental assertion labelled k605,a in the 2020 arXiv invariant list and
      k606 in its published companion. The focal-foot map has paired collinear residues; its cyclic
      area consequently has only two possible simple poles on a reduced torus. Reflection and an
      imaginary antiperiod provide the complementary zeros. The argument uses established elliptic-
      function methods. We explain connections to generalized Landen identities and recent focal
      inversion results; historical priority of this particular application remains undetermined. Exact
      controls and separately labelled numerical diagnostics accompany this unrefereed, extensively
      AI-assisted note.

admissible coprime star winding is included, with the order and signed-area convention above.

different, original-chord pedal polygons. The outer construction is in Figure 1 and Sections 3.4
and 3.7 of those sources; Section 2 specifies signed area. The assertion already occurs as k604 in
Table 5, p. 6 of arXiv:2004.12497v1, submitted 26 April 2020. The source tables do not explicitly
define least-period, star or repetition conventions; these are made precise in the theorem and
corollary here. Hyperbolic, collapsed and grazing caustics are outside the stated domain.

    At r − v and r + v, the values of sn and cn are respectively a/c and −ib/c. The two numerator
vectors in (3) are therefore both (−ab2 /c, −iab2 /c). The denominator derivatives have opposite
signs by (6). The residue vectors of Q are consequently opposite and collinear.
Lemma 2. On X = C/(LZ + 4pZ), L = 4K/N , the function T has at most simple poles, only at
b0 + p, b0 + 3p, where b0 = K − v.
Proof. The poles of Q at r − v and r + v differ by δ = 2v. At a singular phase of the cyclic
sum, exactly two vertices are singular, at adjacent indices: coprimality makes all N phases distinct
modulo 4K. There are no additional copies from the other imaginary row. Write the two adjacent
expansions as

              Q(r − v + ε) = R/ε + A + O(ε),           Q(r + v + ε) = −R/ε + B + O(ε).

The possible double coeﬀicient of their determinant is det(R, −R) = 0. Every other edge determi-
nant has at most one singular endpoint, hence has at most a simple pole. This also handles N = 3,
where the other two incident edges share their regular vertex, and the edge crossing the cyclic index
cut: N δ = 4Kτ is a true period. The same argument applies in the translated row.
    Cyclic relabelling gives period δ for T ; it also has period 4K. Bezout’s identity with gcd(τ, N ) =
1 gives period L. The possible pole phases r − v − jδ and their 2p translates then collapse to the
two displayed points on X.


4    Reflection, complementary zeros and the product
Let J = diag(1, −1). The Jacobi identities give

                          Q(w + 2p) = JQ(w),           Q(2K − w) = JQ(w).

The first changes each determinant sign. In the second, reflection and cyclic order reversal each
contribute a minus sign, which cancel. Thus

                           T (w + 2p) = −T (w),         T (2K − w) = T (w).                         (7)

Since 2v = δ is a period, F (z) = T (b0 + z) is even. It has periods L, 4p, antiperiod 2p, and by
Lemma 2 at most simple poles at p, 3p.
   At the regular point z0 = p + L/2, evenness and real periodicity give F (−p − L/2) = F (−p +
L/2) = F (p + L/2), whereas the antiperiod gives F (−p + L/2) = −F (p + L/2). Hence F (z0 ) = 0.
The same holds at 3p + L/2 by translation. These points are distinct from p, 3p on X. Every
possible pole of
                                         F (z)F (z + L/2)                                      (8)
is cancelled by one of those zeros in the other factor, because all possible poles are simple. The
product is holomorphic on a compact torus and therefore constant. This also covers the pole-free
case (then the antiperiod forces F = 0). No division by a possibly zero area is involved. This
elementary complementary-divisor argument is a classical elliptic-function mechanism, not a new
general method.
    For odd N , 2K = (N/2)L ≡ L/2 (mod LZ). Equations (5) and (8) prove B+ (w)B− (w) =
constant, establishing Theorem 1. The argument used no restriction to winding one, so includes
all admitted primitive stars.

Corollary 3. An s-fold traversal of a fixed odd primitive family has areas sB± and product s2 B+ B− .
Orientation reversal changes both area signs and preserves the product. For a circular boundary,
the coincident-focus pedal polygons are regular stars and their product is constant by rotation.

Proof. Each ordered edge determinant occurs exactly s times in a repeated list; reversal negates the
sum. On a circle the feet from its center to its tangents are the original regular-star vertices.

   For even primitive periods the focus shift is a full reduced real period, yielding equal focal areas
rather than constancy of their square. The exact counterexample in Section 6 shows that the odd
hypothesis is essential for primitive families.


5    Related identities, current work and priority
The auxiliary circle, canonical coordinates, Jacobi identities and compact torus reasoning are es-
tablished tools. Complex continuation and pole removal occur in earlier Poncelet-area work [5, 6]
and bicentric-invariant work [7]. General cyclic elliptic-function identities and principal-part recon-
struction also predate this application [8].
    There is a second exact proof route using the old arbitrary-order Landen identity of Khare
and Sukhatme [9, equation (7), p. 923; Section 2.1]. For clarity, the full geometric reduction, sign
analysis for stars and an independent Fourier-series derivation are in the accompanying source
archive, under verification/audits/priority_classical_general_01/ in EXACT_BICENTRIC_
DN_BRIDGE.md. Briefly, the focal foot is the restriction to E of the projectivity
