## Family1: ordinary Hopf finite-ideal mechanism

Let B be an ordinary Hopf algebra over a field with antipode S, not assumed bijective. Suppose 0!=I is a finite-dimensional right ideal. For a finite basis i_a of I, every Delta(i_a) is a finite algebraic sum; the span J of all first-factor slices (id tensor f)Delta(i), i in I, f in B* is therefore finite-dimensional. Counit slicing gives I subset J, so J nonzero. Coassociativity gives Delta(J) subset J tensor B.

To check the crucial right-ideal closure explicitly, for j=(id tensor f)Delta(i) and b in B,

sum (id tensor f_{b_(2)})Delta(i b_(1))
= sum i_(1)b_(1) f(i_(2)b_(2)S(b_(3)))
= sum i_(1)b f(i_(2)) = j b,

where f_c(z)=f(zS(c)); the second equality uses sum b_(1) tensor b_(2)S(b_(3))=b tensor1. Each i b_(1) lies in I, so the sum is in J. No S inverse was used.

If epsilon(J)=0, applying epsilon tensor id to Delta(j) in J tensor B would give j=0 for all j, contradiction. Choose j in J scaled to epsilon(j)=1. Then sum j_(1)S(j_(2))=1 belongs to J since its first factors belong to the right ideal J. Consequently 1 in J implies J=B, forcing B finite-dimensional. For a left ideal use B^{op,cop}; reversing multiplication and comultiplication together retains the same antipode, so no bijectivity is silently introduced.

Checks to falsify: the slices must remain finite despite B* being huge; they do because only finitely many first factors occur. The coideal orientation must be J tensor B, not B tensor J, to place the first factors in the right ideal. The epsilon argument uses the OTHER counit identity from the slice containing I. A mere finite-dimensional subspace would not suffice; right-ideal closure is indispensable.

## Family2: graded signs and bosonization

For characteristic2 the Koszul signs become trivial, so the underlying algebra and coproduct are ordinary Hopf. In odd characteristic reduce degrees modulo2 and adjoin g of order2 acting by parity. The stated ordinary product is (h#g^i)(a#g^j)=(-1)^{i|a|}ha#g^{i+j}. Delta_B(h#g^i)=sum(h1#g^{|h2|+i}) tensor(h2#g^i). The ordinary tensor product multiplication now produces sign (|h2|+i)|a1|+i|a2| = |h2||a1|+i|a|, exactly the graded Delta_H multiplicativity plus the outer product sign.

For the left antipode identity, expanding S_B(h1#g^{|h2|+i})(h2#g^i) with the stated formula yields signs that combine the graded antipode relation; the right identity is analogous. These require explicit testing/derivation, not merely declaring the smash product a Hopf algebra. A useful small test is an odd primitive x in an exterior Hopf algebra: Delta_B(x)=x tensor1+g tensor x, so S_B(x)=-g x=x g, not simply -x. Dropping the parity g is expected to fail in odd characteristic. For a homogeneous graded right/left ideal I, I#kC2 is closed on the appropriate side because parity acts by signs and preserves I. Dimensions double and infinitude is retained. The lemma concerns graded ideals; an arbitrary ungraded subspace need not be parity stable.

## Family3: cellular approximation as a filtered finite construction

Proxy-smallness means K is compact over A (K in thick_A(A)), K in thick_A(k), and k in Loc_A(K). The first finite construction is over A and the second over k; neither says k itself is compact. Since K is in Loc_A(k) and k in Loc_A(K), the two localizing subcategories coincide.

Compact Morita theory with E=REnd_A(K) gives Loc_A(K) as right E-modules, with inverse N -> N tensor_E^L K. A left E-action on K by endomorphism evaluation commutes with A (with graded signs); Hom_A(K,M) is right E by precomposition, so the handedness can be consistent without an unmentioned commutativity assumption. Exact conventions in DGI need checking.

For any semifree E-resolution, take a well-ordered generator basis with finite support of each differential among earlier generators. A generator dependency tree is finitely branching and every downward edge strictly decreases the well-order. An infinite dependency closure would have an infinite branch by the finite-branching tree argument, impossible for a well-order. Thus every finite set lies in a finite dependency-closed set. Their finite semifree submodules are filtered (closure of a finite union remains finite), exhaust the resolution, and compute its filtered homotopy colimit. No uniform bound on closure size is needed.

Tensoring with K preserves filtered colimits and sends a finite semifree module to an object in thick_A(K). Homology commutes with filtered homotopy colimits of DG modules; equivalently the underlying unit is compact in D(k). Every single x in H_n(M) therefore comes from some P in thick_A(K). Because K is finitely built from k, its total homology is finite-dimensional; this property is preserved under finite sums, shifts, cones (long exact homology sequence) and retracts. Consequently P has finite TOTAL homology, not merely finite dimension degree by degree. M itself can be infinite-dimensional and need not be compact.

Falsifiers: a false claim that arbitrary cellular M lies in thick(k), treating a nonfiltered construction as an ordinary union, or allowing infinite support in a single basis differential would invalidate this argument. None is asserted in the paper. Existence of semifree resolutions and exact compact Morita scope still need primary confirmation.

## Family4: full A-linear image is a finite ideal

Take a single class x in H_n(M) and P->M, y in H_n(P) as above. For A-linear P->A, the TOTAL image of H_*(P)->H_*(A) is a graded left H_*(A)-submodule of its regular module, hence a graded left ideal. It has dimension at most total dim H_*(P), which is finite. It therefore vanishes by the Hopf lemma. In particular f_*(x)=0. This holds separately in every degree and for every map, proving the stated criterion, not just a special source description.

It would be wrong to apply the finite-ideal lemma to the span of x alone: that span need not be an ideal. The proof instead uses the whole finite-stage image, which IS stable under multiplication. A single A-linear map out of P may still be a nonzero ghost in the derived category; homology vanishing is weaker than nullness. The paper carefully keeps this distinction.

## Family6: arbitrary fields via chains, not cochain duals

Every characteristic-p field contains the prime field Fp. Singular chains commute with tensoring coefficients, so R=R0 tensor_Fp k as augmented DG algebras up to the intended natural model equivalence. Field extension is flat. If K0 is a proxy, extension carries finite constructions from R0 to finite constructions from R; finite constructions from Fp to finite constructions from k; and arbitrary localizing constructions of Fp from K0 to those of k from K. Thus K remains compact and a proxy. Infinite dimensionality of a vector space is preserved as k-dimension after extension, since any Fp-basis becomes a k-basis. This covers infinite algebraic and transcendental fields, not merely finite fields.

Crucially this reasoning does not identify Hom_Fp(C,Fp) tensor k with Hom_k(C tensor k,k) for an infinite-dimensional chain complex. Such an infinite-cochain dual interchange could fail, but no such interchange is needed or asserted. It is not a counterexample to the stated criterion.
