## Exact problem and assumptions

The construction is over the prime field `F_29`, with column-vector action and multiplication

`(v,h)(w,k)=(v+h w,hk)`.

The complement is generated by

`A=((0,28),(1,0))`, `B=((2,7),(12,28))`.

Here `mu'(X)` maximizes the size of an independent subset: each element must be outside the subgroup generated by the remaining elements. The subset need not generate all of `X`. The invariant `b(X)` maximizes the size of an **inclusion-minimal** base over all permutation representations, including nonfaithful and intransitive ones. The base is for the permutation image, so its pointwise stabilizer in `X` equals the representation kernel. `b_f` restricts to faithful representations. This is neither the smallest base size nor the ordered irredundant-base invariant.

## Independent reconstruction and attempted failures

### 1. Arbitrary actions and normality

For an inclusion-minimal base, its point stabilizers `L_i` have an irredundant intersection equal to the action kernel `N`, which is normal. Conversely, if an irredundant subgroup family has normal intersection `N`, the disjoint union of left coset actions `X/L_i` has kernel

`intersection Core_X(L_i) = Core_X(intersection L_i) = N`.

The identity cosets therefore give the required minimal base. This proves `b(X)=max_{N normal X} b_f(X/N)` and does not silently require transitivity. Empty families correctly give the zero invariants for the trivial group. Even for an infinite action set, finiteness of the permutation image ensures that a minimal base is finite.

For an arbitrary meet-irredundant family, choose `x_i` in the intersection of all members except `L_i`, but outside `L_i`. The other `x_j` lie in `L_i`, proving independence. Conversely, the omission-generated subgroups of an independent set form an irredundant intersection family. No normality is required for this equivalence. Restricting a family to `L_i` preserves its witnesses and its total intersection, yielding `t-1 <= mu'(L_i)`; when the total intersection is trivial it also yields `t-1 <= b_f(L_i)`.

This distinction is essential. Taking normal cores cannot turn a general irredundant family into an equally large minimal base. The printed rank-four example explicitly loses essentiality after cores are taken. No quotient by a nonnormal subgroup is used. The top-preserving Boolean meet-semilattice embedding follows from the same witnesses; preservation of joins is not asserted.

### 2. Complement order, subgroups and two different base invariants

Direct exact products give `A^2=B^3=(AB)^5=-I`. The usual triangle-group route identifies the projective image with `A_5`. The theorem does not depend on accepting that presentation without verification: the supplied finite certificates also construct a full multiplication-preserving isomorphism with `SL_2(5)` and an explicit quotient map to the even permutations of five letters. The fresh checks reconstruct the 120 matrices without a saved table.

The elementary subgroup analysis of `A_5` is adequate here. An intransitive subgroup fixes a letter or has orbits of sizes two and three. A proper transitive subgroup has possible orders 5, 10, 15, 20 or 30. The Sylow-5 normalizer excludes 15 and 20; generation by 3-cycles excludes index two. Hence every proper subgroup lies in `A_4`, `D_10` or `S_3`. Their independence numbers are at most two, and the degree-five action gives the lower bound three for `A_5`. The conjugacy-class-size proof of simplicity also suffices; no classification of finite simple groups is needed.

Because the characteristic is odd, the only nonidentity involution in `SL_2(29)` is `-I`. Thus every even-order subgroup of `H` contains `Z={I,-I}`. Odd-order subgroups inject into `A_5` and are trivial or cyclic of order 3 or 5. For an independent indexed family in `H`, either its images are independent in `H/Z`, or one element lies in `KZ` but not in `K`, where `K` is generated by the others. The latter forces `K` to be odd and hence cyclic of prime order or trivial. This establishes `mu'(H)<=3`, including possible repeated or identity images in the quotient.

A trivial-intersection irredundant family in `H` must have an odd-order member. If that member has prime order, one other member already intersects it trivially; hence `b_f(H)<=2`. Subgroups of orders 3 and 5 attain two. The printed three matrices have pair subgroup orders 8, 12 and 20, generate `H`, and their omission intersection is `Z`. These give `mu'(H)=b(H)=3`. In particular, it would be incorrect to substitute `b(H)=3` for `b_f(H)=2` in the later restriction argument.

The printed pair-product orders are exactly 4, 3 and 10, in the stated order. I checked the displayed matrix words and these values afresh. Their projective orders are 2, 3 and 5, consistent with the pair subgroup sizes. The generic finite verification uses all actual subgroups, not only conjugacy representatives.

### 3. The critical subgroup bound below the translation subgroup

A line stabilizer `H_W` acts on its line through scalars. The kernel is unipotent with determinant one; a nonidentity element of that kernel would have order 29, impossible in a group of order 120. Therefore the scalar action is injective and `|H_W|` divides `gcd(120,28)=4`. All thirty stabilizers in fact have order four. This proves irreducibility of `H` and is the decisive arithmetic restriction.

Let `K<=G` fail to contain `V`. Since 29 is prime, `K intersection V` is an `F_29`-subspace, so it is zero or a line. If it is zero, projection embeds `K` in `H`, and the subgroup formulations give the required monotonicity `b_f(K)<=2` and `mu'(K)<=3`. There is no need to assume `K` is literally contained in the printed zero-translation complement.

If `K intersection V=W` is a line, then `pi(K)<=H_W` is a cyclic 2-group. A Sylow 2-subgroup of `K` maps isomorphically onto this projection and complements `W`; the induced scalar action on `W` is faithful. Thus `K` is an abstract faithful scalar semidirect product `C_29:C_d`, with `d=1,2,4`, regardless of the affine displacement of its complement.

For the general scalar lemma, an independent set generates some subgroup `J`, not necessarily the whole scalar affine group. If `J` misses the translations it is cyclic of 2-power order. Otherwise it contains all translations, and its image is cyclic of 2-power order. A generating set contains an element mapping to a generator of that image because a cyclic prime-power group has a unique maximal subgroup. The geometric-series identity makes that element a complement. One further generator outside it generates all of `J`. This proves `mu'(K)<=2` in the line case; no generation-only restriction has been smuggled into `mu'`.

As a direct attack on this step, the fresh controls include every affine lift for every line and both nontrivial scalar projections. They produce all 1,770 possible line-intersection subgroups: 30 of order 29, 870 of order 58, and 870 of order 116. Completeness follows because fixing a generator of the projection specifies a lift modulo `W`, leaving exactly 29 cosets; the trivial projection leaves just `W`. The computations confirm distinctness, exact order and translation intersection for all these lifts. Complete subgroup checks on the three scalar models find no irredundant triples. This test includes displaced complements, not just the simplest split representatives.

### 4. Universal upper bounds, including nonfaithful actions

A faithful minimal-base family has intersection one. At least one member misses `V`; restricting to that member gives `t-1<=2`, hence `b_f(G)<=3`. This argument covers arbitrary subgroup families and does not assume maximal, conjugate or transitive stabilizers.

For a normal subgroup `N` of `G`, irreducibility gives `N intersection V=0` or `V`. In the zero case, `[N,V]` is contained in both normal subgroups and therefore is trivial. Thus `N` centralizes `V`. Faithfulness of the matrix action gives `C_G(V)=V`, so `N=1`. Every nontrivial normal subgroup consequently contains `V`. Every nonfaithful action factors through `G/V=H`, where its base is bounded by `b(H)=3`. Together these establish the all-actions upper bound, not merely its faithful version.

For `mu'(G)`, begin with any irredundant intersection family, without requiring a normal bottom. If every member contains `V`, quotienting by the genuinely normal subgroup `V` gives rank at most three. Otherwise restrict to a member missing `V`: its independence bound is three in the zero-intersection case and two in the line case, giving total rank at most four. This covers independent subsets of every subgroup of `G`.

### 5. Witnesses and boundary cases

The displayed set consisting of translation by `e_1` and the three linear matrices is independent: projection proves essentiality of each linear generator, and the translation cannot lie in the zero-translation complement. Irreducibility implies that this set generates all of `G`. Its omission orders are 120, 6728, 10092 and 16820. The latter pair subgroups have orders greater than four, so none can preserve a line; their translation closures are all of `V`. The common omission intersection is the order-two complement center, which translation by `e_1` conjugates outside itself.

The three displayed order-58 subgroups have pairwise order-two intersections but trivial total intersection. Their coset action therefore supplies a faithful minimal base of size three, with degree 5220. The fresh checks deform the last subgroup through every one of the 841 involution offsets: 812 give a faithful minimal triple, while the 29 offsets lying on the diagonal line retain a nontrivial common involution. This negative boundary control would detect the mistake of assuming any three dihedral subgroups automatically work.

The characteristic-11 test was reproduced using the **specific printed** matrix `C`, rather than the supplied Python checker's first-found order-ten matrix. The four subgroup orders are 110, 110, 242 and 605, their total intersection is trivial, and their deletion intersections have orders 11, 11, 5 and 2. This supports the manuscript's stated limitation and refutes the tempting characteristic-independent extension. The proof makes no claim for prime-power translation fields where additive subgroups need not be one-dimensional subspaces over the larger field.
