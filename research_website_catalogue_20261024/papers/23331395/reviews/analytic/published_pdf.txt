                Positive tangent-bundle Seshadri constants
                       characterize projective space

                                             Abstract
          Let X be a smooth integral projective variety over an algebraically closed field of
      arbitrary characteristic. We prove that positivity of the relative Seshadri constant of TX
      at one closed point implies X ≃ Pdim X , resolving the conjecture of Fulger and Murayama.
      An absolute minimum of ample degree among rational curves through the specified point
      gives a proper pointed family without boundary. Its very-free members yield surjective
      evaluation. A contracted marking section and line-bundle descent force Picard number one
      and ampleness of −KX , reducing the problem to the established Fano case. No general-
      point or characteristic-zero assumption is used. This is an unrefereed preprint developed
      and checked extensively with AI tools.


1    The statement and its context
For a vector bundle E on a projective variety X, let ρ : P(E) = ProjX Sym E → X use the
quotient convention, and write ξ = c1 (OP(E) (1)). The relative Seshadri constant of Fulger–
Murayama is
                                                  ξ·Γ
                                 (E; x) = inf             ,                             (1)
                                            Γ multx (ρ∗ Γ)

where Γ ranges over integral curves in P(E) whose images contain x and which are not
contracted by ρ. If Γ → C has total generic degree q, then ρ∗ Γ = q[C]; the degree includes
its inseparable factor. Global nefness of E is not required.
Theorem 1. Let X be a smooth integral projective variety of dimension n ≥ 1 over an
algebraically closed field k of arbitrary characteristic. If (TX ; x) > 0 at a closed point x ∈ X,
then X ≃k Pn .
    This is [1, Conjecture 4.9], also stated in Fulger’s contribution to the 2019 Oberwolfach
workshop [2, p. 3289]. Fulger–Murayama prove the Fano case in every characteristic, the
surface case, and the general-point case in characteristic zero. Chang proves the smooth
projective toric case [3, Theorem 0.2]. The conclusion here retains the original arbitrary point
and removes the additional Fano hypothesis.
    Proper pointed rational-curve families and their numerical-generation properties are stan-
dard tools; see, for example, [4, Proposition 2.20(b)]. We give the numerical argument ex-
plicitly using a genuine contracted section. The proof combines these tools with a minimum
taken over all rational images through the given point and a characteristic-free construction
of the actual marked family. The final characterization is imported from the established Fano
clause [1, Proposition 4.8(1)], which uses Mori’s theorem as presented in [6, Theorem V.3.2].
The conjecture itself is never an input.

2    A complete family through the specified point
Lemma 2. Every normalization f : P1 → C ,→ X of an integral rational curve C through x
has f ∗ TX ample.
                       ⊕
Proof. Write f ∗ TX = ni=1 OP1 (ai ), and put m = multx C. A quotient onto O(ai ) defines a
lift si : P1 → P(TX ) of f . Its image Γi is birationally parametrized by si : its function field lies
between the equal fields k(C) and k(P1 ). Hence ξ · Γi = ai and ρ∗ Γi = [C]. Equation (1) gives
ai /m ≥ (TX ; x) > 0. Thus each integer ai ≥ 1. This argument works in every characteristic,
including singular rational images, without replacing Frobenius-normalized slopes on general
curves by ordinary slopes.

    The proof of [1, Corollary 4.6] produces an integral rational curve through the same point
x from the hypothesis of Theorem 1. Fix a very ample line bundle A on X, and set

                    d = min{A · C : C ⊂ X is integral rational and x ∈ C}.                        (2)

This is an attained positive integer.

Lemma 3. Every geometric genus-zero stable map of A-degree d with one marking evaluating
to x has smooth domain P1 and is birational onto its image.

Proof. First the minimum in (2) survives any algebraically closed extension K/k. A rational
image through xK of degree c < d would give a degree-c normalization map P1K → XK ,
reparametrized so that 0 7→ xK . The degree-c pointed Hom scheme is of finite type over k.
Its nonemptiness would give a k-point, since k is algebraically closed. The image of that map
is a rational curve through x of degree at most c, contradicting (2). Rationality of the image
follows from Lüroth’s theorem, also in positive characteristic.
    Consider a geometric map (g : D → X, p) as in the statement. Its dual graph is a tree of
projective lines. Starting at the marked component, follow components contracted to x until
reaching a nonconstant component Dv . Its image Cv is a rational curve through x. If ew is
the full generic degree of each nonconstant component onto its image, then
                             ∑
                     d=             ew (A · Cw ) ≥ ev (A · Cv ) ≥ A · Cv ≥ d.
                          w nonconstant

All summands are positive integers. There is therefore just one nonconstant component, and
it maps birationally to a degree-d image. Any connected contracted subtree with t > 0
vertices attaches to it along one edge and has at most one marking. Its total number of
special-point incidences is at most 2(t − 1) + 1 + 1 = 2t, whereas stability requires at least
3t. Such a subtree cannot exist. This includes a marking initially on a contracted component
and excludes inseparable multiple covers by their full degree.

Lemma 4. There are an integral projective scheme H, a smooth projective-line bundle π :
U → H, a section σ : H → U , and a surjective morphism e : U → X such that eσ = x. Every
fiber map is birational onto a degree-d rational curve through x.

Proof. Abramovich–Oort [5, Theorem 2.8 and §2.5] give a proper finite-presentation Artin
stack of one-marked genus-zero maps to X of A-degree d, with a projective coarse scheme Q.
Let Mx be the actual evaluation fiber over x. It is proper and nonempty. Lemma 3 describes
all its geometric objects.
    Their stabilizer group schemes are trivial. A geometric automorphism preserving a bira-
tional map is the identity on the open set where the map is an isomorphism onto its image.
Infinitesimal automorphisms are vector fields on P1 vanishing at the mark and killed by dg.


                                                  2
The normalization map has generically injective differential, so such a vector field is zero. A
finite group scheme with one geometric point and zero tangent space is the identity: its local
maximal ideal satisfies m/m2 = 0, and Nakayama applies. This also makes inertia trivial over
nonreduced bases. On an atlas its finite algebra has an augmentation split by the identity
section as O ⊕ J. The finite module J has zero geometric fibers and therefore vanishes by
Nakayama.
    Trivial inertia makes Mx an algebraic space [9, Tag 04SZ]. Its natural morphism to Q is
proper, because Mx is proper over k and Q is separated. It is quasi-finite: coarse geometric
points identify isomorphism classes, so each geometric fiber contains at most one point. Tags
03XX and 02LS of [9] make Mx a scheme finite over Q, hence projective. No commutation
of coarse moduli with a closed evaluation fiber in wild characteristic is assumed.
    The stack’s universal curve restricts to an actual smooth proper P1 -family on Mx , with
marking section. The marking is a relative effective Cartier divisor. Its line bundle has
fiber degree one; cohomology and base change give a rank-two pushforward and a fiberwise
surjective evaluation map. This identifies the family with the quotient projective bundle of
that pushforward, also over nonreduced bases. One can check the identification by noting
that the induced morphism is proper and fiberwise an isomorphism, hence finite; Nakayama
makes its algebra map surjective, and flatness over the base makes its defining ideal have zero
fibers, so that ideal vanishes.
    Choose a degree-d normalization f : P1 → X with f (0) = x. Let Rd be the degree-d Hom
scheme with 0 7→ x. Its morphism to Mx is the torsor of pointed parametrizations under the
smooth group Aut(P1 , 0). Indeed the projective-line bundle with section just constructed is
locally a pointed trivial bundle. By Lemma 2,

                  H 1 (P1 , f ∗ TX (−0)) = 0,     H 1 (P1 , f ∗ TX (−0 − ∞)) = 0.

Fixed-source deformation theory makes Rd smooth at f and evaluation at ∞ smooth there.
For the latter assertion the obstruction is the second displayed group: local lifts into smooth
X respecting the two prescribed values differ by a Čech cocycle in f ∗ TX (−0 − ∞), whose
vanishing obstruction allows the lifts to glue. This is infinitesimal smoothness, not generic
smoothness in characteristic zero; see also [7, Proposition 4.7].
    Smooth descent along the parametrization torsor makes Mx smooth at [f, 0]. Let H be
its unique reduced irreducible component there, and restrict the universal curve to U → H. A
neighborhood of f in Rd maps into H. Its smooth evaluation at ∞ factors through e : U → X,
so e(U ) contains a nonempty open subset of X. It is closed because U is proper; irreducibility
of X gives e(U ) = X. The total space U is integral: smoothness over reduced H gives
reducedness, and flatness with geometrically integral fibers over integral H gives irreducibility.
It is projective because it is a projective-line bundle over projective H. All asserted fiber
degrees and birationality follow from Lemma 3.

We spell out the standard numerical-generation argument for the family above. In particular
it tests curves avoiding x, as well as curves of positive genus.

Lemma 5. For every Cartier divisor D on X, put b = D · C, where C is the image of a fiber
of U → H. Then b is independent of the fiber and

                                    d[D] = b[A]       in N 1 (X)R .

Proof. Degrees of a line bundle on fibers of a proper flat curve family are locally constant.
They are constant here since H is integral. The line bundle L = OX (dD) ⊗ A−b thus pulls
back to a bundle M = e∗ L of degree zero on every geometric P1 -fiber. Each such restriction
is trivial and has h0 = 1, h1 = 0. Proper flat cohomology and base change give a line bundle
B = π∗ M with π ∗ B ≃ M ; the adjunction map is an isomorphism on every geometric fiber and
hence everywhere. The applicable perfect-base-change result is [9, Tag 0B91]; direct fiberwise
descent is also given by Tags 0EX7 and 0GKA. Pulling back by σ gives

                                B ≃ σ ∗ e∗ L ≃ L|x ⊗k OH ≃ OH .

Thus e∗ L is trivial.
   Let Γ ⊂ X be any integral curve and let η be its generic point. Surjectivity of e makes
Uη nonempty. A closed point of this finite-type k(Γ)-scheme has residue field K finite over
k(Γ), of total degree q > 0. Its reduced closure in U is a proper integral curve Γ′ dominating
Γ with generic degree q. The degree projection formula gives

                                 0 = deg(e∗ L|Γ′ ) = q deg(L|Γ ).

These are integer degrees, so the conclusion remains valid even if the characteristic divides
q. Hence L is numerically trivial on X, proving the asserted relation. The ample class [A] is
nonzero since A · C = d > 0, so the Picard number is exactly one.

Verification, priority, and AI-use disclosure
The supporting package contains the source, the original frozen candidate, reproducible in-
tegrity and finite controls, independent mathematical audit reports, and a dated priority-
search ledger. The finite controls supplement the written proof; they do not formally verify
the geometric theorem. A bounded primary-source priority audit on 12 October 2026 identi-
fied no earlier full resolution or explicitly matching immediate consequence. It does not certify
first historical priority. The standard numerical-generation principle and the previously estab-
lished special cases are credited above. The full Kollár monograph and full CMSB text were
not independently obtained in this audit; the exact published Fulger–Murayama propositions
used here were directly inspected. An in-preparation reference on moving Seshadri constants
