Abstract
Let M be a smooth, Hausdorﬀ, second-countable n-manifold without boundary, with a positive
Riemannian metric and two prescribed, mutually dual, torsion-free connections. For n ≥ 1 we construct
a proper smooth embedding into an open subset of R(2n+4
3 ) carrying one smooth positive Hessian potential.
Orthogonal projection of the ambient standard connection and its metric dual induces exactly the ordered
pair of given connections. Finite polynomial features provide a smooth global scalar third-jet lifting;
a variable tubular neighborhood and a pointwise Schur estimate provide ambient positivity without
compactness or uniform bounds. For a general Riemannian dual pair, vanishing of both torsions is
necessary and suﬃcient for such realization. This supplies a self-contained answer in the smooth statistical
category to the historical dually ﬂat realization question. Historical novelty and present openness are
not asserted; important prior constructions and unresolved primary-source coverage are speciﬁed.

1 Statement and conventions
Amari asked whether an arbitrary manifold with a Riemannian dual structure can be realized as a higher-
dimensional submanifold of a dually ﬂat manifold with the induced dual structure [ 1, p. 87, question 1].
The question appears in Furuhata–Matsuzoe–Urakawa [ 2, p. 125, item 1(a)], reporting the December 1996
workshop. The cited deﬁnition requires zero torsion and curvature for both ambient connections, with dual
aﬃne coordinates understood locally [ 1, pp. 84–85]. No ﬁxed probability model, convexity, completeness, or
globally injective dual coordinate map is imposed here.
Throughout, manifolds and data are smooth. A dual pair satisﬁes
Xg (Y, Z ) = g(∇X Y, Z ) + g(Y, ∇∗

It is statistical when both connections are torsion-free. The connections induced along an isometric immersion
are the metric-orthogonal tangential projections of the respective ambient connections. Flatness below refers
to these aﬃne connections, not to Levi–Civita curvature.
Theorem 1. Let (M, g, ∇, ∇∗) be a statistical manifold of ﬁxed ﬁnite dimension n ≥ 1, Hausdorﬀ and second-
countable, without boundary, with g positive deﬁnite. There exist an open set U ⊂ RN , N =

f : M → U such that f ∗G = g. The standard ﬂat connection D and its G-dual D∗ are torsion-free and ﬂat
and induce, in that order, ∇ and ∇∗.
There is no connectedness or completeness hypothesis. The domain U depends on all the data and may
be nonconvex and incomplete. The theorem neither constructs a ﬁnite probability model nor optimizes N .
Its proof uses the proper Whitney embedding theorem and the smooth Euclidean tubular neighborhood
theorem; the jet lifting and positivity argument are given explicitly below.

5 Prior constructions and the scope of the answer
The conclusion answers the historical existence question in its smooth positive statistical category. It is not
a claim that this is the ﬁrst answer or that the question remained open until this note. Several ingredients
have substantial precedents. Lauritzen’s statistical, cubic-tensor, dual-connection, and induced-connection
framework is in [ 3]. The separate Lancaster note [ 4] is credited for pair embeddings by Marugame [ 13, §2.4];
its original body was not obtained in the present source audit. Matumoto [ 5, Theorem 1 and proof, pp. 328–
331] constructs global contrast functions by compatible scalar third-order data and explicitly distinguishes
the further ﬂat-embedding question.
Finite jets, freedom, and right inverses belong to the Nash–Gromov embedding tradition. In statistical
geometry they occur in Lê’s long preprint [ 8, pp. 33–39] and in Loi–Matta’s robustness theorem and minimum-
norm inverse [ 14]. The latter starts with already induced statistical data; openness near such data does not
alone realize an arbitrary prescribed metric and cubic. Marugame’s local equivalence [ 13, Theorem 2.2, §2.4]
constructs a Hessian extension from a pair using a local primitive and a positive transverse quadratic term.
Those are direct antecedents of the extension stage here. The present proof supplies a global pair with
ϕ df = 0 and a variable-neighborhood positivity argument. Osipov’s positive Hessian cones are genuine
global realizations for the stated constant-statistical-curvature classes [ 15]; their restriction is the curvature
hypothesis, not the absence of a single global aﬃne chart.
The ﬁnite-probability-model literature needs a separate distinction. Lê’s earlier texts make unrestricted
ﬁnite-simplex claims [ 8, 10, 11]; the inspected arXiv v6 explicitly assumes compactness in its Main Theorem
and Theorems 5.1 and 5.6 [ 9]. In the actual 2017 book, Theorem 4.10 on p. 222 gives ﬁnite-sample immersion
for compact input and a countable-sample model for noncompact input; Theorem 4.11 on p. 238 makes the
corresponding embedding distinction [ 12]. These statements were inspected in a Springer-authorized preview;
they should not be replaced by the broader chapter abstract. The complete chapter, including the unavailable
p. 239, was not audited.
A valid unrestricted smooth ﬁnite-simplex embedding theorem would already answer the historical ques-
tion: in mixture coordinates the potential ∑
a pa log pa has positive Hessian equal to the Fisher metric, and
its derivative cubic is minus the Amari–Chentsov cubic. Applying such a theorem to the appropriate cubic
sign induces both ordered connections by ( 2). Properness is not a historical novelty shield either: an embed-
ded image S is open in its ambient closure, so removing the closed set S \ S makes the embedding proper in
a smaller allowed open ambient. Neither observation proves the particular dimension bound here to be new.
The inspected older noncompact gluing arguments leave compatibility of the separately embedded pieces
and their derivatives unproved; this is not a counterexample to their conclusions. The ﬁnal 2006 article
and ﬁnal 2007 chapter were not obtained as authenticated full bodies, and are not assumed identical to an
arXiv version. Along with the separate Lauritzen note and Abe’s article [ 16], they remain material primary-
source gaps. The accepted manuscripts inspected for Marugame and Loi–Matta are distinguished from their
publisher versions in the references. This bounded literature audit establishes neither absence of an earlier
full solution nor current openness. The note presents the explicit proof above with those limitations.

Finite exact checks and numerical examples corroborate identities; they do not replace the universal proof or
