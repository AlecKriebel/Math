Hopfian wreath products and the stable finiteness conjecture

Henry Bradford1 · Francesco Fournier-Facio2

Received: 6 December 2022 / Accepted: 29 August 2024 / Published online: 21 October 2024
© The Author(s) 2024


Abstract
We study the Hopf property for wreath products of finitely generated groups, focusing on
the case of an abelian base group. Our main result establishes a strong connection between
this problem and Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture. Namely, the latter holds true if
and only if for every finitely generated abelian group A and every finitely generated Hopfian
group  the wreath product A   is Hopfian. In fact, we characterize precisely when A   is
Hopfian, in terms of the existence of one-sided units in certain matrix algebras over F p [],
for every prime p occurring as the order of some element in A. A tool in our arguments is
the fact that fields of positive characteristic locally embed into matrix algebras over F p thus
reducing the stable finiteness conjecture to the case of F p . A further application of this result
shows that the validity of Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture is equivalent to a version
of Gottschalk’s surjunctivity conjecture for additive cellular automata.


1 Introduction

theory, providing examples of interesting behaviour, while still being intuitive and susceptible

    One of the first and most influential occurrences of wreath products in combinatorial group
theory was in Gruenberg’s study of residual properties of solvable groups [23]. Along the
way, he showed the that a wreath product of two (not necessarily solvable) groups    is
residually finite if and only if  and  are residually finite, and either  is abelian or  is
finite. When  and  are both finitely generated,    is also finitely generated, and so in
particular if  is abelian and  is residually finite, then    is Hopfian.
    Recall that a group is Hopfian if every self-epimorphism is an automorphism. They are
named after Hopf, who first showed that surface groups are Hopfian (see [37, p. 415]). The
fact that finitely generated residually finite groups are Hopfian was proven by Mal’cev [33].

The first examples of finitely generated non-Hopfian groups were given by Neumann [42],
and of finitely presented ones by Higman [27], but the simplest and most important example
is probably that of Baumslag–Solitar groups, such as BS(2, 3) = a, t | ta 2 t −1 = a 3  [6, 17,
36]. The class of Hopfian groups has received much attention in geometric group theory ever
since, and the connection with residual finiteness is an important reason for this. A standout
example is the fact that hyperbolic groups are Hopfian [47, 51], which may be seen as a
weaker version of the longstanding conjecture that all hyperbolic groups are residually finite.
In another direction, let us mention that the Hopf property is undecidable [8], and this is not a
straightforward application of the Adian–Rabin Theorem, since the Hopf property is neither
Markov nor co-Markov [39].
    The starting point of this paper is the following general question:

Question 1.1 Let ,  be finitely generated groups. When is the wreath product   
Hopfian?

    Despite the fact that finitely generated residually finite groups are Hopfian, the two prop-
erties are very different in flavour. Indeed, residual finiteness ensures the existence of many
normal subgroups, while Hopficity prevents the existence of certain normal subgroups. The
most striking illustration of this, is that an infinite simple group cannot be residually finite,
but it is Hopfian. Therefore, without any assumption of residual finiteness, trying to establish
Hopficity of a group requires a different approach. Hence, while Question 1.1 is related to
Gruenberg’s result, the techniques needed to attack it are bound to be different.
    Let us start by mentioning some easy reductions for Question 1.1:

Proposition 1.2 (Lemmata 2.4 and 2.5) If    is Hopfian, then  is Hopfian. If moreover
 is abelian, then  is Hopfian.

    Given this, and the relevance of abelian groups in Gruenberg’s result, for most of the
paper we focus on the case in which  is a finitely generated abelian group. To make this
assumption clear, we denote it by A instead.
    As mentioned above, by Gruenberg’s result, if  is finitely generated residually finite then
A   Hopfian. In light of this, and of Proposition 1.2 above, it is natural to conjecture that 
finitely generated Hopfian implies A   Hopfian. Our main result shows that this conjecture
is equivalent to one of the most longstanding open problems in group theory:

Theorem 1.3 (Theorem 4.11) The following are equivalent:
1. For every finitely generated abelian group A and every finitely generated Hopfian group
   , the wreath product A   is Hopfian.
2. Kaplansky’s direct finiteness conjecture holds.

Remark 1.4 All of the statements in this paper assume  to be finitely generated. Other less
elegant assumptions on  also ensure that our main results hold (see Remark 2.12).

    Recall that a ring with identity R is directly finite if every element with a one-sided inverse
is a unit; equivalently, if x y = 1 implies yx = 1 (Lemma 3.1). It is stably finite if the matrix
rings Md (R) are directly finite for all d ≥ 1. Kaplansky’s direct (resp. stable) finiteness
conjecture asserts that the group ring F[] is directly (resp. stably) finite for every group
 and every field F. It turns out that the two conjectures are equivalent [16] and hold over
fields of characteristic 0 [31, p. 122]. The case of positive characteristic is still wide open.
It is known to hold for sofic groups [18], and more generally for surjunctive groups [45],

had already appeared in the literature. This can be traced back to the remark that surjunctive
groups satisfy Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture over finite fields [18], to which we

products via geometric methods, and apply this to give a description of the automorphism
group of A  , where A is a finite cyclic group and  is a one-ended finitely presented
group, in terms of the units of the group ring A[]. Their starting point is similar to ours, but
their analysis is completely geometric. Moreover, we believe that our methods could help to
extend their analysis to the case in which the base is an arbitrary finite abelian group.

Corollary 1.8 (Corollary 3.15) Let p be a prime. Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture
over some field of characteristic p implies Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture over all
fields of characteristic p.

   After reading a preliminary version of this paper, Giles Gardam pointed out to us that a
reduction to finite fields can be achieved for all of the Kaplansky Conjectures via a Nullstel-
lensatz argument: see [35, Section 3.4.3] for a proof in the case of the zero-divisor conjecture.
That said, a peculiarity of the stable finiteness conjecture is that it allows to move between
matrix algebras of different degrees. This makes it possible to reduce to F p , rather than to the
class of all finite fields. We do not know if such a strong reduction holds for the zero-divisor
and idempotent conjectures.
   Corollary 1.8 gives a new, elementary proof of the following fact:

Corollary 1.9 (Corollary 3.25) Surjunctive groups satisfy Kaplansky’s stable finiteness con-
jecture.

    Indeed, it was already noticed in [18] that surjunctive groups satisfy Kaplansky’s stable
finiteness conjecture over finite fields, which follows easily from the definition. The statement
for surjunctive groups was recently proven by Phung [45] via operator algebras (see [15] for
an alternative proof via model theory). Via the Gromov–Weiss Theorem [22, 50], we also
obtain a new proof of the following classical result:

Corollary 1.10 (Corollary 3.25) Sofic groups satisfy Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture.

   This statement has now been proven several times: via operator algebras [18], via linear
cellular automata [10] and via metric approximations of groups [4]; Corollary 1.10 adds a
commutative algebra approach to this list of proofs.
   A further application of Proposition 1.7 is to provide yet another equivalence with the
stable finiteness conjecture:

Theorem 1.11 (Theorem 3.24) Let  be a group. Then Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjec-
ture holds over  if and only if  is A-surjunctive.

   Here a group is said to be A-surjunctive if every injective additive cellular automaton over
 is surjective (Definition 3.21). The analogous statement for linear cellular automata, which
yields the notion of L-surjunctive groups, was proven in [10].
   We are also able to treat some non-abelian base groups. On the one hand, we treat nilpotent
groups, where an induction argument on the nilpotency class allows to extend our results in
the abelian case:

Theorem 1.12 (Theorem 5.5 and Corollary 5.6) Let  be a finitely generated nilpotent group,
with upper central series {Z i }i=0c . Let  be a finitely generated group and suppose that

(Z i /Z i−1 )   is Hopfian for all i = 1, . . . , c. Then    is Hopfian.
    In particular, Kaplansky’s stable finiteness conjecture holds if and only if    is Hopfian
for every finitely generated nilpotent group  and every finitely generated Hopfian group .
