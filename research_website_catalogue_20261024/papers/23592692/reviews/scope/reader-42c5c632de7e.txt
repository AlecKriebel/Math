          A sharp forest bound and an improved whisker bound
                         for ﬁnite WARM graphs
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                        Independent researcher
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                           21 October 2026         Version 1.0.0       Preprint


                                                 Abstract
        Consider power reinforcement with exponent α > 1 on a ﬁnite simple loopless graph: vertices
     ﬁre at positive rates and select an incident edge proportionally to its current count to the power
     α. We prove that the inﬁnitely reinforced edges almost surely form a forest for α > 4/3, for
     arbitrary positive vertex rates and positive integer initial counts. The known triangle obstruction
     shows that the constant 4/3 cannot be decreased; its equality case is excluded. For equal rates
     we prove that every surviving component has diameter at most three when α ≥ 17/4. The
     main deterministic step excludes an arbitrary weighted odd support cycle of length m when
     α > sec2 (π/(2m)); the corresponding uniform cycle threshold and the even-cycle method are
     known. We use the published ﬁnite WARM convergence and support theorems, and credit
     the earlier least-weight and champion arguments. The forest result answers the forest-bound
     improvement requested in Oberwolfach Reports 12/2023; the suggested sharp whisker bound
     remains open. AI tools were extensively involved. This is an unrefereed preprint.

Theorem 1. For every graph and every positive integer initial count vector as above:

    1. If α > 4/3, then (V, E∞ ) is almost surely a forest, for arbitrary positive rates.

    2. If the rates are equal and α ≥ 17/4, then almost surely every component of (V, E∞ ) is a
       whisker.

For each 1 < α < 4/3 the equal-rate triangle with unit initial counts has positive probability that
all three edges survive. Consequently no smaller constant can replace 4/3 in the ﬁrst assertion. No
assertion is made at α = 4/3.

    The new mechanism considered here is the weighted odd-cycle estimate, which applies to every
equilibrium with that support, even with unequal rates, chords and attachments. The threshold
for the uniform equilibrium on a bare odd cycle was already given in van der Hofstad–Holmes–
Kuznetsov–Ruszel [2, Proposition 2(iv)]. The even-cycle cancellation is already in [4, Lemma 2],
whose proof extends immediately to positive rate coeﬃcients. The triangle obstruction is also
known [2, Proposition 2(v), Theorem 4(ii)]. The whisker proof sharpens the established least-
weight/champion approach. The earlier stated bound is 25; in fact the displayed constants in [3,
Section 5.1], together with [4], already imply the weaker improvement α > 24. A precise derivation
is recorded in Section 5. Thus we do not claim the ﬁrst possible numerical improvement of 25.

Lemma 2. Every almost-sure tail assertion just described, including the support conclusions de-
duced from them, transfers from unit counts to any ﬁxed positive integer initial count vector.

Proof. In the embedded jump chain, prescribe ne − 1 reinforcements of each edge e, in any ﬁxed
order. At each step prescribe one endpoint of that edge to ﬁre. The vertex probability is λv /Λ > 0,
and every prescribed edge probability is positive because all counts are positive. The event that this
ﬁnite prescription occurs has positive probability, and leaves exactly the count vector n. Conditional
on this event, the future chain has precisely the law started at n. The Poisson memoryless property
gives the corresponding continuous-time future law. Failure of an almost-sure tail assertion with
positive probability from n would therefore give failure with positive probability from unit counts.
Finite preﬁxes and ﬁnite time shifts do not change convergence of counts divided by time, their
limit, or which counts diverge. This proves the assertion.

     For the known sharpness obstruction take a triangle with all rates and limiting weights equal to
one. Its Jacobian has diagonal α/2 − 1 and oﬀ-diagonal −α/4, giving eigenvalues −1 and 3α/4 − 1
twice. It is strictly stable for 1 < α < 4/3, and the published attainment theorem gives positive
probability from unit counts. All three edges survive. At 4/3 the two transverse eigenvalues
vanish, and neither this attainment clause nor our strict instability argument decides the stochastic
outcome.

4.3   The matching conclusion
Every internal edge consequently has weight greater than 3/2. For such an edge e = uv, its
probability at v is greater than 1/2, since its other endpoint contributes at most one to ae = q + r.
Two internal edges cannot meet at a vertex, where their probabilities would sum to more than one.
Hence internal edges form a matching. Every cycle has adjacent internal edges and is excluded
directly. A tree of diameter at least four also has adjacent internal edges: take the two middle
edges of a four-edge path. Every support component therefore has diameter at most three. This
proof of the whisker bound does not depend on the new odd-cycle theorem. Apply the published
stochastic input and Lemma 2 to ﬁnish Theorem 1(2).


5     Priority, attribution and precise limits
The main comparison is with the arbitrary-rate forest threshold in [3, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1],
the equal-rate whisker theorem [3, Theorem 2(a)], the uniform odd-cycle calculation in [2, Proposi-
tion 2(iv)], and the even-cycle proof in [4, Lemma 2]. We make no new claim for the stochastic con-
vergence or support-identiﬁcation theorems, the triangle obstruction, or the least-weight/champion
mechanism. A bounded current literature audit recovered no earlier theorem giving the arbitrary-
equilibrium weighted odd-cycle estimate or either substantive bound in Theorem 1. This is not a
certiﬁcate of exhaustive priority or worldwide ﬁrstness. Repository creation dates do not certify
historical public visibility.
    The earlier published argument already has a small numerical improvement of its stated 25.

results for loops or noninteger initial oﬀsets. The ﬁnite simple graph model and all quantiﬁers
appear explicitly above.


6    Reproducibility and research disclosure
The proof, including every rational margin and the unbounded tail, is contained in this paper and
does not depend on numerical searches. The supporting package preserves the eight original PR
artifacts, separate mathematical and priority audits, exact ﬁnite controls and instructions for re-
production in a disposable copy. Historical source wrappers label the work partial by requiring the
sharper suggested whisker bound; that label is explicitly superseded here for the literal improvement
requests. The added integer-count conditioning argument and prior-method credits are identiﬁed,
and original inputs remain unchanged. Finite rational checks and rejected deliberately false alter-
natives corroborate the derivations; hashes establish custody rather than proof. No exploratory
ﬁnite tree search is used as universal evidence. Third-party primary texts and private operational
captures are not redistributed. Standard background results are cited at their interfaces.
    AI tools were extensively involved in mathematical research, proof development, symbolic and
computational checks, adversarial auditing, literature investigation and manuscript preparation.
Independent internal AI reviews are not independent human peer review. This manuscript is a
preprint and has not undergone formal independent human peer review, journal acceptance or
proof-assistant certiﬁcation.
