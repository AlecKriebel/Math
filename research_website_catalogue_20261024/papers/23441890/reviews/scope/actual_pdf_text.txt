    A two-tail obstruction to ambient conjugation in Dlab groups
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           17 October 2026
                                               Version 1.0.0


                                                 Abstract
          Let G = Dh2i ([0, 1]) be the compactly supported, locally right 2Z -aﬃne Dlab group. We
      construct an automorphism preserving both Dlab orders and give a proof that it cannot be im-
      plemented after any embedding in either of two explicitly deﬁned groups: all locally right aﬃne
      increasing interval homeomorphisms, and all locally right aﬃne increasing real homeomorphisms
      that are aﬃne on an initial ray. The ambient slopes are unrestricted, and the embedding need
      not preserve an order. Perfectness separates the supports of commuting local groups; a two-tail
      construction handles both possible orientations of their least action components. This extends
      a one-tail mechanism used in prior work. The deﬁnitions and aﬃne-germ decomposition of the
      standard Dlab families place them in these ambient groups. Consequently the construction gives
      an aﬃrmative answer to the corrected Kourovka Problem 21.149, including arbitrary positive-real
      slope groups and the full and reﬂected extended-real families.


1     The precise claim and its context
For an interval X, let Homeo+ (X) denote its increasing homeomorphisms, with ordinary composition
of functions. For f in this group, supp(f ) = {x : f (x) 6= x} is its open support. An open support
component is called a support component. For intervals U, V , write U < V if every point of U precedes
every point of V .
Deﬁnition 1. A map is locally right aﬃne if, at every ﬁnite point a other than a right endpoint, some
ϵ > 0 and s > 0 satisfy
                           f (x) = f (a) + s(x − a)    (a ≤ x ≤ a + ϵ).
Let AI ≤ Homeo+ ([0, 1]) consist of all such maps, including this condition at a = 0. Let AR ≤
Homeo+ (R) consist of all such maps which, in addition, agree with x 7→ sx + t, for some s > 0, on an
initial ray. No restriction is placed on the positive slopes.

   These are groups: right aﬃne germs are preserved by composition and inversion, as are eventual
aﬃne germs at −∞. Neither deﬁnition requires ﬁnitely many aﬃne pieces on a compact interval;
accumulation of pieces from the left is permitted. Put

                    G = {f ∈ AI : all right slopes lie in 2Z ,   f = id near 0 and 1}.

This is Dlab’s compact-support group. For L = (a, b) with 0 < a < b < 1, put

                        G(L) = {f ∈ G : supp(f ) is compactly contained in L}.

Theorem 2 (Explicit ambient theorem). There is an automorphism α of G preserving both Dlab bi-
orders such that, for A = AI or A = AR , every injective homomorphism e : G −→ A and every u ∈ A
satisfy
                               e(α(f )) 6= u−1 e(f )u for some f ∈ G.
Consequently the same assertion holds for every subgroup of either explicit ambient group. No order
on A or order preservation by e is assumed.

   Gong, Yang and Zeng [3, Theorem 1.1] already give an ambient nonconjugation result for six
rank-one Dlab families and order-preserving embeddings. Their proof uses a normalizer with support
components descending to one endpoint. They explicitly exclude higher-rank ambient slopes and the
reﬂected extended-real family from their theorem. We credit that one-tail support obstruction. The
additional argument here uses perfect centralizers instead of ambient order classiﬁcation, and uses
both tails to accommodate an embedding that reverses the order of least local action components.
The conclusion concerns the stronger ambient quantiﬁers, rather than a new claim about the already
known one-tail mechanism.


2      Germs and perfect centralizers
The external group theorem needed for the support obstruction is Dlab’s simplicity theorem [1, Theo-
rem 4.3]. It states that G is algebraically simple. The group is nontrivial, for it contains ﬁnite two-slope
bumps. An increasing homeomorphism of an interval has no nontrivial ﬁnite order: a moved point has
strictly monotone iterates. Thus G cannot be an abelian simple group, and is nonabelian and perfect.
An aﬃne change from [0, 1] to [a, b], followed by extension by the identity, identiﬁes G with G(L). In
particular every G(L) is also nonabelian simple and perfect.
Lemma 3. For every q ∈ AI or q ∈ AR , its support components are well ordered from left to right. If
q belongs to a perfect subgroup of the ambient group, every support component has a ﬁnite left endpoint.
Proof. First consider a nonempty family of components with ﬁnite left endpoints whose inﬁmum a is
ﬁnite. If no component has left endpoint a, choose distinct such endpoints an ↓ a. They are ﬁxed
points of q, so continuity gives q(a) = a. A right aﬃne germ at a then has slope 1, because it contains
the ﬁxed points an for arbitrarily large n. It is therefore the identity on a right neighborhood of a. No
left endpoint of a nonempty open support component can lie in that neighborhood, a contradiction.
Thus the family has a ﬁrst component. On [0, 1] its inﬁmum is always ﬁnite and less than 1, so this
proves the assertion there.
    On R, if q is the identity on an initial ray, all support components have left endpoints bounded
below and the argument applies. Otherwise its eventual nonidentity aﬃne formula moves every point
suﬃciently far to the left. There is a single initial support component, with left endpoint −∞, and
all other component endpoints are bounded below. A family containing that component has it as ﬁrst
member; a family excluding it is handled by the ﬁnite-inﬁmum argument.
    For AI , the right slope at 0 is a homomorphism to the abelian group R×       >0 . It is trivial on any
perfect subgroup, and a map ﬁxing 0 with slope 1 is the identity on an initial interval. For AR , the
eventual aﬃne formula deﬁnes a homomorphism into Aﬀ + (R). This group is metabelian: its slope
quotient and translation kernel are abelian. A perfect subgroup has trivial image in a solvable group,
so every element of the perfect subgroup is the identity on an initial ray. These neighborhoods can
depend on the element; no common neighborhood is asserted.
Lemma 4 (Perfect centralizers). Let q be an ambient element all of whose support components have
ﬁnite left endpoints. If a perfect subgroup P of the ambient group centralizes q, then every element of
P ﬁxes supp(q) pointwise.

3     Least components and the orientation dichotomy
Fix an embedding e : G → A. Its action on every action component J is faithful. Indeed, the restriction
kernel is normal in G, and it is proper because J is an action component; simplicity makes the kernel
trivial. Fix f 6= 1 in G. Every such J contains a support component of e(f ), since faithfulness makes
its restriction nonidentity and a support component cannot cross a common ﬁxed endpoint of J. The
least support component of e(f ) therefore lies in the least action component of e(G); denote the latter
by J0 . To justify minimality explicitly, an earlier action component would contain an even earlier
support component of e(f ).
    For each source interval L, the action of e(G(L)) on J0 is nontrivial, since the action of e(G) there
is faithful. Every local action component within J0 carries a faithful action of G(L), by its simplicity.
Applying the preceding ﬁxed-nonidentity-element argument to G(L) gives a least such component,
denoted K(L). These are also global action components of e(G(L)): the boundary of J0 is ﬁxed by
e(G) and thus cannot be crossed by a local action component. Corollary 5 shows that K(L) and K(M )
are disjoint whenever L and M are disjoint.
    Conjugation by any g ∈ G maps G(L) onto G(gL). Moreover e(g) preserves J0 and sends least
components to least components. Consequently
                                    K(gL) = e(g)K(L)          (g ∈ G).                                  (1)
Lemma 6. Exactly one of the following holds for every pair of relatively compact source intervals
L < M with a positive gap:
                            K(L) < K(M );        K(M ) < K(L).
Proof. We ﬁrst verify ﬁnite-tuple transitivity. Given two increasing tuples of distinct interior points
of equal length, choose common outer endpoints c, d ∈ (0, 1) containing both tuples. Prescribe the
tuple map and ﬁx c, d. On any successive source segment of length ℓ and target segment of length rℓ,
choose s = 2k with s ≤ r ≤ 2s. Use slope s for length ℓ(2s − r)/s and slope 2s for length ℓ(r − s)/s.
These lengths are nonnegative, sum to ℓ, and give target length rℓ. Omit zero-length pieces. The
resulting ﬁnite piecewise aﬃne map, extended by the identity outside [c, d], belongs to G and realizes
the prescribed tuple map.
    Apply this construction to the four ordered endpoints of any two positively separated interval pairs.
It maps one pair to the other. Equation (1) and the fact that e(g) increases show that the relative
order of the two K-intervals is the same for every pair. The intervals are disjoint and nonempty, so
exactly one of the stated alternatives holds.

slope unchanged, by cancellation of the conjugator’s right slope. This gives a bi-order; the inverse cone
gives its reverse. Dlab’s order theorem [1, Theorem 4.1] identiﬁes these as the two Dlab orders for 2Z .

5    Proof of the ambient theorem
Proof of Theorem 2. The automorphism and its order preservation were established above. Suppose,
contrary to the remaining assertion, that

                                 e(α(f )) = u−1 e(f )u    for all f ∈ G.                              (5)

Then u normalizes e(G), permutes its action components increasingly, and ﬁxes their least member
J0 setwise. For L = Cm or Dm , equations (4) and (5) imply that u normalizes e(G(L)). It therefore
preserves the least local component K(L) within J0 setwise.
   The restriction of u to K(L) is nonidentity. Otherwise (5) would give equal restrictions of e(α(f ))
and e(f ) to K(L) for every f ∈ G(L). The action of G(L) there is faithful, so this would imply
α(f ) = f for all f ∈ G(L), contradicting (4). Choose a point of K(L) moved by u. Its support
component U (L) for u lies entirely inside K(L): ﬁnite boundary points of the invariant interval K(L)
are ﬁxed by u, and an inﬁnite boundary cannot be crossed.

6     Why the left-end condition cannot be dropped
The eventual aﬃne condition at −∞ is a mathematical hypothesis, not a harmless convention. Here
is an exact countermodel for the larger group B of all increasing real homeomorphisms that are locally
right 2Z -aﬃne at every ﬁnite point and have support bounded above. No condition at −∞ is imposed.

7     Scope, dependencies, and disclosure
The explicit theorem includes every interval Dlab subgroup from Dlab’s original deﬁnition, with any
positive-real slope subgroup and any group order. The source simplicity and order classiﬁcation used
here are respectively Theorems 4.3 and 4.1 of [1]; none of the arguments imposes rank one on an
ambient slope group. There are no computational claims or numerical approximations in the proof.
    For the extended-real families, the deﬁnitions and decomposition in Zenkov–Medvedev [5, §2, Rus-
sian pp. 538, 542–543] supply the additional left-end condition. Their full group DH consists of locally
right H-aﬃne maps; the subgroup DH∗ ﬁxes an initial ray. In the decomposition proof on p. 542,
every element of DH is a product of a globally aﬃne map x 7→ rx + t and an element of DH∗ . In
particular, if an element is not already in DH∗ , the proof identiﬁes its ﬁrst support point as −∞ and
gives an aﬃne formula on a right neighborhood of that point. Such a neighborhood is an initial real ray.
Each full-group element is therefore eventually aﬃne at −∞, and DH ≤ AR . The compact-support,
bounded-below, and reﬂected bounded-above families are subgroups of this full group, so they inherit
the same conclusion. This deduction concerns deﬁning aﬃne behavior; it does not apply a rank-one
classiﬁcation of ambient group orders. Proposition 7 explains why retaining that left-end condition is
essential.

     The contribution relative to [3] is the unrestricted ambient slope and embedding quantiﬁers, in-
cluding the full and reﬂected extended-real families. The aﬃne-germ centralizer mechanism is classical
(cf. [1, Lemma 3.6 and Theorem 4.6]); the proof above includes its required one-sided version rather
than treating it as a new result. A literature audit through 17 October 2026 found no prior result
covering these full quantiﬁers. This is a bounded priority assessment, and no independent human
peer-review or proof-assistant veriﬁcation is claimed.
     Related questions include classifying embeddings beyond their orientation dichotomy, and charac-
terizing ambient left-germ groups for which the perfect-centralizer argument survives.
