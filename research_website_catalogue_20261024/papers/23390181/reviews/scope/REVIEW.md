## Exact model and contribution boundary

The theorem fixes at least three nontrivial finitely generated factors and a nonempty finite inverse-closed generating subset of nonidentity elements in each. The cyclic relator universe consists of rooted exact-length syllable words with all adjacent factors different, including the last–first boundary. It is not a quotient by rotations or inversions. At fixed 1/3 < d < 1, sample floor(|Sℓ|^d) independent uniform relators with replacement and impose their normal closure. The probability of property (T) tends to one through all integer lengths.

The main contribution is a model-specific completion of an existing program: exact weighted endpoint counts, negligible inverse-fixed-block deletion compatible with torsion, a type-matrix limit for unequal block lengths, independent whole-relator concentration and a suffix-based transfer to an index-at-most-two subgroup. The argument handles arbitrary fixed finite alphabets and hence fixed-radius word balls. It neither assumes the factors finite nor requires their finite presentation. The linear-normal-word version in §6 is a separately justified law, obtained by retaining enough cyclic words at a smaller density and using quotient permanence.

The primary sources support direct credit for the proposed three-part strategy, prior treatment of all length residues, the common-suffix mechanism and the spectral/concentration tools. The substantive density addition includes (1/3,1/2]; collapse above one-half was already known. One-third is not claimed sharp. The theorem does not settle the two-factor problem, d = 1/3, growing parameters, finite-length guarantees, arbitrary sampling laws, infinitude or factor embeddings. Odd-length cyclic universes for two factors can be empty. The all-integer-length statement must not be generalized from this fixed at-least-three-factor setting.
