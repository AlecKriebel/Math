Property (T) above one-third density
in random free-product quotients
Alec Kriebel ∗
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

Abstract
Fix at least three nontrivial ﬁnitely generated groups and a ﬁnite inverse-closed generating
alphabet of nonidentity elements in each factor. At every ﬁxed density 1/ 3 < d < 1, their
random free-product quotient, formed from independent uniform cyclically reduced syllable
relators of exact length with replacement, has Kazhdan’s property (T) with probability tending
to one through all integer lengths. This answers the property-(T) question in Problem 16 of
the 2026 Oberwolfach report on median geometry. The proof completes model-speciﬁc steps of
an earlier proposed syllable-block spectral program: a weighted endpoint calculation handles
unequal alphabets, torsion, and all three length residues. It uses the established triangular-link
criterion and matrix concentration. The conclusion includes ﬁxed-radius word-ball alphabets
and makes no uniform assertion for growing parameters.

For ball alphabets this is the at-least-three-factor property-(T) question of [ 1, Problem 16,
pp. 535–536], with the precise sampling convention of [ 2, Deﬁnition 1.1]. Theorem 1 allows torsion

mity as the factors, alphabets, or density vary. The endpoint d = 1/ 3 and two factors are excluded.

3 Triangularization and ﬁnite-index transfer
Write ℓ = 3L + e, e ∈ {0, 1, 2}, and cut each sampled word at ﬁxed positions into lengths

as its formal inverse. Normal form distinguishes diﬀerent lengths, and deletion ensures that every
pair has two members.
Keep a sample only when all three blocks belong to their corresponding Vt, and impose its
formal triangular relation uvw = 1. Discarded samples impose no relation. Denote this triangular
group by Γ ℓ. Each retained relation is cyclically reduced formally: adjacent inverse blocks would
have meeting syllables in the same factor, contradicting the original cyclic reduction. Its link has
the three undirected edges
{u− 1, v }, {v− 1, w }, {w− 1, u }. (6)
There are no loops. Parallel edges and repeated relator cells are counted with multiplicity; this
does not change the presented group. Global inversion of signed vertices identiﬁes this convention
with [ 8, Deﬁnition 2.12].
Mapping formal generators to their blocks gives an epimorphism from Γ ℓ onto Hℓ = ⟨Vt : t ∈
I⟩ ≤ Gℓ. We next verify the ﬁnite-index claim without probabilistic assumptions.
Lemma 1. For L ≥ 4, the subgroup H0 = ⟨VL⟩ ≤ F has index at most two. Consequently
[Gℓ : Hℓ] ≤ 2.
Proof. For a, b ∈ B, choose a factor C diﬀerent from both their factors. We seek a common suﬃx v
of length L − 1 with av, b − 1v ∈ VL. If L is even, any reduced suﬃx starting in C works, since even-
length words are never inverse-ﬁxed. If L is odd, then L ≥ 5. Choose an even-length suﬃx starting
and ending in C: length four is realized by factor sequence C, D, E, C for three distinct factors,
and appending pairs D, C gives every larger even length. Each resulting block has diﬀerent ﬁrst
and last factors, so cannot be inverse-ﬁxed. Nonemptiness of each alphabet supplies the syllables.
