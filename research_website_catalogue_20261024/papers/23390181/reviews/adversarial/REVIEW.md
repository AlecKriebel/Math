## Exact strongest verified claim

For fixed n≥3 nontrivial finitely generated factors, fixed finite nonempty inverse-closed generating sets of nonidentity **group elements** in each factor, and fixed 1/3<d<1, sample floor(|S_l|^d) independent uniform rooted cyclically reduced syllable words of exact length l, **with replacement**. Their normal-closure quotient has property (T) with probability tending to one through every integer length. Torsion, unequal alphabet sizes, infinite factors, and factors without finite presentations are allowed. The linear-normal-word variant in §6 follows by the stated thinning/quotient argument.

No claim is verified at d=1/3, for two factors, for varying/growing factors or alphabets or densities, for finite-length guarantees, or for a different conjugacy-class/rotation/orbit-weighted relator law. The proof addresses the property-(T) question in OWR Problem 16; it is not a separate verification of every conjecture in that problem or of all parameters in the broader Question 1.9. ## Proof attacks and their resolution

1. **Law and counting (pp. 1–2).** Distinct reduced syllable words over actual group elements are distinct by free-product normal form. The alphabet adjacency matrix is complete multipartite. Connectivity plus a triangle makes it primitive, and its quadratic form is nonpositive on the codimension-one zero-sum subspace. Hence its sole positive eigenvalue is Perron λ≥2, with every other eigenvalue in (−λ,0]. This gives both trace(A^l)∼λ^l and the endpoint estimates for fixed data. Inversion preserves the factor of each letter. Even reduced words cannot be inverse-fixed; odd inverse-fixed words are conjugates of a middle involution. The O(λ^((t−1)/2)) deletion count is negligible relative to every endpoint class, because the finitely many Perron coordinates are positive. No independence assumption about endpoints is used.

2. **Transfer, including noncommuting factors (p. 3).** Formal generators are pairs of non-inverse-fixed blocks; unequal lengths cannot coincide in the free product. A formal adjacent cancellation would force equal factor labels at a forbidden syllable boundary. Each retained triangle therefore gives a valid loop-free link cell, with parallel edges and repetitions retained. The block map is a surjection onto a subgroup of the random quotient. For each pair a,b the specified common suffix gives (av)(b⁻¹v)⁻¹=ab for L≥4, including odd L with involutions. The group generated by all two-letter products is normal since c(ab)c⁻¹=(ca)(bc⁻¹), and the quotient identifies every letter with one involution. Thus the subgroup index is at most two. This uses no commutativity, factor embedding, finite presentation of a factor, or model-preserving replacement by free groups. Fresh S3*C2*C3 controls specifically exercise noncommutativity.

3. **Exact mean and unequal residues (pp. 4–5).** Count each oriented corner at its actual cut position. The completion factor is A_ac T_h(b,f), where the inverse changes endpoint letters but not their factor labels. Counting both orientations gives the published C matrices; in particular their total mass is six, not three. T_h symmetry follows from inverse-word bijection and factor invariance. The count includes rejected samples as zero contributions and never conditions on how many survive. This avoids a hidden independence or rotation-law assumption. Summing over destination classes supplies the necessary v_u(c,f) multiplicity and gives the published degree exponent. Fixed-size reversible compression is exact; the remaining full-walk eigenvalues are zero. The unequal-length type eigenvalue −1/2 multiplies negative alphabet eigenvalues, creating positive nonprincipal eigenvalues bounded by ρ/2<1/2. These products cannot simply be omitted. Simplicity and separation of the limit's eigenvalue 1 justify eventual connectivity and a common gap bound for all three residue subsequences.

4. **Concentration and centering (pp. 5–6).** Independence is needed only between sampled relators. Each relator contributes a PSD Laplacian of norm at most six; its three edges may be dependent or repeated. Every normalized summand kills the same deterministic vector Dbar^(1/2)1, so restriction to its complement preserves independence and self-adjointness. With R=6/δ_l and mean minimum μ≥γ, Tropp gives the claimed exp(−ε²γδ_l/12) bound: replacing μ by the smaller threshold γ weakens the event bound legitimately. A signed vertex has degree contribution at most three, since one block occurrence contributes separately to a block and its distinct inverse. Scalar upper-tail concentration with bound three gives denominator nine. The union factors grow exponentially in l while δ_l grows exponentially with strictly positive exponent d−1/3. No independence between vertex degrees is claimed.

5. **Actual normalized gap (p. 5).** The lower quadratic-form bound on the Dbar-centered codimension-one space first forces a one-dimensional kernel, hence connectivity and positive actual degrees. The generalized Courant–Fischer principle then permits that same subspace even though it is not D-centered. This yields λ₁≥(1−ε)γ/(1+ε)>1/2. Fresh irregular weighted-graph controls verified this comparison and explicitly detected the incorrect replacement of Dbar-centering by D-centering. Applying the triangular link criterion, taking the quotient, and passing across the finite-index extension completes the argument.

6. **Linear-normal variant (p. 6).** The cyclic subset has an asymptotically positive proportion λ/(Σr_a)² of the linear universe. An independent uniform draw can be represented by an independent membership mark and a uniform conditional identity. With d′ strictly between 1/3 and d, there are sufficiently many cyclic draws with high probability; their first prescribed number have the required iid uniform law. The full presentation is a further quotient. This is a thinning argument, not an assertion that the two original sampling laws coincide.
