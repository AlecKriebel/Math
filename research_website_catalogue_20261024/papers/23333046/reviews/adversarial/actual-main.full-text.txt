A characteristic-two counterexample to dévissage
for local deformation functors
Alec Kriebel ∗
12 October 2026 (research note)
Abstract
We give a negative answer to the unrestricted ﬁbre-product question for local deformation
functors posed by Byszewski. For the weakly ramiﬁed action of the additive group of F4 given
by t ↦→ t/(1 + at), and its subgroup F2, restriction–induction cannot be formally smooth over
any prorepresentable recipient, regardless of its tangent dimension. The proof combines the
known involution deformation pathology with an exact calculation showing that restriction has
injective tangent map. The variant assuming prorepresentability of the subgroup and quotient
deformation functors is untouched. The involution pathology and additive action are established
prior ingredients; the contribution is their use to obstruct the proposed factorization.

4 Failure of formal smoothness and scope
Proof of Theorem 1. Suppose such F , f and g exist; identify F naturally with hR. At A = k[ε]/ε2,
naturality from k sends the residual basepoint 0N ∈ DQ
N (A) under f to the residue basepoint
of F (A). Lemma 2 gives the same image for gA(v), with v ̸= 0 . Therefore (0N , v) belongs to
(DQ
N ×F DQ)(A). Its reduction is the unique residual point. The surjection A → k is small:
its kernel is annihilated by the maximal ideal of A. Formal smoothness would lift this pair to
w ∈ DG(A) with
res(w) = 0 N , ind(w) = v.
Restriction injectivity forces w = 0 G. Naturality of induction then gives ind(w) = 0 Q, contradicting
v ̸= 0.
The cubic ring is used only to constrain detection of a ﬁrst-order tangent by an arbitrary
prorepresentable recipient. The obstruction to smoothness already occurs on A → k. No extension
of f from DQ
N to DN is assumed, and no cohomological tangent formula for f or g is prescribed.
Nor is universality of a Klein-four deformation ring used.
This answers the unrestricted original question negatively and does not contradict the condi-
tional obstruction-space theorems cited above. The involution component is in the exceptional
nonprorepresentable case. The variant imposing prorepresentability of DN and DQ remains unad-
dressed; changing to coordinate automorphisms ﬁxing the zero section would likewise change the
category and exclude the crucial nilpotent translation. The introductory and reﬁned formulations
are one question, not separate discoveries.
