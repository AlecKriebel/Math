                                                 Abstract
          We give applications of published ﬁnite-type classiﬁcation and classical genus-two symmetry
      to the locus of string links with free exterior group. The category is ordinary unframed smooth
      ordered string links with ﬁxed boundary, upward orientation, and rational invariants. Freeness
      means that the actual three-dimensional exterior group is abstractly Fk . Recalling a ﬁltered
      Habegger–Masbaum corollary, the intersection of the entire Milnor algebra with the two-strand
      order-at-most-two space is spanQ {1, ℓ, ℓ2 }. Meilhan’s classiﬁcation and known free tangles give
      a component Conway coeﬃcient outside the Milnor-plus-vanishing span at (k, n) = (2, 2), and
      give zero vanishing kernel for every k ≥ 2 and 0 ≤ n ≤ 2. An exact marked genus-two
      reversal argument, combined with the prior Duzhin–Karev ordinary orientation detector, gives
      a nonzero kernel invariant of exact order seven on two strands. The seven-wheel is credited
      to Bar-Natan following Duzhin–Karev’s journal proof. Historical priority of these combined
      free-exterior applications is undetermined; no ﬁrstness is claimed.

1    Category, question, and scope
Let C be the corner-rounded oriented cylinder D2 × [0, 1], with distinct marked points p1 , . . . , pk
in the interior of D2 . An element of SLk is an ordered smooth proper embedding of k intervals,
with strand i directed from (pi , 0) to (pi , 1), standard in product endpoint collars. Equivalence is
smooth ambient isotopy ﬁxing the boundary; isotopies can be taken ﬁxed on a smaller boundary
collar. Upward speciﬁes orientation, and imposes no height-monotonicity condition. No strand
framing is part of an object.

    The ﬁrst formula is recalled from the ﬁltered concordance theorem of Habegger–Masbaum [2,
Corollary 6.4 and Theorem 15.1], after the ordinary specialization explained below. The low-degree
applications use Meilhan’s complete classiﬁcation [3], Nogueira’s free-tangle construction [8], and
the Kauﬀman–Lambropoulou rational example [11, 12]. The order-seven application uses classical
genus-two hyperelliptic machinery [17, 18, 19], Hatcher’s relative-ball theorem [21], and the Duzhin–
Karev detector [15]. This is an assembly and exposition of those inputs, with the marking and collar
obligations made explicit.
    This work does not determine Nn (2) for 3 ≤ n ≤ 6, the least positive kernel order, higher-strand
higher-order kernels, or higher-order spanning and general classiﬁcation. Failure of spanning at
order two does not by itself imply failure at larger order bounds. No claim about the current
worldwide open status of those residual questions is made.

Proof. Every based Milnor invariant, including repeated indices, is invariant under smooth ﬁxed-
endpoint concordance [4, §2.1.4 and §5.1], where the attribution is to Casson [5]. Therefore every
ﬁnite polynomial in arbitrary-length Milnor invariants is concordance invariant. This property
concerns the resulting function and remains valid if high-degree terms in a presentation cancel.
    Pull an ordinary concordance invariant back to framed string links by forgetting the framing.
Habegger–Masbaum’s tree quotient, Corollary 6.4, and ﬁltered Theorem 15.1 [2, §4 and §15] control
its order-at-most-two symbols. In degree two the only connected tree has three legs. With only two
colors, two legs share a color; exchanging these changes the vertex orientation, so antisymmetry
kills that diagram over Q. Framing independence kills the two self-struts. Through degree two the
surviving ordinary tree diagrams are therefore the empty diagram, the mixed strut, and its square.
They are integrated by 1, ℓ, ℓ2 ; the ﬁltered theorem gives the upper bound of three dimensions. Pure
two-braids attain every integer value of ℓ, proving independence. These three functions belong to
the Milnor algebra and have orders at most 0, 1, 2, respectively. The upper and lower inclusions
follow.

3    Known free two-tangles and their ordinary markings
Lemma 3. There are R, L, Ls ∈ F2 with

            (a1 , a2 , w)(R) = (0, 0, 1),    (a1 , a2 )(L) = (1, 5),        (a1 , a2 )(Ls ) = (5, 1).   (8)

No linking number or value of w(L), w(Ls ) is needed.

Proof. Nogueira’s institutional preprint [8, Appendix §4, pp. 14–15, Figures 9–10] gives a free
two-arc ball tangle with individual capped knots trefoil and T (3, −4). Its explicit trefoil tunnel
and tunnel-end slide establish the actual handlebody exterior. This is the appendix construction,
not a consequence of the paper’s main essential-surface theorem. The related tunnel-slide method
appears already in [10, §8]. We use the full institutional drawings: the later arXiv edition [9] has
missing relevant artwork, and is not the controlling visual source.
     Round the ﬁnite tame PL corners in disjoint small neighborhoods and straighten endpoint
collars. This preserves complement topology and individual knot types. Choose one endpoint
of each arc as bottom, and use an orientation-preserving sphere isotopy moving the four labeled
endpoints to their prescribed destinations. Extend through a ball collar and make the strand
germs standard. Orient each strand bottom to top. This produces L in the speciﬁed smooth
ordered category, preserving its actual handlebody exterior. The exterior boundary is the sphere
with four disks removed and two annuli attached, a closed genus-two surface; its group is therefore
F2 .
     To compare individual caps, ﬁrst discard the other strand. Any two simple arcs joining the
surviving two endpoints on the unpunctured sphere are isotopic relative to those endpoints, by
bigon removal. Extend across the outside closing collar. Thus the individual knot types survive the
chosen marking. This is not a statement about arbitrary cap systems on a four-punctured sphere.
