## Exact claim and success criteria

For fixed finite N≥1, the domain is the unit sphere with its round geodesic metric and n≥2. The target is U(N) with either the unnormalized Hilbert–Schmidt length metric or operator-norm length metric. On the whole space of maps of Lipschitz constant L≤1/2, with uniform topology, the same explicit formula must continuously deform each map to its value at a chosen p, fix every constant map at every time, stay within that Lipschitz sublevel, and satisfy the stronger bound q(t)L, q(t)=(1−t²)/(1+t²). The strict sublevel must be preserved too. There is no stabilization or contraction of the copy of U(N) to a preselected group element. The separate S⁰ assertion uses angular separation π, not an intrinsic length metric on a disconnected space.

## Distinct checks and reconstruction

1. **Normalization and spectral localization.** The domain's diameter is π. Left multiplication by A*=Φ(p)* preserves either intrinsic target metric, giving d(I,A*Φ(x))≤Lπ≤π/2. For an eigenvector of W with eigenangle θ, any unitary connecting curve acts on that vector to give a real-sphere curve from v to e^{iθ}v. Its speed is at most the operator norm of the matrix derivative and hence at most its HS norm. Its length is at least |θ|. Infimizing yields all principal angles in [−π/2,π/2] and Re W≥0. No differentiability of Φ or a choice of eigenvectors varying with x is used.

2. **Endpoint singularities and inverse bound.** For R=I+tW, R*R=(1+t²)I+2t Re W≥(1+t²)I. Thus all eligible inputs have a uniformly invertible denominator, including t=1 and eigenvalues ±i on the boundary. The excluded eigenvalue −1 would be singular only at t=1; the hypotheses actually exclude it. A claim merely on the whole unitary group would fail here.

3. **Unitarity, order and fixed constants.** With B=W+tI, B*B=R*R, so (BR⁻¹)*(BR⁻¹)=I. F₀(W)=W and F₁(W)=I. For a constant original map, W=I, and F_t(I)=I. Multiplying back by A proves exactly the asserted fixed-constant and terminal-map properties. No homotopy to one globally fixed element of U(N) follows or is needed.

4. **Noncommuting difference identity.** Multiplying F_t(U)−F_t(V) on the left by I+tU and on the right by I+tV leaves (U+tI)(I+tV)−(I+tU)(V+tI). Expand in this fixed order: U+tUV+tI+t²V minus V+tUV+tI+t²U equals (1−t²)(U−V). The UV terms cancel without swapping U and V. This validates the two-sided-resolvent identity in a noncommutative algebra, not only after diagonalization.

5. **Differential and metric issue.** Differentiating BR⁻¹ in an arbitrary ambient matrix direction E gives E R⁻¹−tF_t(W)E R⁻¹=(I−tF_t(W))E R⁻¹. Since I−tF_t(W)=(1−t²)R⁻¹, the displayed derivative is correct in its exact order. Each stated norm obeys the two-sided ideal inequality. The inverse bound gives ||DF_t(W)[E]||ν≤q(t)||E||ν. A pairwise chord bound by itself would not prove an intrinsic Lipschitz estimate; this paper explicitly uses lengths of curves instead.

6. **Curves stay in the controlled set.** For any minimizing domain geodesic γ from x to y, W_Φ∘γ remains in Re W≥0 because the pointwise estimate relative to p applies at every point of γ. It is Lipschitz into the target length metric and hence absolutely continuous as a finite-dimensional matrix curve. Its metric length is at most L length(γ); the integral matrix speed equals this induced Finsler/Riemannian length. The ordinary almost-everywhere chain rule and the derivative estimate then prove length(F_t∘W_Φ∘γ)≤q(t)L length(γ). This avoids assuming the semicircle subset is intrinsically geodesically convex or that a target minimizing geodesic stays inside it.

7. **Nonsmooth input and boundary L=1/2.** The argument only needs Lipschitz curves and the finite-dimensional chain rule. Eigenvalues at ±i leave the denominator bound intact. At t=0, q=1 and the original map is recovered; at t=1, q=0 and the image is constant. For all times 0≤q≤1, so both closed and strict Lipschitz sublevels are preserved. The endpoint continuity is quantitative rather than a limiting choice of logarithm branches.

8. **Input-map continuity and time.** If δ is the uniform ambient norm distance between Φ and Ψ, evaluating at p gives ||A−B||≤δ. Adding and subtracting A*Ψ gives ||A*Φ−B*Ψ||≤2δ, using unitary invariance. The chord estimate and unitarity give uniform output distance ≤δ+2qδ≤3δ. The finite-dimensional compact set [0,1]×S has a continuous rational formula with its uniform inverse bound, so time continuity is uniform as well. On compact U(N) the ambient and intrinsic metrics induce uniformly equivalent topologies for each fixed N, which is sufficient. No dimension-uniform equivalence constant has been claimed.

9. **Based maps and arbitrary families.** At p the normalized input is I, so H_t(Φ)(p)=Φ(p). Thus the based subspace ends at I and remains based. For any continuous family on Y×S^{n−1}, evaluation at the fixed p and the same rational operations give a jointly continuous Y×[0,1]×S^{n−1} formula directly. No regularity or compactness assumption on Y is needed for this pointwise formula. This says nothing about choosing a global p in nontrivial sphere bundles.

10. **Frame equivariance.** For QΦR, the new base value is QAR and new normalized map is R*W_ΦR. The rational formula respects conjugation. Multiplying gives Q A F_t(W_Φ)R, exactly the asserted left/right frame equivariance. This uses only constant Q,R, not x-dependent gauge changes.
