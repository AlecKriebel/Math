## Exit kernel attack and endpoint normalization

For the killed Brownian heat kernel H with generator one half, the endpoint density at the left endpoint is **+one half** the inward spatial derivative H_beta(t;alpha,0), and at the right endpoint it is **-one half** H_beta(t;alpha,ell). Applying those derivatives to `(2/ell) sum sin(n pi alpha/ell) sin(n pi beta/ell) exp(-n^2 pi^2 t/(2ell^2))` gives exactly the paper's factors `pi/ell^2`, the right-end alternating sign, and the exponent with 2 in its denominator.

This can be justified without a signed improper Fourier interchange. The left-exit tail is the killed transition integral of `1-beta/ell`; its time derivative, using the heat equation and two integrations by parts, gives the positive exit flux. The corresponding right weight is beta/ell. For every epsilon>0, the differentiated sine series converges uniformly on compact time intervals t>=epsilon. Almost sure finite exit follows by stopping B_t^2-t at tau wedge t: the stopped position is bounded, so sup_t E(tau wedge t)<infinity. The stopped coordinate is a bounded martingale and supplies masses `1-alpha/ell` and `alpha/ell`. Thus the flux is a nonnegative integrable density even though its Fourier terms have signs. The paper explicitly sums the series before improper integration near zero; it does not make the invalid absolute-interchange claim there.

At a singleton target on the circle, the lifted complement is an interval of length one with **both** endpoints identified with the same target. Its density must be f0+f1; dropping either endpoint loses probability. The article includes both indicators in its kernel. As alpha tends to 0 or ell, an exit-time atom develops only at the excluded exact endpoint; such a limiting base is not falsely covered by a positive-time density assumption.

An independent discounted gambler's-ruin check solves the exact finite-difference boundary equation with mesh 1/n and jump generator one half. For alpha=1/4, s=3/2, n=4,8,16,32 it approaches `sinh((1-alpha)sqrt(2s))/sinh(sqrt(2s))`, with errors approximately 0.001592, 0.000405, 0.000102, 0.0000254. The formula with sqrt(s), corresponding to a missing factor two in the Brownian generator, is separated by about 0.0578. This detects the natural generator convention error; it is a diagnostic and not a discrete-to-Brownian proof.

## Ordering, correlations, and the clock falsification control

For a single walker, removal of the target just reached makes its current location lie outside the *remaining* set. Its next first-unvisited target is the first exit endpoint of the remaining component. Old targets and old territory must not remain absorbing. Repeating finite stopping times and applying strong Markov yields a density for each possible permutation and the inter-arrival increments, with no missing conditional bridge: the new spatial start is the deterministic target just reached. Distinct targets cannot be reached simultaneously by a continuous path. All targets are reached after finitely many such steps almost surely.

The product does **not** make the first-hitting times independent within a walker: query j's physical clock is the cumulative sum through its position in that walker's order. Distinct walkers are independent, so their full ordered-density products can be multiplied. The strict owner inequalities must compare those cumulative clocks.

Exact negative control: two walkers visit two queries in orders `(1,2)` and `(2,1)`. Give their elapsed increments `(2,2)` and `(3,1/2)`. Their physical hitting vectors are `(2,4)` and `(7/2,3)`. Correct ownership is `(walker1,walker2)`. Comparing increments query by query instead reverses **both** owners. This discrepancy persists on an open neighborhood of the increments, so it is not a tie-boundary artifact. The published formulas (6)–(7) use the correct cumulative clocks and pass this attack.

My exact independent global-clock continuous-time finite-cycle model also computes complete query-owner laws by solving rational first-step equations, without using the ordered product construction. On the four-cycle with starts 0,2 and queries 1,3, its four owner probabilities are `3/16,5/16,5/16,3/16`. On five- and six-cycles, the three-query laws are nontrivial rational distributions. Each law is nonnegative and normalized, and every one-query marginal agrees with a separate one-query first-step solution. The two-query result in particular rejects the tempting independent-query factorization, which would give 1/4 for all four probabilities. Separate scalar gambler's-ruin order sums normalize to one; of 24 orders for four queries only eight have positive mass, confirming the need to allow impossible orders with zero factors. Singleton endpoint masses sum to one.

These finite-cycle examples do not numerically validate the Brownian coefficient integrals. They independently test clock, correlation, normalization, impossible-order and target-removal mechanisms that the Brownian proof must handle.

## Measurability, ties, and spatial integration

On continuous-path space, `(path,x) -> dist(x, path[0,t])` is jointly measurable, since the image is compact and distances may be reduced to countably many rational times plus t. Its zero set is exactly `{T(x)<=t}`. These sets for rational t imply joint measurability of extended hitting times. Ownership with least-label tie breaking is consequently measurable. Adding a deterministic seed by torus translation also makes seed dependence measurable, as required for the independent-uniform average.

For each fixed nonseed x, each walker's hitting time has the atomless singleton density and is finite almost surely. Independence removes pairwise ties at that fixed x. Applying Fubini to the jointly measurable bad-location set yields a null spatial set almost surely. It does **not** prove no ties anywhere simultaneously; boundary ties are permitted and the paper explicitly distinguishes this. The finitely many seeds and spatial query coincidences have zero Lebesgue measure. Arbitrary bounded definitions on those null sets do not change the moment integrals. This exclusion does not license discarding them in a finite lattice model, where the controls instead handle them literally.

## Coefficients, transform, and domain boundaries

Given the ordered densities, the finite union over permutations is the complete joint query-hitting law. Each order has an absolutely continuous km-dimensional increment density; clock equalities between different walkers are proper linear hyperplanes. Their union is null. The ownership regions partition increment space up to those equalities, so `sum_a Q_a=1`, Q_a>=0, and each coefficient is bounded even when queries or seeds become very close. No uniform pointwise bound on a nearly singular exit density is needed.

For theta_i>=0, put `Z=int theta_{I(x)} dx`. Because the spatial measure is one, `0<=Z<=theta_*`. Nonnegative product integration gives `E Z^m=M_m` and the sharper bound `M_m<=theta_*^m` rather than `(sum_i theta_i)^m`. The sum over all owners introduces no extra k^m bound once the probabilities sum to one. The exponential series is absolutely expectation-dominated by exp(theta_*). Its degree-N Taylor remainder has bound `Z^(N+1)/(N+1)!`, since the derivatives of exp(-z) have modulus at most one for nonnegative z. Thus the exact displayed remainder is valid at every N, including N=0, and uniformly over distinct seeds, even arbitrarily near coincident ones.

Independent finite-cell calculations check moments m=0,...,6 against the discrete query expansion, including seeds and repeated queries; the theta_* bound and equal-variable normalization hold. The finite-cell transform also obeys the same outer remainder at N=0,...,6. All theta_i=t forces Z=t deterministically, so the Brownian transform must be exp(-t); k=1 forces exp(-theta_1). theta=0 gives one. These checks do not rely on cell connectedness or a particular named distribution.

The theorem's remainder is only asserted for nonnegative real theta. An explicit anti-control is Z=-2 at N=0: `|exp(2)-1|>2`; the same expression cannot be extended to negative theta with an absolute-value replacement. Bounded support does imply an entire transform with an appropriate exponential factor in general complex remainder bounds, but that is an independent extension, not a required claim or missing proof here.

All homogeneous moment polynomials determine their mixed coefficients, or equivalently the entire transform gives mixed derivatives at zero. On the compact simplex, polynomial density then determines the Borel measure uniquely. This is an actual deterministic coefficient representation: no Brownian hitting probability or unknown conditional law remains in the integrands. Inner spectral summation is an explicit operation performed at positive time; coefficient integration is an ordinary nonnegative improper integral, not an unprovided integral-equation solution. The construction may be expensive, and the outer factorial error alone gives no certified error for kernel truncation or numerical quadrature, as the paper says.

The seed average excludes collisions of independent uniform seeds, a set of product Lebesgue measure zero. The bound is uniform in distinct seeds, so interchanging the series and seed average is justified. Equally spaced seeds need cyclic and dihedral symmetry, not arbitrary permutation symmetry; the paper makes that distinction. The statement about common diffusivity and circumference follows from Brownian spatial and time scaling of all walkers together, not from changing just one walker's clock.
