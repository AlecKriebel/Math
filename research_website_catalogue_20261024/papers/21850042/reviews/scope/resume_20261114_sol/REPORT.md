## Checkable auxiliary gap

The support theorem permits T_s to grow and states max_t lambda_t=o(s) as its incidence no-concentration assumption. Let

    s=k^4, Q_s=k^2, H=1,
    # heavy blades=k, lambda_h=k^3,
    # light blades=s−k, lambda_l=1/(1−k^−3).

Then B=4 exactly, Q_s/s=k^−2→0, max lambda_t/s=k^−1→0, and max portal edge1/(Q_s−1)→0. All portal incidences are exchangeable and positive. Yet each heavy endpoint has outer degree k, and heavy blades get exactly1/2 of the child-type mass f. Thus the type of a newly produced child cannot be disregarded as a vanishing-population exception.

With one mutant portal and one newly mutant heavy endpoint, exact dB pair-conversion and pair-erasure rates while portal types are frozen are

    u_k=r(1+1/k)/[r(1+1/k)+k−1/k]→0,
    d_k=(1+k−1/k)/[1+k−1/k+r/k]→1.

The isolated-pair success mark1/2 is not approached, and an outer replacement at the mutant endpoint has order-one probability. The claimed O(Q_s/s) external-intervention estimate therefore does not follow from the stated hypotheses. DIFFUSE_INTERFACE_FINDING.md contains the exact affected inference; DIFFUSE_INTERFACE_WITNESS.json retains rational rates and verify_scope.py recomputes them. This is a missing analytic interface, not a numerical nonconvergence conjecture or a graph-level counterexample. Its final no-overlap polynomial may be correct for the ideal trace while the asserted trace need not describe the admitted growing-type graphs.

The manuscript's item3 says “singular load scaling” is outside scope without defining this boundary. Here its stated B,H loads are fixed, but an incidence class concentrates. This is why a whole-original-claim verdict should preserve the issue explicitly. No new theorem with additional hypotheses is being offered as a replacement.
