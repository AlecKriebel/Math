## Exact conclusion and proof reconstruction

The theorem concerns the oriented real mass ball
\(C_{4,12}=\operatorname{conv}\{p\in\Lambda^4\mathbb R^{12}:p\text{ simple},\|p\|=1\}\), with **both orientations**. It excludes every finite projected semidefinite lift. Consequently some supporting affine function is not a sum of squares of affine functions modulo the full real vanishing ideal. The unnormalized linear functional can have rational coefficients. After division by its strictly positive comass, it gives a constant closed real four-form with no finite pointwise norm completion, hence no local completion.

1. **Kähler face.** For the orthogonal complex structure on the real space underlying \(\mathbb C^6\), \(\Phi=\omega^2/2\) has maximum1 on the unit simple real four-vectors. Restricting the orthogonal complex structure to a four-plane gives a skew contraction; its Pfaffian is the signed product of the two skew singular values. Equality+1 forces preservation of all norms, so the plane is complex and has its positive complex orientation. Convex averaging at a support maximum forces each positive-weight generator into that face.
2. **Real-linear, injective projector map.** The map \(A(H)=\sum H_{IJ}u_I\wedge\overline{u_J}\) is real on Hermitian matrices, because the factors have even degree and conjugation transposes the Hermitian coefficients. The 225 complex type-(2,2) basis vectors are linearly independent. Thus restriction to the 225-dimensional real Hermitian space is injective. Using unnormalized \(w_j=e_j-if_j\) requires precisely the factor1/4. The sign in \(A((a\wedge b)(a\wedge b)^*)=a\wedge Ja\wedge b\wedge Jb\) is positive: the two factors−i and the one interchange each contribute−1. The complex phase of a decomposable unit bivector can be absorbed in one orthonormal factor. Therefore the entire exposed face, not just selected extreme points, equals \(A(K)\).
3. **Exact normalized complex separable section.** With \(\mathbb C^6=U\oplus Z\), each summand having complex dimension3, the functional \(Q\) evaluates as \(\operatorname{tr}(P_DH)\), where \(D=\Lambda^2U\oplus\Lambda^2Z\). On \(K\), vanishing is a positive sum of squared norms. It forces **every generating bivector** into \(U\wedge Z\). A nonzero simple bivector in that mixed subspace has one-dimensional projection spans on each side and equals \(x\wedge y\). Its unit norm is exactly the product of the two factor norms. Consequently the section is the normalized **complex** \(\operatorname{Sep}(3,3)\), not real separability, all density matrices, or a cone lacking trace normalization.
4. **Correct nonrepresentation direction.** If the mass ball had a finite lift, impose both affine equations on its lift variables. Apply a real-linear extension of the left inverse ofA, followed by the mixed-block identification. This would lift \(\operatorname{Sep}(3,3)\), contradicting the published Fawzi theorem. This uses a section and a linear inverse; no invalid inference from a nonlinear parametrization or from nonrepresentability of an arbitrary subset is made. No dual-cone closure theorem or exposedness assertion outside the Kähler face is needed.
5. **Supporting SOS implication.** In ambient dimension 495, the full degree≤2 part of the real vanishing ideal is a finite-dimensional space. Its sphere equation forces every feasible 496-square moment matrix to have trace2. The feasible spectrahedron and its projection are compact. Rank-one moment matrices contain the mass ball. If every support had an affine SOS, evaluating its degree≤2 discrepancy at any feasible moment matrix would give the corresponding support inequality. Intersection of all supports would make this projection equal to the mass ball, a finite lift. This is a direct primal argument without Slater, SDP duality, or closure of a possibly nonclosed shadow.
6. **Rational unnormalized witness and calibration.** Strict separation of a point in the compact relaxation outside the mass ball gives a positive gap. The support function is1-Lipschitz; the moment Schur complement also gives \(\|x\|\le1\), so perturbing the coefficient vector by less than half the gap preserves strict separation. Density of rational vectors yields rational unnormalized coefficients. Signed coordinate wedges span the exterior space, giving positive maximum for a nonzero coefficient vector. Scaling by that maximum preserves failure of affine SOS. The constant normalized form is closed. The identity
   \(1-\varphi=\frac12(1-\varphi)^2+\frac12\sum_j\psi_j^2\)
   would follow from any norm completion on unit simple vectors and is exactly the forbidden affine SOS. Local differential forms necessarily give a pointwise identity; hence varying the completion locally cannot evade the obstruction.
7. **Stated pointwise converse.** Evaluating \(1-\varphi(p)=\|a+Bp\|^2\) at±p yields \(B^Ta=-\varphi/2\). At a maximizing unit simple vector, \(Bp=-a\); evaluation at its negative gives \(\|a\|^2=1/2\). Orthogonal projection onto \(a^\perp\) then gives \(2\|P_{a^\perp}Bp\|^2=1-\varphi(p)^2\). Homogeneity extends to all simple vectors. No smooth-selection result is inferred.
