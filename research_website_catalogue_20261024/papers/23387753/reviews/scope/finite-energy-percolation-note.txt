Abstract
For every integer d ≥ 2 we construct a bond percolation on Zd that is invariant under all
lattice automorphisms, mixing under translations, and has ordinary ﬁnite energy. Almost surely
it has a unique inﬁnite component, yet every independent Bernoulli thinning with retention p < 1
destroys all inﬁnite components. The construction perturbs a one-ended spanning tree, using the
maximum ﬁnite-past size along each non-tree edge’s tree path to suppress every bypass of large
tree cuts. This answers the historical ordinary-energy question of Benjamini, Häggström and
Schramm. We also make explicit a planar threshold-one consequence of Häggström–Mester’s
earlier construction. No ﬁrst-priority claim is made.
1 Statement and context
Write Ed for the nearest-neighbor edges of Zd. A bond conﬁguration X ⊆ Ed has ordinary ﬁnite
energy if, for every edge e,
0 < P(e ∈ X | σ(1{f ∈X} : f ̸= e)) < 1 almost surely . (1)
No common positive lower bound is required. For a ﬁxed countable graph H, deﬁne pc(H) as
the inﬁmum of retention parameters at which independent bond thinning of H has an inﬁnite
component with positive probability. The internal site threshold is deﬁned analogously. These
deﬁnitions apply to disconnected graphs as well.
Benjamini, Häggström and Schramm asked whether an automorphism-invariant, ﬁnite-energy
bond percolation can percolate almost surely while satisfying pc(X) = 1 [1, Question 1.1]. Their
ﬁnite-energy convention is ( 1). The question reappears in Benjamini’s lecture notes [ 2, Open
problem 8.15, p. 61 of the notes version] and Benjamini–Tassion [ 3, Question 1]. Finite components
are allowed: almost-sure percolation means existence of an inﬁnite component.
Theorem 1. For every integer d ≥ 2, there is a bond law on (Zd, Ed) invariant under every graph
automorphism and mixing under translations, satisfying (1), such that almost surely X has a unique
inﬁnite component C and
pc(X) = pc(C) = 1 .
The internal site thresholds of X and C also equal one. All threshold assertions are quenched: the
realized graph is ﬁxed before introducing independent thinning.

Now ﬁx a good pair (T, X ) and introduce independent bond thinning with retention p < 1.
An inﬁnite path from v must retain every suﬃciently late tn. These are distinct ﬁxed thinning
coordinates, so the probability of such a path is at most pk for every k, and is zero. A countable
union over vertices excludes all inﬁnite thinned components. The same deterministic witness works
for every real p < 1, without intersecting an uncountable collection of probability-one events. At
p = 1 the graph percolates. For C, the late cut identity is
∂C(Sn ∩ C) = {tn},
since both endpoints lie in CR ⊆ C. This proves both bond thresholds. An inﬁnite site-thinned
path must retain inﬁnitely many distinct vertices of the same ray, giving the identical pk obstruction
and both site thresholds.
For completeness, the witness-to-output step is measurable. At ﬁxed rational p and vertex
v, the thinning probability of an inﬁnite connection is a decreasing limit of measurable ﬁnite-
ball connection probabilities. Requiring these limits to vanish for countably many p, v is Borel;
monotonicity supplies all real p < 1. Alternatively, the bond obstruction has the graph-only Borel
certiﬁcate that, for every v and k, there are ﬁnite sets containing v with singleton boundaries at k
pairwise distinct edges. No measurable selection of a hidden tree from X is needed.
To prove energy after forgetting T , realize ( 2) by independent uniforms Ue, independent of T .
Put Fe = σ(Xf : f ̸= e) and He = σ(T, Uf : f ̸= e). Then Fe ⊆ H e, Ue is independent of He, and
P(Xe = 1 | F e) = E[rT (e) | F e], P(Xe = 0 | F e) = E[1 − rT (e) | F e]. (5)
Both conditional expectations are strictly positive almost surely. Indeed a conditional expectation
of a strictly positive integrable random variable cannot vanish on a set of positive probability, by
integration over that set. Countability of edges gives a common null set. Conditional versions may
be assigned value 1/2 on excluded conditioning conﬁgurations.
Finally, the ﬁnite-side and path functions are Borel: enumerate ﬁnite connected vertex sets with
their ﬁnite incident boundaries and ﬁnite paths. Connectedness, acyclicity and one-endedness of a
spanning tree also have countable Borel descriptions. Assigning rates 1/2 outside the probability-
one good input domain gives a globally measurable kernel. Finite-side cardinalities and tree paths
commute with automorphisms, so the kernel does too.
3 Lattice input and symmetry
Timár’s one-ended spanning-tree theorem [ 6, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2] supplies a one-ended span-
ning tree as a measurable isomorphism-equivariant factor of iid vertex labels on an ergodic amenable
one-ended unimodular random graph. We use the precise inspected version arXiv:1805.10690v1.
The deterministic rooted lattice Zd, d ≥ 2, meets these hypotheses. The mass-transport identity
follows by summing over displacement vectors; invariant uniformly shifted ﬁnite box partitions ver-
ify amenability; and the complement of a suﬃciently large box is connected, verifying one-endedness.
Its rooted isomorphism class is deterministic and hence ergodic.
This factor input and independent iid edge marks have a joint law invariant under every lattice
automorphism. Proposition 2 therefore gives the required full symmetry. The joint iid ﬁeld is mixing
under translations: ﬁnite-coordinate events are independent after suﬃciently large translations, and
approximation in probability by such events proves the assertion for arbitrary measurable events.
An equivariant measurable factor inherits mixing. Proposition 2 now proves Theorem 1.

4 The planar antecedent
We record explicitly why the older HM construction deserves stronger credit than similarity of its
ﬂip rule. In the square lattice let T be the primal UST and T ∗ its complementary one-ended dual
tree, as in [ 4, Section 2]. The exact ﬁxed-phase midpoint coordinates of HM’s scale-two site model,
interpreted as coarse bonds with all coarse vertices present, have conditional probabilities

{
1 − 4−sT (e), e ∈ T,
4−sT ∗ (e∗), e / ∈ T. (6)
Indeed HM ﬂips a site x with probability 2−b(x)−1, where b(x) counts the ﬁnite vertices separated
from monochromatic inﬁnity by removing x. A midpoint with a ﬁnite coarse side of s vertices has
b(x) = s + (s − 1) = 2 s − 1. Taking these coordinates preserves their conditional independence.
This is a coordinate marginal of the ﬁxed-phase construction, not the induced graph on its ﬁnally
open site set. The random parity phase of the full site model is absent from the coarse bond law.
