## Claim and success criterion

For the free group F_n on the indicated ordered generators, use the **integral** lower central series Γ_1=F_n and Γ_(r+1)=[Γ_r,F_n]. Let A_n(k) be the kernel of the action on F_n/Γ_(k+1). The representation algebra R is the **Q-algebra of matrix-entry functions** on Hom(F_n,SL(2,C)), and J is generated by all a_ij(g)−δ_ij. D_n(k) is the kernel of the substitution action on the **entire quotient J/J^(k+1)**. It is not defined using only the degree-k graded piece, trace functions, characters, or a rational isolator of Γ.

With group commutator [g,h]=ghg^−1h^−1, the words are

- w = [[[x1,x2],[x1,x3]],x1];
- v = [[[x1,x2],[x1,[x1,x2]]],x1].

The exact claims are: fixing all other generators, x4↦x4w gives an element of D_n(5)\A_n(5) for every n≥4; x3↦x3v gives an element of D_n(6)\A_n(6) for every n≥3. ## Reconstruction of the central proof

### The matrix identity is universal at the claimed size

Set P(X,Y,Z)=[[[X,Y],[X,Z]],X] with associative commutator. For arbitrary 2×2 matrices over a commutative ring, Cayley–Hamilton gives X²−tX+dI=0, where t=tr(X), d=det(X). For U=[X,Y], V=[X,Z], this yields XU+UX=tU and XV+VX=tV. Therefore

XUV=(tU−UX)V=tUV−U(tV−VX)=UVX,

and likewise XVU=VUX. Thus X commutes with UV−VU and P=0. This proves the identity without division, diagonalization, genericity, distinct eigenvalues, or a trace-zero assumption. Substituting Z=[X,Y] proves Q(X,Y)=P(X,Y,[X,Y])=0. The latter has weighted degree six, not five, when X and Y have degree one.

The current printed proof is even more direct than the original support's trace-zero presentation. Both are valid: the main proof applies to arbitrary matrices over the commutative associated graded ring. The identity is special to size two. A negative control with 3×3 matrices X=diag(1,0,−1), Y=E12 and Z=E23 gives P=−2E13; no larger-matrix conclusion is inferred.

### Actual ideal membership, not vanishing along selected representations

For A=1+u and B=1+z, direct multiplication gives

ABA^−1B^−1−1=(uz−zu)A^−1B^−1.

If u has entries in I^r and z in I^s, r,s≥1, the result has entries in I^(r+s). Both inverse matrices equal the identity modulo I, so the leading class modulo I^(r+s+1) is the associative bracket of the leading classes of u and z. These statements use exact ideal containments.

Apply the calculation successively to the universal matrices M(x_i)=1+B_i, B_i∈Mat_2(J). The group-word shapes first place M(w)−1 in Mat_2(J^5), and M(v)−1 in Mat_2(J^6). Their classes in those successive quotients are P(X1,X2,X3) and Q(X1,X2), respectively, for X_i the degree-one classes. Since gr_J(R) is commutative, the matrix identity applies, and the zero leading classes imply

M(w)−1∈Mat_2(J^6), and M(v)−1∈Mat_2(J^7).

There is no transfer from one-parameter arcs to ideal membership and no assumption that gr_J(R) is polynomial, reduced, or smooth. Higher-order terms in the inputs cannot change the particular leading classes computed by the filtered identity. The nilpotent-ring controls below test mixed terms as a separate falsification route, but the universal argument is the written filtration proof.

### The lower central obstruction is genuinely integral

Map the generators into units of the noncommutative power-series ring Z<<X1,…,Xn>> by x_i↦1+X_i. Their inverses are the formal geometric series. Commutators add degree, so induction proves E(Γ_r)⊆1+T_(≥r). This is only the easy forward implication; neither injectivity of the Magnus map nor a converse dimension-subgroup theorem is needed.

The degree-five component of E(w)−1 is the free associative polynomial P, and the degree-six component of E(v)−1 is Q. They are not the zero free polynomials, although they vanish upon evaluating in 2×2 matrices over a commutative ring. In P, the coefficient of X1 X1 X2 X1 X3 is −1. In Q, the coefficient of X1 X1 X1 X2 X1 X2 is +1. Expanding [[U,V],X1] into its four products checks these coefficients directly: only −X1UV contributes to the first indicated monomial; only +X1VU contributes to the second with V=X1²X2−2X1X2X1+X2X1². Thus w∉Γ_6 and v∉Γ_7. No denominator, isolator, characteristic-zero inference about an integral group, or torsion assumption enters this argument.

Adjoining further free generators leaves these coefficients intact. The exact integral unitriangular certificates printed on page four provide another route. The displayed assignments into UT_6(Z) give w=I−E16, and the assignments into UT_7(Z) give v=I+E17. If N_r denotes matrices supported at least r places above the diagonal, then [1+N_r,1+N_s]⊆1+N_(r+s), while N_d=0 in size d. Hence Γ_d(UT_d)=1. The nonidentity images give the same nonmembership conclusions. Both untruncated integral matrix evaluations were independently reproduced.

### Automorphism and full-quotient requirements are satisfied

The spare generator is essential to the stated construction. If u omits x_t, both the substitution x_t↦x_tu and the proposed inverse x_t↦x_tu^−1 fix u; their two compositions fix every free generator. This proves the displayed substitutions are automorphisms at all claimed ranks. The first requires n≥4, and the second n≥3. An independent negative control that uses x3 as moving generator for w makes this proposed inverse fail; it is only a test of the inverse argument and is not a claim that every such substitution is noninvertible.

For the moving generator, M(φ(x_t))−M(x_t)=M(x_t)(M(u)−1), whose entries belong to J^(k+1). Every other generator matrix is fixed. The finitely many entries of M(x_i) generate all of R: products give word entries, while inverses are adjugates because the determinants are one. Differences of substituted polynomial products are sums of terms each containing a generator difference. Consequently φ fixes every element of R/J^(k+1), not merely a degree-one subspace. Substitution preserves J, either directly by permuting its word generators or by the trivial-representation augmentation. Therefore it acts identically on the whole J/J^(k+1).

Finally x_t^−1φ(x_t)=u∉Γ_(k+1) shows that the induced action on F_n/Γ_(k+1) is nonidentity. This is exactly the required exclusion from A_n(k). An opposite inverse convention for the action yields the same kernel and does not alter the conclusion.

## Independent falsification controls

`fresh_controls.py` was written without importing or reading the packaged helper implementations. It completed **4,502 exact assertions**, recorded in `fresh-control-results.json`.

- Freely reduced group words and both automorphism compositions were checked. Full truncated integral Magnus products of the actual group words were compared against independently bracketed free associative polynomials; all 12 leading terms of P and all 10 of Q matched, including the integral witness coefficients.
- The two published unitriangular evaluations were checked with untruncated integer matrix arithmetic and exact nilpotent geometric inverses.
- All 4,096 triples of 2×2 matrices over F_2 were tested for P=0, and all 256 pairs for Q=0. These concern only the universal commutative-ring matrix lemma; they are not a specialization of the rational representation algebra to characteristic two.
- Eight trials in Z[e,t]/(e,t)^9, also viewed inside the corresponding Q-algebra, used mixed-variable determinant-one charts M=[[1+a,b],[c,(1+bc)/(1+a)]]. Exact finite geometric inverses retained mixed terms. The actual group-word outputs began at degree six for w and degree seven for v in each trial. Moving-generator differences satisfied the same bounds. These controls attack the possible confusion between single-arc vanishing and ideal membership.
- Negative controls detected failure of the identity in size three and failure of the simple inverse formula when the spare-generator condition was dropped.

These finite tests cannot prove all ranks, all rational functions, or every ideal-power containment. Those quantifiers are supported by the independent algebraic reconstruction above. No false universal inference is made from the sample counts.
