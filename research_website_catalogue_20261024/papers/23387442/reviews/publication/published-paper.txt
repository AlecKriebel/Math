                          Counterexamples to Satoh’s SL(2)
                          representation-ﬁltration conjecture
                                                 Alec Kriebel
                                         Independent researcher
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                    Abstract
          Let An (k) be the integral Andreadakis ﬁltration of the automorphism group of a free group,
      and let Dn (k) be the kernel of its action on J/J k+1 in the rational SL(2, C) matrix-entry algebra
      at the trivial representation. We construct elements of Dn (5) \ An (5) for every n ≥ 4, and of
      Dn (6) \ An (6) for every n ≥ 3. Thus the equality in all degrees conjectured in Satoh’s 2018
      report is false at every rank in its stated range. The construction uses a classical degree-ﬁve
      Lie identity, elementary free-group automorphisms, and integral Magnus coeﬃcients. The proof
      works with actual powers of J, rather than only values along one-parameter representations.


1    The conjecture and the counterexamples
Write Fn = hx1 , . . . , xn i and Γ1 Fn = Fn , Γr+1 Fn = [Γr Fn , Fn ]. All lower central subgroups in this
note are integral. Put                      (                                 )
                                An (k) = ker Aut Fn −→ Aut(Fn /Γk+1 Fn ) .
For a representation ρ : Fn → SL(2, C), let aij (g)(ρ) = ρ(g)ij . Let R be the Q-algebra of functions
generated by all aij (g), and set
                                                                                    (                   )
     J = (aij (g) − δij | g ∈ Fn , 1 ≤ i, j ≤ 2) ⊂ R,             Dn (k) = ker Aut Fn −→ Aut(J/J k+1 ) .

We use the substitution action aij (g)ϕ = aij (ϕ(g)). These are the component-function algebra and
the full ideal quotient of [1, pp. 88–89], not a trace algebra or a single associated graded piece. That
source records An (k) ⊆ Dn (k) and equality for 1 ≤ k ≤ 4, then conjectures equality for all k ≥ 1
in the discussion with n ≥ 3. See also [2, §4.2, Theorem 6.3] for the deﬁnitions and low-degree
comparison.
    Throughout, [g, h] = ghg −1 h−1 for groups and [U, V ] = U V − V U for algebras. Deﬁne the
words

                                     w = [[[x1 , x2 ], [x1 , x3 ]], x1 ],                                   (1)
                                      v = [[[x1 , x2 ], [x1 , [x1 , x2 ]]], x1 ].                           (2)

Theorem 1. For every n ≥ 4, the automorphism ﬁxing all generators except x4 7→ x4 w belongs to
Dn (5) \ An (5). For every n ≥ 3, the automorphism ﬁxing all generators except x3 7→ x3 v belongs
to Dn (6) \ An (6).

    The degree-ﬁve identity used below is classical. With a change of sign and variables it is pre-
cisely Burde’s identity (4); his Theorem 4.14 attributes its occurrence in a basis of sl2 identities
to Razmyslov [4]. We report that historical attribution through the inspected Burde source. The
weighted substitution yielding the degree-six identity, Cayley–Hamilton, leading commutator ex-
pansions and Magnus expansions are also classical ingredients. No new polynomial identity or
general PI method is claimed here. The contribution presented is their explicit application to the
equality formulated in [1].


2    The matrix identity and actual ideal powers
Lemma 2. For 2 × 2 matrices over any commutative ring,

                                P (X, Y, Z) := [[[X, Y ], [X, Z]], X] = 0.

Consequently Q(X, Y ) := P (X, Y, [X, Y ]) = 0.

Proof. Put t = tr X, U = [X, Y ] and V = [X, Z]. Cayley–Hamilton gives X 2 − tX + det(X)I = 0,
whence
                        XU + U X = [X 2 , Y ] = tU,    XV + V X = tV.
It follows that XU V = (tU − U X)V = tU V − U (tV − V X) = U V X. Likewise XV U = V U X, so
X commutes with U V − V U . The second assertion is substitution. This elementary proof uses no
division or diagonalization.

Lemma 3. Let I be an ideal in a commutative ring S. If 1 + u and 1 + z are invertible matrices,
with u ∈ Mat2 (I r ) and z ∈ Mat2 (I s ), r, s ≥ 1, then

                          [1 + u, 1 + z] − 1 = (uz − zu)(1 + u)−1 (1 + z)−1 .

Its entries lie in I r+s , and its class modulo I r+s+1 is the bracket of the leading classes of u and z.

Proof. Subtract (1 + z)(1 + u) from (1 + u)(1 + z) and multiply on the right by the two displayed
inverses. Both inverses are congruent to 1 modulo I, so their product does not change the class in
degree r + s.

   Let M (g) = (aij (g)) ∈ SL2 (R) be the universal matrix of a word. Write M (xi ) = 1 + Bi ,
so Bi ∈ Mat2 (J), and let Xi be its degree-one class in Mat2 (grJ R). Successive applications of
Lemma 3 show that the degree-ﬁve class of M (w) − 1 is P (X1 , X2 , X3 ), and the degree-six class of
M (v) − 1 is
                      [[[X1 , X2 ], [X1 , [X1 , X2 ]]], X1 ] = P (X1 , X2 , [X1 , X2 ]).
The latter substitution has weight two, giving total degree six. The associated graded ring is
commutative, so Lemma 2 applies. The zero leading classes give the actual ideal containments

                         M (w) − 1 ∈ Mat2 (J 6 ),       M (v) − 1 ∈ Mat2 (J 7 ).                      (3)

No assertion that a graded ring is a polynomial ring, no smoothness or reducedness assumption,
and no passage from vanishing along arcs is needed.

3    Integral lower central obstructions
Let T = ZhhX1 , . . . , Xn ii be the ring of noncommutative formal power series, ﬁltered by total degree.
The assignment xi 7→ 1 + Xi deﬁnes a group homomorphism E : Fn → T × , since 1 + Xi has its
formal geometric inverse. The same commutator calculation, now in the degree ﬁltration, gives

                                   E(Γr Fn ) ⊆ 1 + T≥r        (r ≥ 1).                               (4)

This follows inductively because [1 + T≥r , 1 + T≥s ] ⊆ 1 + T≥r+s . Only this implication is required;
neither injectivity of E nor a converse dimension-subgroup theorem is used.
    The leading terms of E(w) − 1 and E(v) − 1 are the free associative polynomials P (X1 , X2 , X3 )
and Q(X1 , X2 ), respectively. They do not vanish in the free algebra. Indeed,

                       [X1 X1 X2 X1 X3 ]P = −1,         [X1 X1 X1 X2 X1 X2 ]Q = 1,                   (5)

where [m] denotes the coeﬃcient of the monomial m. For the ﬁrst coeﬃcient, in [[U, V ], X1 ] =
U V X1 − V U X1 − X1 U V + X1 V U with U = [X1 , X2 ], V = [X1 , X3 ], only −X1 U V contributes to
the indicated monomial. For the second take U = X1 X2 −X2 X1 and V = X12 X2 −2X1 X2 X1 +X2 X12 ;
only X1 V U contributes, using the ﬁrst monomial in each of V and U . Thus (4) proves

                            w∈
                             / Γ6 Fn     (n ≥ 3),       v∈
                                                         / Γ7 Fn    (n ≥ 2).                         (6)

All coeﬃcients are integral. Adjoining additional free generators leaves these coeﬃcients unchanged,
so these assertions require no stability or rational-isolator assumption.


4    Automorphisms and the full quotient action
Proof of Theorem 1. If a word u uses no xt , the substitution ϕu (xt ) = xt u ﬁxing the other genera-
tors is an automorphism. Its inverse sends xt to xt u−1 and ﬁxes all others: both substitutions ﬁx
u, and their two compositions ﬁx every free generator.
    Apply this ﬁrst to (u, t) = (w, 4), and then to (u, t) = (v, 3). For the moving generator,

                              M (ϕu (xt )) − M (xt ) = M (xt )(M (u) − 1).

Equation (3) puts all its entries in J k+1 for k = 5 and 6, respectively; every other generator matrix
is ﬁxed.
    The entries of the ﬁnitely many matrices M (xi ) generate R: products give the entries of products
of words, and inverse entries are adjugate entries since all determinants are one. Therefore ﬁxing
these algebra generators modulo J k+1 ﬁxes every element of R/J k+1 . Explicitly, the diﬀerence
between a product of substituted generators and the original product is a sum of products each
containing one generator diﬀerence. Each summand lies in J k+1 . The trivial representation is
ﬁxed by substitution, so J is invariant. Hence ϕu acts identically on the whole J/J k+1 , proving
ϕu ∈ Dn (k).
    Finally x−1
             t ϕu (xt ) = u. The nonmembership statements (6) show that ϕu fails to act identically
on Fn /Γk+1 Fn . Thus ϕu ∈ / An (k), as required.

    For each n ≥ 3 the second construction already refutes equality in all degrees. The ﬁrst gives
the earlier failure in degree ﬁve when n ≥ 4. We make no claim about degree ﬁve at rank three,
failure in every degree, larger matrix sizes, character algebras, or the diﬀerent comparison of An (k)
with the lower central series of IAn .

5    Veriﬁcation, related work and status
The accompanying archive preserves the original candidate packet and separate independent math-
ematical and priority audits. Exact computations check the matrix identities and ﬁnite ideal
quotients. A distinct integral check uses upper unitriangular matrices. In size six, assigning

                    x1 7→ I + E12 + E23 + E45 ,   x2 7→ I + E34 ,   x3 7→ I + E56

sends w to I − E16 . In size seven, assigning

                      x1 7→ I + E12 + E23 + E34 + E56 ,    x2 7→ I + E45 + E67

sends v to I + E17 . Here Eij is a matrix unit and other generators map to I. Direct integral
multiplication veriﬁes both identities. The dth lower central subgroup of the size-d unitriangular
group is trivial, since commutators add distances above the diagonal. These give independent ﬁnite
certiﬁcates for (6). The archive’s 489 colored-path evaluations recover all coeﬃcients through the
relevant degrees. These exact checks supplement the written proof; they are not a formal proof
certiﬁcate or a literature certiﬁcate.
     The bounded priority audit inspected the literal 2018 conjecture, Satoh’s public 2017 preprint
version, and classical PI and related automorphism literature. It found no veriﬁed earlier complete
application to this exact conjecture. Full journal texts of [3] and the 2021 publication related to
[2], as well as some related 2025–2026 journal articles, were unavailable. Earlier versions, abstracts
and related shorter sources are identiﬁed separately in the archive. No ﬁrstness, exhaustive novelty
clearance, or continuously open status through 2026 is certiﬁed. The theorem is a negative answer
to the equality as formulated in the inspected 2018 source.
