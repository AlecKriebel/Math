## Exact strongest supported result

Let `A_n(k)` be the kernel of the action of `Aut(F_n)` on the **integral** lower-central quotient `F_n/Gamma_{k+1}F_n`. Let R be the rational algebra of **matrix-entry functions** on `Hom(F_n,SL(2,C))`; J is the ideal of entry deviations from the trivial representation. Let `D_n(k)` be the kernel of the action on the **whole quotient** `J/J^{k+1}`.

With group commutator `aba^{-1}b^{-1}`, define

`w = [[[x1,x2],[x1,x3]],x1]`,

`v = [[[x1,x2],[x1,[x1,x2]]],x1]`.

The automorphism fixing every generator except `x4 -> x4 w` lies in `D_n(5) \ A_n(5)` for every **n>=4**. The automorphism fixing every generator except `x3 -> x3 v` lies in `D_n(6) \ A_n(6)` for every **n>=3**. The latter already refutes the universal all-degrees equality for each rank in the original stated range; the former gives degree-five failure when an unused fourth generator is available.

This does not decide rank-three degree five, rank two, failure in every degree, higher matrix sizes, trace/character algebras, or the different Andreadakis-versus-lower-central-series comparison for `IA_n`.

## Reconstruction of the universal argument

### 1. Original conjecture and conventions

The original OWR contribution, printed pp.88–89, defines the Q-subalgebra generated by all component functions `a_ij(g)`, its ideal of deviations, and `D_n(k)=ker(Aut F_n -> Aut(J/J^{k+1}))`. It states `A_n(k) subset D_n(k)`, equality through degree four, and conjectures equality for all k>=1 in the discussion for n>=3. Printed p.89 was visually inspected to confirm that the quotient is J, not `J^k`, and that the rank restriction is not a stable-rank condition. The manuscript targets this exact formulation. [Original report](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46724)

Satoh v2 §3 gives the matrix-entry algebra, generator-entry finite generation and invariant ideal; §§4.1–4.2 define the two filtrations; Theorem 6.3 records equality through degree four. Its action on entry functions is the substitution action used here. Replacing a right-action convention by the inverse action leaves its kernel unchanged. The final 2017 and 2021 journal bodies were not newly acquired; the original 2018 formulation and exact cited preprint suffice for the claim under review. [Satoh v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.05411v2)

### 2. The matrix identity is universal over a commutative base

For 2x2 matrices let `U=[X,Y]`, `V=[X,Z]`, and `t=tr X`. Cayley–Hamilton gives `XU+UX=tU` and `XV+VX=tV`. Substituting twice yields `XUV=UVX`, and similarly `XVU=VUX`. Thus `[[U,V],X]=0`. No division, diagonalization, distinct-eigenvalue hypothesis, reducedness or characteristic-zero assumption is used. Consequently substituting `Z=[X,Y]` gives the weighted degree-six identity Q.

As a separate pre-helper mechanism, I expanded generic integer polynomial matrices and verified the stronger centralizer formula

`[[X,Y],[X,Z]] = -det(c(X),c(Y),c(Z)) (2X-tr(X)I)`,

where the determinant's rows are `c(A)=(A11-A22,A12,A21)`. Its right side visibly commutes with X. All coefficients were checked in the polynomial ring over Z, with twelve independent entries, and Q was independently checked using eight generic entries. This does not rely on the submitted helper or on choosing traceless matrices. The formula is review evidence, not a new-identity or priority claim.

Burde's displayed identity (4) is the negative of P after renaming variables. The paragraph before his Theorem 4.14 and that theorem attribute the sl2 identity basis to Razmyslov. The manuscript correctly reports that attribution **through Burde**. The theorem quoted there is over a characteristic-zero field; the manuscript's elementary proof establishes the stronger commutative-ring matrix identity it actually needs. Neither the full classification of sl2 polynomial identities nor access to Razmyslov's original text is a missing premise. [Burde v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0950v2)

### 3. Actual ideal powers, without an arc-testing inference

For units A=1+u and B=1+z,

`ABA^{-1}B^{-1}-1 = (uz-zu)A^{-1}B^{-1}`.

The displayed inverse order is correct because `(BA)A^{-1}B^{-1}=1`. If u and z have entrywise orders r and s in an ideal, their commutator has order at least r+s. The inverse factors are congruent to the identity modulo that ideal, so the leading class is the corresponding algebra bracket.

Apply this repeatedly to `M(x_i)=1+B_i`, where all entries of B_i lie in J. The first candidate degree of `M(w)-1` is five, with class P, and of `M(v)-1` is six, with class `P(X1,X2,[X1,X2])`. The substitution has weight two, not one. Since `gr_J R` is commutative, the universal polynomial identity applies even if it has relations or nilpotents. A zero class in `J^d/J^{d+1}` is exactly containment in `J^{d+1}`. Thus all entries lie in J^6 and J^7, respectively.

No identification of `gr_J R` with a polynomial ring is needed. No vanishing on one-parameter representations is used to infer ideal membership. The required native proof gives a narrower trace-zero leading-class proof using `tr(B_i)=-det(B_i) in J^2`; that argument is valid too and is consistent with the stronger published lemma.

### 4. Integral lower-central nonmembership

The formal Magnus assignment `x_i -> 1+X_i` is a homomorphism to the units of the noncommutative formal series ring over Z. Products, inverses and the filtered commutator calculation show inductively that `E(Gamma_r F_n) subset 1+T_{>=r}`. Only this easy implication is required. Injectivity of the Magnus map, a converse dimension-subgroup theorem, torsion-free quotient structure and rational-isolator comparisons are unnecessary.

The actual reduced group words have lengths 32 and 30. Fresh full truncated evaluation gives `E(w)=1+P` through degree five and `E(v)=1+Q` through degree six, with no lower terms. In P the coefficient of `X1 X1 X2 X1 X3` is -1. In Q the coefficient of `X1 X1 X1 X2 X1 X2` is +1. The manuscript's explanation of the sole contributing product in each case is correct. Therefore w is not in Gamma_6 and v is not in Gamma_7, integrally. Additional free generators do not alter these coefficients.

There is also a concrete finite group certificate. Direct, untruncated integer matrix multiplication reproduces the published maps to `UT_6(Z)` and `UT_7(Z)`, sending w to `I-E16` and v to `I+E17`. In the size-d unitriangular group, matrices supported at least r steps above the diagonal form a multiplicative filtration; commutators add these distances. Its dth lower-central subgroup is therefore trivial. The two nonidentity images independently rule out the asserted lower-central memberships. This argument uses integral matrices, not numerical approximations.

### 5. Genuine automorphisms and the full quotient action

The word u uses no moving generator x_t. Thus both substitutions `x_t -> x_t u` and `x_t -> x_t u^{-1}` fix u, and their two compositions fix every free generator. They are actual automorphisms. This explains the minimum ranks in the statement: w uses x1,x2,x3 and needs x4, while v uses only x1,x2 and needs x3.

For the moving generator,

`M(phi_u(x_t))-M(x_t)=M(x_t)(M(u)-1)`.

Multiplication by a matrix over R preserves entrywise J^{k+1}. All other generator matrices are fixed. Every word entry is a polynomial in the finitely many generator entries: use matrix multiplication and the determinant-one adjugate inverse. Therefore identity on those generators modulo J^{k+1} extends to identity on all of R/J^{k+1}, hence on the **full** J/J^{k+1}. This is not a claim about just a single associated-graded piece. The ideal is invariant because substitution fixes the trivial representation.

Finally, `x_t^{-1}phi_u(x_t)=u`. The integral nonmembership just proved means the induced map on `F_n/Gamma_{k+1}F_n` is not the identity. This establishes both set differences and the stated negative answer to the original universal conjecture.
