# Independent exact proof coverage

Checkpoint: 2026-10-25 UTC. This reconstruction began with the entire four-page original and TeX and was sealed before archived reviews were read. It makes no use of sibling verdicts or of numerical evidence to prove a uniform assertion.

Let n be an integer at least 1, r>0, and K a compact convex subset of R^n with nonempty interior. Every extreme point has Euclidean norm at least r. Use n-dimensional Lebesgue volume throughout.

1. Extreme-point representation is a standard finite-dimensional import, verified in an actual author-hosted proof (Bertsekas, *Convex Optimization Theory*, Exercise 2.2 and solution, PDF pp. 343–344). It gives even K=conv(ext K), stronger than the closure equality used here. An interior ball ensures ext K affinely spans R^n. Every subspace of Euclidean space is second countable, hence has a countable dense subset; ext K need not be closed. Enlarge that subset by any fixed affinely independent extreme vertices and enumerate without repetition.
2. Each new extreme vertex v lies outside the earlier finite hull P: otherwise v is a finite nontrivial convex combination of other points of K. At a first ray intersection y with P, at least one active facet inequality is strictly violated at v. If none were, active inequalities hold on the incoming segment, and all inactive inequalities continue to hold sufficiently near y, a contradiction. Thus strictly visible facet pyramids cover the shell. A pyramid interior determines a unique first intersection in the relative interior of its base facet. Distinct base facets therefore give disjoint interiors, all outside P. Coplanar facets cause only boundary overlaps.
3. Triangulate each finite facet by vertex coning and dimension induction, then cone to v. These cones are full dimensional by strict visibility, and use only original extreme points. Existing simplices need never be subdivided. Matching subdivisions across lower dimensional faces and local finiteness are not required. The finite or countable union is the convex union C of the finite hulls.
4. Closure C=K. To justify actual almost-everywhere coverage, surround any x in int K by a small simplex with x strictly inside; approximate its vertices by points in C. Small perturbations retain x inside, so x belongs to C. Thus K minus C is contained in the boundary. The boundary is null: for any interior c, (1-t)K+tc lies in int K for 0<t<1; its volume is (1-t)^n|K|, and t tends to zero. Hence |int K|=|K|. Countably many simplex boundaries also remain null.
5. With uniform barycentric coordinates on a nondegenerate simplex S with vertices x_i, integration of monomials gives E lambda_i^2=2/((n+1)(n+2)), E lambda_i lambda_k=1/((n+1)(n+2)). Consequently C2(S)=[q_S+(n+1)||c_S||^2]/(n+2), where q_S is the average squared vertex norm. This coincides algebraically with the prior FPS simplex identity. The factorial monomial formula in the manuscript uses nonnegative integer exponents.
6. Put w_j=|S_j|/|K|>0. Null overlaps and boundedness justify sum w_j=1, sum w_j c_j=g_K, and C2(K)=sum w_j C2(S_j). If M=max_K ||x||, then ||c_j||<=M, q_j<=M^2, and every weighted moment/variance series converges absolutely. The variance expansion is therefore legitimate for the countable family:

   Delta_r(K) = [sum_j w_j(q_j-r^2)]/(n+2) + [(n+1)/(n+2)] sum_j w_j||c_j-g_K||^2.

7. Every term is nonnegative. Equality forces every q_j=r^2 and every c_j=g_K, since each weight is positive. All vertices of each simplex then have norm r. Two nondegenerate simplices with the same centroid cannot have disjoint interiors, since their centroid belongs to both interiors. There is exactly one simplex, whose closure is K by the established density. The converse follows from the simplex identity.
8. A second finite-limit route retains two fixed simplices. If initial S differs from K, choose an extreme u outside S and a strictly visible facet opposite v_i. The adjacent T has positive volume b, disjoint interior from S of volume a, and c_T-c_S=(u-v_i)/(n+1). Completing the square for two centroid terms gives the fixed lower bound

   Delta_r(P_m) >= ab||u-v_i||^2 / ((n+1)(n+2)|K|(a+b)) > 0.

   Its denominator uses |P_m|<=|K|. Bounded integrands on K, almost-everywhere convergence of the nested hull indicators, and |P_m|>=a>0 give convergence of volume, first moments, second moments, and normalized deficits. The same fixed positive bound survives the limit. Merely knowing each finite deficit is positive would not suffice; the manuscript supplies the needed fixed witness.

Boundary checks: in n=1, K=[a,b] and Delta=(a^2+b^2-2r^2)/6, so equality is exactly [-r,r]. The triangle (1,0),(0,1),(-1,0) has g=(0,1/3), C2=1/3 and strengthened equality at r=1 although it is nonregular. The triangle (1,0),(3/5,4/5),(3/5,-4/5) excludes the origin, has g=(11/15,0), C2=49/75, and strengthened equality at r=1. These follow from the exact simplex identity, without an extra centering assumption. For the weaker inequality C2>=r^2/(n+2), equality additionally requires g=0. A strictly greater radius hypothesis, even without a uniform margin over r, precludes equality. Lower dimensional K, n=0, and r=0 are outside the stated theorem. The retained-pair bound scales as squared length and is body dependent; no uniform stability estimate is asserted.

Strongest verified result: the full written universal equality classification under the exact stated hypotheses.
