## Exact claim and limits

The input is a finite specified bipartite graph with disjoint vertex classes, rational edge demands, any subset of integer coordinates, and unrestricted signs on all variables. Dimensions, the number of integer coordinates, demand numerators and denominators, and rational objective/query encodings all vary. The feasible set is

\[
S=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:x_u+x_v\ge b_{uv},\quad x_i\in\mathbb Z\ (i\in I)\}.
\]

Theorem 1 asserts exact polynomial-time linear minimization, or an integral improving recession direction together with a feasible point; a closed rational **actual** convex hull with a computable polynomial facet-encoding bound; and exact strong separation and membership at every rational query, including boundary points.

This theorem has no empirical or stochastic assumptions. Its mathematical model assumptions matter: no additional bounds, nonnegativity requirements, nonbipartite edges, arbitrary inequality directions, or irrational data are inserted. The statement does not give a compact extended formulation, classify facets, prove Conforti's separate subtree conjecture, claim a strongly polynomial total algorithm, or implement a complete efficient optimizer. “Polynomial” refers to binary input length, not to the numerical size of the common denominator.

## Reconstructed proof

### Sign transformation, rays, and proximity — pages 2–3

Changing the signs on the second bipartition gives inequalities `y_u-y_v >= b_uv` and preserves the designated integer coordinates. Feasibility of the original set follows from a sufficiently large common integer. An unbounded rational LP supplies a rational recession vector satisfying the homogeneous differences and a strictly improving objective inequality. Clearing its coordinate denominators uses only polynomially many bits and yields an integral vector. Integer steps from the integral feasible point prove unboundedness of the mixed set. In the bounded case an exact LP optimum is available even when the relaxation has lineality; the argument does not incorrectly require a vertex of the unanchored polyhedron.

I reconstructed Lemma 2 from its signed level sets. The positive and negative coordinate levels give at most `n` layers. Every layer is conformal both to each coordinate and to every pairwise difference. Thus a weighted layer lies rowwise between the LP optimum and a mixed feasible point. Optimality of the LP point implies a nonnegative objective increment along each layer. A layer with weight at least one can be removed from the mixed point; its coordinates are integral, the result remains feasible, its cost does not increase, and its distance to the LP optimum strictly decreases.

The proof properly avoids assuming that a mixed optimum already exists. For any feasible sublevel, a nearest point exists after intersecting with a sufficiently large closed distance ball. Removing a unit layer contradicts nearestness if any weight is at least one. Every feasible sublevel therefore has a representative within sup-distance strictly less than `n`. A single compact box consequently contains a representative no more expensive than every feasible point, so a global mixed optimum is attained. This supplies both attainment and the strict proximity statement without importing attainment from the classical proximity theorem.

### Exact slice domain and submodular value — page 3

Each integer coordinate is restricted to the integer labels between `ceil(y*_i-n)` and `floor(y*_i+n)`, with width at most `2n`. Only edges whose two endpoints are integer coordinates are rounded. Necessity of their rounded difference inequality is immediate. Sufficiency is special to this model: undo the sign change on the fixed labels and choose every remaining original coordinate equal to a sufficiently large common integer. One-fixed and two-free endpoint edges are then satisfied. This also handles negative fixed values and negative demands.

For each feasible label vector, the exact continuous completion LP is feasible, bounded below, and attains its value. Difference constraints, the label box, and integer–integer differences are closed under coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Taking minimum and maximum of two optimizing completions and using the modularity of a linear objective proves the stated **submodular** inequality with its correct direction. The corresponding claim would fail if made directly in the original sum-constraint coordinates, but the paper uses the difference coordinates throughout this step.

The oracle has a uniform polynomial encoding bound, not merely a finite value. In a connected component without a fixed label, anchoring one coordinate to zero preserves the objective because boundedness forces it to annihilate component translation. The anchored slice is pointed. At a vertex, every connected component of tight edges reaches a fixed coordinate or anchor; otherwise it could translate while preserving all active equations. A forest path therefore writes each coordinate as an integer anchor plus at most `n-1` signed demands. It has magnitude at most `H*+nB`, and its denominator divides the demand-denominator LCM `k`. Multiplying objective denominators into `q` gives a denominator dividing `kq` and the paper's value magnitude bound. The logarithms of `k` and `q` are bounded by sums of input denominator lengths. Their possibly enormous numerical values are never enumerated.

### Explicit ring representation and minimization — pages 3–4

The threshold ground set contains at most `2n|I|` nodes, regardless of demand denominators or the magnitude of the labels. Prefix implications enforce initial segments. For an integer–integer edge, the baseline condition at the lower label of its second endpoint is necessary and is included. Each subsequent threshold either implies a corresponding threshold at the first endpoint, is automatic, or is forbidden because its target exceeds the upper label. This reproduces the rounded difference condition exactly, including forced, forbidden, and empty-family cases.

Reachability supplies the least member and the least member containing a specified node, or detects its nonexistence. Thus this is an explicit polynomial-size representation, not an unsupported arbitrary lattice oracle. It meets both Schrijver's ring-family interface and GLS's digraph representation after the stated normalization. Exact rational submodular minimization returns a minimizing label vector, and one final exact LP returns its completion. Proximity shows that this is a global mixed optimum. All oracle evaluations and their values have polynomial bit complexity; the proof does not derive a bit bound merely from a count of arithmetic operations.

### Actual hull and facet bits — pages 4–5

For a real objective bounded below on the relaxation, anchoring whole graph components again yields an optimum vertex with coordinates bounded by `nB`. The proximity proof also works for a real objective, since it uses only signs, feasibility, and compactness. Hence the same box with `H=nB+n` contains a mixed optimum for **every** bounded objective, including the real separating objective needed later.

Let `K` be the convex hull of the mixed points in this fixed box, and `R` the difference recession cone. The bounded mixed set is a finite union of bounded rational slice polyhedra, so `K` is a rational polytope. The signed-threshold construction decomposes every vector of `R` into nonnegative multiples of integral recession layers. Integer steps from mixed feasible points remain mixed feasible; interpolation between successive integer steps gives every real nonnegative step in their convex hull. Extending first to finite convex combinations and then to finite sums of layers proves `K+R` is contained in the actual convex hull.

Conversely, a mixed point outside the closed polyhedron `K+R` would admit strict separation with a finite infimum. The separating objective must be nonnegative on `R`, hence bounded below on the relaxation. Its mixed optimum is attained in `K`, contradicting the separation. This proves equality with the actual convex hull, rather than only equality after taking its closure.

The slice matrices are directed incidence rows with unit fixings and box bounds, and are totally unimodular. Scaling the right sides by the demand LCM places all slice vertices in `(1/k)Z^n`, with norm at most `H`. Recession layers have integral coordinates of absolute value at most one. The hull is full-dimensional because it contains the convex hull of all sufficiently large integral vectors in the original coordinates, namely a translated positive orthant. Homogenized point and ray generators span each facet with `n` independent active generators, including opposite lineality generators where needed. Cofactors bound all inequality coefficients, including the constant, by `n![k(H+1)]^n`. The stated `phi` safely dominates their total signed binary encoding length, including the no-facet whole-space case. This is an existence and encoding argument; enumerating the exponentially numerous slices, vertices, rays, or facets is not an algorithmic step.

### Strong separation — page 5

An optimizer in the mixed set is also an optimizer over its convex hull. The rays supplied above lie in its recession cone and can be rescaled to the normalization required by the strong-optimization oracle. An optimal point need not be a vertex. With the established facet-encoding bound, GLS's well-described-polyhedron theorem applies to this possibly unbounded hull. It provides genuine strong separation and exact rational membership, not approximate membership, a closure substitute, or a test restricted to interior points. The diagonal sign change preserves the conclusions for the original variables.
