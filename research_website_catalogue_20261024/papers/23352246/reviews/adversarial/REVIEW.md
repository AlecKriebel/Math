## Proof attacks and findings

1. **Signs, feasibility and unboundedness.** Negating the V coordinates is an integral diagonal isomorphism and preserves every selected integer coordinate. A sufficiently large common integer in the original variables gives feasibility, including empty-edge cases. An unbounded continuous relaxation has a rational improving recession vector; coordinate-denominator clearing retains its inequalities and strict objective improvement with polynomial output bit length. Integer steps from the integral feasible point remain mixed feasible. Hence a finite mixed infimum cannot hide an unbounded relaxation.

2. **Proximity without circular attainment.** Both signs are needed in the threshold decomposition. Each layer is conformal on every coordinate difference, not just on coordinates; this ensures the forward layer point is feasible. LP optimality implies nonnegative layer cost. Removing an integral unit from a layer with coefficient at least one preserves all selected integer values, feasibility and objective sublevel while strictly reducing distance. The closed sublevel intersected with a finite distance ball is nonempty and compact. Its distance minimizer therefore has every layer coefficient below one, giving strict sup-distance less than n. This supplies a representative for every objective sublevel; minimization over the resulting compact mixed box proves attainment without assuming it. The proof works for nonvertex LP optima, component lineality and objective ties.

3. **Integer-label projection and completion.** Only an edge whose two endpoints are selected integer coordinates is rounded. Its necessary rounded constraint is also sufficient because all other original cover variables may be set sufficiently large. This would fail for arbitrary added upper bounds or general mixed difference systems, neither of which is claimed. The interval width is at most 2n, with up to 2n+1 integer labels but at most 2n threshold nodes per coordinate. The manuscript uses the correct node count.

4. **Partial minimization.** Slice feasibility follows from completion; its objective is bounded because the whole relaxation is bounded below. Finite rational LP optima are attained. Meet and join preserve the difference inequalities and integer slice domain; modularity of a linear objective gives the displayed submodular inequality in the stated direction. The corresponding assertion in unflipped cover variables would be false.

5. **Uniform exact-value bits.** A component with no fixed coordinate may be anchored because boundedness annihilates its translation direction. After fixed/zero anchoring, the recession lineality is trivial. Every vertex has a tight spanning forest linked to anchors. Coordinates therefore consist of an anchor plus at most n−1 signed demands, so denominators divide k and magnitudes obey the stated bound. Consequently objective values have denominator dividing kq and bounded numerator length. Numerical lcm magnitude may be exponential, but its binary encoding is polynomial; nothing enumerates its residues. Rational LP output and every oracle query have polynomial bit length.

6. **Domain oracle and SFM.** Prefix implications alone are insufficient: the baseline condition at the lower label is essential. The paper includes it, as well as targets below range, targets above range and prohibited source nodes. Reachability computes forced closure, infeasibility, and every smallest containing member; impossible nodes can be excluded before minimization. This supplies the required explicit ring-family data. Original GLS and Schrijver sources match the claimed rational and combinatorial interfaces; this is not an inference from an arithmetic operation count alone.

7. **Actual hull rather than its closure.** Anchoring the original relaxation gives a small optimum vertex for any objective bounded below, including real objectives used in the separation argument. Proximity gives a mixed optimum in the fixed bounded core. The core is a finite union of bounded rational slices before convexification. Every recession vector decomposes into integral threshold directions. A point of the mixed set outside K+R would strictly separate from this closed polyhedron; the separating objective is nonnegative on R and contradicts the common small-optimum property. Both inclusions are justified.
