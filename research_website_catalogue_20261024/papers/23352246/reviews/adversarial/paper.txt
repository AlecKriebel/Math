            Polynomial-time optimization and exact separation
                  for rational mixed-integer bipartite covers
                                                  Alec Kriebel∗

                                      12 October 2026 (research note)



                                                     Abstract
              We give polynomial-time exact linear optimization, strong separation and membership for
          mixed-integer bipartite covers with arbitrary rational edge demands, unrestricted variable signs
          and any selected integer coordinates. All data and query points are rational in the total binary-
          input model; dimension and demand denominators vary. This resolves the ﬁnite rational in-
          terpretation of Conforti’s 2008 hull-membership problem. A direct specialized proximity proof
          localizes integer labels to intervals of length 2n and proves attainment. Exact continuous com-
          pletion yields a submodular value oracle on a polynomial-size threshold lattice. A bounded-core
          argument establishes closedness of the actual hull and a polynomial facet-encoding bound, allow-
          ing classical optimization–separation equivalence to apply. The result is an explicit application
          of classical mixed proximity, discrete-convex and submodular-minimization ideas. It does not
          prove the separate subtree-hull conjecture or assert a compact formulation or strongly polyno-
          mial total algorithm.


1        Problem and contribution
Let U, V be speciﬁed disjoint vertex classes of a ﬁnite bipartite graph, n = |U | + |V |, E ⊂ U × V ,
I ⊂ U ∪ V , and b ∈ QE . Deﬁne

                           S = {x ∈ Rn : xu + xv ≥ buv (uv ∈ E), xi ∈ Z (i ∈ I)}.

There is no nonnegativity assumption. Numerators, positive denominators, graph and integer-
coordinate indices are encoded explicitly in binary. Conforti [1, pp. 2904–2905, Problem 1] asks the
complexity of membership in conv(S) and discusses linear optimization. Since that report leaves
encoding of real query points implicit, our theorem speciﬁes rational query points in the standard
ﬁnite bit model.

Theorem 1. For the data above and rational objective c, in polynomial total binary-input time one
can return an exact rational minimizer of cT x on S, or a feasible point and an integral improving
recession direction if the inﬁmum is −∞. The actual hull conv(S) is a closed rational polyhedron
with a polynomially computable facet-encoding bound. Strong separation and exact membership
at every rational query, including boundary queries, are polynomial-time tasks. All n, |I| and
denominators may vary.

sets are allowed. Feasibility follows by setting every coordinate to a common integer at least zero
and all demands. If I is empty, ordinary rational LP gives optimization directly; the hull argument
below still applies.
    Mixed proximity is classical: Cook–Gerards–Schrijver–Tardos [2, p. 258, Remarks (1)] give
all-coordinate distance at most n∆ for integral combined constraint matrices, assuming a mixed
optimum exists. Our signed-threshold proof is a direct specialization, and supplies attainment
without that premise. Hybrid partial minimization and discrete-convex optimization are prior
mechanisms [3, Proposition 2]; [4, §4.1] documents an earlier n2 -element ring-family lineage. We
claim no new general proximity or abstract ring-minimization method. The contribution is the
explicit rational application, exact LP completion oracle, denominator-independent label count
and actual-hull bit accounting.
    Earlier network and bipartite-cover formulations [5, 6] depend polynomially on a numerical
common denominator and explicitly discuss the remaining binary-size gap. They do not justify
enumerating denominator residues here. A bounded source audit located no earlier full solution to
this exact claim in inspected bodies; exhaustive priority is unveriﬁed.


2    Diﬀerence variables and proximity
Put yu = xu for u ∈ U , yv = −xv for v ∈ V , and transform c to a by the same sign change. This
integral invertible map preserves the selected integer coordinates. Write

                      P = {y : yu − yv ≥ buv },       T = P ∩ {y : yi ∈ Z (i ∈ I)}.

Rational LP decides boundedness. If aT y is unbounded on P , rational recession theory supplies a
polynomial-bit rational solution of

                                ru − rv ≥ 0    (uv ∈ E),       aT r ≤ −1.

Clearing coordinate denominators produces a polynomial-bit integral improving ray. From the
integral feasible point, every nonnegative integer step stays in T . Thus boundedness on P and
T agrees. Otherwise rational LP returns a polynomial-bit minimizer y ∗ on P . No vertex of this
unanchored, generally nonpointed polyhedron is assumed.

Lemma 2. If a is bounded below on P , the minimum on T is attained, and at least one minimizer
z satisﬁes ∥z − y ∗ ∥∞ < n.

Proof. Every d ∈ Rn has a signed nested-threshold decomposition d = sh=1 λh rh , with s ≤ n,
λh > 0 and nonzero rh ∈ {0, 1}n or {0, −1}n . For positive coordinates, list distinct values 0 <
t1 < · · · < tj , use indicators of {i : di ≥ th } and successive diﬀerences th − th−1 ; do the same with
negative magnitudes and negative indicators. Nesting makes every layer conformal on coordinates
and diﬀerences: rih has the sign of di or is zero, and rih − rjh has the sign of di − dj or is zero.
Consequently each weighted coordinate and diﬀerence lies between zero and the corresponding
value of d.

With at most n layers, its sup-distance is less than n. Hence every point of T has a no-more-

3     Exact partial minimization on a short-label lattice
For i ∈ I set

      li = ⌈yi∗ − n⌉,   hi = ⌊yi∗ + n⌋,   D = {p ∈ ZI : li ≤ pi ≤ hi , pu − pv ≥ ⌈buv ⌉ if u, v ∈ I}.

Each width is at most 2n. Exactly these assignments have completions in P : necessity is immediate;
for suﬃciency undo the sign change on ﬁxed coordinates and set every remaining original coordinate
to a suﬃciently large common integer. Only edges between two integer coordinates are rounded.
The free-sign bipartite model makes this completion possible. By Lemma 2, D is nonempty.
    For p ∈ D, the slice value

                                f (p) = min{aT y : y ∈ P, yi = pi (i ∈ I)}

is ﬁnite, attained and computable exactly by polynomial-size rational LP. Both P and D are closed
under coordinatewise meet and join. For example, two inequalities yu ≥ yv + b, zu ≥ zv + b imply
their min and max counterparts. Optimal completions y, z of p, q give

                f (p) + f (q) = aT y + aT z = aT (y ∧ z) + aT (y ∨ z) ≥ f (p ∧ q) + f (p ∨ q).

Thus f is submodular on the ﬁnite distributive lattice D. This closure is in diﬀerence variables; it
would be false for original cover variables.

Uniform rational encoding. Let B = max {1} ∪ {⌈|be |⌉ : e ∈ E} , A0 = max(1, maxi ⌈|ai |⌉),
H ∗ = ⌈∥y ∗ ∥∞ ⌉ + n, and let k, q be the lcms of demand and objective denominators (empty lcm is
one). Every p has magnitude at most H ∗ . Anchor one coordinate at zero in each slice component
with no ﬁxed coordinate; boundedness makes its objective invariant under translation. The resulting
slice is pointed. At an optimal vertex each tight-edge component connects to a ﬁxed or zero anchor,
since otherwise it could be translated locally, contradicting full active rank. A tight spanning forest
therefore expresses every coordinate as an anchor plus at most n−1 signed demands. Its magnitude
is at most H ∗ + nB and its denominator divides k. Consequently

                               |f (p)| ≤ nA0 (H ∗ + nB),      den(f (p)) | kq.

The value encoding bound is uniform and polynomial in binary input size; log k and log q are at
most sums of input denominator lengths.


4     Polynomial-size domain interface and minimization
Make a node (i, t) for each i ∈ I, li < t ≤ hi , meaning pi ≥ t. The ground set W has size at
most 2n|I|. Encode A(p) = {(i, t) : li < t ≤ pi }. Union/intersection correspond to join/meet. The
feasible family C = {A(p) : p ∈ D} has explicit implications:
    1. Preﬁx arcs (i, t) → (i, t − 1) when t > li + 1.

                                                      3
    2. For an integer–integer edge put d = ⌈buv ⌉. The base condition pu ≥ lv + d is automatic if
       lv + d ≤ lu , forces node (u, lv + d) if in range, and makes the family empty if above hu .

    3. Each node (v, t) implies (u, t + d). Targets at most lu are automatic; targets above hu forbid
       (v, t); other targets give arcs.
These conditions are equivalent to p ∈ D, including the essential base condition when pv = lv . There
are O(n2 + n|E|) arcs/nodes and polynomial-bit labels, independently of numerical denominators.
    A member contains all forced nodes, avoids all forbidden nodes and is closed under arcs. Reach-
ability computes the smallest member and the smallest member containing any given node, or
detects its nonexistence. This is the ring-family interface of Schrijver [7, §6], supplied explicitly
rather than assumed from a value oracle. Equivalently compute the intersection and union of
members and the closure digraph on the remaining nodes, giving the lattice encoding of [8, §10.3].
    Apply established exact rational submodular minimization to F (A(p)) = f (p). The uniform
encoding bound above and [8, §10.2, problem 10.3.8 and its reduction] give polynomial oracle-bit
complexity on this lattice; [7, §6] supplies the classical combinatorial ring reduction as well. Count-
ing arithmetic operations alone would not suﬃce. All oracle calls here are polynomial-bit rational
LPs. A minimizing assignment and one optimizing completion give a global mixed minimizer by
Lemma 2. The full algorithm is polynomial in binary length; a strongly polynomial total LP-based
algorithm is not claimed.


5     The actual closed hull and facet complexity
Let H = nB + n, R = {r : ru − rv ≥ 0} and K = conv(T ∩ [−H, H]n ). These are mathematical
objects for a size argument, not an enumeration performed by the algorithm.
    For every objective bounded below on P , the objective annihilates component translation lin-
eality. Anchor one coordinate per graph component at zero. An optimal vertex of this pointed
relaxation has tight spanning trees connecting each component to its anchor. Its coordinates are
sums of at most n − 1 signed demands and have norm at most nB. Lemma 2 puts a mixed opti-
mum within norm H, uniformly over all bounded objectives. Thus every such objective attains its
minimum on T inside K.
    The bounded mixed set is a ﬁnite union of bounded rational slice polytopes, so K is a rational
polytope and K + R is a closed rational polyhedron. Every r ∈ R decomposes into nonnegative
multiples of integral signed threshold layers in R. For such an integral layer d and z ∈ T , z +md ∈ T
for all nonnegative integers m. Interpolation of consecutive steps puts z + td in conv(T ) for every
real t ≥ 0. The same holds for ﬁnite convex combinations, then ﬁnite sums of layers. Hence
K + R ⊂ conv(T ).
    If some z ∈ T lay outside K + R, strict separation would give a with aT z < inf w∈K+R aT w. The
right side is ﬁnite, so a is nonnegative on R and bounded below on P . The uniform small-optimum
conclusion makes its minimum on T equal its minimum on K, a contradiction. Therefore

                                         conv(T ) = K + R,

which proves closedness of the actual hull, without substituting its closure.
    Slice matrices consist of directed incidence rows, unit ﬁxings and integer box bounds, and are
totally unimodular. Each slice vertex lies in (1/k)Zn and has norm at most H. Thus K is generated
by ﬁnitely many such vertices; R is generated by integral threshold rays of norm at most one. The
hull is full-dimensional, since original cover variables contain a translated positive orthant in the
convex hull of large integral vectors. Homogenize point and ray generators as (v, 1), (r, 0) and

Every facet has n independent homogeneous generators on it; cofactors give an integral facet
inequality, including constant term, with coeﬃcients bounded by n![k(H + 1)]n . Lineality causes
no problem: spanning generators may include both signs of lineality directions.

facets or denominator residues are enumerated.


6    Strong separation, scope and validation
The optimization procedure returns exact feasible optimizing points or improving integral reces-
sion directions. Points need not be vertices. Rescale an improving ray to the normalization in
the strong-optimization interface of [8, Deﬁnitions 6.2.1–6.2.2]. Together with φ, this gives a well-
described rational polyhedron. The exact strong optimization–separation equivalence [8, Theo-
rem 6.4.9, pp. 179–180] therefore provides a polynomial-time strict separator or exact membership
conﬁrmation at any rational query. Undo the diagonal sign change to obtain Theorem 1. Un-
bounded polyhedra and boundary queries are included; no approximate membership or closure
surrogate is used.
    The separate subtree-hull Conjecture 2 of [1] remains unresolved here. No polynomial-size
extended formulation, facet classiﬁcation, arbitrary-real oracle result or full polynomial-optimizer
implementation is claimed. The ﬁnite exact checkers enumerate only small cases; they do not
implement LP, submodular minimization and GLS in polynomial time or prove complexity from
observed runtime. Independent adversarial families checked proximity, attainment and oracle bits,
and ring/hull/separation interfaces. The supplement records primary-source comparisons, exact
diagnostics and limitations of the bounded priority audit. Classical ingredients and earlier formu-
lations receive credit above; no ﬁrst-ever or exhaustive novelty certiﬁcate is asserted.
    AI tools were used extensively in solving, verifying, source auditing and preparing this note and
supporting materials. This is an unrefereed preprint. Independent adversarial reviews are AI re-
views; conventional human peer review, journal acceptance and formal proof-assistant certiﬁcation
