## Exact claim and scope

## 1. Sphere and exact pole witness

For a=k/q, the squared horizontal radius is4a(1-a), and height squared is(1-2a)^2; their sum is1. Distinct heights make distinct points. The dot product with z0=(0,0,1) is1-2k/q, so ||z0-zk||^2=4k/q and the k=1 chord gives the claimed upper bound exactly. No angular arithmetic is needed for this witness. At n=3,q=2 there is one pair and it has squared chord2. At n=4,q=3 and n=5,q=5, the same proof applies; q=1 is correctly excluded.

## 2. Elementary arithmetic lower bound

For any1<=m<q and any integer ell, choose the unique j>=2 with F_j<=m<F_(j+1); the repeated F1=F2=1 is handled by starting j at2. Since m<F_n, j<=n-1, so h=n-j>=1. The columns(F_j,F_(j-1)) and(F_(j+1),F_j) have determinant(-1)^(j-1), hence form a Z-basis. Write(m,ell)=S*column1+T*column2.

S cannot be0, since then the positive integer m<F_(j+1) would be a nonzero multiple of F_(j+1). Both coefficients positive give m>=F_j+F_(j+1), contradicting the upper bound; both negative give m<0. If T=0, S must be positive. Therefore either T=0,S>0 or S and T have opposite signs. The recurrence identities give mp-ell*q=(-1)^(j-1)*(S*F_h-T*F_(h-1)). Opposite signs prevent cancellation, so its magnitude is at least F_h. For h=1 the second Fibonacci factor is0 and S nonzero still gives at least1; there is no terminal gap. The addition identity q=F_j*F_(h+1)+F_(j-1)*F_h, together with F_(h+1)<=2F_h and F_(j-1)<=F_j, gives q<=3F_j*F_h<=3m*|mp-ell*q|. Thus the weaker product lower bound is independently established for every ell, without importing the stronger classical index theorem. Determinant/error/addition identities follow by induction from the same recurrence.

## 3. Geometric lower bound independent of planar distance

For0<=a<b<=1,d=b-a and c=cos(longitude difference), define U=2(a+b-2ab),V=4sqrt(a(1-a)b(1-b)). Expanding the spherical dot product gives D^2=2(U-cV). Direct algebra gives U-2d=4a(1-b)>=0 and V^2=U^2-4d^2. If c<=0, D^2>=2U>=4d. If0<c<1, U>V>=0 and U-cV>0. Expanding (U-cV)^2-4d^2(1-c^2) yields(V-cU)^2, so D^2>=4d*sqrt(1-c^2). No equal-area-map Lipschitz or distance-preservation claim appears; that would be an invalid shortcut near the poles.

## 4. Exhausting the longitude cases

For i<j let m=j-i and choose a nearest residue r=mp-ell*q with |r|<=q/2. Consecutive Fibonacci integers are coprime (Euclidean recurrence), so r is nonzero when0<m<q. Thus c cannot equal1. If c<=0, the geometry bound gives D^2>=4m/q>=4/q. If c>0, then0<|r|<q/4, and the longitude sine is positive in absolute value. Concavity on[0,pi/2] gives sin(2*pi*|r|/q)>=4|r|/q. Combining the geometric bound and arithmetic product estimate yields D^2>=16m|r|/q^2>=16/(3q)>4/q. The strictly positive margin means this branch cannot contain equality, including endpoint degeneracies of the sine estimate. The c=0 case belongs to the first branch; c=-1 causes no problem.

## 5. Equality and uniqueness

Equality in the c<=0 chain requires m=1 and U=2d. Since b=j/q<1 for the stated half-open index range, U-2d=4a(1-b) forces a=0. Therefore i=0,j=1. Conversely the pole computation attains equality. No additional condition cV=0 is required for sufficiency because V=0 at a=0. This establishes the full uniqueness assertion, not only the separation value. Including b=1 would change the equality analysis and the number of points; the half-open convention is load bearing.
