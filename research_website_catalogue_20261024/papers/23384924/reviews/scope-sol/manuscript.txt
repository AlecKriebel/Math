                                                  Abstract
         For normalized univalent functions with real Taylor coeﬃcients, the classical centered pre-
     Schwarzian disk can be strictly shrunk at every ﬁxed nonreal point, uniformly over the class.
     On the real diameter its classical radius remains sharp. We prove this qualitative answer to
     Hayman–Lingham Problem 6.86 by combining classical coeﬃcient equality, reﬂection symmetry
     of the equality domains, and compactness. The gap is uniform on compact nonreal sets. No
     explicit upper-gap formula or sharp radius is determined. The proof uses classical methods, and
     no historical ﬁrstness is claimed.

Montel’s theorem makes S a normal family. A locally uniform limit retains derivative 1 at zero
and is nonconstant. To check injectivity, ﬁx w ∈ D and apply Hurwitz to the zero-free holomorphic
divided diﬀerences (fn (z)−fn (w))/(z−w), extended at w by fn0 (w). Their limit cannot be identically
zero, since that would make the limit of fn constant. Thus the limit remains injective, with
nonzero derivative. Consequently S is compact, and reﬂection makes SR a closed compact subfamily.
Derivative convergence and a nonzero limiting derivative imply joint continuity of (f, z) 7→ |Af (z)|
at interior points. The ﬁxed-point maximum is attained, and the equality analysis above excludes
4 at every nonreal point. On SR × K joint continuity similarly gives an attained maximum below
4. The real Koebe maps prove sharpness on the real diameter. (For empty K the assertion is
vacuous.)

4    Scope, prior work, and veriﬁcation
The result is a qualitative application of classical equality rigidity and compactness to the ﬁxed-
point question. It determines neither an explicit positive upper gap nor the sharp function M (it),
its general extremizers, or the full variability region. The 2018 source’s dated no-progress update
does not certify present openness.
