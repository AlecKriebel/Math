\section*{Diffusion--equilibrium heterogeneity trade-off}
Let
\[
\chi_H=\frac{h_{\max}}{h_{\min}}
=\frac{x^*_{\max}}{x^*_{\min}}.
\]
This quantity measures equilibrium concentration-scale separation; it is not
interpreted as a universal measure of biological expense or implausibility.
For every interior chain species,
$h_Z/h_j\ge h_{\min}/h_{\max}=1/\chi_H$.  Therefore
\[
T(H)=8\sum_{j=2}^{m-1}\frac{h_Z}{h_j}
\ge\frac{8(m-2)}{\chi_H}.
\]
Combining this with $\chi_D>T(H)$ gives the topology-wide necessary inequality
for stationary crossings of $\widehat N_m$,
\[
\boxed{\chi_D\chi_H>8(m-2).}
\]
The product bound is sharp as an infimum in the homogeneously stable
realization space: $H=I$ is stable and the unit-equilibrium approaching
sequence gives products tending to $8(m-2)$.  Equal diffusion cannot
destabilize: $J-sdI$ simply shifts the stable nonzero homogeneous spectrum and
the conservation zero strictly to the left.

The product sharpness does not by itself construct a homogeneously stable
family balancing the two contrasts.  The separately certified
equilibrium-scaled family does so at square-root order by reallocating a fixed
product between $\chi_D$ and $\chi_H$; it does not determine the
constant-optimal global Pareto frontier.
