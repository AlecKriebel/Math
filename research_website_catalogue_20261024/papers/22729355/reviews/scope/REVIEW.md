## Precise mathematical scope

For every n ≥ 4, put m = n−1. The paper constructs one explicit **synthetic n-species, n+1-reaction classical mass-action topology**, with at most two molecules on both sides of every reaction. Its positive-equilibrium flux cone is parametrized by a,b > 0; every positive equilibrium Jacobian is J = A_m(a,b)H, where H = diag(h_i) has positive entries h_i = 1/x_i*. Rate constants are reconstructed from the chosen flux and equilibrium. This is not arbitrary kinetics, an arbitrary network topology, or a claim about an observed biochemical system.

The topology contains an inflow 0 → X₁ and a semipositive conserved quantity cᵀx, with c = (0,4,…,4,2,1). X₁ is omitted by this conservation law. Therefore “fixed integrated mass” means the integral of this specific nonnegative stoichiometric conservation law; it does not mean total mass of all species, a strictly positive conservation law, or a global bound on every concentration. The homogeneous Jacobian has a conservation zero eigenvalue, and homogeneous stability is tested on c⊥, not strict Hurwitz stability of the full n×n matrix.

For **all** positive-equilibrium realizations, every nonempty principal subsystem of order below n−1 is Hurwitz, while the X₁,…,X_m subsystem of order n−1 has a positive real eigenvalue. The proof covers the whole complex spectrum of the small blocks, not just the absence of positive real roots. This does not assert that every full realization is homogeneously stable. The upper endpoint n−1 in abstract subsystem classifications is old; the proposed addition is realizing it uniformly throughout this constrained mass-action topology.

For a homogeneously stable realization, the stationary positive-diagonal diffusion-ray crossing is exactly

    d_Z > 8 h_Z Σ_{j=2}^{n−2} d_j / h_j.

The determinant has a conservation factor s. Strict positivity of the remaining higher coefficients yields a unique positive crossing when the first remaining coefficient is negative. The condition concerns **stationary instability**; it does not exclude complex unstable eigenvalues or classify arbitrary wave instability outside that band. A finite Neumann interval has discrete modes, so a diffusion multiplier or interval length must be aligned with the crossing. No intrinsic finite wavelength selection under domain enlargement is asserted.

The fixed-equilibrium stationary contrast infimum is T(H) = 8h_ZΣ1/h_j and is **not attained**. Unit equilibrium gives 8(n−3). Across the topology χ_Dχ_H > 8(n−3), with χ_H the inverse-equilibrium/ordinary concentration contrast. The homogeneous-stability set is not completely characterized, and T(H)>1 is a necessary condition in the stable set, not its full characterization. The stationary linear infimum is not automatically the infimum for a stable nonlinear pattern branch.

The rational unit-equilibrium design gives a primary supercritical stationary bifurcation on (0,π) with Neumann boundary conditions. For each fixed dimension, small positive distance past onset gives positive patterns locally exponentially stable **within the fixed-integrated-mass class**. The proof requires homogeneous reduced stability, one simple first-mode zero, all remaining Fourier modes stable, a positive crossing derivative and negative cubic coefficient. It is not inferred from the determinant criterion alone. Reflection symmetry gives the odd normal form; there is no periodic translation-neutral family on this interval.

The scaled family changes H, the physical diagonal diffusion, the left vector and conservation gauge together. It is not an isospectral similarity or arbitrary free redistribution of diffusion constants. It proves both contrasts Θ(√n), matching the topology-specific stationary minimax **exponent**. It does not give the optimal leading constant or the complete Pareto frontier. The certified interval of the scaling parameter is sufficient, not the complete dynamical admissibility domain. Robustness is local for fixed dimension and includes retuning one scalar diffusion multiplier to the onset. There is no dimension-uniform stability radius or global attraction claim.

The supplement's m=3 near-threshold family has a positive cubic coefficient and is subcritical on the stated small-parameter interval. This is a useful boundary example for that family, not a proof of a universal gap between linear thresholds and stable-pattern thresholds.

## Contribution and primary credit

Relevant original passages were read from fresh downloaded primary PDFs, with URLs/hashes in `primary-sources/`. This is a bounded comparison; none is a substitute for checking the new all-dimension formulas and certificates.

- **Satnoianu–Menzinger–Maini (2000), Turing instabilities in general systems:** abstract and subsystem-stability theorem/proof passages read. General n-component theory already allows responsible subsystem sizes up to n−1. The current work must credit this endpoint and distinguish its all-realizations, binary-complex construction.
- **Anma–Sakamoto–Yoneda (2012), Unstable Subsystems Cause Turing Instability:** Theorem 1.1 and associated stationary/wave remarks read. The distinction between stationary and oscillatory instability is established background, not a new distinction discovered here.
- **Diego–Marcon–Müller–Sharpe (2018), Physical Review X:** abstract, introduction and graph/characteristic-polynomial framework inspected. Topological control of diffusion constraints and pattern properties already exists. Their reaction-Jacobian graph framework is broader than the present fixed classical mass-action realization. The new page must not claim the first topology-based Turing design.
- **Haas–Goldstein (2021), Physical Review Letters:** abstract and setting inspected. Random reaction–diffusion Jacobian thresholds and collective behavior provide important motivation but do not give this deterministic all-positive-realization mass-action construction or its local nonlinear branch theorem.
- **Conradi–Mincheva–Uecker, arXiv:2605.16049:** monomial steady-state parametrization, stationary-instability theorem interface and numerical example/conclusions inspected. It supplies closely related mass-action/steady-state methods and a nine-species application, including numerical nonlinear branches. The present proposed addition is the explicit dimension-indexed topology with exact thresholds and separately certified local branch signs, not the first use of monomial equilibria in pattern formation.
- **Designing reaction-cross-diffusion systems with Turing and wave instabilities, arXiv:2409.06860v2:** introduction and theorem interfaces inspected. Designing generally nondiagonal diffusion matrices for arbitrary stable reaction matrices is a different admissible design class. Its freedom must not be conflated with this fixed topology and strictly positive diagonal diffusion.
- **Crandall–Rabinowitz (1971), Theorem 1.7:** original simple-eigenvalue bifurcation hypotheses and beginning of proof read. Kernel/cokernel dimension and transversality are imported classical conditions. Henry's parabolic center-manifold/stability framework and Kato's perturbation framework are explicit textbook imports; their complete books were not reacquired or independently reproved here.

The claim worth presenting is the assembly of (i) uniform maximal proper subsystem size under mass-action constraints, (ii) an exact stationary diffusion law, and (iii) independent nonlinear stable designs with an exponent-optimal trade-off for this topology. A claim of new universal Turing classification, biological applicability, first collective instability, or globally optimal heterogeneity would exceed the evidence.
