## Exact claim and strongest verified result

For every integer m >= 3, the specified network has n=m+1 species and m+2 binary-complex mass-action reactions. Every positive-equilibrium realization has Jacobian A_m(a,b)H with a,b>0 and H positive diagonal. Every nonempty principal block of order <m is Hurwitz, whereas the m-species X core has a positive real eigenvalue. This realizes maximal proper-subsystem instability order n-1 throughout that positive realization space.

On the subset whose homogeneous Jacobian is Hurwitz on its stoichiometric subspace, a positive diagonal diffusion ray sD has a nonzero stationary zero precisely when

    d_Z > 8 h_Z sum_{j=2}^{m-1}(d_j/h_j).

The positive threshold is unique, simple, and transverse. Below it the ray has exactly one positive real eigenvalue; this criterion by itself does not rule out unstable nonreal eigenvalues. At fixed H, the stationary-crossing diffusion contrast has the strict, unattained infimum T(H)=8h_Z sum(1/h_j). The product lower bound is chi_D chi_H>8(m-2).

The paper separately constructs explicit realizations on (0,pi), with homogeneous Neumann boundaries and fixed integrated conserved mass, for which the first spatial mode is the unique critical mode, all complementary spectrum is stable, the reduced cubic is negative, and two nearby reflection-related positive steady patterns are locally exponentially asymptotically stable in H^1 for sufficiently small positive diffusion-unfolding parameter. This holds for every fixed m and, in the equilibrium-scaled family, every L in the stated closed interval [L0,L1]. Its two physical contrasts are Theta(sqrt(m)) at L0; this establishes optimal simultaneous exponents within this topology, not optimal constants or the complete global Pareto frontier.

No substantive mathematical defect was identified in these claims.

## Reconstruction and proof audit

### 1. Network, complete realizations, and principal subsystems

I reconstructed the source/target vectors, stoichiometric matrix, mass-action field, Jacobian, and reaction Hessian directly from the indexed reactions, without importing the submitted helper modules. The chain is empty at m=3, and the reaction count and formulas still agree. The two-dimensional flux kernel is generated by the long-circuit unit flux and the equal forward/reverse flux of the final pair; rank is m. The conserved covector is (0,4,...,4,2,1), so conservation is semipositive, not a claim of a strictly positive mass weighting. The inflow into the zero-weight species is compatible with the conservation law.

Positive fluxes and arbitrary positive equilibria are realized by k_r=v_r/(x*)^{y_r}. Differentiation gives J=A_m(a,b)H. Conversely every positive realization has exactly those two flux parameters, so the parameterization does not omit a positive-equilibrium branch.

The induced strongly connected components below order m consist of negative singletons, either complete long cycle, or principal subsets of the boundary triad. The two long cycles exclude the closed right half-plane by strict diagonal-product versus cycle-product bounds. The triad has positive cubic coefficients and a strictly positive 14-term Routh-Hurwitz gap. Deleting the edge at b=2a cannot introduce another component type. The Schur remainder of the core has determinant 2a^2b, giving signed determinant -2a^(m-1)b times the positive column product. The characteristic polynomial therefore crosses zero on the positive real axis. Fresh exact tests cover every smaller principal set for m=3,4,5,6, including b=2a and nonconstant H; these finite checks supplement the all-m graph argument.

### 2. Diffusion-ray theorem and sharp linear contrast

The general theorem requires n>=2, det J=0, positive signed principal minors through order n-2, a positive sum of the order-(n-1) signed minors, and positive **diagonal** D. Expanding det(sD-J) gives s times a polynomial whose coefficients after its constant term are strictly positive. A positive root therefore exists iff beta_1<0 and is unique and simple. Differentiating det(lambda I+sD-J) in lambda gives a strictly positive result for lambda>=0: the order-(n-1) contribution is its positive sum, and the lower-order contributions are positive. This establishes both the positive-real-eigenvalue band and spectral simplicity at threshold, with no hidden all-spectrum claim.

For the network the complete omission table has one negative entry (omit Z), positive interior omissions, and zero omissions at X1 and Xm. It produces the factor eight in the displayed criterion. Homogeneous relative stability makes the conservation zero simple and gives T(H)>1. The lower contrast bound follows by replacing every interior d_j by d_min; setting all X diffusivities to one and d_Z=T(H)+epsilon proves the infimum. Equal diffusivities cannot cause this stationary instability. Equality in the threshold inequality gives no positive threshold, and the infimum is not attained. A fresh n=2 example also checks the low-dimensional boundary of the general matrix theorem.

The scalar polynomial can be bracketed and bisected with exact/sign-certified coefficients; no polynomial-time bit-complexity claim or algorithm for unspecified noncomputable real inputs is asserted or inferred.

### 3. Explicit stable design and all spatial modes

For unit flux/equilibrium, the stated critical vectors and positive diagonal diffusion satisfy both nullvector equations exactly. Their nonzero left-right pairing rules out a generalized critical eigenvector, and both pairing signs yield positive transversality for the convention D(mu)=(1-mu)D.

The homogeneous determinant identity reduces nonzero right-half-plane roots to the 35-term modulus certificate. The spatial determinant is QF-G. At t>=1, its chain product has the required lower modulus bound, and the 77-term polynomial is strictly positive except at lambda=0,t=1. The derivative of the determinant at zero is positive. Thus the proof excludes unstable complex modes too, for this construction; it is not extrapolating the real-eigenvalue diffusion-ray theorem.

I independently regenerated all four modulus source polynomials, with 35,77,84,22 terms respectively, and checked exact rational coefficient signs, even imaginary powers, support counts, and their origin equality. Parameter-polynomial coefficients were checked coefficientwise. The scaled-family homogeneous certificate uses 5/4, with its precise coefficientwise boundary 256B-320; the construction does not claim that this certificate boundary is a necessary dynamical threshold.

### 4. Cubic coefficient and nonlinear branch

The quadratic reaction expansion has no cubic tensor. Fourier projection gives forcing -B(r,r)/4 in the zero and second harmonics and the factor 1/2 in the cubic contraction. The homogeneous correction is uniquely fixed by the conserved-mass gauge. The second harmonic uses A-4D, not A-2D. Direct reaction-derived linear solves at m=3,4,5,7,9 produce negative cubics and positive transversality; intentionally wrong Fourier factors fail.

The all-m reduction is supported by the printed chain recurrence, nonsingular four-boundary-equation solve, exact finite polynomial sums, and telescoping reciprocal sum. I read the standalone generic recurrence verifier and checked its mechanism; it does not infer a universal formula by interpolation from a few dimensions. The shifted-polynomial positivity and harmonic-sum bound prove the numerator sign for all m. The published symbolic replay independently recomputes those identities, and the fresh controls use direct Hessian contractions instead of the submitted closed-form harmonic formulas.

On the real fixed-mass spaces, only the homogeneous Fourier coefficient is constrained by c; the critical cos(xi) function has zero mean automatically. The high-mode inverse is O(k^-2), proving the closed Fredholm range from H_N^2 to L^2, with one-dimensional kernel and cokernel. The Crandall-Rabinowitz transversality pairing is (pi/2)ell^TDr, which is nonzero. Spatial reflection preserves the mass space, commutes with the equation, and reverses the critical coordinate. A symmetry-preserving local center-manifold choice therefore gives an odd reduced field and the two reflected branches.

The sectorial setting is valid in one dimension: H^1 is an algebra and embeds into C^0, the quadratic nonlinearity is smooth H^1->L^2, and positive diagonal Neumann diffusion has compact resolvent and half-power space H^1. A scalar time rescaling can fix the leading diffusion operator when treating mu as a center parameter. Small positive branches remain pointwise positive by H^2->C^0. Their center eigenvalue is -2 eta mu+O(mu^2), and the complementary gap persists by a bounded reaction perturbation and uniform high-frequency coercivity. This establishes local exponential stability within the same integrated mass class, not stability against changes of conserved mass.

### 5. Equilibrium scaling, gauges, and sharp scope

The physical equation is f_m(Hx)+(1-mu)H Delta x''. Its physical reaction Jacobian is A_m H; in normalized variables Hx it is H A_m. These are similar, but arbitrary row scaling is not presumed to preserve stability. The manuscript proves it anew by the 84-term spatial and 22-term homogeneous certificates on the stated L interval. The special m=3 homogeneous cubic is checked separately.

The dynamic adjoint is H^-1 ell. The mass covector becomes H^-1 c, so the zero-harmonic correction changes by tau rho while the second harmonic does not. I independently checked those transformations and a deliberately wrong unscaled adjoint. The universal numerator bounds N_ref>1/100, -1/10<S<0, and tau<1/20 give N(L)>1/200; the scaled denominator is strictly negative. The derivative formulas used to bound tau have the correct signs on the required harmonic interval and L range.

The diffusion and equilibrium extrema, fixed contrast product, nonempty certified interval, and L0 minimization are correct. The paper expressly leaves the complete homogeneous stable realization set and the constant-optimal global Pareto frontier undetermined. The near-threshold m=3 example has positive cubic on 0<epsilon<=10^-3; the exact verifier checks the whole interval and all t>=1 by coefficientwise substitutions. It is a countercontrol to automatic supercriticality, not a proof of a universal nonlinear gap.

The robustness claim is local and retuned: for each fixed m,L, small admissible parameter and interval changes admit a nearby scalar critical diffusion multiplier. It does not say arbitrary perturbed parameters remain critical or claim a dimension-uniform neighborhood.
