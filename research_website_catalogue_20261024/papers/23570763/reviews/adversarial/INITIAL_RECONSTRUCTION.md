## Independent algebra reconstruction

Construct a graded abelian algebra B_n directly, for n >= 2: degree zero Z; degree one freely on x_i,y_i; degree two freely on u_i,v_i; higher degrees zero. Set x_i^2=u_i, x_i y_j=v_i if i != j, and every other degree-one product zero; all degree-two times positive-degree products zero. This multiplication is commutative and associative: every triple of positive-degree elements is zero. Each proposed ideal generator maps to zero. Conversely all surviving quadratic monomials reduce to these 2n labels, so the induced quotient surjects onto B_n and is spanned by its basis. This gives mutually inverse degreewise maps and proves integral freeness, including saturation, without using a characteristic-dependent rank test. At n=2 each v_i has exactly one off-diagonal representative, so there is no missing three-index relation. At n=1 there are no off-diagonal representatives: the independent algebra is Z{1,x,y,x^2}, with all other quadratic products and all cubics zero.

For l=sum a_i x_i+b_i y_i and B=sum b_i, directly multiplying yields l*x_j=a_j u_j+(B-b_j)v_j and l*y_j=sum_{i != j} a_i v_i. Therefore its matrix has upper-right zero and diagonal blocks D(a) and D(a)(J-I), so determinant=(prod a_i)^2 det(J-I). A division-free determinant derivation expands each column as all-ones minus its standard vector. Two chosen all-ones columns give zero. The zero-chosen term is (-1)^n, each of n single-chosen terms (-1)^(n-1), yielding (-1)^(n-1)(n-1). This identity is over Z and specializes even when n=0 in K; no eigenbasis or 1/n is used.

At a bad width, z=sum y_j is nonzero because there are no degree-one relations, while x_i*z=(n-1)v_i and y_i*z=0. Thus z is a common kernel for all linear forms, over K and every extension; failure cannot be escaped by non-invariant coefficients or extension-field points. In fact, for an arbitrary degree-one socle vector q=sum alpha_i x_i+beta_i y_i, multiplying by x_j forces alpha_j=0 and S-beta_j=0 (S=sum beta_i), so all beta_j=S and (n-1)S=0. Hence the degree-one socle is exactly K*z at bad widths and zero at good widths. The nonzero independent u_j are essential in forcing alpha_j=0.

At a good width choose l=sum x_i. Its degree-one matrix diag(I,J-I) is invertible. Its square is sum u_i, including characteristic two: squares survive and cross-products vanish. It is nonzero by coordinate independence, not by the scalar n. Thus the only extra nontrivial SLP map, from degree zero to degree two, is injective. Degree >=3 targets are zero and automatically maximal rank. At n=1, x has ranks 1 from degree 0 to1, 1 from1to2, and x^2 nonzero. This separately handles the exceptional initial width which the formula n-1=0 would otherwise misclassify.
