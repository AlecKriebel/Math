   An ideal I of R is S∞ -invariant if σ · f ∈ I whenever f ∈ I. If I is a S∞ -invariant
ideal, then the sequence of ideals In = I ∩ Rn are symmetric with respect to the action
of Sn on Rn . Moreover the chain satisfies the property that for m ≤ n, σ ∈ Sn and
f ∈ Im , it follows that σ · f ∈ In . A sequence {In }n∈N of ideals that has this property
is called a S∞ -invariant chain. Conversely, given a S∞ -invariant chain {In }n∈N , the
colimit I = limn∈N In is a S∞ -invariant ideal.
