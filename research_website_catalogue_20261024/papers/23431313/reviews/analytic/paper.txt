       A torus-collar proof of the real-spectrum compact-support
                  classiﬁcation for Nijenhuis operators
                                            Alec Kriebel
                                       Independent researcher
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                   15 October 2026, version 1.0


                                               Abstract
         We give a complete smooth compact-support proof of the classiﬁcation asked for in the
     historical integrable-systems problem source: a real Jordan type occurs on a nonempty open
     subset of Rn for a compactly supported smooth Nijenhuis operator exactly when its spectrum
     is real. The construction realizes arbitrary speciﬁed numerical real Jordan data in a commuting
     local frame. On a same-dimensional torus collar, the torsion equation reduces to (A − λI)A0 = 0;
     an ordered ﬁnite path removes every Jordan block and reaches an exact zero band. The nonreal
     obstruction and earlier semisimple and two-dimensional examples are prior results. We include
     the precise zero-point rigidity deduction needed for necessity. The historical priority of the
     full result and of this construction remains unestablished. Extensive AI tools assisted solving,
     veriﬁcation and writing. This preprint is unrefereed and has no external human peer review or
     formal proof-assistant certiﬁcation.


1    Statement, historical target and attribution
For a smooth real ﬁeld L of tangent-space endomorphisms, use the convention

                    NL (X, Y ) = L2 [X, Y ] + [LX, LY ] − L[LX, Y ] − L[X, LY ].

An operator is Nijenhuis when NL = 0. Jordan type here means the block sizes and grouping by
eigenvalue; our existence statement also ﬁxes any desired numerical real eigenvalues.

Theorem 1. For every n ≥ 1 and every prescribed real Jordan matrix J with entirely real spectrum,
there exists a globally C ∞ , compactly supported real Nijenhuis operator on Rn whose matrix is J
in a smooth commuting local frame on a nonempty open domain. Conversely, every eigenvalue at
every point of a compactly supported smooth real Nijenhuis operator on Rn is real.

    The type, rank and eigenvalues may change outside the realization domain. The theorem
prescribes neither the matrix in the ambient Cartesian frame nor global regularity, and is a smooth,
not a real-analytic, statement. The zero matrix is permitted.
    The target is Problem 5.11 on p. 29 of the 14 January 2020 author-v2 of Bolsinov–Matveev–
Miranda–Tabachnikov [1]; its 10 April 2018 author-v1 counterpart is Problem 4.10 on p. 37. The
original journal work appeared in 2018. A corpus label “5.9” is not the locator in either inspected
version. The full wording asks for necessary and suﬃcient conditions for compact support with
a prescribed unchanged algebraic type on a domain. Theorem 1 answers that printed question,
without asserting that it remained open worldwide before this note.

3    A same-dimensional torus collar and extension
For every d ≥ 1, the torus Td embeds as a two-sided smooth hypersurface in Rd+1 . Here is the
needed all-dimensional induction. Start with a circle in R2 . If f : Td−1 ,→ Rd is an oriented embed-
ded hypersurface, it has a global unit normal ν. Inside Rd+1 its rank-two normal bundle has the
explicit frame (ν, 0), (0, 1). By compactness and the tubular-neighborhood theorem, a suﬃciently
small normal disk bundle is embedded. Its boundary is Td−1 × S 1 = Td , with parametrization
                                          (                           )
                              F (p, φ) = f (p) + ε cos φ ν(p), ε sin φ .

The tubular coordinates recover p, cos φ, sin φ, proving injection and immersion. Compactness
makes this an embedding. A global unit normal is (cos φ ν(p), sin φ): orthogonality follows from
ν · df = ν · dν = 0. This veriﬁes two-sidedness for the next step and completes the induction.
     For n ≥ 2 a thin normal collar of the embedded Tn−1 ⊂ Rn is diﬀeomorphic, after rescal-
ing, to Tn−1 × (−1, 1). Push the preceding tensor through this diﬀeomorphism. Its frame still
commutes, and torsion vanishes by naturality. Its support lies in the image of the compact set
Tn−1 × [−3/4, 3/4], strictly inside the open collar. Extend it by zero to Rn . Each collar-boundary
or exterior point has a neighborhood outside that compact support. Thus the extension is globally
smooth, and its torsion is zero by locality. No exterior angular frame is required. The central band
is nonempty and open; a connected coordinate box inside it is a realization domain.
     For n = 1, a compactly supported smooth scalar function equal to the desired eigenvalue on
an interval, multiplied by Id, suﬃces. The alternating vector-valued two-form NL vanishes in
dimension one. The zero matrix is also realized by the zero tensor. This proves the existence
direction in every stated boundary case.


4    The credited nonreal obstruction
BKM’s author-v2 Theorem 6.1 [2], pp. 41–43, states nonreal spectral persistence with constant alge-
braic multiplicity on a closed connected smooth manifold. It has no analyticity or regularity hypoth-
esis. The following weaker consequence is all that is needed here. We give its maximum-principle
justiﬁcation using their local splitting and canonical complex/holomorphic results (Theorems 3.1
and 3.3).

                                                  3
Lemma 2. A smooth real Nijenhuis operator on a closed connected manifold which vanishes at
one point has entirely real spectrum everywhere.

Proof. Suppose otherwise. Let µ(x) be the largest positive imaginary part of an eigenvalue, with
value zero for an all-real spectrum. Roots depend continuously on polynomial coeﬃcients, so µ is
continuous. On the compact manifold it has a positive maximum µ0 . Among roots on the line
Im z = µ0 at maximum points choose the largest algebraic multiplicity m0 . The locus E having such
a root of multiplicity m0 is nonempty and closed: along a convergent sequence choose a convergent
subsequence of those roots; vanishing polynomial derivatives shows multiplicity at least m0 , and
maximality gives equality.
    At p ∈ E, isolate the root λ0 and its conjugate by the local splitting theorem. On this real block
the canonical complex structure makes its complex characteristic factor P (z) = z m0 +a1 z m0 −1 +· · ·+
am0 holomorphic in the block’s complex coordinates and independent of complementary coordinates.
At p, P (z) = (z − λ0 )m0 . All nearby roots have imaginary part at most µ0 . Therefore − Im a1 ≤
m0 µ0 with equality at p. The holomorphic maximum principle makes a1 constant locally. Every
root consequently has imaginary part µ0 . The shifted polynomial P (w+iµ0 ) has only real roots. Its
coeﬃcients are real-valued holomorphic functions and hence constant. Thus P remains (z − λ0 )m0
locally. The complementary block can be empty; otherwise independence of its coordinates gives
the same conclusion on the product neighborhood. Hence E is open. Connectedness gives E = M ,
contradicting the zero point.

    For compactly supported L on Rn , identify Rn with a standard chart of S n and extend by zero
near inﬁnity. Compact support makes this a smooth Nijenhuis ﬁeld on closed connected S n , zero
on a neighborhood of inﬁnity. Lemma 2 excludes nonreal spectrum, completing Theorem 1.
    The attribution is substantive: both the obstruction and its local splitting/holomorphic mech-
anism are prior. Our argument avoids relying on the ﬁnal polynomial-reduction sentence of the
author-v2 proof, whose literal count of removed nonreal pairs is imprecise. For example, rotation
blocks with spectra {±i, ±i/2} all acquire real eigenvalues under z 7→ z 2 + 1, although two original
pairs were nonreal. This is not a counterexample to the theorem; it explains why the exact applica-
tion is proved by the preceding maximum argument. We do not certify every step of the stronger
persistence proof or identify the unread Advances in Mathematics publisher body with author-v2
bytes.


5    Relation to prior constructions and veriﬁcation
The arbitrary-type point construction of BKM Theorem 4.5 and its remark is generically semisimple
in independent characteristic-coeﬃcient coordinates. Repeated-root conditions there do not supply
a ﬁxed nonsemisimple type on an open domain. In Geometry III [3], the actual journal regular-
layer theorem is Theorem 1.10 (author-v1 Theorem 1.4). It realizes regular layers near a nonzero
nilpotent block, rather than arbitrary repeated blocks with a compact zero transition. The journal
and arXiv numberings must be distinguished.
    Geometry IV [4] does allow singular or zero output symmetries of a regular generator. Its
functional coeﬃcients nevertheless obey compatibility equations. In its size-three nilpotent formula
an unwrapped angular coordinate multiplies a transverse derivative, so naive periodization does
not provide the collar construction. This observation does not exclude a diﬀerent use of that
framework. Local constant-type or spectral splitting results and ﬁxed functional calculus likewise
do not by themselves supply compact support: a product block sum retains unbounded cylinders
