Its 2013 author PDF §8 (pages 28–29) repeats the natural h^2-bit adjacency encoding and the few-reversal target restriction. Both state an O(h^2) zero-reversal nondeterministic source cost without specifying a strict total malformed-block convention in that discussion. The binary note correctly credits these sources, acknowledges their different target restriction, and confines its cubic optimality statement to its own strict total language. It would be incorrect to interpret its cubic lower bound as applying to every binary encoding or to the language implicit in those two short discussions.

The actual R816 HTML's complete visible authored and machine-readable body was read. It describes an intermediate alphabet {0,1,#,$}, row separators, a block terminator, and a 3h-state source with behavior defined away from canonical words by that automaton. A uniform two-bit coding gives at most 9h binary states. Its deterministic macro pullback costs at most 2s+2 and imports Adeogun–Kapoutsis's quadratic obstruction, yielding s>=h(h+1)/8-1. The record labels evidence self-reported/source-only, has literal formal_statement:null, and explicitly says the resulting bound is quadratic. Thus it is relevant prior coding/pullback work and not independent verification of either an exponential input or this paper's precise strict-block cubic result. Different total-language conventions explain why its linear source count does not contradict the h^3 fooling set here.
