               A localized Gaussian-witness lower bound
                  for fractional iterated area on uniform grids
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                         Independent Researcher
                                        ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                     14 October 2026 Version 1.0


                                                  Abstract
         For two independent standard fractional Brownian motions with the same ﬁxed Hurst pa-
     rameter H ∈ (1/4, 1), we give a localized Gaussian-witness proof that every square-integrable
                                                         RT
     estimator of the canonical iterated integral XT = 0 Bs1 dBs2 , based on both complete uniform
     grids of n observations on [0, T ], has root mean-square error at least cH T 2H n1/2−2H . The posi-
     tive constant is explicit and depends only on H; the inequality holds for every T > 0 and integer
     n ≥ 1, including nonlinear estimators. Smooth compactly supported covariance paths, a Fourier
     periodization upper Gram bound, and an exact deterministic area pairing provide the witness.
     The Brownian optimum and the smooth asymptotic rate are prior results. Fourier localization
     and Gaussian projection have established antecedents; historical theorem and method priority,
     full novelty, and worldwide current-open status remain unveriﬁed. The note uses canonical-area
     existence and suﬃcient L2 convergence, without endorsing exact Euler-error coeﬃcients of the
     pinned arXiv v1.

Keywords: fractional Brownian motion; iterated integral; Gaussian projection; approximation
lower bound; uniform sampling.

iterated integral and vector observations. Its historical conjecture does not establish present-day

worldwide openness. The exact Brownian optimum is T /(2 n), in the Clark–Cameron lineage
as recorded in [1, 3]; our constant at H = 1/2 is weaker. Neuenkirch–Shalaiko [3] prove positive

    The auxiliary isonormal directions need not be path-measurable in an originally supplied model.
To justify the transfer, ﬁnite left sums and observations have the same joint law in the representation
and the original model; their L2 limits preserve that joint law. Every L2 variable measurable with
respect to the ﬁnite observation vector is a Borel function of it up to null sets. The proved bound
transfers for every such function, so availability of Y on the enlarged representation is suﬃcient.

6    Boundaries and veriﬁcation status
The proof includes n = 1 directly and every T > 0 through cell scaling. At H = 1/4, the diagonal
in (9) is T /4, so this canonical bridge does not extend there. At H = 1, DH diverges and fBm
is linear, making the iterated integral recoverable from endpoints. These diﬀerent mechanisms
preclude endpoint extrapolation. No uniform positivity as H approaches an endpoint is asserted.
     The polygonal identity gives I12 + I21 = BT1 BT2 . The halved antisymmetric area AT = (I12 −
I21 )/2 = XT − BT1 BT2 /2 has the same optimal conditional residual, since the product is observed.
The unhalved antisymmetric diﬀerence has twice that residual. Theorem 1 uses XT throughout.
