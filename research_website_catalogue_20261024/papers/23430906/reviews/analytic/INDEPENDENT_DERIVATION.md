## 1. Literal target

For each *fixed* H in (1/4,1), two independent centered Gaussian processes B^1,B^2 have covariance R_H(s,t)=(s^(2H)+t^(2H)-|s-t|^(2H))/2. The target X_T is the L² limit of canonical polygonal integrals integral_0^T B_s^1 dB_s^2. Both component observations at kT/n, 1<=k<=n, are present; B_0=0 is deterministic. For all T>0, integers n>=1, and every real F in L² of the sigma-field generated by those 2n observations, the theorem asserts

    ||X_T-F||_2 >= c_H T^(2H) n^(1/2-2H),
    c_H = 2 pi a / [D_H sqrt(Pbar_f Pbar_g)] >0,
    a=4/45045,
    D_H=integral_R |exp(i xi)-1|² |xi|^(-1-2H) dxi,
    alpha=2H+1,
    Pbar_r=pi^alpha L_r²+2 N_r² pi^(alpha-6)[1+1/(2(5-alpha))],
    L_r=||r||_1, N_r=||r'''||_1,
    phi=x^4(1-x)^4 1_[0,1], f=phi', g=phi.

No sharp constant, normalized-error limit, limiting distribution, endpoint H, uniform-in-H constant, nonuniform/adaptive grid, correlated pair, or unequal-H pair is asserted. “n observations” means n observations in each coordinate, including T. The bound also holds for the conditional mean, and consequently is a lower bound for the optimal root mean square error.

## 2. A tensor/projection proof of the conditional-error interpretation

This gives a structural check distinct from treating a proposed numerical certificate as authority.

Let K be the *real* Hilbert space of conjugate-symmetric complex L² functions, with real-valued inner product integral u conjugate(v). Put K_t(xi)=D_H^(-1/2)(exp(itxi)-1)|xi|^(-H-1/2). Near zero the integrand defining D_H is O(|xi|^(1-2H)); at infinity it is O(|xi|^(-1-2H)). Thus 0<D_H<infinity for 0<H<1. Changing variable shows ||K_t-K_s||²=|t-s|^(2H); polarization gives the exact standard covariance. Let W_1,W_2 be independent real isonormal processes on K.

The map J(u tensor v)=W_1(u)W_2(v) extends as a real isometry from K tensor K to L² because independence gives E[J(u tensor v)J(u' tensor v')]=<u,u'><v,v'>. There is **no factor two** or symmetric-tensor convention here: the two Gaussian coordinates are independent. Let

    tau_m=sum_(k=0)^(m-1) K_(kT/m) tensor (K_((k+1)T/m)-K_(kT/m)).

Once left sums converge to the canonical X_T, tau_m is Cauchy by this isometry and its limit tau satisfies J(tau)=X_T.

Let E=span{K_(kT/n):1<=k<=n}; P is its orthogonal projection. Gaussian orthogonality makes W_i(E-perp) independent of the observations, and the two Gaussian processes remain independent. On a simple tensor, conditioning gives E[J(u tensor v)|G]=W_1(Pu)W_2(Pv). Conditional expectation and P tensor P are contractions, so this holds on all tensors. Hence

    E[X_T|G]=J((P tensor P)tau),
    inf_(F in L²(G)) ||X_T-F||_2 = ||tau-(P tensor P)tau||.

The second equality uses the elementary Pythagorean identity for conditional expectation, valid also for arbitrary nonlinear F. It does not assume optimal estimators are linear or quadratic in advance. Any test tensor w in E-perp tensor E-perp therefore yields

    ||X_T-F||_2 >= |<tau,w>|/||w||

provided w is nonzero. The positive pairing below proves that nonzero condition.

## 3. The stochastic input can be reduced directly to canonical existence

We do not need exact Euler-error coefficients or a long-lag asymptotic theorem. Assume the established canonical polygonal L² construction of X_T for H>1/4. For the polygonal integral P_m and the left sum L_m, direct deterministic cell integration gives

    P_m-L_m=Q_m=(1/2) sum Delta_k B^1 Delta_k B^2.

This is an exact finite identity. Coordinate independence and stationary Gaussian increment covariance imply

    E Q_m² = T^(4H) m^(-4H)/4 *
       [m+2 sum_(r=1)^(m-1) (m-r) rho_H(r)²],
    rho_H(r)=[|r+1|^(2H)+|r-1|^(2H)-2|r|^(2H)]/2.

For r>=2 the second finite difference of x^(2H), written as a triangular weighted integral of its second derivative, has absolute value <=H|2H-1|(r-1)^(2H-2). The r=1 term is finite. Therefore E Q_m² is O_H(T^(4H)m^(1-4H)) for H<3/4, O_H(T³m^(-2)(1+log m)) at H=3/4, and O_H(T^(4H)m^(-2)) for H>3/4. All tend to zero for H>1/4. Thus canonical polygonal existence alone implies L_m -> X_T in L². This avoids any circular use of the paper's disputed exact Euler coefficient.

The independent-coordinate Brownian case gives rho(r)=0 at nonzero integer lags and E Q_m²=T²/(4m). Its usual Itô integral coincides with the canonical integral because the mutual quadratic variation vanishes.

The paper's *additional* analytic-area bridge can also be checked without exact coefficients: Chen's relation expresses X_T-L_m as the sum of m scaled local areas; self-similarity and stationarity give equation (5). For r>=2 the covariance of those local areas is a fourfold integral of two off-diagonal covariance derivatives. If H<1/2 both derivative factors have negative sign, so their product is positive. All relevant separations lie in [r-1,r+1]. The two triangular time domains have total product volume 1/4; hence

    0 <= c_r <= H²(2H-1)² (r-1)^(4H-4)/4.

Same-cell and adjacent-cell terms need only c_0 finite and |c_1|<=c_0. Summing gives exactly the sufficient vanishing regimes claimed in equations (5)-(7). This argument requires the canonical/analytic identification supplied by its cited construction; our direct Q_m route above requires only canonical existence and proves the needed left-sum interface independently.

## 4. Construct covariance paths with the exact Fourier sign

Write hat h(xi)=integral exp(-itxi)h(t)dt. Define

    u_h(xi)=sqrt(D_H)/(2pi) |xi|^(H+1/2) conjugate(hat h(xi)).

Real h gives conjugate symmetry of u_h. Its weighted L² membership follows once integral |xi|^(2H+1)|hat h|² is finite. Direct substitution in <K_t,u_h> gives (2pi)^(-1) integral (exp(itxi)-1)hat h(xi)dxi=h(t)-h(0). The conjugation cannot be omitted: doing so changes h(t) to h(-t) in general and destroys the intended positive-time covariance bumps.

The zero extensions of f,g have zero traces through their second derivative. Therefore they lie in W^(3,1)(R), with third weak derivatives equal to the displayed interior polynomials and no endpoint delta masses. Three integrations by parts give |hat r(eta)|<=min(L_r,N_r|eta|^(-3)). This proves absolute Fourier inversion as well as weighted L² integrability for alpha=2H+1 in (3/2,3): infinity exponent alpha-6<-3 and zero exponent alpha>0 suffice. The finite bound in fact holds for the entire claimed open H interval with room to spare.

Let Delta=T/n and r_j(t)=Delta^H r(t/Delta-j). Then hat r_j=Delta^(H+1)exp(-ijDelta xi)hat r(Delta xi), and h_j(0)=0. All covariance paths vanish at every sample time, including 0 and T, so u_(r_j) belongs to E-perp exactly. There is no approximate invisibility or conditioning on a subset of the observations.

## 5. Periodic Bessel bound; dependence across cells is allowed

Set w_r(eta)=|eta|^alpha|hat r(eta)|². Scaling cancels Delta since 2H+2-alpha-1=0. For real finite coefficients b_j,

    ||sum b_j u_(r_j)||² = D_H/(4pi²)
       integral_R w_r(eta)|sum b_j exp(ijeta)|² deta.

Periodization is legal by nonnegative Tonelli and periodicity of the finite trigonometric polynomial. For |theta|<=pi, the ell=0 term in sum_ell w_r(theta+2pi ell) is <=pi^alpha L_r². The two tails are bounded by

    2 N_r² pi^(alpha-6) sum_(ell>=1)(2ell-1)^(alpha-6)
    <=2 N_r² pi^(alpha-6)[1+1/(2(5-alpha))].

Here alpha-6<-1; the displayed integral test is valid. Thus the Gram operator of any finite translated family is bounded by M_r I, M_r=D_H Pbar_r/(2pi), after Parseval integral |sum b_j exp(ijtheta)|² dtheta=2pi sum b_j². Integer shifts are essential for this proof; it gives no arbitrary-mesh conclusion.

This is an *upper* bound for the witness Gram matrix. It does not assert a lower bound on fBm increment covariance. That tempting substitute fails for H<1/2 because the all-ones vector of n unit increments has variance n^(2H), whose ratio to its Euclidean squared norm n tends to zero.

## 6. Exact area pairing and norm of the test tensor

Take w=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) u_(f_j) tensor u_(g_j). On tau_m its pairing is the deterministic sum

    sum_k f_j(kT/m)[g_j((k+1)T/m)-g_j(kT/m)].

For fixed T,n,j, this converges to integral_0^T f_j(t)g_j'(t)dt=Delta^(2H)integral_0^1 (phi')² dx=a Delta^(2H). This is ordinary C¹ Riemann integration. Continuity of the tensor inner product passes the left side from tau_m to tau. Consequently <tau,w>=n a Delta^(2H)>0.

Let A_jk=<u_(f_j),u_(f_k)> and C_jk=<u_(g_j),u_(g_k)>. These real PSD matrices satisfy A<=M_f I, C<=M_g I. Direct tensor expansion gives

    ||w||²=sum_jk A_jk C_jk=tr(AC)
             <=M_f tr(C)<=n M_f M_g.

The trace inequality follows from positivity of C^(1/2)(M_f I-A)C^(1/2). Neither entrywise positivity nor commutation nor independence of cells is required. Combining the dual bound from section 2 gives

    ||X_T-F||_2 >= n a Delta^(2H)/sqrt(n M_f M_g)
                  = [2pi a/(D_H sqrt(Pbar_f Pbar_g))]
                    T^(2H)n^(1/2-2H).

This is the exact published constant. Its positivity is analytical, not numerical: a>0, D_H finite positive, L_r,N_r finite, Pbar_r finite positive. It holds at n=1 without an asymptotic step. There is no simultaneous limit in n and m.

To transfer from the representation to any originally supplied fBm model, the observation vector and all finite sums have the same joint Gaussian-derived law. Their L² limit preserves joint law with the finite observation vector. Every L²(G) estimator is almost surely a Borel function of that vector, so the error law transfers. Auxiliary directions need not be measurable functions of the original complete fBm paths.
