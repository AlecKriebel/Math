                                                Abstract
         Classical compact-support suspension theory yields a smooth foliated surface-times-interval
     with product endpoint collars and nonzero Godbillon–Vey integral. We place arbitrarily many
     copies inside one ﬁxed pseudo-Anosov mapping torus. One embedded closed transversal meets
     every leaf, and the Godbillon–Vey numbers grow linearly with the number of copies. This
     gives a negative answer to the unrestricted volume-bound question printed as Question 10.6 in
     Calegari’s 2002 manuscript. The construction uses classical ingredients; historical priority of
     this speciﬁc consequence remains unestablished.


1    Statement and scope
For a smooth cooriented codimension-one foliation F of a closed oriented three-manifold, choose a
deﬁning form α and a form η with dα = α ∧ η. We use the ordinary Godbillon–Vey number

The closed integral is independent of these choices. We call F taut when a closed transversal meets
every leaf.
Theorem 1. There exist a closed connected oriented hyperbolic three-manifold M , a number a > 0,
and an embedded smooth circle γ ⊂ M such that, for every integer N ≥ 1, M admits a smooth
cooriented taut foliation FN with
                                       gv(FN ) = N a.
The same γ is positively transverse to, and meets every leaf of, each FN .
    Fixing one hyperbolic metric on M ﬁxes its positive ﬁnite volume. The theorem therefore rules
out any ﬁnite bound determined solely by that volume, including a bound on | gv |. It answers the
literal Question 10.6 of [1, p. 25], using the single-circle deﬁnition in [1, Deﬁnition 1.1]. Compact
leaves are permitted. This note makes no assertion for minimal foliations, a condition imposed in
the diﬀerent Question 13.1 of that source.

Attribution and priority. The nonzero suspension input is classical Mather–Thurston theory,
with the precise compatibility recorded by Tsuboi [2]. Interval-bundle replacement is classical; see
Gabai [3, Operation 2.1.3]. Hyperbolization is due to Thurston [6]. A bounded primary-source audit
did not authenticate an earlier explicit statement of Theorem 1, but does not establish its novelty,
ﬁrstness, or present-day open status. Several relevant historical full texts remained inaccessible
or unread. Our target is the speciﬁed 2002 manuscript version; the ﬁnal 2003 chapter was not
compared. Detailed read-depths and gaps accompany this note.

Veriﬁcation and limitations. The existence proof uses the cited classical suspension and hy-
perbolization theorems. It supplies no explicit numerical holonomy representation or certiﬁed
hyperbolic metric. The accompanying exact-rational and symbolic controls check local form iden-
tities, compression, and ﬁnite bookkeeping; they do not replace the global proof. Their explicit
failure checks remain active under Python optimization. Historical veriﬁer deﬁciencies, independent
adversarial audits, and the bounded priority search are documented separately. All primary-source
PDFs are excluded from the supplement.
