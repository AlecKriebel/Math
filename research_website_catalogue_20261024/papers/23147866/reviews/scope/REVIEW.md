## Exact operational claim

The resource is precisely the symmetric four-qubit W state in order A₁A₂B₁B₂. Sender i owns Aᵢ, receiver i owns Bᵢ. On n independent copies, sender i independently chooses a uniformly distributed message from a separate fixed codebook, applies a local unitary encoder and transmits Aᵢⁿ noiselessly to receiver i, costing n qubits. The decoding cut is (A₁B₁)ⁿ : (A₂B₂)ⁿ. Joint many-copy operations within each laboratory and receiver-to-receiver classical communication are allowed. No extra shared entanglement, sender-message disclosure, feedback to senders or quantum communication between receivers is provided.

For nonnegative rates the claimed interior is R₁<3/2, R₂<3/2 and R₁+R₂<3/2+h₂(1/4). Separate Pauli codebooks and vanishing uniform average error are obtained via Winter. Its sum-rate supremum is approximately 2.311278 bits per resource copy, a lower bound on optimal LOCC capacity, not capacity equality. The explicit interior point (9/8,9/8) totals 9/4>2. Boundary attainment is asymptotic, not an exact finite-block code claim. Zero-error, maximal-error convergence, constructive code families and finite-block error guarantees are not established.

The first receiver performs a complete Bell measurement and forwards every outcome. There is no postselection. Its classical outcome and the second receiver's retained quantum state constitute one local finite classical–quantum MAC output. Entropy values are 3/2 on each input pair, 3 after averaging either sender, and 3+h₂(1/4) after averaging both. The induced three Holevo constraints match the claimed rate region. A dephasing argument makes the decoder conditional on the classical outcome string while retaining all quantum operations locally at the second receiver. Fano's inequality supplies the bridge to the source's accessible-information formulation; the one-message degenerate case is separated in the manuscript.

The source's local-unitary LO expression is 2h₂(1/4)<2 and its ordinary classical fallback is two bits. The result concerns that specified classification convention, not a general theorem about all nonunitary local encoders. The both-Bell one-copy comparison yields two bits only for the displayed uniform 16-letter ensemble and that particular decoder; it is not a one-copy LOCC converse.

## Primary-source comparison

Downloaded predecessor PDFs and hash receipts are in `primary-sources/`. Relevant model and theorem passages, rather than every page of every predecessor, were read.

- [Bruß et al. 2004 v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0407037v3) sets up the distributed unitary-encoding task and capacity classification. [Bruß's 2005 Oberwolfach contribution](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45978), printed 203–205, explicitly asks about the four-qubit W state's LOCC-dense-codeable classification. The 2006 review's two-receiver discussion allows collective block operations within each receiver and distinguishes upper bounds from achieved capacities. These support the target's many-copy interpretation.
- [Winter v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9807019v3), code definitions and Theorem 9, uses independent product priors, separate encoder maps, a single common quantum output, and uniform average decoding error. The paper must—and explicitly does—supply the localization to the second receiver before invoking this theorem. Merely computing a global Holevo quantity on a split output would not suffice.
- [Pradhan–Agrawal–Pati v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1917v1), §4.2.3, displays a W-state two-bit protocol with Bell measurement and forwarding. It uses a single sender holding two qubits and describes four distinguishable codewords; it does not by itself give the independent-message many-copy region here. The mechanism is prior and must be credited, not advertised as a new Bell-forwarding idea.
- [Huang–Zhang–Hou v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911120v5), §6, already applies multiple-access coding to dense coding. The general MAC/dense-coding connection is not novel. The presently claimed addition is the specific mixed cq channel for the symmetric W₄ split-receiver problem and its operationally valid achieved rate.
- Das et al. [v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6247v1), §II/Theorem 1, and Muhuri et al. [v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13057v2), receiver-bound formulas, provide relevant LOCC upper bounds. An upper bound above two bits is not an achieved W-state rate. The comparison is limited to these inspected versions.
- [Hayashi–Wang's published 2022 paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.030346), introduction and §II, has a sender, receiver and helper: the transmitted system is separated from the helper's preshared system. Its partition and routing are different from the two independently transmitting senders here. No direct transplantation of its capacity formula is warranted.
