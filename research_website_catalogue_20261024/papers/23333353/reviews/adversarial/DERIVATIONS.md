# Independent deductions and exact boundary controls

The deductions below were formulated in the sealed early ledger before reading foreign primary bodies. This expanded account uses the actual manuscript's convention, subsequently checked against Liebscher's complete contribution. Finite computations in `checks.py` authenticate matrices and custody only. The universal conclusions follow from the arguments here.

## D1. Exact algebra, marginal and source quantifiers

Order the points as 00,01,10,11. The columns 1,p,q,pq have determinant of absolute value one, so they form a basis of A=C^4. Each of B=span(1,p), C=span(1,q) is a two-dimensional proper unital *-subalgebra. Evaluating a+bp=c+dq at 00,10,01 forces a=c and b=d=0, so B and C are distinct and intersect in C1.

For 0<t<1, write phi_t=(1-t)delta00+t delta11 and u_t=sqrt(1-t)e00+sqrt(t)e11. For every diagonal element a, u_t* a u_t=(1-t)a(00)+t a(11). Thus the diagonal inclusion into M4 with its vector state preserves the *entire* original state, not just p and q moments.

The witness is a state on the free product of infinitely many copies. Its restriction to B in copy 1 and C in copy 2 must equal the original state on *all* of B*C. Extremality is in all permutation-invariant states. It is not extremality in a prescribed-marginal slice, a tensor-product state space, a tracial state space or a faithful-state class. Liebscher's printed equation and wording impose no such additional constraint.

## D2. Algebraic GNS needs no hidden boundedness hypothesis

For a positive unital functional eta on a unital *-algebra, Cauchy–Schwarz holds for the semidefinite form <x,y>=eta(y*x). If eta(x*x)=0, apply it to x and a*a x. The scalar eta(x*a*a x) is zero because its squared modulus is bounded by eta(x*x) eta((a*a x)*(a*a x))=0. This scalar is eta((ax)*(ax)); hence the null space is a left ideal. Left multiplication consequently defines the GNS representation on the quotient pre-Hilbert space. Its invariant algebraic domain contains the cyclic vector and every finite generator word. All subsequent products act on this domain; no completion or unbounded-operator extension is required.

For a projection R, R*=R and R²=R on that domain. The common-vector argument below uses these algebraic identities only. In particular, equality of two operators on the cyclic vector is never promoted to equality on the whole space.

## D3. Every possible downstairs witness is forced

Suppose omega satisfies the entire B*C law for phi_t and is exchangeable. Put P_i=pi(j_i(p)), Q_i=pi(j_i(q)), and let Omega be its cyclic vector. For every i!=j, a finite permutation carries (1,2) to (i,j). Therefore

    omega(P_i)=omega(Q_j)=omega(P_i Q_j)=omega(Q_j P_i)=t.

The square of P_i-Q_j has expectation t+t-t-t=0. Hence P_i Omega=Q_j Omega whenever i!=j.

For i!=k choose j distinct from both; then P_i Omega=Q_j Omega=P_k Omega. Thus all P_i Omega coincide. Every Q_j Omega equals some P_i Omega with i!=j. Call the common vector v. For any R among all P_i,Q_i,

    R Omega=v,       R v=R² Omega=R Omega=v.

Induction from the rightmost factor proves that **every nonempty finite word**, with arbitrary copy indices and arbitrary order, sends Omega to v. Its expectation is <Omega,v>=t. Since each copy of A is generated by p and q and has basis 1,p,q,pq, these words and the unit span the algebraic free product.

Let chi0 and chi1 evaluate every copy at 00 and 11. Every nonempty generator word has value zero under chi0 and one under chi1. Consequently

    omega=(1-t)chi0+t chi1

on the whole algebraic free product. These are distinct positive unital exchangeable characters, so omega is not extreme for 0<t<1. The proof excludes *every* potential witness; it does not assume a preferred extension. For the universal C*-product, both sides are continuous and agree on a norm-dense *-subalgebra, giving the same uniqueness and nonextremality. Infinite copies supply the third index; three copies would already suffice for this particular implication.

## D4. Purity survives the surjective fold, in both categories

Let f:T→U be a surjective unital *-homomorphism and eta a pure state of U. If eta f=lambda eta1+(1-lambda)eta2 with 0<lambda<1, then for x in ker f positivity forces eta1(x*x)=eta2(x*x)=0. Cauchy–Schwarz with the unit gives eta1(x)=eta2(x)=0. Each functional descends well-definedly to U. If a=f(z), its descended functional at a*a equals eta_i(z*z)>=0, so descent preserves positivity and the unit. Purity of eta forces both descended states to equal eta. Thus eta f is pure. In the C*-case the descended positive unital functionals are automatically continuous.

For a unit vector u in C^d let e=uu*. In any state decomposition of its vector state, positivity forces each constituent to have value zero at the projection 1-e. Cauchy–Schwarz kills every term with 1-e as a left or right factor. Thus every constituent has value eta_i(eTe) at T. Since eTe=(u*Tu)e and eta_i(e)=1, each is the original vector state. This proves algebraic as well as C*-purity directly.

The fold f:F(M4)→M4 is surjective because it is the identity on each full copy. It is unchanged by every permutation. Hence phi'_t f is permutation-invariant and pure among *all* states, and in particular extreme among invariant states. For every word in B*C, folding evaluates the same product of original diagonal matrices; D1 then gives equality of the full law. Its marginal on each full copy is exactly phi'_t. No conditional-expectation or de Finetti assertion is needed for this step.

At t=0 or 1 the downstairs state is itself a character, so the claimed dependence appropriately disappears. For A=B=C=C² the same proof works, using the common generator from different copies, and the downstairs fair state is faithful. The pure matrix extension remains nonfaithful. None of this establishes a theorem with both ambient states faithful or with tracial witnesses.

## D5. Exact negative control: two copies behave differently

This is a falsification of an unclaimed finite variant, not a counterexample to the infinite theorem. Let tau:A→A interchange p and q; on points it interchanges 01 and10 and fixes00,11. Let g:A*A→A restrict to id on copy 1 and tau on copy 2. It is surjective. Interchanging the two copies intertwines with tau on A, because tau²=id.

Let psi=(delta01+delta10)/2. It is tau-invariant and extreme among tau-invariant states on A: positivity forces each state in an invariant convex decomposition to be supported on {01,10}, and invariance forces equal weights. To show psi g is extreme among two-copy invariant states, start any such decomposition. Cauchy–Schwarz and vanishing on ker g, exactly as in D4, descend both constituents to positive states on A. Equivariance and surjectivity make these states tau-invariant. Extremality of psi then makes the decomposition trivial. The argument works in both free-product categories.

Under g, p in copy 1 and q in copy 2 both become p. The full image of B*C is the algebra generated by this one projection. The state psi gives it the fair Bernoulli law. Under the original law phi, the two coordinates p,q agree on its support {00,11} and have exactly that same fair law. Equality therefore holds for every B*C word. Thus a downstairs extreme witness *does* exist for two copies.

This state has different full-copy marginals: psi(pq)=0, whereas phi(pq)=1/2. The original definition fixes only the indicated B*C law. This countercontrol demonstrates the force of the third-index step and prevents an illicit extension to two copies. It also shows that two prescribed single-coordinate marginals do not prescribe the whole ambient marginal.

## D6. Exact negative control: the fixed-marginal slice

Set omega_t=(1-t)chi0+t chi1 and prescribe full marginal phi_t on every copy. Suppose omega_t=lambda sigma+(1-lambda)rho inside that marginal slice. For i!=j, the positive element (p_i-q_j)² has value zero under omega_t. Positivity makes its value zero in each constituent. The common-vector and all-word proof D3 therefore applies separately to sigma and rho; the prescribed marginal fixes their moment t. Both equal omega_t. This proves omega_t is extreme in the slice, even if the slice allowed states without permutation invariance. In particular it is extreme in the exchangeable fixed-marginal slice.

The decomposition into chi0,chi1 does not lie in that slice for 0<t<1. Changing the convex set thus changes the example, as the manuscript states. Merely prescribing full marginals while continuing to demand extremality in the unrestricted exchangeable set does not change D3/D4.

An independent operator control is R=(1/2)[[1,1,0],[1,1,0],[0,0,0]], S=R+diag(0,0,1), Omega=e0. Both are projections and R Omega=S Omega=(e0+e1)/2, but R!=S. Both fix their common vector. This explicitly refutes the stronger operator inference that D3 deliberately avoids.

## D7. Algebraic non-lifting is an actual positivity obstruction

R=C[x] and S=C[x,x^-1] are unital *-algebras with x*=x and (x^-1)*=x^-1. Their ordinary inclusion is injective. The canonical inclusion of their algebraic unital free products is also injective: choose unit complements R0=span{x^n:n>=1} inside S0=span{x^n:n in Z, n!=0}; the alternating reduced tensor decomposition of the free product identifies the former direct sum as a vector subspace of the latter.

Evaluation at zero in every R copy is a unital *-character chi. It is positive, since chi(a*a)=|chi(a)|²; it is pure by D4 applied to its surjection onto C. It is exchangeable. Any positive unital extension to F(S) restricts in one copy to selfadjoint X,Y with XY=1 and eta(X²)=0. The 2-by-2 Gram matrix for X,Y would be [[0,1],[1,m]], m=eta(Y²), with determinant -1, violating positivity. Equivalently Cauchy–Schwarz gives 1<=0. All functional values are finite complex scalars; no limiting argument occurs.

Evaluation at one is a separate common state on R and S. The construction does not assert that this common state fails to extend. It proves failure to lift the unrestricted extreme state chi, exactly the stated algebraic assertion.

## D8. C*-extreme extension uses a genuine compact face

Let D included in E be an equivariant unital C*-inclusion for S_infinity and delta an extreme invariant state on D. The C*-state extension theorem supplies an extension eta on E. For the finite subgroup S_n set eta_n=(1/n!)sum_{g in S_n} eta composed with alpha_g. Its restriction is delta. The state space of E is weak-* compact. A cluster subnet (not an assumed convergent subsequence) has a limit eta_infinity. Every fixed finitely supported permutation belongs to S_n for all sufficiently large n, so invariance of the finite averages under that permutation passes to the subnet limit.

The fiber K of invariant states restricting to delta is nonempty, compact and convex. If a state in K decomposes in the whole invariant state space, restrictions decompose delta into invariant states of D. Extremality of delta forces both restrictions to be delta; thus K is a face. Krein–Milman gives an extreme point of K; being in a face, that point is extreme in the whole invariant state space. This proves the proposition with no separability, normality, trace, faithfulness or prescribed density assumption.

## D9. Full proof of canonical universal C*-free-product injectivity

This reconstructs the manuscript's brief dilation argument independently, without reading its historical verification package. Let D included in E be unital and let pi be any unital representation of F(D) on H. Each pi_i=pi composed with j_i is a representation of D on H. Decompose pi_i into orthogonal cyclic reducing summands (a maximal orthogonal family of cyclic subspaces suffices). Each summand with a unit cyclic vector is the GNS representation of its vector state on D. Extend that state to E by the C*-state extension theorem. In its E-GNS space, the closed D-cyclic subspace is a reducing D-subspace and carries the original cyclic representation. The direct sum over all these summands gives an E-representation rho_i on H direct-sum K_i whose D-restriction reduces H and equals pi_i there.

Choose any nonzero unital representation rho0 of E on H0, for example from a state, and an infinite cardinal kappa at least the dimensions of H0 and all K_i. Pad every rho_i by kappa copies of rho0. Each complementary Hilbert space K_i direct-sum H0^(kappa) now has dimension kappa; identify all of them with one common K, leaving H fixed. The padded E-representations on H direct-sum K induce by universality a representation rho of F(E). The image of F(D) reduces H and its restriction there is pi, because this holds on each generating D-copy.

For x in F(D) this gives ||pi(x)||<=||iota(x)||. Taking the supremum over all representations pi yields ||x||<=||iota(x)||. The reverse inequality follows because the canonical *-homomorphism iota is contractive. Thus it is isometric and injective, and it intertwines copy permutations. D8 consequently applies to universal free products. For the zero algebra the statement is vacuous; here states ensure the nonzero case. Cardinal padding covers nonseparable representations.

## D10. What survives, and what is not inferred

The complete original Theorem 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 3 and Proposition 4 survive this bounded falsification. D3 and D4 prove the all-state/all-word quantifiers. D7 proves non-lift by an exact positivity contradiction. D8/D9 settle the claimed C* contrast. D5 and D6 are exact controls delineating changed definitions; they are not defects or replacement results.

The original printed contribution also discusses classical/tensor intuition and other open questions. That discussion does not add a commutative-quotient requirement to its displayed free-product definition. The note explicitly targets the printed free-product convention; it does not settle every intended classical, conditional-independence, tail-field, normal W* or natural-product variant. No inference of conditional independence from exchangeability alone is used. The DKW folding mechanism is established prior work and is credited; no exhaustive priority or novelty determination follows from checking these interfaces.
