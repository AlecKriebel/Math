Ambient Dependence of Extremal-Exchangeable Independence
Alec Kriebel
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
12 October 2026
Abstract
Liebscher asked whether independence deﬁned by an extreme exchangeable free-product state
needs to retain its ambient algebra. We give a state-preserving inclusion of a four-point algebra
into M4(C) for which two distinct proper coordinate subalgebras, intersecting only in the scalars,
are dependent downstairs and independent upstairs. Positivity forces every downstairs witness
to be the same nontrivial character mixture; upstairs a pure vector state supplies a pure folding
witness. Pure folding is an established construction, credited below. We also exhibit an extreme
exchangeable algebraic state with no positive extension to a larger algebra, addressing the source’s
separate non-lifting concern. In contrast, extreme invariant states do lift along equivariant unital
C ∗-inclusions. The deﬁnition is unrestricted: extremality is among all exchangeable states, without
a ﬁxed-marginal, faithfulness or trace condition.

Lemma 2. A pure state pulls back to a pure state along a surjective unital *-homomorphism. A unit
vector state on Md(C) is pure. Both statements hold for positive algebraic states.
Proof. In a convex decomposition of η ◦ f , positivity forces each constituent to vanish on x∗x for
x ∈ ker f . Cauchy–Schwarz then makes it vanish on x. Each constituent descends to a unital positive
functional on the quotient: lift any a to z, and evaluate a∗a as z∗z. Purity of η makes the decomposition
trivial. For a vector state, let e = uu∗. The zero expectation of 1 − e in every constituent kills all
blocks outside e, by Cauchy–Schwarz. Since eT e = ( u∗T u)e and each constituent has value one on e,
each is the original vector state.

to ordinary symmetric extremality. Lemma 2 gives the needed special case directly, with the stronger
conclusion of purity among all states.

Both R, S admit the common state given by evaluation at one. The forbidden χ is a diﬀerent state;
the assertion concerns the unrestricted extreme-state sets in the source, not failure to extend that
speciﬁed common marginal.
Proposition 4. For an equivariant unital C ∗-inclusion D ⊂ E under the group of ﬁnitely supported
permutations of N, every extreme invariant state of D has an extreme invariant extension to E.
Proof. Extend the state to E by the C ∗-state extension theorem. A verage the extension over Sn and
take a weak-* cluster point, using a subnet when necessary. Every ﬁxed ﬁnite permutation ﬁxes all
suﬃciently late averages; the limit is an invariant extension. Its compact convex extension ﬁber is a
face of the invariant state space of E, since restriction of a decomposition would decompose the extreme
state of D. An extreme point of this nonempty compact face is extreme in the whole space.
The canonical map between universal unital free products of a C ∗-inclusion is injective. One
may check this by extending each copy representation using state extension and cyclic GNS dilations,
padding them to a common Hilbert space, and applying the universal property. The veriﬁcation
package gives this argument in detail. Thus Proposition 4 applies to those products. Extreme states
can lift while their restrictions fail to remain extreme. Theorem 1 proves ambient dependence in that
opposite direction; Theorem 3 concerns the algebraic category, where C ∗-extension and compactness
are unavailable.
4 Scope, attribution and veriﬁcation
The printed free-product deﬁnition is the target. We impose no global commutativity on its state
algebra merely because A is commutative. A classical commutative/tensor interpretation requires a
diﬀerent coproduct or states factoring through a commutative quotient; it is not assumed here.
The counterexample uses nonfaithful states, as permitted by the deﬁnition, and no tracial witness.
It does not address faithful-only, tracial-only, trivial-tail or separately speciﬁed normal W*-deﬁnitions.
Adding a prescribed full marginal while retaining extremality in E(A) leaves the upstairs witness valid.
Changing the convex set to the ﬁxed-marginal slice changes the result: the forced downstairs mixture
is extreme in that slice, as independently proved in the supporting audit.
The known pure-folding mechanism is credited above. Our source audit does not certify historical-
ﬁrst priority, and we make no such claim. The note supplies explicit counterexamples and full deduc-
tions for the stated original question, rather than resolving all the other questions in the workshop
contribution.
The source archive contains the manuscript, immutable authored input, independent proofs and
rational veriﬁcation scripts, source hashes and the priority audit. Finite checks supplement the all-
state, all-word and inﬁnite-copy proofs; they are not formal certiﬁcation. AI tools were used extensively
in proof development, reproduction, adversarial review and preparation. All listed reviews were AI-
assisted. This preprint is unrefereed and has not received human peer review.
