# Independent universal reconstruction

## 1. All exchangeable downstairs witnesses are forced

Let t∈(0,1), with the displayed case t=1/2. The full B-copy1/C-copy2 joint law and finite-permutation invariance give, for every distinct i,j,

    ω(P_i)=ω(Q_j)=ω(P_iQ_j)=ω(Q_jP_i)=t.

In algebraic GNS, positivity gives ∥(P_i−Q_j)Ω∥²=2t−t−t=0. Given any two P indices, a third Q index equates their vectors. Every Q vector then equals them as well. Denote their common value byv. For every generatorR in this family, RΩ=v and Rv=R²Ω=v. Induction on finite word length, starting at the rightmost letter, gives WΩ=v for every nonempty projection wordW. Its state value is⟨Ω,v⟩=t.

Each copy ofC4 is generated byp,q because1,p,q,pq form a basis. Consequently these finite projection words and1 span the entire algebraic free product, not merely B*C. Thus the only exchangeable state capable of witnessing the prescribed joint law is

    μ_t=(1−t)χ_00+tχ_11,

where both characters evaluate every copy at the same displayed point. They are distinct and exchangeable. This is a nontrivial decomposition inside the full exchangeable state set, so μ_t is not extreme and no downstairs witness exists. In the universal C* free product, equality on the dense algebraic words gives the identical conclusion by continuity.

No unbounded-operator assumption enters the algebraic calculation. Its null space is a left ideal: if η(x*x)=0, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to x and a*a x forces η(x*a*a x)=0, hence ax is null. The common quotient domain is invariant under all represented algebra elements. Projection identities and the word induction are valid there. A null-vector equation is never used as an unjustified global operator equation.

## 2. The upstairs witness is globally pure

For a surjective unital *-homomorphism f:D→E, suppose a pure stateη ofE pulls back to a nontrivial convex decomposition onD. For x∈kerf each constituent has value zero onx*x by positivity and hence onx by Cauchy–Schwarz with1. It descends to a unital positive functional onE: if a=f(z), its positive squarea*a is evaluated usingz*z. Purity ofη forces both constituents equalη. Thereforeη∘f is pure, in both algebraic and C* categories.

For a unit vectoru onM_d, let e=uu*. In a decomposition of its vector state, every constituent has value zero on1−e. Cauchy–Schwarz kills all terms containing1−e. Therefore its value onT equals its value oneTe=(u*Tu)e, and it has value1one. Each constituent equals the original vector state, proving purity without an extra faithfulness condition.

The folding map F(M4)→M4 is surjective and unchanged by any copy permutation. Pulling back the coherent vector state therefore gives an exchangeable state pure among **all** states, hence extreme among exchangeable states. On every word from B*C, folding multiplies the original diagonal matrices in the original order. Their vector-state restriction is the givenφ, so the entire joint-law equality follows. It also has the full prescribed marginalφ′ on every copy.

## 3. Why extremal restriction and extremal lifting differ

For an equivariant unital C* inclusionD⊂E and an extreme invariant stateφ onD, the ordinary C* state-extension theorem gives an extension toE. Average it overS_n. The averages still restrict toφ. The state space is weak-* compact, so take a cluster subnet. Any fixed finite permutation belongs toS_N and fixes every average withn≥N, hence fixes the limit. Thus invariant extensions exist even without separability or sequential compactness.

The compact convex fiber of invariant extensions ofφ is a face of the invariant state space ofE. Indeed, restricting a decomposition of one of its members would decomposeφ into invariant states onD, forcing both restrictions to equalφ. An extreme point of this nonempty compact face exists by Krein–Milman and is extreme in the whole invariant state space. This is the complete lift argument. It does not imply that restrictions of extreme upper states are extreme downstairs; the pure folding witness above restricts precisely to the nonextremeμ_t.

The canonical universal unital free-product map is genuinely injective. Given a representationπ of the smaller product onH, decompose each copy representation into cyclic reducing representations. Extend their vector states from the smaller C* algebra to the larger one; GNS supplies representations onH⊕K_i withH reducing for the original copy and the prescribed restriction there. Choose a sufficiently large infinite Hilbert-space dimensionκ. Embed everyK_i in a commonκ-dimensional complement with aκ-dimensional remainder, and fill each remainder using an amplification of a nonzero representation of the larger algebra. The resulting representations act on a common Hilbert space and fixH. The universal property extends them to the larger free product; restriction toH recoversπ. Taking allπ proves equality of universal norms and injection. No conditional expectation is required.

For algebraic free products, choose vector-space complements to the common unit, extending complements from the smaller to larger factors. Alternating tensor-word normal forms give the injection directly. For the finite-dimensional factors of the main example, every positive algebraic state is automatically bounded in the universal C* norm: for a factor elementa, write ∥a∥²1−a*a=b*b inside that factor and evaluatex*b*b x in its GNS domain. This bounds each copy representation and supplies the universal C* representation. Thus no discontinuous-state loophole separates the main example's two categories.

## 4. Algebraic nonextension is a separate theorem

Evaluation atzero in every polynomial copy gives a characterχ onF(C[x]); characters are pure by the quotient argument. It is exchangeable. If it had a positive extension toF(C[x,x^−1]), writeX,Y for the first copy's self-adjoint generators withXY=1. The Gram matrix ofX,Y would be

    [[η(X²),η(XY)],[η(YX),η(Y²)]]=[[0,1],[1,b]].

Its determinant is−1 for every realb, contradicting positivity; equivalently Cauchy–Schwarz gives1≤0. Both polynomial/Laurent algebras have the common state evaluation atone, butχ has a different marginal. This theorem concerns unrestricted extreme-state lifting and does not assert failure to extend that common specified state. The C* extension/compactness theorem cannot be applied to these algebraic factors.

## 5. Boundary controls beyond the main proof

**Fixed-marginal extremality.** The quotientq:F(A)→C² evaluates all copies at00/11. μ_t=θ_t∘q. Any constituent of a state decomposition ofμ_t vanishes onkerq by positivity/Cauchy–Schwarz and descends toC². If the full copy marginal remainsφ_t, its value onq(p) is fixed tot, which fixes the descended stateθ_t. Thusμ_t is extreme in the fixed-marginal slice despite being nonextreme in the full exchangeable set. This verifies the manuscript's additional slice assertion independently.

**Exactly two copies.** Take two characters: copy values(10,01), and the swapped values(01,10). Their equal mixture isS2-invariant. The corresponding quotient isC² with the copy-swap interchanging its two points, so it has a unique invariant state. The quotient-annihilation face argument shows its pullback is extreme among S2-invariant states of the two-copy free product. Its B-copy1/C-copy2 law has both projections equal within each character and therefore matches the fair00/11 joint law for every word. Its same-copy pq moment is0, rather than1/2. This is a precise finite-two-copy boundary example, not a counterexample to the infinite-copy statement. The equality graph P_iΩ=Q_jΩ fori≠j is disconnected for two copies and connected starting with three.

**Trace and endpoints.** At t=0 or1 the downstairs state is itself a character and folding is already a downstairs extreme witness. The coherent M4 state is nonfaithful and, for0<t<1, nontracial. A tracial upper witness would have the normalized trace on each M4 copy, giving the same-copy pq moment1/4; the cross-law positivity argument would force that moment to1/2. Thus this example cannot simply be promoted to a tracial-witness assertion. The note excludes that variant.

Own exact matrix, finite-word, equality-graph and symbolic-Gram controls corroborate these constructions. The universal deductions above, rather than the finite word cutoff, establish all-state/all-word/infinite-copy claims.
