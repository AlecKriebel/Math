# Independent reconstruction of the full published claim

The target relation uses the countable **unital free product** of the ambient algebra. A witness must be extreme among **all exchangeable states**, invariant under finite permutations, and agree with the entire original joint state on B*C. The claim is not tested in a convex set with the full marginal fixed. Both the positive algebraic-state and universal unital C*-free-product categories are specified.

Let A be the four-point function algebra ordered 00,01,10,11, with p(s,t)=s, q(s,t)=t, B=span{1,p}, C=span{1,q}, and state φ=(δ00+δ11)/2. The four functions 1,p,q,pq are independent; comparing a+bp=c+dq at 00,10,01 gives b=d=0 and a=c. Thus B,C are distinct proper two-dimensional subalgebras with scalar intersection. Diagonal inclusion in M4 and u=(e00+e11)/√2 preserve φ.

Suppose any downstairs exchangeable witness ω agrees with the full B*C law. Write Pi,Qi in its algebraic GNS representation. Each is a self-adjoint projection. For every i≠j, the law and exchangeability give ω(Pi)=ω(Qj)=ω(PiQj)=ω(QjPi)=1/2. Consequently ||(Pi−Qj)Ω||²=0. A third index connects PiΩ with PkΩ for arbitrary i,k and then with every QjΩ. Denote the common vector by v. Every generator R satisfies RΩ=v and Rv=R²Ω=v. Induction gives the same vector v for every nonempty word in these projections, so every such word has moment 1/2. These words and the unit span the algebraic free product because each copy of A is spanned by 1,p,q,pq.

Let χ0 and χ1 evaluate all copies at 00 and 11. All words force ω=(χ0+χ1)/2, a nontrivial mixture of distinct exchangeable characters. This excludes **every** extreme downstairs witness, not merely one chosen candidate. In the C* category, equality extends from the dense algebraic words by continuity.

The GNS step is legitimate even algebraically. If η(x*x)=0, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to x and a*a x gives η(x*a*a x)=0, so ax is null. The null space is a left ideal, and multiplication acts on the quotient domain. The argument uses equality on cyclic vectors and idempotence on that domain; it never replaces a null-vector equation by an operator identity.

Upstairs, folding every free copy of M4 onto M4 is a surjective unital *-homomorphism f invariant under copy permutations. The rank-one vector state φ' is pure: in any positive decomposition, the zero expectation of 1−uu* kills all blocks outside the rank-one corner by Cauchy–Schwarz; the remaining corner fixes every matrix value. A pure state pulls back through a quotient: every positive decomposition constituent annihilates x*x for x in ker f, hence ker f itself, and descends positively to the quotient. Thus ω'=φ'∘f is pure among all states and therefore extreme exchangeable. Folding any B*C word multiplies the original diagonal matrices in their original order; φ'|A=φ gives the entire required joint law. Every whole-copy marginal is also φ'. This establishes the full finite-dimensional ambient-dependence theorem.

For 0<t<1, replacing the weights by 1−t,t changes each nonempty-word moment to t. The same all-witness proof forces (1−t)χ0+tχ1 downstairs and the coherent rank-one vector supplies the upstairs witness. At t=0,1 the downstairs state is a character, so this counterexample disappears. The smaller A=B=C=C2⊂M2 example has faithful downstairs state but coincident subalgebras; the main four-point example provides distinct proper subalgebras and scalar intersection.

A different extremality set changes the conclusion. Let h be the all-copy quotient onto C2 given by the two characters, and let ω be its pullback of the faithful weighted state. In any positive decomposition of ω, each constituent annihilates the quotient kernel by the same square/CS argument and therefore is a character mixture. Fixing the full marginal fixes its weight t, so the decomposition is trivial. Thus the forced downstairs mixture is extreme in the fixed-full-marginal slice, even without requiring exchangeability. This is a boundary of the stated theorem; it is not silently replaced by a narrower positive result.

The algebraic non-lifting theorem is separate. Set R=C[x], x*=x, inside S=C[x,x^-1] with self-adjoint inverse. Uniform evaluation at 0 is a character, hence pure and exchangeable, on F(R). A positive extension would have self-adjoint X,Y with XY=1 and η(X²)=0. Cauchy–Schwarz gives1=|η(XY)|²≤η(X²)η(Y²)=0. Equivalently the two-by-two moment Gram determinant is identically −1, independently of the unknown η(Y²). This proves impossibility for all positive extensions, not just exchangeable ones. Both R,S admit evaluation at 1; that common marginal is a different state. The example does not claim failure of C*-state extension or failure to extend a marginal that is already known to extend.

For an equivariant unital C*-inclusion D⊂E under the locally finite permutation group, extend an extreme invariant state ψ by the standard C*-state extension theorem. Average over the increasing finite symmetric groups. A weak-* convergent subnet exists by compactness of the state space. Every fixed finite permutation leaves every sufficiently late average invariant, and each average still restricts to ψ; thus an invariant extension exists. The invariant extension fiber is nonempty, compact and convex. It is a face because any invariant decomposition restricts to a decomposition of the extreme ψ. A compact-convex extreme-point theorem gives an extreme point in that face and hence in the full invariant state space. These standard imports are explicit; no original full proof of Hahn–Banach, Banach–Alaoglu or Krein–Milman was reread here.

The free-product map is an inclusion. Algebraically, choose compatible vector-space complements to the common unit and use the alternating tensor normal form. In the universal C* category take any representation of the smaller free product on H, decompose each copy restriction into cyclic reducing representations, extend each cyclic state to the larger copy, and use its GNS representation to dilate that restriction. Pad the countably many dilations onto one sufficiently large common Hilbert space, using unital representations on complements and fixing H. The universal property produces a representation of the larger free product whose smaller algebra acts on H as the original representation. Taking the supremum over original representations preserves the universal norm. The detailed deposited extension proof was read after the independent early reconstruction and agrees with this mechanism; its historical acceptance verdict is not a premise.

This C* lifting result concerns extreme states downstairs extending upwards. Ambient dependence here uses the reverse-direction failure: an upstairs pure state can restrict to a nonextreme downstairs state. The two claims are compatible. For the main finite-dimensional copies, every positive algebraic-state representation of a copy is norm bounded using the positive square root of ||a||²1−a*a; thus the algebraic and universal C* readings are compatible.

The original Liebscher contribution, read in full at printed 540–541, uses unit-amalgamated free products and unrestricted exchangeable extremality. It does not add faithful, tracial, fixed-marginal or normal-W* conditions. Its classical comparison should not be turned into global commutation of all free copies. Earlier DKW Example 5.2.1 (credited there to Weihua Liu) and Crismale–Fidaleo Section 7 already give Boolean representations from a commutative single-copy algebra into a noncommutative sequence algebra, so the distinction is substantive prior mathematics.

DKW Proposition 8.7 explicitly gives the maximal-amalgamation folding construction; Theorem 8.1 connects quantum-symmetric extremality to ordinary symmetric extremality. These are credited prior results. The target's elementary quotient-purity proof independently supplies its special case and does not import the complete DKW classification. None of these checks establishes historical firstness, independent discovery, or continued openness in 2026. No central gap was detected in the literal published claims, and no optional historical-audit strengthening is promoted into those claims.
