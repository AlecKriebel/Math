                     An odd-period product for focal inversions
                            of outer billiard polygons
                                                 Alec Kriebel
                                        ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                                15 October 2026


                                                   Abstract
          For a periodic billiard family between an ellipse and a strictly nested nondegenerate confocal
      elliptical caustic, form the outer polygon by intersecting consecutive tangents at the billiard
      vertices. Invert its vertices in the unit circles centered at the two original billiard foci, and
      join successive images by straight segments. We prove that the product of the resulting signed
      areas is constant for every odd least period, including primitive stars. This proves the invariant
      conjectured as k904,a in the pinned 2020 list of Reznik, Garcia and Koiller. An explicit inverse-
      edge factorization reduces the area to a cyclic elliptic sum. Its double principal parts cancel,
      leaving two simple poles and complementary half-period zeros. The triangular critical case and
      repeated traversals are included. The analytic methods are classical; no ﬁrst-proof or absolute-
      priority claim is made. This AI-assisted research note is unrefereed.

3    Poles and cyclic cancellation
We use the standard Jacobi periods, zeros, poles and quarter shifts [3, §§22.4,22.8]. Put p = iK ′
and r0 = 3K + p. The function e has period 4K and anti-period 2p, hence period 4p.

Lemma 2. Modulo 4K, 2p, the only possible poles of e are double poles at r0 ±v and simple poles at
r0 ± 3v. In the exceptional primitive triangular case, the latter two points coincide, but the resulting
pole still has order at most one. Moreover

Its square in (5) permits double poles.
    By (11), the zeros of the other factor are r0 ± 3v. They are distinct and simple except when
3v = 2K. Indeed, 6v ∈ (0, 6K) can be a multiple of 4K only at 6v = 4K; the same exceptional
value is the only possible critical ﬁber, since sn(3v) = 0 then. Primitivity makes 3v = 2K exactly
N = 3, τ = 1. In that case L0 = Z, L1 = −Z, and the factor is Z(1 − k sn u), with a double zero at
K + p. At this point dn u has a simple zero, leaving order at most one in (5). This point is diﬀerent
from r0 ± v. The ﬁrst and second zero sets cannot otherwise intersect, since that would make 2v
or 4v a multiple of 4K, impossible for 0 < v < K.

or poles. This exhausts the possible poles. Finally Jacobi parity and shifts give sn(2r0 − u) = sn u
and dn(2r0 − u) = − dn u, proving (13).

two possible poles are already at most simple and diﬀer from R by δ and −2δ. The triangular

   Both poles actually occur. If neither occurred, compactness would make F constant and its
anti-period would force zero, contradicting the positive real sum (12). The anti-period pairs the
poles, so each has order exactly one.


4    Complementary zeros and the other focus
Cyclic reindexing in (13) shows that F is odd about r0 . Since 2v = δ is a period, it is also odd
about R:
                                     F (2R − u) = −F (u).
At R + L/2 reﬂection is a real-period translation, so F vanishes there. Its anti-period gives a second
zero at R + L/2 + 2p. These are distinct points of X and neither is a pole. A nonzero meromorphic
function on a compact torus has equally many zeros and poles counted with multiplicity. The two

and pole divisors, so

                   F (u)F (u + L/2)    is holomorphic on X and hence constant.                    (16)

This step is the classical complementary-divisor argument, applied to the target-speciﬁc pole cal-
culation above.
    Since Q(u + 2K) = −Q(u), inversion about f− is the central reﬂection of inversion about f+ at
the opposite point. Central reﬂection preserves signed area. Hence

                             B− (w) = B+ (w + 2K) = F (w + v + 2K).

For odd N , 2K = N L/2 ≡ L/2 (mod L). Equation (16) proves Theorem 1. Both areas are positive
in the chosen orientation by (12). Reversal negates both and preserves their product.

Corollary 1. For a ﬁxed r-fold traversal of an odd least-period family, the product is r2 times the
least-period product. In particular this includes every odd repeated traversal counted with an odd
total number of vertices.

Proof. Each shoelace sum is repeated r times, so each signed area is multiplied by r.

    The circular case is a separate rigid-rotation calculation: for billiard radius a and step angle
2πτ /N , the outer radius is Ro = a/ cos(πτ /N ). Both focal inversions are central, and each signed
inverse area is N sin(2πτ /N )/(2Ro2 ). The product is its square. Under a similarity of the entire
original conﬁguration by s > 0, while retaining unit inversion, the product scales by s−4 . Replacing
both unit inversion radii by ρ multiplies it by ρ8 .


5    Credit, priority scope and veriﬁcation
Stachel supplies the canonical billiard coordinates. Jacobi identities and elliptic principal-part
methods are established tools: cyclic master identities are developed in [5, 6], and complex analysis
of billiard invariants in [4]. In particular, complementary area zeros and poles already occur in
Chavez-Caliz’s proof of an even-period product for untransformed Poncelet and tangent polygons
[7, Theorem 6]. We claim no new general elliptic-function principle. The proof here supplies the
speciﬁc original-focus outer-edge expression, cancellation and critical-case check needed for the
pinned conjecture.
    Related original-vertex triangular products precede this note [8, 9]. Ferudun’s recent general
odd-period original-vertex counterpart [10] likewise concerns diﬀerent vertices. In (4) the two linear
factors are generally distinct; an original-ellipse focal-distance square cannot simply be substituted.
