Abstract
Let F = Q3 and let E/F be its unramiﬁed quadratic extension. We give an explicit check-
able argument that the Langlands quotient with segments [4, 6], [2, 5], [3, 3], [1, 4], [0, 2] is Galois
invariant, is not an entire proper parabolic induction, and has no invariant linear form for any
Hermitian stabilizer in GL15(E). The analytic proof assembles the period bases and functional
equations of Feigon–Lapid–Oﬀen: two invertible nested-segment swaps expose ﬁve necessary
opposite-lift conditions forming an odd cycle. Noninduction follows from the Lapid–Mínguez
ladder-side matching criterion, with all ﬁfteen partition obstructions displayed. The total mul-
tiplicity is zero rather than the two predicted by the historical k = 1 multiplicity conjecture.

KNOWN: their complete paper was unavailable for comparison. No ﬁrstness or worldwide
novelty claim is made.

Theorem 1. The unique irreducible Langlands quotient πof
I1 =δT ×δD ×δC ×δB ×δA
belongs to Irr(GL15(E)), satisﬁes πτ ≃ π, and is not itself an entire normalized induction from any

HomU (V )(π,C) = 0.
The factor lengths are 3, 4, 1, 4, 3, and their real central exponents in I1 are 5, 7/ 2, 3, 5/ 2, 1.
Classiﬁcation gives the unique irreducible quotient. Each factor is τinvariant, so induction and
its unique quotient are τinvariant. In particular, πis a quotient of an induction; the theorem
separately excludes its being an entire proper induction.
There are two Hermitian isometry classes in this local setting, even when their stabilizer groups
in odd dimension are isomorphic. The total in Lapid’s historical Conjecture 4 [ 1, p.734, §5.3]
sums over these orbits. At k = 1 its single factor may be πitself, and its required entire-product
irreducibility is automatic. The theorem therefore supplies total multiplicity 0 instead of 2 in the
characteristic-zero p-adic smooth-complex interpretation. No assertion for all local ﬁelds or other
coeﬃcient categories is needed.

5 Excluding every entire proper induced realization
Suppose πwere an entire irreducible proper induction. Exactness and preservation of nonzero
objects force the inducing data to be irreducible; otherwise a nonzero proper subobject and its
nonzero quotient survive induction. Grouping any greater number of Levi blocks likewise gives a
two-factor entire irreducible product L(m1)×L(m2) of positive degrees. By the additive constituent
property in [4, Proposition 2.5(5), Theorem 2.6], L(m1+m2) occurs in this product with multiplicity
one. Its irreducibility and injectivity of Langlands classiﬁcation force
m1 +m2 =A +B +C +D +T
as multisets of segments, a stronger conclusion than equality of cuspidal supports. Thus it suﬃces
to exclude all ﬁfteen unordered nonempty partitions of these ﬁve distinct occurrences.
For the matching criterion, deﬁne precedence by
[a,b ] ≺ [c,d ] ⇐ ⇒ a<c ≤ b + 1 and b<d.

required [ 4, Deﬁnition 5.13]. Each singleton is a ladder. Among pairs only BC and CD are not
ladders; their complements ADT and ABT are ladders. Every partition therefore has at least one
ladder side.

6 Consequences, antecedents, and the unknown comparison
Theorem 1 contradicts the historical OWR Conjecture 4 at k = 1, with the source’s “imprimitive”
meaning of noninduced [ 1, p.734]. Its individually τ-invariant cuspidal support has τ-Witt index
zero, so the same example contradicts FLO Conjecture 6.12 [ 2, p.246]. Since it is τinvariant but
has no period even for the quasi-split Hermitian stabilizer, it also disproves the unrestricted local
unitary converse attributed to Jacquet in [ 1, Conjecture 1, p.729]. These are consequences of one
example, not separate priority claims. The OWR page numbers here refer to the EMS copy; the
MFO copy numbers the corresponding pages 736 and 731.
The period bases, functional equations, normalizations, and adjacent equal-label exclusion are
due to FLO, especially pp.291–294. Classiﬁcation, matching, and the ring-involution argument use
LM; moving rank-one kernels through invertible swaps already occurs in [ 5, §6.4]. JPSS supplies
the segment L-factor computation. This note claims none of these general tools as a contribution.

Gurevich–Ma–Mitra [ 6, §8] already constructed a ﬁve-segment imprimitive negative-distinction
example of character-line degree 20, including an all-partitions noninduction strategy. Their sub-
group is GLn(F ) (and its quadratic-character variant), not a Hermitian stabilizer. Mitra–Oﬀen–
Sayag [ 7, Example 9.4] give an earlier negative symplectic-distinction example of degree 8, with
six Langlands segments; their displayed Zelevinsky-dual data have three segments. These are sub-
stantive antecedents, not solutions of the present unitary-period statement. Sharma [ 12, Theorem
8.1, §9] treats a degree- 8 ﬁve-segment endpoint-order obstruction and distinguishes combinatorial
conditions from existence of symplectic periods. No unitary-period theorem is imported from it.
The segment-length multiset here is {1, 3, 3, 4, 4}, diﬀering from these inspected examples and from
the OWR degree- 6 and LM degree- 8 examples. A ﬁnite check of translation, contragredient, and
reordering equivalence conﬁrms these stated nonidentities; it cannot establish absence of all prior
results.
Beuzart-Plessis [8, Theorems 1–3] establishes tempered spectral results and generic multiplicity
formulas; the generic conjectural formulation has already been resolved. Lu–Matringe [ 9, §5] proves
induced-space completeness in its square-integrable/tempered setting. Neither result supplies de-
scent of every standard-module period to an arbitrary nongeneric non-tempered quotient. Likewise,
the general intertwining-period results of [ 10, 11] concern the displayed inducing hypotheses, and
[10] explicitly notes quotient-descent limitations. Our conclusion does not contradict these positive
classes. Unramiﬁed character-line data do not make this Langlands quotient spherical; nor does
“unitary periods” assume the representation is unitarizable.
