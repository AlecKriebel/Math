                                                    Abstract
          Fix an oriented compact surface of genus g ≥ 1 with one boundary circle, and ﬁx that bound-
      ary pointwise in both maps and isotopies. Ordered positive factorizations of its single boundary
      twist, along non-null-homotopic simple closed curves with pairwise geometric intersection at
      most one, have ﬁnitely many simultaneous-conjugacy classes. Repetitions are permitted. Their
      lengths therefore have a ﬁnite attained maximum M (g), with 4g(2g + 1) ≤ M (g); credited
      low-genus deductions give M (1) = 12 and M (2) = 40. This is a credited ﬁniteness deduc-
      tion and exposition: the ﬁnite-alphabet group lemma is a veriﬁed corollary of Cossu–Tringali’s
      factorization theory, and the support and embedding mechanisms are prior curve-system and
      ribbon-surface methods, with the marked bordered adaptation supplied here. It answers nega-
      tively the separately stated restricted arbitrary-length clause attributed to Smith in Wajnryb’s
      chapter. Exact M (g) for g ≥ 3, explicit upper formulas, a stopping criterion, extremizers and
      ﬁner classiﬁcations remain unresolved in this work. Historical novelty and exact priority are not
      certiﬁed.


1    The precise deduction and its boundary
Let S = Sg,1 be compact, connected and oriented, g ≥ 1, with boundary δ. Write Mod(S) for
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms ﬁxing δ pointwise, modulo isotopy relative to δ. Curves
are unoriented interior simple closed curves, considered up to isotopy in S. For a curve c, tc denotes
its positive Dehn twist; tδ means the twist along an interior boundary-parallel curve. An admissible
word is an ordered factorization

                             tc1 · · · tcn = tδ ,    i(cj , ck ) ≤ 1   for all j, k,                      (1)

where i is geometric intersection and every cj is non-null-homotopic. Repeated isotopy classes are
allowed and counted repeatedly in n. The support is the set of distinct classes occurring. Peripheral
curves are permitted in this broader convention.
Theorem 1 (Credited ﬁniteness deduction). For each ﬁxed g ≥ 1, the admissible ordered words
have ﬁnitely many classes under simultaneous conjugation by Mod(Sg,1 ). Their length maximum is
attained and satisﬁes
                                   4g(2g + 1) ≤ M (g) < ∞.
A peripheral factor occurs only as the singleton word. Every other word has only nonseparating
factors. The credited low-genus values are M (1) = 12 and M (2) = 40.

    The source asks separately for a maximum and whether length can be arbitrary: Wajnryb [1,
printed p. 126, PDF p. 133] attributes this restricted question to Smith. The theorem completely
answers the latter clause negatively under the stated essential-curve convention. The source de-
scribes actual 0/1 intersections; those conﬁgurations satisfy our geometric condition. Its genus-two
discussion expressly contemplates repetitions. The preceding unrestricted question (printed p. 125),
positive Artin equivalence, and subsequent A5 /Hurwitz questions are separate. We do not adopt
the motivating A5 /D6 classiﬁcation suggestions as theorems.
    This note computes no exact M (g) for g ≥ 3, explicit numerical upper formula, eﬀective stop-
ping rule, extremizer, Hurwitz, positive Artin, T-move or A5 /D6 classiﬁcation. It makes no ﬁrst-
discovery or worldwide continuing-openness claim. Fixed genus, one boundary twist, positivity,
essentiality and boundary ﬁxing are substantive hypotheses: disk-bounding twists would permit
identity insertions, and capping the boundary removes the positive-identity obstruction. For the
disk (g = 0), there are no admissible curves; only the empty word is possible if permitted.
    The inputs are credited precisely. Honda–Kazez–Matić [2] supply right-veering positivity; the
positive-identity obstruction is already attributed to Smith in [1, printed p. 124]. Cossu–Tringali [3,
Corollary 4.13] imply the exact ﬁnite-alphabet lemma, as veriﬁed below. Juvan–Malnič–Mohar [5,
Theorem 3.3] bound bordered curve-system cardinality; Malestein–Rivin–Theran [6, Proposition
4.1 and Section 6] use the mod-two/disjointness mechanism. Cahn–Fanoni–Petri [7, Lemma 10
and Section 3.2] give the ribbon/complement mechanism for a single curve on a punctured surface.
Section 3 explicitly adapts it to disconnected marked unions on a pointwise bordered surface. Smith
[8] and Baykur–Monden–Van Horn-Morris [9] supply the ﬁnal low-genus interfaces. No new generic
method or theorem is claimed.


2    Positivity and ﬁnite alphabets
Lemma 2. On Sg,1 , no nonempty product of positive twists about non-null-homotopic curves is
the identity.

Proof. For proper oriented arcs starting at the same boundary point and ending at the same
prescribed boundary point, compare lifts from a ﬁxed lift of the starting point. Write a ≤ b when
b is weakly right of a; this reverses the inequality notation, but not the geometry, in [2, Deﬁnition
2.1]. Boundary-ﬁxing orientation-preserving maps preserve this order. Right-veering maps satisfy
a ≤ f (a) for every proper arc. They form a monoid, since a ≤ h(a) ≤ f (h(a)).
   Every positive non-null-homotopic twist is right-veering by [2, Lemma 2.5]. Its stated positive
monoid includes separating and peripheral curves: the proof begins with an annulus, extends by
handles, and treats the remaining separating twists through positive nonseparating expressions.
This premise does not substitute the narrower nonseparating convention of [9].
    The action on all endpoint-relative proper arc classes is faithful. Choose disjoint arcs with
distinct endpoints cutting S into a disk. A map ﬁxing every arc class can be isotoped relative to
the boundary to ﬁx these arcs successively, and then pointwise; the remaining disk map is isotopic
to the identity relative to its boundary. A non-null-homotopic twist is nontrivial: a proper crossing
arc detects its annular winding. For a separating curve away from the boundary, choose an arc
entering and returning from the separated region. Thus such a right-veering twist t strictly moves
some arc a. Its inverse sends t(a) strictly left and is not right-veering. If t1 · · · tn = 1 with n ≥ 1,
then t−1
       1 = t2 · · · tn is right-veering, including the empty residual product, a contradiction. The
disk case is vacuous, and the annulus case follows directly from its integer twist number.

Lemma 3 (Finite-alphabet prior corollary). Let G be a group and P ⊂ G be conjugation invariant,
with no nonempty product from P equal to 1. Assign the letters of a ﬁnite alphabet A values in P ,
allowing repeated values. For any h ∈ G, only ﬁnitely many ordered A-words evaluate to h.

Derivation from Cossu–Tringali. Let Q be the submonoid generated by P , with 1 adjoined. It is
normal and Q ∩ Q−1 = {1}, since expressions for a nontrivial q and q −1 would give a nonempty
positive identity. Deﬁne
                                  x ≤ y ⇐⇒ yx−1 ∈ Q.
Reﬂexivity and transitivity follow from the monoid property and antisymmetry from reducedness.
Right multiplication preserves the order because (yv)(xv)−1 = yx−1 ; left multiplication preserves
it because (uy)(ux)−1 = u(yx−1 )u−1 ∈ Q. Translations also preserve strict inequality by injectivity.
This is a two-sided invariant partial order, with no total-order assertion.
    Let H be the submonoid generated by the ﬁnitely many chosen letter values, with the restricted
order. Each member is at least 1, and the only order-unit (element order-equivalent to 1) is 1.
Hence H is strongly positive in [3, Deﬁnition 2.3]. It is ﬁnitely generated and therefore weakly
locally ﬁnitely generated up to order-units: the germ at a target is generated by its divisors, and
[3, Deﬁnition 4.1, Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3] give this implication. Concretely, a divisor
expressed in the ﬁnite generators uses only generators that themselves divide the target, which
supplies ﬁnite generation of the germ here.
    Corollary 4.13 of [3] now makes this premonoid FF-atomic. In the source’s deﬁnitions, an order-
atom is an order-nonunit that cannot be a product of two order-nonunits; here it is an ordinary
indecomposable nonidentity element. Atomicity gives an atomic expression for each nonidentity
element. FF counts atomic factorizations modulo the source’s equivalence under arbitrary matching
of equivalent letters. Such equivalence preserves length; Deﬁnition 3.2 and Remark 3.3(2) therefore
give BF-atomicity, namely a ﬁnite nonempty set of atomic lengths for each nonunit. This use
retains the source’s atomic theory and does not assume the chosen letters are atoms.
    If h ∈
         / H, its ﬁber is empty; if h = 1, only the empty word occurs. Otherwise choose one ﬁxed
nonempty atomic expansion for each chosen letter value. Replacing every letter in a word for h by
its expansion gives an atomic word for h. If B(h) is the largest atomic length, the original word
has length at most B(h), because expansions never erase letters. Only ﬁnitely many A-words have
those lengths. The matching equivalence in CT can identify permutations; the BF length argument,
not an identiﬁcation with ordered equality, proves our conclusion. Neither normality nor a left-duo
property of H is required: the order uses the ambient normal cone Q. The target h need not be
central.

prior corollary, with no numerical or algorithmic upper bound extracted.


3    Bounded supports and ﬁnite support orbits
Lemma 4. An admissible support has at most

                                        Ng = 22g (3g − 2) + 1

distinct classes, and there are ﬁnitely many support orbits under Mod(Sg,1 ).

Proof. Remove the at most one peripheral class. Partition the other classes by H1 (S; F2 ), which
has 22g elements. Equal homology classes have intersection pairing [c] · [d] = [c] · [c] = 0, by
alternation. Geometric intersection has this parity, so i(c, d) ≤ 1 forces i(c, d) = 0. Each ﬁber,
including the zero ﬁber containing separating curves, is a collection of disjoint nonisotopic essential
nonperipheral classes. It extends to a pants decomposition and has at most 3g − 2 members.
Summing and restoring the peripheral class proves the coarse bound. This is the mod-two support
mechanism of [6]; [5, Theorem 3.3] supplies the earlier general bordered-system cardinality result.
Ng counts distinct classes, not occurrences or length.
   For the orbit assertion, choose a hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary, possible since χ(S) =
1 − 2g < 0. Simple geodesic representatives of nonperipheral classes realize all pairwise geometric
minima simultaneously. Diﬀerent geodesics cannot share an interval or be tangent at a meeting. At
a multiple crossing their distinct local branches have pairwise alternating endpoints on a suﬃciently
small disk. A small generic perturbation, preserving endpoint order, separates concurrence into
ordinary double crossings with exactly one crossing for each participating pair. For example, slightly
translate the distinct straight local branches generically: every former pair still crosses in the disk
and no triple concurrence remains. Keep other branches outside the disk. Place a peripheral
representative in a separate collar. The total crossings are at most m(m − 1)/2, m ≤ Ng .
    Encode the resulting union as a graph with four-valent crossing vertices and one auxiliary
bivalent vertex on each isolated circle. If v4 , v2 are their counts, the number of edges is 2v4 + v2 .
Label all constituent curve cycles, record each vertex’s cyclic orientation order, and record its
opposite crossing pairing. With bounded graph size and labels, there are ﬁnitely many such marked
ribbon graphs. The record determines the oriented regular neighborhood R and its marked curves.
An abstract intersection graph alone would not do this.
    Record also the partition of boundary circles of R among complementary components, each
component’s genus, and which contains the original boundary δ. There are ﬁnitely many boundary
circles, ﬁnitely many partitions, and each complementary genus is at most g. The number of

complementary components is bounded by the number of those circles plus one. Empty support is
separate and harmless.
    Two embeddings having the same record are related by an orientation-preserving homeomor-
phism. Start with the marked ribbon-neighborhood map. Classiﬁcation of compact oriented sur-
faces gives homeomorphisms of matching complementary components with their boundaries as-
signed as prescribed. Adjust these maps in disjoint collars to match the actual circle maps on ∂R:
orientation-compatible circle homeomorphisms are isotopic, and the isotopy extends over a collar.
This covers annular complements as well. A twist integer can change a chosen gluing homeomor-
phism, but produces no further invariant of the support’s ambient-homeomorphism orbit. The
matching maps then glue. Finally adjust in a collar of δ disjoint from the curves to make its
boundary restriction the identity.
    This explicitly adapts the prior ribbon/complement argument of [7, Lemma 10], whose literal
setting is a single curve on a punctured surface: our neighborhood can be disconnected, the simple
cycles and isolated circles are marked, and the original boundary is distinguished and ultimately
ﬁxed pointwise. Disk complements, non-ﬁlling unions and annular pieces are allowed; no no-disk
hypothesis from a diﬀerent homotopy statement is imported. These ﬁnite records prove the required
support-orbit ﬁniteness.

    Apply Lemma 3 to the set P of all positive twists about non-null-homotopic curves. Conjugation
by orientation-preserving maps preserves sign and essentiality. Lemma 2 excludes a positive identity.
Thus each ﬁxed ﬁnite curve alphabet has ﬁnitely many words for any given target.
    Choose one representative Cr of each support orbit from Lemma 4. Simultaneous conjugation
carries an admissible word to an ordered word over the twists about Cr . Its product remains tδ ,
because f tδ f −1 = tδ for a boundary-ﬁxing f . This is where centrality enters; it was unnecessary for
the generic ﬁber lemma. The ﬁnite union, over r, of these ﬁnite word ﬁbers contains a representative
of every admissible simultaneous-conjugacy class.
    The even chain of 2g curves has the classical relation

                                        (ta1 · · · ta2g )4g+2 = tδ

displayed in [1, printed p. 125, relation (3)]. Adjacent curves intersect once and other pairs are
disjoint; repetition in the power adds zero intersection between repeated classes. Its length is
2g(4g + 2) = 4g(2g + 1). The admissible length set is therefore nonempty and ﬁnite, establishing
the ﬁniteness and lower-bound part of Theorem 1.

Suppose a peripheral factor occurs m ≥ 1 times. Centrality moves them to the front, giving

singleton assertion in every genus.
    Assume now g ≥ 2 and there are no peripheral factors. If a separating factor curve c occurs,
every closed curve crosses c an even number of times, so the intersection condition forces c disjoint
from every factor class. Cap δ with a disk. All factor curves remain essential: if a curve bounded
a disk afterward, that disk either avoids the cap and was already a disk in S, or contains the cap
and gives an annulus to δ before capping. Both cases were excluded. In particular, a nonperipheral
essential separating c has positive genus on both sides after capping.

    The capped nonempty positive identity is the monodromy of a nontrivial genus-g Lefschetz
ﬁbration over S 2 . Explicitly attach the ordered positive Lefschetz handles to a ﬁber product over
a disk, one per factor, with framing one less than the ﬁber framing, and close the base by a second
disk using the identity total monodromy. The one-boundary lift supplies a section of square −1.
All capped vanishing cycles are essential, so the ﬁbration is relatively minimal; a section sphere of
square −1 does not violate relative minimality. Its intersection number with a regular ﬁber is one.
The ﬁber class is therefore homologically nonzero, and the positive Thurston–Gompf symplectic
realization applies, as summarized in [8, PDF p. 1].
   Smith’s common-invariant-essential-curve obstruction [8, Theorem 1.3, Proposition 4.2 and
Corollary 4.3] applies to this nontrivial, relatively minimal genus-at-least-two positive ﬁbration.
The essential capped c is disjoint from every vanishing cycle and hence invariant under all mon-
odromies, a contradiction. This is a credited theorem interface and is separate from the ﬁniteness
proof.
    Only the singleton invariant-curve part of Smith’s lift argument is needed. The twists can be
supported away from c, pushing the twist along c itself to a parallel curve. Choose lifts ﬁxing a lift
of c; their circle actions ﬁx its two ideal endpoints and have the same directed monotonicity on the
intervening intervals. Positive motion cannot cancel. A product representing the identity mapping
class is a deck action; a nontrivial hyperbolic deck action ﬁxing those two endpoints moves in op-
posite directions on the two intervals, whereas the identity deck action cannot equal this nontrivial
positive product. No ﬁxed-point-count assertion is used. In particular, the countable-ﬁxed-points
wording in the retained Smith preprint is not adopted: a ﬁxed positive-genus complementary sub-
surface may have a Cantor limit set. This caveat does not change the required singleton obstruction.
    Thus every nonsingleton admissible word uses nonseparating curves. In genus one this holds
directly. The abelianization of Mod(S1,1 ) is Z, assigning a nonseparating positive twist weight one
and tδ weight twelve; see [9, Proposition 1 and its proof]. All nonperipheral genus-one words have
length twelve, attained by the two-curve chain, so M (1) = 12.
    The length function in [9] counts nonseparating factors. The separating exclusion is consequently
necessary before applying their Theorem A in genus two with one boundary component. Its value is
forty, and the four-curve chain attains forty under our pairwise restriction. Hence M (2) = 40. These
values are prior-result deductions, not A5 /D6 or Hurwitz classiﬁcations. BMVHM’s unrestricted
inﬁnite-length regime for g ≥ 3 imposes no pairwise intersection restriction and is compatible with
the present ﬁniteness. The external gauge-theoretic dependencies behind their bounds are not
independently re-proved in this note.


5    Veriﬁcation, provenance and status
The accompanying veriﬁcation kit retains the six original authored ﬁles, complete mathematical
and priority reports and frozen derivations, root adoption records, and selected source/custody
metadata. Historical verdicts remain unchanged and describe their own earlier scopes. The original
six-ﬁle edition contains no executable. The kit’s portable checks are newly authored adapters of the
subsequent reviewers’ ﬁnite programs, with explicit guards active under ordinary and optimized
Python. They reproduce ﬁnite algebraic insertion, parity, ribbon, multiple-crossing, normal-cone
and nonerasing-expansion controls. They do not enumerate all surfaces or words, prove the universal
theorem computationally, or replay omitted historical programs. Exact copying and individual pin
omissions are documented.
