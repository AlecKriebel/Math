               A modular-storage proof of NP-completeness for
                   chip-ﬁring halting on simple digraphs
                                               Alec Kriebel
                                         Independent researcher
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                     Version 1.0, 16 October 2026


                                                  Abstract
          We give a self-contained proof that ordinary conservative chip-ﬁring halting is NP-complete
      on ﬁnite loopless simple strongly connected digraphs. Hardness holds with polynomial numeri-
      cal total initial chips and a speciﬁed vertex whose deletion leaves an acyclic graph of depth at
      most one. A separate construction forbids antiparallel arcs. The proof combines the primed-
      toppler mechanism of Holroyd–Levine–Winkler with the classical prime/Boolean/clause encod-
      ing of Stockmeyer–Meyer, also reconstructed by Gramlich. We prove the physical conservative
      realization, its legal checkpoint dynamics and the arbitrary-schedule implication in full; NP
      membership is credited to Farrell–Levine and reproved with an explicit certiﬁcate bound. This
      is an attributed proof of the historical simple-digraph question. Exact full-result and realization
      priority and preexisting worldwide openness remain unknown after a bounded audit; no ﬁrst-
      ness is claimed. AI tools were used extensively. The paper is unrefereed and has not received
      external human peer review.


1    Question, conventions and attribution
An input is a ﬁnite loopless digraph G = (V, E) with N = |V | ≥ 2, at most one arc per ordered
pair, strong connectivity, and a conﬁguration x ∈ ZV≥0 encoded in binary. A vertex v of outdegree
dv may ﬁre when xv ≥ dv : it loses dv chips and sends one along each outgoing arc. All vertices obey
this rule; there is no dissipative sink. A conﬁguration is stable if xv < dv for every v. The question
HALTING asks whether some legal ﬁnite ﬁring word reaches a stable conﬁguration. Lemma 1
below supplies the usual schedule independence, without a fairness assumption. An oriented graph
additionally has no antiparallel pair.
    Farrell–Levine [1, §3.1, Table 1] explicitly posed the simple-digraph NP-completeness question
while proving membership in NP and hardness for binary directed multigraphs. Their historical
question is the target here; an archived open-problem label is not evidence of current worldwide
openness. We establish the following exact statement.
Theorem 1. HALTING is NP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions
on the input class above. Hardness persists when a speciﬁed root r has the property that G − r
contains no directed path of two arcs and is acyclic, and when the numerical total initial chips is
polynomial in the 3-SAT input length. The same assertions hold on the oriented subclass.
    The ingredients require precise credit. The literal multiplicities in Lemma 2 specialize Holroyd–
Levine–Winkler’s parallel primed-toppler construction [2, Lemma 4.1]. The Boolean residues and lo-
cal clause products are the classical Stockmeyer–Meyer encoding [3, Theorem 6.1], as reconstructed

by Gramlich alone [4, Fact 3], presented here in complement form. We claim neither encoding as
new. Root-last stabilization and conserved-mass comparison have antecedents in Farrell’s thesis [5,

initial chips are prior [6]. The argument below supplies a complete conservative graph realization
and SAT equivalence without asserting priority for that realization or its oriented version.


2    Least action and a polynomial certiﬁcate
Put ∆v,v = dv and ∆v,u = −1 for u → v, with other entries zero. A word with ﬁring-count vector
g changes x to x − ∆g.
Lemma 1 (Classical least action). If f ∈ ZV≥0 has stable z = x − ∆f , every legal word has ﬁring
counts at most f coordinatewise. In particular a nonnegative stable z is a sound certiﬁcate of
halting, even if f is not given as a legal word.
Proof. At a ﬁrst ﬁring that would exceed fv , the current count at v is fv and all other counts
are at most their coordinates of f . Nonnegative incoming arc counts put the chips at v at most

xv − dv fv + u→v fu = zv < dv , contradicting legality. Thus a legal word has length at most v fv .
Repeatedly ﬁring any legal vertex must stop at a stable conﬁguration. Conversely, an inﬁnite legal
word rules out every nonnegative stabilizing vector. Early voluntary stopping at an active state is
not halting.

method. Strong connectivity and the directed matrix-tree theorem give a strictly positive primitive

vertex, divided by their common gcd. At each nonroot tree vertex there are at most N − 1 outgoing
choices, so

PDF page 10 of [1] omits this slack; the self-contained argument here retains it. This observation
is about that line, not a rejection of the prior membership theorem or a certiﬁcation of every other
source proof.

ordinary conservative chip-ﬁring implementation; the conservative-gate distinction is explicit in [2,

4    The classical Boolean/CRT predicates
Use an explicitly encoded 3-CNF formula of length L, with clauses of length at most three. Reject
malformed encodings, remove duplicate literals, delete tautological clauses and relabel the variable
identiﬁers actually occurring. An empty clause is a ﬁxed NO case; no clauses is a ﬁxed YES case.
Binary names or declarations of unused variables are never expanded. Otherwise there are n ≥ 1
variables, every clause uses 1 ≤ t ≤ 3 distinct variables, and the numbers of variables and clauses
are O(L).

6    Legal checkpoints and the SAT equivalence
Analyze one legal order only for the proof: at a checkpoint all nonroot vertices are stable and
the returns empty. If r is legal, ﬁre it once, then process all counters, then all returns, before
considering r again. A stable counter gains exactly one chip from that root ﬁring and has no other
incoming arc; it ﬁres at most once, precisely when it reaches qb . After counters have been processed,
each return legally ﬁres once per chip it holds. These ﬁnite phases restore every emitted chip to
the root, and no counter is reactivated before another root ﬁring. Delaying a legal root while these
other legal vertices ﬁre is permitted in ordinary asynchronous chip-ﬁring. No sink rule has been
substituted.
    Inductively, at checkpoint k, after k root ﬁrings, each oﬀset-a counter holds (a + k) mod qb , all
returns are zero, and the root holds

                                   C − S(k) = Q + B − 1 − S(k).

Using integral storage and S(k) ≤ B gives the exact equivalences

             r stable ⇐⇒ S(k) > B − 1 ⇐⇒ S(k) = B ⇐⇒ every bank accepts k.                          (6)

A bank misses its maximum by exactly qb when its predicate is false. These positive deﬁcits cannot
cancel, proving the last equivalence.
    If the formula is satisﬁable, CRT provides a ﬁnite successful k ≥ 0. The canonical legal process
either stops earlier, already proving halting, or reaches that checkpoint and is stable there. If it is
unsatisﬁable, each checkpoint permits a root ﬁring followed by ﬁnite legal nonroot phases. Their
concatenation is an inﬁnite legal word. Lemma 1 rules out every terminating legal word, regardless
of schedule and without fairness. We have therefore proved

            3-CNF is satisﬁable     ⇐⇒     the constructed ordinary conﬁguration halts.

The canonical order and possibly enormous stopping time belong to the proof, not to the polynomial
reduction algorithm.

NO instance with a circulating chip. For the empty conjunction use the same graph with (0, 0),
a stable YES instance. These cases make the reduction total on encoded strings and preserve all
main graph restrictions, including root deletion. For nontrivial formulas the construction above
applies.
    For the oriented version use P returns, omit every counter-to-root arc, and instead give a
modulus-qb counter the qb arcs v → t1 , . . . , v → tqb . Keep r → v and all tj → r. Every arc follows
the three-part order root–counter–return–root, so no opposite pair, loop or repeated ordered pair
occurs. Every counter reaches r through a return, and a largest-bank counter reaches all P returns;
strong connectivity follows. Root deletion again leaves only counter-to-return arcs. Its exact counts

entire oriented case, including trivial inputs. Together with membership, Theorem 1 follows.


8    Priority limits and veriﬁcation
The bounded source audit found exact antecedents for the bank priming and CRT encoding, which
are explicitly credited above. It did not establish a source statement or proved corollary containing
the entire restricted theorem. That is not evidence of worldwide novelty. Priority of the full result,
the conservative simple realization, the oriented realization and any remaining method contribution
remains unknown. No claim that the question was globally open immediately before this work is
made.
    Several nearby interfaces must be kept separate. Generalized rotor reachability [8] has branch-
ing arc states and a speciﬁed target; ordinary chip-ﬁring has ﬁxed outputs and asks for any stable
endpoint. Bond–Levine’s sandpilization [9, Deﬁnition 6.5, Corollary 6.6] preserves halting on all
inputs, not automatically one reset-state input. Hardness for containing weighted-net classes does
not automatically restrict to one consuming transition per place, degree thresholds, unit outputs,
conservation and a supplied marking. Esparza’s sole-author survey [10] does not remove those
hypotheses. The full Mayr–Weihmann construction and complete Hujsa–Devillers weighted-net
proofs were unavailable to this audit; their possible additional implications remain unknown. Op-
timization over added chip distributions, inﬁnite backgrounds and binary arc expansion are also
diﬀerent decision interfaces. These statements record exact transfer gaps, rather than exclude every
possible earlier derivation.
