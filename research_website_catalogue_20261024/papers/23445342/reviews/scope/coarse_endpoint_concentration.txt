Coarse endpoint concentration at every diverging scale
for one-dimensional space-time random walks
Alec Kriebel
Independent researcher
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
18 October 2026
Abstract
For a nearest-neighbor walk on Z in a ﬁxed i.i.d. space-time environment with transition
probabilities strictly between zero and one, we prove that its quenched and averaged endpoint
laws agree, after coarse graining into deterministic intervals of any diverging length, outside an
environment event of probability smaller than every inverse power of time. No uniform ellipticity
or quantitative rate of divergence of the interval length is required. This is the aﬃrmative
one-dimensional assertion contrary to Conjecture 1 in Berger’s contribution to Oberwolfach
Report 38/2009. The proof combines replica overlap estimates, environmental martingales,
same-parity coalescence and concentration over a short ﬁnal segment. Priority qualiﬁcation:
novelty and historical priority remain unresolved. A bounded literature audit established no
exact full anticipation, but four relevant older original full texts could not be accessed. We
make no claim of ﬁrstness, independent discovery or that the conjecture remained open until
this work. Established late-layer and coalescing methods, including those of Dunlap–Gu and
Barraquand–Rychnovsky, are explicitly credited.

The constants may depend on the ﬁxed environment law. The eventual threshold may also depend

Here and below superpolynomially small means O(N − K) for every ﬁxed K > 0, with its own
constant and eventual threshold. The theorem includes biased averaged walks, nearest-neighbor
parity, integer cells containing MN sites, real interval lengths and arbitrarily slowly diverging
nonmonotone scales. It does not assert a simultaneous bound for environment-selected partitions,
a law-uniform bound as ellipticity degenerates, or a result for static or correlated environments.
Berger’s report [ 1, pp. 2151–2152, Theorem 1 and Conjecture 1] states the analogous concen-
tration assertion in transverse dimension at least two and conjectures its failure in dimension one.
Theorem 1 gives the aﬃrmative assertion for its i.i.d., nearest-neighbor, elliptic one-dimensional
model. A deterministic elliptic law has µN = αN ; the theorem also covers every nondegenerate law
under ( 1), so its conclusion is not based on that trivial special case.
The diﬀerence-walk and quenched-mean mechanisms already occur in Balázs–Rassoul-Agha–
Seppäläinen [ 2]. Independent particles and coupling in dynamical environments are treated in
Joseph–Rassoul-Agha–Seppäläinen [ 5]. Dunlap–Gu [ 3] isolate a short ﬁnal layer in a Gaussian-times-
stationary-corrector local limit theorem for a continuum stochastic ﬂow with molecular Brownian
noise. Barraquand–Rychnovsky [ 4, Section 1.4, Proposition 1.13 and Section 4.5] prove a full-space
beta-environment local limit theorem and use ﬁnal-layer decomposition and ordered coalescence.
Those mechanisms, as well as replica calculations, martingale exposure and bounded diﬀerences,
are established methods. All analytic inputs needed here are proved below.
The modern distribution-valued ﬂuctuation limits of Drillick–Parekh [ 6] and Parekh [ 7] are also
relevant. The inspected statements concern diﬀerent observables and convergence topologies and
do not state ( 2)’s shrinking-cell ℓ1 conclusion with a superpolynomial environment tail. Nearest-
neighbor parity is not a reason to exclude that framework: Parekh [ 7, Section 6.1, Remark 6.2]
handles it by two-step kernels on 2Z. A qualitative local or weak limit does not, by itself, supply
every inverse-power probability bound.

6 Scope, veriﬁcation and disclosure
The proof uses the ﬁxed-law inequalities 0 < δ ≤ a ≤ 1/ 4, fresh space-time independence and
bounded nearest-neighbor jumps. It uses neither inverse moments of p or 1 − p nor a common
positive lower support bound. Same-parity coupling is suﬃcient because the old quenched and
averaged laws have the same reachable parity. An arrow law with p ∈ { 0, 1} has δ = 0 and is
excluded; constants and thresholds need not remain bounded as a family of elliptic laws approaches
such a law.
The asymptotic claim follows from the proofs above. The accompanying veriﬁcation package
includes ﬁnite exact calculations of replica transitions, overlap moments, martingale identities,
parity residue bounds, coalescing kernels and strip inﬂuences; such calculations check identities
and edge cases and are not a substitute for the analytic argument.
