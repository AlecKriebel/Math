L1651@P33: Homomorphisms of algebraic groups: representability and rigidity
L1652@P33: Michel Brion
L1653@P33: This talk is based on the preprint [1] with the same title. A motivation comes from
L1654@P33: the problem of classifying algebraic group actions on (say) projective varieties, that
L1655@P33: we briefly present.
L1656@P33: We work over a field k of characteristic p ≥ 0. Given a projective variety X,
L1657@P33: there exists a group scheme AutX such that AutX(R) = AutR−sch(XR) for any
L1658@P33: algebra R, where XR denotes the R-scheme obtained from X by base change.
L1659@P33: Moreover, the automorphism group scheme AutX is locally of finite type. As a
L1660@P33: consequence, the connected component of the identity in AutX is an algebraic
L1661@P33: group (i.e., a group scheme of finite type) that we denote by Aut0
L1662@P33-34: X.Algebraic Groups 1121
L1663@P34: An action of an algebraic group G on X is given by a homomorphism (i.e., a
L1664@P34: morphism of group schemes) f : G → AutX. Two actions are equivalent if
L1665@P34: the corresponding homomorphisms are conjugate by an element of Aut(X) =
L1666@P34: AutX(k). So classifying G-actions on X amounts to describing the orbit space
L1667@P34: Homgp(G, AutX)/ Aut(X) for the conjugation action. If G is connected, then we
L1668@P34: may replace AutX with Aut0
L1669@P34: X.
L1670@P34: This motivates the following:
L1671@P34: Questions 1. Let G and H be algebraic groups.
L1672@P34: (i) Is there a scheme M such that HomR−gp(GR, HR) = M(R) for any algebra R?
L1673@P34: (ii) If so, H acts on M via its action on itself by conjugation. How to describe
L1674@P34: the orbits?
L1675@P34: (In fact, one should allow H to be locally of finite type for applications to
L1676@P34: G-actions as above. We refer to [1] for this technically more complicated setting).
L1677@P34: Question (i) is equivalent to the representability of the functor of homomorphisms Homgp(G, H). For example, Homgp(Gm, Gm) is represented by the constant scheme Zk by rigidity of tori. In particular, the scheme M is not necessarily affine, nor of finite type (if it exists). But M is locally of finite type, since
L1678@P34: R 7→ HomR−gp(GR, HR) commutes with direct limits.
L1679@P34: Also, M has an action of Endgp(G)×Endgp(H) by composition. We will mostly
L1680@P34: use the action of H by inner automorphisms. If H is commutative, then M is a
L1681@P34: group scheme via pointwise multiplication.
L1682@P34: A related parameter space exists when p = 0 and G, H are linear: then the set
L1683@P34: Homgp(G, H)(¯k) has the structure of an affine finite-dimensional ind-variety, see
L1684@P34: [5, Part 8]. Yet the functor Homgp(G, H) contains more information than its sets
L1685@P34: of K-points for all field extensions K/k, and is not necessarily representable. For
L1686@P34: example, every homomorphism Ga,K → Gm,K is trivial, but Homgp(Ga, Gm) is
L1687@P34: not representable (see e.g. [8, Ex. 1.1]). More generally, given a connected linear
L1688@P34: algebraic group H, one can show that Homgp(Ga, H) is representable if and only
L1689@P34: if H is unipotent (when p = 0), resp. trivial (when p > 0).
L1690@P34: Next, we introduce a class of algebraic groups for which Questions (i) and (ii)
L1691@P34: have positive answers. We say that an algebraic group G (possibly non-affine) is
L1692@P34: linearly reductive if every finite-dimensional representation of G is semi-simple.
L1693@P34: Examples of such groups include tori, abelian varieties (since their representations are trivial), and reductive groups in characteristic 0. The affine linearly reductive groups have a well-known structure, due to Nagata and Demazure-Gabriel
L1694@P34: (see [2, IV.3.3]); the general case is analyzed in [1].
L1695@P34: We may now state our main result:
L1696@P34: Theorem 2. Let G be a linearly reductive group, and H a smooth algebraic group.
L1697@P34: Then Homgp(G, H) is represented by a smooth scheme M. Moreover, every Horbit in M is open and the stabilizer is smooth.
L1698@P34-35: The openness of orbits is a rigidity result: the only way to deform a homomorphism is via conjugation on the target.1122 Oberwolfach Report 20/2021
L1699@P35: The above theorem is due to Grothendieck when G is of multiplicative type and H
L1700@P35: is affine over an arbitrary base scheme. See [3, Exp. XI] and a recent generalization
L1701@P35: by Romagny in [9]. Grothendieck’s proof is based on “local” methods (from what is
L1702@P35: now known as deformation theory) together with the restriction of homomorphisms
L1703@P35: to n-torsion subgroups of G for all positive integers n. By contrast, our proof
L1704@P35: uses many structure results for algebraic groups over a field, together with some
L1705@P35: “global” arguments such as a rigidity lemma adapted from the theory of abelian
L1706@P35: varieties.
L1707@P35: Here is a first application of the main result:
L1708@P35: Proposition 3. Assume k algebraically closed. Let G be a linearly reductive group,
L1709@P35: and H a smooth algebraic group. Then the natural map
L1710@P35: Homgp(G, H)/H(k) −→ HomK−gp(GK, HK)/H(K)
L1711@P35: is a bijection for any algebraically closed field extension K/k.

L1713@P35: Proof sketch. The scheme M representing Homgp(G, H) is a disjoint union of
L1714@P35: open orbits of k-rational points. So the connected components of M are the orbits
L1715@P35: of the neutral component H0. Thus, we obtain

L1717@P35: where π0(M) denotes the set of connected components of M.
L1718@P35: A further application is the following:
L1719@P35: Proposition 4. Assume k algebraically closed. Let G be a finite group of order
L1720@P35: prime to p, and H an algebraic group. Then Homgp(G, H)/H(k) is finite.
L1721@P35: Proof sketch. Given a finite scheme X, the functor R 7→ HomR−sch(XR, HR) is represented by an algebraic group: the Weil restriction RX/k(H). As a consequence,
L1722@P35: the functor Homgp(G, H) is represented by a closed subscheme M ⊂ RG/k(H).

L1724@P35: main result.

L1726@P35: equivalence classes of G-actions on a projective variety X, if AutX is algebraic.
L1727@P35: But this fails in general, since there exist smooth projective complex varieties X
L1728@P35: such that AutX is discrete and has infinitely many conjugacy classes of involutions.
L1729@P35: Examples of such varieties were first constructed by Lesieutre in dimension 6 (see

L1732@P35: Proposition 5. Let G be an algebraic group. Assume that the main result holds
L1733@P35: for any smooth affine algebraic group H. Then G is linearly reductive.
