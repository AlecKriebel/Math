Title: Polyhedral parametrizations of canonical bases & cluster duality
Authors: Volker Genz, Gleb Koshevoy, Bea Schumann
Date: Mon Nov 20 07:35:54 2017

We establish the relation of the potential function constructed by Gross-Hacking-Keel-Kontsevich's and Berenstein-Kazhdan's decoration function on the open double Bruhat cell in the base affine space $G/\mathcal{N}$ of a simple, simply connected, simply laced algebraic group $G$. As a byproduct we derive explicit identifications of polyhedral parametrization of canonical bases of the ring of regular functions on $G/\mathcal{N}$ arising from the tropicalizations of the potential and decoration function with the classical string and Lusztig parametrizations.--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

L35: # Redundancy in string cone inequalities and multiplicities in potential functions on cluster varieties
L36: 
L37: Gleb Koshevoy Address: Institute of Information Transmission Problems Russian Academy of Sciences, National Research University Higher School of Economics, Russian Federation Email address: koshevoyga@gmail.com and Bea Schumann Address: Mathematical Institute, University of Cologne Email address: bschuman@math.uni-koeln.de
L38: ###### Abstract.
L39: 
L40: We study defining inequalities of string cones via a potential function on a reduced double Bruhat cell. We give a necessary criterion for the potential function to provide a minimal set of inequalities via tropicalization and conjecture an equivalence.
L41: 
L42: ###### Key words and phrases:
L43: 
L44: Cluster Algebras; Quantum Groups; Canonical Bases; String Polytopes
L45: ## Introduction
L46: Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be simple simply laced complex Lie algebra. To every reduced expression ${\bf i}$ of the longest element $w_{0}$ of the Weyl group of $\mathfrak{g}$ is associated a polyhedral cone $\mathcal{C}_{{\bf i}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ called the string cone. Here $N$ is the length of $w_{0}$. The string cones arise in many different contexts, e.g.
L47: they are closely connected to the dual canonical basis of the universal enveloping algebra of the negative part of $\mathfrak{g}$ ([【23†BZ93】]), may be seen as generalisations of Gelfand-Tsetlin cones ([【24†Lit98】]), play a great role in toric degenerations of flag varieties ([【25†FFL17】]) and are closely related to tensor product multiplicities of representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ ([【26†BZ01】]).
L48: This paper deals with the problem of determining a minimal set of inequalities for $\mathcal{C}_{{\bf i}}$, i.e. describing the facets of $\mathcal{C}_{{\bf i}}$. The way we approach this is as follows.
L49: If $\mathbf{j}$ is another reduced word of $w_{0}$ there is a piecewise linear bijection $\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{i}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{i}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{j}}.$ The cone $\mathcal{C}_{{\bf i}}$ consists of all $t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ such that for any reduced word ${\bf i}^{\prime}$ the last coordinate of $\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{i}}(t)$ is non-negative.
L50: This fact was used in [【27†GKS20】] to define a function $\varsigma_{{\bf i}}\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm}_{j}\mid j\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}]$ (see Definition 【28†3】) such that $\mathcal{C}_{{\bf i}}$ is given by all $t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ with $[\varsigma_{{\bf i}}]_{trop}(t)\geq 0$. Here trop means the tropicalization as defined in Section 【7†1.3】. We prove the following sufficient criterion.
L51: ###### Theorem 1 (Theorem 【29†2】).
L52: 
L53: If the exponents of all variables in $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}$ have absolute value less or equal to $1$, then the set of inequalities given by $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}]_{trop}$ is non-redundant.
L54: 
L55: We can refine the above thanks to the observation that the Laurent-polynomial $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}$ naturally splits into a sum of Laurent polynomials $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}=\sum_{i\in I}\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ where $I$ is the index set of simple roots of $\mathfrak{g}$.
L56: ###### Proposition 1 (Proposition 【30†3】).
L57: 
L58: The inequalities arising from $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}]_{trop}$ and $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},j}]_{trop}$ are independent, i.e. the set of inequalities given by $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}]_{trop}$ is non-redundant if and only if the sets inequalities given by $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}]_{trop}$ are non-redundant for all $i\in I$.
L59: Proposition 【30†3】 allows us to study each summand $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ independently. We call $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ multiplicity-free if the exponents of all variables have absolute value less or equal to $1$ and thus the monomials of $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ correspond to facets of the string cone via tropicalization.
L60: We provide several classes of examples of reduced words ${\bf i}$ where $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is multiplicity-free. In Theorem 【31†3】 we show that this is the case if ${\bf i}$ is simply-braided for $i$, a notion that was studied in [【32†SST18】]. Furthermore $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is multiplicity-free for all $i\in I$ if ${\bf i}$ is a nice word in the sense of Littelmann [【24†Lit98】] by Theorem 【33†4】.
L61: In the case that $\omega_{i}$ is a minuscule weight of $\mathfrak{g}$ we show that $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is given via Berenstein-Zelevinsky’s $\mathbf{i}$-trails in Section 【19†6.2】. Theorem 【34†5】 proves that $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is again multiplicity-free in that case. Thus, in particular, $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is always multiplicity-free for $\mathfrak{g}$ of type $A$.
L62: 
L63: We conjecture that Theorem 【29†2】 is indeed also a necessary criterion.
L64: ###### Conjecture 1 (Conjecture 【35†3】).
L65: 
L66: The inequalities arising from $[\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}]_{trop}$ are non-redundant if and only if $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is multplicity-free.
L67: Finally in Section 【20†7】 we provide an example for which $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is not multiplicity-free. In this example there is exactly one Laurent monomial of $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ whose tropicalization leads to a redundant inequality. We note that this monomial is the only monomial of $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ with a coefficient greater than $1$. This leads us to the final conjecture given a criterion to determine the facets of the string cone.
L68: ###### Conjecture 2.
L69: 
L70: (Conjecture 【36†4】) An inequalities arising from the tropicalization of a monomial of $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is redundant if and only if the coefficient of this monomial is greater than $1$.
L71: Note that this criterion cannot be seen merely in the tropicalization of $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ since coefficients do not play any role there. However, it is visible in the tropicalization wether $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i},i}$ is multiplicity-free or not. It would be very interesting to find a conceptual explanation of our result. In Remark 【37†3】 we suggest a relation to $F$-polynomials of cluster variables.
L72: The proofs of Theorem 【29†2】 and Proposition 【30†3】 are making use of the following fact proven in [【27†GKS20】]. The function $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}$ is the pullback of the potential function of [【38†GHKK18】] on the big reduced double Bruhat cell (see Proposition 【39†5】) by an isomorphism of tori. An important point here is that the potential function (expressed in appropriate torus coordinates) only has non-positive exponents (Proposition 【40†4】).
L73: The results may be obtained from this using cluster combinatorics.
L74: The paper is organized as follows. The first section introduces important notion related to reduced words and tropicalization. The second section deals with string cones and their defining inequalities and states our main results. The third section recalls the notion of $\mathcal{A}$- and $\mathcal{X}$-cluster varieties and potential functions in the sense of [【38†GHKK18】].
L75: In Section $4$ we restrict ourself to the big reduced double Bruhat cell as specific cluster variety and recall how to obtain string cone inequalities via potential functions. The proofs of Theorem 【29†2】 and Proposition 【30†3】 are obtained in the fifth section. The sixth section provides examples of reduced words for which our system of inequalities is non-redundant. In the final Section $7$ we provide an example of a redundancy corresponding to a multiplicity in $\varsigma_{\mathbf{i}}$.

L80: ## 1. Background
L81: ### 1.1. Notation
L82: For a positive integer $m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ we denote by $[m]$ the set $\{1,2,\ldots,m\}$. Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be simple simply laced complex Lie algebra of rank $n$, $I:=[n]$, $C=(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}$ its Cartan matrix and $\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}$ a Cartan subalgebra. We choose simple roots $\Delta^{+}=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ and simple coroots $\{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathfrak{h}$ with $\alpha_{i}(\alpha^{\vee}_{j})=c_{j,i}$.
L83: We denote by $\Delta^{+}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ the set of positive roots and by $\Delta$ the set of roots associated to $\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}$.
L84: The fundamental weights $\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ are given by $\omega_{i}(\alpha^{\vee}_{j})=\delta_{i,j}$. We denote by $P=\langle\omega_{i}\mid i\in[n]\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}$ the weight lattice of $\mathfrak{g}$ and by $P^{+}=\langle\omega_{i}\mid i\in[n]\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\subset P$ the set of dominant weights.
L85: The Langlands dual Lie algebra ${}^{L}\mathfrak{g}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ is the simple, simply laced complex Lie algebra with Cartan matrix $C$, Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}^{*}$, simple roots $\{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i\in I}$, simple coroots $\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}$ and $\alpha^{\vee}_{i}(\alpha_{j})=c_{i,j}$. The fundamental weights of ${}^{L}\mathfrak{g}$ are $\{\omega^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathfrak{h}$ where $\alpha_{i}(\omega^{\vee}_{j})=\delta_{i,j}$.
L86: ### 1.2. Weyl groups and reduced words
L87: 
L88: The Weyl group $W$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a Coxeter group generated by the simple reflections $s_{i}$ ($i\in I$) with relations
L89:  | $\displaystyle s_{i}^{2}$  | $\displaystyle=id,$  |
L90:  | $\displaystyle s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}$  | $\displaystyle=s_{i_{2}}s_{i_{1}}\qquad\,\,\,\text{if }c_{i_{1},i_{2}}=0\quad\text{ ($2$-term relation)},$  |
L91:  | $\displaystyle s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}s_{i_{1}}$  | $\displaystyle=s_{i_{2}}s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\quad\text{ if }c_{i_{1},i_{2}}=-1\quad\text{ ($3$-term relation)}.$  |
L92: 
L93: We sometimes call a $2$-term relation also a commutation relation.
