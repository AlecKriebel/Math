## Reconstruction and attempted falsification

### 1. Domain and genericity

W is the countable restricted wreath product N semidirect Z, where N is the direct sum of integer lamps. It is generated by a=a_0 and t, with t a_i t^-1=a_(i+1). It is not the uncountable unrestricted product or a Polish wreath product. The ambient action space is the homomorphism subspace of Homeo(C)^W. The homomorphism equations are closed because composition and inversion are continuous, so this is a closed Polish, hence Baire, space.

Genericity is a statement about this full action space. It does not mean a generic point of an individual phase space, a conull restriction, or a single comeager conjugacy class. The manuscript constructs a dense G_delta **subset** of actions with the required property; it need not prove that the entire set of hyperfinite actions is itself G_delta.

For each fixed continuous finite residue label c, the equations defining D(c,m) are clopen conditions. Compactness and the finite clopen fibres give a uniform separation scale for their output labels. The two generator equations imply the character equation for all group elements and their inverses. There are only countably many continuous maps to each finite target, because a clopen subset of compact Cantor space is a finite union from a countable clopen basis.

The open obligation U(c;m,n) is relative: either c is not an equivariant m-label, or it has a compatible n-label. Empty D(c,m) causes no problem. The density proof does not perturb the two generators independently and hope that all infinitely many wreath-product relations persist. Instead, the fibre product carries an exact group action, and conjugation by a homeomorphism gives another exact action.

For a prescribed finite symmetric set F of action coordinates, the refinement P contains Q, every alpha(g)^-1 Q for g in F, and the old c-fibres. Each nonempty P-atom is Cantor, and its inverse image in the fibre product is a nonempty finite disjoint union of Cantor copies, hence Cantor. The atomwise homeomorphism preserves the coarse location. Its two uses in the proof ensure beta(g)x and alpha(g)x lie in the same Q-atom. Including inverse elements in F also controls the homeomorphism topology. The second coordinate supplies exactly the required old-label compatibility.

The Baire intersection ranges over all continuous finite labels in advance. Consequently labels selected during the later factorial recursion are already covered; there is no uncountable intersection or circular choice of obligations. Compatible residues give a continuous map into the inverse limit. Its nonempty compact image is invariant under both integer directions and therefore is all of the profinite integers. No uniform Borel choice of the factor as the action varies, or of finite exhaustions as the action varies, is asserted or needed.

### 2. Slab coordinates and stabilizers

For a Borel height factor, the residue r_m determines the coordinate z=t^-r_m(x)x uniquely. Conversely (z,r) maps to t^r z. This is a Borel bijection with B_m times the finite set of residues, even if the action of N has nontrivial or varying stabilizers. A height factor itself prevents a nonzero-shift element from fixing a point, but the theorem does not assume that the full W-action is free.

The lamp edge projects to a_-r on the base, not a_r. Interior shifts leave the base coordinate unchanged. The cut at the final residue is essential: an uncut wrap sends the base through t^m. The subgroup generated by the m indicated lamps is abstractly Z^m; its action may have a kernel, which the cited polynomial-growth input permits.

The graph is Borel, has degree at most four after loops are removed, and has finite radius balls. Its distance-at-most-k relation is a finite union of graphs of partial Borel move words. Hence the extended metric is Borel and proper in the exact finite-cardinality sense used by CJMST. Properness does not mean that every component is finite. For example, for the left-translation action on W with integer shift height, the width-one slab already has an infinite a_0-line. The proof correctly uses finite asymptotic dimension, not a false assertion of finite slab classes.

### 3. Finite Borel asymptotic dimension

For each fixed m, CJMST Corollary 5.5 applies to every Borel action of Z^m, with a finite dimension depending on m. The finite-move characterization gives a fixed finite palette and uniformly finite monochromatic components for each chosen finite move set. Disjointizing a finite Borel cover only shrinks components.

A slab path of length at most R projects to a K_m word of length at most ceil(R). Thus each same-colour distance-R edge projects to an edge of the appropriate base threshold graph. Intermediate vertices along a shortest path are not required to have that colour. The lifted threshold component has at most m C_R points. A simple path in that threshold component then bounds its original metric diameter by R(m C_R−1). The case 0<R<1 consists of singletons, and m=1 is covered without a vertical edge. There is no claim of a common dimension or a common component bound across all slab widths.

This is the correct finite-colour criterion for Borel asymptotic dimension, not the weaker assertion that individual points have finite components, and not a measured version of that criterion.

### 4. Compatibility and the regular part

When m divides M, any residue excluded at width M was already excluded at width m. Therefore G_m is a subgraph of G_M, and rho_M≤rho_m on finite old distances. This verifies the exact uniform compatibility inequality in CJMST Theorem 7.3. That theorem requires each dimension to be finite; it does not require the dimensions to have a common upper bound. Its proof was inspected, including the varying dimension parameters.

The embedded integers are a countable Borel subset of the profinite integers. Both their full preimage and its complement are invariant under all W-moves. On the complement, a forward shift edge excluded at every factorial width would have height exactly−1. This is impossible there. An admitted edge stays admitted. Since a generator word has only finitely many edges, all its edges appear together at one late stage. Invariance ensures that restricting the metric to the regular part does not discard intervening paths. This proves the claimed equality of orbit relation with the compatible union of finite-distance relations.

The manuscript uses the specific compatible-metric theorem. It does not assume that an arbitrary increasing union of hyperfinite Borel relations is hyperfinite. Finite residue tests cannot establish the infinite assertion: for every tested maximum factorial N!, the different integer−1+N! has the same forbidden residues through that stage but is admitted at the next stage. This limitation is explicitly recorded in the fresh controls.

### 5. The entire exceptional preimage

The exceptional set is not assumed countable. The Borel integer height h is obtained by the unique integer preimage of p(x), and q(x)=t^-h(x)x is Borel by a countable partition into height fibres. The normalization calculation gives x E_W y exactly when q(x) E_N q(y). Conversely an N-move on normalized points conjugates back to a W-move.

N has local polynomial growth: each finitely generated subgroup lies in a finite-rank free abelian subgroup. CJMST Corollary 7.5 therefore supplies increasing finite Borel relations F_j on the zero-height base, without requiring freeness or a uniform polynomial degree for all of N.

The displayed H_j are equivalence relations: within the height band they are pullbacks of F_j, and outside it every point is a singleton. They are Borel and increasing. Each pair eventually lies within a common band and the needed base F_j. The height/base coordinates identify the exceptional space with B times Z, so a band class has at most (2j+1)|[z]_F_j| points. The size may depend on z; finiteness, not uniform finiteness, is required here.

Removing the band would invalidate finiteness even when every F_j-class is a singleton. A genuine infinite model is X=C times Z, with lamps trivial, t shifting the second coordinate, and p(c,k)=k. The exceptional preimage is then all of an uncountable space, and each q-fibre is infinite. The banded construction works exactly as stated. A non-surjective height also causes no issue: X=Z with translation and its embedded integer height is a valid auxiliary example. The empty-space cases are harmless. Taking the union of the two finite exhaustions stage by stage is legitimate because the pieces are disjoint, Borel and invariant.
