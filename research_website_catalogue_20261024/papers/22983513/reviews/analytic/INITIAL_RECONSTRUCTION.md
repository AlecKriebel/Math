## Exact original claim
For every integer n>=1, every nonempty bounded open Omega in R^n, the form Dirichlet Laplacian on H_0^1(Omega), its eigenvalues 0<E1<=E2<=... counted with multiplicity, and every finite integer J>=1, let A=J^-1 sum E_j, B=J^-1 sum E_j^2, M1=(n+2)A/n, M2^2=(n+4)B/n. Then D=M1^2-M2^2>(E_{J+1}-E_J)^2/4. Supporting Proposition3 asserts sum_{E_j<z}(z-E_j)^2 < (4/n)sum_{E_j<z}(z-E_j)E_j for every real z>E1. Lemma2 asserts each coordinate times every real normalized eigenfunction has infinite spectral support. Neither connectedness nor boundary smoothness is assumed. The question addressed is corrected squared-M2 saturation, not quantitative growth, a scale-free deficit, firstness, or current worldwide openness.

## Mechanism A: form domain and derivative-domain contradiction
1. Put Omega inside a bounded box B. Zero-extension embeds H_0^1(Omega) into H_0^1(B), preserving gradient/L2 norms. Poincare on B gives positive lower bound; compact embedding of H_0^1(B) into L2(B), restricted to supported functions, gives compact resolvent. Nonempty open implies infinitely many independent compactly supported functions; spectrum infinite, finite multiplicities. These statements do not require finite number of components.
2. Bounded coordinate multiplication is bounded on H1 on Omega and preserves H_0^1 via multiplying compactly supported approximants. For eigenfunction u, distribution product rule gives H(x_alpha u)=E x_alpha u-2 partial_alpha u in L2. The form-domain operator characterization therefore puts w=x_alpha u in Dom H; no global H2 assertion is needed.
3. For compactly supported real smooth u_m tending to u in H1, 0=int partial_alpha(x_alpha u_m^2)=||u_m||2^2+2 int x_alpha u_m partial_alpha u_m. Bounded x_alpha permits passage to limit. Thus <w,(H-E)w>=1. This also rules out partial_alpha u=0, for each coordinate independently.
4. In fact w cannot belong to Dom H^2. If it did, v=(H-E)w=-2 partial_alpha u would be in Dom H. Differentiating the interior Helmholtz equation gives (-Delta-E)v=0 in distributions; the operator characterization then gives (H-E)v=0. Self-adjointness yields ||v||2^2=<(H-E)w,v>=<w,(H-E)v>=0, contradicting <w,v>=1. This is stronger regularity information than the finite-support consequence but uses exactly the same underlying obstruction, not a new claimed theorem for publication.

## Mechanism B: finite spectral algebra in distributions
As a cross-check avoiding the transfer of w into Dom H^2, suppose w=sum_{k in K} a_k u_k with finite K. Locally (-Delta-E)^2(x_alpha u)=0 because the commutator gives a derivative solving Helmholtz. Hence sum_{k in K}(E_k-E)^2 a_k u_k=0 in distributions. This finite L2 combination is zero; orthogonality gives (E_k-E)^2 a_k=0 separately (including degenerate eigenspaces). Therefore w lies entirely in eigenspace E and (H-E)w=0, contradicting <w,(H-E)w>=1. This is a materially different finite-expansion verification of the obstruction, though not a wholly separate underlying scientific approach family.

## Mechanism C: remainder derived without imported inequality
Let a_jk^alpha=<u_k,x_alpha u_j>, a real symmetric matrix. Spectral expansion of w in Dom H gives sum_k(E_k-E_j)|a|^2=1 and sum_k(E_k-E_j)^2|a|^2=4||partial_alpha u_j||2^2. The latter converges nonnegatively; the former absolutely by Cauchy-Schwarz with sum|a|^2 finite. Sum coordinates to get n and 4E_j.
For t_j=z-E_j, t_j^2(E_k-E_j)-t_j(E_k-E_j)^2=t_j(E_k-E_j)(z-E_k). For j,k below z, paired terms cancel exactly since t_j t_k(E_k-E_j)+t_k t_j(E_j-E_k)=0. Terms at z are zero. Negating yields F(z)=4 sum_L t_j E_j-n sum_L t_j^2=sum_alpha sum_{j in L,k:E_k>z} t_j(E_k-E_j)(E_k-z)|a_jk|^2. Only finitely many j occur; each k-series is absolutely convergent from the previous two sums, so no conditional-rearrangement problem. For z>E1, every term with j=1 and E_k>z has strictly positive weight. F=0 forces every such coefficient zero, giving finite spectral support of x_alpha u1, contradiction. Hence F>0 at every real z>E1, including eigenvalue thresholds with arbitrary multiplicity.

## Mechanism D: quadratic endpoint/midpoint calculation
Q(t)=t^2-(2+4/n)At+(1+4/n)B=(t-M1)^2-D. Q(EJ)<=0, with equality possible only when EJ=E1; if EJ>E1 strict Proposition3 applies because all eigenvalues below EJ are among the first J and any extra threshold terms vanish. Likewise Q(E_{J+1})<0 whenever E_{J+1}>E1. Write m=(EJ+E_{J+1})/2 and g=E_{J+1}-EJ>=0. Then (Q(EJ)+Q(E_{J+1}))/2=(m-M1)^2+g^2/4-D<0. This independently checks the last step and proves D>g^2/4+(m-M1)^2>=g^2/4. If E_{J+1}=E1, all first J+1 eigenvalues equal E1 and direct substitution gives D=4 E1^2/n^2>0 with g=0. No squaring of an uncontrolled signed inequality is required.

## Mechanism E: interval model, analytically derived before program checks
On (0,L), c=pi^2/L^2, E_j=c j^2. Direct sine integration gives <u_k,x u_j>=L/2 if k=j, zero for unequal same-parity k,j, and -8Ljk/[pi^2(j^2-k^2)^2] for opposite parity. ## Boundary and counterexample attacks
- z=E1 or z<E1: all positive parts vanish, so strict assertion would be false. Original z>E1 is exact. For z=E1+delta below next distinct eigenvalue, F=r(4E1 delta-n delta^2)>0 near delta=0; tends to0, so no uniform threshold deficit follows.
- Infinite disconnected open sets within a box retain compactness by zero extension. Ground eigenspace may be multiple; one real normalized ground eigenvector is enough. Neither positivity on every component nor simplicity is used.
- J cutting a multiple eigenvalue is harmless because all omitted same-threshold terms have zero weight. Case E_{J+1}=E1 handled separately. n=1 included; no n=0 or J=0.
- Rough, fractal, slit or cuspidal boundaries: distribution domain description and H_0^1 approximation suffice. No trace smoothness, normal derivative, unique continuation across a boundary or H2 extension is smuggled in.
- Translations change only within-eigenspace/diagonal coordinate coefficients and not the commutator; dilation by s sends E to s^-2 E and the claimed slack to s^-4. No positive absolute deficit uniform over domain scale is possible.
- Neumann ground eigenvalue0 makes the proposed strict gap statement fail in degenerate cases; coordinate multiplication generally fails to preserve its operator domain. Periodic coordinates are not global bounded coordinate multipliers. Unbounded domains and added nonconstant potentials are outside the original hypotheses; differentiating the equation would introduce potential gradients. These are scope boundaries, not counterexamples to the paper.
- Weyl leading terms cancel in D: A~[n/(n+2)]C J^(2/n), B~[n/(n+4)]C^2 J^(4/n). Finite strictness supplies neither a uniform normalized deficit nor an improved leading Weyl constant.
