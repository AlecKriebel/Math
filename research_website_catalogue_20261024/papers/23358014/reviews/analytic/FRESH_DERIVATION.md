# Fresh analytic reconstruction before submitted-helper reading

Exact target: real defocusing quintic wave on the smooth unit ball in dimension three, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary, zero Cauchy data, horizon one. The control cap is the **spatial L2 norm at almost every time**, not a pointwise space-time amplitude bound. Only beta2 is removed: the sparse L1(time;L2(space)) penalty and fourth-power L4(time;L12(space)) state penalty remain. The optional velocity weight is each fixed finite nonnegative nu.

The following reasoning was developed from the current manuscript and actual primary bodies without reading any submitted mathematical checker, dedicated historical audit, or sibling review.

1. Write X for compatible zero-data positions with norm C H01 + C H velocity + L4 L12. This is a Banach space; derivative compatibility and zero initial traces are closed conditions. The bounded linear wave operator L:L1 H -> X exists by KM Lemma 2.9. BSS Theorem 1.1 permits n=3,p=4,q=12,gamma=1: 1/4+3/12=1/2 and 3/4+2/12=11/12<=1. Its homogeneous estimate plus Minkowski on Duhamel gives the needed L1 H forcing estimate on a fixed finite horizon. No uniform infinite-time estimate is required.

2. Four factors in L4 L12 and one in Linfinity L6 give a bounded five-linear multiplication X^5 -> L1 H, since 4/12+1/6=1/2. Its diagonal is the quintic. For small A=||u||1, L(u-y^5) maps the radius 2KA ball to itself and is a contraction. Thus ||y||X<=EA and ||y-Lu||X<=RA^5. The argument runs on [0,1], so does not need a global large-data theorem or numerical estimates of K,E,R. Local uniqueness can be extended across a finite partition with small L4 L12 norm; it is the KM energy/Strichartz mild state. The residual derivative at zero is the identity on this compatible space, avoiding an inappropriate inverse on arbitrary independent position/velocity pairs. The state map is smooth near zero with S'(0)=L and S''(0)=0. The fourth-power state norm has zero first/second variations there.

3. The radial e is regular at r=0, vanishes at r=1, has eigenvalue pi^2 and norm one by the spherical Jacobian. Linear endpoint projection is pi^-1 integral sin(pi t)<e,u(t)> dt. This is not a nonlinear invariant-mode reduction; e^5 is generally outside span(e). All nonlinear deviations are estimated in the full PDE space.

4. For f(t)=||u(t)||H, the cap yields A<=rho, A<=r and r^2<=rho A. With target pi e the gap is bounded below by integral(1-sin(pi t))f minus pi R A^5, after dropping only nonnegative position-square, quartic and velocity terms. Let s=t-1/2. Concavity of sin on [0,pi/2] gives 1-cos(pi s)=2 sin^2(pi s/2)>=2s^2. Among 0<=f<=rho of mass A, the centered saturated interval has minimum second moment A^3/(12rho^2): subtract the threshold b^2 and pair its sign with f-fstar. Therefore Delta>=A^3/(6rho^2)-C0 A^5. C0 rho_star^4<=1/12 proves the stated A^3/(12rho^2) bound for every feasible u. Zero is the unique global minimizer as an equivalence class; A=0 and A=rho cause no exception. All choices are independent of nu.

5. Bounded truncations prove the tangent cone at zero is all L1 H or L2 H, without claiming zero is an interior point in these topologies. First variation is integral(||v||H-sin(pi t)<e,v>). It is strictly positive for any nonzero equivalence class because the nonnegative lower weight vanishes at only a null singleton. Thus C(0)={0}. KM explicitly defines its substitute j''(0;v^2)=0; no classical second derivative of a norm is asserted. Strict positivity on the empty set of nonzero critical directions is genuinely vacuous.

6. For amplitude h and half-width h, A=2h^2,r^2=2h^3 and the exact linear terminal position is 2h sin(pi h)/pi^2 times e. Symmetry makes the linear terminal velocity exactly zero. Taylor expansion yields gap=(pi^2/3+2/pi^2)h^4+O(h^6). Full nonlinear endpoint errors are O(h^10), quartic cost O(h^8), and velocity cost O(nu h^20), so cannot change the leading term for fixed nu. Both r and ||u||Linfinity(time;H)=h go to zero and gap/r^2->0. The result refutes both the source's Linfinity localization and OWR's displayed L2 localization. The normalized pulse v_h has norm one and is weakly null by absolute continuity of the squared integral of any L2 test on the shrinking support. Its displayed normalized second variation is exactly the tracking-plus-sparse linear gap divided by r^2, hence tends to zero. This agrees with the NDC expression in BW Theorem 2.6; no application of a theorem requiring NDC is made.

7. Existence for every terminal d and fixed finite nu: the capped feasible set is weakly compact in L2 H. All associated states have uniform X bounds. Rellich plus uniform H-valued time Lipschitz continuity yields position compactness in C H. The equation gives y_tt bounded in Linfinity H^-1, since the cap controls u and y^5 lies in Linfinity L6/5. Compact H -> H^-1 and equicontinuity give velocity compactness in C H^-1. Almost-everywhere convergence and boundedness in reflexive L6/5(time-space) identify the weak quintic limit. The weak L4 L12 and Linfinity H01 bounds survive, so the limit quintic is also L1 H. The distributional equation and two initial traces pass. Subtract its linear Duhamel solution; every Dirichlet spectral coefficient of the remainder solves a homogeneous scalar ODE with zero Cauchy data, hence vanishes. The limit is the unique actual mild state. Endpoint squares are continuous and both retained norm penalties weakly lower semicontinuous. This is a direct method for the actual nonlinear PDE, including optional velocity cost.

8. For d_eps=(pi+eps)e, fixed amplitude a=rho/2 and half-width pi eps/(2a) give mass pi eps. Linear terminal position is eps e+O(eps^3), with zero terminal velocity. The perturbed gap is -eps^2/2+O(eps^3), with quartic/nonlinear/velocity corrections of orders eps^4,eps^5,nu eps^10 respectively. Thus a competitor beats zero by eps^2/4 for sufficiently small eps at each fixed nu. For arbitrary feasible u, the negative part of its first-order coefficient is g_eps=[(1+eps/pi)sin(pi t)-1]+. It is bounded by [eps/pi-2s^2]+, supported on length sqrt(2eps/pi), with height at most eps/pi. Consequently ||g||2<=2^(1/4)pi^(-5/4)eps^(5/4). Gap>=-||g||2 r-C1 A^5. If any optimizer has r<=Keps, this is -o(eps^2), contradicting the common competitor for each fixed K. The contradiction is uniform over the set of all global optimizers, not merely one selected optimizer. Therefore every selection has r/eps->infinity.

9. With x=<e,y(1)> and b=A/pi+RA^5, Delta>=A-pi x+x^2/2. Choosing delta with b<=pi/2 makes this quadratic decreasing on all x<=b; even negative x is covered. Evaluation at b gives A^2/(2pi^2)-pi RA^5+RA^6/pi+R^2 A^10/2. The smallness condition pi R delta^3<=1/(4pi^2) proves Delta>=A^2/(4pi^2). Comparing any target-pi e-w optimizer to zero yields Delta<=E||w|| A. The global cubic bound first puts A in the delta-neighborhood; then A<=4pi^2E eps, r<=2pi sqrt(rho E eps). These upper estimates and their neighborhood are uniform over finite nu because only nonnegative velocity costs were discarded. On the radial ray, insert A=O(eps) into the preceding perturbation inequality and use the negative competitor to obtain r>=c eps^(3/4). The Linfinity bound on g similarly gives A>=c'eps. Hence A=Theta(eps) and the claimed exponent bracket for every optimizer. These constants/neighborhoods may depend on rho, and the radial trial threshold on nu. No pairwise Lipschitz property, perturbed uniqueness, Linfinity convergence, or sharp rate is implied.

10. A useful counter-control is d=0, for which J>=A>=r^2/rho. Thus beta2=0 does not force failure of quadratic growth for every target. The zero cone is explicitly part of this construction. Larger cones, adapted sufficient conditions and weaker-norm theorems are not refuted.
