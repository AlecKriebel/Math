## Proof reconstruction and checks

**Model and linear estimate (pages 1–3).** The domain is the smooth unit ball in R3, the horizon is one, data are zero, boundary conditions are Dirichlet, the equation is real and defocusing, and forcing is distributed. The cap concerns ||u(t)||L2(B), not a scalar space-time bound. The retained objective terms are terminal tracking, gamma=1 fourth-power L4 L12 state cost, beta1=1 time-group sparsity, and optional nu>=0 terminal velocity H^-1 cost. Only beta2 is zero.

The correct compatible state space is Banach. KM's linear energy/Strichartz estimate gives L:L1 H -> C H01 with C H velocity and L4 L12 position. Its BSS input admits n=3,p=4,q=12,gamma=1 on the smooth compact ball; all exponents and boundary hypotheses match. Four L4 L12 factors and one Linfinity L6 factor multiply into L1 L2. Thus the quintic is a bounded polynomial on that space and a small-input contraction works on the full fixed interval [0,1]. This proves ||y||<=EA and ||y-Lu||<=RA5 with constants independent of target and nu. The compatible-space residual has identity derivative at zero, so its local implicit-function argument gives S'(0)=L,S''(0)=0. It does not require inversion onto arbitrary independent continuous position/velocity pairs. That avoids relying on the overbroad codomain step in KM's displayed global differentiability proof; the current paper's local argument is sufficient on its own.

**Global minimum (pages 2–3).** The radial eigenfunction has norm one with the actual spherical Jacobian, is smooth at the origin, and satisfies the Dirichlet eigenvalue equation. Only the linear endpoint projection is used; the nonlinear state is not assumed to remain a single mode. With f=||u(t)||, A=integral f and r=||u||2, one has A<=rho, A<=r, r²<=rho A. Expanding the tracking square and discarding only nonnegative costs gives Delta>=integral(1-sin(pi t))f-C0 A5. The weight dominates 2(t-1/2)². The centered saturated interval minimizes its second moment at fixed mass under 0<=f<=rho, yielding A³/(12rho²). This is a full same-mass sign argument, including zero and full mass, not a numerical minimization. Consequently Delta>=A³/(6rho²)-C0 A5; C0 rho_star⁴<=1/12 gives the claimed cubic bound. Every nonzero equivalence class has A>0, so zero is uniquely globally optimal. No numeric value for a PDE constant is asserted.

**Cone and failure of growth (pages 3–4).** Bounded truncations show that the closed tangent cone at zero is the whole L1 H or L2 H, without incorrectly claiming norm interiority. The directional derivative is integral(||v||-sin(pi t)<e,v>); its nonnegative lower bound is strictly positive on every nonzero equivalence class because the weight is zero only at a null singleton. The cone is exactly {0}. KM's substitute j'' at zero is expressly zero and is not a classical second derivative of a norm. Strict positivity on nonzero critical directions therefore holds vacuously.

The h-amplitude, h-half-width pulse has A=2h²,r²=2h³, terminal linear position 2h sin(pi h)e/pi², and zero terminal linear velocity. Its linear tracking-plus-sparse gap is (pi²/3+2/pi²)h4+O(h6). The true nonlinear endpoint correction is O(h10), the retained quartic cost O(h8), and the velocity cost O(nu h20). Hence the same leading coefficient holds for the actual PDE for each fixed finite nu. Both the L2 and time-Linfinity norms tend to zero and the gap/r² tends to zero. The normalized pulses are weakly null by absolute continuity of any L2 test's integral on shrinking intervals. Their displayed normalized F+G second-order expression equals the linear gap/r² and tends to zero, consistently violating NDC. The paper does not invoke a sufficient-condition theorem whose NDC hypothesis fails.

**Existence of actual nonlinear optimizers (pages 4–5).** The feasible set is weakly compact in L2 H. Uniform small-input bounds, compact H01 -> H, and the bounded time derivative give position compactness in C H. The equation and the pointwise-in-time cap give y_tt bounded in Linfinity H^-1, because y5 is bounded in Linfinity L6/5. Compact H -> H^-1 then gives velocity compactness in C H^-1. Almost-everywhere convergence with the reflexive L6/5 bound identifies the weak quintic limit. Weak energy and L4 L12 bounds survive, so the limit quintic also lies in L1 H. The distributional PDE and both initial traces pass to the limit. Subtracting the Duhamel solution and using the complete Dirichlet eigenbasis proves that the remainder is zero. This identifies the actual unique mild solution, rather than an unspecified weak solution. Endpoint costs are continuous and the two retained norm penalties are weakly lower semicontinuous. The direct method therefore proves global existence for every d in H and each finite nu in the capped small-input setting.

**All-optimizer perturbation bounds (page 5).** For target (pi+epsilon)e, the fixed-amplitude trial of half-width pi epsilon/(2a), a=rho/2, has mass pi epsilon, position epsilon e+O(epsilon³) and zero linear velocity. Its actual perturbed gap is -epsilon²/2+O(epsilon³), so eventually at most -epsilon²/4. For every feasible u, the adverse first-order coefficient is bounded by g_epsilon=[(1+epsilon/pi)sin(pi t)-1]+. The parabolic envelope gives ||g||2<=2^(1/4)pi^(-5/4)epsilon^(5/4). Thus gap>=-||g||2 r-C1 A5. An optimizer with r<=Kepsilon would have gap>=-o(epsilon²), contradicting the common trial for each fixed K. This proves the universal selection statement, not merely existence of one unstable optimizer.

For the weaker growth estimate, x=<e,y(1)> is bounded above by b=A/pi+RA5. The scalar quadratic A-pi x+x²/2 is decreasing for x<pi; choosing b<=pi/2 permits substitution even if x is negative. The exact expansion gives A²/(4pi²) after the stated smallness absorption. Comparing an arbitrary perturbed-target global optimizer against zero first uses the global cubic bound to enter this neighborhood and then yields A<=4pi²Eepsilon and r<=2pi sqrt(rho Eepsilon). All these upper estimates are uniform over finite nu. On the radial ray the negative trial and A=O(epsilon) give r>=c epsilon^(3/4) and A>=c'epsilon. The respective upper bounds complete Corollary 3. This concerns a fixed basepoint; it proves no pairwise Lipschitz selection, perturbed uniqueness, Linfinity convergence or sharp exponent.

**Boundary and counter-controls.** rho=0 is excluded; A=0/full-cap mass and nu=0 are covered. The h pulses remain feasible for any fixed positive permitted rho. Nu may be arbitrarily large but is fixed before the radial limit; no uniform-in-nu radial neighborhood is inferred. General real signed/vector controls are handled by Cauchy–Schwarz and f=||u||, so the global argument is not limited to radial controls. A singleton threshold does not define a nonzero Lp direction. Other dimensions, nonzero initial states, Neumann boundary, removal of the retained penalties and space-time amplitude constraints are outside the theorem. With terminal target zero the same model has J>=||u||1>=||u||2²/rho, correctly demonstrating that unregularized strong growth does not fail universally.
