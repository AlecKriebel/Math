        Failure of strong quadratic growth in unregularized
                sparse control of a critical wave equation
                                                 Alec Kriebel
                                        ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                   October 14, 2026            (version 1.0)


                                                   Abstract
         We give an explicit counterexample to retaining ordinary critical-cone strict positivity and
     strong control-L2 quadratic growth after removing quadratic control regularization from the
     energy-critical quintic-wave problem of Kunisch and Meinlschmidt. On the three-dimensional
     unit ball, zero is the unique global optimizer for a suﬃciently small positive pointwise control cap.
     Its ordinary critical cone is {0}, so the strict condition is vacuous, but feasible controls converging
     in L∞ have objective gap o(kuk2L2 ). Nearby radial terminal targets admit global optimizers, and
     every such optimizer has L2 displacement divided by target displacement tending to inﬁnity.
     Local L1 quadratic growth survives. The construction realizes the established concentration and
     noncoercivity mechanism in the actual nonlinear source model, using classical wave estimates.


1    Question, conventions and precise result
Kunisch–Meinlschmidt’s wave-control analysis [1, Theorem 4.17] proves strong control-L2 quadratic
growth from strict positivity on the ordinary critical cone when the quadratic control coeﬃcient
β2 is positive. The Oberwolfach contribution [2], printed pp. 438–440, places the β2 = 0 question
immediately after this implication. We answer the question of retaining that implication unchanged.
Adapted suﬃcient conditions, enlarged cones and weaker growth metrics remain distinct questions.
The counterexample uses the zero critical cone explicitly.

4    Existence and terminal-target perturbations
We record existence for the optional velocity term as well. The set Uρ is closed, convex and
bounded in L2 (H), hence weakly compact. Along a minimizing sequence, Lemma 2 bounds the
states uniformly in energy and L4 L12 . Compactness of H01 ,→ H and the bound on yt imply strong
compactness of positions in C([0, 1]; H). Moreover ytt = ∆y − y 5 + u is bounded in L∞ (H −1 ):
y ∈ L∞ L6 , so y 5 ∈ L∞ L6/5 ,→ L∞ H −1 . Compactness of H ,→ H −1 then gives strong compactness
of velocities in C([0, 1]; H −1 ).
    A subsequence of states converges almost everywhere. The bounded L6/5 quintics converge
weakly to the quintic of the limit; the limit also retains the weak energy and L4 L12 bounds. Con-
sequently its quintic lies in L1 (H) by (7). Passing to the distributional equation and initial traces
identiﬁes a zero-data weak solution. Subtract the linear Duhamel solution forced by u − y 5 . The
remaining homogeneous Dirichlet wave has zero initial data; projection onto a complete Dirichlet
eigenbasis proves it is zero. Thus the limit is the actual mild state, by uniqueness. Endpoint costs
are continuous under the two strong convergences, and the sparse and quartic norms are weakly

    These are estimates relative to the ﬁxed zero optimizer. They assert no pairwise Lipschitz
selection, perturbed optimizer uniqueness, L∞ convergence, or sharpness of the exponent bracket.
Weaker-norm and strengthened-cone theories are developed in [9, 6, 7, 8]; the elementary estimate
here uses the terminal tracking square and the cap. For example, taking target zero in the same
model gives J0,ν (u) ≥ kuk1 ≥ kuk22 /ρ. Thus the theorem is a speciﬁc obstruction to the unchanged
implication, not universal failure of all unregularized quadratic growth.
