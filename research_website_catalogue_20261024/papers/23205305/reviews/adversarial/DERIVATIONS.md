## A. The actual decision problem and geometric transfer

An input is a finite homogeneous integer presentation of a projective scheme over Q, with N and all equations encoded. The promise is smooth and geometrically integral; the oracle need only terminate on promised inputs. Dimension, degree, equation count, ambient dimension and coefficient heights vary. Geometric integrality includes nonemptiness. An affine polynomial input may have no variables or be constant; those cases can be decided before any geometric oracle call.

For a nonconstant polynomial f, take its affine zero scheme U and its projective closure X, retaining U as an open subset. The actual published Poonen Theorem 1.3 supplies an algorithm producing a regular projective Y with a morphism Y→X and image Y(Q)=U(Q). It does not promise that Y is connected or geometrically integral. The effectivity proof in Section 9 does not compute the unknown finite exceptional set: a computable cardinality bound supplies a finite list of parameters, every construction has image contained in U(Q), and at least one has image equal to U(Q). Their disjoint union works. Confusing this with selecting one effectively recognizable good parameter would lose the algorithm; the target makes the necessary finite union explicit.

Compute Y's connected components and test geometric integrality. Every retained component is regular, projective and geometrically integral, so every oracle query is valid. Every rejected connected regular component has no Q-point. One useful proof in the present proper characteristic-zero setting is that H0(Y,O) is a finite separable field L, evaluation at a rational point embeds L into Q, and hence L=Q. Proper base change gives geometric connectedness; geometric regularity makes distinct geometric irreducible components disjoint, so connectedness forces a single component. This also agrees with the broader published Poonen Lemma 10.1. Empty components contribute nothing. A finite OR of the retained oracle answers decides f=0 over Q.

This is a finite, nonadaptive, disjunctive oracle reduction. No single-query many-one reduction has been established. Conversely the N+1 standard affine charts turn a homogeneous presentation into finitely many affine systems, and over Q a finite conjunction is equivalent to its sum of squares. Thus a rational-solvability oracle decides the presented geometric promise. No invalid-presentation recognition is needed to derive the contradiction.

Two potential shortcuts fail and were explicitly tested. The affine curve x²+1=0 has no rational point, while its plane projective closure X²+Z²=0 contains [0:1:0]. The rational singular origin on x²+y²=0 disappears on normalization, whose normalization ring is Q(i)[x]. Therefore naive closure or resolution cannot replace Poonen's image-preserving construction. These failures invalidate the shortcuts, not the construction actually used in the paper.
