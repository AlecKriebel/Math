**Geometric lemma.** If a connected smooth projective Q-scheme C has a Q-point, then C is geometrically integral. Smoothness makes C regular, so connectedness makes it integral. Properness makes L=H0(C,O) a finite extension of Q. Evaluation at the rational point is a Q-algebra map L→Q, so L=Q. After algebraic closure, H0 records the disjoint geometric smooth components, so there is exactly one. Smoothness also gives geometric reducedness. Thus C is geometrically integral.

**Reduction.** Assume an algorithm D terminates correctly on every smooth projective geometrically integral homogeneous integer presentation. Given an integer polynomial f, decide constant cases directly. Otherwise let U be its affine zero variety and X its projective closure, retaining the open inclusion U⊂X. The actual published Poonen Theorem1.3 effectively constructs regular projective Y→X with image Y(Q)=U(Q). Its effectivity construction may take a finite disjoint union; it does not effectively recognize one good parameter. Compute all connected components of Y, discard the empty or non-geometrically-integral ones, and call D on every remaining component. Every discarded connected regular component has no rational point by the lemma, and every retained component satisfies the oracle promise because Q has characteristic zero. A finite OR of the answers decides f=0 over Q. This contradicts the arithmetic input. The conclusion is a finite nonadaptive disjunctive reduction, with no fixed-dimension or many-one strengthening.

**Height corollary.** Assume a total computable B(s) bounds the naive height of at least one rational point whenever a valid presentation of bit length s has a point. On a valid presentation in P^N, compute M=B(s), enumerate all nonzero integer vectors in [-M,M]^(N+1), and test all homogeneous equations exactly. A successful vector is a rational point. Conversely the assumed bound places a primitive representative of some point in the box whenever a point exists. Therefore exhaustive finite search decides existence on the valid promise, contradicting the preceding theorem. The same proof treats a computable presentation-dependent bound terminating on every valid input. It does not prohibit the usual partial search which terminates only on point-bearing inputs.
