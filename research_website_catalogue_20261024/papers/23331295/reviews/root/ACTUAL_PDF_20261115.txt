                                            Abstract
         Let V be the ordinary, uncompleted complex algebra of all ﬁnite-order ﬁnite-type
     invariants of links. We give a principal ideal with a nonempty actual-link zero set whose
     vanishing ideal strictly contains its radical. The generator is g(4g − 1), where g is the
     sum of squared pairwise linking numbers. The obstruction is a character on the entire
     algebra, obtained by evaluating the standard twist-restriction polynomial at a noninte-
     gral parameter. This gives a negative answer to the arbitrary-ideal formulation of Bar-
     Natan’s Problem 2.27, as recorded by Ohtsuki; the separate linking-number-generated
     ideal question is not decided.


1    The question and the algebra
Bar-Natan asks whether an ideal in the algebra of ﬁnite-type link invariants has the same
radical as the ideal of invariants vanishing on its common zeros among actual links [1,
Problem 2.27, p. 422]. The present note records an elementary counterexample to that
literal arbitrary-ideal assertion.
    Fix m ≥ 2, and let Xm consist of oriented, unordered m-component links in S 3 , including
split links. Let Vm be the unital complex algebra of their ordinary ﬁnite-type invariants of
some ﬁnite order, with pointwise operations. An invariant has order at most d when its
positive-minus-negative crossing extension vanishes on every link with d + 1 double points.
Each element has a ﬁnite order; there is no common cutoﬀ on all elements and no inﬁnite-
order completion. Products of invariants of orders d, e have order at most d + e: on a
crossing-resolution cube their multilinear representatives have degrees at most d, e, and the
product has degree at most d + e.
    For an ideal A ⊂ Vm , set

      Zact (A) = {L ∈ Xm : a(L) = 0 for all a ∈ A},          Jact (S) = {v ∈ Vm : v|S = 0}.

Always rad(A) ⊆ Jact (Zact (A)), because a power vanishing at a complex-valued evaluation
forces its base to vanish there. The same deﬁnitions apply to the algebra V on all ﬁnite-
component links, with each invariant having a ﬁnite order on that whole domain.

2    Polynomial restriction on the whole algebra
The polynomial behavior of ﬁnite-type invariants on twist or braid sequences is established
prior work; see, for example, Stanford–Trapp [2, Theorem E], Eisermann [3], and Stoimenow
[4, Theorem 2.1, pp. 690–691]. Stoimenow explicitly credits his earlier braiding-sequence
work. We give the particular diﬀerence argument needed here, including split components,
so that no extension of a character from a smaller algebra is assumed.
    Let σ be the positive generator of the two-strand braid group. For integers n ≥ 0, let
Ln be the coherently oriented closure of σ 2n , together with m − 2 split unknots. Its two
braid components have linking number n, all other pairwise linking numbers vanish, and
consequently g(Ln ) = n2 .

most d, satisfying Pv (n) = v(Ln ) for all n ≥ 0. The map v 7→ Pv is a unital complex-algebra
homomorphism.

3    The counterexample
Proof of Theorem 1. Pairwise linking numbers are integer-valued and have order at most
one. On any prescribed crossing-resolution cube, each is aﬃne in the binary resolution
variables: an intercomponent crossing change changes the relevant linking number by one
up to sign, and a self-crossing change does not change it. Its square has degree at most two
on that cube. Hence g has order at most two and f = 4g 2 − g has order at most four. The
sum deﬁning g is unchanged by relabeling components or reversing their orientations.
    For every actual link, g(L) is a nonnegative integer. Thus

              f (L) = 0   ⇐⇒     g(L) = 0     ⇐⇒      lk(Li , Lj ) = 0 for all i < j.

Since f generates A, this is precisely its actual-link zero set. It contains the unlink, and g
vanishes on it.

   The character is not evaluation at an actual link, since its g-value is 1/4. In particular,
formal evaluation at n = 1/2 is not evaluation on the one-component closure of σ. The
obstruction concerns the use of actual links as the evaluation set, and does not contradict
the classical Hilbert Nullstellensatz.


4    Scope, attribution, and veriﬁcation
The same construction and diﬀerence proof apply to ordered, labelled links as well. The
source’s separate ideal generated by the indexed pairwise linking numbers is understood
in that labelled theory. There it diﬀers from our principal ideal although their actual-link
zero sets coincide; its radical/vanishing-ideal question is not decided here. The symmetric
witness and the proof also apply after labels are forgotten, as in Theorem 1. No conclusion
about completed invariant algebras, homogeneous-ideal variants, or positive characteristic
is claimed. The proof also works over any characteristic-zero ﬁeld and, by pulling back
invariants to oriented links, after forgetting orientations.
    The contribution recorded here is the explicit principal ideal and its nonempty actual-link
zero-set obstruction, as an elementary application of older polynomiality. No new polyno-
miality method is claimed. A bounded primary-source priority audit located no explicit
equivalent earlier counterexample in the bodies examined; it does not establish historical
ﬁrstness. The accompanying audit states its source/version access limits and records other
older routes, including the connected-sum polynomiality of Jeong–Park [5].
    The accompanying exact-rational veriﬁcation checks braid resolutions, Newton identities,
linking-square symmetries, and the character calculation. These ﬁnite controls support the
written proof; they do not replace the argument for every ﬁnite-order invariant.
