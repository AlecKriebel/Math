## 1. Static reconstruction

Writing g=e^(i k r)/(4 pi r), radial differentiation gives tangential Hessian g'/r and radial Hessian g''. The tangential coefficient of -(D^2+k^2 I)g is e^(i k r)(1-i k r-k^2 r^2)/(4 pi r^3); its radial-minus-tangential coefficient is e^(i k r)(k^2 r^2+3 i k r-3)/(4 pi r^3). Thus K_0=(I-3ee^T)/(4 pi r^3) and R has coefficients -k^2(I+ee^T)/(8 pi r) and -i k^3 I/(6 pi). The distributional shift is +I delta/3 in -D^2 g_0, hence the target is A-I/3.

Each radial lattice shell is invariant under all signed coordinate permutations. Its off-diagonal sums vanish by reflections, diagonal sums agree by permutations, and trace zero forces every sum to vanish. No infinite rearrangement is required. For a direct near-field majorant group j by q=max|j_i|. There are (2q+1)^3-(2q-1)^3=24q^2+2 such points and |j|>=q. Therefore h^3 sum_(0<|hj|<=R) |hj|^-2 <= h sum_(q<=R/h) 26 <=26R. This explicit majorant justifies both manuscript uses of the lattice bound.

On smooth compactly supported test fields, subtracting the center value from the radially cut off near part yields O(delta); the far part is an ordinary uniformly translated Riemann sum. The whole-space output tails are uniformly O(|x|^-3) because the input sampled L1 sums are bounded, so squared tails are O(R^-3). CDN v3 Proposition 3.15, via Theorem 1.6, Proposition 2.2 and Section 2.4, supplies the uniform full-lattice bound. Density then gives strong D_h -> D. Condition (G) gives strong mask multiplication by absolute continuity of the integral; uniformly bounded strong products yield B_h -> B. The compression form bound uses 0 in J, which is valid because [-1/3,2/3] is included in J.

The Hilbert-space scaling is material: the sequence inner product is h^3 sum u_m conjugate(v_m), making J_h an isometry and J_h^* cell averaging. The finite matrix operator norm is unchanged by multiplication of every squared vector norm by the same scalar h^3.

## 3. Spectral reconstruction and sensitive countermodels

For selfadjoint B_n with spectra in fixed J, R_n(z)=(z-B_n)^-1 are uniformly bounded by distance(z,J)^-1. The resolvent identity, then finite nets in z, gives strong convergence locally uniformly off J. Critically, R_n(z)^*=R_n(conjugate(z)), so adjoints converge strongly as well. Strong convergence alone controls (R_n-R)K for compact K; the adjoint argument is needed for K(R_n-R).

F_n=R_n C_n converges in norm locally uniformly to compact analytic F=RC. Factorization is z-S_n=(z-B_n)(I-F_n). Fredholm reduction to a finite-dimensional Schur complement plus invertibility at infinity yields only isolated finite-rank poles on the connected complement of J. Compactness is used on F, not assumed for B_h or for B_h-B. On a contour Gamma off J, G_n=(I-F_n)^-1 F_n converges in norm and is compact. The analytic R_n contribution integrates to zero; G_n R_n converges in norm because G_n->G and the adjoints of R_n converge strongly, uniformly on the contour. Thus the Riesz projections converge in norm. Difference less than one gives mutual injectivity of the two projection restrictions even for nonorthogonal projections. Ranks, algebraic multiplicities and both gaps follow. Convergence for all h is the sequential contradiction consequence of the corresponding argument along every sequence h->0.

Sensitive exact countermodels are specified before code execution: (a) X_n=e_1 tensor e_n is strongly zero but K X_n=X_n for K=P_1, so compact multiplication from the left need not converge in norm without adjoint convergence; (b) nonselfadjoint B_n=X_n and C=3P_1 have a projection at 3 equal to P_1+X_n/3, which does not converge in norm; (c) B_n=0, C_n=3P_n strongly zero but not norm-zero creates off-background pollution at 3, showing norm convergence of compact corrections cannot be weakened to strong convergence; (d) a 2x2 Jordan block [[2i,1],[epsilon,2i]] has two simple eigenvalues for epsilon>0 and a defective limiting block, so individual Jordan block sizes need not be preserved. These are out-of-hypothesis controls, not counterexamples to the paper.

For a nontrivial exact in-hypothesis family use the block on e_1,e_n: B_n=[[0,t],[t,1]], C=3P_1, t=2q/(q^2-1), q>=3, and zero elsewhere. B_n converges strongly to zero, with spectra in [-1,2] but operator norm at least one. S_n has lambda_+=3+2/(q^2-1), lambda_-=1-2/(q^2-1), and its off-interval rank-one projection is (q^2+1)^-1 [[q^2,q],[q,1]]. The projection difference from P_1 squared is I/(q^2+1) on this block, proving norm convergence despite failed static norm convergence. Infinite zero padding is outside every chosen contour about lambda_+.

## 4. Negative theorem reconstruction

On intrinsic L2(B), closed gradients split orthogonally into Dirichlet gradients and harmonic gradients; their perpendicular consists precisely of fields whose zero extension has zero divergence. The longitudinal Fourier projection is identity on the first part and zero on the last. For r^ell Y_ell, the divergence of the zero-extended gradient is -ell Y_ell delta_boundary. The Newton single layer interior coefficient is 1/(2ell+1), obtained by solving continuity and unit derivative jump. Hence A_0 has harmonic eigenvalues ell/(2ell+1). Completeness in the harmonic Dirichlet norm follows by the spherical harmonic expansion and the energy identity integral_B |grad(r^ell Y)|^2=ell integral_sphere |Y|^2. After shifting, zero has exactly the three constant fields; all other harmonic values are at least 1/15, with the 1/6 accumulation retained.
