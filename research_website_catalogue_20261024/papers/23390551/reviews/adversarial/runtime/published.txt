                                                  Abstract
         Costabel’s 2022 Oberwolfach report asks for a precise description and proof of the observed
     approximation of nonreal eigenvalues by the discrete dipole approximation away from a real
     lattice interval. We give an independent proof for bounded particles whose center-selected cubic
     voxels converge in volume. This includes every bounded Lipschitz particle, with arbitrary grid
     translations. At each ﬁxed complex frequency, the actual zero-diagonal matrices have no spec-
     tral pollution outside the exact static lattice interval; isolated eigenvalues are approximated with
     algebraic multiplicity and norm-convergent embedded Riesz projections. The proof combines
     the established static lattice bound of Costabel–Dauge–Nedaiasl with strong static consistency,
     Hilbert–Schmidt convergence of the sampled dynamic correction and classical spectral approxi-
     mation. A separate compact, positive-volume, empty-interior support has a nonreal continuum
     eigenvalue at frequency 10−4 while every matrix on the grids n−1 Z3 is 3 × 3 zero. This example
     clariﬁes the geometric hypothesis. The classical ingredients and previously announced dynamic
     work are explicitly credited; ﬁrst priority is not certiﬁed. AI tools were used extensively, and no
     external human peer review is asserted.

  1. For every compact L ⊂ (C \ Jlat ) ∩ ρ(Sκ ), the matrices zI − Tκh are invertible for all z ∈ L

     ity. If a closed disk about λ misses Jlat and contains no other continuum spectral point, the
     embedded discrete Riesz projections for its boundary converge in operator norm to the contin-

3    The classical compact spectral argument
We give the argument to specify the topology of projection convergence. It is an application of
classical compact approximation and spectral theory, not a new abstract principle; compare [5, 6, 7].

                                                          5
Lemma 4. Let Bn , B be bounded selfadjoint operators on a complex Hilbert space, with spectra in
a ﬁxed bounded real interval J. Suppose Bn → B strongly and Cn → C in norm, where Cn , C are
compact. Put Sn = Bn + Cn , S = B + C. Then the three conclusions of Theorem 1, in the common
space, hold outside J.
Proof. For z ∈/ J, put Rn (z) = (z−Bn )−1 and R(z) = (z−B)−1 . The common bound is dist(z, J)−1 .
The resolvent identity proves strong convergence Rn (z) → R(z), uniformly on compact z-sets on
each ﬁxed vector, by ﬁnite nets and the resolvent bounds. Selfadjointness gives the corresponding
strong convergence of adjoints through Rn (z)∗ = Rn (z).
    If Xn → X strongly and is uniformly bounded, then ∥(Xn − X)K∥ → 0 for compact K, by
ﬁnite-rank approximation. If also Xn∗ → X ∗ strongly, taking adjoints gives ∥K(Xn − X)∥ → 0.
Finite nets give uniform versions for norm-continuous compact families of K. In particular,

                               Fn (z) = Rn (z)Cn −→ F (z) = R(z)C

in norm, uniformly on compact subsets of C \ J. The family F is compact and analytic, and

                                    z − S = (z − B)(I − F (z)).                                   (9)

For large |z| it is invertible. The analytic Fredholm argument on the connected set C \ J gives
isolated poles with ﬁnite-rank principal parts. One may obtain this directly by choosing a ﬁnite-rank
orthogonal projection so that the remaining block of F (z0 ) has norm less than 1/2. Local analytic
block elimination reduces invertibility to a ﬁnite-dimensional Schur determinant. Its singularities
are discrete unless the determinant vanishes identically; connectedness and invertibility at large
z exclude that alternative by continuation. The resulting resolvent poles have ﬁnite-rank Riesz
projections, whose ranges are exactly the generalized eigenspaces. This last assertion also follows
by comparing Laurent coeﬃcients in (z − S)(z − S)−1 = I.
    On a compact set L ⊂ (C \ J) ∩ ρ(S), norm convergence of Fn and a Neumann inverse estimate
give invertibility of I − Fn , with a uniform bound. Factorization (9) proves the claimed resolvent
bound and excludes pollution there.
    Let Γ bound an isolating disk whose closure misses J. On Γ deﬁne

                            Gn = (I − Fn )−1 Fn ,       G = (I − F )−1 F.

These are compact, and Gn → G uniformly in norm. The full resolvent is

                                     (z − Sn )−1 = Rn + Gn Rn .                                  (10)

The integrals of Rn and R over Γ vanish, because they are analytic throughout the disk. Moreover

                      ∥Gn Rn − GR∥ ≤ ∥Gn − G∥∥Rn ∥ + ∥G(Rn − R)∥ −→ 0

uniformly on Γ. The last term uses strong convergence of the adjoint resolvents; ordinary strong
convergence alone would not suﬃce. Integrating proves norm convergence Πn → Π of Riesz projec-
tions.
    Once ∥Πn − Π∥ < 1, the restrictions Πn : ran Π → ran Πn and Π : ran Πn → ran Π are injective.
Hence their ﬁnite ranks agree. This argument does not require orthogonal projections or semisimple
eigenvalues. Repeating it for smaller circles and using stability on the intervening compact annuli
proves convergence of the whole eigenvalue cluster with algebraic multiplicity. For unit vectors in
either range, projecting to the other range bounds each subspace gap by ∥Πn − Π∥. Finally, ∥Sn ∥
is uniformly bounded because the static spectra lie in J and Cn converges in norm.

4     A compact-support obstruction
The following supplementary result prevents extending the aﬃrmative statement to arbitrary com-
pact point-membership masks. Nonreal sphere poles, their radiation sign and static/dynamic per-
turbation methods are classical [11, 12, 10, 13, 9]. We include an elementary quantitative certiﬁcate
to keep the frequency and all error bounds checkable.
Theorem 5. There is a compact set Ω ⊂ R3 of positive volume and empty interior such that, at
the ﬁxed real frequency κ = 1/10000, Sκ on HΩ has an isolated nonreal eigenvalue, whereas every
matrix (3) on h = 1/n, ah = 0, is the 3 × 3 zero matrix. The sole sampled point is an isolated,
volume-zero anchor. The support is speciﬁed existentially within the explicit sequence below; no
eﬀective index or numerical eigenvalue location is claimed.
