# Independent analytic reconstruction

This record was written before executing any archived verification program. It records a fresh derivation from the selected manuscript, the actual primary inputs and standard Hilbert-space analysis; historical audit verdicts and sibling reviews are not inputs.

## Claim and spaces

The target is the actual center-sampled, zero-block-diagonal cubic matrix at one fixed complex frequency k. Omega is bounded Lebesgue measurable with a specified center-membership representative. The sole geometric limit is |Omega_h triangle Omega| -> 0. This is sufficient, not asserted necessary. All comparisons are on L2(R3;C3), with intrinsic continuum and occupied-cell operators extended by zero. Grid-vector squared norm is h^3 sum |u_m|^2, so the value-preserving embedding is an isometry and its adjoint is cell averaging. This scalar change of the Euclidean squared norm preserves every matrix operator norm. Full-lattice operator norms equal their nonzero embedded restrictions' norms; finite occupied subspaces have the same property. Intrinsic zero-dimensional spaces cause no problem.

The distribution -Hess(1/(4 pi r)) has away-zero part (I-3ee^T)/(4 pi r^3), plus I delta_0/3. The spherical principal value has zero trace and angular average; the delta coefficient follows from -Delta g0=delta_0. Fourier transformation gives xi xi^T/|xi|^2-I/3 for the static principal value, a bounded selfadjoint multiplier with eigenvalues -1/3,-1/3,2/3. It is a distributional L2 operator; no pointwise principal-value existence on every representative is required.

For g=e^(ikr)/(4 pi r), differentiation into tangential and radial pieces gives K=-(g'/r+k^2g)I-(g''-g'/r)ee^T. Thus the I coefficient is e^(ikr)(1-ikr-k^2r^2)/(4 pi r^3) and the ee^T coefficient is e^(ikr)(k^2r^2+3ikr-3)/(4 pi r^3). Removing K0 yields -k^2(I+ee^T)/(8 pi r)-ik^3 I/(6 pi)+O(r). There is no extra delta in Hess(g-g0): its local leading constant has zero derivative and its next radial r term has integrable second derivatives, with vanishing boundary flux in the distributional differentiation. These bounds are only on bounded difference sets, avoiding any false infinite-volume outgoing-wave bound when k is real or has negative imaginary part.

## Static lattice input and consistency

The CDN v3 hypothesis is componentwise homogeneous quadratic numerator times |x|^-5, with spherical cancellation in d=3. Here the numerator (|x|^2 I-3xx^T)/(4 pi) has those properties. Their Ewald construction gives a bounded matrix multiplier, real symmetric by evenness and real coefficients. The closed numerical range J of the infinite selfadjoint lattice operator is a bounded real interval containing [-1/3,2/3]. No numerical endpoint extremum is needed. For every finite selected set, extending a vector by zero transfers the infinite lattice form bound directly, including highly irregular or null-volume center masks. Selfadjointness and the spectral theorem give the common off-J bound 1/dist(z,J).

For a smooth compactly supported vector phi, let phi_h be its center values on cubes. These approximate phi in L2 uniformly over grid translations. At a center, the sampled lattice action is a finite sum because phi is compactly supported. A radial near cutoff produces a cubically invariant finite set. Off-diagonal tensor sums vanish by sign changes; diagonal sums are equal by coordinate permutations and their total is zero by trace-free numerators. Consequently the near contribution may subtract phi(x_m). Its norm is bounded by const Lip(phi) h^3 sum_{0<|hj|<2delta} |hj|^-2. The number of integer points in a unit radial shell is O(q^2), proved by disjoint unit cubes lying within a thickened spherical annulus. Hence the last expression is O(delta), uniformly in h including an empty sum when 2delta<h. The same integral bound holds in the continuum.

For fixed delta the complementary kernel times phi has a smooth compactly supported integration variable locally uniformly in the observation center. Riemann quadrature errors are uniform in the grid origin modulo hZ3. Continuity of Dphi changes center values to cell values. This proves local L2 consistency. Outside a sufficiently large fixed ball, |x_m-x_n| is comparable to |x| whenever phi(x_n) is nonzero, and h^3 sum |phi(x_n)| is uniformly bounded. Both outputs have O(|x|^-3) tails; their squared tail integrals are O(R^-3). Dense-core consistency therefore holds on the entire space. The lattice uniform operator bound extends it to every L2 field, first replacing phi_h by phi and then using density. The argument needs cubic symmetry and this specific tensor's cancellation: it is not an arbitrary-kernel or arbitrary-grid theorem.

Geometric consistency gives strong mask convergence by absolute continuity of the L2 integral: ||(M_h-M)u||^2=integral_{symmetric difference}|u|^2. Products of uniformly bounded strongly convergent operators give M_h D_h M_h -> M D M strongly. Both are selfadjoint and their spectra lie in J, since 0 is in J and continuum static compression retains the smaller form bounds.

If the bounded topological boundary has zero volume, disagreement between a cell point and its center forces the segment between them to meet the boundary, so the disagreement lies within sqrt(3)h of that boundary. Shrinking bounded neighborhoods have volume tending to zero. No boundary smoothness stronger than this is used; bounded Lipschitz particles qualify for every translation and either boundary-membership convention.

## Dynamic sampled correction

The operator with masked R kernel is Hilbert-Schmidt because ||R(x-y)||^2=O(|x-y|^-2), locally integrable in three dimensions. The sampled kernel is constant on ordered pairs of distinct occupied cubes and zero on each same-cube block. It integrates a cell average, exactly h^3 times the sampled matrix on constants, and vanishes on their orthogonal complement. It introduces neither an integrated-cell self term nor a different discretization.

All occupied centers belong to the fixed bounded Omega; occupied cubes therefore lie in a common bounded box even for arbitrary a_h. In |x-y|<delta the relevant distinct-center displacement has length <delta+sqrt(3)h. Its kernel L2 mass is at most const h^6 #I_h sum_{0<|hj|<delta+sqrt(3)h}|hj|^-2 = O((h^3#I_h)(delta+h)). The volume h^3#I_h is bounded. The continuum near-diagonal mass is O(delta). This also accounts for the omitted self blocks.

Outside a fixed diagonal strip, center displacements approach x-y uniformly. The smooth R kernel is uniformly bounded and continuous there; product-mask error has six-dimensional measure at most 2|box| |Omega_h triangle Omega|. Thus the far L2 error tends to zero. Sending h->0 followed by delta->0 proves HS, hence norm, convergence. A rate and a frequency varying with h do not follow.

## Uniform resolvent and Riesz projection argument

Let B_n,B be the static selfadjoint operators with common real interval J; C_n->C in norm, all compact. For z off J, R_n=(z-B_n)^-1 have common bound d(z,J)^-1. Resolvent identity gives R_n-R=R_n(B_n-B)R, hence strong convergence. A finite net in z and the resolvent Lipschitz bound upgrade this to uniform convergence on compact z sets for each vector. The adjoints converge strongly too, because R_n(z)^*=R_n(conjugate(z)); this conjugation is visible in the actual published PDF p6.

For compact K, uniform boundedness plus strong convergence gives ||(R_n-R)K||->0 by finite-rank approximation. Taking adjoints gives ||K(R_n-R)||->0 using the strong adjoint convergence. Uniformity for compact norm-continuous K(z) families follows from finite nets. Therefore F_n=R_n C_n -> F=RC in norm locally uniformly off J. The factorization z-S=(z-B)(I-F) is in the displayed order; it does not exchange B and C.

C\J is connected. F is analytic compact-valued and I-F invertible at sufficiently large |z|. Analytic Fredholm reduction, or a finite-rank Schur block reduction locally followed along paths from a large z, yields a discrete set of noninvertibility points and finite-rank polar parts. The finite-rank spectral subspace at each pole is the total generalized eigenspace; algebraic multiplicity is its dimension. This establishes isolated finite-multiplicity off-J spectrum without assuming diagonalizability.

For compact L in rho(S) off J, continuity of (I-F)^-1 and compactness give a finite uniform inverse bound. Norm convergence of F_n plus a Neumann estimate makes I-F_n invertible throughout L eventually, uniformly. Multiplication by R_n proves the required uniform full resolvent bound. A hypothetical matrix eigenvalue sequence tending to z0 in this set eventually lies in a small compact disk with the same property, a contradiction.

For a fixed isolating disk with closed disk missing J, write G_n=(I-F_n)^-1 F_n. Uniform norm convergence on its boundary gives G_n->G. Each G is compact. The full resolvent equals R_n+G_n R_n. The R_n integral is zero because it is analytic on the full disk. The difference of the compact remainder terms is bounded by ||G_n-G|| ||R_n||+||G(R_n-R)||; the second term needs the adjoints just established. Thus the Riesz integrals converge in operator norm. Full static resolvent norm convergence is not required and generally fails.

Two idempotents within norm less than one inject each other's ranges into one another. Since the limiting off-J projection has finite rank, dimensions agree eventually. No orthogonality is needed. Smaller nested isolating circles force the enclosed eigenvalue cluster to shrink to its limiting eigenvalue with total algebraic multiplicity. Both subspace gaps are bounded by the projection norm difference. The static bound and norm convergence of C_n give uniform matrix norms, excluding eigenvalue escape to infinity. Extra whole-space zero eigenvectors have zero Riesz integral on these disks, since 0 is in J. Quantifiers are a compact set or a fixed isolating contour followed by sufficiently small h, not a single h threshold uniform over all off-J points.

## Deliberate failure controls to implement

1. Exact integer shell numerator sums test cubic cancellation; deleting one point from a complete shell must give a nonzero matrix numerator. Keeping the unshifted delta term or changing the zero diagonal changes the target operator.
2. Strong convergence alone cannot be multiplied by a fixed compact operator on the left: X_n=|e1><en| tends strongly to zero but K X_n=X_n for K=|e1><e1|, with norm one. X_n^* fails strong convergence. Replacing the selfadjoint static background by X_n makes the off-zero projection of 2K+X_n equal K+X_n/2, at fixed distance 1/2 from K. This is an exact infinite-space mutation, checked in its active 2x2 block.
3. B_n=|en><en| tends strongly to zero with spectrum in [0,1], but its resolvent at z=2 differs from that of zero by (1/2)|en><en|. A demand for full resolvent norm convergence would be false.
4. A Jordan 2x2 block [[2,1],[0,2]] approximated by [[2,1],[epsilon^2,2]] has two simple eigenvalues 2+/-epsilon and the identical total projection. Total algebraic multiplicity is stable, individual Jordan sizes are not.
5. A fixed rational grid meets the countably punctured compact support only at its anchor. Finite enumerations do not prove the infinite rational-exclusion assertion; that is a set-theoretic deduction from the construction.
