## Exact claim and boundaries

Theorem 1 (PDF p.1; TeX 55–66, proof 240–279) is about **every characteristic-zero field** `k`, every **nonzero** polynomial `h∈k[x]`, and the ordinary cohomology `HH•(A_h,A_h)` of

`A_h = k⟨x,Y⟩/(Yx−xY−h(x))`.

Both the cup product and Gerstenhaber bracket are the canonical ones. A generator is any `k`-linear degree-minus-one operator satisfying displayed identity (1); the additional BV condition is `Δ²=0`. The four equivalent conditions are: `h` squarefree (including nonzero constants), zero canonical bracket, existence of any bracket generator, and existence of a BV generator. The squarefree case uses `Δ=0`. Every repeated irreducible factor forbids a generator even when square-zero is not required.

There is no algebraic-closure hypothesis, no finite-dimensionality or Frobenius hypothesis, and no base-change argument. Characteristic zero is indispensable to the reasoning: polynomial integration, cancellation of positive integers, separability of irreducibles and nonvanishing of `u′` are used. The proof does not cover positive characteristic.

At `h=0`, the original **embedded** subalgebra generated by `x` and `yh` is `k[x]`; Section 5 separately proves its negative-divergence BV operator. Substitution into the abstract two-generator presentation instead gives `k[x,Y]`. These are explicitly distinguished and no plane result is smuggled into the nonzero-parameter theorem. Neither general-algebra nor Poisson classification, nor any Keller/local/Jacobian/Dixmier extension, is established or used.

## Reconstructed proof and primary interfaces

1. **Structural complex and degree-zero/degree-two cohomology.** The actual Lopes–Solotar publisher PDF, Proposition 2.1 and Section 3 equation (3.1), gives precisely the bimodule differential printed on p.2 of this manuscript. Its Proposition 3.9 proof and Corollary 3.11 give `HH²=A/dA`, `d=monic gcd(h,h′)`; the length-two resolution gives `HHⁿ=0` for `n≥3`. Theorem 4.8 and its proof were inspected for the contracting-homotopy conclusion. Independently, a positive weighted filtration with `wt(x)=1`, `wt(Y)=max(1,deg h)` reduces this complex to the polynomial-plane bimodule Koszul resolution. Exactness lifts by decreasing leading weight. The PBW computation gives `C_A(x)=k[x]`, hence `Z(A)=k`. It also proves `[D_f]=0` iff `h|f`, using integration. The printed cochain image is indeed `hA+h′A=dA`; `dA` is a two-sided ideal. These points are valid for arbitrary nonzero scalar normalization of `h`.

2. **Complete degree-one span, not merely a few example derivations.** The actual Benkart–Lopes–Ondrus v2 body was checked at Lemma 2.13, Theorem 2.17 and its characteristic-zero proof, Proposition 3.1, extension criterion Theorem 4.2, Theorem 4.9, Lemma 4.11, and Theorems 5.3/5.7 with proofs. These close the normalizer, Weyl-derivation and spanning interfaces. Proposition 5.9 and Theorem 5.13 with their proofs, and Lopes–Solotar Section 6.1/Theorem 6.2, corroborate the center statement. The primary monic radical differs from the manuscript's `π=h/d` by a nonzero scalar, leaving the spans unchanged. Adding the localized `a₀=1/d` is legitimate; its adjoint preserves `A`, and multiples by `d` are inner. The proof needs no positive-characteristic normalizer assertions.

3. **Central annihilator (Lemma 2, p.3).** Extend `D_d` by `D_d(y)=1/π`. Literal Leibniz insertion yields the binomial derivative formula. With `P₀=1` and `P_{j+1}=πP′_j−(j+1)π′P_j`, each term of `D_d(g a_n)` is a polynomial multiple of `h^(n−1−j)y^(n−1−j)` and therefore lies in `A`. Its adjoint is inner. The spanning theorem then proves centrality for every degree-one class. When `d≠1`, the classes of `D_d` and `D_{d/u}` are nonzero and independent by their distinct degrees below `deg h`.

4. **Canonical cup product (Lemma 3, pp.3–4).** I checked the normalized-bar comparison, including constant and linear terms. The boundary of the polynomial bar sum telescopes to the exact small-resolution correction; the remaining `−[h]` contributes the linear term with the correct sign. This is a chain map lifting the identity, so the pullback really computes the canonical product. For arbitrary derivations the final factor `x^(s−1−i)` remains on the right. Substituting `D_f` leaves exactly `fE(x)` modulo `dA`. Consequently `c=[D_d]` annihilates all of `HH¹` under the cup product. This step is a universal chain calculation, not an inference from finite diagnostics.

5. **Obstruction to every generator (Lemma 4, p.4).** Since `H⁰=k`, let `λ=Δ|H¹`. A nonzero central `c` with `cH¹=0` forces `λ(c)b=λ(b)c`. An independent companion first forces `λ(c)=0`, and then forces `λ=0` everywhere. A cup-zero degree-one pair would therefore have zero bracket under any generator. The reasoning does not assume a particular formula for `Δ`, finite-dimensional `H¹`, a choice of `Δ|H²`, or `Δ²=0`.

6. **All repeated irreducibles, over the original field (Theorem 1, p.4).** For any irreducible `u|d`, put `g=d/u`, `L=πd′/d`, and define `b(x)=π`, `b(Y)=LY`. Factorization in `k[x]` proves `L∈k[x]`. Direct application to the relation gives `Lh+hπ′−πh′=0`, so `b` is a derivation. The cup is zero because `gπ=d(π/u)`. Their commutator is `D_q`, where `q=Lg−πg′=hu′/u²`. It is a nonzero polynomial of degree `deg h−deg u−1<deg h`, so it is not inner. This contradicts item 5 and also proves that the canonical bracket is nonzero. The examples `h=xⁿ` and `h=(x²+1)²` follow exactly. In the `Y=yh` convention, identifying `b` literally with `ad_(πy)` would be wrong; the manuscript correctly records the extra term `πh″−Lh′` and uses the direct definition instead.

7. **Squarefree and degenerate cases.** When `d=1`, every normalizer contribution is inner and `HH¹` is spanned by mutually commuting `D_f`. With `HH⁰=k` and `HH≥²=0`, every canonical bracket vanishes, so `Δ=0` works. This includes constants. For the embedded `h=0` line, the two-term `k[x]^e` resolution gives `HH⁰=k[x]`, `HH¹=k[x]∂x` and nothing higher. Checking mixed evaluation and vector-field commutators gives the claimed `Δ(f∂x)=−f′`, and `Δ²=0`.

## Original question and attribution

The actual OWR 46/2023 contribution, printed pp.2702–2703, was read in full. Question (2) concerns this embedded polynomial Weyl-subalgebra family in characteristic zero and its nonsemisimple Nakayama branch. Its BV sign convention agrees because negative cohomology makes `Δ(1)=0`. The `hy` versus `yh` conventions generate the same subalgebra since their difference is `h′`.

Lopes–Solotar p.1381 records `ν(x)=x`, `ν(Y)=Y+h′`. For nonconstant `h`, the invariant span of `Y,h′` is a genuine nontrivial unipotent block; for a nonzero constant, `ν` is the identity. The manuscript therefore addresses the concrete nonsemisimple branch, while its squarefree nonconstant examples demonstrate that nonsemisimplicity alone is not an obstruction.

The actual Kowalzig–Krähmer Theorem 1.7/Section 7.4 was inspected: its conclusion is ordinary cohomology, and semisimple modular automorphism is a hypothesis; twisted coefficients occur in the homology used for duality. It cannot simply be applied here after dropping semisimplicity. Herscovich–Li Proposition 4.15 (pp.1802–1803, proof read) already rules out any bracket generator for FK(3), so the manuscript's explicit predecessor credit is appropriate. Bian et al. v2 Criterion 7.7 and Theorem 7.11 concern a different truncated-cycle family; the criterion includes commuting odd generators. These interfaces support the manuscript's distinctions. Their entire long papers were not independently re-proved. No exhaustive firstness or present-openness conclusion is drawn.
