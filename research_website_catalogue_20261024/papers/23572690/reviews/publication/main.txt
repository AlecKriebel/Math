                                                  Abstract
           Let k be any ﬁeld of characteristic zero and let 0 6= h ∈ k[x]. For Ah = khx, Y i/(Y x − xY −
       h(x)), the ordinary Hochschild cohomology, with its canonical cup product and Gerstenhaber
       bracket, admits a Batalin–Vilkovisky generator if and only if h is squarefree. In the squarefree
       case its bracket is zero. A repeated irreducible factor produces a cup-zero, noncommuting pair of
       degree-one classes, and a central annihilator forces every putative generator to vanish on degree
       one. Thus in that case no degree-minus-one bracket generator exists, even without requiring
       its square to be zero. The argument uses the published resolution and derivation structure of
       this family, and answers the concrete-family question recorded by Lopes in Oberwolfach Report
       46/2023, p. 2702, Question (2).


1     Question, conventions, and statement
Throughout, cohomology means ordinary HH• (A, A); its product and bracket are the canonical
ones. A bracket generator is a k-linear map ∆ : HHn → HHn−1 such that, for homogeneous a, b,

                          [a, b] = (−1)|a| ∆(a ⌣ b) − (−1)|a| ∆(a) ⌣ b − a ⌣ ∆(b).                         (1)

A BV generator additionally satisﬁes ∆2 = 0. Negative cohomology is zero, so ∆(1) = 0. This is
the convention of the original question [5, p. 2702]; in degree one the bracket is the commutator of
derivations. Units are regarded as squarefree polynomials.

Theorem 1. For every characteristic-zero ﬁeld k and every nonzero h ∈ k[x], the following are
equivalent:

    1. h is squarefree;

    2. the canonical Gerstenhaber bracket on HH• (Ah ) is zero;

    3. HH• (Ah ) admits a bracket generator;

    4. HH• (Ah ) admits a BV generator.

When h is squarefree, ∆ = 0 is a BV generator. When h has a repeated irreducible factor, no
bracket generator exists.

0 6= c ∈ H 1 is Lie-central, cH 1 = 0, and dimk H 1 > 1. Every degree-minus-one bracket generator
on H vanishes on H 1 . Consequently every cup-zero pair in H 1 would have zero bracket.

Proof. Write λ = ∆|H 1 : H 1 → k. The degree-one part of (1) is

5    Original scope, predecessors, and veriﬁcation
The recorded Nakayama automorphism is ν(x) = x, ν(Y ) = Y + h0 [6, p. 1381]. For nonconstant
h, its restriction to spank {Y, h0 } is a nontrivial unipotent Jordan block, so it is not semisimple. For
nonzero constant h, it is the identity. Thus Theorem 1 gives both positive and negative answers in
the whole original nonsemisimple branch; it is not an extension of a semisimple-Nakayama theorem
by omission of a hypothesis.
     If h = 0 in the original embedded deﬁnition, the generated algebra is k[x]. Here HH0 = k[x],
HH = k[x]∂x , and higher cohomology vanishes. Set ∆(f ∂x ) = −f 0 , ∆(k[x]) = 0. The Leibniz rule
    1

veriﬁes (1) in degrees (0, 1) and (1, 1), and the other cases vanish; ∆2 = 0. This separately answers
that degenerate specialization. Setting h = 0 in the abstract two-generator presentation gives a
polynomial plane, which is a diﬀerent algebra.
     Kowalzig–Krähmer’s ordinary-cohomology theorem [4, Theorem 1.7] assumes semisimplicity of
the modular automorphism; its construction uses twisted-coeﬃcient homology in a duality. It does
not classify the present nonsemisimple branch. Herscovich–Li’s earlier no-generator theorem [3]
concerns the ﬁnite-dimensional three-generator Fomin–Kirillov algebra, not this Ore-domain fam-
ily, and already uses the stronger notion of generator without square-zero. The recent positive
theorem for truncated basic cycle algebras [2, Theorem 7.11] concerns another family; its Crite-
rion 7.7 includes commuting odd generators, unlike the obstruction pair here. Neither theorem is
contradicted or extended to an unstated class.
