                The least-period criterion for outer focal products
                                in elliptic billiards

                                           Independent researcher

                                                   Abstract
            Fix a noncircular ellipse and a proper nested confocal elliptical caustic supporting periodic
        billiards of least period N ≥ 3. Intersect the boundary tangents at consecutive impact points.
        The product of the ordinary distances from these outer vertices to either original focus is in-
        dependent of phase if and only if 4 | N . The two constant products then agree. The positive
        direction is the primitive interpretation of the invariant k115 and is also proved in Ferudun’s
        contemporaneous note, which we explicitly credit. A paired-distance divisor proves both di-
        rections, including odd periods and the four-period degree drop. Repeated traversals cannot
        restore constancy. Historical priority and independent discovery are not certiﬁed.

Proof of Theorem 1. Write R± (u) = Nj=0 |Q(u + jδ) − F± |. For all N , the antipodal phase shift
gives
                    R− (u) = R+ (u + 2K),    R+ (u)2 R− (u)2 = F(u)N/L .                   (12)
If either focal product were constant, both would be constant by the ﬁrst identity. The second
would make the meromorphic function F N/L constant on a real interval. It has no real poles; the
identity theorem then makes it constant on T . For 4 ∤ N this contradicts its nonzero divisor (10).
This proves necessity also for odd N , without assuming that an antipodal point belongs to the
same odd primitive orbit.
    If 4 | N , then L = N/2 and coprimality makes τ odd. Now Lδ = 2Kτ really does pair the
vertices inside the orbit: Q(u + (j + L)δ) = −Q(u + jδ). Therefore
                                R+ (u) = R− (u),        R+ (u)2 = F(u).
The unique positive square root of the positive constant proves suﬃciency and equality of the two
constants. No complex square-root branch is used.

    The use of compact-torus pole cancellation is classical; compare Akopyan, Schwartz and Tabach-
nikov [7]. The period condition depends on the subgroup, not the chosen coprime generator, so
includes every star winding in (3).

Corollary 1. For any positive integer r, the product on r traversals is phase independent exactly
when the primitive product is.
Proof. The repeated product is R± (u)r and x 7→ xr is injective on (0, ∞).

   For an explicit same-caustic check, take a = 3, b = 1 and λ = 9/16. Two primitive six-periodic
orbits have vertices

    For a circle, both foci coincide with the center and the outer radius of a regular star is
a/ cos(πτ /N ), so every admitted period has a constant product. Noncircularity is therefore essen-
tial to necessity. Hyperbolic or collapsed caustics, separatrices and N = 2 are outside Theorem 1;
no conclusion about them is drawn by a limit.
