    CoNP-completeness of binary directed chip-ﬁring reachability
                                           Alec Kriebel
                                      Independent researcher
                                     ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                               Abstract
         Ordinary legal chip-ﬁring reachability is coNP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time
     many-one reductions for ﬁnite loopless strongly connected directed multigraphs, with binary
     adjacency entries and nonnegative binary initial and target conﬁgurations. We give an explicit
     reduction from the complement of the halting problem of Farrell and Levine. A ﬁnite-state cutoﬀ
     controls an exact simulation, a gate supplies a cleanup reserve, and a marker forces that gate
     to have ﬁred in every target-reaching word. Together with the prior coNP-membership theorem
     of Hujter, Kiss and Tóthmérész, this answers the historical general-digraph hardness question
     in the stated model. We credit earlier period, recurrence and reserve-simulation arguments.
     Exact-result, gadget and method priority, independence of discovery, and present-day openness
     remain unknown after a bounded source audit. This extensively AI-assisted note is unrefereed.


1    The language, result and attribution
A ﬁnite directed multigraph has adjacency entries auv ∈ N, counting arcs from u to v, and outgoing
              ∑
degrees dv = w avw . A conﬁguration is x ∈ NV . A legal ﬁring at v requires xv ≥ dv ; it subtracts
dv chips there and sends avw chips to each w. Firings are sequential. A legal word may stop at
any conﬁguration, whether or not more ﬁrings are available. The empty word is allowed. With
column-vector convention, its ﬁring matrix is

                                        Lwv = avw − dv 1w=v .

Thus a word of ﬁring count f has endpoint x + Lf . Loops cancel from L but remain in the
ﬁring threshold. All graph and conﬁguration integers are written in binary; multiplicities are never
expanded into lists of arcs.
    Let Reachable consist of well-formed triples (G, x, y) with G nonempty, loopless and strongly
connected, and x, y ∈ NV , for which a legal word from x reaches y. Invalid encodings are rejected.
Strong connectivity and all these validity conditions are polynomial-time testable. The one-vertex
loopless case is included and has reachability exactly when x = y. The number of vertices is part
of the input.
Theorem 1. Reachable is coNP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduc-
tions.
    The hardness construction uses parallel arcs, large degrees and large chip counts. It establishes
no corresponding classiﬁcation for simple graphs, unary encodings, bounded degrees or bounded
total chip counts. The membership assertion is for nonnegative conﬁgurations; it is not an extension
to every signed formulation.

    The source NP-completeness theorem is Farrell–Levine [2, Corollary 3.2], including its total-
Laplacian hardness interface. Hujter–Kiss–Tóthmérész [3, Theorem 12] already proved the coNP
membership used here. We explain that certiﬁcate with attribution in Section 4; period deletion
and reduced ﬁring counts go back to Björner–Lovász [1]. The general-digraph hardness question
appears in [3, Problem 19, arXiv v4] and in the Egres problem list [6]. Its survey separates simple
digraphs from multigraphs. Hardness on our nonnegative subclass answers its general multigraph
question, including when that survey permits signed conﬁgurations. Historical open labels do not
establish openness in 2026.
    There are close prior proof mechanisms. Tóthmérész [5, Claim 2.4] gives a deterministic
recurrence-to-reachability conversion: after one chosen legal ﬁring from x, return to x is equivalent
to recurrence of x. Proposition 2.8 there instead uses the existence of a recurrent representative
of a nonhalting class; it does not deterministically construct that representative as a Karp output.
The resulting Theorem 2.3 is an earlier polynomial-hierarchy barrier. HKT’s recurrent-target theo-
rem [3, Theorem 14] likewise retains a material recurrence premise. Kiss–Tóthmérész [4, Section 4]
already use large reserves for ﬁnite-horizon simulation in a distance-to-nontermination/divisor-rank
reduction. Scaling and bounded-drift reserves in general are not claimed as new. Here the speciﬁed-
target argument is the gate/marker equivalence below. These comparisons establish neither priority
for that argument nor the absence of equivalent earlier proofs.


2    Source conventions and preprocessing
The credited source problem asks whether a nonnegative conﬁguration on a strongly connected
binary-adjacency directed multigraph halts [2]. Loops are allowed. The standard halting dichotomy
states that either every maximal legal game terminates at a common stable conﬁguration, or every
maximal game is inﬁnite [3, Theorem 1]. We use Nonhalting for valid instances in the latter case.
It is coNP-hard. To be precise about strings, a reduction to HALTING can replace any invalid
output by a ﬁxed valid nonhalting instance without changing its HALTING answer; complementing
then gives valid nonhalting outputs for the coNP source. Equivalently one can use the literal com-
plement of HALTING, including invalid strings, and send those strings to a ﬁxed YES reachability
instance.
    For valid-instance Nonhalting with invalid strings rejected, our map sends invalid strings to
the two-cycle with X = (0, 0) and Y = (1, 0), a NO instance by conservation. For the literal-
complement convention it sends them to the same graph with X = Y = (0, 0), a YES instance.
These conventions are alternative total extensions, not conﬂicting promises.

Lemma 2. The source can be restricted to loopless strongly connected graphs on at least two
vertices, with nonnegative conﬁgurations. Signed intermediate conﬁgurations can also be normalized
in polynomial time without changing legal words before loop removal.

Proof. If a signed intermediate vector is used, set kv = max(0, −xv ) and add kv loops and kv
chips at each v. The net ﬁring matrix is unchanged. After any word, its current coordinate and
its threshold both diﬀer from their old values by kv . Induction therefore gives equivalent legality
at every step, not merely equality of Laplacians. Stability and inﬁnite play correspond, and the
new vector is nonnegative. This supplies an explicit bridge for signed lattice representatives in the
source hardness interface.
    For each vertex having ℓv > 0 loops, replace them by ℓv arcs v → rv and ℓv arcs rv → v, with a
fresh relay rv initially empty. Keep other arcs and old initial counts. The old degree is unchanged.
An old word lifts by returning each relay packet immediately after its parent ﬁres. Conversely, in


                                                 2
any expanded legal word let tv , sv be the parent and relay counts. The relay holds ℓv (tv − sv ) ≥ 0,
hence sv ≤ tv . At each old vertex the projected original state exceeds the expanded state by exactly
that pending return. Every projected old move is legal. An inﬁnite expanded word has inﬁnitely
many old moves, since relay counts are bounded by parent counts. Thus existence of inﬁnite play is
preserved in both directions, and the dichotomy gives the halting equivalence. Strong connectivity
survives, at most one relay is added per vertex, and all integers retain polynomial bit length.
     A singleton with ℓ loops never changes its count and is nonhalting exactly when x ≥ ℓ, including
ℓ = 0. Classify it by one binary comparison and replace it by the two-cycle at (1, 0) for nonhalting or
(0, 0) for halting. Thereafter n ≥ 2 and all degrees are positive. All steps are polynomial-time.


3    The gate and marker reduction

                       N = (C + 1)n ,       M = N + 1,       B = N (M D + 1).                        (1)

The integer N is a ﬁnite-state cutoﬀ, not a polynomial running-time bound.

Lemma 3. The source is nonhalting if and only if it has a legal word of length N .

Proof. Conservation and nonnegativity put every legal source state in {0, . . . , C}n , a set of at most
N states. A word of length N visits N + 1 states and repeats one. The intervening nonempty legal
closed word repeats forever. By the dichotomy no terminating game is then possible. Conversely
a nonhalting game supplies a length-N preﬁx. The case C = 0, N = 1 is included: positive source
degrees make every vertex initially illegal.

    Construct H with the old vertices and a gate g and marker m: scale every old multiplicity by
M ; add one arc v → g and B arcs g → v for each old v; add one arc in each direction between g
and m. The thresholds are

                           dH
                            v = M dv + 1,       dH
                                                 g = nB + 1,        dH
                                                                     m = 1.

There are no loops, and the gate connects every old vertex and marker in both directions. Thus H
is strongly connected. Put

          Xv = M xv + N,                               Xg = nB + 1 − N,              Xm = 0,         (2)
           Yv = M xv + M N (iv − dv ) + B,             Yg = (n − 1)N,                Ym = 1.         (3)

These are valid nonnegative vectors. Indeed Xg ≥ 1 and Yv ≥ B − M N D = N . Both totals are
M C + (n − 1)N + nB + 1, and X ̸= Y .

Lemma 4. Before the ﬁrst gate ﬁring, the ﬁrst N old moves simulate the source exactly, in every
possible order. The gate cannot ﬁre before N old moves and becomes legal after exactly N such
moves.

Proof. The marker remains empty before the ﬁrst gate and cannot ﬁre. After t old moves with
individual counts tv , let z denote the source state obtained by projecting those moves, inductively
while they are source-legal. Direct comparison of updates gives

                    hv = M zv + N − tv ,      hg = nB + 1 − N + t,         hm = 0.                   (4)

Just before any of the ﬁrst N old moves, t < N and 0 ≤ tv ≤ N − 1. If zv ≥ dv , then hv ≥ M dv + 1;
if zv ≤ dv − 1, then
                            hv ≤ M (dv − 1) + N = M dv − 1 < M dv + 1.
This proves legality equivalence inductively for each chosen move. The gate formula proves the
claimed threshold timing, including the boundary just before move N .

Proposition 5. (G, x) ∈ Nonhalting if and only if (H, X, Y ) ∈ Reachable.

Proof. If the source is nonhalting, lift a legal word of exactly N old moves by Lemmas 3 and 4, and
ﬁre g once. Every old vertex had at least zero chips before that ﬁring and now has at least B. If
its old count was tv ≤ N , complete it to N , ﬁring neither gate nor marker again. Every ordering
of these remaining moves is legal: ignoring all future incoming chips, each vertex needs at most N
payments of M dv + 1, and
                                  B = N (M D + 1) ≥ N (M dv + 1).
Before each requested payment fewer than that vertex’s allotted payments have occurred. The ﬁnal
count is fv = N at old vertices, fg = 1, fm = 0. Multiplication by LH gives exactly (3). This is
reachability, so other available moves need not be taken.
    Conversely take any legal word from X to Y , with actual counts k, not assumed equal to f .
The marker balance gives kg − km = 1, hence kg ≥ 1. Before its ﬁrst gate ﬁring there was no
marker ﬁring, and the gate must have received at least N chips from old moves. Therefore at least
N old moves preceded that ﬁrst gate. Their ﬁrst N moves project legally by Lemma 4. Lemma 3
gives source nonhalting. Delayed ﬁrst gates, extra subsequent gate/marker ﬁrings and added period
vectors do not aﬀect this argument.

Polynomial-time bound. For binary input length s, n ≤ s and the bit lengths of C + 1 and D are
polynomial in s. The bit length of N is at most 1 + n log2 (C + 1), with the constant retained
when C = 0; those of M and B are also polynomial. Preprocessing uses at most linearly many new
vertices. The n + 2 dimensional output is formed by polynomially many operations on polynomial-
bit integers. Integer exponentiation in (1) takes polynomial bit time. The reduction writes neither a
length-N ﬁring word nor unary copies of parallel arcs. Lemma 2 and the total encoding conventions
complete the deterministic Karp map. Credited source NP-completeness therefore proves coNP-
hardness.


4    The credited coNP certiﬁcate
For completeness we give the strongly connected case of the prior membership argument [3, Theorem
12], using the period deletion of [1]. This is an exposition of prior membership machinery. Assume
n ≥ 2. The integer kernel of L is generated by a positive primitive vector p. Directed spanning-tree
cofactors compute p up to their gcd; each has polynomial bit length. Integer-image membership
for y − x and an integer solution h of Lh = y − x are computable in polynomial time by integer
linear algebra. If no such solution exists, the pair is not reachable. Otherwise deﬁne
                                              (            )
                                     g = h − min ⌊hv /pv ⌋ p.
                                                  v

Then g ≥ 0, Lg = y − x, and gw < pw for some w. Every nonnegative solution is g + kp with integer
k ≥ 0, so g is the unique reduced solution.

The retained word has at least as many chips at its ﬁring coordinate and remains legal. Its endpoint
is unchanged. Iterating shows that a reachable target has a legal realization with exactly count g.
    A negative certiﬁcate, once g exists, is an integer vector u satisfying

                  0 ≤ u ≤ g,      u ̸= g,     (x + Lu)v < dv     whenever uv < gv .                (5)

It is not necessary that u itself be legally realizable. In a hypothetical legal word of count g, take
the ﬁrst ﬁring exceeding u. Its own preceding count is uv and the other counts are at most their u
coordinates, so its available chips are at most (x+Lu)v < dv , a contradiction. Conversely a maximal
legal word bounded by g supplies such a proper u if g cannot be realized. Its existence does not
require the veriﬁer to run that potentially long word. The vector has polynomial bit length and all
inequalities are checked in polynomial time. Integer-image nonmembership and invalid encodings
are themselves polynomial-time decidable branches of the complement veriﬁer. The singleton case
is decided by x = y. This proves the credited coNP membership and completes Theorem 1.


5    Veriﬁcation, source limits and disclosure
The accompanying standard-library veriﬁcation kit supplies fresh exact ﬁnite controls for the cutoﬀ,
all-order preﬁx simulation, cleanup budgets, arbitrary target counts, loop/signed interfaces, binary
sizes and the reduced-count certiﬁcate, with deliberate faults. Checks stay active under optimized
Python. They supplement the written proof and are not universal or formal certiﬁcates. The
original proof edition distributed no executable: this package does not claim to replay its omitted
historical programs or aggregate counts. Exact source editions, hashes, reading scopes, selected
authored audit records and individual custody exclusions are supplied; third-party source bodies
are not redistributed.
    As of preparation on 15 October 2026, a bounded priority audit read speciﬁed relevant scopes in
22 primary/related bodies, with 60 recorded queries. It established no earlier full antecedent among
those interfaces, which is not an exhaustive absence result. Unavailable older Petri-net originals
and every uninspected passage remain unknown. Exact-result, exact-gadget and broader method
priority, independence of discovery and current worldwide openness are unknown. The theorem is
presented as a complete attributed proof answering the historical mathematical question, without
a ﬁrst-discovery claim.
