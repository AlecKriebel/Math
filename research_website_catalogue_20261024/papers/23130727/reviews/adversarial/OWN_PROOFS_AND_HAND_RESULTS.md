## Family A: finite equality graph, with a self-contained ordinary-period proof

**C01 — involution really acts on letters.** Set z=θ(ε). Antimorphy gives z=zz, hence |z|=2|z| and z=ε. Involutivity makes θ injective, so no nonempty word maps to ε. If θ(a)=xy for a letter a and nonempty x,y, applying θ gives a=θ(y)θ(x) with two nonempty factors, impossible in a free monoid. Thus θ restricts to an involutive letter permutation σ and reverses the order of letters. No finite-alphabet assumption was used. The erasing or variable-length-antimorphism route is therefore excluded by the actual hypotheses.

**C02 — threshold anchors both witnesses.** Let d=gcd(p,q)>0. Since p≥d and q≥d, p+q−d≥p,q. Thus L≥max(p,q) and each witness u of length p or q is its actual prefix in w. In particular v=w[0:d] exists. Empty/too-short prefixes cannot evade any required constraint at the asserted threshold. Nonminimal periods are entirely allowed.

**C03 — independent ordinary Fine–Wilf graph lemma.** Let a≤b be ordinary periods of a word of length N≥a+b−g, g=gcd(a,b). Construct the undirected equality graph on positions0,…,N−1 with edges at distances a or b when endpoints lie inside the interval. Distance-a edges connect every residue class modulo a into a chain. Collapse those chains to vertices0,…,a−1. For every r=0,…,a−g−1 there is a distance-b edge from r to r+b, because r+b≤a+b−g−1≤N−1. It induces the edge r→r+b mod a.

Translation by b modulo a has exactly g cycles: one full cycle for each residue modulo g, since b/g is coprime to a/g. The last g residues a−g,…,a−1 contain exactly one representative in each such cycle. Keeping all translation edges except those starting at these last g vertices removes exactly one edge from each cycle. The remaining undirected path connects every vertex of that cycle. This also covers a/g=1, when a singleton needs no edge, and a/g=2, when the two oriented edges have the same unordered endpoints. Therefore every pair of actual positions congruent modulo g is connected using actual in-interval equality edges. Their letters agree and g is an ordinary period. Additional available b-edges can only add equalities. This is a complete threshold proof, rather than reliance on an uninspected Fine–Wilf citation or a Bézout path that may leave the finite word.

**C04 — graph control a4,b6,N8.** Here g2. Collapse positions modulo4. The usable starting residues0,1 give b-edges0→2 and1→3. The full connected components are {0,2,4,6} and {1,3,5,7}. Hence ordinary periods4 and6 on a word of length8 force period2. This is exactly the doubled p2,q3,L4 boundary. The two chains verify the quotient argument in the smallest coprime nontrivial alternating-period case; they do not establish the universal case by enumeration.

## Family B: finite reflection, phase and a falsified shortcut

**C05 — common finite extension, including incomplete blocks.** For each actual p-prefix u, define s_i periodically modulo2p by s_r=u_r for0≤r<p and s_r=σ(u_(2p−1−r)) for p≤r<2p. In BOTH residue halves, s_(−1−i)=σ(s_i). Since s_i=w_i on0≤i<L, the restriction to−L,…,−1 is σ(w_(L−1))…σ(w_0)=θ(w). Consequently the restriction to[−L,L−1] is the same W=θ(w)w for p and q, regardless of the incomplete last block. This does not assert that the two infinite witness sequences agree outside that finite interval. All equalities are exact letter identities.

**C06 — universal recovery of alternating phase.** Apply C03 to W of length2L with ordinary periods2p,2q; gcd is2d and the length condition doubles exactly. Thus positions congruent modulo2d in W agree. The central length2d block at[−d,d−1] is θ(v)v. For each i≥0 in w, choose its representative j=i mod2d in[0,d−1] if that residue is<d, and otherwise j=(i mod2d)−2d in[−d,−1]. Both i,j lie in W and the full period-step chain stays inside its contiguous index interval. Hence

w_i=v_r if r<d; w_i=σ(v_(2d−1−r)) if d≤r<2d.

This is exactly the prefix of (vθ(v))^ω from the actual initial phase. The proof establishes the full p,q/L/A/σ claim. It uses no fixed-point-free, binary, minimal-period or full-last-block assumption. The remaining external obligations concern identification/attribution and the stated comparisons, not a missing uniformity step in this symbolic argument.

**C07 — bare Bézout reduction outside the word is invalid.** For reversal take w=abb,L3,p2,q3. W=bbaabb has ordinary periods4 and6: only distance4 gives the two checks W_0=W_4=b, W_1=W_5=b; distance6 is vacuous. But W_0=b≠a=W_2, so it does not have period2 despite gcd(4,6)=2. Its length6 is below threshold8. Thus a purported proof that arbitrary finite ordinary periods always imply their gcd, simply by integer linear combinations of steps, is false; some needed intermediate positions leave the interval. This is a checked failure of that route, not a defect of the theorem whose length hypothesis fails here.

**C08 — ordinary period2d of w does not recover the phase alone.** For reversal and w=abab, ordinary period2 holds. With d1 the alternating conclusion would require the constant prefix aaaa…, which fails at position1. The common reflected extension is babaabab, and it does not have ordinary period2 (for example its positions3 and5 differ). The argument must apply ordinary periodicity to the REFLECTED extension and preserve the central phase, rather than to w alone.

## Family C: symbolic local boundary and degenerate periods

**C09 — exact p2,q3,L4 equations for every σ.** Write w=xyzt. Alternating period2 forces z=σ(y),t=σ(x). Alternating period3 forces t=σ(z)=y. Therefore y=σ(x), z=x and t=σ(x). This is precisely alternating period1. No assumption about σ(x)=x or σ(x)≠x was made. In particular the threshold works simultaneously for reversal, swapped letters and mixtures of fixed/paired letter orbits.

**C10 — period1 is not always constant.** With σ exchanging a and b, u=a generates (ab)^ω. The word aba has alternating period1 and period3 (its entire length3 block may be used), and satisfies the length threshold3. The d1 conclusion is aba…, not aaa…. Under reversal the corresponding period1 words are constant. This distinction is necessary for any finite checker or proof that normalizes the alphabet.

**C11 — equal periods.** For p=q=r the threshold is r and d=r. The given alternating r-period is already the conclusion, with the unique r-prefix witness. There is no extra relatively-prime requirement. A length r−1 word need not even have the conclusion's length-r prefix; this is outside the theorem, not an exception silently patched into it.

**C12 — divisibility and per-pair sharpness.** If p|q then d=p and the bound is q; the alternating p-period in the hypothesis is already the conclusion. For reversal, w=abb has alternating period2 and can be extended to an arbitrary length4 witness, so also has alternating period4 even though L3<4. Its alternating period2 conclusion already holds. Thus the uniform formula is not asserted to be a minimal threshold for every pair. The example cannot support a stronger per-pair optimality assertion.

## Family D: distinct involution, definition and alphabet countercontrols

**C13 — independent fixed-point-free failure one below threshold.** Let σ swap a↔b and θ=σ∘reversal. Take w=aab,p2,q3,L3,d1. The length2 block aa has θ(aa)=bb, so w is a prefix of aabb…. The length3 block aab has θ(aab)=abb, so w is a prefix of aababb…. Thus both alternating periods occur at L=p+q−d−1. Alternating period1 with the actual prefix a would generate abab…, which differs at position1. This is a genuine below-threshold counterexample even without fixed letters, independently distinct from the manuscript's reversal example. It proves no statement of per-pair optimality beyond these parameters.

**C14 — arbitrary block powers are weaker than alternating powers.** For reversal,u=ab, w=abab is a prefix of uu, a concatenation using {u,θ(u)}, but not a prefix of uθ(u)=abba. The first offending index is2. With σ exchanging a↔b,u=aa gives a second instance: aaaa is an allowed uu prefix but the alternating word begins aabb. A primary statement about unrestricted block choices therefore needs exact comparison; it cannot simply be substituted for the alternating definition.

**C15 — arbitrary alphabets have a finite orbit closure locally.** At the theorem threshold each witness is a prefix of w. Let A_0 be the set of letters appearing in w together with all their σ-images. It has size≤2L and is σ-stable. Restricting σ to A_0 preserves w and both witness blocks and every letter comparison above. Thus an infinite alphabet introduces no unseen choice needed by this proof. This is a reduction of each FIXED finite instance, not a proof that one binary or one fixed/swapped finite alphabet exhausts every instance or a bounded machine test proves all lengths.

**C16 — vacuous boundaries and units.** p,q are positive integers, so p+q−d≥1. Hence L0 cannot meet the theorem hypothesis. If A is empty then A^p is empty for p>0 and there is no alternating-period witness at all. L1 can meet the threshold only at p=q=1 and is covered by C11. All quantities are integer word lengths/position indices; there is no analytic limiting process, floating point tolerance, numerical stability or physical-unit conversion in any proof step.
