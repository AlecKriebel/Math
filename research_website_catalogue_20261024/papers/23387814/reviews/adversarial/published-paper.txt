    A Minimal-Surface Counterexample to a Seshadri Lower Bound
                                              Alec Kriebel
                                        Independent researcher
                                      ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                                           October 14, 2026


                                                Abstract
        We give an explicit family of counterexamples to the proposed lower bound ε(S, L; x) ≥
     (2 + |KS2 |1/4 )−1 for minimal complex projective surfaces. For every integer d ≥ 7, a classical
     plane-pencil construction followed by a double base change gives a smooth surface with ample
     canonical bundle, KS2 = 6(d − 1)(d − 3), and an ample integral line bundle L with L2 = 6 and
     ε(S, L; x) ≤ 1/(d−1). Already d = 7 contradicts the bound. The pencil and covering ingredients
     are classical; historical priority has not been fully cleared. This unrefereed note used AI tools
     extensively and has not received human peer review.

It is restated as Conjecture 2 in his Oberwolfach contribution [2, p. 1124] and as Question 6.1.6

Theorem 1. For every integer d ≥ 7 there are a smooth integral complex projective surface S, an
ample integral line bundle L on S, and a point x ∈ S such that KS is ample and

            KS2 = 6(d − 1)(d − 3),        L2 = 6,          ε(S, L; x) ≤        <               .

In particular S is minimal of general type and (1) is false.
    The starting pencil and its polarization are Miranda’s construction, recorded by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[4, Example 3.1, p. 182], Bauer [5, Proposition 3.3 and its proof], and Bauer–Szemberg [6, Propo-
sition 2.1, p. 2]. We use a double base change branched at four smooth ﬁbres. We claim neither
newness of these operations nor historical ﬁrstness of this counterexample. Section 5 gives the
priority and veriﬁcation limits.

2    An integral pencil and an ample line bundle
We supply the existence argument at ﬁnite degree, rather than relying on the asymptotic d = m + 1
assertion in [5, Proposition 3.3(b)]. In P2 use coordinates [X : Y : Z] and set

                 fd = Z(X d−1 − Y d−1 ) + X d ,          C0 = V (fd ),      p = [0 : 0 : 1].

The coeﬃcients of this linear polynomial in Z are coprime in C[X, Y ]. Gauss’s lemma, applied over
C(X, Y ), proves irreducibility. In the chart Z = 1, the tangent cone at p is xd−1 − y d−1 , a product
of distinct linear factors. Thus p is an ordinary (d − 1)-fold point. It is the only singular point:
the equations fZ = fY = 0 force either X = Y = 0, or X, Y ̸= 0 and Z = 0; in the latter case
fX = dX d−1 ̸= 0.
    Write Ne = e(e + 3)/2. The reducible or nonreduced degree-d locus R in PNd is the ﬁnite union
of the closed images of multiplication maps

                             PNa × PNd−a −→ PNd ,              1 ≤ a ≤ d − 1.

Since Nd − Na − Nd−a = a(d − a) ≥ d − 1, its dimension is at most Nd − d + 1. The closure of
the union of lines joining [fd ] to R has dimension at most Nd − d + 2 < Nd for d ≥ 3. Choose
a form g outside that closure. Then the entire pencil ⟨fd , g⟩ avoids R. We can simultaneously
choose g smooth, nonzero at p, and transverse to the smooth locus of C0 : these are nonempty open
conditions in the same irreducible parameter space, by Bertini and transversality for the complete
degree-d system. Bézout gives exactly d2 distinct simple base points pi , all away from p. Thus
every pencil member is integral and reduced.
    Blow up these base points once each, obtaining b : Y → P2 . Let H = b∗ OP2 (1), and let Ei be
the exceptional curves. Transversality resolves the pencil and gives f : Y → P1 , with ﬁbre class

All ﬁbres are strict transforms of integral reduced pencil members; the Ei are sections, not vertical
components. General ﬁbres are smooth. The special ﬁbre C, the strict transform of C0 , still has
multiplicity d − 1 at the point y above p.
    Put A = 2F + E1 . The blowup intersection rules give

             F 2 = 0,   F · Ei = 1,     Ei2 = −1,        A2 = 3,      A · E1 = 1,   A · F = 1.

Here F = f ∗ OP1 (1) is nef. For every integral curve D ̸= E1 , E1 ·D ≥ 0. If F ·D > 0, then A·D > 0.
If F · D = 0, D is vertical, hence is an entire ﬁbre because each ﬁbre is integral and reduced; in
this case A · D = 1. Together with A · E1 = 1 and A2 > 0, the Nakai–Moishezon criterion proves
that A is ample. The assertion about every ﬁbre is essential here.


3    The cover and minimality
Choose four distinct smooth ﬁbre values of f , avoiding the value of C. Let h : B → P1 be the
connected double cover simply branched at these values. For example its equation in a coordinate
                                            ∏
avoiding the four values at inﬁnity is v 2 = 4j=1 (t − tj ). Its smooth projective completion is
connected of genus one by Hurwitz. Form the ﬁbre product

                                      S = Y ×P1 B,         π : S → Y,

                                                     2
so π is ﬁnite ﬂat of degree two. It is étale oﬀ the four branch ﬁbres, including above the singularities
of all other ﬁbres. Along a branch ﬁbre, f is smooth and the local cover has equation t = v 2
in smooth coordinates (u, t) on Y , so S is smooth there as well. The map S → B is proper
with connected ﬁbres, because they are the ﬁbres of f . Since B is connected, S is connected: a
decomposition into two open and closed subsets would give a decomposition of B, using properness
and connected ﬁbres. A connected smooth scheme has a single irreducible component here, since
distinct components of a smooth scheme cannot meet. Hence S is integral. It is projective as a
ﬁbre product of projective varieties.
    The branch divisor on Y is linearly equivalent to 4F . The double cover is associated with
OY (2F ). Hurwitz therefore gives the equality of line bundles

                                          KS = π ∗ (KY + 2F ).                                      (2)

This is the classical cyclic-cover formula; compare [7, Section 2, p. 3]. In the present local model
the ramiﬁcation divisor has line bundle π ∗ OY (2F ) and is reduced, also giving (2) directly. Since
               ∑
KY = −3H + Ei , put
                                    N = KY + 2F = (d − 3)H + F.
We have N · Ei = 1, and for every other integral curve D, its image in P2 is a curve of degree e > 0,
so
                            N · D = (d − 3)e + F · D > 0       (d ≥ 4).
Moreover H 2 = 1 and H · F = d, whence

                          N 2 = (d − 3)2 + 2d(d − 3) = 3(d − 1)(d − 3) > 0.

Nakai–Moishezon proves N ample. A ﬁnite surjective pullback of an ample line bundle is ample
(alternatively apply the projection formula and Nakai on S). Thus (2) proves KS ample. In
particular S is of general type and has no rational (−1)-curve, since adjunction would give canonical
degree −1 on such a curve. Consequently S is minimal.


4    The pointed curve and the comparison
Set L = π ∗ A, an ample integral line bundle. The degree-two intersection formula gives

                          L2 = 2A2 = 6,         KS2 = 2N 2 = 6(d − 1)(d − 3).

The ﬁbre value of C is unbranched. Its two inverse images under h are distinct complex points, so

                                π −1 (C) = C ′ ⊔ C ′′ ,   C ′ ≃ C ≃ C ′′ .

In particular this is not a connected degree-two curve over C. Choose x ∈ C ′ mapping to y. The
local isomorphism, or étaleness and the split ﬁbre identiﬁcation, gives multx C ′ = d − 1. Projection
gives L · C ′ = A · C = 1. Therefore

                                                    L · C′      1
                                    ε(S, L; x) ≤             =     .
                                                   multx C ′   d−1
For d ≥ 7 the desired strict inequality is equivalent, after subtracting 2 and taking fourth powers
of positive numbers, to
                                        (d − 3)3 > 6(d − 1).

At d = 7 this is 64 > 36. The forward diﬀerence of the left side minus the right side is 3(d −
3)2 + 3(d − 3) − 5 > 0 for d ≥ 7, proving the claim for every such integer. In the smallest example
KS2 = 144, L2 = 6, and 144 < 44 yields 1/6 < (2 + 1441/4 )−1 . This proves Theorem 1.
    Only the displayed upper bound is asserted; an exact Seshadri value is unnecessary. The surfaces
vary with d, and x is a special point. The construction does not resolve positivity of the inﬁmum
over all ample integral line bundles and points on one ﬁxed complex surface. It does not contradict
very-general-point or Picard-rank-one lower bounds, or canonical and adjoint polarization bounds.
For d = 4, 5, 6 this particular curve does not give the strict comparison; this says nothing about
other curves or lower bounds on those surfaces.


5    Attribution, priority and veriﬁcation limits
Miranda’s pencil polarization is classical, with the direct record in [4], its extension in [5], and
the close arbitrary-rational construction in [6]. Those examples use nonminimal blowups. Earlier
double-cover examples in [8, Example 1.3] concern the canonical polarization and produce a quotient
1/2; that quotient cannot violate (1). Kollár’s example in [4, Example 3.7] concerns unbounded
indices of very ampleness, which is a diﬀerent assertion. Fuentes García [7] treats cyclic-cover
constants in other settings, including very general points over Picard-rank-one bases and points on
ramiﬁcation divisors. The present curve is over an unbranched singular ﬁbre of a plane blowup.
    Two independent internal primary-source priority audits, supplemented by the author’s source
comparisons, found no explicit earlier resolution of this precise bound in the text inspected. His-
torical priority nevertheless cannot be fully cleared. In particular the full Bansal–Majumder 2025
survey [9], the full Roy 2019 thesis, and some other theses were not inspected; longer sources and
later citation bodies were partly or selectively read, and ﬁnal journal versions were not globally
compared. The veriﬁcation package records exact reading scopes, access limits, ﬁngerprints and
search ledgers. We make no claim of ﬁrst discovery, novelty of the covering method, exhaustive
priority clearance, or independently veriﬁed openness immediately before this note.

PR 585, new independent derivations and adversarial reviews, source receipts, exact symbolic
