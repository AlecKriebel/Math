## Exact target

Fix a finite simple graph G, including isolated vertices and the empty graph. F₂(G) consists of normalized nonnegative binary masses p on the entire cube whose global MTP₂ inequalities hold, and whose mass is an actual product of finite nonnegative unary and original-edge factors. Factors may vanish; infinity and hidden vertices are not allowed. They need not individually be attractive. A(G) comprises the strictly positive normalized Ising laws exp(Σhᵢxᵢ+ΣJᵢⱼxᵢxⱼ)/Z, with finite real h, finite J≥0 on the same graph. Closure is ordinary pointwise/Euclidean closure in a fixed finite probability simplex, not union over graphs or node counts.

The whole theorem is F₂(G)=closure A(G), hence F₂(G) is closed. The forward density and reverse finite-factor recovery are separate nontrivial directions. MTP₂/Markov alone at zeros would not establish either finite factorization or this equality. The corollary is the precise edge-only signed-factor convention of Lauritzen's MI(G) intersected with global Markov MTP₂ M₂(G), with special isolated/edgeless cases. ## Universal independent forward route

Let S be the positive support. MTP₂ makes S a meet/join-closed subset of the Boolean cube. From an actual finite local product, S equals the set of assignments satisfying every **actual support projection** on vertices and original edges: if each selected local cell occurs in some support point, every corresponding factor entry is positive, so the whole product is positive. This uses existence of the factorization and cannot be inferred from MTP₂ alone.

The meet of S and join of S pin exactly the constant coordinates. After deleting those, the all-zero and all-one active assignments belong to S. Thus each active edge projection contains 00 and11 and is either full, missing10, missing01, or equality {00,11}. These are respectively no constraint, i⇒j, j⇒i, and both implications. Active-to-pinned projections add no further condition. Original-edge implication paths describe S exactly. Strongly connected implication classes force repeated equal coordinates; the condensation is an acyclic partial order, and its allowed assignments are exactly upsets. Each class is connected in the original graph, though lifting the following coefficients only needs a representative vertex and existing interclass edges.

Take real logarithms of all used positive factor cells. Missing cells can be assigned arbitrary finite log extensions; only equality on S is needed. Every binary pair function expands as constant+linear+quadratic. Substituting pins and equalities turns intraclass pair terms into unary terms. A pair of comparable classes also satisfies yKyL=yK or yL and contributes only a field. What remains on S is c+ΣHKyK+Σincomparable BKL yKyL. BKL is an **aggregate** of original cross-edge interactions; individual supplied log potentials can have either sign.

For each incomparable K,L, set all strict successors of either class to1 and everything else to0. This is an upset excluding K,L. Adding K alone, L alone or both preserves the upset property. These four support assignments differ on exactly those classes. Applying the logarithmic MTP₂ inequality to the two middle assignments leaves exactly BKL≥0: all other constant/linear/quadratic terms cancel. An edge must exist between the classes whenever BKL is nonzero. Put BKL on one such original edge and HK on one original coordinate of its class. This gives an attractive quadratic L(x) agreeing with logp−c on S even if the original factors were not attractive.

Let D be the number of violated pinned values and oriented original-edge implications, counting both directions for equality. It is an integer≥0, vanishes exactly on S, and is≥1 elsewhere. The polynomial −tD has only nonnegative pair interactions because −t xi(1−xj)=−t xi+t xi xj. Pins contribute fields only. Thus exp(L−tD), normalized, belongs to A(G) for finite t≥0. On S its unnormalized values equal e^(−c)p; outside they vanish as t→∞. A finite sum has limit e^(−c)>0, proving density. If all coordinates are pinned, use just the pin penalties. If there are no coordinates, the unique law is1. This is an all-graph proof, not a finite graph enumeration.

## Universal independent reverse route

For one positive attractive law set E=−Σhᵢxᵢ−ΣJᵢⱼxᵢxⱼ. Orient each undirected edge arbitrarily. The identity −Jxi xj=Jxi(1−xj)−Jxi gives one nonnegative nonterminal arc J and modifies the unary coefficient b. Represent bxi by i→t capacity b for b≥0, or s→i capacity−b for b<0, with the latter adding a constant−b to the energy. Source-side bits xi=1 identify all configurations with s–t cuts. Thus cut capacity C(x)=E(x)+κ, on only the old vertices plus s,t.

A maximum finite-capacity real flow exists because its feasible polytope is compact. The max-flow/min-cut theorem gives v=min C. With fuv=0 on missing arcs, ruv=cuv−fuv+fvu is nonnegative. For any cut, Σcross r=Σcross c−(net outward flow)=C−v=E−min E. This identity comes from conservation, not from a pointwise min-energy computation used as a substitute for an edge factorization. The only new nonterminal residual arcs are reversals of old edges; both directions remain factors on that same original edge. Backward terminal arcs never cross a valid cut; forward terminal arcs are unary. Consequently exp(−Σcross r) is a product of finitely many local entries in(0,1] and equals W(x)=exp(−E+min E).

The crucial lower normalization bound is ΣW≥1, since some configuration minimizes E and has W=1; also ΣW≤2^n. For any converging sequence of attractive laws, choose a subsequence of all finitely many bounded factor entries converging in[0,1]. Their products W* still sum to at least1 by finite continuity, so they cannot all disappear. The given probability limit is W*/ΣW*. Absorb the nonzero scalar into a unary factor for n>0. MTP₂ survives by polynomial inequalities. This yields actual finite original-edge factors, not just a toric limit. For n=0 the unique mass is1.

## Literal corollary and independent failure boundaries

An edge-only product is independent of each isolated bit. If E is nonempty, normalization makes isolated coordinates uniformly independent, and the law on nonisolated coordinates has an edge product after absorbing one scalar. Theorem1 on that induced graph and a limit preserve MTP₂ and independence. Absorb each recovered unary factor into an incident original edge, and absorb the isolated uniform scalar into one original edge. Replacing all finite signed entries by absolute values preserves a nonnegative product pointwise. If E is empty and n>0, the literal empty product is1 everywhere and not normalized, so MI is empty; if a tacit scalar is allowed it consists of the uniform law. Both are closed. For n=0 the sole law is1.

Finite-factor Markovity at zeros follows directly by conditioning on a positive-probability separator assignment and separating the resulting local product into connected components with positive finite normalizers. At null conditioning events the conditional requirement is vacuous; polynomial conditional-independence equalities cover the boundary. Hammersley–Clifford strict-positivity arguments cannot be used backwards to factor all zero-containing Markov laws.

For the stated four-cycle law with support x3=x4, all eight support masses are1/9 except p1111=2/9. The support is a lattice. Incomparable supported assignments cannot include the top1111, so their RHS is1/81 while meet/join weight is at least1/81; off-support RHS is zero. The two nonedge conditional independences follow because one of the conditioned bits determines the opposite variable. Every finite cycle-edge product satisfies the division-free balanced monomial identity p0000 p0111 p1011 p1100=p0011 p0100 p1000 p1111, since both sides use each edge cell equally often. Its values here are1/9⁴ and2/9⁴, so this law is not even in the full cycle edge toric closure. It therefore is not a counterexample to closedness of the MTP₂ finite-factor subclass: it is outside that subclass to begin with.

Necessary failure controls: MTP₂ alone does not guarantee original-edge support feasibility; arbitrary smoothing by mixing uniform mass need not preserve edge factorization; zeros forbid cancellation of local products; an originally supplied potential can be repulsive while deterministic equality aggregates interactions to an attractive coefficient; bounding factors without a nonvanishing normalization can lose all mass. The residual gauge supplies the missing bound. Positive laws have J≥0 iff their binary edge log interaction is attractive; for missing edges no interaction is introduced. The method is specifically finite, binary, pairwise and fixed-graph, not arbitrary higher-order, infinite-state or hidden-variable closure.
