## Exact theorem and assumptions

Write Ω={0,1}^V. The class F₂(G) consists of masses p with Σp=1, p≥0, all MTP₂ inequalities p(x∧y)p(x∨y)≥p(x)p(y), and an actual finite product

`p(x) = ∏ᵢ φᵢ(xᵢ) ∏ᵢⱼ∈E ψᵢⱼ(xᵢ,xⱼ)`.

Factors may vanish, but may not take infinity. The product itself is normalized. Individual supplied potentials need not be MTP₂. The strictly positive family A(G) has finite fields hᵢ∈R, nonnegative finite couplings Jᵢⱼ≥0 on original edges, and density proportional to `exp(Σhᵢxᵢ+ΣJᵢⱼxᵢxⱼ)`. The theorem is F₂(G)=closure A(G), in the finite-dimensional fixed-graph probability simplex.

It is a finite, binary, pairwise, same-graph statement. It does not assert that every MTP₂ global Markov law has an edge factorization, does not replace edges by maximal cliques, does not introduce hidden variables or extra nonterminal edges, and does not cover arbitrary higher-order or infinite-state models. The graph need not be connected. For an edgeless graph F₂(G) allows arbitrary independent Bernoulli coordinates, including pinned ones; its unary factors must not be confused with the separate edge-only source convention.

## Independent universal density proof

Let S={p>0}. It is nonempty and closed under meet/join. Actual finite local factorization implies that S is precisely the intersection of its vertex and original-edge projection cylinders: every local cell occurring in any support point has a positive corresponding factor entry, so an assignment meeting all projections has positive product. This conclusion uses finite factorization. MTP₂ alone does not provide it.

Delete pinned coordinates. The meet and join of all support states are 0 and1 on the remaining active coordinates, so each active edge projection contains 00 and11. The only possibilities are the full square, either one-direction implication support, or equality {00,11}. Therefore pins and original-edge inequalities xᵢ≤xⱼ describe S exactly. Strongly connected implication classes are repeated equal coordinates; the condensation order has S as its set of upsets. An active-to-pinned projection adds no further constraint. Each equality class is connected by original implication edges.

Take logs of used positive factor cells and extend only the missing logarithmic entries by arbitrary finite numbers. A two-bit function is constant+linear+quadratic. Pins and equality contraction turn intraclass terms into fields; a comparable-class product yKyL is also a field because one variable implies the other. On S one obtains `log p = c+ΣHK yK+Σ BKL yK yL`, with the last sum only over incomparable classes. A nonzero BKL can occur only if some original edge joins K to L.

Fix incomparable K,L. The union of their strict successors is an upset excluding both; adding K, L, or both gives four support states. Apply logarithmic MTP₂ to its two middle states. The mixed difference cancels every other term, leaving BKL≥0. This sign concerns the **sum** of interactions across tied classes. It does not authorize cancellation at zero cells or require each original supplied potential to be attractive.

Choose one representative coordinate per class for HK, and one actual joining edge for each BKL. The resulting L(x) is a finite attractive quadratic agreeing with logp−c on S. Let D(x) count pin violations and original-edge implication violations xᵢ(1−xⱼ), using both directions for equality. It is a nonnegative integer, zero exactly on S and at least1 outside. The exponent L−tD has nonnegative pair coefficients for every finite t≥0, because an implication penalty contributes `−t xᵢ+t xᵢxⱼ`. Thus its normalized exponential is in A(G). On S its weights are e^(−c)p, and outside they tend to0; the finite normalizer tends to e^(−c)>0. This proves every p∈F₂(G) is an attractive limit on the unchanged graph.

All-pinned laws use the same unary penalties directly. For no vertices the unique law is1. One may also see the convergence quantitatively: conditionally on S, every approximating law is exactly p; if outside unnormalized mass divided by e^(−c) is δₜ, then its L1 distance to p is `2δₜ/(1+δₜ)`, with δₜ bounded by a finite constant times e^(−t). This merely makes the written finite-sum convergence mechanism explicit.

## Independent universal reverse proof

For an attractive positive law put `E(x)=−Σhᵢxᵢ−ΣJᵢⱼxᵢxⱼ`. Orient each old edge arbitrarily and write `−Jxᵢxⱼ=Jxᵢ(1−xⱼ)−Jxᵢ`. The first term is the cost of a nonnegative capacity J on arc i→j. Collect the unary bᵢxᵢ terms; represent bᵢ≥0 by i→t, or bᵢ<0 by s→i with a constant adjustment. Taking xᵢ=1 on the source side gives a cut cost C(x)=E(x)+κ on exactly V plus two terminals.

Real capacities cause no existence gap. The finite feasible-flow polytope is closed, bounded and nonempty, so a maximum f exists. If its residual graph had an s–t path, a positive minimum residual along that finite path would augment the flow, contradicting maximality. Hence t is not reachable from s; the reachable-source cut has zero residual capacity and certifies v=min C. This argument does not assume a Ford–Fulkerson iteration terminates for arbitrary real capacities.

With absent reverse arcs assigned capacity/flow0, residual capacities `rᵤᵥ=cᵤᵥ−fᵤᵥ+fᵥᵤ` are nonnegative. Conservation gives, on every valid cut U,

`Σᵤ∈U,ᵥ∉U rᵤᵥ = C(U)−v = E(x)−min E`.

The only nonterminal residual arcs are old edges or their reversals. Combining the two directions produces the original-edge factor `exp(−rᵢⱼ a(1−b)−rⱼᵢ b(1−a))`; terminal arcs give unary factors. Reverse terminal arcs never cross a valid cut. Every factor entry lies in(0,1], and their product is `W(x)=exp(−E(x)+min E)`. A minimizing configuration has W=1, so `1≤ΣW≤2^|V|`.

Now take any convergent sequence of attractive laws. There are only finitely many unary/edge entries, each in[0,1], so a common subsequence converges entrywise. The limiting product W* has sum Z*≥1 by finite continuity. Consequently the given probability limit is W*/Z*, an actual finite nonnegative original-edge product after absorbing the scalar into one unary factor. MTP₂ survives by its polynomial inequalities. The empty-vertex case is again1. This proves closure A(G)⊆F₂(G). The other inclusion was proved above, and positive attraction itself is MTP₂ because each Jxᵢxⱼ is supermodular. The full theorem follows without relying on a finite sample or a conjectural factorization theorem at zeros.
