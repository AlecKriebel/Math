Exact target and hypotheses

For every finite family of complex projective K3 surfaces S_i, with distinct or repeated bases and Brauer classes α_i∈Br(S_i), take M_i to be an actual smooth projective moduli space of Gieseker-stable α_i-twisted sheaves with fixed numerical/sheaf data and polarization, or an actual smooth projective moduli space of σ_i-stable objects in D^b(S_i,α_i), for σ_i generic relative to that fixed numerical problem and within the exact scope of cited Bülles Theorem 0.1. The target is surjectivity CH^p(∏M_i)_Q→H^{2p}(∏M_i,Q)∩H^{p,p}(∏M_i,C) for every integer p. All cycles and correspondences are rational. This is the rational ordinary Hodge conjecture, not integral Hodge, generalized Hodge, effectivity of representatives, or finite-dimensionality of motives.

There is no asserted blanket existence, nonemptiness, smoothness or projectivity for arbitrary Mukai vectors or stability chambers. The stated absence of additional primitivity and fine-moduli assumptions is an author claim about the imported theorem's scope and must be checked against that actual theorem, not accepted from the note. Brauer classes are arbitrary only within that exact geometric scope. The source does not claim arbitrary deformations of K3^[n] type, singular or semistable compactifications, or moduli on abelian surfaces.

The explicitly stated specialization is every finite mixed product S_i^[n_i], with all n_i nonnegative integers, n_i=0 a point and n_i≥1 represented by rank-one ideal-sheaf moduli of Mukai vector (1,0,1−n_i). This geometric identification is a standard imported interface, not a new construction in this note. The target includes all finite numbers of factors, including the point for zero factors. It allows empty moduli factors, for which the product variety is empty and the cycle/cohomology groups vanish; this is distinct from an empty indexing family, whose product is a point.

Own checkable theorem-chain reconstruction

1. Let X and K be smooth projective of dimensions d and e. For A∈CH^a(X×K)_Q, the action A_* is p_K*(p_X^*(−)·A). The projection p_K has relative complex dimension d, hence A_* raises cohomological degree by 2(a−d), Chow codimension by a−d, and Hodge bidegree by (a−d,a−d). Thus a rational (p,p) class goes to a rational (q,q) class, q=p+a−d. This verifies the manuscript's degree convention directly without choosing the sign convention for a Tate twist.

2. For B∈CH^b(K×X)_Q, composition uses pullback to X×K×X, product of codimension a+b and pushforward through K of dimension e. Therefore B∘A has codimension a+b−e on X×X. Requiring it to contribute to Δ_X gives a+b=d+e. Applying B to codimension q then gives q+b−e=p. These identities check both directions; confusing d and e would break the composition degree.

3. Assume a finite family A_j,B_j with a_j+b_j=d+e_j and Σ_jB_j∘A_j=Δ_X. For any z∈Hdg^p(X), assume rational Hodge on K_j in degree q_j=p+a_j−d. If 0≤q_j≤e_j, choose Z_j with cl(Z_j)=A_j*z. If q_j is outside that range, H^{2q_j}(K_j,Q)=0, and Z_j=0 suffices. Proper pushforward, projection pullback and intersection commute with the cycle class. Therefore cl(Σ_jB_j*Z_j)=Σ_jB_j*A_j*z=Δ_X*z=z, and each summand has codimension p. All sums are finite. This proves the local conditional transfer. No equality, positivity, statistical, numerical, or empirical assumption is used.

4. The motive assertion supplies the required A_j,B_j only if its splitting is algebraic and degree homogeneous in rational Chow motives. A splitting of abstract Hodge structures alone does not supply algebraic cycles or an algebraic inverse. A weaker algebraic-correspondence identity on cohomology would suffice for this local Hodge consequence, but the published note claims the stronger Chow-motive identity from Bülles. We have not independently checked that imported construction.

5. For factor i, assume the actual algebraic splitting uses K_{ij}=S_i^{k_{ij}}, with finite J_i, 1≤k_{ij}≤dim M_i and integer Tate twists. Put d_i=dim M_i, e_{ij}=2k_{ij}, and choose algebraic representatives with a_{ij}+b_{ij}=d_i+e_{ij}. Exterior products, conjugated by the canonical algebraic reordering of factors, give A_J and B_J. Their degrees add: a_J+b_J=Σ_id_i+2Σ_ik_{ij_i}=dim X+dim K_J. Correspondence composition distributes, so Σ_JB_J∘A_J=⊠_i(Σ_jB_{ij}∘A_{ij})=⊠_iΔ_{M_i}=Δ_X. This is a Chow-correspondence identity, not an assumption that every Hodge class on a product decomposes into Hodge classes on factors. The even degrees of algebraic cycle classes introduce no odd-degree permutation sign here.

6. The resulting target K_J=∏_i S_i^{k_{ij_i}} is itself a finite mixed product of projective complex K3 surfaces, of dimension e_J=2Σ_ik_{ij_i}. Rational Hodge in every codimension on each entire K_J is precisely the central required input. Substitution into steps 1–3 yields the target conclusion conditional on both the splitting and that mixed theorem.

7. Reverse control: choosing α_i=0 and M_i=S_i^[1]=S_i in the claimed universal moduli theorem gives rational Hodge on every finite mixed K3 product. Consequently the moduli conclusion contains the full universal base assertion. Given the valid splitting, the two universal statements are equivalent. This route transfers the central difficulty to the mixed-K3 theorem; it does not independently solve it. Separate self-power statements cannot simply be substituted for the mixed premise in this written proof.

Mixed products and boundary controls

The Künneth summand H^2(S_1)⊗H^2(S_2) in H^4(S_1×S_2) can contain rational (2,2) tensors arising from Hodge morphisms between transcendental parts. Their algebraicity is not implied by multiplying divisor classes on individual factors. For S×S, the diagonal's transcendental component already illustrates why exterior products of individual algebraic divisor classes do not exhaust the relevant tensors. This is a warning against an invalid elementary reduction, not a counterexample to Hodge or to a separate theorem which might prove such correspondences by additional arguments.

The finite-disjoint-union point case has CH^0_Q and H^0_Q generated by the components' point classes, with no other nonzero targets. Products distribute over these components, so they can be removed componentwise. Positive-dimensional factors are the only case invoking the cited bound 1≤k≤dim M. The source does not improperly use that bound for a point. For non-pure disconnected smooth projective spaces, homogeneous degree formulas require a componentwise interpretation; actual fixed-data moduli are normally equidimensional, but the imported theorem's hypotheses must be checked before silently broadening “variety.” This is a scope check, not a demonstrated defect of the stated fixed-data theorem.

Negative or over-dimensional codimensions vanish. Twists may move q_j outside K_j's cohomological range even when p is in X's range; the zero choice in step 3 covers that case. Repeated K3 bases and different bases make no difference to the tensor formalism, provided the imported mixed input has the stated quantifiers. There is no restriction to Picard-rank, CM, quadratic-locus or specially polarized bases in the written target; any such restriction in the actual imported proof would affect the universal conclusion directly.

Non-fine control: if one formally replaces a family E by E^{⊕ρ}, then RHom(E^{⊕ρ},E^{⊕ρ}) is ρ² copies of RHom(E,E); its K-class and Chern character scale by ρ². Raw total Chern classes do not scale linearly: for example c_2(V⊕V)=2c_2(V)+c_1(V)^2. Therefore the mere existence of quasi-universal data is not a license to reuse an unnormalized Ext-diagonal formula. A correctly normalized rational construction can handle multiplicity, but that is part of the imported splitting interface. This hand calculation checks the need for the source's caution without verifying its cited construction.
