Quadratic convergence of SOS-density bounds
on convex bodies
Alec Kriebel
ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
17 October 2026
Abstract
For every ﬁxed compact convex body K ⊂ Rn with nonempty interior and every real poly-
nomialf , the normalized Lebesgue sum-of-squares density bound of degree at most 2r has error
OK,f(r−2) for every integer r ≥ 1. We give a self-contained proof and an explicit constant, using
one global square. A quadratic Taylor upper estimator reduces the objective to a scalar endpoint
problem. Homothetic contraction compares every sublevel shell with the same core mass that
normalizes a ﬁxed power of a Dirichlet polynomial; that mass cancels before summation. This
proves the quadratic upper rate in the convex-body/Lebesgue regime of the 2018 Oberwolfach
question. A classical interval example shows that the exponent is sharp in the worst case. The
proxy and the broad localization and relative-mass mechanisms have established precedents; no
ﬁrstness or new-method claim is made.

Laurent’s contribution, joint work with de Klerk, to the 2018 Oberwolfach report asks for the
convergence regime for general convex bodies with Lebesgue measure [ 1]. Its displayed density
constraints (3)–(4) omit the value of the integral. We explicitly use unit mass, as required by
its probability formulation and by the full hierarchy deﬁnition ( 1) in Slot–Laurent [ 2, Eq. (3)].
Thus our statement concerns the normalized problem, rather than a literal unnormalized inﬁmum.
Slot–Laurent’s Theorem 11 gives O(log2r/r 2) for this convex-body/Lebesgue hierarchy, and their
concluding questions ask whether the logarithm can be removed. Theorem 1 supplies the quadratic
upper rate in that precise setting.
The constrained-gradient and quadratic-upper-estimator reductions are already present in de
Klerk–Laurent [3, preprint Cor. 4.3] and Slot–Laurent [ 2, Lemmas 1 and 4]. Endpoint polynomial
concentration is classical. In particular, Totik–Varga [ 4, Thm. 2.1], with endpoint parameter a = 0
and ψ(v) = √v, supplies degree- m polynomials equal to 1 at zero, bounded by 2 on [0, 1], and
bounded by A exp[−c(m2t)1/ 4] for t > 0, with A,c > 0 independent of m. Markov’s inequality
gives a positive core of width proportional to m−2; the relative-mass estimate below then gives
another deduction of our theorem from those older ingredients. Totik–Varga also compare shell
masses with local core mass in their Christoﬀel-function arguments. We assign no originality to
the proxy, endpoint localization, or broad shell/core cancellation mechanism.
Our proof instead uses an explicit ﬁnite trigonometric identity and imports no approximation-
theory convergence theorem. The accompanying source audit distinguishes a deduction assembled
here from an earlier explicitly printed resolution. Its bounded search found no such full-target
theorem in the inspected passages; this does not establish ﬁrstness, historical disclosure, or current
worldwide openness. The weaker rate recorded in the ﬁnal 2026 overview [ 5, Table 1] is evidence
about that source’s contents, not an openness certiﬁcate.

4 Scope, supporting material, and review status
The reference measure in Theorem 1 is Lebesgue measure. The homothety identity is not asserted
for arbitrary reference measures. The construction also uses a quadratic proxy g, rather than
densities constrained to be polynomials of the original objective f ; it therefore does not settle the
same rate for that more restrictive hierarchy for general nonconvex f . The proof extends to f of
class C2 on an open neighborhood of K, since the same Taylor bound applies and the density still
uses only the quadratic proxy.
The accompanying veriﬁcation package contains proof and source audits, independent deriva-
tions and adversarial reviews, exact arithmetic consistency checks, and an exact moment-matrix
formulation at the nonsimple apex of a square pyramid. These computations and ﬁnite checks sup-
plement the analytic proof; they do not certify its universal quantiﬁers. Source retrieval, passages
actually read, and bounded literature-search limitations are recorded separately.
AI tools were used extensively in developing the solution, derivations, source checks, veriﬁcation,
drafting, and adversarial reviews. Independent reviewing agents were AI systems, not human peer
reviewers. This research note is unrefereed; it has not received external human peer review and is
