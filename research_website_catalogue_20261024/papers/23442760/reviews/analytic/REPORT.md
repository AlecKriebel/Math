For every fixed n>=1, compact convex K in R^n with nonempty interior, and real polynomial f, define H_r as the infimum of integral_K f q over global SOS polynomials whose square-root total degrees are <=r and whose Lebesgue integral is one. For any global minimizer a, let H=max_K ||Hess f||op, gamma=1+H/2, ell(x)=grad f(a).(x-a), g(x)=ell(x)+gamma|x-a|^2, G=max_K g, L=n+2, and C_n=4L^2(pi/2)^(2L)(1+2^(n+2)). The claim is 0<=H_r-f(a)<=C_n G/r^2 for every integer r>=1, witnessed by one normalized global square. G is positive and finite even for constant f because of the added quadratic. The bound is deliberately coarse; it is not an asymptotic equality or a constant uniform over n,K,f.

The separate lower proposition says H_r(t,[0,1])>=1/[4(r+1)^2] and the same for t on [0,1] times [-1,1]^(n-1). Combined with the upper theorem this gives one fixed Theta(r^-2) instance in each dimension, not a positive lower rate for each objective.

## Complete universal proof analysis

**Constrained Taylor estimator.** For x in K, a+s(x-a) lies in K for s in [0,1]. Since s=0 minimizes f on this segment, its right derivative gives ell(x)>=0. The integral Taylor remainder is at most H|x-a|^2/2. Consequently 0<=f(x)-f(a)<=g(x). The positive gamma quadratic gives g(x)>0 for x!=a. Compactness bounds its maximum; full dimension guarantees another point and hence G>0. This applies at any nonsmooth boundary minimizer and does not assume f convex or grad f(a)=0.

**Relative volume, with the actual quantifiers.** Set u=g/G and S_t={x in K:u(x)<=t}, F(t)=vol_n(S_t). The identity g(a+alpha(x-a))=alpha ell(x)+alpha^2 gamma|x-a|^2<=alpha g(x) for 0<=alpha<=1 shows that the injective homothety of ratio s/t takes S_t inside S_s whenever 0<s<=t. Its Jacobian is (s/t)^n. Thus F(t)<=(t/s)^n F(s), including t>1. In particular F(h)>=h^n vol(K)>0 for 0<h<=1. No coarea formula, density of the distribution of u, differentiability of F, regular boundary, or positive density at u=0 is assumed. All mass remains in the actual body K.

**Endpoint polynomial.** For integer m>=1 and N=2m+1, the degree-m polynomial A_m(t)=(1+2 sum_(j=1)^m T_j(1-2t))/N has A_m(0)=1. At v=arcsin sqrt(t), the finite geometric sum gives A_m(t)=sin(Nv)/(N sqrt(t)). The cosine sum and sine numerator yield |A_m(t)|<=min(1,1/(N sqrt(t))) for 0<t<=1. With h=N^-2, convexity of arcsin gives Nv<=pi/2 for 0<t<=h. Concavity of sine on [0,pi/2] and v>=sqrt(t) imply A_m(t)>=2/pi there. The endpoint zero follows directly from A_m(0)=1. At t=1 the formula gives (-1)^m/N, with no singularity.

**Same-core cancellation.** Put Q_m=A_m(u)^(2L). This is the square of a real polynomial globally, including outside K where u need not lie in [0,1]. Its denominator Z_m is at least (2/pi)^(2L)F(h)>0. Its numerator integral_K u Q_m has core contribution <=hF(h). On each disjoint shell 2^j h<u<=2^(j+1)h the contribution is at most

    2^(j+1) h * 2^(-jL) * F(2^(j+1)h)
    <= h F(h) 2^(n+1) 2^(j(n+1-L)).

For L=n+2 the geometric series has sum two. There are only finitely many nonempty shells, so extending their nonnegative bounds is valid. Hence integral uQ_m<=hF(h)(1+2^(n+2)). Dividing by Z_m cancels the same F(h), obtaining (pi/2)^(2L)(1+2^(n+2))/N^2. Tiny core volume, kernel oscillation, zeros away from the core, and the outermost shell crossing t=1 cause no gap. Replacing L by n+1 would destroy this summability argument; it is not silently assumed.

**Degree, normalization, every r.** For r>=2L choose m=floor(r/(2L)). Since u is quadratic, deg(A_m(u)^L)<=2mL<=r; Q_m has degree <=2r. Dividing by the positive Z_m is still one global square, with square root A_m(u)^L/sqrt(Z_m). The objective error is at most G times the ratio above. For b=r/(2L)>=1, 2floor(b)+1>=b, so N^-2<=4L^2/r^2. For 1<=r<2L the constant density 1/vol(K) has error <=G, and C_n/r^2>=1. Thus the literal theorem holds at all claimed integer orders, including the transitions. The SOS degree convention is sound: if a sum of polynomial squares had a component degree above r, its top homogeneous squares could not cancel identically over the reals.

**Lower proposition.** The shifted Legendre polynomials with factors sqrt(2j+1) form an orthonormal basis on [0,1]. Rodrigues orthogonality/norm and the finite integral representation establish |P_j|<=1 on [-1,1]. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives p(t)^2<=(r+1)^2 integral p^2 for every p of degree <=r. Summing over a unit-mass SOS density yields 0<=q<=(r+1)^2=M. With alpha=1/(2M), at most half the mass lies below alpha, hence integral tq>=alpha/2=1/(4M). On the product, apply this polynomial inequality at each fixed y, then sum and integrate over y; the t marginal has unit mass and the same cap M. No factor vol([-1,1]^(n-1)) is missing. This proves the precise lower bound independently of a spectral asymptotic citation.

## Boundary and scope audit

The proof covers n=1, r=1, r=2L-1, r=2L, arbitrary nonunique minimizers, minimizers at corners, interior minimizers with ell=0, H=0, and constant objectives. Constant f gives error zero even though this construction uses a positive auxiliary G. Arbitrarily thin fixed convex bodies are allowed; the argument does not promise a useful uniform constant as the body degenerates. The measure-zero singleton u=0 needs no positive mass. No boundary smoothness or semialgebraicity is used. All shell estimates apply to the full actual body, without local affine cube/simplex assumptions.

Lower-dimensional sets are excluded because ambient Lebesgue normalization can vanish; noncompact sets are excluded; nonconvex sets need not obey the segment/homothety argument. The displayed volume scaling is not asserted for arbitrary reference measures. The witness is a polynomial of the quadratic proxy, not generally a polynomial of f, so this is not a proof for the restrictive original-objective pushforward hierarchy. Locating a and computing integrals over arbitrary K are not claimed efficient. There is no exact optimal asymptotic coefficient, optimal dimension dependence, or universal positive per-objective lower rate. Extension to C^2 objectives on an open neighborhood of K follows from the identical bounded-Hessian Taylor proof, while the density remains polynomial through g.
