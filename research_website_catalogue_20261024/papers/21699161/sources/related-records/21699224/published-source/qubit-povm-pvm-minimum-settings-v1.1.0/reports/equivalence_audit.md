# Equivalence and scope audit

## Audited statement

For every fixed finite output architecture with two inputs per party, the
paper proves
\[
\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{POVM}}_2(2,2)
=
\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{PVM}}_2(2,2).
\]

Here the subscript \(2\) is a **branchwise local Hilbert-space bound**:
every quantum system in every component has local dimension at most two.
Both sets are convexified by arbitrary shared classical randomness. On the PVM
side, a projective measurement may contain zero projectors and may be followed
by arbitrary input-dependent stochastic relabeling or coarse-graining.
Classical local randomness is free. No quantum ancilla may enlarge a branch
beyond dimension two.

This is a behavior-level equivalence. It says that the same finite table
\(p(a,b\mid x,y)\) can be generated by some convex mixture of fixed-qubit PVM
strategies. It does **not** say that the original POVM effects can be replaced,
operator by operator, by PVM effects on the same state.

## What is held fixed and what may change

| Object | Must be preserved by the PVM realization? | Comment |
|---|---:|---|
| Declared input labels | Yes | Two per party. |
| Declared output labels | Yes | Zero projectors and stochastic postprocessing allow unused or split labels. |
| Full conditional behavior \(p(a,b\mid x,y)\) | Yes | Equality is entrywise for the entire behavior table. |
| Local Hilbert-space dimension bound | Yes | Every quantum branch remains at most two dimensional locally. |
| Original bipartite density operator | No | Different shared-randomness components may use different two-qubit states. |
| Original POVM effects | No | No measurement-level operator identity is asserted. |
| Purity or entanglement of the original state | No | The simulating convex decomposition may have different pure, mixed, entangled, or product components. |
| Number of shared-randomness values | No fixed number in the theorem statement | Finite support follows from finite-dimensional convexity/Carathéodory. |
| Larger Naimark space | Forbidden | A dilation that turns a ternary qubit POVM into a qutrit PVM is outside the comparator. |

The distinction in the state row is essential. A particularly strong
same-state simulation statement would contradict known measurement-level
non-simulability and is neither proved nor needed.

## Why genuine POVMs are not made projectively simulable

A measurement \(\mathsf M=(M_a)_a\) is projectively simulable in the usual
measurement-resource sense if there is a random choice of PVM and a classical
postprocessing such that
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho M_a)
\]
is reproduced for **every** state \(\rho\). Equivalently, the effects satisfy
an operator-level convex decomposition. Oszmaniec et al. prove that on a qubit
the resulting set is the convex hull of measurements with at most two nonzero
outcomes. An extremal trine or tetrahedral POVM lies outside that hull.

The new theorem asks a weaker local question but a stronger global one. It
fixes only the finitely many probabilities generated jointly by:

- one shared bipartite state;
- two measurement choices on Alice;
- two measurement choices on Bob.

It then allows a convex decomposition into complete PVM strategies. In a
component indexed by the shared variable \(\lambda\), the state
\(\rho_\lambda\), both of Alice’s PVMs, and both of Bob’s PVMs may all differ.
There is no requirement that any one component, or their local average as an
operator tuple, reproduce an original genuine POVM on arbitrary test states.

Thus the two statements can consistently coexist:

1. a ternary qubit POVM is not PM-simulable as a measurement; and
2. every standard two-input-per-party Bell behavior containing that POVM is
   PVM-realizable after convexifying whole strategies.

## Role of shared randomness

Shared randomness is not cosmetic in the set equality.

- For maximizing one linear Bell functional, shared randomness never raises
  the optimum: a convex mixture cannot exceed its best component.
- For reproducing an arbitrary interior behavior, a convex mixture may be
  essential. The theorem equates convex behavior sets, not necessarily the
  raw nonconvex sets of unrandomized strategies.
- The shared variable may select a different state and a different complete
  measurement family. It is not limited to one bit or to a dimension-two
  classical register.
- Local private randomness and stochastic output postprocessing can be folded
  into the shared variable by pre-sampling all counterfactual local choices.

This is why “unlimited shared classical randomness” must appear in the formal
theorem statement. It also explains why fixed-resource or randomness-free
measurement-certification results are not counterexamples.

## State optimization and convexification

The equality includes arbitrary mixed two-qubit states. For a single Bell
functional, linearity allows the state optimizer to be pure. For the behavior
set, a mixed state can be decomposed into pure states while retaining the
measurements, and its classical mixture is already included. These facts do
not authorize fixing the state across the PVM simulation.

For the exact \(3\times2\) separator:

- the simple lower strategy fixes the maximally entangled state;
- the strengthened lower strategy uses a specified partially entangled pure
  state;
- the PVM upper certificate ranges over all fixed-qubit PVM strategies and all
  two-qubit states;
- shared randomness cannot beat that upper bound, because every component
  obeys it;
- neither displayed lower strategy is proved globally POVM-optimal, and the
  PVM upper bound is not proved tight.

## PVM conventions and edge cases

The comparator must include all of the following.

1. **Zero effects.** A three-label qubit PVM can have rank pattern
   \((1,1,0)\) and permutations, or \((2,0,0)\) and permutations.
2. **Deterministic measurements.** The rank-two identity projector with all
   other effects zero is a valid PVM.
3. **Stochastic postprocessing.** Outcomes may be relabeled, coarse-grained,
   or probabilistically split without changing the quantum dimension.
4. **Input-dependent classical maps.** Each input can have its own
   postprocessing.
5. **Branchwise dimension.** Convex mixing does not permit a direct-sum quantum
   encoding whose local dimension grows with the number of branches.

The 2018 Schwarz et al. corrigendum is a concrete warning: omitting zero effects
from a numerical PVM comparison led to a false genuine-POVM claim. The present
exact \(3\times2\) verifier exhausts the six rank partitions of a
three-label qubit PVM, and the closure proof treats deterministic/zero
strata explicitly.

## Consequences that do follow

For the standard convex fixed-qubit Bell model:

- no linear Bell functional can separate fixed-qubit POVM and PVM behaviors
  when both parties have two inputs, regardless of finite output counts;
- every nonlinear witness whose value depends only on the behavior also has
  the same attainable range on the two equal sets;
- any device-independent certification of a genuine qubit POVM against this
  PVM comparator needs an assumption or resource outside the theorem, such as
  a third input on one side, fixed-state constraints, restricted randomness,
  sequential structure, network independence, or a different operational
  task;
- since one-input-on-either-side Bell scenarios are local and the exact
  \(3\times2\) separator exists, \(3\times2\) is the minimum input architecture
  up to exchange of the parties.

## Consequences that do not follow

The paper should explicitly disclaim the following statements.

- Individual qubit POVMs are mixtures/postprocessings of qubit PVMs.
- A POVM strategy has a PVM realization on the same state.
- A POVM strategy has an unrandomized PVM realization.
- POVMs never outperform PVMs for state discrimination, tomography,
  metrology, prepare-and-measure tasks, steering, sequential measurements,
  network scenarios, or randomness certification.
- The equality holds with restricted or absent shared randomness.
- The equality holds for three inputs on either party.
- The equality holds in local dimension three or higher.
- The equality holds for multipartite Bell scenarios.
- The equality holds for infinite output alphabets without a separate
  topological argument.
- The equality persists when the instrument/post-measurement state, rather
  than only the POVM outcome probabilities, is operationally relevant.
- \(L_1\) or \(U\) is the exact global optimum of the \(3\times2\) functional.

## Recommended terminology

Use:

- “fixed-qubit POVM behavior”;
- “fixed-qubit projective-simulable behavior” or the explicitly defined
  \(\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{PVM}}_2\);
- “behavior-level equivalence”;
- “convexified by shared classical randomness”;
- “branchwise local dimension at most two”;
- “arbitrary finite output alphabets.”

Avoid the unqualified phrases:

- “POVMs equal PVMs”;
- “POVMs are projective on qubits”;
- “two POVMs are simulable”;
- “dimension-two quantum theory needs only PVMs.”

## Referee-facing scope test

A reader should be able to answer “yes” to every question below from the
theorem statement alone.

- Are there exactly two inputs per party?
- Are outputs arbitrary but finite?
- Is local quantum dimension bounded by two in every convex component?
- Are arbitrary mixed states and shared classical randomness included?
- Are zero projectors and stochastic output maps permitted on the PVM side?
- Is the equality between behavior sets rather than measurement sets?
- Are dimension-increasing dilations excluded?

If any answer is not explicit, the statement is vulnerable to an apparent
contradiction with established measurement-simulability or certification
literature.
