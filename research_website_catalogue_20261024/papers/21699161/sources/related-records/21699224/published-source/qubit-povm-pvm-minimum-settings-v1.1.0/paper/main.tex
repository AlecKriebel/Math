\begin{abstract}
V\'ertesi and Bene exhibited a \(3\times2\) Bell scenario in which qubit
positive-operator-valued measurements (POVMs) outperform qubit
projection-valued measurements (PVMs). We prove the matching setting lower
bound. For arbitrary finite, input-dependent output alphabets, the
shared-randomness convex hulls of bipartite qubit-POVM and qubit-PVM behaviors
are equal when both parties have two inputs. Equivalently, no two-input
linear Bell functional separates the two convexified sets. We also give a
rational \(3\times2\) functional, an exact qubit-POVM strategy, and a global
analytic qubit-PVM upper bound with a strict exact gap. Hence \(3\times2\) is
the minimum input architecture, up to exchanging the parties. The equality
proof combines a one-binary-party Lorentz-cone simulation, an extremal
binary--ternary reduction, a physically exact Lorentz incidence manifold,
finite-POVM duality, and a rank trichotomy based on the incidence second
variation. The result concerns convexified behavior sets; it does not assert
equality of the raw nonconvex strategy images or same-state simulation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{maintheorem}
For every finite input-dependent output architecture
\((A_0,A_1;B_0,B_1)\),
\[
\conv\Qset^{\POVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B)
=
\conv\Qset^{\PVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B).
\]
For the rational \(3\times2\) Bell functional in
\cref{eq:bell-functional},
\[
\beta_{\POVM}\ge
L_0:=20\sqrt2+\frac{16}{25}
>
U:=20\sqrt2+\frac35+\frac{4+3\sqrt2}{250}
\ge\beta_{\PVM}.
\]
Consequently, up to exchanging the parties, three inputs for one party and
two for the other are necessary and sufficient for a strict fixed-qubit
POVM--PVM separation by a linear Bell functional.
\end{maintheorem}

\noindent
Throughout, ``\(m\times n\)'' counts Bell inputs (measurement settings), not
measurement outcomes.

\section{Introduction}

A positive-operator-valued measurement (POVM) is more general than a
projection-valued measurement (PVM) on a fixed Hilbert space. Naimark dilation
represents a POVM projectively only after adding degrees of freedom, so it
does not preserve a fixed local dimension. Bell experiments therefore offer
an operational question: how many measurement inputs are needed before
generalized measurements on local qubits generate correlations unavailable
to projective qubit measurements? This belongs to the broader study of Bell
nonlocality and dimension-constrained quantum behavior
\cite{brunner2014,navascues2015}.

Gisin asked whether some Bell inequality and quantum state can require
nonprojective measurements for optimal violation, noting that binary outcomes
cannot furnish such an example \cite{gisin2007}. V\'ertesi and Bene answered
that existence question for two qubits using three inputs on Alice and two on Bob
\cite{vertesi2010}; subsequent work refined that family
\cite{barra2012}, and related device-independent constructions certify
nonprojective qubit measurements with larger input architectures
\cite{gomez2016}. These results do not settle the two-input behavior-set
question.

Two recent results approach nearby boundaries from different directions.
Cerf and Ollivier develop a second-order perturbative method for
\((n,2,d)\) Bell scenarios near deterministic correlations and propose,
without resolving, a route to distinguish raw fixed-dimensional POVM and PVM
correlation sets \cite{cerf2026}. Their proved qubit result concerns local
dichotomic geometry and neither establishes nor implies the global,
convexified arbitrary-output equality proved here. Panahi \emph{et al.},
using an intermediate reduction attributed to Masanes together with Schmidt
compression, show that when one party has two binary settings, every
bipartite convexly extremal quantum correlation admits a two-qubit
realization even if the other party has arbitrary settings and outcomes
\cite{panahi2025}. That is a Hilbert-space dimension theorem, not a
projectivization of the arbitrary-output measurements. The
one-binary-party theorem below instead supplies a common PVM mixture of the
complete fixed-qubit behavior.

To our knowledge, equality of the shared-randomness-convexified fixed-qubit
POVM and PVM behavior sets for all bipartite two-input architectures with
finite, input-dependent output alphabets, and the resulting \(3\times2\)
minimum-input classification, were not previously known.
It also differs from operator-level projective simulability of individual
POVMs \cite{oszmaniec2017,guerini2017}: a Bell behavior may admit a projective
realization using a different state even when the original local POVM is not
itself a mixture of PVMs.

Strong reductions are known when every measurement is dichotomic
\cite{masanes2005,toner2006}; genuine ternary effects are precisely what those
arguments do not address. They also make exhaustive PVM conventions
essential. A published ternary-measurement claim was later corrected after a
qubit PVM with a zero effect was found to attain the same value
\cite{schwarz2016,schwarz2018corrigendum}. We therefore retain zero
projectors, deterministic inputs, every support pattern, and stochastic
postprocessing throughout.

\subsection*{Contributions}

We work throughout with local Hilbert-space dimension at most two and with
shared-randomness convexification. Our results are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item An explicit rational \(3\times2\) Bell functional has an exact
  algebraic qubit-POVM strategy above a global analytic upper bound for all
  qubit-PVM strategies (\cref{thm:separation}).
\item If one party has binary outcomes on both of its two inputs, every qubit
  POVM behavior is a common convex mixture of complete qubit-PVM behaviors
  (\cref{thm:binary-party}).
\item Any remaining strict two-input separator reduces to a pure entangled
  strategy with one binary PVM and one ternary rank-one POVM per party
  (\cref{thm:residual-reduction}).
\item The residual architecture cannot strictly exceed the PVM support
  function (\cref{thm:two-input-equality}). The proof treats every smooth and
  singular rank of an exact Lorentz incidence model.
\item Consequently \(3\times2\) is the minimum input architecture, up to
  exchanging the parties (\cref{cor:minimality}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Proof roadmap}

The exact separation combines a CHSH locking term with a three-state
discrimination task. For the equality theorem, convex separation first turns
any counterexample into a global Bell maximizer. A Lorentz-cone circuit
argument handles every case in which one party is binary. A common-span
filtering perturbation and extremal-POVM dimension bound reduce all other
counterexamples to one binary and one ternary measurement per party.

In the residual architecture, the five local effects form a unique
binary-versus-ternary circuit. Lorentz coordinates describe the complete
strategy by a \(4\times4\) probability block \(P\) and a four-parameter
metric \(g\), subject to five quadratic null equations. Finite POVM duality
makes every determinant multiplier strictly positive at a hypothetical strict
separator. The constrained second variation then has ambient inertia
\((4,12)\). If the metric differential has rank at least two, dimension alone
forces an uphill direction. Rank one contradicts positivity through an exact
projective-fiber theorem, while rank zero has an explicit deterministic
decomposition.

\section{Model and convexified behavior sets}
\label{sec:model}

Let \(X=Y=\{0,1\}\) unless stated otherwise. Each input has a finite declared
output alphabet \(A_x\) or \(B_y\), which may depend on the input.

\begin{definition}[Fixed-qubit behavior]
A single fixed-qubit POVM strategy consists of Hilbert spaces
\(\mathcal H_A,\mathcal H_B\) of dimensions at most two, a density operator
\(\rho\) on \(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\), and effects
\[
 M^x_a\ge0,\quad \sum_{a\in A_x}M^x_a=\id_A,\qquad
 N^y_b\ge0,\quad \sum_{b\in B_y}N^y_b=\id_B.
\]
It produces
\[
 p(a,b|x,y)=\Tr\!\left[\rho(M^x_a\otimes N^y_b)\right].
\]
The resulting set is denoted
\(\Qset^{\POVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B)\).
\end{definition}

A PVM strategy requires all effects in each measurement to be mutually
orthogonal projections. Zero projections are permitted. Therefore a
qubit PVM with arbitrarily many declared labels has at most two nonzero
effects. No ancillary system or Naimark dilation may increase a local
dimension above two.

We denote the shared-randomness hulls by
\[
\Qbar^{\POVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B)
 :=\conv\Qset^{\POVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B),\qquad
\Qbar^{\PVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B)
 :=\conv\Qset^{\PVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B).
\]
Local stochastic postprocessing is included on the PVM side.

The raw sets \(\Qset^{\POVM}_2\) and \(\Qset^{\PVM}_2\) are strategy images
and need not be convex: a single strategy has no external shared-randomness
register. Their barred versions are convex hulls and do permit a common
classical variable selecting the state and all local measurements
simultaneously. For a Bell tensor \(c\), define the support values
\[
\beta_{\mathsf X}(c)
:=\max_{p\in\Qbar^{\mathsf X}_2}\langle c,p\rangle,
\qquad \mathsf X\in\{\POVM,\PVM\}.
\]
Linearity gives the same maximum over the corresponding raw set, but equality
of all support values determines only the convex hulls, not the raw sets.

\begin{lemma}[Convexification and postprocessing]
\label{lem:convexification}
For a linear Bell functional, shared randomness and stochastic
postprocessing do not increase the optimum beyond one deterministic
projective component.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Linearity gives
\[
\mathcal F\!\left(\sum_\lambda q_\lambda p_\lambda\right)
=\sum_\lambda q_\lambda\mathcal F(p_\lambda)
\le\max_\lambda\mathcal F(p_\lambda).
\]
A finite stochastic map is a convex combination of deterministic maps.
Absorb all deterministic choices into the shared variable. A deterministic
map merges mutually orthogonal projectors assigned to the same displayed
label and inserts zero projectors for unused labels, hence remains a declared-
output PVM.
\end{proof}

For fixed finite alphabets, all strategy spaces above are compact. Indeed,
density matrices and tuples of effects form closed bounded sets in
finite-dimensional matrix spaces, and the Born map is continuous. The convex
hull of a compact subset of a finite-dimensional affine space is compact:
affine dependence reduces every convex combination to at most \(m+1\) points
in affine dimension \(m\).

\begin{lemma}[Nearest-point separation]
\label{lem:nearest}
Let \(\cP\) be a compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean
space. If \(q\notin\cP\) and \(p_0\) minimizes \(\|q-p\|\) over \(p\in\cP\),
then \(c=q-p_0\) satisfies
\[
c\cdot q>\sup_{p\in\cP}c\cdot p.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(p\in\cP\), differentiate
\(\|q-p_0-t(p-p_0)\|^2\) at \(t=0^+\). Minimality gives
\((q-p_0)\cdot(p-p_0)\le0\), and strictness follows from \(q\ne p_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Convex hulls and Bell support functions]
\label{prop:support-functions}
For fixed finite alphabets,
\[
\Qbar^{\POVM}_2=\Qbar^{\PVM}_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta_{\POVM}(c)=\beta_{\PVM}(c)
\ \text{for every real Bell tensor }c.
\]
This equivalence makes no claim that
\(\Qset^{\POVM}_2=\Qset^{\PVM}_2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equality of the convex sets implies equality of their maxima under every
linear functional. Conversely, if the compact convex sets differed, a point
in one but not the other would, by \cref{lem:nearest}, yield a linear
functional with unequal maxima. Finally, a linear functional has the same
maximum on a compact set and on its convex hull.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Notation for arbitrary outputs]
The shorthand
\(\Qbar^{\POVM}_2(2,2)=\Qbar^{\PVM}_2(2,2)\) means an equality schema:
the equality holds separately for every choice of finite
\((A_0,A_1;B_0,B_1)\). It is not an identification of behavior vectors
living in different ambient spaces.
\end{remark}

\section[An exact rational 3-by-2 separation]
{An exact rational \(3\times2\) separation}
\label{sec:separation}

For this section take Alice inputs \(x=0,1,2\), Bob inputs \(y=0,1\), Alice
labels \(a=0,1,2\), and Bob labels \(b=0,1\). Define signs
\[
\alpha_0=\alpha_2=1,\quad\alpha_1=-1,\qquad
\beta_0=1,\quad\beta_1=-1
\]
and correlators
\[
E_{xy}=\sum_{a,b}\alpha_a\beta_b\,p(a,b|x,y).
\]
For \(x=0,1\), Alice's labels \(0\) and \(2\) have identical coefficients
and may be deterministically coarse-grained. Thus the common three-label
notation is equivalent to the heterogeneous architecture
\((2,2,3)\)-by-\((2,2)\).
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bell-functional}
\begin{aligned}
\Bell(p)={}&10(E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11})\\
&+\frac35p(0,0|2,0)+\frac35p(1,1|2,0)
 +\frac45p(2,0|2,1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For this declared-output architecture, write
\[
\beta_{\POVM}(\Bell)
:=\sup_{p\in\Qbar^{\POVM}_2}\Bell(p),\qquad
\beta_{\PVM}(\Bell)
:=\sup_{p\in\Qbar^{\PVM}_2}\Bell(p).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-qubit separation]
\label{thm:separation}
For the rational functional \eqref{eq:bell-functional},
\[
\beta_{\POVM}(\Bell)\ge
L_0:=20\sqrt2+\frac{16}{25}
>
U:=20\sqrt2+\frac35+\frac{4+3\sqrt2}{250}
\ge\beta_{\PVM}(\Bell).
\]
The certified gap is
\[
L_0-U=\frac{3(2-\sqrt2)}{250}>0.
\]
These are an attained POVM lower bound and a global PVM upper bound; neither
is asserted to be the corresponding exact optimum.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The simple exact strategy}

Let \(X,Z\) be Pauli matrices and
\[
|\Phi^+\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
Bob uses \(B_0=Z\), \(B_1=X\). Alice uses
\[
A_0=(Z+X)/\sqrt2,\qquad A_1=(Z-X)/\sqrt2
\]
for her first two binary measurements, with a zero third label. Her final
measurement is
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_0&=(17\id-8X+15Z)/50,\\
M_1&=(17\id-8X-15Z)/50,\\
M_2&=(16\id+16X)/50.
\end{aligned}
\]
The rank-one factorizations
\[
M_0=\frac1{25}\binom4{-1}(4,-1),\quad
M_1=\frac1{25}\binom1{-4}(1,-4),\quad
M_2=\frac8{25}\binom11(1,1)
\]
prove positivity. Direct addition of the displayed matrices gives
\[
M_0+M_1+M_2=\id,
\]
which proves normalization separately. Their nonzero eigenvalues are
\(17/25,17/25,16/25\), so the measurement is not a PVM.

The identity
\[
\langle\Phi^+|R\otimes S|\Phi^+\rangle
=\frac12\Tr(R^TS)
\]
gives CHSH value \(2\sqrt2\) and
\[
p(0,0|2,0)=p(1,1|2,0)=p(2,0|2,1)=\frac8{25}.
\]
Thus the simpler value
\[
L_0=20\sqrt2+\frac{16}{25}
\]
already exceeds \(U\), since
\[
L_0-U=\frac{3(2-\sqrt2)}{250}>0.
\]

At the ideal point the auxiliary term is a state-discrimination problem with
\[
W_0=\frac3{20}(\id+Z),\quad
W_1=\frac3{20}(\id-Z),\quad
W_2=\frac15(\id+X).
\]
The dual operator
\[
\Gamma=\frac8{25}\id+\frac2{25}X
\]
satisfies \(\Gamma\ge W_i\) and
\((\Gamma-W_i)M_i=0\), so the POVM value is \(16/25\).
Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma-W_0&=\frac1{50}\binom14(1,4),&
\Gamma-W_1&=\frac1{50}\binom41(4,1),\\
\Gamma-W_2&=\frac3{25}\binom1{-1}(1,-1),
\end{aligned}
\]
so dual feasibility is positive-semidefinite by inspection.
Every one- or two-label qubit PVM support scores at most \(3/5\). The ideal
discrimination advantage is therefore \(1/25\).

\subsection{Global projective bound}

Write \(\Bell=10S+T\). For a PVM strategy, the displayed CHSH observables are
Hermitian unitaries, including possible values \(\pm\id\). If one is
degenerate, the CHSH identity
\[
C_{\rm CHSH}^2
=4\id-[A_0,A_1]\otimes[B_0,B_1]
\]
gives \(S\le2\), while \(T\le7/5\), which is below \(U\).

Otherwise optimize the state to a pure state and put it in Schmidt form
\[
|\psi_\eta\rangle=
\sqrt{\frac{1+\eta}{2}}|00\rangle+
\sqrt{\frac{1-\eta}{2}}|11\rangle,\qquad0\le\eta\le1.
\]
Its correlation matrix is
\[
R=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{1-\eta^2},-\sqrt{1-\eta^2},1).
\]
If \(u=|\bm b_0\cdot\bm b_1|\) and
\(\delta=2\sqrt2-S\), optimizing Alice's directions first gives
\[
S\le
\lVert R(\bm b_0+\bm b_1)\rVert+
\lVert R(\bm b_0-\bm b_1)\rVert.
\]
The normalized sum and difference directions are orthogonal. Since the two
largest eigenvalues of \(R^TR\) sum to \(2-\eta^2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
S\le2\sqrt{2-\eta^2}
\le2\sqrt2-\frac{\eta^2}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The last comparison follows by squaring two nonnegative sides; their squared
difference is \(\eta^4/2\). Independently, \(R\) is a contraction, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
S&\le\sqrt{2+2u}+\sqrt{2-2u}\\
&=\sqrt{4+4\sqrt{1-u^2}}
\le\sqrt{8-2u^2}
\le2\sqrt2-\frac{u^2}{2\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sqrt{1-u^2}\le1-u^2/2\) and
\(\sqrt{4-u^2}\le2-u^2/4\); the exact squared differences and their
nonnegative right sides are recorded in
\cref{app:separation-algebra}. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deficit-bounds}
S\le2\sqrt2-\frac{\eta^2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
S\le2\sqrt2-\frac{u^2}{2\sqrt2},
\end{equation}
hence
\[
\eta\le2^{1/4}\sqrt\delta,\qquad
u\le2^{3/4}\sqrt\delta.
\]

A ternary qubit PVM has one of the six labeled rank patterns obtained by
permuting \((2,0,0)\) or \((1,1,0)\). For every singleton support and the
pair \(\{0,1\}\), \(T\le3/5+2(\eta+u)/5\). A pair involving label \(2\)
reduces to binary discrimination between
\[
\Pi(\bm v):=\frac12(\id+\bm v\cdot\bm\sigma),\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\sqrt{\frac{1+\eta}{2}},\sqrt{\frac{1-\eta}{2}}\right).
\]
Writing \(\widetilde{\bm b}_y\) for the reflection of \(\bm b_y\) induced by
matrix transposition, the three score operators are
\[
K_0=\frac35D\Pi(\widetilde{\bm b}_0)D,\quad
K_1=\frac35D\Pi(-\widetilde{\bm b}_0)D,\quad
K_2=\frac45D\Pi(\widetilde{\bm b}_1)D.
\]
The reflection preserves \(z\)-coordinates, dot products, and \(u\). In a
pair involving label \(2\), write the two selected score operators as
\[
G=\frac35D \Pi(\bm n)D,\qquad
H=\frac45D \Pi(\bm m)D.
\]
With \(x=n_z\), \(y=m_z\), and \(r=\bm n\cdot\bm m\),
\[
20F_\eta
=7+\eta(3x+4y)
+\sqrt{[-1+\eta(3x-4y)]^2+24(1-\eta^2)(1-r)}.
\]
The exact comparison
\[
F_\eta\le F_0(r)+\frac{2\eta}{5}
\le\frac35+\frac25(\eta+u)
\]
is proved by the manifestly nonnegative polynomial certificate in
\cref{app:separation-algebra}. Consequently
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Nonzero labels & Rank pattern & Bound used for \(T\)\\
\midrule
\(\{0\}\) & \((2,0,0)\) & \(\Tr K_0\le3(1+\eta)/10\)\\
\(\{1\}\) & \((0,2,0)\) & \(\Tr K_1\le3(1+\eta)/10\)\\
\(\{2\}\) & \((0,0,2)\) & \(\Tr K_2\le2(1+\eta)/5\)\\
\(\{0,1\}\) & \((1,1,0)\) & \(\Tr(K_0+K_1)\le3/5\)\\
\(\{0,2\}\) & \((1,0,1)\) & \(F_\eta\le3/5+2(\eta+u)/5\)\\
\(\{1,2\}\) & \((0,1,1)\) & \(F_\eta\le3/5+2(\eta+u)/5\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each of the first four displayed estimates is no larger than the common
bound \(3/5+2(\eta+u)/5\). Therefore
\[
T\le\frac35+\frac25(\eta+u)
\le\frac35+\kappa\sqrt\delta,
\quad
\kappa=\frac25(2^{1/4}+2^{3/4}),
\]
where \(\kappa^2=(16+12\sqrt2)/25\). Completing the square,
\[
\Bell\le20\sqrt2+\frac35+\kappa\sqrt\delta-10\delta
\le20\sqrt2+\frac35+\frac{\kappa^2}{40}=U.
\]
This covers every state, support pattern, direction, relabeling, and
degeneracy. \Cref{lem:convexification} covers shared randomness and
postprocessing. A stronger algebraic strategy and its exact gap comparison
are given separately in \cref{app:strengthened}.

\section{Two-input reductions}
\label{sec:reductions}

\subsection{One binary party}

\begin{lemma}[Circuits of a three-dimensional pointed cone]
\label{lem:lorentz-circuit}
Let \(K\) be a pointed convex cone of real dimension at most three. Consider
a support-minimal equality between positive sums of nonzero extreme-ray
vectors of \(K\), after canceling any ray that occurs on both sides. Positive
coefficients may be absorbed into the ray vectors. Then either the equality
is a one-versus-one cancellation on the same ray, or \(K\) has dimension
three and the relation has the form
\[
r_0+r_1=s_0+s_1
\]
with four distinct rays. In particular, every nontrivial circuit is
two-versus-two.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Move the two sums to opposite sides and attach the corresponding positive and
negative coefficients. Support minimality makes this a minimally supported
linear dependence. Indeed, if a proper subset admitted any nonzero
dependence, add a sufficiently small signed multiple of it to the original
coefficient vector and vary the multiple until one coefficient first
vanishes; all original signs remain weakly unchanged, yielding a smaller
positive relation after cancellation. A minimally dependent set in
dimension \(d\) has at most \(d+1\) vectors, so at most four rays occur.

Pointedness excludes a dependence with all signs equal. If one side contained
a single extreme ray and the other side contained at least two different
rays, division by its positive coefficient would express that extreme ray as
a nontrivial positive sum of other cone rays, a contradiction. Thus a
nontrivial relation contains at least two rays on each side and at most four
total, forcing \(d=3\) and the displayed two-versus-two form. A pointed cone
of dimension one or two has at most two extreme rays, so only
one-versus-one, same-ray cancellations remain there. A zero vector is not an
extreme ray and is simply omitted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One-binary-party simulation]
\label{thm:binary-party}
Suppose one party in a two-input bipartite qubit scenario has binary declared
outputs on both inputs. The other party may have arbitrary finite outputs.
Then every qubit-POVM behavior belongs to the convex hull of fixed-qubit PVM
behaviors. The statement is symmetric under exchanging the parties.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose each binary qubit POVM into PVMs. If \(E\) has eigenvalues
\(0\le\mu\le\lambda\le1\) and \(P\) is the spectral projector for \(\lambda\),
then
\[
\{E,\id-E\}=(\lambda-\mu)\{P,\id-P\}
+\mu\{\id,0\}+(1-\lambda)\{0,\id\}.
\]
Taking the product of the decompositions for both inputs reduces, in each
shared-random component, Bob to fixed binary PVMs with observables \(B_0,B_1\).

For Alice outcome \(a|x\), let \(\sigma_{a|x}\) be Bob's subnormalized steered
state and define
\[
\Phi_B(\sigma)=
\bigl(\Tr\sigma,\Tr(\sigma B_0),\Tr(\sigma B_1)\bigr)\in\R^3.
\]
These data determine all joint probabilities:
\[
p(a,b|x,y)=\frac12\left[
(\Phi_B\sigma_{a|x})_0+(-1)^b(\Phi_B\sigma_{a|x})_{y+1}
\right].
\]
No-signaling gives equality of the sums of the two compressed ensembles.

If \(\id,B_0,B_1\) are linearly independent, the image of the positive
qubit cone is a three-dimensional Lorentz cone: the cone over the elliptical
projection of the Bloch ball onto the span of the two Bloch axes. Parallel,
antiparallel, or scalar observables give a two-dimensional simplicial cone or
a local deterministic degeneration; these cases are included in
\cref{app:binary-degeneracies}.

Decompose every compressed positive vector into extreme rays, retaining its
original output label. Repeatedly extract a support-minimal positive
subrelation and subtract the largest multiple that leaves every residual
coefficient nonnegative. At least one coefficient vanishes at each step, so
this circuit elimination terminates. By \cref{lem:lorentz-circuit}, every
nontrivial circuit is
\[
r_0+r_1=s_0+s_1.
\]
The original positive coefficients have been absorbed into the displayed ray
vectors. One-versus-one, repeated-ray, zero-ray, and lower-dimensional cases
produce only the degenerate components described below.

Every extreme compressed ray has a unique positive rank-one lift inside
\(\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{\id,B_0,B_1\}\). Thus the circuit lifts to
\[
R_0+R_1=S_0+S_1=\Omega
\]
between rank-one positive operators. If \(\Omega\) is invertible, the two
rank-one decompositions become two orthonormal bases after applying
\(\Omega^{-1/2}\). Define Alice's two binary PVMs by
\[
P_i^{(0)}=
\left(\Omega^{-1/2}R_i\Omega^{-1/2}\right)^T,\qquad
P_i^{(1)}=
\left(\Omega^{-1/2}S_i\Omega^{-1/2}\right)^T
\quad(i=0,1).
\]
Under the vectorization convention used here, the normalized canonical
purification
\[
|\Psi_\Omega\rangle=
(\id\otimes\sqrt{\Omega/\Tr\Omega})(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)
\]
obeys
\[
\Tr_A\!\left[(P_i^{(0)}\otimes\id)
|\Psi_\Omega\rangle\langle\Psi_\Omega|\right]
=\frac{R_i}{\Tr\Omega},\qquad
\Tr_A\!\left[(P_i^{(1)}\otimes\id)
|\Psi_\Omega\rangle\langle\Psi_\Omega|\right]
=\frac{S_i}{\Tr\Omega}.
\]
The transpose is required because
\(\Tr_A[(P\otimes\id)|\Omega_2\rangle\langle\Omega_2|]=P^T\) for
\(|\Omega_2\rangle=|00\rangle+|11\rangle\).

Rank-one \(\Omega\), zero terms, and repeated rays require separate
bookkeeping. If
\(\Omega=|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|\) has rank one, every nonzero summand
is collinear with \(\Omega\), so
\[
R_i=\rho_i\Omega,\quad S_i=\sigma_i\Omega,\qquad
\rho_0+\rho_1=\sigma_0+\sigma_1=1.
\]
Put \(|\widehat\omega\rangle=|\omega\rangle/\sqrt{\Tr\Omega}\) and use the
product state \(|0\rangle\otimes|\widehat\omega\rangle\). For input \(0\),
take the first projector onto
\(\sqrt{\rho_0}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\rho_1}|1\rangle\); for input \(1\), take the
first projector onto
\(\sqrt{\sigma_0}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\sigma_1}|1\rangle\). The complementary
projectors give the second outcomes. This realizes both arbitrary binary
weight vectors. Zero terms
receive zero projectors. Collinear terms are combined only within their
retained output labels; if the same geometric ray occurs under different
labels, classical splitting restores those labels branchwise. Thus no
outcome probability is lost when repeated rays are removed.

Give this circuit strategy shared weight \(\Tr\Omega\). Circuit elimination
preserves total trace, so the weights sum to one. The retained output labels
and fixed Bob PVMs reconstruct every original compressed vector and hence
every joint probability. The shared variable records the binary-POVM
decomposition choices, the circuit component, and any repeated-label split.
Conditional on that single variable it fixes a state, both Alice PVMs, and
both Bob PVMs simultaneously. This is one common decomposition of the
complete behavior, not a separate decomposition for each input.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Extreme residual architecture}

\begin{lemma}[Extreme points of a compact hull]
\label{lem:extreme-hull}
If \(K\) is compact in a finite-dimensional real vector space, every extreme
point of \(\conv K\) belongs to \(K\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every point of \(\conv K\) is a finite convex combination of points of \(K\).
If an extreme point \(p\) had a representation containing two distinct
positive-weight points, separating one term from the normalized sum of the
others would express \(p\) as a nontrivial convex combination. Hence every
positive-weight point in the representation equals \(p\), and \(p\in K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Common-span filtering]
\label{lem:common-span}
Let Alice's two POVMs have a common Hermitian combination
\[
H=\sum_a s_{a|0}M_{a|0}=\sum_a s_{a|1}M_{a|1}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(\eps>0\), define
\[
S_\pm=\id\pm\eps H,\quad
r^\pm_{a|x}=1\pm\eps s_{a|x},
\]
\[
M^\pm_{a|x}
=r^\pm_{a|x}S_\pm^{-1/2}M_{a|x}S_\pm^{-1/2}.
\]
With
\[
q_\pm=\Tr[(S_\pm\otimes\id)\rho],\qquad
\rho_\pm=
\frac{(S_\pm^{1/2}\otimes\id)\rho(S_\pm^{1/2}\otimes\id)}
{q_\pm},
\]
the original complete behavior satisfies
\[
p=\frac{q_+}{2}p_++\frac{q_-}{2}p_-.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The effects normalize because
\(\sum_a r^\pm_{a|x}M_{a|x}=S_\pm\), and \(q_++q_-=2\).
Direct cyclicity of trace gives
\[
q_\pm p_\pm(a,b|x,y)=r^\pm_{a|x}p(a,b|x,y).
\]
Averaging the signs proves the identity for all inputs and outputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Residual-architecture reduction]
\label{thm:residual-reduction}
If a Bell functional with two inputs per party has a strict qubit-POVM advantage over
\(\Qbar^{\PVM}_2\), a global maximizing component may be chosen with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=2pt]
\item a pure full-Schmidt-rank two-qubit state;
\item one nondegenerate binary PVM on each party;
\item one extremal ternary rank-one POVM on each party;
\item local real measurement spans intersecting only in \(\R\id\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, four-outcome extremal POVMs are unnecessary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose an extreme point \(p\) of the exposed maximizing face. Because an
extreme point of a face is extreme in the whole convex set,
\cref{lem:extreme-hull} supplies one unrandomized raw strategy realizing
\(p\).

\emph{Pure state.} Decompose its state into finitely many pure states while
holding all measurements fixed. The resulting behaviors form a convex
decomposition of \(p\), so extremality forces every positive-weight behavior
to equal \(p\). A product component is local and cannot attain a strict value
above the PVM support; choose a pure entangled component. Its two reduced
states have full rank.

\emph{Extremal measurements.} For a fixed finite outcome alphabet, the POVM
set is compact and convex in a finite-dimensional affine space. An elementary
induction on the dimension of its smallest containing face shows that it is
the convex hull of its extreme points: if a point is not extreme, extend a
nontrivial line segment through it to the relative boundary in both
directions, then apply the induction to the two proper faces containing the
endpoints. Affine dependence reduces the resulting representation to a
finite one. Apply such a decomposition to one input at a time while fixing
the state and all other measurements. Again every positive-weight behavior
equals \(p\), so an extremal component may be retained for every input.
Temporarily delete zero effects; they contribute no probabilities and their
declared output labels can be reinserted at the end. Full-rank reduced states
make every remaining outcome marginal strictly positive.
For one party set
\[
V_x=\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{M_{a|x}\}\subset\Herm(2).
\]
If \(V_0\cap V_1\) contained a nonscalar \(H\), \cref{lem:common-span} would
split the behavior. Equality of both branch behaviors would imply, using
strictly positive outcome marginals,
\(r^\pm_{a|x}=q_\pm\) for every nonzero outcome, making all coefficients
equal and \(H\) scalar. Thus
\[
V_0\cap V_1=\R\id.
\]

Since \(\dim_{\R}\Herm(2)=4\), one of \(V_0,V_1\) has dimension at most two.
Every two-dimensional real operator system containing \(\id\) has the form
\(\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{\id,H\}\) for a nonscalar Hermitian \(H\).
All of its elements commute. If
\(H=h_+P_++h_-P_-\) is the spectral decomposition, every effect
\(M_a=\alpha_a\id+\beta_aH\) equals
\[
M_a=q(a|+)P_++q(a|-)P_-,
\]
where \(q(a|\pm)=\alpha_a+\beta_a h_\pm\ge0\) and
\(\sum_aq(a|\pm)=1\). Thus the POVM is a stochastic postprocessing of the
binary spectral PVM \(\{P_+,P_-\}\).

\emph{Deterministic postprocessing.} A finite stochastic map is a convex
combination of deterministic maps. It therefore decomposes \(p\) into
behaviors obtained from deterministic relabelings of the spectral PVM.
Extremality retains one component realizing \(p\); merging spectral
projections and adding zero labels is again a PVM. Apply
\cref{lem:common-span} once more to that realization, so its two final spans
still intersect only in \(\R\id\). The PVM is nondegenerate: if it were
deterministic, that party would have only one nontrivial input, and the
behavior would be local.

The other span has dimension at most three. Its nonzero effects are linearly
independent; otherwise \cref{lem:common-span} with \(H=0\) splits the extreme
behavior. One nonzero effect would be \(\id\), a deterministic PVM. An
extremal two-effect POVM is a PVM: diagonalizing one effect and perturbing
either eigenvalue strictly between \(0\) and \(1\) would decompose it, so its
eigenvalues are \(0\) or \(1\). A genuine nonprojective residual measurement
therefore has at least three nonzero effects, while linear independence in a
three-dimensional span gives at most three.

For an extremal qubit POVM, support-preserving perturbations give
\[
\sum_a\rank(M_a)^2\le4.
\]
The standard extremality criterion \cite{dariano2005,haapasalo2012} is
reproved in \cref{app:extremal-povm} for completeness. With three nonzero
effects, any rank-two effect would make the rank-square sum at least
\(4+1+1>4\); hence all three effects have rank one. If both spans on one party were
at most two, \cref{thm:binary-party} would make the full behavior
PVM-simulable. Apply the argument to both parties, then reinsert any deleted
zero-output labels.
\end{proof}

\section{Lorentz incidence model}
\label{sec:incidence}

Write every Hermitian \(2\times2\) matrix as
\[
X=x_0\id+x_1\sigma_x+x_2\sigma_y+x_3\sigma_z
\]
and set \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)\). Then
\[
\det X=x^TJx,\qquad
\Tr(XY)=2x^Ty,
\]
where the second pairing is the ordinary Euclidean coordinate pairing.
Nonzero positive rank-one effects are precisely future-null vectors:
\(x_0>0\), \(x^TJx=0\).

In the residual architecture label one party's two binary effects by
\(E_1,E_2\) and ternary effects by \(E_3,E_4,E_5\). Set
\[
r_1=e_1,\ r_2=e_2,\ r_3=e_3,\ r_4=e_4,\quad
r_5=(1,1,-1,-1)^T,\quad u=(1,1,0,0)^T.
\]
Their unique coefficient circuit is
\[
r_1+r_2=r_3+r_4+r_5=u.
\]

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\input{figures/five_ray_circuit.tex}
\caption{The local coefficient circuit after choosing four effects as an
operator basis. The physical effects are future-null rays in a Lorentz
metric.}
\label{fig:circuit}
\end{figure}

The first four physical effects form a real basis of \(\Herm(2)\). Indeed,
\(E_1,E_2\) span the binary operator system \(V_0\), while the linearly
independent ternary effects span \(V_1\). Since
\(V_0\cap V_1=\R\id\), the quotient \(V_1/\R\id\) has dimension two.
The images of any two ternary effects are independent there: a dependence
modulo \(\id=E_3+E_4+E_5\) would give a linear dependence among all three
ternary effects. After relabeling those two effects as \(E_3,E_4\), the four
operators \(E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4\) are a basis.

Let \(\mathsf E_A\) contain their Lorentz vectors as columns and define
\(g=\mathsf E_A^TJ\mathsf E_A\). Nullness and normalization force
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metric}
g=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\tfrac12&a&b\\
\tfrac12&0&c&d\\
a&c&0&e\\
b&d&e&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad e=a+b+c+d-\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
On the genuine residual stratum all distinct-ray products are positive; in
particular \(a,b,c,d,e>0\) and
\[
a+b,c+d,a+c,b+d<\frac12.
\]

\begin{definition}[Strict residual domain]
\label{def:strict-residual}
A metric \(g\) of the form \eqref{eq:metric} is \emph{strict residual} when
it has signature \((1,3)\), \(u^Tgu=1\), and the five \(g\)-null coefficient
rays are distinct and lie in one chosen future cone. Equivalently for this
normal form, all products between distinct rays are positive, which includes
\[
a,b,c,d,e>0,\qquad
a+b,c+d,a+c,b+d<\frac12.
\]
The genuine residual domain additionally requires \(P\) invertible,
\(u^TPu=1\), all five incidences below, strictly positive marginals
\(u^TPr_j\), and a full-rank reconstructed reduced state. These conditions
exclude zero effects, repeated rays, product states, and loss of time
orientation; all are open conditions around a genuine residual strategy.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Lorentz representation of a residual strategy]
\label{lem:lorentz-representation}
Choose the first four effects on both parties as bases, with coordinate
frames \(\mathsf E_A,\mathsf E_B\), and let \(P\) be their \(4\times4\)
joint-probability block. For a normalized full-Schmidt-rank pure state
\[
|\psi_\Xi\rangle=\sum_{m,n=0}^1\Xi_{mn}|mn\rangle,
\qquad \Tr(\Xi^\dagger\Xi)=1,\quad\det\Xi\ne0,
\]
let \(\Theta_\Xi(N)=\Xi N^T\Xi^\dagger\) and let \(L_\Xi\) be the real matrix
of this map in Pauli coordinates. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=2\mathsf E_A^TL_\Xi\mathsf E_B,\\
g&=\mathsf E_A^TJ\mathsf E_A,\qquad
h=\mathsf E_B^TJ\mathsf E_B,\\
L_\Xi^TJL_\Xi&=|\det\Xi|^2J,\\
P^Tg^{-1}P&=4|\det\Xi|^2h.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(e_i\) and \(f_j\) are the Pauli-coordinate columns of the two frames,
the Born rule and the trace pairing give
\[
P_{ij}=\Tr\!\left(E_i\Theta_\Xi(N_j)\right)
=2e_i^TL_\Xi f_j,
\]
which is the first identity; the two Gram identities are definitions.
Moreover,
\[
\det\Theta_\Xi(N)
=\det(\Xi N^T\Xi^\dagger)
=|\det\Xi|^2\det N.
\]
In Pauli coordinates this says
\((L_\Xi x)^TJ(L_\Xi x)=|\det\Xi|^2x^TJx\) for every real \(x\).
Polarizing the two quadratic forms yields
\(L_\Xi^TJL_\Xi=|\det\Xi|^2J\). Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P^Tg^{-1}P
&=4\mathsf E_B^TL_\Xi^T
\mathsf E_A(\mathsf E_A^TJ\mathsf E_A)^{-1}
\mathsf E_A^TL_\Xi\mathsf E_B\\
&=4\mathsf E_B^TL_\Xi^TJL_\Xi\mathsf E_B
=4|\det\Xi|^2h,
\end{aligned}
\]
because
\(\mathsf E_A(\mathsf E_A^TJ\mathsf E_A)^{-1}\mathsf E_A^T=J\).
\end{proof}

The complete table is
\[
p_{ij}=r_i^TPr_j,\qquad u^TPu=1.
\]
The final identity in \cref{lem:lorentz-representation} and
\(\det\Xi\ne0\) show that \(P\) is invertible. With \(Q=g^{-1}\), nullness
of Bob's five effects becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:incidence}
F_j(P,g):=(Pr_j)^TQ(Pr_j)=0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,5.
\end{equation}

\section{Physical exactness and smoothness}
\label{sec:physical}

\begin{theorem}[Local physical completeness]
\label{thm:physical-completeness}
Near every genuine residual point, every nearby \((P,g)\) satisfying
\eqref{eq:metric}, the five incidence equations \eqref{eq:incidence},
normalization, Lorentz signature, and the strict time-orientation inequalities
is realized by a genuine residual two-qubit strategy. Every incidence tangent
therefore integrates to a two-sided physical curve.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because \(g\) has signature \((1,3)\), \(u^Tgu=1\), and the restriction to
\(u^{\perp_g}\) is negative definite, choose at the reference metric three
vectors spanning \(u^{\perp_g}\). For nearby metrics, project these fixed
vectors by
\[
v\longmapsto v-(v^Tgu)u.
\]
They remain independent. Ordinary Gram--Schmidt for the positive inner
product \(-g\) on \(u^{\perp_g}\) produces a smooth negative orthonormal frame:
all denominators stay nonzero in a neighborhood of the reference point.
Mapping \(u\) to the standard time vector and this frame to the three standard
spatial vectors gives a smoothly varying \(\mathsf E_A\) with
\[
\mathsf E_A^TJ\mathsf E_A=g,\qquad
\mathsf E_Au=(1,0,0,0)^T.
\]
Each \(r_i\) is \(g\)-null and has positive product with \(u\); hence
\(\mathsf E_A r_i\) is future null and represents a positive rank-one Alice
effect. The circuit makes both measurements sum to the identity.

Put \(x_j=Pr_j\) and
\[
s_j=\frac12\mathsf E_A^{-T}x_j.
\]
The factor \(1/2\) is forced by the Pauli trace pairing: if \(S_j\) has
coordinate vector \(s_j\), then the four basis probabilities obey
\[
x_j=(\Tr(E_iS_j))_{i=1}^4=2\mathsf E_A^Ts_j.
\]
The incidence equations give \(s_j^TJs_j=0\), while
\(2(s_j)_0=u^Tx_j\) is the strictly positive Bob marginal. Thus \(s_j\)
represents a future-null positive rank-one steered operator \(S_j\).
The circuit yields
\[
S_1+S_2=S_3+S_4+S_5=:\rho_A.
\]
Normalization is explicit:
\[
\Tr\rho_A=\Tr(S_1+S_2)
=u^TPu=1.
\]
Full rank is open near the original point. Let
\(\Xi=\rho_A^{1/2}\) and set
\[
N_j^T=\Xi^{-1}S_j\Xi^{-\dagger}.
\]
The \(N_j\) are positive rank-one effects and normalize on both inputs.
The reconstructed pure state
\[
|\psi_\Xi\rangle=\sum_{m,n}\Xi_{mn}|mn\rangle
\]
is normalized because
\(\langle\psi_\Xi|\psi_\Xi\rangle=\Tr(\Xi^\dagger\Xi)
=\Tr\rho_A=1\).
Moreover
\[
\Tr(E_iS_j)=r_i^TPr_j,
\]
so every reconstructed probability is the trace of a product of positive
operators and is nonnegative, including when that trace is zero.

The five incidence constraints and normalization have independent
differentials by the calculation in \cref{prop:smooth}. The elementary
regular-level-set construction is obtained by choosing six coordinates in
which the constraint Jacobian is invertible and solving those coordinates as
smooth functions of the remaining fourteen. In that local chart, every
kernel vector of the constraint differential is the derivative of a straight
coordinate line, hence integrates to a curve in the incidence set.
Signature, time orientation, positive marginals, and full rank are open
conditions, giving a two-sided physical curve for sufficiently small
parameter values even when a joint probability is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Smooth incidence manifold]
\label{prop:smooth}
Restricted to the open genuine residual stratum---in particular, where
\(g\) has Lorentz signature, all strict time-orientation inequalities hold,
and \(P\) is invertible---the normalized incidence space is a smooth real
\(14\)-dimensional manifold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\[
\nabla_PF_j=2QPr_jr_j^T.
\]
If a linear combination vanishes, invertibility of \(QP\) gives
\[
\sum_j\mu_jr_jr_j^T
=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_4)+\mu_5r_5r_5^T=0.
\]
Off-diagonal entries first give \(\mu_5=0\), then diagonal entries give all
other coefficients zero. Normalization is independent because the radial
variation \(\delta P=P\) annihilates every \(dF_j\) but changes \(u^TPu\).
There are \(16+4-5-1=14\) dimensions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proposition is local to the open stratum used by the residual equality
proof;
singular normalized incidence points outside that stratum are not asserted to
be smooth. The projected behavior image has dimension at most \(14\); no claim
of exact projected dimension is needed for the equality theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Stationarity and strictly positive multipliers}
\label{sec:multipliers}

Every entry of the complete residual behavior is \(r_i^TPr_j\). Thus, after
collecting the original Bell coefficients into numbers
\(\gamma_{ij}\), its restriction to the incidence coordinates is the linear
objective
\[
\mathcal L_c(P):=\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}r_i^TPr_j
=\Tr(\mathsf B_c^TP),\qquad
\mathsf B_c:=\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}r_ir_j^T.
\]
At a stationary point, the six constraint gradients are linearly independent.
Thus there are unique multipliers
\(\alpha,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_5\), with sign convention
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lagrange}
\mathsf B_c-\alpha uu^T=-2QP\Lambda,\qquad
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^5\lambda_jr_jr_j^T.
\end{equation}
Stationarity in the four metric variables says
\(\lambda\in\ker\cD^T\), where \(\cD\) is defined in
\cref{sec:second-variation}.

\begin{lemma}[Finite POVM duality]
\label{lem:povm-duality}
For
\[
\max_{N_j\ge0,\ \sum_jN_j=\id}\sum_j\Tr(K_jN_j),
\]
there is a Hermitian \(\Gamma\) such that
\[
\Gamma\ge K_j,\qquad(\Gamma-K_j)N_j=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weak duality is immediate. Strong duality follows by separating the closed
finite-dimensional cone
\[
\left\{\left(\sum_jX_j,\sum_j\Tr(K_jX_j)-t\right):
X_j\ge0,\ t\ge0\right\}
\]
from \((\id,V+\eps)\), where \(V\) is the primal optimum, and taking
\(\eps\downarrow0\). Equality of primal and dual values gives
\[
\sum_j\Tr[(\Gamma-K_j)N_j]=0.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, hence zero; for positive matrices,
\(\Tr(AB)=0\) implies \(AB=0\). Full details are included in
\cref{app:duality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Determinant pullback for one input]
\label{lem:determinant-pullback}
At a stationary residual strategy, fix one Bob input \(y\), write its effects
as \(N_{j|y}\), and let \(K_{j|y}\) be their Bell score operators. There is a
Hermitian normalization multiplier \(\widehat\Gamma_y\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effect-kkt}
K_{j|y}-\widehat\Gamma_y+
4|\det\Xi|^2\lambda_{j|y}\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y})=0
\end{equation}
for every outcome \(j\). Here, for
\(N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\[
\operatorname{adj}(N):=
\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}
\]
is the matrix adjugate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:lorentz-representation},
\[
F_j=(Pr_j)^Tg^{-1}(Pr_j)
=4|\det\Xi|^2r_j^Thr_j
=4|\det\Xi|^2\det N_{j|y}.
\]
For an arbitrary Hermitian variation \(\Delta N\),
\[
d(\det)_N[\Delta N]=\Tr(\operatorname{adj}(N)\Delta N),
\]
as follows by differentiating \(ad-bc\). Hold the state, Alice's
measurements, and Bob's other input fixed. The first variation of the Bell
objective is
\(\sum_j\Tr(K_{j|y}\Delta N_{j|y})\). Pulling the incidence Lagrangian
\eqref{eq:lagrange} back to these effect variables, and imposing
\(\sum_j\Delta N_{j|y}=0\) with a separate Hermitian multiplier
\(\widehat\Gamma_y\), gives
\[
\sum_j\Tr\!\left[
\left(K_{j|y}-\widehat\Gamma_y+
4|\det\Xi|^2\lambda_{j|y}\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y})\right)
\Delta N_{j|y}\right]=0.
\]
The unconstrained outcome variations are arbitrary after introduction of the
normalization multiplier, so every coefficient vanishes, proving
\eqref{eq:effect-kkt}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Strict multiplier positivity]
\label{prop:positive-multipliers}
At a residual global Bell maximizer whose value is strictly above the PVM
support value,
\[
\lambda_j>0\quad(j=1,\ldots,5),\qquad\Lambda>0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each Bob input \(y\), hold the state and all other measurements fixed and
collect the terms of \(\mathcal L_c\) depending on that input as
\[
\sum_{j\in B_y}\Tr(K_{j|y}N_{j|y}).
\]
Apply \cref{lem:povm-duality} separately to Bob's binary and ternary inputs,
with distinct dual operators \(\Gamma_y\). Complementary slackness says
\((\Gamma_y-K_{j|y})N_{j|y}=0\). Since \(N_{j|y}\) is nonzero rank one, every
positive semidefinite matrix annihilating its range is a nonnegative multiple
of its adjugate. Thus
\[
\Gamma_y-K_{j|y}
=\tau_{j|y}\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y}),\qquad \tau_{j|y}\ge0,
\]
\(\operatorname{adj}(N_{j|y})N_{j|y}=0\).

It remains to identify the normalization multiplier
\(\widehat\Gamma_y\) from \cref{lem:determinant-pullback} with the finite-POVM
dual \(\Gamma_y\). Both differences from \(K_{j|y}\) annihilate
\(\operatorname{ran}N_{j|y}\), so
\((\widehat\Gamma_y-\Gamma_y)N_{j|y}=0\) for every \(j\). Those ranges span
\(\C^2\), because \(\sum_jN_{j|y}=\id\); hence
\(\widehat\Gamma_y=\Gamma_y\). Comparing the two adjugate coefficients gives
\[
4|\det\Xi|^2\lambda_{j|y}=\tau_{j|y}\ge0.
\]

If \(\lambda_{j|y}=0\), then \(K_{j|y}=\Gamma_y\). Replacing that entire input
by the deterministic PVM \(N_{j|y}=\id\), \(N_{k|y}=0\) for \(k\ne j\),
preserves the score because both the old optimum and the replacement score
equal \(\Tr\Gamma_y\).

For completeness, the resulting behavior has an explicit local model. Let
\(y_*\) be Bob's remaining nontrivial input, let
\(q(\ell)=p_B(\ell|y_*)\), and omit zero-weight \(\ell\). Use hidden variable
\(\ell\), Alice response
\[
p_A(a|x,\ell)=\frac{p(a,\ell|x,y_*)}{q(\ell)},
\]
and Bob response \(\ell\) on \(y_*\) and the fixed label \(j\) on the replaced
input. This reproduces the \(y_*\) block by construction and the deterministic
block because nonsignaling gives
\(\sum_\ell p(a,\ell|x,y_*)=p_A(a|x)\). Expanding the local response
probabilities into deterministic choices yields a convex combination of
identity-and-zero qubit PVM behaviors. A strict value above the PVM support
therefore forces every \(\tau_{j|y}\), and hence every \(\lambda_j\), to be
positive. Since \(r_1,\ldots,r_4\) form a basis, \(\Lambda>0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Exact second variation}
\label{sec:second-variation}

A tangent metric variation has the form
\[
H(\dot a,\dot b,\dot c,\dot d)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\dot a&\dot b\\
0&0&\dot c&\dot d\\
\dot a&\dot c&0&\dot a+\dot b+\dot c+\dot d\\
\dot b&\dot d&\dot a+\dot b+\dot c+\dot d&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define
\[
\Phi(y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
y_2(y_0+y_3)\\
y_3(y_0+y_2)\\
y_2(y_1+y_3)\\
y_3(y_1+y_2)
\end{pmatrix},
\quad y_j=QPr_j,
\]
and
\[
\cD:H\longmapsto(y_1^THy_1,\ldots,y_5^THy_5).
\]
Then
\[
\rank\cD=\dim\operatorname{span}\{\Phi(y_1),\ldots,\Phi(y_5)\}.
\]
Let \(K=\ker\cD^T\), \(k=\dim K=5-\rank\cD\). Metric stationarity says
\(\lambda\in K\).

For a tangent \((\delta P,H)\), set
\[
S=P^TQP,\qquad \delta P=PW+HQ P.
\]
Since \(dQ=-QHQ\), direct differentiation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
dF_j
&=2(QPr_j)^T\delta P\,r_j-(QPr_j)^TH(QPr_j)\\
&=2r_j^TSWr_j+y_j^THy_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the first-order equations \(dF_j=0\) ask the metric differential
\(\cD(H)\) to equal the vector with entries \(-2r_j^TSWr_j\).
Finite-dimensional linear algebra says this equation is solvable exactly
when its right side is orthogonal to \(K=\ker\cD^T\). Indeed,
\(\operatorname{ran}\cD=(\ker\cD^T)^\perp\), because both spaces have
dimension \(\rank\cD\). Therefore the Fredholm compatibility conditions are
\[
\Tr(SW\Lambda_\mu)=0\quad(\mu\in K),
\qquad
\Lambda_\mu=\sum_j\mu_jr_jr_j^T.
\]
The compatible space
\[
\mathcal H_K=
\{W:\Tr(SW\Lambda_\mu)=0\ \forall\mu\in K\}
\]
has dimension \(16-k\). To see that the \(k\) displayed functionals are
independent, suppose a combination indexed by \(\nu\in K\) vanishes for every
\(W\). Then \(\Tr(SW\Lambda_\nu)=0\) for all \(W\), so
\(\Lambda_\nu S=0\). The matrix \(S=P^TQP\) is invertible, and the map
\(\nu\mapsto\Lambda_\nu=\sum_j\nu_jr_jr_j^T\) is injective by
\cref{prop:smooth}; hence \(\nu=0\).

\begin{proposition}[Weighted second form]
\label{prop:second-form}
\[
\sum_j\lambda_jD^2F_j=2q(W),\qquad
q(W)=\Tr(SW\Lambda W^T),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{inertia}(q)=(4,12).
\]
Every positive-\(q\) compatible tangent can be adjusted to satisfy
normalization without changing \(q\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating \(g^{-1}\) twice and completing the square after
\(\delta P=PW+HQ P\) gives
\[
D^2F_j=2(PWr_j)^TQ(PWr_j),
\]
and summing against \(\lambda_j\) yields the trace formula. The exact
expansion is recorded in \cref{app:square-completion} and independently
checked by the verifier.

The matrix \(S\) has signature \((1,3)\), while \(\Lambda>0\).
Invertible congruences therefore reduce \(q\) to
\[
q_0(Z)=\sum_{j=1}^4Z_{1j}^2-
\sum_{i=2}^4\sum_{j=1}^4Z_{ij}^2,
\]
giving inertia \((4,12)\).

For \(\mu\in K\),
\[
\Tr(S\Lambda_\mu)=\sum_j\mu_jr_j^TSr_j
=\sum_j\mu_jF_j=0.
\]
Hence the identity matrix belongs to \(\mathcal H_K\). Taking
\(\mu=\lambda\) also gives \(q(\id)=\Tr(S\Lambda)=0\), while compatibility
gives the polar cross term \(\Tr(SW\Lambda)=0\). Thus
\(q(W+t\id)=q(W)\). Adding \(t\id\) changes the normalization derivative
by
\[
t\,u^TPu=t,
\]
so choose \(t\) to impose \(u^T\delta P\,u=0\).
\end{proof}

With the convention \eqref{eq:lagrange}, a normalized physical curve has
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal L_c(P(t))\big|_{t=0}=2q(W).
\]
Hence local maximality requires \(q\le0\) on every normalized tangent.

\section{Rank trichotomy and universal equality}
\label{sec:closure}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/rank_trichotomy.tex}}
\caption{The exhaustive rank trichotomy for the metric differential.}
\label{fig:trichotomy}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Rank at least two}

If \(\rank\cD\ge2\), then \(k\le3\) and
\[
\dim\mathcal H_K=16-k\ge13.
\]
A subspace on which a form of inertia \((4,12)\) is nonpositive has dimension
at most \(12\): projection to its twelve negative coordinates is injective.
Therefore \(\mathcal H_K\) contains \(W\) with \(q(W)>0\). Normalize it using
\cref{prop:second-form} and integrate it using
\cref{thm:physical-completeness}. The resulting physical curve has positive
Bell second derivative, contradicting local maximality.

\subsection{Rank one}

The coefficient form of the metric differential is the quadratic map
\[
\Phi(y)=
\bigl(y_2(y_0+y_3),\,y_3(y_0+y_2),\,
y_2(y_1+y_3),\,y_3(y_1+y_2)\bigr)^T.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Projective fiber theorem]
\label{prop:fiber}
On the strict \(g\)-null quadric, the base locus of \(\Phi\) is exactly the
five coefficient rays \([r_1],\ldots,[r_5]\). Away from the base locus,
\(\Phi\) is projectively injective, including on the exceptional divisors
\[
y_2=0,\quad y_3=0,\quad y_0=y_1,\quad y_2=y_3.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The generic rational inverse and the exact exceptional-divisor calculations
are given in \cref{app:fibers}. Each resultant and factorization is reproduced
by the exact verifier. The strict metric inequalities exclude extra null
roots without excluding any genuine residual point.
\end{proof}

If \(\rank\cD=1\), all nonzero \(\Phi(y_j)\) are projectively equal.
The five \(y_j=QPr_j\) are distinct rays because \(QP\) is invertible.
\Cref{prop:fiber} therefore permits at most one nonzero row of \(\cD\).
Consequently
\[
\ker\cD^T=\{\mu:\mu_{j_0}=0\}
\]
for some \(j_0\), which contains no strictly positive vector. This
contradicts \(\lambda_j>0\).

\subsection{Rank zero}

If \(\rank\cD=0\), every \(y_j\) belongs to the five-ray base locus.
Invertibility gives
\[
y_j=t_jr_{\pi(j)},\qquad t_j>0,
\]
for a permutation \(\pi\). Let
\(\varepsilon=(1,1,-1,-1,-1)^T\). The \(4\times5\) matrix
\([r_1\ \cdots\ r_5]\) has one-dimensional kernel
\(\R\varepsilon\). Applying the invertible map \(QP\) to its circuit gives
\[
\sum_j\varepsilon_jt_jr_{\pi(j)}=0.
\]
This coefficient vector, reordered by \(\pi\), must be a scalar multiple of
\(\varepsilon\). Positivity of the \(t_j\), together with the unequal
cardinalities of the positive and negative parts of \(\varepsilon\), forces
\(\pi\) to preserve the binary pair \(\{1,2\}\) and ternary triple
\(\{3,4,5\}\); coefficient comparison then forces every \(t_j\) to have one
common positive value \(t\). After relabeling, \(QP=t\id\), hence \(P=tg\).
Since \(u^TPu=u^Tgu=1\), one has \(t=1\), and therefore
\[
QP=\id,\qquad P=g.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Rank-zero deterministic simulation]
\label{prop:rank-zero}
Every rank-zero probability table \(p_{ij}=r_i^Tgr_j\) is a convex
combination of deterministic local behaviors, and hence belongs to
\(\Qbar^{\PVM}_2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(i,j\in\{3,4,5\}\), define directly from the Bell table
\[
a_i:=p(1,i|0,1),\qquad b_i:=p(2,i|0,1),\qquad
c_{ij}:=p(i,j|1,1).
\]
Because \(P=g=g^T\), the other mixed block satisfies
\[
p(i,1|1,0)=a_i,\qquad p(i,2|1,0)=b_i.
\]
Moreover \(c_{ij}=c_{ji}\), and nullness gives \(c_{ii}=0\).
Normalization and the two local circuits give
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}=a_i+b_i,\qquad
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=\frac12.
\]
\Cref{app:transport} constructs a matrix \(q_{ij}\) satisfying
\[
q_{ii}=0,\qquad 0\le q_{ij}\le c_{ij},\qquad
\sum_jq_{ij}=b_i,\qquad
\sum_iq_{ij}=a_j.
\]
For each ordered ternary pair \((i,j)\), use the two deterministic branches
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{weight}&\text{Alice binary}&\text{Bob binary}
&\text{ternary outputs }(A,B)\\
\hline
q_{ij}&1&2&(i,j)\\
c_{ij}-q_{ij}&2&1&(i,j).
\end{array}
\]
The weights with ternary outputs \((i,j)\) sum to \(c_{ij}\), reproducing the
ternary--ternary block. For fixed Alice ternary output \(i\), the Bob-binary
outcome-\(2\) mass is \(\sum_jq_{ij}=b_i\), and its outcome-\(1\) mass is
\(\sum_j(c_{ij}-q_{ij})=a_i\). For fixed Bob ternary output \(j\), the
Alice-binary outcome-\(1\) mass is \(\sum_iq_{ij}=a_j\), and its
outcome-\(2\) mass is \(b_j\). Finally,
\[
\sum_{i,j}q_{ij}=\frac12,\qquad
\sum_{i,j}(c_{ij}-q_{ij})=\frac12,
\]
so the binary--binary block has probabilities \(1/2\) on \((1,2)\) and
\((2,1)\), and zero on \((1,1)\) and \((2,2)\), exactly as
\(r_k^Tgr_\ell\) for \(k,\ell\in\{1,2\}\). The four blocks are therefore
reproduced simultaneously. Identity and zero projectors implement every
deterministic branch.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equality}

\begin{theorem}[Universal two-input equality]
\label{thm:two-input-equality}
For every finite input-dependent output architecture
\((A_0,A_1;B_0,B_1)\),
\[
\boxed{
\Qbar^{\POVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B)
=
\Qbar^{\PVM}_2(\mathbf A,\mathbf B).
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The PVM-to-POVM inclusion is immediate. Suppose the reverse inclusion failed.
The nearest-point argument in \cref{lem:nearest} would give a Bell functional
whose global POVM maximum strictly exceeds its PVM maximum. Choose an extreme
point of its exposed maximizing face. By
\cref{thm:residual-reduction}, a maximizing component lies in the genuine
residual architecture. Every local measurement is therefore globally optimal
with the other variables fixed, so \cref{prop:positive-multipliers} applies.

The metric differential has rank \(0,1,2,3,\) or \(4\). Rank at least two has
an uphill physical direction; rank one cannot carry the required positive
multiplier; rank zero is PVM-simulable by \cref{prop:rank-zero}. Every case
contradicts a strict value above the PVM support function.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Minimum input architecture]
\label{cor:minimality}
Up to exchanging the parties, \(3\times2\) is the minimum input architecture
for a strict fixed-qubit POVM-over-PVM Bell separation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
With one input on either party every nonsignaling behavior is local, hence a
deterministic PVM behavior maximizes every Bell functional. Two inputs on both
parties are excluded by \cref{thm:two-input-equality}.
\Cref{thm:separation} supplies an exact \(3\times2\) separation.
\end{proof}

\section{Operational consequences and limitations}

For every Bell functional with two inputs per party, optimizing over
fixed-qubit PVMs already
provides the fixed-qubit POVM bound. Thus a value above that bound excludes
all local-qubit realizations, not merely projective ones.

Likewise, no qubit behavior with two inputs per party can certify that a
nonprojective local
measurement was indispensable: after shared-randomness convexification, a
shared-randomness mixture of PVM realizations always exists. This does not identify the state or
measurements in a particular laboratory realization, and it does not assert
raw-set equality without shared randomness.

Equivalently, the theorem rules out every two-input linear Bell-functional
separation for bipartite qubits after shared-randomness convexification. It
does not prove equality of the raw nonconvex behavior sets.

We do not claim the exact global POVM or PVM optimum of
\eqref{eq:bell-functional}. We also do not infer a private-randomness entropy
bound: such a statement requires an adversarial extension of the convex
decomposition, not only equality of observed behavior sets.

\section{Discussion}

The threshold result is special to local dimension two. Natural open
questions include minimum-input laws in dimension three and higher,
multipartite scenarios, equality without convexification, constructive
simulation algorithms for generic two-input behaviors, instruments and
sequential measurements, and experimentally robust exact \(3\times2\)
witnesses. Determining the exact two-qubit POVM and PVM optima of
\eqref{eq:bell-functional} is also open but unnecessary for the separation.

The proof illustrates a distinction between operator-level and behavioral
generality. Ternary qubit POVMs are genuine generalized measurements, yet two
inputs do not provide enough Bell structure to make them indispensable after
the state and shared-randomness decomposition may change. A third input can
lock the state and steering geometry while reserving another measurement for
a discrimination advantage.
