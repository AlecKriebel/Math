                                                    Abstract
        We construct a ﬁnite two-dimensional abstract simplicial complex that is doubly Cohen–Macaulay
     over every ﬁeld of characteristic zero, but contains no two-dimensional homology-sphere subcomplex over
     any ﬁeld. This gives a negative answer to Nevo’s question asking whether every doubly Cohen–Macaulay
     complex admits a decomposition into homology spheres with speciﬁed overlap properties. We use a
     ﬁnite quotient of an aﬃne building, with all links equal to the incidence graph of the projective plane of
     order eleven. A direct spectral estimate controls the links after any vertex deletion; a weighted cocycle
     argument veriﬁes the required homology vanishing. The sphere obstruction is an Euler-characteristic
     count. The construction and the spectral and curvature mechanisms build on established work, credited
     below.


1    The question and the result
All complexes in this note are ﬁnite abstract simplicial complexes unless a building is explicitly being dis-
cussed. A pure complex K is Cohen–Macaulay over a ﬁeld k if, for every face σ, including the empty
face,
                                e i (lkK σ; k) = 0
                                H                    for all i < dim(lkK σ).
It is doubly Cohen–Macaulay, or 2-CM, if it is Cohen–Macaulay and the deletion K − v of every vertex is
Cohen–Macaulay of the same dimension.
    Nevo proved that a d-dimensional 2-CM complex can be expressed as a union of minimal d-cycle complexes
with overlaps containing a d-face and a reordering condition, for d ≥ 1 [1, Theorem 7] [2, Theorem 3.4]. He
then asked whether the pieces could instead be homology spheres [1, Problem 8, p. 961] [2, Problem 3.7,
p. 417]. We use the standard local-link convention for a homology sphere: a d-dimensional homology sphere

including σ = ∅. Here {∅} is the sphere of dimension −1. This convention, also used in the surrounding
sphere and ear decomposition literature, is explicitly stated in [3, Section 4]. Having the homology of a
sphere only globally is a weaker condition.
Theorem 1. There exists a ﬁnite pure two-dimensional abstract simplicial complex K which is 2-CM over
every characteristic-zero ﬁeld and has no two-dimensional homology-sphere subcomplex over any ﬁeld. Con-
sequently Nevo’s homology-sphere decomposition question has a negative answer.
    A single such complex over Q disproves the universal question. The ﬁnite ﬁeld F11 used in the geometry
is distinct from the homology coeﬃcient ﬁeld; no claim of 2-CM over every positive-characteristic ﬁeld is
made. No subdivision, immersion, or enlarged ambient complex is used.
    The substantial ingredients are classical. Lubotzky–Samuels–Vishne [4, Sections 2–4] provide the aﬃne
building, a vertex-regular lattice and a faithful polynomial matrix representation. The method comparing

    We apply the lemma to K and to every deletion K − z. Each edge of K belongs to twelve triangles by
(2). A surviving edge loses at most one triangle when z is removed, so it still belongs to at least eleven. Each
surviving vertex has at least 265 neighbors, hence lies in a triangle. Thus K − z remains nonempty, pure and

two-dimensional. It is connected: a path in K that passes through z can bypass z along its connected link
G, whose edges are surviving edges of K. For v ̸= z, the link of v in K − z is G if vz was not an edge, and
is G − w for the corresponding neighbor w otherwise. Equations (2)–(4) and Lemma 2 give H 1 (X; R) = 0
for X = K or K − z. Finite-dimensional duality gives H1 (X; R) = 0. The boundary matrices have integer
entries, whose ranks agree over Q, R, and every characteristic-zero ﬁeld. Hence the same vanishing holds
over every such ﬁeld.
    For completeness, these facts check all faces in the Cohen–Macaulay criterion. At the empty face, connect-
edness and H1 = 0 give all the required below-top reduced homology vanishing. Vertex links are nonempty
connected one-dimensional complexes. Edge links are nonempty zero-dimensional complexes. Facet links
are {∅} of dimension −1, with no required below-top nonzero homology. Nonemptiness covers the negative-
degree conventions. This proves Cohen–Macaulayness of K and all K − z, of the same dimension, and
therefore the 2-CM assertion of Theorem 1.


4    There are no homology-sphere pieces
Suppose that a two-dimensional homology sphere S over some ﬁeld is a subcomplex of K. Its edge links
have the reduced homology of S 0 , so every edge belongs to exactly two triangles of S. Every vertex link in
S is connected, and each of its vertices has degree two by that edge condition. It is therefore a simple cycle.
It embeds as a subgraph of the corresponding link G in K, so its length is at least six. Writing fi = fi (S),
we obtain

Global sphere homology, over any ﬁeld, instead gives χ(S) = 2. This contradiction proves the sphere
exclusion. Neither orientability nor a classiﬁcation of surfaces is needed, and S need not be induced. In
particular K cannot even be covered by permitted sphere subcomplexes; the further overlap and reordering
requirements cannot be met. This completes the proof of Theorem 1.


Veriﬁcation, priority, and review disclosure
The accompanying source and veriﬁcation archive contains the paper source, authored audit records, and
reproducible ﬁnite checks. The default standard-library veriﬁer constructs the projective-plane incidence
graph, checks its exact Gram identity and all single-vertex deletions, and checks a triangulated torus at
the strict 1/2 spectral boundary using rational homology and exact cocycle energies. Actual corrupted
constructions are required to fail in normal and optimized Python modes. A separate optional NumPy
diagnostic checks the explicit polynomial neighbor matrices and local adjacency; it is not required by the
oﬄine default veriﬁer. None of these programs enumerates the ﬁnite quotient, replaces the symbolic proof,
or independently constructs building theory. Historical review programs and later packaged replays are
distinguished in the archive.
    An extensive bounded priority search, including three separate adversarial literature reviews, did not
verify an earlier recorded full answer to this speciﬁc question. This is not a certiﬁcate of ﬁrstness or a
claim that all literature has been inspected. The construction and the spectral, congruence, and curvature
tools have substantial antecedents, as credited above. Recent work on related algebraic and numerical
consequences, including [8], does not itself assert the sphere-subcomplex decomposition considered here.
Material access gaps remain for the full bodies of Chari’s 1997 convex-ear paper and Baclawski’s 1982 paper;
some other original bodies, books, theses, versions, and subscription indexes were uninspected. The exact
primary versions, selective reading scopes, query inputs, near matches, and gaps are recorded in the authored
audit ﬁles. Third-party source bodies and extracts are not redistributed.
    AI tools were used extensively in developing and checking the argument, writing, literature research,
computation, and adversarial review. This preprint is unrefereed. Internal reviews by AI agents do not
constitute independent external human peer review, journal refereeing, or formal proof veriﬁcation.
