      The outer-pedal area centroid in even-period elliptic billiards
                                                Alec Kriebel
                                           Independent researcher
                                       ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X

                            13 October 2026         (version 1.0, unrefereed)


                                                 Abstract
         Reznik, Garcia and Koiller observed that the area centroid of the outer-side pedal polygon of
     an even-period elliptic billiard is independent of the orbit phase (invariant k307). We prove this
     observation on the natural domain of the signed-area centroid and give its explicit value. The proof
     uses antipodal pairing, support coordinates and telescoping edge moments. For simple even orbits
     the centroid exists for every ﬁxed pedal point. For even star orbits it can be undeﬁned on a ﬁxed
     circle, which we determine exactly and realize in a noncircular eight-period example. The note
     includes a reproducible veriﬁcation package; the written identities give the all-period proof.

    The theorem proves the k307 observation in Table 4 and Section 3.4 of [1], with the denominator
qualiﬁcation already inherent in its signed centroid deﬁnition. An unqualiﬁed assertion that a centroid
exists for every M in every even star family would be false. Equation (2) does not assert that S(0)
is phase independent. Reversal changes both signed moments by a sign and leaves the centroid
unchanged. Repeated traversals of an even least-period orbit give the same centroid; repeating an odd
primitive orbit does not create the antipodal hypothesis. Hyperbolic and degenerate caustics, and the
circular boundary case, are outside the theorem.
    The originating observation is due to Reznik–Garcia–Koiller. Bialy–Tabachnikov [2, Theorems
4.1 and 4.3] prove the neighboring vertex-centroid invariant k306 and recall the classical even-period
symmetry. Schwartz–Tabachnikov [3] study signed centroid loci of Poncelet polygons between conics.
We do not claim novelty for those methods, the symmetry, or the cyclic lemma below. Two independent
bounded primary-source audits completed on 13 October 2026 found no earlier proof covering the
present full statement; this is not a certiﬁcation of historical ﬁrstness.


2    Support coordinates and antipodal pairing
Let T be the counterclockwise Poncelet map on E with the speciﬁed tangent choice, and A(P ) = −P
its half-turn. The porism gives T N = id, and A commutes with T . The ﬁnite group generated
by them consists of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms and is cyclic. Indeed a ﬁnite order

point has strictly monotone iterates. The group therefore acts faithfully on a ﬁnite orbit, preserving
its circular order, and embeds into its cyclic permutation group. Uniqueness of the involution gives
T N/2 = A. Consequently

5    Deﬁnedness and an exact star example
Positivity of S(0) and F makes (2) an exact classiﬁcation of the zero-area locus. For a simple winding-
one family with N ≥ 6, the ﬁrst N/2 normals occupy a half-turn in circular order. Their squares are
at least three distinct circularly ordered unit-circle points. The full Z-polygon traverses their convex
polygon twice, so SZ > 0. Equation (9) implies D > 0, hence d > 0. Period four was treated above.
This completes Theorem 1.

Veriﬁcation and disclosure
The accompanying source and veriﬁcation archive provides exact symbolic checks of the displayed edge
identities, unrestricted Cartesian star polynomials and a separately derived real-coordinate certiﬁcate.
Independent orbit generators supply high-precision numerical controls, including stars, large periods
and near-degenerate caustics. Initial ﬂoating-point failures and their precision repairs are recorded.
Numerical tests corroborate, but do not replace, the all-period proof above.
