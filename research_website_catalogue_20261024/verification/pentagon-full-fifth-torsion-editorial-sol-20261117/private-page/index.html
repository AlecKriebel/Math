Full 5-torsion of the regular-pentagon quintic pencil

Alec Kriebel · Independent researcher
4 October 2026 · Native record version 1.0 · Unrefereed preprint

This note computes the full fifth kernel on the smooth normalization of a specified regular-pentagon plane pencil. The kernel consists of 25 geometric points including the origin; its 24 nonzero points have exact order five. Five points form the known infinity subgroup, so the calculation supplies the other twenty and describes the division field.

The question and earlier work

McCallum's Question 17 in the AIM workshop problem list already observes the five-point infinity subgroup. The contribution here is the explicit bridge from the fixed pentagonal pencil to a classical elliptic-curve family, giving the complete geometric kernel and its arithmetic extension data.

Tate normal form, division-polynomial theory and the Weil pairing are classical. The full-level cover is due to Fisher, and the full-torsion specialization criterion is due to Verdure. Morton's prior Tate-family formulas, also available in the cited arXiv version, give the universal residual polynomial, radical parameter and coordinates of the twenty nonmarked points. This note does not claim a new universal radical solver, exclusive priority or current openness of the original question.

The fixed pencil and its smooth normalization

Set r=√5, K=Q(r), φ=(1+r)/2 and c=φ⁵=(11+5r)/2. In projective coordinates [X:Y:T], let

Q = X² + Y² − T²,
P = 2(X⁵ − 10X³Y² + 5XY⁴) + 5φ(X²+Y²)²T − 5φ³(X²+Y²)T³ + cT⁵.

The plane member is Γλ: P+λTQ²=0. Its coordinates and scaling are fixed; rescaling P changes the numerical parameter. Write Eλ for the smooth projective normalization, with origin O=[0:1:0]. The torsion points are points of Eλ; distinct points there can have the same image on the singular plane curve.

For a finite λ in any characteristic-zero extension of K, the member has a geometrically integral genus-one normalization exactly when λ∉{0, −5r, −c}. The infinite pencil member is excluded. The plane-cuspidal member at λ=−(25+10r)/4 is allowed: its normalization remains elliptic.

For every allowed member, the manuscript gives a cubic model, both birational maps and a degree-ten residual polynomial with two ordinate branches. Its ten roots yield the twenty points outside the known five-point subgroup. Together with that subgroup, including O, these are all of Eλ[5].

The division field over K=Q(√5)

For the following arithmetic statements, take λ∈K in the allowed range. Choose δ with δ²=5+2r, a primitive fifth root of unity ζ₅, and θ with θ⁵=λ+c. The exact division field is

K(Eλ[5]) = K(δ, ζ₅, θ).

  • If λ+c is a fifth power in K, the degree over K is 4 and the Galois group is C2×C2.
  • Otherwise the degree is 20 and the Galois group is D10×C2, where D10 is the dihedral group of order ten.

The only K-rational point in the fifth kernel is O. Over the quadratic extension K(δ) of K, the fifth kernel has exactly the five points of the infinity subgroup. These degree, group and rational-torsion statements are made over the specified base K, rather than arbitrary larger coefficient fields.

Separately, with λ indeterminate over K, the same division-field description over K(λ) has degree twenty. The geometric all-member statement and this generic arithmetic statement have different quantifiers.

Scope and verification

The written proof covers the entire stated characteristic-zero parameter range. The accompanying archive contains exact symbolic and arithmetic controls for inspecting the formulas; finite tests supplement the universal argument. The result does not establish local solubility or a Tate–Shafarevich construction, and does not solve the nonregular or star-pentagon variants.

Original manuscript abstract

We compute all twenty-five geometric fifth-torsion points of the genus-one normalization of the regular-pentagon quintic pencil asked about in McCallum's Question 17 in the AIM workshop problem list on rational and integral points. For a fixed equation normalization over K=Q(√5), we give a cubic model, both birational maps, a degree-ten polynomial and both ordinate branches for the twenty points outside the known infinity subgroup. The complete division field is K(√(5+2√5), ζ₅, ⁵√(λ+φ⁵)), where φ=(1+√5)/2. Its degree is four or twenty, with the exact splitting criterion and Galois action specified. The formulas include every elliptic normalization, including the allowed cuspidal plane member. The method uses classical Tate normal form, division-polynomial theory, Fisher's full-level cover and Verdure's torsion criterion. A reproducible verification supplement accompanies this unrefereed, extensively AI-assisted note.
