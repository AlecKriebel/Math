		28 container abstract
			29 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				30 text Abstract
			31 text For every m≥3 and every finite k≥1, the kth ordinary derived subgroup of the pure braid group contains a braid whose standard closure is not integrally smoothly 1-solvable. Consequently P_m^{(n+2)}⊄F_n^m for every integer n≥1, answering negatively the derived-series containment question in the Harvey–Park–Ray Oberwolfach contribution. The proof combines established signature-quasimorphism results with amenable-quotient rigidity and a pointwise signature obstruction to 1-solvability. The general quasimorphism ingredients are prior results. An accompanying exact verification package also preserves an explicit 104-crossing example in P_3^{(3)}.
		32 container plain-language
			33 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				34 text The idea in plain English
			35 text Pure braids return each strand to its starting position. Repeatedly taking commutators creates the derived series, an increasingly restrictive sequence of subgroups. A separate notion, called 1-solvability, asks whether the closed braid fits into a four-dimensional construction with specified surfaces and intersection properties.
			36 text The paper shows that for every number of strands at least three, a braid can lie arbitrarily far down the derived series and still fail 1-solvability. For each finite depth there is a witness; the argument does not use one braid that works simultaneously at every depth.
			37 text The general argument connects established results about braid signatures and quasimorphisms. The supporting materials also give a concrete three-strand word with 104 crossings at the third derived level. That finite example illustrates one case of the broader existence theorem.
		38 container result
			39 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				40 text The precise result
			41 text Let P_m be the classical disk pure braid group, with P_m^(0)=P_m and P_m^(k+1)=[P_m^(k),P_m^(k)]. For every m≥3 and every finite integer k≥1, some braid in P_m^(k) has standard oriented closure that is not integrally smoothly 1-solvable in the sense of Otto Definition 2.3.
			42 text Equivalently, the string-link concordance classes represented by P_m^(k) are not all in F₁^m. Since F_n^m⊂F₁^m for integers n≥1, this gives P_m^(n+2)⊄F_n^m for every m≥3 and integer n≥1, a negative answer to Question 3 in the Harvey–Park–Ray Oberwolfach contribution.
			43 text For the explicit third-derived example, set a=σ₁², b=σ₂², c=[a,b], d=a⁻¹ca, e=b⁻¹cb, u=[c,d], v=[d,e] and β=[u,v], using [x,y]=xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. The paper gives a 104-letter freely reduced Artin word for β with closure signature −2.
		44 container proof
			45 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				46 text Why the result holds
			47 text The ordinary signature s of a three-strand braid closure has bounded failure of additivity. Its homogenization q is a homogeneous quasimorphism, exactly linear on powers. Published signature formulas show that q restricted to the pure braid group P₃ is not a homomorphism.
			48 text A nonhomomorphic homogeneous quasimorphism cannot vanish on a finite derived subgroup. Otherwise it would descend to a solvable, hence amenable, quotient, where homogeneous quasimorphisms are homomorphisms. The general ingredients are established results.
			49 text The ordinary signature and its homogenization are distinct. A suitable positive power is essential: nonzero q on a braid does not by itself give nonzero ordinary signature on that same braid. The powered braid has nonzero ordinary signature and zero pairwise linking numbers. A pointwise signature obstruction rules out an integral smooth 1-solution; adding split trivial strands reaches every m≥3.
		50 container limits
			51 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				52 text Scope and limitations
			53 text The result uses the ordinary derived series of classical disk pure braid groups and integral smooth solvability of standard closures. It makes no claim for power subgroups, lower central series, rational derived series, surface braid groups, rational solvability or a merely topological solvability category.
			54 text The witness may depend on the finite depth k. No single braid in the intersection of all derived subgroups is asserted, and the explicit 104-crossing word is supplied only for k=3. No minimal crossing number or effective word-length bound at arbitrary depth is claimed.
			55 text For m=1 and m=2, the positive derived subgroups are trivial. The consequence for the solvable filtration is stated for integers n≥1; it does not contradict the prior n=0 containment discussed in the original report.
			56 text The general signature, quasimorphism and amenable-group tools are credited prior results. The contribution presented is their application to this particular containment question, together with the explicit finite example. No historical firstness, exhaustive novelty clearance or continued worldwide openness is claimed.
			57 text For the separate explicit example, the supporting derivation reconstructs the Goeritz form from crossing geometry and records a printed index discrepancy in Erle’s matrix formula. A separate Burau–Meyer calculation gives the same signature. This qualification concerns the finite example; the arbitrary-depth existence proof is independent of that calculation.
