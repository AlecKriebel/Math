		28 container abstract
			29 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				30 text Abstract
			31 text We give a self-contained proof that ordinary conservative chip-firing halting is NP-complete on finite loopless simple strongly connected digraphs. Hardness holds with polynomial numerical total initial chips and a specified vertex whose deletion leaves an acyclic graph of depth at most one. A separate construction forbids antiparallel arcs. The proof combines the primed-toppler mechanism of Holroyd–Levine–Winkler with the classical prime/Boolean/clause encoding of Stockmeyer–Meyer, also reconstructed by Gramlich. We prove the physical conservative realization, its legal checkpoint dynamics and the arbitrary-schedule implication in full; NP membership is credited to Farrell–Levine and reproved with an explicit certificate bound. This is an attributed proof of the historical simple-digraph question. Exact full-result and realization priority and preexisting worldwide openness remain unknown after a bounded audit; no firstness is claimed. AI tools were used extensively. The paper is unrefereed and has not received external human peer review.
		32 container plain-language
			33 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				34 text The idea in plain English
			35 text Place a pile of chips at each vertex of a directed graph. A vertex can fire when it has enough chips to send one along every outgoing arrow. Firing moves chips without creating or removing any. The question is whether the game can eventually reach a position where no vertex can fire.
			36 text This paper proves that deciding whether the game halts is NP-complete even when the graph has no loops or repeated arrows and every vertex can reach every other vertex. A separate construction also forbids arrows pointing in opposite directions between the same two vertices.
			37 text The difficulty persists with polynomially many initial chips and a graph that becomes very shallow after removing one specified vertex. The construction encodes a Boolean formula through counters that cycle modulo small primes: a stable position can be reached exactly when the formula has a satisfying assignment.
		38 container result
			39 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				40 text The precise result
			41 text The input is a finite loopless simple strongly connected digraph with at least two vertices and an arbitrary nonnegative integer chip configuration encoded in binary. Each vertex fires at its actual outdegree and sends one chip along each outgoing arc. There is no dissipative sink.
			42 text HALTING asks whether some finite legal firing sequence reaches a stable configuration. The problem is NP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions. The same conclusion holds for the oriented subclass, which additionally forbids antiparallel arcs.
			43 text Hardness holds when a specified root r is supplied and deleting r leaves an acyclic graph with no directed path of two arcs. The reduction also has polynomial numerical total initial chips in the source 3-SAT input length, so the hardness persists when chip counts are written in unary.
			44 text Membership in NP covers arbitrary binary chip counts. A certificate records how many times each vertex fires, with polynomially many bits, and certifies a nonnegative stable endpoint. It need not list the full firing sequence.
		45 container proof
			46 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				47 text Why the result holds
			48 text A modular bank stores a number of chips that is largest exactly at selected residues. Its priming specializes the established primed-toppler construction of Holroyd–Levine–Winkler. The prime/Boolean/clause encoding is due to Stockmeyer–Meyer and is also reconstructed by Gramlich.
			49 text The proof realizes these counters as an ordinary conservative graph. At each checkpoint, every emitted chip has returned to the correct part of the graph. The root becomes unable to fire precisely when all residue conditions hold. The Chinese remainder theorem connects such a checkpoint to a satisfying Boolean assignment.
			50 text A legal checkpoint sequence establishes both directions of the reduction. Classical least action relates this sequence to all legal firing orders, without a fairness assumption. The proof also gives an explicit bound for a short firing-count certificate, using the NP-membership method of Farrell–Levine.
		51 container limits
			52 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				53 text Scope and limitations
			54 text These are worst-case complexity statements. Unless P = NP, no polynomial-time algorithm solves every valid input. This does not mean that every instance is difficult, and the polynomial-bit certificate does not give a polynomial-time simulation of the game.
			55 text The theorem does not claim bounded-degree or Eulerian hardness, and it does not concern a fixed graph, a dissipative-sink model, weighted firing thresholds or a specified target configuration. The oriented construction is separate from the simple construction, which allows antiparallel arcs.
			56 text The primed-toppler mechanism, prime-residue encoding, root-last stabilization, least action, tree periods and NP-membership method have prior sources. The paper supplies a complete conservative graph realization and halting reduction with those antecedents credited.
			57 text Exact full-result and realization priority remain unknown. A bounded source comparison does not establish that the historical question was still open worldwide immediately before this work. Finite computational checks support the written proof; they do not establish the universal theorem by exhaustion.
