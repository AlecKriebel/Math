		29 container abstract
			30 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				31 text Abstract
			32 text We prove that a finite two-dimensional simplicial complex can have minimum piecewise-linear embedding dimension three and minimum original-simplex linear embedding dimension five. This answers affirmatively the minimum-dimension question posed by Brehm in 2006, already in dimension two. The construction makes the random affine obstruction used by Newman uniform under small facet deletions, then removes triangles sharing an edge. The resulting linear hypergraph admits a PL embedding in three dimensions. We include an elementary embedding of its first barycentric subdivision and a finite parameter choice with exact arithmetic checks. The existence proof does not generate an explicit facet list.
		33 container plain-language
			34 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				35 text The idea in plain English
			36 text A finite simplicial complex is built by joining vertices, edges and triangles along specified faces. There are two different embedding rules: keep each original triangle flat, or first divide the triangles into smaller pieces and place those pieces consistently. The second rule is called a piecewise-linear embedding.
			37 text This paper proves the existence of a two-dimensional complex whose minimum ambient dimension drops from five to three when subdivision is allowed. Four dimensions do not suffice under the original-triangle rule, while the first barycentric subdivision already embeds in three dimensions.
			38 text The construction starts with a random family of triangles whose crossings cannot all be avoided in four dimensions. It strengthens that obstruction so it survives every small deletion. Triangles sharing an edge can then be removed, leaving a family that can be embedded piecewise linearly in three dimensions.
			39 text This is an existence proof with finite numerical parameters, not a displayed small model. The argument uses an enormous number of possible vertices and does not produce a list of the final triangles. Its exact calculations check the probability bounds rather than enumerate the complex.
		40 container result
			41 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				42 text The precise result
			43 text There exists a finite two-dimensional simplicial complex K with minimum piecewise-linear embedding dimension e_PL(K) = 3 and minimum original-simplex affine embedding dimension e_lin(K) = 5.
			44 text The first barycentric subdivision of K admits a linear embedding in R³. Its new vertices are freely assigned positions in space; they need not be arithmetic barycenters of the images of original vertices.
			45 text The construction gives a family of triangles in which distinct triangles share at most one vertex. The generated complex has a nonplanar one-skeleton, forcing the lower bound e_PL(K) ≥ 3.
			46 text A random affine obstruction uniform over all small facet deletions rules out every original-simplex linear embedding in R⁴, including nongeneric placements. A general-position placement in R⁵ gives the matching upper bound.
			47 text The finite existence calculation uses n = 2²⁵⁶ vertex labels, triangle-selection probability p = 2⁻³⁸⁴ and deletion allowance m = 2³²⁰. It establishes a positive-probability outcome without generating its facet list.
		48 container proof
			49 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				50 text Why the result holds
			51 text Every seven generic points in R⁴ contain two disjoint triples with intersecting convex hulls. This supplies many potential crossing pairs in every placement, whose pattern is determined by the labeled order type.
			52 text Select triangles independently. A Janson inequality bounds the chance that no crossing pair survives, and a union bound covers both all order types and every deletion set of the allowed size. That second quantifier permits cleanup after the random sample is observed.
			53 text There are few selected triangle pairs sharing an edge. Deleting one triangle from each such pair leaves a family whose facets meet only at vertices, while preserving the obstruction to a linear embedding in R⁴.
			54 text A straight embedding of the incidence graph in R³ can be thickened into small six-triangle fans representing the first barycentric subdivision. Finite distance and angle bounds keep different fans disjoint except at their prescribed common vertices.
			55 text Enough triangles survive that the one-skeleton is nonplanar, so the piecewise-linear minimum is exactly three. General position in R⁵ gives the original-simplex upper bound, making that minimum exactly five.
		56 container limits
			57 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				58 text Scope and limitations
			59 text The complex is general and need not be a surface or manifold. The result does not answer a version restricted to manifolds or to a prescribed topology.
			60 text The proof supplies no explicit list of facets, practical small example, efficient construction algorithm or minimal vertex count. Exact finite parameters do not make the enormous witness explicitly enumerated.
			61 text Original-simplex linear embedding fixes the original triangulation and is affine on each of its simplices. Piecewise-linear embedding permits subdivision. These different rules are essential to the dimension gap.
			62 text The result is the exact gap two for one existence construction. It does not establish arbitrarily large gaps, a classification of embedding dimensions or a new threshold theorem in odd ambient dimensions.
			63 text Brehm–Sarkaria already separated the two embedding notions in fixed ambient dimensions, Newman supplied the random affine-obstruction method, and Lee–Nevo proved the relevant PL hypergraph embeddability mechanism. The added claim is a deletion-resistant obstruction combined with cleanup and exact minimum dimensions.
			64 text The finite arithmetic and geometric tests supplement the universal probabilistic and embedding arguments. They are not a proof by exhaustive testing, a formal proof certificate or completed human peer review. The bounded source comparison does not certify exclusive historical first priority.
