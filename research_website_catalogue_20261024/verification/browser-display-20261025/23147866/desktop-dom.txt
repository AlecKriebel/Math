		27 container abstract
			28 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				29 text Abstract
			30 text The distributed dense-coding question posed by Bruß and collaborators asks whether the symmetric four-qubit W state gives a strict advantage when two independent senders each transmit one qubit to a different receiver and the receivers decode using local operations and classical communication (LOCC). We give an affirmative answer in their asymptotic unitary-encoding model. Independent Pauli encodings followed by a complete Bell measurement at one receiver produce a finite classical–quantum multiple-access channel whose entire output is local to the other receiver. Winter’s coding theorem gives an achievable sum-rate supremum of 3/2 + h₂(1/4) = 2.311278… bits per resource copy. In particular, both senders can transmit 9/8 bits per copy with vanishing average error. This settles the stated W-state classification question using established measurement and coding ingredients; it does not determine the optimal LOCC capacity or assert a one-copy advantage.
		31 container plain-language
			32 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				33 text The idea in plain English
			34 text Two senders and two receivers share a four-qubit W state. Each sender encodes an independent message and sends one qubit to their own receiver. The receivers can exchange ordinary classical messages, but cannot send quantum systems to each other.
			35 text The paper shows that, by coding across many copies, they can transmit more than the two-bit total available without the shared entangled resource. Its protocol approaches a combined rate of about 2.311278 bits per copy. A concrete rate safely inside the proven region is 1.125 bits per sender, or 2.25 bits in total.
			36 text One receiver measures in the Bell basis and forwards the outcome. The other keeps their quantum systems and decodes them together across many copies. Keeping those systems quantum until the final decoding step is what allows a standard multiple-access coding theorem to be used.
			37 text The result answers the specified W-state classification question in an asymptotic unitary-encoding model. It does not say that a single copy beats two bits, or that 2.311278 is the best possible rate for all allowed protocols.
		38 container result
			39 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				40 text The precise result
			41 text For the symmetric state |W₄⟩ = (|1000⟩ + |0100⟩ + |0010⟩ + |0001⟩)/2 in the order A₁A₂B₁B₂, sender i initially holds Aᵢ and receiver i holds Bᵢ. Each sender transmits their encoded qubits noiselessly to their corresponding receiver.
			42 text With independent uniformly distributed messages, separate fixed codebooks and local unitary encoders, every nonnegative rate pair with R₁ < 3/2, R₂ < 3/2 and R₁ + R₂ < 3/2 + h₂(1/4) is achievable with vanishing uniform average decoding error.
			43 text The protocol’s achievable sum-rate supremum is 3/2 + h₂(1/4) ≈ 2.311278 bits per resource copy, giving a lower bound on the optimal LOCC capacity. In particular, R₁ = R₂ = 9/8 gives a total rate of 9/4 bits per copy.
			44 text Independent single-qubit Pauli operations suffice for encoding. A complete Bell measurement at the first receiver, classical forwarding and a collective local measurement at the second receiver implement the decoder; no extra shared entanglement or quantum communication between receivers is supplied.
			45 text This places the symmetric W₄ state in the LOCC-dense-codeable class of the stated original unitary-encoding model. The paper also connects its vanishing-error rate statement to the source’s asymptotic accessible-information formulation.
		46 container proof
			47 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				48 text Why the result holds
			49 text A paired Bell decomposition makes the effect of independent Pauli encodings explicit. The first receiver performs a complete Bell measurement and forwards every outcome, without postselecting successful events.
			50 text That measurement produces a finite classical–quantum multiple-access channel. The classical outcome and remaining quantum state are both held by the second receiver, so Winter’s ordinary single-output coding theorem applies to this channel.
			51 text The channel output entropy is 3/2 for each input pair. Averaging over either sender gives entropy 3, while averaging over both gives 3 + h₂(1/4). These yield the two individual bounds 3/2 and the sum bound 3/2 + h₂(1/4).
			52 text The many-copy decoder can be conditioned on the forwarded classical outcome string, leaving its quantum measurement entirely inside the second receiver’s laboratory. Separate Pauli codebooks implement independent senders.
			53 text The rate pair (9/8,9/8) lies strictly inside the achievable region and exceeds the two-bit classical benchmark. A Fano inequality transfers reliable message decoding to the accessible-information formulation of the original question.
		54 container limits
			55 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				56 text Scope and limitations
			57 text The rate is asymptotic over many independent resource copies and allows collective quantum decoding inside a receiver’s laboratory. It is not a one-copy advantage, a finite-block performance guarantee or an explicit practical family of codebooks.
			58 text The theorem gives a capacity lower bound. It does not prove that 3/2 + h₂(1/4) is the optimal LOCC capacity, characterize every achievable rate pair or rule out better protocols.
			59 text The error criterion is uniform average error over independent messages. Zero-error and maximal-error coding are not established.
			60 text The ownership and routing are fixed: each independent sender sends one qubit per copy to their own receiver, and receiver operations are local across A₁B₁ : A₂B₂. There is no sender message disclosure, feedback to senders, additional shared entanglement or quantum communication between receivers.
			61 text The two-bit calculation when both receivers immediately use Bell measurements concerns that particular one-copy measurement and input ensemble. It is not a converse bound for arbitrary one-copy LOCC strategies.
			62 text Bell forwarding and multiple-access dense coding have earlier precedents. The claimed addition is the specific retained-quantum-output channel and its achievable region for this W₄ problem, using Winter’s established coding theorem. This bounded comparison does not certify worldwide first priority, formal proof verification or completed human peer review.
