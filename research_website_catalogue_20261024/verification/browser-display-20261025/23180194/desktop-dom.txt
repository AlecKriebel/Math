		29 container abstract
			30 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				31 text Abstract
			32 text Let G be a locally compact second countable Hausdorff Abelian group, acting jointly measurably on an arbitrary measurable space. Let Q be sigma-finite and let ξ be a covariant locally finite Radon random measure, nonzero Q-almost everywhere. We prove that full ambient Q-invariance under every invariant, ξ-preserving, ξ-measurable Markov transport kernel implies the joint Mecke identity, and hence mass-stationarity. Together with known transport invariance this answers Last–Thorisson's Problem 7.3 in the stated setting. The argument uses oriented local symmetric transports away from periods of the measure and normalized period-subgroup transports on the remaining set. It requires neither a standard Borel ambient space nor a sigma-finite law of ξ, and imposes no finite-intensity assumption.
		33 container plain-language
			34 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				35 text The idea in plain English
			36 text Imagine a landscape whose mass sits at isolated points, spreads continuously, or does both. A mass-stationary origin is a location that is typical of that mass. This paper gives a way to recognize such an origin using randomized changes of viewpoint.
			37 text The allowed rules inspect the mass measure, choose a random displacement, and move the origin. Each rule must respect translations and redistribute the existing mass while preserving that same measure. If every allowed rule leaves the full environment’s governing measure unchanged, the origin is mass-stationary.
			38 text The interesting part is that the rules see only the mass, while the conclusion concerns the entire environment. For example, a translation can leave a periodic mass pattern unchanged while moving other information attached to the landscape. The proof accounts for that hidden information too.
		39 container result
			40 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				41 text The precise result
			42 text Let G be a locally compact second countable Hausdorff Abelian group acting jointly measurably on an arbitrary measurable space Ω. Let Q be a sigma-finite measure on Ω, and let ξ be a measurable, covariant, locally finite Radon random measure that is nonzero Q-almost everywhere. Q may be infinite; expectations below mean integration with respect to Q.
			43 text The test class consists of every invariant, pointwise ξ-preserving, ξ-measurable Markov transport kernel T. Markov means each row has mass one; ξ-measurable means the rule depends on the ambient state only through ξ. Pointwise preservation requires ∫ T(ω,s,B) ξω(ds) = ξω(B) for each state ω and measurable set B. The hypothesis is full ambient invariance: E_Q ∫ f(θ_tω) T(ω,0,dt) = E_Q f for every nonnegative measurable function f on Ω.
			44 text This hypothesis implies the joint Mecke identity, or Campbell reversal: E_Q ∫ g(θ_tω, −t) ξω(dt) = E_Q ∫ g(ω,t) ξω(dt) for every nonnegative measurable g on Ω × G. The classical Mecke/Palm and Palm/mass-stationarity equivalences then give mass-stationarity. Together with the known converse transport invariance, this answers Last and Thorisson’s Problem 7.3 from 2009 in the stated setting.
		45 container proof
			46 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				47 text Why the result holds
			48 text The proof separates displacements that change the mass measure from its periods, the translations that leave the measure unchanged. Away from periods, oriented local symmetric transports recover reversal on pieces that can be distinguished by observing the measure. Normalization and a holding probability turn these tests into genuine Markov kernels.
			49 text On the remaining set, normalized transports along the period subgroup recover reversal even when a shift changes hidden parts of the environment. The two arguments combine to give the joint identity for the whole ambient state. Standard Borel structure is used for the space of Radon measures; it is not imposed on Ω.
		50 container limits
			51 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				52 text Scope and limitations
			53 text The theorem requires an Abelian group, sigma-finite Q, and a locally finite Radon measure that is nonzero Q-almost everywhere. Atoms, diffuse or mixed mass, periodic measures, infinite total mass and infinite Q are included. There is no finite-intensity assumption and no assumption that the marginal law of ξ is sigma-finite. The theorem does not cover non-Abelian groups or the zero random measure.
			54 text The hypothesis tests the full ambient measure against every admissible Markov transport. Invariance of the law of ξ alone is not a substitute. A converse based only on deterministic transports, or on the narrower class arising from Cox allocations, is outside this result.
			55 text The new step is deriving joint Mecke reversal from measure-only Markov tests. The Palm equivalences and converse transport theorem are established inputs, and earlier discrete, aperiodic and positive-density cases receive credit in the paper. This page does not certify exhaustive novelty, historical first priority or the present status of related open questions.
