		28 container abstract
			29 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				30 text Abstract
			31 text For a fixed odd integer q, consider the nonnegative real homogeneous forms whose qth powers are sums of squares. We give a negative answer to the general-dimensional convexity question recorded by Reznick in 2023. For sextics and q = 3, the cone is nonconvex in the explicit finite dimension 3 · 10^62. For every odd q ≥ 3, it is nonconvex in some finite dimension depending on q. The proof combines a classical modified Motzkin form with an SOS cube, separation from the SOS cone, positivity of product truncated functionals, and elementary distinct-index counting. An explicit rational moment functional gives the displayed dimension; its 220 × 220 local positivity certificate can be checked without floating-point arithmetic. No ternary or smallest-dimension conclusion is made.
		32 container plain-language
			33 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				34 text The idea in plain English
			35 text A sum of squares is a particularly transparent way to show that a polynomial is never negative. Some nonnegative polynomials do not have such a representation themselves, but their cubes do. This paper asks whether averaging polynomials with that property preserves it.
			36 text The result is that it can fail. The construction takes many separate copies of one classical polynomial, each with a sum-of-squares cube. Their average remains nonnegative, yet its cube cannot be written as a sum of squares. The displayed example uses 3 · 10^62 variables: a finite existence result, not a small or practical example.
			37 text The same obstruction works for every fixed odd exponent at least three, in a finite number of variables that may depend on the exponent. Keeping the exponent fixed is essential. Earlier convexity results allow a larger odd power after the polynomials are added.
			38 text The classical polynomial and its sum-of-squares cube come from earlier work. This note uses them with established separation and product arguments to address the general convexity question recorded by Reznick in 2023. It does not settle the three-variable case or claim a verified first resolution.
		39 container result
			40 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				41 text The precise result
			42 text For real homogeneous forms f of positive even degree m, let C(n,m,q) consist of those that are nonnegative on R^n and whose fixed positive odd qth power is a finite sum of squares of real polynomials. Nonnegativity follows automatically from the power condition when q is odd. These sets are closed and stable under nonnegative scaling; addition is the issue under investigation. Here n counts variables.
			43 text Put N = 10^62 and use N disjoint triples of variables. For each triple define p_i = x_i^4 y_i^2 + x_i^2 y_i^4 + z_i^6 − x_i^2 y_i^2 z_i^2. Each p_i belongs to C(3N,6,3), but N^−1 times their sum does not. Thus C(3 · 10^62,6,3) is nonconvex.
			44 text For every fixed odd q ≥ 3, there is a finite n(q) for which C(n(q),6,q) is nonconvex. The construction may use a different separating functional and a different number of variable blocks for each q. Each counterexample also persists after adding unused variables.
			45 text For q = 3, the paper supplies a rational linear functional on polynomials of degree at most 18 in three variables. Its order-nine moment matrix has 220 monomials and splits into eight parity blocks. The accompanying archive contains the manuscript source, the exact local certificate and a standalone rational-arithmetic checker.
		46 container proof
			47 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				48 text Why the result holds
			49 text Start with the classical coefficient-1 modified Motzkin sextic p. It is nonnegative and not itself a sum of squares, while its cube has an explicit sum-of-squares identity. Multiplying that identity by the square of p^((q−3)/2) supplies an SOS representation at every odd q ≥ 3.
			50 text For a chosen exponent q, separate p from the finite-degree sum-of-squares cone. This gives a normalized linear functional L that is nonnegative on the necessary squares but has L(p) < 0. Taking finite products of this functional preserves positivity on squares involving separate variable blocks.
			51 text When the qth power of a sum of many disjoint copies is expanded, terms using q different copies give the leading contribution. Their coefficient is L(p)^q, which is negative because q is odd. Terms that repeat a copy grow at a lower rate, so the product functional eventually assigns a negative value to the entire power. That excludes every sum-of-squares representation.
			52 text For the cube, a displayed rational moment table makes the argument finite and explicit. With L(p) = −1 and the specified second and third moments, the resulting cubic expression is negative at N = 10^62. The local positivity certificate and the written product argument play separate roles.
		53 container limits
			54 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				55 text Scope and limitations
			56 text The explicit number of variables is a sufficient bound, with no claim of optimality. The theorem does not prove nonconvexity for three variables, for a prescribed small dimension, or in one common dimension that works for all odd exponents.
			57 text The result concerns sextics and a prescribed odd exponent. It is not a classification of all degrees, dimensions and exponents. The ordinary sum-of-squares cone at q = 1 remains convex.
			58 text Failure at one odd power does not say that every odd power fails. The established convexity of the union over odd exponents is consistent with the result, and no stubbornness claim is made for the constructed average.
			59 text The separating functional is positive on squares within its specified degree range. It is not integration against a positive measure: it assigns a negative value to a nonnegative polynomial. The enormous product matrix is handled by a finite algebraic argument, not materialized numerically.
			60 text The seed, its cube identity, SOS separation, product positivity and distinct-index counting have earlier sources. The contribution is their application to a fixed-exponent obstruction and the explicit finite cube certificate. The bounded literature comparison does not establish worldwide priority.
