		28 container abstract
			29 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				30 text Abstract
			31 text The union of complete affine lines tangent to three pairwise disjoint convex subsets of ℝ³ has Lebesgue outer measure zero. Tangency means meeting the set while lying in a supporting plane. The sets may be nonclosed, unbounded, lower dimensional or nonsmooth. For compact sets, two tangency constraints admit countably many Lipschitz two-parameter covers after contact localization. At almost every tritangent parameter, the transverse derivative at each of three distinct contacts has rank at most one. A quadratic determinant then vanishes identically along the line, and the area formula makes its swept locus null. Countably many compact caps give the arbitrary-set statement. This proves the measure-zero formulation of Goaoc's Conjecture 4 in Oberwolfach Report 44/2008; it does not prove the stronger countable-union-of-2-manifolds formulation.
		32 container plain-language
			33 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				34 text The idea in plain English
			35 text Take three convex sets in three-dimensional space, with no point belonging to more than one set. A convex set contains the straight segment between any two of its points. Here a tangent line must actually meet the set and lie in a supporting plane: a plane with the entire set on one side.
			36 text Now collect every line tangent to all three sets and extend each line infinitely in both directions. The paper proves that the whole collection occupies zero three-dimensional volume. This remains true for unbounded sets, sets missing some boundary points, nonsmooth shapes, and even sets lying in a plane or a line.
			37 text The striking point is the change from two sets to three. Common tangents to two disjoint rectangles can fill a region of positive volume. A third disjoint convex set imposes an additional contact condition that forces the volume swept out by the common tangents to vanish. Zero volume does not mean there are only finitely many lines.
		38 container result
			39 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				40 text The precise result
			41 text For every three pairwise disjoint convex subsets K₁, K₂ and K₃ of ℝ³, the union of all complete affine lines tangent to each set has three-dimensional Lebesgue outer measure zero. Equivalently, this union is contained in a Lebesgue-measurable null set. If any set is empty, the union is empty.
			42 text Tangency requires actual intersection with the set and containment of the line in a supporting plane. Contacts and supporting planes need not be unique, and a contact may be an entire segment. The sets need not be closed, bounded, full dimensional, strictly convex or smooth; their closures may intersect.
			43 text This is the measure-zero statement of Xavier Goaoc’s Conjecture 4 in Oberwolfach Report 44/2008, section 13, page 2552. The paper does not establish the separate countable-union-of-2-manifolds statement in Conjecture 3.
		44 container proof
			45 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				46 text Why the result holds
			47 text The proof first localizes actual contacts to countably many disjoint compact caps. After separating the lower-dimensional cases, it constructs countably many Lipschitz families of lines tangent to two compact convex sets, each described by two parameters. The construction uses support inequalities and a contraction argument, so it does not require smooth boundaries or differentiable support functions.
			48 text At almost every parameter corresponding to a common tangent, each contact forces a transverse derivative to have rank at most one. The determinant that measures the volume swept out along the line is a polynomial of degree at most two in the height coordinate. The three distinct contacts give it three distinct roots, so it vanishes identically. The area formula then gives zero volume. Separate cases handle points, segments and planar sets.
		49 container limits
			50 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				51 text Scope and limitations
			52 text Nullness is a statement about ordinary three-dimensional volume. The paper does not prove containment in countably many 2-manifolds, a Hausdorff-dimension bound of two, or a description by smooth surfaces.
			53 text Pairwise disjointness and supporting tangency are essential to the stated theorem. Lines that merely pass through the interiors of the sets are not covered. Contact with a closure alone is not enough when the original set omits that contact point.
			54 text For nonclosed inputs the union of tangent lines need not itself be closed. The outer-measure conclusion avoids assuming either input measurability or closedness of that union.
			55 text This is a geometric existence theorem, not an algorithm for enumerating tangents, a bound on their number, or a theorem in arbitrary ambient dimensions. The source package’s exact finite checks support algebraic and rational steps; they do not replace the proof for arbitrary convex sets.
			56 text The attribution to the 2008 conjecture identifies the historical question answered by the stated theorem. It is not a certification of first priority, an exhaustive literature search, or a claim that the question remained unresolved until this preprint.
