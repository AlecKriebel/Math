		28 container abstract
			29 heading Abstract, Value: 2
				30 text Abstract
			31 text We give an explicit family of counterexamples to the proposed lower bound ε(S,L;x) ≥ (2+|K_S²|^(1/4))⁻¹ for minimal complex projective surfaces. For every integer d ≥ 7, a classical plane-pencil construction followed by a double base change gives a smooth surface with ample canonical bundle, K_S² = 6(d−1)(d−3), and an ample integral line bundle L with L² = 6 and ε(S,L;x) ≤ 1/(d−1). Already d = 7 contradicts the bound. The pencil and covering ingredients are classical; historical priority has not been fully cleared. This unrefereed note used AI tools extensively and has not received human peer review.
		32 container plain-language
			33 heading The idea in plain English, Value: 2
				34 text The idea in plain English
			35 text A Seshadri constant measures how positive a line bundle is near one point of an algebraic surface. One way it becomes small is when a curve has little intersection with the line bundle but many branches through that point.
			36 text A proposed lower bound tied this local quantity to the surface’s canonical self-intersection. This paper gives a family that falls below that bound, while keeping the line bundle’s self-intersection equal to 6.
			37 text The surfaces are minimal in the algebraic-geometric sense: they contain no smooth rational curve of self-intersection −1. The small value occurs at a deliberately chosen singular point of a curve on the smooth surface.
		38 container result
			39 heading The precise result, Value: 2
				40 text The precise result
			41 text For every integer d ≥ 7, the paper constructs a smooth integral complex projective surface S with ample canonical bundle K_S, an ample integral line bundle L and a point x such that K_S² = 6(d−1)(d−3), L² = 6 and ε(S,L;x) ≤ 1/(d−1) < 1/(2+(K_S²)^(1/4)).
			42 text At d = 7, the displayed curve has L-degree 1 and multiplicity 6 at x. Thus ε(S,L;x) ≤ 1/6, while K_S² = 144 and the proposed lower bound is larger than 1/6.
			43 text The target is Szemberg’s arbitrary-point bound for minimal surfaces, restated as Conjecture 2 in Oberwolfach Report 21/2009 and Question 6.1.6 of the cited primer. The additive 2 is outside the fourth root.
		44 container proof
			45 heading Why the result holds, Value: 2
				46 text Why the result holds
			47 text The proof starts from Miranda’s classical pencil construction. A finite-degree argument chooses a plane pencil whose fibres are all integral and reduced. Blowing up its simple base points gives a surface Y with fibre F and a section E₁; the divisor A = 2F+E₁ is ample and has degree 1 on the chosen singular fibre.
			48 text A double base change, branched at four smooth fibres and away from the chosen singular fibre, produces a smooth surface with ample canonical bundle. The chosen fibre splits into two isomorphic copies, so its degree 1 and multiplicity d−1 survive on either copy.
			49 text Pulling back A gives L² = 6. The curve then supplies the stated upper bound directly from the definition of the Seshadri constant, and the strict comparison holds for every d ≥ 7.
		50 container limits
			51 heading Scope and limitations, Value: 2
				52 text Scope and limitations
			53 text Only an upper bound for ε(S,L;x) is asserted; the paper does not determine its exact value.
			54 text The surfaces vary with d and x is a special point. This does not establish vanishing of the infimum on one fixed complex surface, or contradict very-general-point, Picard-rank-one, canonical or adjoint polarization bounds.
			55 text The second pencil member and branch values are chosen through nonempty-open existence arguments; the paper does not list explicit coefficients for every choice.
			56 text The pencil, polarization and covering methods are classical. Historical priority and the problem’s status immediately before this note have not been fully established.
			57 text This is an unrefereed preprint made with extensive AI assistance. Internal AI checks and finite symbolic controls do not constitute human peer review or a proof-assistant certificate.
