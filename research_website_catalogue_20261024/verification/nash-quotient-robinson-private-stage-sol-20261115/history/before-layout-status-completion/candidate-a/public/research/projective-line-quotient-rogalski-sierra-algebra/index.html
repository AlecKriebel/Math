A projective-line quotient of the Rogalski–Sierra algebra

Alec Kriebel · ORCID 0009-0001-9320-500X · 16 October 2026 · Version 1.0 · Unrefereed preprint

Original abstract

For the six-relation presentation R(α, β) in Rogalski and Sierra’s 2010 report and 2012 article, the linear ideal (x₃−x₁, x₄−x₂) has a projective-line twisted homogeneous coordinate ring as its exact quotient. It is a nonzero homogeneous completely prime ideal strictly below the augmentation ideal. This gives negative answers to both printed assertions: uniqueness of the nontrivial graded prime and absence of nontrivial maps to twisted homogeneous coordinate rings. The calculation works over every field whenever β²≠1, hence for all the prescribed algebraically independent parameters. The classical twist machinery is credited; historical priority of this explicit quotient remains unresolved.

What the quotient says

A noncommutative algebra can change substantially when two pairs of generators are identified. This note studies one specific identification in the Rogalski–Sierra algebra. The resulting quotient is a familiar twisted coordinate ring of the projective line, with its multiplication written explicitly.

The proof identifies the entire kernel, so the map gives a nonzero completely prime ideal as well as an infinite-dimensional quotient. These supply counterexamples to two literal printed assertions about the original presentation. The construction uses classical machinery, and its historical priority remains unresolved.

The complete result

Let k be any field and let α, β∈k. Give each generator degree one and define the connected graded associative algebra R=R(α,β)=k⟨x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄⟩/(f₁,…,f₆) by the following six quadratic relations. Products are ordered; the second left factor in both f₅ and f₆ is x₄.

f₁=x₁(αx₁−x₃)+x₃(x₁−αx₃); f₂=x₁(αx₂−x₄)+x₃(x₂−αx₄); f₃=x₂(αx₁−x₃)+x₄(x₁−αx₃); f₄=x₂(αx₂−x₄)+x₄(x₂−αx₄); f₅=x₁(βx₁−x₂)+x₄(x₁−βx₂); f₆=x₁(βx₃−x₄)+x₄(x₃−βx₄).

Theorem 1. For every field k, every α∈k and every β∈k with β²≠1, let I=(x₃−x₁,x₄−x₂) be the two-sided ideal and let σ[s:t]=[s+βt:βs+t] on P¹_k. Then R/I≅k⟨X,Y⟩/(βX²−XY+YX−βY²)≅B(P¹_k,O(1),σ). The map sends x₁,x₃ to s and x₂,x₄ to t, and its kernel is exactly I.

The ideal is homogeneous and completely prime, with 0≠I⊊R₊, where R₊ is the augmentation ideal consisting of positive-degree elements. The quotient is a domain for arbitrary elements, so I is both an ordinary prime and a graded prime. Its degree-n dimension is n+1 for every n≥0, its Hilbert series is (1−z)⁻², and its Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is 2.

The twisted homogeneous coordinate ring uses positive pullbacks: Lₙ=O(1)⊗σ⁎O(1)⊗⋯⊗(σⁿ⁻¹)⁎O(1), Bₙ=H⁰(P¹_k,Lₙ), with L₀=O. For f of degree m, multiplication is f⋆g=f⊗(σᵐ)⁎g. The fixed linear lift gives Lₙ≅O(n), and O(1) is σ-ample.

For algebraically independent α,β over the prime subfield of an uncountable algebraically closed field, β²≠1 automatically. The nontrivial prime I contradicts the printed claim that R₊ is the only nontrivial graded prime. The unital graded surjection onto this infinite-dimensional curve ring contradicts the printed claim that no nontrivial homomorphism to a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring exists. Those assertions impose no injectivity, birationality or lower bound on image dimension.

Why the kernel is exact

Substituting x₃=x₁=X and x₄=x₂=Y sends the six defining relations to 0,0,0,0,Q,Q, where Q=βX²−XY+YX−βY². The universal property of free-algebra quotients gives exactly the displayed two-generator quotient. Showing that a relation vanishes in a target would by itself give only a map; the rest of the argument proves there is no additional kernel.

On k[s,t], use the invertible linear automorphism θ(s)=s+βt and θ(t)=βs+t. Its determinant is 1−β². The positive graded twist f⋆g=fθᵐ(g), for f of degree m, is associative, generated in degree one and a domain. It has the same graded vector spaces as the polynomial plane. Its multiplication agrees with the stated projective-line section multiplication.

The relation Q vanishes in this twist, giving a surjection from the two-generator algebra. With U=X−Y and V=Y, the relation becomes VU=((1−β)/(1+β))UV−(β/(1+β))U². Every replacement decreases fixed-length lexicographic order with V>U. Thus the n+1 words UⁱVⁿ⁻ⁱ span degree n. The target already has dimension n+1, so the surjection is an isomorphism in every degree. This proves the exact kernel without assuming confluence or extrapolating from finite rank tables.

There are no linear relations among the four generators of R, so x₃−x₁ is nonzero. The image of x₁ is nonzero, which puts I strictly below R₊. The target domain gives complete primality. Summing the dimensions n+1 gives cumulative dimension (n+1)(n+2)/2 and the stated growth.

Scope and boundaries

The all-field statement concerns the displayed six-relation presentation. In characteristic different from two, the geometric parameter changes c=(ϑ−1)/(ϑ+1) and d=(ρ−1)/(ρ+1) preserve the independent-parameter setting. In characteristic two these fractions collapse to 1 when defined. The independent-parameter characteristic-two presentation is not thereby identified with that geometric family, and the latter’s noetherianity or GK-dimension-four conclusions are not transferred through the collapse.

The condition β²≠1 is essential: at β=±1, Q factors as (X+Y)(X−Y) or −(X−Y)(X+Y), giving nonzero linear zero divisors. The allowed value β=0 gives the commutative polynomial plane. The change U=X−Y,V=Y and the proof do not require division by 2 or infinite automorphism order.

This is a quotient map with a nonzero kernel. It does not claim an injective or birational map, a classification of all primes, or equality of the graded quotient rings of the source surface algebra and a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring. The separate same-graded-quotient-ring surface obstruction is unaffected by this curve quotient.

Historical priority remains unresolved. Comparisons use selected primary passages and specified preprint versions; the original Artin–Van den Bergh body, some final journal editions, parts of the Campbell thesis and broader older or unindexed literature were not all inspected. Classical context and priority

The two assertions and the literal six-relation presentation occur in Sierra’s 2010 Oberwolfach contribution with Rogalski and in Rogalski–Sierra’s 2012 article. Their specific formulas, including the x₄ factors in f₅ and f₆, are part of the statement addressed here. The contribution recorded by this note is the explicit substitution and exact projective-line quotient.

Twisted homogeneous coordinate rings are the classical construction of Artin and Van den Bergh. Polynomial graded twists, the associated quantum/Jordan or Ore-plane descriptions and degree-reduction arguments are also classical. These ingredients and the Rogalski–Zhang canonical-map machinery retain their prior credit.

Strong noetherianity is a sufficient hypothesis of Rogalski–Zhang’s main canonical-map theorem. Their broader Theorem 4.4 provides another route using subexponential growth and eventual stabilization of point-module truncation maps. Strong noetherianity is therefore not the only route in that work. The required stabilization hypotheses have not been established here for the source algebra merely by citation.

Nevins–Sierra’s point-tail and geometric results have their own moduli and dimension hypotheses, and a universal canonical factor need not itself be a twisted homogeneous coordinate ring. Repetitions of the contrary no-map assertion are attributed to the inspected arXiv v1 and v2 preprints, rather than an unread final journal body. Selected Campbell thesis results on infinitesimal deformations and function skew fields concern a distinct deformation family; they are credited without claiming an exhaustive thesis or literature search.
