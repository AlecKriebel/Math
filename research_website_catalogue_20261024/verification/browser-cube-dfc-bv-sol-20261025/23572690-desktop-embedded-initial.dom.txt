    - heading "Abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Let k be any field of characteristic zero and let 0≠h∈k[x]. For A_h=k⟨x,Y⟩/(Yx−xY−h(x)), the ordinary Hochschild cohomology, with its canonical cup product and Gerstenhaber bracket, admits a Batalin–Vilkovisky generator if and only if h is squarefree. In the squarefree case its bracket is zero. A repeated irreducible factor produces a cup-zero, noncommuting pair of degree-one classes, and a central annihilator forces every putative generator to vanish on degree one. Thus in that case no degree-minus-one bracket generator exists, even without requiring its square to be zero. The argument uses the published resolution and derivation structure of this family, and answers the concrete-family question recorded by Lopes in Oberwolfach Report 46/2023, p. 2702, Question (2).
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Hochschild cohomology packages information about an algebra and its possible deformations. It comes with both a product and a bracket. A Batalin–Vilkovisky, or BV, generator is a single operator that recovers the bracket from the product and whose square is zero.
    - paragraph: "This paper considers a family of algebras governed by one polynomial h. The classification is simple to state: a BV generator exists exactly when h has no repeated irreducible factor. Nonzero constant polynomials are included in the positive case."
    - paragraph: The negative case is stronger than failure to find a suitable formula. A repeated factor produces cohomology classes whose product is zero but whose bracket is nonzero. Together with a central annihilating class, these relations rule out every degree-minus-one bracket generator, even if its square is not required to vanish.
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: For any characteristic-zero field k and nonzero h∈k[x], let A_h=k⟨x,Y⟩/(Yx−xY−h(x)). Theorem 1 states that h being squarefree, the canonical Gerstenhaber bracket on ordinary HH•(A_h,A_h) being zero, existence of a bracket generator, and existence of a BV generator are equivalent.
    - paragraph: For squarefree h, the zero operator is a BV generator. If h has a repeated irreducible factor over k, no bracket generator exists. The argument works over the original field and does not require the polynomial to split into linear factors.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Published results describe the algebra’s resolution and derivations. The paper compares that resolution with normalized bars to identify the canonical cup product needed for the obstruction.
    - paragraph: When d=gcd(h,h′) is nonconstant, a central degree-one class annihilates every other degree-one class under the cup product. This forces any possible bracket generator to vanish on degree one. An explicit cup-zero pair with nonzero bracket then gives a contradiction.
    - paragraph: When d=1, the second and higher cohomology groups vanish and the remaining degree-one derivations commute. The entire bracket is therefore zero.
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The classification concerns this one-variable polynomial family over fields of characteristic zero, with its ordinary Hochschild cohomology and canonical product and bracket. It does not classify arbitrary Weyl subalgebras, positive-characteristic cases, twisted-coefficient variants, or Poisson analogues.
    - paragraph: For h≠0, A_h is the subalgebra of the first Weyl algebra generated by x and yh(x). The literal embedded h=0 case instead gives k[x], which the paper treats separately using Δ(f∂x)=−f′. Setting h=0 in the abstract two-generator presentation gives a different algebra, k[x,Y].
    - paragraph: The result uses established resolution and derivation theorems. The accompanying finite exact computations support selected identities; they are not proof-assistant certification or a replacement for the universal argument.
    - paragraph: Earlier work already ruled out bracket generators for other ordinary Hochschild cohomology algebras. This paper gives a classification for the stated family, with bounded historical coverage. It makes no first-discovery claim.
