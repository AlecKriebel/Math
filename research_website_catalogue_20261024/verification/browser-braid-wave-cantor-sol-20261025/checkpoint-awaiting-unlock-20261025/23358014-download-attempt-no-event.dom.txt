    - heading "Abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "We give an explicit counterexample to retaining ordinary critical-cone strict positivity and strong control-L² quadratic growth after removing quadratic control regularization from the energy-critical quintic-wave problem of Kunisch and Meinlschmidt. On the three-dimensional unit ball, zero is the unique global optimizer for a sufficiently small positive pointwise control cap. Its ordinary critical cone is {0}, so the strict condition is vacuous, but feasible controls converging in L∞ have objective gap o(‖u‖²L²). Nearby radial terminal targets admit global optimizers, and every such optimizer has L² displacement divided by target displacement tending to infinity. Local L¹ quadratic growth survives. The construction realizes the established concentration and noncoercivity mechanism in the actual nonlinear source model, using classical wave estimates."
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: In optimal control, a control is an input chosen to make a system behave as desired. This paper studies a nonlinear wave equation, where the objective balances a desired final wave profile against penalties on the state and on the amount of control used.
    - paragraph: A useful stability property says that moving away from the best control raises the objective by at least a fixed multiple of the squared distance. In this example, doing nothing is the unique global optimum, but increasingly short, small pulses have an objective penalty much smaller than their squared L² size. Uniqueness therefore does not supply that stronger growth property.
    - paragraph: "The striking feature is that the ordinary critical-cone test still holds: its cone contains only zero, so there are no nonzero directions on which the strict test can fail. The concentrating pulses reveal what this test misses. A weaker L¹ quadratic-growth estimate survives, and the paper also bounds how optimizers respond to nearby terminal targets."
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The model is the real defocusing quintic wave equation yₜₜ − Δy + y⁵ = u on the three-dimensional unit ball B, with zero position and velocity initially, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and final time 1. Let H=L²(B). Controls lie in L²(0,1;H) and obey ‖u(t)‖H ≤ ρ almost everywhere in time. There is ρ*>0 such that the conclusions hold for every fixed 0<ρ≤ρ* and every fixed finite ν≥0.
    - paragraph: "The objective Jd,ν retains one half of the squared terminal-position tracking error, one quarter of the fourth power of the L⁴(0,1;L¹²(B)) state norm, and the L¹(0,1;H) sparse-control penalty: γ=β₁=1. Only the quadratic control penalty is removed, β₂=0. The optional terminal-velocity term is ν‖yₜ(1)‖²H⁻¹(B)/2."
    - paragraph: "For target d=πe, with e the normalized first radial Dirichlet eigenfunction, zero is the unique global optimizer over all admissible controls. With A=‖u‖L¹(0,1;H), the objective gap Δ(u)=Jπe,ν(u)−Jπe,ν(0) satisfies Δ(u)≥A³/(12ρ²). The ordinary critical cone is {0}, so the strict substitute second-variation condition is vacuous. The cap threshold and this lower bound can be chosen independently of ν."
    - paragraph: "The actual nonlinear feasible pulses uₕ=he·1{|t−1/2|<h}, for 0<h<min(ρ,1/2), satisfy ‖uₕ‖L∞(0,1;H)=h, ‖uₕ‖²L²(0,1;H)=2h³ and Δ(uₕ)=(π²/3+2/π²)h⁴+O(h⁶). Strong control-L² quadratic growth therefore fails in both L² and L∞ neighborhoods of zero."
    - paragraph: Global optimizers exist for every terminal target d∈H in this fixed small-cap setting. Along dε=(π+ε)e, as ε decreases to zero through positive values, every choice of global optimizers satisfies ‖uε‖L²/ε→∞. No uniqueness of perturbed optimizers is required.
    - paragraph: "Corollary 3 retains local weaker-norm growth: Δ(u)≥‖u‖²L¹/(4π²) for every admissible control with sufficiently small L¹ norm. For an arbitrary nearby target πe+w, put ε=‖w‖H. Every global optimizer, compared with the fixed zero optimizer, satisfies ‖u‖L¹=O(ε) and ‖u‖L²=O(√ε). The constants and this upper-bound neighborhood can be chosen uniformly over all finite ν≥0."
    - paragraph: On the positive radial ray w=εe, every global optimizer satisfies ‖uε‖L¹=Θ(ε) and cε³ᐟ⁴≤‖uε‖L²≤C√ε for sufficiently small ε. The lower-comparison neighborhood may depend on the fixed ν. These bounds do not assert a sharp L² response exponent.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Classical energy and Strichartz estimates make the nonlinear state close to the linear wave for small control mass: the state is O(‖u‖L¹), and the nonlinear remainder is O(‖u‖⁵L¹). This estimate is in a compatible energy-and-Strichartz state space; it does not assume that the nonlinear solution stays in one eigenmode."
    - paragraph: The first eigenmode gives an explicit sine kernel for the terminal linear response. At the midpoint in time, the tracking gain exactly meets the sparse-control threshold. A cap-constrained moment estimate proves global optimality of zero, while pulses concentrating at that midpoint make the quadratic-growth quotient vanish.
    - paragraph: The terminal tracking square also provides a weaker L¹ quadratic lower bound. A separate pulse comparison for perturbed targets yields the lower response bounds for every optimizer. These arguments use analytic estimates; the accompanying finite algebra checks are not a numerical solution of the nonlinear PDE.
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: This is a counterexample to retaining the ordinary critical-cone implication unchanged when the quadratic control penalty is removed. It does not rule out adapted sufficient conditions, enlarged cones, nondegeneracy assumptions or growth in weaker norms.
    - paragraph: The ordinary critical cone is zero, so strict positivity there is vacuous. The result should not be described as a counterexample with a uniformly positive Hessian on every control direction.
    - paragraph: The control cap is pointwise in time on the spatial L² norm. It is not a pointwise bound on |u(t,x)|. Here L¹ and L∞ mean time norms with values in L²(B), while control L² is the time-space L² norm. The state penalty remains present; “unregularized” refers to removing the quadratic control term.
    - paragraph: The construction uses the three-dimensional unit ball, the defocusing quintic, zero initial data, a small positive cap and a particular target. It does not assert the same failure for every domain, every target, arbitrary large caps, Neumann conditions or boundary control.
    - paragraph: The cap and base-target lower bounds can be chosen independently of ν. Upper target-response estimates and their neighborhood are uniform in finite ν; the radial lower-comparison neighborhood may depend on the fixed ν. No assertion concerns ν = ∞.
    - paragraph: The response estimates compare optimizers with the fixed zero optimizer. They do not prove perturbed-optimizer uniqueness, a pairwise Lipschitz selection, L∞ convergence of perturbed optimizers, or a sharp exponent for the L² response.
    - paragraph: The paper gives no certified numerical PDE constant or numerical value of the small-cap threshold. Its finite checks do not replace the analytic proof. The historical question and earlier mechanisms are attributed without a worldwide-firstness or present-day-openness claim.
    - paragraph: The failure is specific to the selected target. For target d=0 in the same model, J0,ν(u)≥‖u‖L¹≥‖u‖²L²/ρ, so strong quadratic growth does hold. The theorem does not assert failure for all targets or all control caps.
