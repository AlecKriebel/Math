                                               Abstract
         For the annulus 1 < |z| < e, we exhibit a normalized minimal Martin kernel that is strictly
     below the envelope of normalized positive harmonic functions at every point of a punctured
     neighborhood of the base point. Four explicit positive harmonic competitors give a uniform
     ﬁrst-order gap in every direction. The result disproves the extension to multiply connected
     domains of the equality-along-a-Green-line property in Hayman and Lingham’s Problem 7.42,
     attributed to Arsove and Johnson. We give direct constructions of the Green and Martin kernels
     and a proof of minimality. The broader negative conclusion also has a simpler punctured-disc
     derivation from classical results; our theorem gives a smooth-annulus construction. OpenAI
     tools were used extensively in developing and independently checking the argument. This note
     has not undergone human peer review or formal proof-assistant veriﬁcation.


1    The question and the counterexample
For a plane Green domain D and a ∈ D, deﬁne

                       Ha (z) = sup{h(z) : h > 0 harmonic on D, h(a) ≤ 1}.

A ﬁnite Harnack chain shows that Ha (z) < ∞ for every z ∈ D; also Ha (a) = 1. In the disc centered
at a, a normalized boundary Poisson kernel equals Ha on the radius toward its pole. Problem 7.42
of Hayman and Lingham [1] asks whether the corresponding property, equality along a Green line
issuing from a for each minimal Martin kernel, persists in multiply connected domains. We use
this equality-along-the-line meaning, supplied by the disc example, rather than equality only at a
single point. The latter holds automatically at a.

Theorem 1. Let D = {z ∈ C : 1 < |z| < e}, a = e1/2 and ξ = −1. The minimal Martin kernel Kξ
at the inner-boundary point ξ, normalized by Kξ (a) = 1, satisﬁes

                                  Kξ (z) < Ha (z)       (z ∈ U \ {a})

for some neighborhood U of a in D.

    Thus the kernel cannot equal the envelope along any nontrivial curve issuing from a, and in
particular along a Green line. The theorem does not address isolated contact farther from a. A
single such kernel in this smooth doubly connected Green domain negates the proposed universal
extension.

4    Attribution, scope and veriﬁcation
The question is Problem 7.42 in the inspected 2018 version of [1], printed pages 173–174. Its
update reports no progress known to the editors then; this does not certify current worldwide
openness. The strip kernels, conformal coordinates, reﬂection and maximum principle are classical.
This note gives an explicit counterexample in a smooth annulus with two nondegenerate boundary
components.
    Related metric work includes Herron [2], Bear and Smith [3], and Shiga [4]. Shiga gives a
Martin-kernel formulation of Harnack distance and equality results with the hyperbolic metric.
Such symmetric metric statements do not determine contact of each prescribed kernel with the
one-sided envelope used here. The accompanying dated priority audit records inspected sources
and full-text gaps. No ﬁrst historical resolution is claimed; failure to identify a predecessor is not
proof of priority.
    For domains with a polar puncture, a negative example follows by an elementary deduction from
classical positive-harmonic representations. Köhn [5], p. 63, identiﬁes the minimal functions on the
punctured disc as disc Poisson kernels and − log |z|; see also [3, Proposition 2.6]. At a = 1/2, the
normalized kernel with pole −1 has gradient strictly inside the triangle spanned by the gradients
of the normalized kernels with poles ±i and the normalized logarithmic function. A common
Taylor remainder gives local isolation; the supplement provides the calculation and its scope. This
deduction neither establishes prior recognition of the Green-line consequence nor gives the smooth-
annulus theorem.
    The supplement contains authored proof source, independent AI reviews and reproducibility
checks. The analytic argument proves the theorem; integrity and numerical checks do not replace
it. OpenAI tools were used extensively in solving, drafting and adversarial veriﬁcation. This
preprint is unrefereed, without completed human peer review or proof-assistant certiﬁcation.
