An explicit counterexample to three-summand Kato-kernel splitting

Alec Kriebel · Independent researcher · 17 October 2026 · Native version 1.0.0 · Unrefereed preprint

ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X · Zenodo record and DOI

Combining field extensions can kill a cohomology class even when the class cannot be assembled from the kernels of the individual extensions. This note gives an explicit characteristic-two example where that obstruction survives the addition of a third, bilinear Pfister function-field kernel.

A class outside all three kernels together

Write Kn(E/F) for the kernel of the restriction map H2n+1(F) → H2n+1(E). Laghribi’s Question 2 in Oberwolfach Report 5/2016 asks whether the kernel for the composite is always the sum

Kn(LK(B)/F) = Kn(L/F) + Kn(K/F) + Kn(F(B)/F).

Here L/F is finite purely inseparable, K/F is separable quadratic, and B is an anisotropic bilinear Pfister form that remains anisotropic over L.

For the counterexample, let b, z, s and t be algebraically independent over the two-element field and set

F = 𝔽2(b,z,s,t),   L = F(β),   β4 = b,

K = F(θ),   θ2 + θ = s−1,   B = ⟨1,t⟩b.

The extension L/F has degree four and exponent two. The form B remains anisotropic over L. At n = 1, consider the class ξ in H22(F) represented by

s−4 · bz2/(1 + bz2) · dlog z.

The class ξ dies over LK, and hence over LK(B), but does not belong to K1(L/F) + K1(K/F) + K1(F(B)/F). This is a counterexample to the proposed general equality at its allowed original index n = 1. It does not assert failure at every index.

What the binary function field means

F(B) is the function field of the projective quasilinear quadric defined by v ↦ B(v,v). This convention includes binary forms: for B = ⟨1,t⟩b, the equation X2 + tY2 = 0 in the projective line defines an integral, zero-dimensional quadric. Its function field is

F(B) = F(γ),   γ2 = t.

The example therefore uses the binary case allowed by the original question. The independent variable t matters: using z in that Pfister slot instead would kill dz and absorb the displayed class.

Why the third summand does not remove the obstruction

Under the identification H22(E) ≅ Br(E)[2], write [u,v) for the characteristic-two quaternion class. Over L, put f = 1 + β2z. The cohomological relations give ξL = [s−1,f), which vanishes after adjoining θ.

Suppose ξ were a sum of classes from the three individual kernels. Every class split by K/F has the form [s−1,c) for some arbitrary rational element c ∈ F×. Restrict to M = LF(B) = 𝔽2(β,z,s,γ). Both the L-kernel term and the Pfister-kernel term vanish there. The proposed decomposition would force f/c to be a norm from M(θ) to M.

Complete at s, with k′ = 𝔽2(β,z,γ) and D = k′((s)). For π = θ−1, the norm formulas are

N(π) = s,   N(A + Bπ) = A2 + sAB + sB2.

The two possible leading valuations have opposite parity; the mixed term has larger valuation. Consequently every unit norm has square residue in k′. For any rational c = src0(1 + O(s)), with r any integer and c0 ∈ 𝔽2(b,z,t)×, multiplication by the norm sr forces

(1 + β2z)/c0 ∈ k′×2.

This square condition is impossible. Set R = 𝔽2(β4,z2,γ2) and S = k′2. Every such c0 has a unique expression u + vz with u,v ∈ R, including arbitrary rational denominators. Since z ∉ S, comparing coefficients in 1 + β2z = q(u + vz), q ∈ S×, gives qu = 1 and qv = β2. Thus β2 would lie in R, contradicting its odd valuation against the even valuations of nonzero elements of R.

The obstruction covers every rational parameter c, including negative s-valuations. It is a universal norm-and-residue argument, rather than a finite test of selected polynomials.

The degree-one boundary is positive

The same proposed equality holds at n = 0, in H21, for all of the stated data with B a positive-fold bilinear Pfister form. The composite kernel is exactly the K/F kernel; the purely inseparable and Pfister kernels are zero. Artin–Schreier roots cannot first appear in the purely inseparable or rational extensions used in the projective-quadric description.

The negative result is therefore a specific n = 1, exponent-two example. The positive exponent-one regime is treated in the earlier work of Aravire, Laghribi and O’Ryan.

Prior work and scope

The mixed class and its vanishing mechanism are a specialization of Aravire–Laghribi–O’Ryan’s Definition 1.3 and Lemma 3.1 in their 15 August 2019 author manuscript, published in Journal of Algebra 542 (2020). This note gives a direct proof that the class remains outside the sum after the additional independent Pfister kernel is included.

No firstness claim is made for the class, example or deduction. Bounded source comparisons do not certify historical priority, causal independence or continued worldwide openness. Original manuscript abstract

Question 2 in Laghribi’s contribution to Oberwolfach Report 5/2016 asks whether a mixed Kato kernel is the sum of the kernels of a finite purely inseparable extension, a separable quadratic extension, and a bilinear Pfister function field. We give an explicit negative instance in H₂², using an exponent-two extension and a binary bilinear Pfister form that remains anisotropic. The mixed class is exactly a specialization of the generators of Aravire–Laghribi–O’Ryan (2019/2020). We prove directly that it remains outside the sum after the additional Pfister kernel is included, by a universal norm and residue obstruction. The original degree-one cohomology case is separately positive. No firstness claim is made.
