                                               Abstract
        The rank completion printed in Ohtsuki’s Conjecture 7.30 cannot interpolate the standard
     uniﬁed sln invariants of an integral homology sphere with nonzero Casson invariant. A ﬁnite
     quotient forces its ﬁrst Taylor coeﬃcients to be aﬃne in n, contrary to the classical cubic
     coeﬃcient formula. The speciﬁed Poincaré sphere gives the incompatible values 0, −6, −24.
     This is a negative answer to the literal displayed statement, obtained from established quantum-
     invariant results.

The transition maps are quotient maps. This R is the ordinary integral Habiro ring: q is already a
unit in every polynomial quotient, since the deﬁning polynomial has constant term ±1. For F ∈ S,
evaluation at t = q n is deﬁned through any coordinate m ≥ n; compatibility makes the value
independent of that choice.
   Conjecture 7.30 asks for an invariant FM ∈ S of every integral homology 3-sphere M such that

             FM (q n ) = In (M )   (n ≥ 1),     In (M ) = I sln (M ) (n ≥ 2),   I1 (M ) = 1.

Thus n denotes matrix size, not the Lie rank n − 1. Its ﬁxed-Lie-algebra premise, Conjecture 7.29,
is supplied by the Habiro–Lê uniﬁcation theorem [3, Theorem 1.1].

Theorem 1. Use the coeﬃcient ring and standard invariant normalization above. If λC (M ) 6= 0,
there is no FM ∈ S with these evaluations. Here λC is the Casson–Walker invariant in Lê’s Lescop
normalization. In particular, the orientation of the Poincaré homology sphere given by −1 surgery
on the left-handed trefoil is a counterexample to the printed conjecture.


2    A ﬁnite-jet constraint
Let D = Z[x]/(x2 ). The substitution q 7→ 1 + x, q −1 7→ 1 − x kills (q − 1)(q 2 − 1). Projection to
the second coordinate therefore deﬁnes a ring map T1 : R → D. No injectivity assertion for Taylor
expansion is needed.

Lemma 1. For F ∈ S, write T1 (F (q n )) = un + vn x for n = 1, 2, 3. Then u1 = u2 = u3 and
v3 − 2v2 + v1 = 0. Consequently, F (q) = 1 implies v3 = 2v2 .

Proof. The third coordinate of F has a representative P (t) = a + bt + ct2 , with a, b, c ∈ R, by
division by the monic polynomial E3 . Evaluation on this quotient is well-deﬁned at q, q 2 , q 3 . Write
T1 (a) = a0 + a1 x, and similarly for b, c. Since (1 + x)k = 1 + kx in D,
                                                              (                                   )
                     T1 (P (q n )) = a0 + b0 + c0 + a1 + b1 + c1 + n(b0 + 2c0 ) x.

The coeﬃcient of x is aﬃne in n. If F (q) = 1, its value at n = 1 is zero, which gives the last
assertion.

    Only one ﬁnite interpolation quotient and one ﬁnite coeﬃcient quotient occur in this argument;
it diﬀerentiates no inﬁnite sum.


3    The quantum coeﬃcient and counterexample
Habiro–Lê identify the Taylor series of the uniﬁed invariant with the Ohtsuki series [3, Proposi-
tion 1.9]; their Proposition 1.2 also gives the projective specialization. Lê’s published ﬁrst-coeﬃcient
formula [2, p. 821], in the variable x = q − 1, specializes on an integral homology sphere to

                            [x] T1 (In (M )) = n(n2 − 1)λC (M ),                         n ≥ 2.       (1)

The factor involving |H1 (M ; Z)| is 1 here. These sources use a common type-A parameter: roots
have squared length 2, and q = v 2 = exp(h) [3, §3A1–2]. Changing from h to q − 1 does not change
a ﬁrst coeﬃcient. Thus (1) concerns the invariants in the printed question, with no rank-dependent
rescaling.
    If FM existed, Lemma 1 would give

                      0 = v3 − 2v2 + v1 = 24λC (M ) − 2 · 6λC (M ) = 12λC (M ).

This proves the nonexistence assertion in Theorem 1.

4    Scope, attribution, and veriﬁcation
The obstruction concerns the displayed ordinary completion. It gives no existence criterion when
λC (M ) = 0. Omitting the assigned value at n = 1 does not remove the obstruction: the same ﬁnite-
quotient argument at sizes 2, 3, 4 gives a second diﬀerence of 18λC (M ). Poles in q − 1, a diﬀerent
completion, or rank-dependent normalization change the question; no corrected formulation is
proposed here.
     The cubic type-A behavior is classical, already explicit in Lê–Murakami–Ohtsuki [5, Proposi-
tion 5.3 and Remark 5.4] and in the all-rank free-energy formalism [6, §3]. The coeﬃcient and
uniﬁcation inputs above are credited prior results. Fixed-rank uniﬁcation, link-interpolation stabil-
ity, and enlarged knot-color completions [7, 8, 9] do not by themselves assert the printed S-valued
homology-sphere interpolation. No novelty, ﬁrstness, or continued-openness claim is made. The
accompanying bounded priority audit records its source-access and coverage gaps. The collection’s
volume year is 2002; the publisher records its release on 1 June 2004.
     Source-free veriﬁcation materials provide exact arithmetic checks, preserved original ﬁles, in-
dependent adversarial reports, and reproduction instructions. Portable ﬁnite-checker stages were
reproduced on macOS; the legacy Linux/bubblewrap whole-ﬁlesystem read-only replay was not
reproduced there. The cited topology theorems remain mathematical inputs. AI tools were used
extensively in developing and checking this work. This note is unrefereed; no independent human
peer review or formal proof-assistant certiﬁcation is asserted.
