A first-order obstruction to Habiro's rank interpolation

By Alec Kriebel, independent researcher · 16 October 2026 · Version 1.0 · Unrefereed preprint

Rank interpolation asks for one expression recovering the standard unified quantum invariant of a homology sphere at every matrix size. For the ordinary integral completion displayed in Ohtsuki’s Conjecture 7.30, this note gives a negative answer whenever the sphere has nonzero Casson invariant. A finite quotient already forces a relation that the classical first-coefficient formula violates.

The literal completion and the theorem

The coefficient ring is the ordinary integral Habiro ring, and the rank variable is completed using the displayed products:

R = lim←m ℤ[q, q−1] / (∏j=1m (qj − 1)).

Em(t) = ∏j=1m (t − qj), and S = lim←m R[t] / (Em(t)).

The transition maps are quotient maps. For F in S, evaluation at t = qn uses any coordinate m ≥ n. Here n is the matrix size for sln; its Lie rank is n − 1. All sizes use the same parameter q and the standard invariant normalization, with I1(M) assigned the value 1.

For every integral homology 3-sphere M with nonzero Casson invariant, there is no FM in S satisfying FM(qn) = Islₙ(M) for all integers n ≥ 2 and FM(q) = 1. The Casson–Walker invariant λC is taken in Lê’s stated Lescop normalization. This is an obstruction to the literal displayed ordinary completion and normalization.

A contradiction in the first coefficient

Work only to first order in x = q − 1, so x² = 0. The substitutions q = 1 + x and q−1 = 1 − x define a map from R to ℤ[x]/(x²). In the third interpolation coordinate, a representative of F is a polynomial a + bt + ct². Its first coefficient after substituting t = qn has the form A + nB: it is affine, or a straight line, in n.

Write those first coefficients as vn. At sizes 1, 2 and 3, any interpolating F must therefore satisfy

v3 − 2v2 + v1 = 0.

The established quantum-invariant coefficient is instead

vn = n(n² − 1)λC(M), for n ≥ 2.

The common type-A convention has roots of squared length 2 and q = v² = exp(h). Replacing h with q − 1 leaves the first coefficient unchanged; no rank-dependent rescaling is introduced. Since the assigned size-1 invariant is 1, its first coefficient is zero. Sizes 1, 2 and 3 then give a second difference of 12λC(M), which cannot vanish when λC(M) ≠ 0. The argument uses finite coefficient and interpolation quotients, without Taylor injectivity or differentiation of an infinite sum.

Omitting size 1 does not remove the obstruction: sizes 2, 3 and 4 give the incompatible second difference 18λC(M).

An explicit oriented witness

Take the Poincaré homology sphere obtained by −1 surgery on the left-handed trefoil. The source’s unified series has constant term 1 and first coefficient −6 at size 2; its remaining terms contribute nothing in the fixed first-order quotient. The coefficient formula fixes λC = −1 in the stated convention.

The second difference is −24 − 2(−6) + 0 = −12, giving the concrete contradiction.

Scope and established inputs

This is a credited deduction from established quantum-invariant results and finite algebra. Lê’s 2000 coefficient formula supplies the first coefficient, and Habiro–Lê’s 2016 unification theorem and Taylor identification connect it to the standard unified invariants. The classical cubic type-A behavior is already explicit in Lê–Murakami–Ohtsuki (1998) and Garoufalidis–Lê–Mariño (2008). The sphere-series locator in Habiro’s work refers specifically to arXiv:math/0605314v1, §16.1, equation (16.1).

The result gives no existence criterion when λC(M) = 0 and proposes no corrected formulation. Allowing poles in q − 1, changing the completion or introducing rank-dependent normalization changes the question. Fixed-Lie unification, link-interpolation stability and enlarged knot-color Newton rings, including the works of Beliakova–Gorsky, Fang–Zhou and Fang cited in the manuscript, do not by themselves assert this S-valued homology-sphere interpolation.

No novelty, firstness, present-openness or exhaustive absence-of-earlier-resolution claim is made. Ohtsuki’s collection has volume year 2002; its publisher record gives release on 1 June 2004. Those are different bibliographic dates.

Original manuscript abstract

The rank completion printed in Ohtsuki's Conjecture 7.30 cannot interpolate the standard unified sl_n invariants of an integral homology sphere with nonzero Casson invariant. A finite quotient forces its first Taylor coefficients to be affine in n, contrary to the classical cubic coefficient formula. The specified Poincaré sphere gives the incompatible values 0,−6,−24. This is a negative answer to the literal displayed statement, obtained from established quantum-invariant results.
