Hypersingular Riesz-energy constants on surfaces from triangular-lattice universal optimality

Alec Kriebel · 6 October 2026 · Version 1.0.0 · Unrefereed, AI-assisted preprint

How much energy is needed to place many repelling points on a surface? For an inverse-power interaction with a fixed exponent s > 2, this note identifies the exact leading constant. It is the full interaction sum around one point of a triangular lattice scaled to have one point per unit area.

The result is an attributed consequence of OpenAI's triangular-lattice universal-energy theorem and established finite-energy and surface asymptotics. The note gives a self-contained account of the passage between those settings, keeping the pair-counting, density and area conventions explicit.

Original abstract

We deduce the exact two-dimensional hypersingular minimal Riesz-energy constant for every real s>2 from OpenAI's recently released triangular-lattice universal-optimality theorem. The constant is the Epstein sum of the covolume-one triangular lattice. An explicit gap-periodization argument relates the finite square problem to the source theorem's centered-disk lower energy, with an inter-box error of order N^(1−s/2) for each fixed gap parameter. A lattice crop gives the matching upper bound. The corrected Hardin–Saff universality theorem then gives the ordered-pair leading asymptotic on every smooth compact embedded surface of positive area, including surfaces with smooth boundary, for ambient Euclidean distance. This resolves the two-dimensional s>2 case of the explicit lattice-constant conjecture recorded by Brauchart, Hardin and Saff. The universal-optimality breakthrough, finite-to-infinite reduction and manifold theorem are inherited results; this note gives a self-contained account of the known passage and records its planar surface consequence.

The energy and the exact constant

Fix a real s > 2 and let Es(K,N) be the smallest energy of N points in a compact set K:

Es(K,N) = min ∑i≠j |xi−xj|−s.

The sum counts ordered pairs: each pair contributes twice. Coincident points have infinite energy. Distances are measured by the surrounding Euclidean space; on a sphere they are straight chord lengths.

Put b = √3/2 and define the triangular lattice

Λ = b−1/2{(j+k/2, kb) : j,k ∈ ℤ},   Zs = ∑v∈Λ∖{0} |v|−s.

Its fundamental cell has area one, which is the meaning of covolume one. The series Zs is the Epstein sum in the distance-power convention used here; it converges for s > 2. For the unit square, the precise conclusion is

Cs,2 = limN→∞ Es([0,1]²,N)/N1+s/2 = Zs.

This determines the limiting minimum value. It does not describe the local arrangement of every minimizing configuration.

From the square to a surface

Let M be a smooth compact embedded two-dimensional surface in Euclidean space, with positive area A. Smooth boundary is allowed. For each fixed s > 2,

Es(M,N) ∼ Zs A−s/2 N1+s/2.

The symbol ∼ means that the ratio of the two sides tends to one as N grows. Area is ordinary surface area: the Hausdorff measure is normalized to agree with planar area. Enlarging a surface by a factor r multiplies its area by r² and its energy coefficient by r−s, as the formula requires.

The unit sphere has area 4π, so its leading coefficient is Zs/(4π)s/2, using chordal distance. The passage to surfaces uses the corrected Hardin–Saff universality theorem. Compact smooth surfaces with smooth boundary satisfy its finite bi-Lipschitz chart hypotheses.

Why the infinite-plane theorem applies

OpenAI's theorem compares the triangular lattice with all locally finite planar sets of centered disk density one, for nonnegative completely monotone functions of squared distance. The lower energy is an ordered-pair sum inside a centered disk, divided by its point count, followed by a lower limit as the disk grows. The inverse-power interaction here is g(t) = t−s/2, which fits that potential class.

To connect this infinite statement with a finite square, scale an arbitrary N-point configuration by √N and repeat it periodically, leaving a positive gap between neighboring copies. For each fixed gap parameter ε > 0, interactions between different copies contribute at most

8ε−s ζR(s−1) N1−s/2

per point, where ζR is the Riemann zeta function. The estimate includes interactions of a point with its own translated copies and needs no separation assumption on the original finite points. A density rescaling then allows the infinite-plane lower bound to be applied.

The order of limits matters: first take the disk limit for a fixed periodic configuration, then let N grow at fixed ε, and finally let ε decrease to zero. The inter-copy error vanishes because s > 2. Selecting exactly N lattice points from a slightly oversized triangular-lattice crop gives the matching upper bound.

The finite-to-periodic and thermodynamic reductions are established machinery of Hardin–Saff–Simanek and Hardin–Leblé–Saff–Serfaty, with periodic averaging also credited to Cohn–Kumar. The note's explicit gap argument records that known passage in the conventions needed here.

Attribution, verification and scope

The substantive universal-optimality input belongs to OpenAI. Hardin–Saff supply the area-universality theorem, including its correction. The exact two-dimensional constant was conjectured for the sphere by Kuijlaars–Saff and appears as the dimension-two case of Brauchart–Hardin–Saff's lattice-constant conjecture. This note records the resulting consequence and supplies an explicit account of the reductions; it claims neither an independent base breakthrough nor first public priority.

The package contains the proof, dependency notes and finite verification materials. Finite certificates check the stated finite inequalities; the analytic arguments supply the infinite interpolation, global signs, density comparison and limiting steps. A complete independent Lean/Mathlib kernel build was not reproduced, and formal verification of this consequence is not claimed.

The claim concerns each fixed exponent s > 2 and the leading minimum energy. It does not establish microscopic crystallization, uniqueness of finite minimizers, a second-order term, a new critical-exponent result, a joint uniform limit, or constants in other dimensions. Distances are ambient Euclidean distances throughout.
