                                  Negative answers to the printed
                                   totally mixed face questions
                                                     Alec Kriebel∗

                                          October 17, 2026       Version 1.0.0



                                                       Abstract
              For two polytopes, retain the faces of their Minkowski sum whose two canonical summands
          have positive dimensions and whose dimension is the sum of those dimensions. This is the
          deﬁnition in Payne’s printed Oberwolfach question. Let D be the standard four-simplex and
          I = [0, 1]. For the full-dimensional lattice polytopes D × I and (−D) × I in R5 , the qualifying-
          face order complex is a disjoint union of two connected pure graphs, each with 50 vertices and 60
          edges. Its rational Betti numbers are (2, 22, 0, . . .). Thus both printed assertions, shellability and
          the homology of a wedge of three-spheres, fail. The shared interval factor deliberately produces
          a nongeneric example. The argument concerns the printed additive-dimension deﬁnition; it does
          not settle shellability for the diﬀerent mixed faces used in stable tropical intersections.


1        The exact question and the example
Payne’s question [1, p. 2464, Problem 3] concerns polytopes P1 , P2 ⊂ Rn and those faces F = F1 +F2
satisfying
                        dim Fi > 0 (i = 1, 2),     dim F = dim F1 + dim F2 .                     (1)
It asks whether their inclusion poset is shellable and whether its realization has the rational ho-
mology of a wedge of (n − 2)-spheres. There is no stated general-position assumption. We use the
standard order complex: vertices are qualifying faces and simplices are nonempty strict chains. No
artiﬁcial extrema are added.
    For a face exposed by a functional u, its canonical summands are the faces Piu exposed by the
same u. The standard uniqueness theorem for Minkowski-sum faces appears, for example, in [2,
Lemma 4 of the cited arXiv version]. It also removes an existential ambiguity in (1): if nonempty
summand faces Gi have G1 + G2 = P1u + P2u , exposure ﬁrst forces Gi ⊆ Piu . For every other
functional, the two nonnegative deﬁcits between their support functions sum to zero. Each deﬁcit
is therefore zero, so Gi = Piu .
Theorem 1. Let D = conv(0, e1 , e2 , e3 , e4 ) ⊂ R4 and I = [0, 1]. For P1 = D×I and P2 = (−D)×I,
let T be the inclusion poset selected by (1). Its order complex is a disjoint union K ⊔ K, where K
is a connected pure graph with 50 vertices and 60 edges. In particular,

                    H0 (|T |; Q) = Q2 ,       H1 (|T |; Q) = Q22 ,       Hj (|T |; Q) = 0    (j ≥ 2).

The poset is not shellable, under either pure or standard nonpure shellability, and its realization
does not have the rational homology asked for in the printed question.

realizations nor low-dimensional sphere conventions. The simplex-diﬀerence geometry is classical

2    Complete face calculation
Write v0 = 0, vi = ei , and E = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}. Every nonempty proper face of D − D is uniquely of
the form

        F (A, B) = conv{vi : i ∈ A} + conv{−vj : j ∈ B},         ∅ ̸= A, B ⊂ E,     A ∩ B = ∅.

Indeed, for a nonzero functional u, take A and B to be its maximum and minimum index sets.
They are disjoint because the vertices aﬃnely span R4 . Conversely, assign values 1, −1, 0 on A, B
and the remainder, and subtract the value at v0 to obtain a linear functional with exactly these
extrema.
   The two summand direction spaces intersect only at zero: a vector in their intersection gives
an aﬃne dependence on the disjoint vertex sets, with coeﬃcient sum zero on each set. Aﬃne
independence makes every coeﬃcient zero. Consequently,

            dim F (A, B) = |A| + |B| − 2,       dim Du = |A| − 1,     dim(−D)u = |B| − 1.            (2)

Moreover,
                            F (A, B) ⊆ F (C, L)     ⇐⇒     A ⊆ C, B ⊆ L.                             (3)
For the nontrivial direction, evaluate every vi − vj , i ∈ A, j ∈ B, against a functional exposing
F (C, L). Attaining the maximum diﬀerence forces i ∈ C, j ∈ L. The reverse direction follows
directly from convex hulls.                                                           ( )( )
     Thus the base qualiﬁers have sizes (|A|, |B|) = (2, 2), (2, 3), (3, 2). There are 52 32 = 30 minimal
                               ( )
elements of type (2, 2) and 2 52 = 20 maximal elements. Each maximum has three lower minima,
and each minimum has two upper maxima, obtained by placing its one unused index on either side.
There are no three-element strict chains. The base order complex K is therefore a pure graph on
50 vertices with 60 edges.
     To check connectedness, suppress the degree-two minimal vertices. The resulting graph has
signed two-subsets A+ , A− of E as vertices, with A+ adjacent to B − exactly when A ∩ B is empty.
Two distinct intersecting same-sign subsets have the opposite-sign complement of their union as a
common neighbor. If two same-sign subsets are disjoint, a two-subset containing one element of each
intersects both; apply the preceding path twice. All vertices of a ﬁxed sign therefore connect, and
every vertex has a neighbor of the other sign. The graph, and hence its subdivision K, is connected.
Contracting a spanning tree shows that K is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of 60 − 50 + 1 = 11
circles.
     Now P1 + P2 = (D − D) × [0, 2]. A functional (u, t) with t > 0 or t < 0 exposes an upper or
lower endpoint face; its summand dimensions and qualiﬁcation are exactly the base ones. If t = 0,
write a, b for the base summand dimensions and c for the base sum dimension. The two summands
then contain the interval factor, so their dimensions sum to a + b + 2, while their sum has dimension
c + 1 ≤ a + b + 1. No such face passes (1). If u = 0, t ̸= 0, the additional horizontal end face has
dimension 4 but both summands have dimension 4, so it also fails. The whole sum has dimensions
(5, 5, 5) and fails; the empty face fails positivity.
     These cases exhaust all faces. The qualifying poset is precisely two endpoint copies of the base
poset, with no cross-comparisons. This proves the asserted decomposition and homology.

   In any proposed shelling, the ﬁrst edge from the component diﬀerent from the initial edge has
empty intersection with the preceding facets. That intersection has dimension −1, whereas a new
edge requires a pure zero-dimensional intersection [4, Deﬁnition 2.1]. This rules out both shellability
conventions. Finally, a wedge of three-spheres, including the zero-fold point, is connected and has
no ﬁrst homology. The same obstruction would persist if one replaced the printed sphere dimension
by n − 3.


3    Related results, scope, and veriﬁcation
For comparison, D3 and −D3 in R3 yield just six incomparable qualifying faces: the index pairs
partition four vertices into two pairs. Their order complex is six points. This refutes the printed
wedge-of-circles homology assertion but is shellable in dimension zero. Scaled opposite tetrahedra
and their six-ray geometry already occur in Sturmfels–Tevelev [3, Examples 1.3 and 4.10]; this
lower-dimensional control is not presented as new. The ﬁve-dimensional example is needed here for
nonshellability.
   The term mixed has several diﬀerent deﬁnitions. In the published Adiprasito et al. paper [5,
Section 1.2], totally mixed faces require facets of the summands. That counting theorem is not
used. The public 2020 manuscript underlying Adiprasito’s 2024 article [6, p. 2, Corollary 1.3 of the
public manuscript] instead selects faces satisfying
                            (∑      )
                      dim         Fi ≥ |J| for every nonempty index subset J.
                            i∈J

For two summands this is broader than (1). For instance, a proper vertical face F (A, B) × [0, 2]
with |A| = |B| = 2 has summand dimensions 2, 2 and sum dimension 3. It meets that lower-bound
criterion but fails additivity. Such vertical faces are absent from our poset. The counterexample
therefore does not contradict the diﬀerent stable-intersection theorem, or resolve its shellability
question. Related tropical link obstructions occur in Hiatt–Simpson–Wang–Wu’s revised 2026
construction [7, Section 8]. Its Schön variety in (C∗ )8 is a diﬀerent object; no identiﬁcation with
this two-summand exact face poset is supplied or assumed here.
    The supplement records a bounded primary-source priority audit, including current versions
and related tropical results. No earlier counterexample or theorem settling nonshellability for this
exact poset was identiﬁed. Such a result, together with the classical homology antecedent, would
already settle both assertions. This is not an exhaustive literature or ﬁrst-ever priority certiﬁcate.
The 2024 journal metadata for [6] was veriﬁed, but its paywalled ﬁnal body was not inspected; the
deﬁnition comparison above is explicitly with the public 2020 manuscript.
    Two independently authored standard-library programs accompany the analytic proof. One
reconstructs facets and their intersection lattice from integer coordinates, all 543 nonempty sum
faces, and all 8649 summand-face pairs. The other independently constructs the order complex and
its signed rational boundaries, obtaining boundary rank 98. Both run with explicit exceptions in
ordinary and optimized Python; positive and adverse controls are included. These computations
are supplementary, and the proof above requires no program.
