A sharp dyadic threshold in a characteristic-two random-cone construction

Alec Kriebel · ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
6 October 2026 · Native record version 1.0 · Unrefereed preprint

A finite geometry can be made smaller without changing the recipe it supports. This note identifies the precise dyadic threshold for exponential squared-word decay in one random-cone model: the projective-plane order can be reduced from 128 to 32. It also carries the full existential construction through at that order.

The foundational group construction is OpenAI’s. Its October 4 manuscript supplies the torsion-free group-algebra construction and its probabilistic and geometric framework. This note’s contribution is the exact threshold for the unchanged seven-extra model and the parameter analysis that carries that construction to order 32. The embedding, idempotent and module mechanisms below are classical or inherited.

Original abstract

OpenAI's October 4 construction uses a projective plane of order q=128 and seven extra letters to obtain a finitely presented torsion-free group with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex and a characteristic-two group algebra that is not directly finite. We determine the least dyadic order q≥4 at which its unchanged squared turn weights have exponential word-mass decay: it is q=32. A three-class reduction and exact rational certificates prove contraction at 32 and exponential growth at 4,8,16. Checking the remaining parameter-dependent estimates yields the same construction on a 532-edge rose. This is a threshold for this specified model, rather than an optimum among all group-algebra examples. Classical HNN embedding also gives a two-generator realization. We spell out the inherited scalar idempotent and nonzero absorbed cyclic projective module, which remain valid over every characteristic-two field. The construction is existential; no numerical matching or group-ring multiplication certificate is claimed.

What the threshold measures

Take q=2k with k≥2 and v=q2+q+1 ordinary letters, the points of the projective plane PG(2,q). Add seven extra letters. Each extra letter is paired with a distinct ordinary inverse letter; the remaining ordinary letters are paired among themselves. This is the fixed signed alphabet T of the construction.

For consecutive letters t,u, write inv(t) for t’s inverse letter and put p=(q+1)/v. The original turn weights are:

For a word W=t1⋯th, multiply these turn weights to obtain P(W). They are turn-frequency weights, not probabilities assigned to words. For every allowed inverse pairing, the squared-turn matrix M=(w(t,u)2) has spectral radius below one first at the dyadic order q=32, among q≥4.

At q=32, for every h≥1:   ∑W∈ThP(W)2 ≤ (5376/5)(987/1000)h−1.

At q=4,8,16, the same squared-word sum instead has a positive lower bound Cq(503/500)h−1. Thus the decay fails for those smaller dyadic orders in this model. This does not rule out good matchings, idempotents, a different type design or another method at smaller parameters.

From order 32 to a group

At q=32 there are 1057 ordinary letters and seven extras, giving 1064 directed letters and a rose with 532 geometric edges. The parameter analysis carries the entire random-cone argument through: balanced outgoing labels, large-girth conditioned matchings, component diameter, bounded-path estimates, planar arrangement exclusion, asphericity and protection of the distinguished roots.

The resulting existential construction gives a finitely presented torsion-free group G, generated by at most 532 elements, with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex K(G,1). Its characteristic-two group algebra contains finite scalar sums a,b,c satisfying

ab=1,   ac=0,   c≠0   in 𝔽2[G].

These relations force ba≠1. A classifying complex here has fundamental group G and contractible universal cover. The 532 count describes this particular rose construction; it is not a smallest possible generating set for all examples.

Classical embedding and ring consequences

A classical HNN embedding places G inside a two-generator group H while retaining a finite two-dimensional classifying complex and torsion-freeness. The same scalar relations survive under the induced group-algebra injection. The note spells out this specialization; it does not claim a new embedding mechanism.

Set e=1−ba in 𝔽2[H]. Then e is a scalar idempotent with e2=e and e≠0,1. For every characteristic-two field K, the natural coefficient embedding keeps e nontrivial in S=K[H]. The right ideal P=eS is a nonzero cyclic projective right module, and

SS ≅ SS ⊕ P,   [P]=0 in K0(S).

The zero K0 class does not make P the zero module or make K0(S) vanish. It records a failure of cancellation for finitely generated projective modules. These idempotent and module consequences were already recorded in the author’s earlier public triage and are included for interpretation, not offered as a new discovery.

A torsion-free group generated by at most one element is trivial or infinite cyclic, and its group algebra over a field is a domain. This explains the stated two-generator minimum for such an idempotent example. It does not turn the order-32 threshold into an optimum across all construction models.

Scope and attribution

The order-32 result is specific to the unchanged seven-extra types, inverse pairing rule, turn weights and squared-word-decay argument. The construction is existential: it selects a sufficiently large matching outcome without supplying a numerical size threshold, selected matching, relator list, reduced group-ring support or target multiplication certificate.

The source’s central group construction and breakthrough belong to OpenAI, with its September 23 zero-divisor construction also part of the background. HNN embedding is classical. The defect-idempotent reduction and right-module splitting are classical algebraic mechanisms. No first-priority claim is made.

The conclusions concern characteristic-two fields. No characteristic-zero or odd-characteristic conclusion, reduced group C*-algebra projection, full proof-assistant formalization or conventional human refereeing is claimed.
