A Martin kernel with isolated Harnack contact in an annulus

Alec Kriebel · Independent researcher · 12 October 2026 · Version 1.0.0 · Unrefereed preprint

ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X · Zenodo record and DOI

Positive harmonic functions obey Harnack bounds: fixing their value at one point limits how large they can become elsewhere. This note gives an explicit boundary kernel on a smooth annulus that meets the resulting envelope only at the base point within a small neighborhood. It answers the equality-along-a-Green-line extension posed in Hayman and Lingham’s Problem 7.42.

Original manuscript abstract

For the annulus 1 < |z| < e, we exhibit a normalized minimal Martin kernel that is strictly below the envelope of normalized positive harmonic functions at every point of a punctured neighborhood of the base point. Four explicit positive harmonic competitors give a uniform first-order gap in every direction. The result disproves the extension to multiply connected domains of the equality-along-a-Green-line property in Hayman and Lingham's Problem 7.42, attributed to Arsove and Johnson. We give direct constructions of the Green and Martin kernels and a proof of minimality. The broader negative conclusion also has a simpler punctured-disc derivation from classical results; our theorem gives a smooth-annulus construction. OpenAI tools were used extensively in developing and independently checking the argument. This note has not undergone human peer review or formal proof-assistant verification.

The annulus and the isolated contact

Fix the annulus D = {z ∈ ℂ : 1 < |z| < e}, the base point a = e1/2, and the inner-boundary point ξ = −1. For positive harmonic functions normalized at a, define the one-sided Harnack envelope

Ha(z) = sup{h(z) : h > 0 harmonic on D, h(a) ≤ 1}.

A Harnack chain makes this supremum finite at every interior point, and Ha(a) = 1. Let Kξ be the minimal Martin kernel at ξ, normalized by Kξ(a) = 1. The theorem proves that there is a neighborhood U of a in D such that

Kξ(z) < Ha(z) for every z ∈ U ∖ {a}.

The normalized kernel therefore cannot agree with the envelope along any nontrivial curve issuing from a, including a Green line. Equality at a is automatic. The theorem does not exclude isolated contact farther away, and it does not assert the same local behavior for every kernel or every annulus.

Why four harmonic competitors are enough

Use logarithmic coordinates z = ex+iy, with 0 < x < 1 and y taken modulo 2π. The annulus becomes a periodic cylinder, with base A = (1/2, 0). Periodizing the classical strip Poisson density constructs the kernel explicitly:

p(x, t) = sin(πx) / [2(cosh(πt) − cos(πx))],
P(x, y) = ∑k∈ℤ p(x, y − π + 2πk),
K(x, y) = P(x, y) / P(A).

The paper constructs the Green function directly and identifies its boundary Martin limit, including tangential approaches. Odd harmonic reflection near the boundary pole and the maximum principle establish minimality.

For the local gap, set b = 1/[2 cosh(1/2)]. Four explicit competitors are

hx,±(x, y) = 1 ± (x − 1/2),
hy,±(x, y) = 1 ± b sin(y) cosh(x − 1/2).

Each is positive and harmonic throughout the cylinder and equals 1 at A. Their gradients point in the two coordinate directions. The kernel gradient is ∇K(A) = (−c, 0), with 0 < c < 8/83 < 1/10, while b > 1/4. These exact bounds give, for every nonzero direction d,

maxj (∇hj(A) − ∇K(A)) · d > |d|/10.

A common quadratic Taylor remainder for this finite family turns the uniform first-order gap into strict inequality at every sufficiently near point other than A. Since the envelope dominates each competitor, it also dominates their maximum. The argument does not assume that the envelope is smooth, and the all-direction estimate is analytic rather than a claim extrapolated from sampled directions.

The question, classical context and priority limits

The target is the extension of equality along a Green line issuing from the base point in Hayman and Lingham’s 2018 Problem 7.42, attributed to Arsove and Johnson. In a disc centered at the base, a normalized Poisson kernel equals the envelope along the radius toward its pole. Equality at only the base point would hold by normalization and is not the extension addressed here.

The smooth annulus has two nondegenerate boundary components. Strip kernels, conformal coordinates, harmonic reflection and the maximum principle are classical tools. Herron, Bear and Smith, and Shiga provide related Harnack-metric results. Symmetric metric identities do not by themselves decide where each prescribed kernel touches this one-sided envelope.

The broader negative conclusion also has a simpler punctured-disc derivation from classical positive-harmonic representations. Köhn’s 1966 classification and the representation discussed by Bear and Smith supply that context. This deduction does not establish earlier recognition of the Green-line consequence and does not yield the smooth-annulus theorem.

No first historical resolution or certification of current worldwide openness is claimed. The 2018 problem-list update reports what its editors knew then. A bounded source search and its full-text gaps do not establish global priority.
