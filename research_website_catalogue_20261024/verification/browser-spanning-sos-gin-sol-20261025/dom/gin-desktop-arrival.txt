    - heading "Original abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: For the characteristic-zero arrangement defined by Q=xy(x-y)(x-2y)zw(x+z+w), the degree-reverse-lexicographic generic initial ideal of the ambient Jacobian ideal has pure-second-variable cutoff 8 and a degree-7 minimal generator whose largest variable is the third. This explicitly contradicts the second-variable bound of Bigatti–Palezzato–Torielli, also stated in OWR Report 5/2021. We prove the generic sectional dimensions on a nonempty open set of compatible flags, using scalar polar syzygies and Hilbert–Burch, and give exact rational checks. The arrangement is a classical parallel connection; the methods and the valid three-variable arrangement theorem are prior work. Historical novelty and independent discovery are not established. This unrefereed note was developed with extensive AI assistance and has not undergone conventional human peer review.
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: A generic initial ideal records the monomial structure of polynomial equations after a general linear change of coordinates. This note gives a seven-hyperplane arrangement whose Jacobian ideal violates a proposed bound linking a second-variable exponent to the degrees of generators involving the third variable.
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Let K be any field of characteristic zero, S=K[x,y,z,w], and Q=xy(x−y)(x−2y)zw(x+z+w). Form the ambient Jacobian ideal J=(∂Q/∂x,∂Q/∂y,∂Q/∂z,∂Q/∂w) using all four partial derivatives before taking any section.
    - paragraph: "For the degree-reverse-lexicographic generic initial ideal B with variable order x₁>x₂>x₃>x₄, the pure-second-variable cutoff is p=min{d≥0:x₂ᵈ∈B}=8. The full four-variable ideal B has a degree-seven minimal generator in K[x₁,x₂,x₃] divisible by x₃. Its largest variable is therefore x₃, and its degree is strictly below p."
    - paragraph: This contradicts the general-dimensional second-variable bound in Bigatti–Palezzato–Torielli's Conjecture 5.7 and the matching Oberwolfach statement. In the former notation, the proposed threshold is d₀+1=p. The theorem holds over every characteristic-zero field, without assuming the field is algebraically closed.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The proof studies compatible binary and ternary linear sections while keeping all four ambient polars. Reducing a linear polar relation modulo each distinct factor turns it into scalar proportionality equations with a trace condition. On the stated open conditions, there are no ternary linear syzygies and exactly one binary linear syzygy.
    - paragraph: The binary polars have no common projective zero, so the ideal has height two. The classical Hilbert–Burch theorem forces syzygy coefficient degrees 1,2,3. The resulting Hilbert values are H₂(6,7,8)=(3,1,0) and H₃(5,6,7)=(21,24,24).
    - paragraph: An explicit compatible flag shows that the required conditions hold on a nonempty open set. Intersecting that set with the generic-initial open set justifies the generic sectional values. Favorable ranks in one coordinate system alone would not establish this step.
    - paragraph: "Strong stability and the reverse-lexicographic section identity give the cutoff eight. Multiplication by x₃ in the first-three-variable quotient has kernel dimensions zero at degree six and one at degree seven. A minimal-divisor argument then gives the degree-seven generator and proves its minimality in the full ring: a divisor of a monomial in the first three variables cannot introduce x₄."
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The ternary section retains four ambient partial derivatives. It differs from the three-generated Jacobian ideal obtained by restricting the arrangement and then differentiating in three variables. The valid three-variable theorem of Marchesi–Palezzato–Torielli remains intact.
    - paragraph: The arrangement is a classical parallel connection of U_(2,4) and U_(3,4) along the shared factor x, as in Falk–Proudfoot. The logarithmic derivation mechanism, Hilbert–Burch theorem, Galligo generic-initial theory, Conca section identity and sectional counting methods are prior work. Tohaneanu explicitly credits Orlik–Terao for the derivation mechanism.
    - paragraph: Migliore–Nagel's applicable family theorem already gives the resolution 0→S(−7)³⊕S(−9)→S(−6)⁵→J^(top)→0, where J^(top) is the intersection of the codimension-two primary components of J. This is substantive prior information about the family. That five-generated ideal differs from the four-generated ambient Jacobian ideal studied here.
    - paragraph: The full text of Ilardi's 2018 article was unavailable for the priority comparison, so a relevant earlier theorem or family is not excluded. Historical parameter families and code were not exhausted. Firstness, independent discovery, a new arrangement or general method, and present worldwide open status are not established.
    - paragraph: The result does not compute the full generic initial ideal, specify an exponent vector for the degree-seven generator, prove a positive-characteristic extension, or claim the smallest possible number of hyperplanes. Exact checks at specified rational coordinates supplement the open-set proof; they are not full-generic-initial or priority certificates.
