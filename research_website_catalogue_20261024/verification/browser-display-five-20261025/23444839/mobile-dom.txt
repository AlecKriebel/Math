    - heading "Abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: We construct a finite two-dimensional abstract simplicial complex that is doubly Cohen–Macaulay over every field of characteristic zero, but contains no two-dimensional homology-sphere subcomplex over any field. This gives a negative answer to Nevo's question asking whether every doubly Cohen–Macaulay complex admits a decomposition into homology spheres with specified overlap properties. We use a finite quotient of an affine building, with all links equal to the incidence graph of the projective plane of order eleven. A direct spectral estimate controls the links after any vertex deletion; a weighted cocycle argument verifies the required homology vanishing. The sphere obstruction is an Euler-characteristic count. The construction and the spectral and curvature mechanisms build on established work, credited below.
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: A simplicial complex is built from vertices, edges, triangles and their higher-dimensional counterparts. Doubly Cohen–Macaulay complexes satisfy strong homological conditions that survive removal of any single vertex.
    - paragraph: Nevo asked whether every such complex could be assembled from homology-sphere pieces with specified overlap properties. This paper gives a two-dimensional counterexample with no permitted sphere piece at all. The obstruction therefore appears before the overlap requirements can even be considered.
    - paragraph: The construction uses a finite quotient of a well-studied geometric object called an affine building. Earlier building theory, spectral estimates and a curvature argument supply the main tools. The contribution is their application to the precise decomposition question, including the conditions after every vertex deletion.
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: There exists a finite pure two-dimensional abstract simplicial complex K that is doubly Cohen–Macaulay over every field of characteristic zero and contains no two-dimensional homology-sphere subcomplex over any field.
    - paragraph: Here a homology sphere must have the homology of a sphere both globally and in every face link. The excluded sphere subcomplex need not be induced. Having sphere homology only globally is a weaker condition and is not the definition used in the theorem.
    - paragraph: The example gives a negative answer to Nevo’s homology-sphere replacement question, recorded as Problem 8 in the 2005 Oberwolfach report and Problem 3.7 in the 2008 paper. It does not contradict Nevo’s earlier decomposition into minimal cycle complexes, which are a broader class of pieces.
    - paragraph: Every vertex link of K is the incidence graph of the projective plane of order eleven. After a vertex deletion, the relevant links are either that graph or the graph with one vertex removed. Both have normalized Laplacian gap strictly greater than one half.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The construction starts with the vertex-regular affine-building lattice and faithful polynomial matrix representation of Lubotzky, Samuels and Vishne. Reducing those matrices modulo t⁹ gives a finite quotient. A degree bound excludes short identifications, so the quotient retains its projective-plane links.
    - paragraph: The projective-plane incidence matrix satisfies an exact Gram identity. This controls the spectral gap even after one link vertex is removed. A finite weighted-cocycle argument then gives the required first-homology vanishing for K and every vertex deletion; connectivity and the remaining face-link conditions complete the doubly Cohen–Macaulay property.
    - paragraph: Any homology-sphere subcomplex would have exactly two triangles on each edge and a cycle around each vertex. Those cycles have length at least six because they lie in the projective-plane links. Counting vertices, edges and triangles forces Euler characteristic at most zero, whereas a homology two-sphere has Euler characteristic two.
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The doubly Cohen–Macaulay assertion is proved for characteristic-zero coefficient fields. It is not a claim over every field of positive characteristic. The field of order eleven in the geometric construction is distinct from the field used to compute homology.
    - paragraph: The sphere exclusion uses the local-link definition of a homology sphere. It concerns actual subcomplexes of this finite complex, without subdivision or an enlarged ambient complex; it does not exclude all possible immersed or topologically embedded spheres.
    - paragraph: "The ambient complex is two-dimensional but is not a surface: each of its edges belongs to twelve triangles. The construction is an existence argument using a finite quotient; the accompanying finite checks do not enumerate that quotient or replace the proof."
    - paragraph: The building, congruence method, spectral vanishing mechanism and curvature obstruction have substantial earlier sources. The paper applies them to the stated decomposition question. Its bounded literature search is not a certificate of worldwide priority.
    - heading "Status and AI use" [level=2]
    - paragraph: AI tools were used extensively in the argument, writing, literature research, computation and internal review. These internal reviews do not constitute independent human peer review or formal proof verification. The author is responsible for the work.
