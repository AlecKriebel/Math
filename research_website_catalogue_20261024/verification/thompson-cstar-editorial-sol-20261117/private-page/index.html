Reduced C*-algebras of the Thompson family: an attributed consequence of nonamenability

An attributed consequence

This note applies OpenAI’s pinned written proof that Thompson’s group F is nonamenable to the published simplicity reductions of Adrien Le Boudec and Nicolás Matte Bon and the trace theorem of Emmanuel Breuillard, Mehrdad Kalantar, Matthew Kennedy and Narutaka Ozawa. The resulting statements are consequences of those inputs. The note collects their hypotheses and attribution; it supplies no independent proof of nonamenability or new simplicity criterion.

The full automorphism and commensurator groups use the line realizations studied by Matthew Brin and José Burillo, Sean Cleary and Claas Röver. Every conclusion using the new nonamenability input retains exactly the same dependency on its validity. No first-priority claim is made.

The unique-trace property for T was already known unconditionally. Haagerup and Olesen record this prior result and credit Dudko and Medynets.

What the consequence says

A reduced group C*-algebra represents a discrete group by translation operators on square-summable functions on the group, then takes their operator-norm closure. Calling this algebra simple means that it has no nonzero proper closed two-sided ideals. Its canonical trace reads the coefficient of the identity element; a unique tracial state means that every normalized positive trace agrees with it.

Using the pinned nonamenability theorem, each of the following reduced algebras is simple and has its canonical trace as its unique tracial state:

  • C*r(F), for the standard Thompson group F;
  • C*r(T), for Thompson’s circle group T;
  • C*r(Aut(F)), for the full abstract automorphism group of F;
  • C*r(Comm(F)), for the full abstract commensurator of F.

All groups here carry the discrete topology. The full Aut(F) and Comm(F) include the orientation-reversing elements in their line realizations. Comm(F) identifies isomorphisms between finite-index subgroups when they agree after restriction to a further finite-index subgroup.

These groups are countable. F and T have finite dyadic descriptions. An automorphism of the two-generated F is determined by two images. Finite-index subgroups of F form a countable collection and are finitely generated, so the isomorphisms defining Comm(F), and hence their equivalence classes, also form a countable set.

The specified circle action matters

Standard F consists of increasing piecewise-linear maps of [0,1] with finitely many dyadic breakpoints and slopes that are powers of two. It fixes the endpoints; identifying them gives its standard action on the circle. T is the corresponding orientation-preserving dyadic piecewise-linear circle group preserving the dyadic points.

The same consequence holds for every countable subgroup G of Homeo(S¹) containing this specified standard circle action of F: its reduced algebra C*r(G) is simple and has a unique canonical trace. Containment of an arbitrary abstract copy of F in an unrelated action does not supply this hypothesis. This action-level consequence is inherited from Le Boudec and Matte Bon.

Scope and limits

The assertions concern reduced group algebras C*r(G). They do not assert simplicity of the full group algebra C*(G), or simplicity of the group as an abstract group. Simplicity and unique trace are distinct properties for general C*-algebras; the implication used here is the discrete-group theorem.

The upstream nonamenability proof is attributed entirely to OpenAI. A material defect in that input would remove the unconditional status of the conclusions that use it. The established conditional reductions and the previously known unique trace for T would remain.

Original manuscript abstract

Combining OpenAI's announced nonamenability theorem for the standard Thompson group F with the published equivalences of Le Boudec and Matte Bon gives simplicity of the reduced group C*-algebras of F, T, Aut(F) and the abstract commensurator Comm(F). Their canonical traces are their unique tracial states by the discrete-group trace theorem of Breuillard, Kalantar, Kennedy and Ozawa. We record the exact hypotheses, countability and attribution, and the corresponding consequence for countable circle-homeomorphism overgroups of the standard action. The argument is an application of established reductions, not a new simplicity criterion or an independent proof of nonamenability. Unique trace for T was already known unconditionally. All conclusions involving the new input have exactly the same dependency on its validity.
