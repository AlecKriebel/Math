                                                 Abstract
         We give a negative answer to the total-space positivity-preservation question for the relative
     Kähler–Ricci ﬂow deﬁned by Naumann on OE (r). For the ample rank-two bundle E = OP1 (1)⊕
     OP1 (1), we construct a smooth strictly positive initial metric on OE (2) ≃ OP1 ×P1 (2, 2). For the
     deﬁning ﬂow with probability-normalized ﬁber measures, its horizontal curvature at one point
     is exactly 1/96 − (1 − e−2t )/24, hence equals −1/96 at t = (log 2)/2. The argument uses the
     exact evolution of a second base derivative along a stationary central ﬁber and an explicit global
     positivity certiﬁcate.


1    Question and statement
Naumann’s report [1] ends on p. 2443 with the question of preserving positivity on the total space
when a positive metric on OE (1) is evolved through its rth power. The question remains explicit
in [2, §3.3]. We answer it for the ﬂow actually deﬁned in [2, (13)]. The ﬂow’s construction itself
was already given in those sources. The older report abbreviates the denominator without a time
subscript; we use the evolving measure explicit in the published deﬁning equation.

4    Solution and strict negativity
The pushforward of µF under x = v/(1 + v) is Lebesgue measure on [0, 1], since radial integration
gives dv/(1 + v)2 = dx. For a smooth F (x),

Let P1 = 2x − 1 and P2 = 6x2 − 6x + 1. Both have zero mean; ∆F P1 = −P1 , ∆F P2 = −3P2 , and
x2 = 1/3 + P1 /2 + P2 /6. Hence (8) has solution

To justify its identiﬁcation with the actual second derivative, the diﬀerence of two solutions of (8)
with common initial data has zero mean: integrating its equation shows that the mean is constant.
The diﬀerence then solves at = ∆F a + a; the maximum principle applied to e−t a proves uniqueness.
   At z = 0, P1 = −1 and P2 = 1, so w(0, t) = ϵ − δ(1 − e−2t )/6, which is (3). For t = t∗ it equals
−1/96, and is negative for every t > 12 log(4/3). Because the mixed Hessian vanishes there, this is
negative curvature on the actual tangent vector ∂/∂s. All initial data were strictly positive on a
compact total space, and t∗ is ﬁnite. This proves Theorem 1.

5    Normalization, scope, and veriﬁcation
There is a convention discrepancy between the deﬁning equation [2, (13)] and a literal same-
weight/time reading of the displayed rc term in [2, (14)]. A calibration independent of our example
is the positive product weight ϕprod = 2 log(1 + |s|2 ) + 2 log(1 + |z|2 ). It is stationary under (1). At
s = 0 its geodesic curvature is c = 2, the ﬁber Laplacian and Kodaira–Spencer terms vanish, and
(log I)ss̄ = −2. A same-convention rc term with r = 2 would therefore give ct = 2, contradicting
stationarity. Our proof uses (1) and derives (8) directly. We do not assign an editorial explanation
or undocumented intention to the source. For comparison, setting ψ = ϕ/r and τ = t/r transforms
(2) into                                                Z
                              ψτ = log ψz z̄ + rψ + log    e−rψ dAz + C ′ ,

so a coeﬃcient r can occur with the corresponding weight and time changes. Such positive rescaling
leaves the occurrence of positivity loss unchanged. Probability normalization is essential: omitting
log I changes the ﬂow and the second-variation equation.
    The result resolves the source’s universal positivity-preservation question for its deﬁning ﬂow.
It does not settle the Griﬃths conjecture, exclude special initial metrics, or address other normal-
izations. The example’s bundle itself already has Griﬃths-positive metrics. The prior construction
of the ﬂow is not claimed as a new contribution.
    The accompanying veriﬁcation package contains the manuscript source, exact symbolic checks,
analytic derivations, source and priority audit, and adversarial review records. Symbolic compu-
tations check the algebra and global inequality certiﬁcates; smooth parabolic existence is the cited
analytic input, not a computational conclusion. Historical ﬁrst priority is not asserted; the accom-
panying audit records the scope and limits of the literature search. In particular, Berman’s preser-
vation theorem concerns a nonnormalized weight ﬂow, and his earlier [3, Remark 4.11] already
identiﬁes normalized anticanonical negativity for eﬀectively parametrized Fano families. Those
statements do not supply this locally isotrivial projective-space bundle example on OE (r). They
preclude a broad claim of ﬁrst positivity loss for normalized relative ﬂows. A relevant Naumann
habilitation listed by his university in 2025 was identiﬁed, but its text was not recovered; this is a
speciﬁc unresolved priority lead recorded in the accompanying audit.
