                         behavior in positive characteristic

                                                          Abstract
          For each ﬁxed ﬁeld of positive characteristic p, we construct a single compatible symmetric
       chain of Artinian ideals in two rows of variables, generated from width three. At every width
       n ≥ 2, its quotient has both the weak and strong Lefschetz properties exactly when p ∤ n − 1. The
       quadratic quotient is free over the integers, and a determinant for an arbitrary linear form gives
       the classiﬁcation over every ﬁeld extension. In particular, over the ﬁxed inﬁnite ﬁeld Fp (t) both
       properties hold and fail at unbounded widths. This gives a negative answer to the unrestricted
       eventual dichotomies posed by Römer. It gives no negative answer in characteristic zero or in one
       row.


1     The question and the chain
Let K be a ﬁxed ﬁeld and c ≥ 1 a ﬁxed integer. In the setup of Römer [1, pp. 1594–1596], Rn =
K[Xi,j : 1 ≤ i ≤ c, 1 ≤ j ≤ n] and Sym(n) permutes the column index j, simultaneously in every row.
A symmetric invariant chain consists of ideals In ⊆ Rn such that each In is Sym(n)-invariant and
                                                            (          )
                                              Sym(n + 1) In Rn+1 ⊆ In+1 .

For Artinian homogeneous quotients, Römer asks whether each Lefschetz property eventually always
holds or eventually always fails. His contribution ﬁxes an arbitrary ﬁeld and c ≥ 1; the separate
one-row monomial working-group discussion on pp. 1544–1545 is a special setting.
   We use the usual deﬁnitions: a standard graded Artinian K-algebra A has WLP if some ℓ ∈ A1
makes every map ℓ : Ad → Ad+1 have rank min(dim Ad , dim Ad+1 ). It has SLP if one ℓ has this
property for every ℓs : Ad → Ad+s , d ≥ 0, s ≥ 1. These are existence deﬁnitions over K. Over
an inﬁnite ﬁeld the successful forms form a nonempty Zariski-open set, when they exist, since the
conditions are ﬁnitely many nonvanishing minors. Standard background on characteristic-sensitive
Lefschetz maps is given in [3]. Our successful forms are already deﬁned over the prime ﬁeld.
   Set c = 2, write the variables as x1 , . . . , xn , y1 , . . . , yn , and put ⇕n = (x1 , . . . , xn , y1 , . . . , yn ). Deﬁne

     For n ≥ 3, in fact In = (Sym(n)I3 )Rn . One containment follows from compatibility. For the other,
every generator in (1) involves at most three columns, including every cubic. Inject those distinct
supporting columns into {1, 2, 3}, without changing which indices repeat. The resulting generator
belongs to I3 . The reverse injection on its support extends to a permutation of {1, . . . , n} by bijecting
the unused columns. Thus the generator is a translate of one at width three. This uses one ﬁxed ﬁnite
list of orbits, rather than choosing ideals according to the residue of n.

Theorem 1. For every ﬁeld K, the quotients in (1) satisfy the following. At n = 1, A1 has SLP. At
every n ≥ 2, its Hilbert function is (1, 2n, 2n), and

                              An has SLP ⇐⇒ An has WLP ⇐⇒ (n − 1) 1K ̸= 0.

These assertions remain true after any ﬁeld extension.


2     Integral basis and multiplication
The same generators deﬁne an ideal over Z. In degree two ﬁrst remove the monomials xi xj with i ̸= j,
all yi yj , and all xi yi . For n ≥ 2 the remaining symbols are x2i and xi yj with j ̸= i. Fix i and choose
one reference ji ̸= i. The n − 1 symbols xi yj have relations precisely all their pairwise diﬀerences. The
reference symbol, together with xi yj − xi yji for j ̸= i, ji , is a unimodular basis for their free abelian
group. The relation subgroup is exactly the span of those diﬀerences. Its quotient is therefore free
of rank one, with basis vi = [xi yji ]. When n = 2 there is one symbol and no diﬀerences. Diﬀerent
i do not interact, and the ui = [x2i ] are unrestricted. Consequently the degree-two quotient has the
integral basis
                                             u1 , . . . , un , v1 , . . . , vn .
There are no linear relations, and all degrees at least three vanish. Thus the whole integral graded
quotient is torsion free and has graded ranks (1, 2n, 2n). Base change to any ﬁeld gives exactly the
claimed basis and Hilbert function, without a characteristic-dependent dimension argument.

The nonzero element z is in the degree-one socle, so every linear form annihilates it. Its nonvanishing
follows from the degree-one basis, regardless of whether the characteristic divides n. This obstruction
also survives every ﬁeld extension. In particular, failure is not inferred merely from testing one invariant
form.

    At n = 1, the quotient is K[x, y]/(xy, y 2 , (x, y)3 ), with basis 1, x, y, x2 and Hilbert function (1, 2, 1).
Multiplication by x from degree zero is injective, from degree one to two is surjective, and multiplication
by x2 from degree zero to two is nonzero. All remaining positive-power maps have zero target. Hence
x is strong Lefschetz. This completes the proof of Theorem 1.
Corollary 2. For each prime p, over the single ﬁxed inﬁnite ﬁeld K = Fp (t), the chain (1) has both
WLP and SLP at every width n = 2 + kp with k ≥ 0, and neither property at every width n = 1 + kp
with k ≥ 1. Consequently neither unrestricted eventual dichotomy in Römer’s question holds.
Proof. The theorem applies to all widths of this one chain over this one ﬁeld. Both displayed sequences
are unbounded. Thus neither eventual constant truth value is possible for either property.

    For n ≥ 2 the full pattern has period p: only n ≡ 1 (mod p) is bad. In characteristic two, even
widths are good and odd widths at least three are bad. In characteristic zero this construction is
strong Lefschetz at every width. No conclusion about all characteristic-zero chains or the one-row
question follows. If desired, for a ﬁxed c > 2 one can add every variable in each extra row as a linear
generator; the quotient is the same. The two-row example alone disproves the formulation allowing
arbitrary ﬁxed c ≥ 1.


4    Related work, provenance, and veriﬁcation limits
Seceleanu and Şega’s General symmetric ideals [2] studies general ideals in a single row. Its Theorem
B proves WLP under the hypothesis char K = 0 or char K > n, together with its stated size conditions.
Its chain result, Theorem 9.2 (summarized in Theorem C), assumes characteristic zero. A ﬁxed positive
characteristic cannot remain larger than unbounded n. These results therefore do not give the two-
row ﬁxed-characteristic construction above. The underlying use of ranks, socles, and characteristic-
sensitive determinants is standard; no new general Lefschetz mechanism is claimed.
