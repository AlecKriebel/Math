    - heading "Original abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Every discrete initial ordered additive subgroup of the surreal numbers is order-group-isomorphic to a subgroup of the omnific integers that is initial in the surreal numbers. This holds for sets and proper classes in NBG with global choice and answers the discrete-group question posed by Kaplan and Ehrlich. An explicit operation on ordinal-length sign sequences moves the least exponent to zero without creating a right-simplicity-predecessor obligation. Relabeling the actual normal-form supports and normalizing only the bottom coefficient group then gives the required image. An appendix supplies a detailed sufficiency proof of the initial-group characterization used in the argument, addressing support exhaustion and the legality of limit-cut options.
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The surreal numbers contain real numbers, infinite quantities and infinitesimals. Each also has a sign sequence describing its position in a construction tree. An initial group contains every proper sign-sequence prefix of each of its elements.
    - paragraph: This paper considers actual additive subgroups of the surreal numbers with that closure property and a smallest positive element. It constructs an equivalent copy inside the omnific integers. The copy remains initial in the ambient surreal numbers, and the equivalence preserves addition and numerical order.
    - paragraph: The construction relabels the exponents in the normal forms already present and rescales the lowest coefficient group. It applies to sets and proper classes while keeping each individual support set-sized.
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Work in NBG set theory with global choice. Let G be an actual initial ordered additive subgroup of No, with addition and order inherited from No. If G is nonzero, assume that it has a least positive element. Then there is an order-group isomorphism T:G→H onto an actual subgroup H⊆Oz that is initial in No. The statement includes set and proper-class groups; the zero group uses the identity.
    - paragraph: Here initial means closure under all proper ordinal-length sign-sequence prefixes in the ambient No. It is distinct from convexity in numerical order. The isomorphism is required to preserve addition and numerical order; it need not preserve simplicity or multiplication.
    - paragraph: For nonzero G, its least occurring exponent is m=−α for a set ordinal α, with bottom pure coefficient group R_m=2^(−n)ℤ for a nonnegative integer n. The explicit exponent map sends m to zero, and T multiplies only its bottom coefficients by 2^n. Thus H=T[G] has nonnegative exponents and pure coefficient group ℤ at zero.
    - paragraph: This supplies an affirmative answer to the discrete additive-group question in Kaplan’s 2015 thesis, Kaplan’s joint-work contribution with Ehrlich in the 2016 Oberwolfach report, and Question 9.1 of the Ehrlich–Kaplan author manuscript. Those historical formulations do not establish a first-discovery claim.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Write a=(−)^α and let e be the empty sequence. On the ray s≥a, the map sends a to e; sends s>a to (+)s when s does not extend a; and sends a(+)t to (+)at. Ordered concatenation of sign blocks makes this a strictly increasing map with initial image of minimum zero, including limit ordinal lengths and empty tails.
    - paragraph: The map reflects target right-simplicity-predecessor obligations. Since zero cannot be a right predecessor in its nonnegative image, normalizing the bottom coefficient group to ℤ creates no new requirement for dyadic coefficients there.
    - paragraph: Increasing exponent relabeling and positive coefficient scaling preserve addition, cancellation and leading-term order. The construction transports precisely the actual set-sized supports in G. Truncations and pure coefficient groups are tracked directly; the credited Ehrlich–Kaplan characterization then gives ambient initiality, and the standard omnific normal-form criterion gives H⊆Oz.
    - paragraph: The published appendix supplies a detailed sufficiency proof for that credited characterization. It adjoins pure terms, then actual limit-length supports through legal integer-perturbation cuts, and orders those adjunctions by support length. For a proper class, it applies the set argument to each actual support together with its exponent prefixes. Standard surreal arithmetic, simplicity, normal-form and monomial/pure-multiple/limit-series cut facts remain explicit classical inputs.
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The theorem assumes an actual discrete initial additive subgroup of No. It gives no embedding theorem for arbitrary abstract ordered groups or arbitrary noninitial surreal subgroups. It does not say that the original G is already contained in Oz or that H equals all of Oz.
    - paragraph: No ring, field, multiplicative or simplicity-preserving isomorphism is asserted. H is the actual image T[G]; no full Hahn completion is adjoined.
    - paragraph: The setting is NBG with global choice. A group or exponent domain may be a proper class, but every individual normal-form support is a set. The argument uses no class-sized support, sum or cut and no stronger class-recursion principle.
    - paragraph: The initial-group characterization and real-coefficient and least-positive classifications are prior work of Ehrlich–Kaplan. The negative-ordinal minimum observation and general exponent-relabeling/positive-scaling mechanisms also have established antecedents. The contribution presented here is the particular sign operation, its right-prefix control and its application to the actual subgroup, together with the published dependency argument.
    - paragraph: Historical priority, full novelty and worldwide current-open status remain unverified. Bounded literature coverage, including unread full texts of the 2001 predecessor and Ehrlich’s 2024 handbook chapter, does not support an exhaustive absence-of-prior-work claim. Numbering of the main classical interfaces refers to the cited author manuscript; identity with every publisher edition is not asserted.
    - paragraph: Finite verification programs are supplementary diagnostics. The arbitrary-ordinal, infinite-support and proper-class assertions depend on the written proof and its explicitly cited classical foundations. This is an unrefereed preprint prepared with extensive AI assistance, without human or journal peer review or formal proof-assistant certification.
