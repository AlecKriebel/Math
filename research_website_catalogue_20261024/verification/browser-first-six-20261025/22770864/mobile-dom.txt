    - heading "Abstract" [level=2]
    - paragraph: We construct a finite group G for which p = 1009, |G| = |Aut(G)| = 1009^52359. The group is the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff group of an explicitly specified rank-31 Lie ring modulo 1009^1689. Its nilpotency class is at most 846 < 1009. An asymmetric lattice forces every automorphism of the finite Lie ring into an integral logarithm–exponential neighborhood. This gives a bijection between the full automorphism set and the kernel of a linear derivation matrix. Exact independently implemented Python and C++ computations establish the Smith valuations of that matrix. The resulting computer-assisted proof answers the existence question affirmatively. The construction and all exact certificates are supplied with separately implemented verification software.
    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: An automorphism is a way to relabel every element of a group while preserving its multiplication. The question is whether a nontrivial group whose size is a power of an odd prime can have exactly as many of these symmetries as it has elements.
    - paragraph: "This paper constructs an example with 1009^52359 elements and the same number of automorphisms. The number is enormous, but the construction has a finite description: an element is a list of 31 numbers taken modulo 1009^1689, with a specified rule for multiplying two lists."
    - paragraph: The main difficulty is accounting for every automorphism. The proof uses a deliberately asymmetric lattice to constrain them all, then turns their count into an exact linear-algebra calculation. The paper presents this as an affirmative answer to D. MacHale’s Kourovka Notebook problem 16.63.
    - heading "The precise result" [level=2]
    - paragraph: For p = 1009, the paper constructs a finite nontrivial p-group G with |G| = |Aut(G)| = 1009^52359, where Aut(G) is the full automorphism group.
    - paragraph: The group is G = BCH(L/p^1689 L), where L is the explicit rank-31 Lie lattice in Definitions 2.1 and 5.1. Its nilpotency class is at most 846, below p, so the finite Lazard correspondence preserves its order and full automorphism group.
    - paragraph: For this fixed Lie lattice and every integer i ≥ 7, the full Lie-ring automorphism group of L/p^i L has order p^(30i + 1689), while L/p^i L has p^(31i) elements. Choosing i = 1689 makes the two counts equal.
    - paragraph: The exact derivation matrix has 14,415 rows, 961 columns, rank 931, and nullity 30. Its nonzero p-primary Smith valuations have multiplicities 87, 55, 758, 6, and 25 at valuations 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4; their sum is 1689.
    - heading "Why the result holds" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Begin with the rank-31 Lie-algebra template of Omirov and Ruan. A chosen flag has no nonidentity stabilizer in its full finite-field automorphism group. Weighting the lattice along this flag forces every automorphism of a sufficiently deep finite quotient into a domain where logarithms are integral.
    - paragraph: Within that domain, logarithm and exponential preserve the required precision and give a bijection between all automorphisms and solutions of a linear derivation equation. This is the bridge from a nonlinear symmetry problem to an exact matrix count.
    - paragraph: The matrix’s nullity contributes 30i to the exponent of the automorphism count, while its Smith valuations contribute 1689. The ring itself has order p^(31i), so the counts meet at i = 1689. The class bound then lets the Lazard correspondence transfer the equality to a finite group.
    - heading "Scope and limitations" [level=2]
    - paragraph: This gives one example at p = 1009. It does not establish an example for every odd prime, and it makes no minimality claim for the prime, group order, or number of generators.
    - paragraph: The equality is between orders. The paper does not claim that G and Aut(G) are isomorphic.
    - paragraph: The construction is explicit through its Lie bracket and finite Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff rule. It does not enumerate the group’s multiplication table or its automorphisms. Exact computation certifies the matrix data; the full-automorphism reduction and the correspondence with groups also require the written proof.
    - paragraph: The Lie-algebra template is due to Omirov and Ruan. This paper supplies its integral normalization, finite-characteristic arguments, asymmetric lattice, and exact count of finite automorphisms.
    - paragraph: The Kourovka Notebook lists this manuscript as an AI-proposed solution awaiting confirmation by mathematicians. Its listing should not be read as external validation or journal peer review.
    - heading "Status and AI use" [level=2]
    - paragraph: This is an unrefereed preprint whose construction, proof development, and initial verification software were generated using AI tools; subsequent checks are not external human peer review or proof-assistant formalization.
