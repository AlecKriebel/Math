    - heading "The idea in plain English" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Start with an infinite network that looks the same from every vertex. Its “square” adds a direct connection wherever two vertices were previously two steps apart. In this example, each vertex goes from having 8 neighbors to having 40.

    - heading "Why the comparison holds" [level=2]
    - list:
      - strong: Calculate the original value.
      - text: Two finite-network reductions, with explicit convergence arguments, give the exact free effective resistances in G. Summing the edge-inclusion probabilities gives (24 − √2)/7.
      - strong: Certify an upper bound for the square.
      - text: A finite network contains all 40 edges incident to the root. Explicit rational currents satisfy the required unit-flow equations, and their total energy is checked exactly.
      - strong: Pass to the infinite free forest.
      - text: The currents have finite support and extend by zero to larger finite free networks. Thomson’s principle and the free limit transfer the energy bound to the expected root degree.
    - paragraph: This last step is essential. An unrestricted infinite-network flow calculation would generally concern wired resistance and would not establish the free-forest bound used here.
