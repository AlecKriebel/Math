Unbounded Godbillon–Vey numbers on a fixed hyperbolic manifold

Alec Kriebel · 13 October 2026 · Zenodo version 1.0 · Unrefereed preprint

A foliation divides a three-manifold into surfaces called leaves. Its Godbillon–Vey number measures a form of transverse twisting. This note assembles classical ingredients to make that number arbitrarily large on one fixed hyperbolic manifold, while a single closed curve continues to cross every leaf.

Original abstract

Classical compact-support suspension theory yields a smooth foliated surface-times-interval with product endpoint collars and nonzero Godbillon–Vey integral. We place arbitrarily many copies inside one fixed pseudo-Anosov mapping torus. One embedded closed transversal meets every leaf, and the Godbillon–Vey numbers grow linearly with the number of copies. This gives a negative answer to the unrestricted volume-bound question printed as Question 10.6 in Calegari’s 2002 manuscript. The construction uses classical ingredients; historical priority of this specific consequence remains unestablished.

One manifold, unbounded numbers

There exist a closed connected oriented hyperbolic three-manifold M, a constant a > 0, and an embedded smooth circle γ such that, for every finite integer N ≥ 1, M has a smooth cooriented taut foliation FN with ordinary Godbillon–Vey number

gv(FN) = ∫M ηN ∧ dηN = Na, where dαN = αN ∧ ηN.

The same γ is positively transverse to every FN and meets every leaf. This is the one-circle meaning of tautness used here. The manifold, circle, constant and hyperbolic metric are chosen before N varies. The metric’s volume stays fixed, so no finite bound determined solely by that volume can bound these numbers or their absolute values.

How the construction works

The starting piece is a smooth foliated surface-times-interval block with positive ordinary Godbillon–Vey integral a. Classical Mather–Thurston theory, with the suspension compatibility recorded by Tsuboi, supplies this piece. Near both interval endpoints it is exactly a product. Every leaf meets the interval above any chosen point of the surface.

The ambient space is a mapping torus: the two ends of a surface-times-interval are joined by a smooth representative of a pseudo-Anosov mapping class. Thurston’s hyperbolization theorem makes this one closed manifold hyperbolic. A representative fixing a small disk provides a point whose vertical interval closes into the embedded circle γ. The mapping class is pseudo-Anosov; its disk-fixed representative need not be a pseudo-Anosov normal form.

For each finite N, compress N copies of the block into separate interior slabs. Exact product collars make the foliations and defining forms join smoothly, without changing the seam. Each compressed block retains its integral a; the intervening product regions contribute zero, giving Na. Block leaves meet γ by the suspension property, and the remaining product leaves meet it too.

Scope and credit

The target is the literal unrestricted volume question, Calegari’s 2002 manuscript, Question 10.6 at printed page 25. Compact leaves are allowed. The different Question 13.1 assumes minimal taut foliations and asks about a same-sign Godbillon–Vey form; this construction does not answer it. The final 2003 chapter was not compared, so its citation does not certify identical wording.

Only finite stacks are used. Derivatives may grow with N; no uniform regularity bound or infinite-stack limit is asserted. The theorem concerns one suitable manifold, and gives no explicit numerical holonomy representation or certified hyperbolic metric.

The result is a credited consequence of classical constructions. Mather–Thurston and Tsuboi supply the suspension input; Gabai documents interval-bundle replacement; Penner and Fathi supply the pseudo-Anosov twist construction; Thurston supplies hyperbolization. Historical firstness, guaranteed novelty and present global literature status remain unestablished.
