The outer focal-pedal area product in odd elliptic billiards

Alec Kriebel · ORCID: 0009-0001-9320-500X
Originally published 15 October 2026 · Unrefereed preprint
Zenodo and authored metadata version 1.0; no numeric version is printed in the manuscript.

A moving billiard orbit determines another polygon: intersect the tangents at consecutive vertices. Drop perpendiculars from each original focus onto the side lines of this outer tangent polygon. Each focus gives a polygon of feet, with an area that can change as the orbit moves. For an odd least-period family, the product of these two signed areas stays fixed. The result also includes the admitted star-shaped trajectories.

Abstract

For a fixed odd-periodic billiard family between strictly nested confocal ellipses, project each original focus onto the ordered side lines of the outer tangent polygon. We prove that the product of the two signed pedal areas is constant, for every coprime winding, including stars. This proves the experimental assertion labelled k605,a in the 2020 arXiv invariant list and k606 in its published companion. The focal-foot map has paired collinear residues; its cyclic area consequently has only two possible simple poles on a reduced torus. Reflection and an imaginary antiperiod provide the complementary zeros. The argument uses established elliptic-function methods. We explain connections to generalized Landen identities and recent focal inversion results; historical priority of this particular application remains undetermined. Exact controls and separately labelled numerical diagnostics accompany this unrefereed, extensively AI-assisted note.

The invariant and its geometric meaning

Fix an ellipse with semiaxes a > b > 0 and a strictly nested, nondegenerate confocal elliptical caustic. The caustic is the inner ellipse touched by every billiard segment. Consider a family of trajectories with odd least period N ≥ 3: N is the smallest number of reflections needed to close. Every coprime winding actually admitted by that fixed pair is included, rather than only the simplest convex orbit.

The two projection points at step j are the perpendicular feet of the original foci onto the tangent at the original billiard vertex. Those tangents are exactly the side lines of the outer polygon. Join the feet in trajectory order, including for a star, and calculate signed shoelace area. If these areas are B+ and B−, the theorem is

B+ B− = constant along the fixed family.

The statement concerns their product, not constancy of each area. These are outer-side pedal polygons: projecting onto the original billiard chords gives a different construction. Unit inversion of the original or outer vertices about a focus also gives different polygons. Keeping the underlying objects separate matters when comparing their area formulas and earlier results.

Why odd periods give a constant product

The proof uses an explicit formula for a focal foot and classical elliptic-function coordinates for the billiard motion. Each pedal area becomes a cyclic sum. When two adjacent feet become singular in the complex parameter, their residue vectors are opposite and collinear, so the apparent double pole cancels. After cyclic relabelling, the area has only two possible simple poles on a reduced torus.

Reflection and an imaginary antiperiod force complementary zeros. Multiplying by the half-real-period shift therefore removes every possible pole, leaving a holomorphic function on a compact torus, which is constant. For an odd least period, that shift exchanges the two original foci. No division by an area is needed, so the reasoning does not discard a possible area zero. This is an application of established elliptic-function methods.

Scope and boundaries

Areas are signed in the order of the trajectory, rather than filled areas of star-shaped regions. Reversing orientation changes both signs and preserves the product. Traversing a fixed odd primitive orbit s times multiplies each area by s and the product by s2, even when the repeated list has even length. A circular boundary, with coincident foci, is covered separately by rotation of regular stars.

The main theorem excludes hyperbolic, collapsed and grazing caustics. It does not extend to general even primitive families: the paper gives two four-periodic phases on the same confocal pair whose products are 16 and 25. The equality of the two focal areas in an even primitive orbit does not make their common square constant. Numerical diagnostics accompany the note, but are not interval certificates or substitutes for its universal proof.

Earlier work and overlap

Reznik, Garcia and Koiller recorded the experimental outer-pedal assertion as k605,a in their arXiv invariant list and k606 in the published companion; the earlier first version used k604. The published k605 concerns original-chord pedals. The auxiliary-circle geometry, Stachel’s canonical coordinates, Jacobi identities and earlier Poncelet-area methods are credited foundations.

The note also relates this construction to the arbitrary-order Landen identity published by Khare and Sukhatme in 2004. Ferudun’s recent results on original-vertex focal inversion areas and related outer-pedal products are substantive antecedents, including closely related parallel-residue cancellation. An additional geometric and pole-space comparison connects the area traces; a projectivity alone does not preserve Euclidean area. Historical priority of this particular application remains undetermined. The paper makes no first-proof, new general elliptic-method or current worldwide-open claim.
