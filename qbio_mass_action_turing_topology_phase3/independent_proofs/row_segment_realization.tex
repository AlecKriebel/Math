\section{Exact mass-action realization of independent row segments}
\label{sec:row-realization-independent}

Let $p\geq1$. Split the row indices into fixed rows $F$ and variable rows
$V$. For $i\in F$ prescribe $a_i\in\mathbb Q^{1\times p}$; for
$i\in V$ prescribe $a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Q^{1\times p}$. Define $L(q)$ by
\[
 L_i(q)=a_i\quad(i\in F),\qquad
 L_i(q)=a_i+q_i b_i\quad(i\in V),
 \qquad -1<q_i<1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Row-segment realization]
\label{thm:row-realization-independent}
There is an indexed classical mass-action network on exactly $p$ species,
with nonnegative integer source and target complexes and stoichiometric
columns in $\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_p\}$, whose complete positive steady-flux
Jacobian-factor image is
\[
 \bigl\{\Gamma\operatorname{diag}(v)Y^T:
          v>0,\ \Gamma v=0\bigr\}
 =\bigl\{R L(q):R\succ0\text{ diagonal},\ q\in(-1,1)^{|V|}\bigr\}.
 \tag{RS}
\]
The network has full stoichiometric rank. It uses $2|F|+3|V|$ reactions,
and its encoding length is polynomial in the rational input length.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a variable row $i$. Choose an integer $c_i>0$ clearing the denominators
of both $a_i+b_i$ and $a_i-b_i$. Choose a nonnegative integer base complex
$y_i^0$ with $(y_i^0)_i\geq1$ and large enough componentwise that
\[
 y_i^+=y_i^0+c_i(a_i+b_i),\qquad
 y_i^-=y_i^0+c_i(a_i-b_i)
\]
are nonnegative integer vectors. Add the three indexed reactions
\[
 y_i^+\longrightarrow y_i^++e_i,\qquad
 y_i^-\longrightarrow y_i^-+e_i,\qquad
 y_i^0\longrightarrow y_i^0-e_i.
 \tag{RSi}
\]
Their stoichiometric columns are $e_i,e_i,-e_i$. If their positive fluxes
are $u_i,w_i,s_i$, the $i$th steady-flux equation is exactly
$s_i=u_i+w_i$. Their contribution to row $i$ of
$\Gamma\operatorname{diag}(v)Y^T$ is therefore
\begin{align*}
 u_i(y_i^+)^T+w_i(y_i^-)^T-(u_i+w_i)(y_i^0)^T
 &=c_i\bigl((u_i+w_i)a_i+(u_i-w_i)b_i\bigr)\\
 &=\rho_i(a_i+q_i b_i),
\end{align*}
where
\[
 \rho_i=c_i(u_i+w_i)>0,
 \qquad
 q_i=\frac{u_i-w_i}{u_i+w_i}\in(-1,1).
\]
Conversely, every $\rho_i>0$ and $q_i\in(-1,1)$ is attained by
\[
 u_i=\frac{\rho_i(1+q_i)}{2c_i},\qquad
 w_i=\frac{\rho_i(1-q_i)}{2c_i},\qquad
 s_i=\frac{\rho_i}{c_i},
\]
which are all strictly positive.

For a fixed row choose $c_i>0$ clearing the denominators of $a_i$, choose
$y_i^0\geq0$ with $(y_i^0)_i\geq1$ and
$y_i^+=y_i^0+c_i a_i\geq0$, and add
\[
 y_i^+\longrightarrow y_i^++e_i,
 \qquad
 y_i^0\longrightarrow y_i^0-e_i.
\]
The steady-flux equation forces the two positive fluxes to be equal. Giving
each the value $\rho_i/c_i$ produces row $\rho_i a_i$, and every positive
steady flux in this row has that form.

All columns belonging to row $i$ are scalar multiples of $e_i$. Hence the
global equation $\Gamma v=0$ is precisely the collection of the independent
row equations just described; there are no hidden cross-row constraints.
This proves both inclusions in (RS), including zero row entries and repeated
or parallel indexed reactions. Each row contains both $e_i$ and $-e_i$, so
$\Gamma$ has row rank $p$. Sources and targets are distinct because every
reaction changes coordinate $i$ by one. Denominator clearing and taking a
componentwise maximum plus one construct the bases with polynomial bit
length, proving the complexity assertion.
\end{proof}

At $x^*=\mathbf 1$, the rate constants are exactly the chosen positive
fluxes. Thus the construction uses no zero rates and no limiting argument.
