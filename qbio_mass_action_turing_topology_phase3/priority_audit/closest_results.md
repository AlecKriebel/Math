# Closest prior results and exact separation

## 1. Fixed-J stationary theory

Anma, Sakamoto, and Yoneda and related matrix-stability work analyze when an already selected Jacobian can be destabilized by diffusion through unstable principal subsystems. That is the closest antecedent to the signed-principal-minor theorem. Phase III therefore does **not** present the fixed-J theorem as the biological novelty. The new decision problem quantifies over the positive steady-flux cone and positive equilibrium scaling of an indexed classical mass-action reaction hypergraph.

Piskovsky gives necessary-and-sufficient stationary and Turing-Hopf inequalities for a general three-species fixed Jacobian. This is complementary to, rather than subsumed by, the fixed-species algorithm here: the latter decides whether any positive classical mass-action realization of an arbitrary reaction list exists.

## 2. Reaction-network topology and graph criteria

Diego, Marcon, Muller, and Sharpe establish topology-controlled features of general Turing systems at the interaction-network level. Scholes and coauthors provide a broad small-network atlas. Mincheva and Craciun derive graph-theoretic conditions for zero-eigenvalue Turing instability in general chemical reaction networks. None of these sources found in the audit gives the present arbitrary-network classical-mass-action iff formula together with a complexity boundary.

The present Level-5 statement is deliberately narrow: the indexed pair `(Gamma,Y)` is the weakest sufficient parameter-independent representation **among the six levels considered**. It is not a claim that no alternative compressed invariant can ever exist.

## 3. Unstable cores and child selections

Vassena and Stadler's unstable cores and Vassena's child-selection methods provide powerful structural sufficient conditions for instability in parameter-rich kinetics, including generalized mass action. Their kinetic derivatives can be independently varied in ways unavailable under classical mass action. The present theorem instead retains the exact positive steady-flux constraints and positive right-diagonal equilibrium scaling.

## 4. Current mass-action spatial-instability work

Conradi, Mincheva, and Uecker study networks that admit monomial steady-state parametrizations and derive sufficient polynomial conditions for a Turing-like instability. The class and quantifiers are different: their result is structurally restricted and sufficient; the present candidate theorem is an exact arbitrary-network decision result but pays for that generality with semialgebraic decision and NP-hardness.

## 5. Complexity source and matrix boundary

Blondel and Tsitsiklis prove NP-hardness of finding a stable matrix in a rational interval family using `PARTITION`. Their construction is the source matrix family. The reaction-network reduction has two additional load-bearing steps not present there: one-parameter-per-row lifting with arbitrary-right-scaling elimination, and an exact entire-family mass-action realization.

The positive diagonal stabilizability literature does not supply a general necessary-and-sufficient characterization. The reduction does not claim one: it proves the needed equivalence only for the explicit lifted family.

## Priority conclusion

No earlier result was located with all of the following simultaneously: indexed finite classical mass action; existential positive rates, equilibrium, and all-species diffusion; relative homogeneous stability under conservation; weak stationary positive-real-eigenvalue scope; arbitrary network size; exact semialgebraic iff characterization; NP-hardness; fixed-species polynomial algorithms; and finite exact two-sided certificates.

This conclusion remains provisional until specialist feedback and peer review, especially because terminology differs across reaction-network theory, control, and real-algebraic complexity.
