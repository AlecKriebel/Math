## Global model and order of limits

- Graphs are finite, connected, loopless, undirected, and symmetrically weighted; every stated edge has positive weight and every vertex has positive weighted degree (source lines 141–176; PDF pp. 2–3).
- Mutant fitness is fixed at `r>1`; resident fitness is one. Fixation probability is averaged over a uniformly random singleton mutant. The comparator is the complete graph of the same order (lines 141–199; PDF pp. 2–3).
- Central quantifiers:

  `exists one graph sequence {G_t}; for every fixed r in (1,R_hyb); there exists t_0(r); for every t>=t_0(r), both strict amplification inequalities hold.`

  The graph sequence and its least-dyadic cut are fixed before fitness is quantified. The eventual index may depend on the fixed fitness. No endpoint claim is made.
- Population scales are `C=t^4`, `q=t`, `m=floor(lambda_* t)`, `eta=q/C=t^-3`, and `n=C+m+2q`; hence `m/q -> lambda_*` and the normalized gain scale is `Theta(n^-3/4)` (lines 203–225, 1296–1329, 1500–1504).
- Limit order: for fixed finite `(C,m,q)`, take the exact weak-cut trace as `epsilon -> 0`; derive compact-uniform population asymptotics as `t -> infinity`; then choose an exact least-dyadic `epsilon_t` uniformly on the expanding fitness interval (lines 276–441 and 1388–1397).
- Equality/strictness: both first-order responses vanish together only at the algebraic tangency; they are strictly positive below it. Actual connected-graph gains are strict only for sufficiently large `t` at each fixed interior fitness.

## Computational dependency boundary

- `verify_hybrid_lumping.py` exhausts 512 labelled configurations and 108 orbit fibres for one rational nine-vertex instance. It checks finite orientation and aggregation only; the arbitrary-size proof is analytic.
- The two symbolic certificate programs check exact response, threshold, Sturm, tangency, rational-margin, and encoded rate identities. They take the reduced stochastic rates as inputs and do not prove portal asymptotics.
- `verify_paper_claims.py` is a marker/integration audit. It explicitly checks that the repaired stop/`ell=0` text is present and the old unstopped expression is absent, but it is not a semantic proof checker.
- Weak-cut convergence and all stochastic population asymptotics are analytic arguments in the manuscript. No report conclusion credits a finite program with an infinite quantifier.
- v2.0.2 uses explicit fail-closed checks, rejects optimized Python, and includes ordinary/optimized mutation regression tests. These support software reliability, not the analytic theorem by themselves.
