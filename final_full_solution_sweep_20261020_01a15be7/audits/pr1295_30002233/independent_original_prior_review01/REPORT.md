## Independent finite calculation

Let Γ′ be the banana graph with two vertices u′,v′ and four edges, all of length1. Let Γ have two vertices u,v and three edges of lengths1,2,2. Map the first two source edges to the first target edge with slope1, the third to the second with slope2, and the fourth to the third with slope2. The length identities are 1·1=1 and 1·2=2. At either source vertex the slope sums above the three target directions are 1+1,2,2. Thus every local degree is2, there are no contracted edges, and the map is a finite degree-two harmonic morphism. Its genera are 3 and2, respectively.

For the source the reduced Laplacian is [4], giving regularized graph Jacobian Z/4, generated by S=[u′−v′]. For the target subdivide each length-two edge once at a,b. In the order u,a,b, with v deleted, its reduced Laplacian is

```
L = [ 3 -1 -1 ]
    [-1  2  0 ]
    [-1  0  2 ].
```

Its determinant is8; the entries have gcd1 and the minor on rows(u,a), columns(u,b) is−1. Hence its Smith invariants are1,1,8 and the target group is cyclic of order8. Put U=[u−v], A=[a−v], B=[b−v]. The relations are 3U−A−B=0, −U+2A=0, −U+2B=0. Eliminating gives U=2A, B=5A, 8A=0, with A a generator. Alternatively L⁻¹U=(1/2,1/4,1/4), so U has exact order4. The latter computation checks that endpoint class order is4 rather than confusing it with the whole target group order8.

Covariant divisor push sends each vertex to its image. Over the algebraically closed residue field there is no residue-field degree factor, and there is **no local harmonic degree multiplier**. This is the convention in ABBR Lemma8.3, not the pullback convention. Thus S maps to U=2A. The source relation4S maps to4U=8A=0, so the map is well defined. Enumeration gives image{0,2,4,6} in Z/8, kernel0 and cokernelZ/2. The cycle-pairing matrices I₃+J₃ and [[3,1],[1,3]] independently have discriminants4 and8.

The finite regularized Jacobian is being used, not the real torus Jacobian. Nor is this being misread as an ordinary graph map between simultaneous unit subdivisions: the inverse images of the target midpoints a,b lie at half-integral source positions. ## Residue covers and tameness

At every graph vertex attach P¹ over k=C. Label the target tangent directions by 1,0,∞ and the source directions by 1,−1,0,∞. At both source vertices take z↦z². The complete fibers over the three marked target points are {1,−1}, {0}, {∞}, with ramification indices (1,1),(2),(2). These are exactly the graph's slopes. All markings are distinct; the covers are separable and tame; the residue-cover degree and harmonic local degree are both2. No marked preimage is missing.

ABBR Definition2.19 requires these complete marked fibers, the matching ramification indices, and equality of local and residue degrees. All hold. For Definition2.21's stronger tame-covering condition, the graph ramification coefficient at either vertex is

```
2·2 − 2 − ((1−1)+(1−1)+(2−1)+(2−1)) = 0.
```

At an interior point of expansion d it is 2d−2−2(d−1)=0. All ramification of the residue map lies at the marked points0,∞. Take no global punctures, D=D′=∅. The graph has no loop edges, so the actual existence clause Theorem7.4(1) applies, without needing the loop subdivision of7.7. The coefficient calculation is not an assertion that the residue maps are unramified at0,∞; rather their ramification is already represented by the adjacent annuli. The generic curve cover can consequently be étale.

Characteristic two is deliberately excluded from this construction: ±1 would coincide and z² would be inseparable. The original claim is universal and has no requirement that a counterexample work at every residual characteristic.

## Fixed-field lifting, model extension and minimality

ABBR's general analytic framework initially takes an algebraically closed complete valued field. The fixed-field conclusion used here is the actual Remark7.13, read in v3PDF48 and publisherPDF62. There is no hidden fractional modulus in this application. At either expansion-two edge, the target parameter is t² and its required square root t is already in K₀. A standard node-chart map is

```
R₀[[x,y]]/(xy−t²) → R₀[[s,r]]/(sr−t),   x↦s², y↦r².
```

The two expansion-one edges lie above the thickness-one node. Roots of units, when changing tame local coordinates, exist in C[[t]] by Hensel's lemma, and the relevant roots of unity lie in C. Intersection with Cᵢ gives Cᵢ²+C₁C₂=0; C₁C₂=4, so each self-intersection is−4. There are no other vertical irreducible components and therefore no exceptional P¹ of self-intersection−1 to contract.
