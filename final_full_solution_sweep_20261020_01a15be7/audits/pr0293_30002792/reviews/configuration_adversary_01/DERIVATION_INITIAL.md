## Exact target and conditional input

Let `k` be algebraically closed (any characteristic), let `R=k[x0,x1,x2,x3]`, and let `I=intersection p_l` be the saturated radical ideal of a nonempty finite union of distinct projective lines. Write `a=alpha(I)` and suppose `alpha(I^(2))=a+1=d`. The downstream classification requires precisely this surface assertion:

> For every integral degree-e surface in projective 3-space over k, with e at least 2, there is a nonzero degree-(e-2) form vanishing on every reduced one-dimensional component of its singular locus.

No claim about isolated singular points is necessary. No ACM, general-position, smoothness, or separability assumption is allowed in this input. Establishing the assertion itself is outside this derivation.

## Symbolic membership

The associated primes of a reduced line union are its distinct line primes. At a line prime, every other component prime becomes the unit ideal. Each line prime is generated by two independent linear forms and its ordinary powers are primary; in coordinates `(u,v,s,t)` this is immediate from the `(u,v)`-degree filtration. Therefore `I^(2)=intersection p_l^2`. This argument is independent of characteristic. Membership is polynomial ideal membership, not vanishing on the finite set of rational points of a line.

## Repeated factor elimination

Choose a nonzero degree-d form `F` in that intersection. Its squarefree product of distinct irreducible factors `rad(F)` belongs to every target line prime, hence its degree is at least `a=d-1`. If `F` is not squarefree, the excess of degree over the radical is at most one. Consequently exactly one factor repeats twice, that factor has degree one, and `F=P^2 G` with `G` squarefree and coprime to `P`.

If `G` is constant, every target line lies in `P`. Otherwise at least one partial derivative of `G` is nonzero: all partials vanishing over the perfect field k would make `G` a p-th power in positive characteristic, contrary to squarefreeness. For a target line not in `P`, localization and primary contraction give `G in p_l^2`. Differentiating a product of elements of a linear prime gives `partial G in p_l`, including characteristic two. Thus `P partial G` vanishes on every target line, is nonzero, and has degree `d-2`, a contradiction. The derivative argument never differentiates `P^2` and so survives its zero derivative in characteristic two.

## Elimination of nonlinear irreducible factors, conditional on the surface input

Assume `F` squarefree. For an irreducible factor `Q` of degree `e>=2`, take a degree-(e-2) form `A` from the stated input. A target line either belongs to another component, in which case `A(F/Q)` vanishes there, or is contained only in `Q`. In the latter case the other factors are units at its prime, so `Q in p_l^2`. All first partials of `Q` vanish along the line. Since k is perfect and `Q` is irreducible, its hypersurface is generically smooth and the singular locus is proper of dimension at most one. The target line is therefore a reduced singular-curve component and `A` vanishes on it. The nonzero product `A(F/Q)` has degree `d-2`, again impossible.

## Completeness of the resulting plane arrangement

Write `F=P1 ... Pd` with distinct planes. A target line lies in at least two factors: if just one linear factor contained it, `F` would have order exactly one at its line prime. If an intersection `V(Pi,Pj)` is absent, then each target line lies in some plane with index outside `{i,j}`, so `F/(Pi Pj)` vanishes on all target lines. If that intersection belongs to three planes, the same quotient vanishes on it too; every other target line still lies in a remaining factor. Each alternative contradicts initial degree. Hence every pair intersection occurs and no three planes contain a line. Concurrency of arbitrarily many planes at a point causes no problem.

## ACM and saturation

Let `J=(F/P1,...,F/Pd)`. For `d>=2`, the indicated `d x (d-1)` matrix has column i equal to `Pi e_i-Pd e_d`. Its maximal minors are the generators of J up to signs, which remain correct in characteristic two. The common vanishing set is the union of pair intersections, so `height J=2`. The Hilbert-Burch exactness criterion applies over the polynomial ring and makes `R/J` Cohen-Macaulay of dimension two. At the prime `(Pi,Pj)`, all remaining factors are units because no triple shares a line, and `J_(Pi,Pj)=(Pi,Pj)_(Pi,Pj)`. Thus it is generically reduced.

A direct local argument closes the embedded-prime gap: at an associated prime p of `R/J`, depth is zero, and Auslander-Buchsbaum gives `height p=projective_dimension (R/J)_p<=2`. Since every prime above J has height at least two, the associated primes are all the minimal primes. A Noetherian ring with no embedded associated primes and reduced localizations at minimal primes is reduced. Thus J is the radical line union ideal. This also establishes saturation: J equals the intersection of the saturated homogeneous line primes. Alternatively depth two excludes irrelevant-ideal torsion.

For coplanar lines, the ideal is `(P,G)` where G lifts their distinct line product in the plane. It is a reduced complete intersection and ACM. Nonemptiness excludes `I=(1)` and ensures `a>=1,d>=2`; a single line and two intersecting lines fall in the coplanar case.
