## Original task and model conventions

The original finite supercritical discussion asks to improve the sufficient forest bound 2, permitting general positive vertex rates, and separately the equal-rate whisker bound 25. The suggestion that 4/3 might be sharp for both follows these requests. It is a stronger proposed target. The candidate correctly reconciles its historical OPEN/PARTIAL wrappers with that distinction and preserves their original bytes. It does not claim to settle the stronger whisker suggestion.

The operative theorem covers finite simple undirected loopless graphs, independent positive vertex rates, positive integer initial counts, and alpha>1. These conventions include the usual unit-count urn model imported from HHK. A one-vertex loop would survive for every exponent and would already contradict the older forest assertion, so silently adding loops would be invalid; the explicit loopless restriction is appropriate. Parallel-edge extensions are unnecessary to the stated theorem. Isolated vertices and an edgeless graph are explicitly harmless. The source's initial-count notation should not be promoted to all positive real offsets without a separate stochastic argument; the candidate expressly excludes that extension.

The integer-count conditioning lemma is valid. From all ones, prescribe exactly n_e-1 reinforcements of each edge with prescribed positive-rate endpoints. Every step has positive conditional probability. After this finite event the state is exactly n, and the shifted future has the law started at n. A positive tail-failure probability from n would contradict the all-one almost-sure assertion. Finite time shifts preserve limits divided by time and infinite reinforcement. This transfers the needed almost-sure assertions, not attainment of every specified stable point from every initial state. The paper correctly uses unit-state attainment only for the lower triangle obstruction.

## Independent proof audit

On a positive support the field satisfies F=diag(a) grad L for L=-sum a+(1/alpha)sum lambda log S. At an equilibrium its Jacobian is similar to a real symmetric matrix. Changing coordinates to y=a^alpha preserves Hessian inertia there because the gradient vanishes. Direct differentiation gives the asserted quadratic form with a positive `(alpha-1)D` term and negative vertex-incidence squares. No sign, alpha factor or rate coefficient is missing.

At a stochastic limit, each nonisolated positive-rate vertex has positive incident limiting weight because its incident count sum dominates its own firing count. Thus no denominator used in the proof vanishes. For alpha>1 the derivative of a zero coordinate's alpha power is zero; the full Jacobian is the positive-face block direct-summed with -I. This argument needs only a C1 field extension at zero and does not assume a full C2 potential there. Positive eigenvalues are tangent to the fixed-mass simplex since 1^T J=-1^T. Hence neither the unconstrained potential calculation nor a boundary/critical direction manufactures a spurious instability.

For any selected simple cycle, edge equilibrium gives sum D=sum b_v(A_prev^-1+A_next^-1). Since S_v>=A_prev+A_next and `(A+B)^2>=4AB`, one obtains `4 sum c<=sum D`. All off-cycle perturbations are zero. Chords and positive attachments increase the denominators; no cycle is assumed induced, bare, uniformly weighted or at equal rates.

For an even cycle the alternating direction cancels every incidence sum. For an odd m-cycle the two real highest-frequency directions are cyclically consistent and have combined edge-square norm one and combined incidence-square norm `4 sin^2(pi/(2m))`. Summing the two forms leaves at least `[alpha cos^2(pi/(2m))-1]sum D`. Strict positivity forces a positive eigenvalue even if other directions are degenerate. The maximal odd threshold is 4/3 at m=3. This is an all-equilibrium deterministic proof; finite probes are not its universal justification.

The unit triangle spectrum is `-1,3alpha/4-1,3alpha/4-1`. Its strict stability below 4/3 plus the published attainment clause supplies positive probability of a full surviving cycle from an allowed initialization. It therefore rules out every smaller universal strict forest constant. At 4/3 that clause does not apply. A separate formal-series diagnostic along `a=(1+2epsilon,1-epsilon,1-epsilon)` gives `L(a)-L(1)=-epsilon^3+epsilon^4/4+O(epsilon^5)`: zero transverse eigenvalues cannot simply be relabeled strict stability. This local deterministic diagnostic supplies no stochastic equality theorem, and none is claimed.

For equal rates, direct differentiation gives the necessary condition `q^2+r^2 >= (1-1/alpha)(q+r)` for every equilibrium with no positive eigenvalue. The symmetric representative justifies its diagonal use also at critical equilibria. The least-weight leaf argument gives all positive edge weights at least one for alpha>2. An internal edge with weight a<=3/2 consequently has endpoint caps `q,r<=a^alpha/(1+a^alpha)`. Convexity gives the displayed extreme-split bound f(a,s); feasibility and the monotonicity domain are correctly used.

The polynomial bounds on `s_B(a)/a` hold for the full weight interval and 3<=B<=7. The derivative of f along that cap is at least 1/9. All five closed exponent intervals meet exactly, all fractional-power comparisons are valid by positive integer powers, and every margin is positive, including 1/1734 at the first interval. Thus alpha=17/4 is included by a strict certificate, not a continuity guess.

For alpha>=7 the quadratic h has its smaller root in (0,1/2) and its larger root beyond 1/2. The given C=3-sqrt2 substitution bounds the allowed r0 above by C/alpha. The exponential partial sum and geometric tail are exact and strictly below 6. Both endpoint probabilities would then fall below the necessary champion bound, a contradiction. This proves the entire unbounded tail, not a sampled range.

Every internal edge therefore exceeds 3/2 and consumes more than half of the selection probability at each endpoint. Such edges form a matching. Consecutive cycle edges and the two middle edges of a four-edge path violate that property. Thus the whisker proof is genuinely independent of the new odd-cycle result. Unequal rates cannot be inserted into this normalization. The historical rational unequal-rate path has the stated positive leading minors, independently recomputed here, and remains only a credited scope control.

Published HHK Theorem 1 has exactly the finite, alpha>1, positive-rate hypotheses needed for convergence to stable/critical equilibria and strict-stable attainment. ## Priority, chronology and attribution challenges

The numerical odd-cycle threshold itself and the triangle example are established in VHHKR2016. That theorem concerns the uniform equilibrium on a bare equal-rate cycle. It does not imply all-equilibrium weighted-cycle exclusion: for example uniform weights do not even solve the equilibrium equations for an unequal-rate triangle with rates (1,2,3). Its corresponding endpoint totals differ. HHK's even-cycle method does extend immediately with positive logarithmic coefficients and receives explicit credit in the operative paper.

HK2017 supplies the diagonal/champion and least-weight approach. Its displayed c=9/4 constants imply alpha>=24: Lemma 5 needs alpha>81/4; small edges have leaf endpoints above `c(exp(c)+1)`; the exact exponential cap makes this cutoff less than 189/8<24; large edges then exceed 29/16>3/2 and form a matching. At alpha=24 all strict prerequisites still hold. The paper's conservative >24 version and metadata's acknowledged >=24 consequence are compatible. A claim that any improvement of 25 is unprecedented would fail; the candidate avoids that claim.

The old Lemma 5 cannot be quoted at 17/4 because its prerequisite fails. The diagonal test alone also does not improve the forest threshold below 2: the uniform triangle at alpha=3/2 satisfies it while being unstable. HHK's plain-path results cannot be applied to an arbitrary branched support by deleting positive branches; doing so changes its denominators and can change the rates needed for equilibrium. These tempting equivalences leave the central new estimates unsupported rather than proving them earlier.
