# Independent first derivation (sealed before prior reports/code were read)

The literal original finite-graph tasks are to improve the arbitrary-rate sufficient forest threshold 2 and the equal-rate sufficient whisker threshold 25. The subsequent sentence suggests 4/3 as the sharp threshold for both; it is not the literal content of either improvement request. The candidate scope is finite simple loopless graphs, positive rates and positive integer edge counts; this includes unit initial counts of the cited WARM model. No conclusion for all noninteger offsets or infinite graphs is asserted.

Starting from F_e=-a_e+sum_{v in e}lambda_v a_e^alpha/S_v, direct differentiation gives F=diag(a)grad L for L=-sum a+(1/alpha)sum lambda log S. At a positive equilibrium its Hessian has the same inertia as the symmetric Jacobian representative. With y=a^alpha the Hessian quadratic form multiplied by alpha^2 is (alpha-1)sum a^(1-2alpha) w_e^2-alpha sum(lambda/S^2)(sum_incident w)^2. Edge equilibrium gives a^(1-alpha)=b_u+b_v for b=lambda/S.

On any simple cycle the total diagonal coefficient D equals sum b_v(A_prev^-1+A_next^-1). Since S_v>=A_prev+A_next and 1/(A+B)<=(1/A+1/B)/4, 4sum_cycle c<=sum_cycle D. Zero perturbations on all noncycle edges correctly include chords and attachments. Even alternation cancels all incidence sums. For odd m, the cosine and sine pair at angle pi-pi/m has combined unit squared norm at every edge and combined incidence squared norm 4sin^2(pi/(2m)) at every vertex. Summing forms forces a positive direction when alpha cos^2(pi/(2m))>1. This argument is universal over equilibrium weights and rates; no uniformity is presumed. The m=3 maximum threshold is 4/3. Positive eigenvectors are tangent because 1^T J=-1^T. At zero edges derivatives of x^alpha vanish for alpha>1; J splits into the positive face and -I, provided each S_v>0. At a stochastic limit that property follows from the vertex's own positive firing rate. Critical zero transverse eigenvalues do not undermine a strict positive direction.

For equal unit rates the symmetric representative has diagonal alpha-1-alpha(q^2+r^2)/(q+r). Nonpositive spectrum forces q^2+r^2 >= (1-1/alpha)(q+r). The least support weight must have a leaf endpoint when alpha>2, else both endpoint probabilities are <=1/2; hence every positive weight is >=1. For an internal edge of weight a<=3/2, both endpoints have a competitor weight >=1 and q,r<=s_alpha(a)=a^alpha/(1+a^alpha). Convexity bounds (q^2+r^2)/a by f(a,s). The manuscript's derivative is correct: f'=1-2z^2+2(2z-1)s', positive for 1/2<=z<=2/3. Its two polynomial certificates establish that range for 3<=B<=7. The five exact interval endpoints then give a strict contradiction, including alpha=17/4. For alpha>=7, h(r)=alpha r^2-(alpha-1)r+1>=0 with h(1/2)<0 confines r to the lower root. C=3-sqrt2 satisfies h(C/alpha)<=0, so a^alpha<=e^C<6<=alpha-1, contradicting the same diagonal inequality. Thus every internal edge exceeds 3/2 and has endpoint probability >1/2 at both ends; internal edges form a matching. Cycles and paths of four edges contain adjacent internal edges, giving the whisker conclusion independently of the new cycle theorem.

The finite-count extension is a valid positive-probability finite-prefix conditioning argument: from unit counts each fixed integer count vector is reachable through positive probability prescribed firings and selections; a tail failure from that vector would contradict the unit-state almost-sure theorem. This does not extend stable-point attainment to every initial vector, and the manuscript expressly avoids that claim. Published HHK finite Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 are needed to connect these deterministic exclusions to E_infinity; they are imported at their actual interfaces. Independent web access confirms their statements and finite hypotheses. The equal-rate triangle at unit weights has eigenvalues -1,3alpha/4-1,3alpha/4-1; strict stability below 4/3 supports sharpness of the open forest threshold; equality remains outside both deductions.
