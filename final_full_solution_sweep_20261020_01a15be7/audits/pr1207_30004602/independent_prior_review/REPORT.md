At each node two distinct lines cross, so the local Jacobian ideal is the reduced point's maximal ideal. Away from the nodes it is the unit ideal. ## Independent reconstruction of the correction

The following deductions were reconstructed before consulting the historical acceptance portion of AUDIT.md.

Let I=(f0,f1,f2) have height2, D=sum deg(fi), Z its nonempty finite projective zero scheme, A=S/I^sat, M=S/I and N=I^sat/I. The kernel T in 0→T→⊕O(−di)→I_Z→0 is locally free rank2: in a regular local ring of dimension2 a finite-colength ideal has projective dimension1, and the kernel of its free presentation is free. detT=O(−D), so T*=T(D). Line-bundle H¹ vanishing gives N_i=H¹(T(i)) compatibly with multiplication. Let m=min{t:H⁰(T(t))≠0}.

A general ell avoiding Z acts injectively on A. Its Hilbert function stabilizes at lengthZ from p onward. Set t=p−1. If p>0, H¹(I_Z(t)) has positive dimension lengthZ−dimA_t; if p=0, use t=−1 and H⁰(O(−1))=0. In either case H¹(I_Z(t)) injects into H²(T(t)). Serre duality yields a nonzero H⁰(T(D−p−2)). Hence **p≤D−m−2**. This works even for a first Koszul syzygy; no erroneous equality is needed.

For μ_i:H¹(T(i))→H¹(T(i+1)), natural Serre duality gives the transpose map μ_j with **j=D−i−4**, because the source of the dual map comes from dualizing twist i+1 and adding canonical twist−3. The same ell is used. Surjectivity at i follows from injectivity at j. The off-by-one alternative D−i−3 would fail the needed boundary.

If T splits, H¹(T(i))=0 for every i, so M=A and the general form is injective. Otherwise:

- **Strictly unstable:** m<D/2. A minimal section gives 0→O(−m)→T→I_Y(m−D)→0. A divisorial zero would lower m; Y is finite and nonempty, since Y empty would split the extension via H¹(O(D−2m))=0. A general line avoids Y and Z, and T|L=O_L(−m)⊕O_L(m−D). For t≤D−m the quotient has no sections, including equality because Y≠empty. In the restriction sequence, all line sections lift for i≤D−m−2. Thus N multiplication is injective there and, by the dual shift, surjective for i≥m−2. Set c=max(p,m−2). The inequality and 2m<D give c≤D−m−2. For i<c use injection; for i≥c use surjection on N and bijection on A.
- **Semistable, D=2r:** m≥r. Grauert–Mülich gives T|L=O_L(−r)^2. Restriction and duality give injection for i≤r−2 and surjection for i≥r−2. Choose c=r−2=floor((D−3)/2). The saturation inequality gives p≤r−2.
- **Semistable, D=2r+1:** m≥r+1. Balanced splitting is O_L(−r)⊕O_L(−r−1). Injection holds for i≤r−2 and surjection for i≥r−1. Choose c=r−1. The inequality gives p≤r−2<c. There is no missing integer degree. Equality m=D/2 belongs to the even semistable case.

Apply the Snake lemma to 0→N→M→A→0 in consecutive degrees. Since A multiplication is injective, the N and M kernels coincide, and 0→cokerN→cokerM→cokerA→0. The chosen thresholds give injection below c and surjection at/above c on M. Every degree is covered.

For an essential central arrangement of d distinct planes, Euler removes Q from J. A common irreducible divisor of all partials would divide Q; a normal derivative at a generic smooth point of that hyperplane contradicts this. Height is at least2, and pairwise intersection lines give height at most2. A constant linear dependence between partials would define a nonzero constant derivation annihilating every factor; essentiality excludes it. Thus the three nonzero degree(d−1) generators satisfy the setup. No assumption of an Artinian M is introduced.

For rank2 arrangements choose coordinates over K so Q∈K[x,y]; then M=(K[x,y]/(Q_x,Q_y))[z]. Multiplication by z is injective; its quotient is a two-variable homogeneous algebra. Rank1 distinct arrangements consist of one hyperplane, giving J=S; empty arrangements have Q=1 and J=S. The zero quotient is trivial. These cases are covered without undefined saturation or gin minima.
