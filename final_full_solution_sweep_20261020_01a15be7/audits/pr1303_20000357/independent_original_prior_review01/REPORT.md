## Independent mathematical audit

Let f:X->Y be finite dominant of function-field degree n between geometrically integral varieties over a number field. Define delta(Z/k) by density of closed points of exact degree d, not merely by the existence of a degree-d effective zero-cycle. The whole-target dominance hypothesis is essential; the PR states it. In the original surface context a finite map between integral surfaces of equal dimension is automatically dominant onto its integral target. Nothing here asserts whole-target density for a nondominant closed embedding.

### Finite-cover transfers and Galois refinement

Characteristic zero makes the function-field extension separable. Shrink the target so its finite inverse image is finite étale, surjective, of constant rank n. Restriction to a dense open does not change exact-degree density: every open subset of that open is open in the original, and a dense subset of a dense open is dense in the original. Residue fields of closed points are unchanged.

For a closed base point Q on this open, the fiber is a product of finite separable field extensions L_i/k(Q), with positive relative degrees m_i summing to n. Thus1<=m_i<=n and the corresponding closed points have absolute degree deg(Q)m_i. The rank bound is correctly applied on the étale open rather than arbitrary nonflat singular fibers.

For d in delta(X/k), split its dense point set upstairs by the finitely many relative degrees m. Irreducibility forces at least one stratum dense. Its image is dense because otherwise the stratum would lie in the inverse image of a proper closed subset of the surjective target. Its image degrees equal d/m, giving m|d and d/m in delta(Y/k).

Conversely, the full inverse image of a dense degree-e point set downstairs is dense upstairs. For any nonempty open O upstairs, its proper closed complement F has dimension smaller than the common dimension. A finite map is closed and preserves dimensions of closed irreducible subsets; hence f(F) is proper closed. A degree-e point outside f(F) has its entire nonempty finite fiber inside O. Partitioning the dense full inverse image by relative degree then gives em in delta(X/k) for some1<=m<=n. This proof uses all fiber points, not a guessed section of the cover.

If the function-field extension is Galois of order n, further shrink to the smooth/normal target and finite étale normal inverse image. The inverse image is the normalization in the extension, so every field automorphism extends; the generic constant-G torsor structure extends after a further shrinking. In a fiber, G acts transitively on closed components. If their common residue degree is m and a component stabilizer has order s, there are n/s components and total rank n=(n/s)m. Thus m=s divides n. Equivalently, absolute Galois orbits on the geometric G-torsor have subgroup-order sizes. No unjustified action on an initially nonnormal global model is used.

Both general transfer conclusions and the Galois refinement are therefore accepted as written. They do not say the same m works for every degree, nor that arbitrary effective fibers are single points.

### Complete sharp-minimum theorem, including a surface realization

Writing a=min delta(X/k), b=min delta(Y/k), when defined, the first transfer gives a=me with e>=b, so a>=b. The second gives some bm in delta(X/k), with m<=n, so a<=nb. The inequalities are valid. Minima exist in the nice positive-dimensional number-field setting, for instance by the standard finite-map/Hilbert construction; the proof prudently states them conditionally for its broader setup.

For the lower endpoint any degree-n P1 self-map has a=b=1. For the upper endpoint n>=2, Clark's Corollary4 supplies a smooth projective geometrically integral genus-one curve C of index n over every prescribed number field. Index gives an actual k-divisor D of degree n, not merely a rational Picard class. Riemann–Roch gives h0(O(D))=n. After geometric base change, O(D)(-p) has positive degree n-1, so h0=n-1 at every geometric p. Thus O(D) is geometrically globally generated.

Choose a nonzero k-section s0. Each of its finitely many geometric zeros imposes a proper k-linear subspace of sections, since k-sections span all geometric sections after base change. Infinitude of k permits a second section outside their union. The two sections have no common zero and are independent, and define a finite degree-n map C->P1 whose pullback O(1) is O(D). Hilbert irreducibility gives infinitely many rational base fibers that are single closed points of exact degree n. Composing with every degree-r self-map of P1 and applying Hilbert again yields every nr in delta(C/k). Index excludes all other closed-point degrees, so delta(C/k)=nN. Hence a=n,b=1, and the uniform factor n is genuinely optimal for every n. The identity handles n=1. No prescribed-Jacobian or Galois-cover conclusion is attributed to Clark.

These examples can also be made surfaces, if desired in the workshop context, without changing the constants: use C x P1 -> P1 x P1 via the degree-n map on the first factor. Every closed point upstairs has degree divisible by n, since its image on C has degree divisible by n and that degree divides the product point's degree. Conversely, degree-nr points on C together with rational points on the second P1 form a dense degree-nr locus on the surface. Thus delta(C x P1)=nN. P1 x P1 has all positive density degrees. The lower endpoint similarly uses a degree-n self-map on one rational factor. This small completion prevents rejecting the sharp-minimum theorem merely because the displayed examples were curves.

### Residue examples and what they do not prove

For the cubic t->t^3+t over Q, the fiber at q=r^3+r factors exactly as

    (T-r)(T^2+rT+r^2+1).

The quadratic discriminant is -3r^2-4<0; the full cubic discriminant is -4-27q^2<0. The degree-two closed component is therefore on the étale locus, and different rational r give different fibers because the real function r^3+r is strictly increasing. This gives a dense relative-degree-two stratum for a degree-three non-Galois cover. The generic degree is3, and the cubic discriminant is not a square in Q(u), since it has odd-order geometric zeros; thus the non-Galois designation is correct.

For t->t^n over Q(zeta_n), the cover is cyclic Galois of degree n. For m|n, unramified rational primes p give Eisenstein T^m-p at any place above p. The point t=p^(1/m) has relative degree m over p^(n/m), and distinct primes give distinct étale fibers. Every permitted divisor m occurs densely. These statements are accepted, but are statements about residue strata of a map.

The cubic source and target are both P1, so both complete density degree sets equal N. In particular, both divisor-only **set** relations remain true, by taking m=1 for every d or e. A dense m=2 stratum does not negate an existential density-set relation whose witness may be a different multiplier. Similarly, in the upper minimum example delta(X)=nN, delta(Y)=N, the downward maximum multiplier n is forced at e=1, but the upward relation always permits m=1. The examples prove sharp projected constants and possible residue values; they do not establish the optimality of every asserted spectrum-set restriction.

### Curve components and the closed-point bridge

For nice curves C,D and degree-n f:C->D, composing a degree-e map D->P1 with f proves n delta_P1(D) subset delta_P1(C), using the actual published equivalence between map degrees and P1-parameterized points.

For an AV-parameterized degree-e closed point x on D, let [x]+A subset W_D^e with positive-rank abelian subvariety A. Norm after pullback is [n] on J_D, so f* has finite kernel. B=f*(A) is an abelian subvariety with positive rank: an infinite-order rational point cannot become torsion under a homomorphism with finite kernel. Pullback of geometric effective divisors gives f*[x]+B subset W_C^(ne). Multiplication by r>=2 gives r f*[x]+B subset W_C^(rne); only surjectivity of [r] on B as a variety is needed, not the false assertion that [r]:B(k)->B(k) is surjective.

The class must acquire an actual closed representative. Viray–Vogt Proposition5.2.1 supplies a closed P1-parameterized point z on D with [z]=r[x]. Lemma4.1.1 supplies a degree-re map g:D->P1 with z as a rational fiber, hence g*O(1)=O(rx). The composite h=g f has degree rne. Hilbert irreducibility on C produces a single closed degree-rne fiber w, with class [w]=r f*[x]. Therefore [w]+B subset W_C^(rne), making w AV-parameterized, and the actual closed rational fiber also makes it P1-parameterized. The stated r>=2 inclusion is accepted.

At r=1 the argument has a translate of effective *classes*, not a guaranteed degree-ne closed point. If such a point v exists in the translate, its class plus B is the same translate, so AV parameterization follows; existence is precisely the missing step. A reducible effective fiber alone is not a counterexample to the undilated inclusion. This gap is stated honestly in the PR. It is not used here to invalidate the unrelated complete minimum theorem.
