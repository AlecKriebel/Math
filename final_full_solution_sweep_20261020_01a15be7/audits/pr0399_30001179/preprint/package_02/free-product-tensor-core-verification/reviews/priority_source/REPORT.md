## Mechanism comparison, not title matching

### A. The old forward construction cannot furnish this negative answer directly

Let `G(T)` be a free system generated by its embedded spatial tensor system `T`, as in the source's forward theorem. For every finite time partition, the tensor subsystem multiplication identifies `T_t` with an embedded ordered product of its smaller fibres. Thus `T_t` lies in every partition tensor range of `G(T)`, hence in its maximal tensor core `C_t` whenever that core is defined as here. Monotonicity of the generated free hull gives

`G(T) = Hull(T) subset Hull(C) subset G(T)`.

Therefore `Hull(C)=G(T)`. The forward theorem, even applied to a non-Fock/typeII tensor system, cannot by itself produce a free system whose maximal tensor core has a proper hull. This excludes a tempting equivalent-mechanism shortcut. It also explains why the proposed ambient `E`, if the separate proof audit confirms it, contributes something beyond the old `G(T)` construction.

### B. Ordinary Fock approximation is a theorem about a specified ambient space

Attal–Nechita's actual [2008 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.4070), section 3 and Theorem 1(1), uses reduced free Hilbert products and proves density of toy spaces **inside ordinary full Fock space**. Its proof approximates functions on each finite Cartesian-power sector by finite rectangles. It supports the classical finite-word part of the candidate. It neither gives density in the added infinite ordinal sectors nor asserts that every associative continuous pointed free system has no such sectors. Extending its ambient quantifier would assume precisely the missing conclusion.

### C. Nonclassical tensor noises are not already a free generation counterexample

The actual Tsirelson [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0402431), definition 5a1, uses interval **tensor** unitaries. Its classical part and nonclassical remainder are a well-established mechanism. Liebscher's actual [random-set paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306365), definition 3.1 and its measure-class construction, likewise concerns stationary factorizing measure types yielding tensor product systems. Their half-density/Radon–Nikodym identifications and non-Fock phenomena are prior mathematics and should not be described as inventions of this candidate.

What is missing for an exact transfer is the required pointed free-product unitary with its alternating-run decomposition and the proper free hull of the maximal tensor intersection. The source's forward functor applied to those old tensor examples again falls under A and produces a generated free system. A more elaborate old construction might still exist; the selected passages and screens are not a proof against every such construction.

Tsirelson's actual [1998 work](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9806112), theorem 1.6 and the classical-noise tensor identities in§2, concerns stochastic group representations and Brown equivalence. No hypothesis in those passages turns an arbitrary pointed free system into a classical stochastic group-valued Brownian motion. It is not a universal positive-generation obstruction to this candidate.

### D. Superproduct and discrete quadratic systems supply different structure maps

Margetts–Srinivasan's actual [2012 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1283), definition 4.1, requires tensor-product **isometries**, not pointed free-product surjective unitaries. Its Clifford-flow examples and trivial free-flow superproduct invariant cannot be silently substituted for the whole pointed-free-system structure. Shalit–Skeide's actual [2022 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05166v3), definitions 4.1/5.1/5.2, confirms the category distinction and its subsystem conventions. A proper tensor isometry alone does not resolve the target.

Arici–Ge's actual [2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12843), definitions in§2 and4.1 and proposition 4.8 with its proof, studies a free operation on discrete quadratic subproduct systems. Its finite alternating decomposition theorem is related background, not a converse theorem for arbitrary continuous-time pointed free systems. The [publisher metadata](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00020-025-02821-x) dates the journal article29 April 2026, volume 98, article 19; the DOI's 2025 string is not its publication date. The arXiv and journal files were not asserted byte-identical.

### E. Fowler's terminology must not be misread

In the actual publisher [1995 paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1995-039-0), complete§2 printed 752–756, a system called free over an isometric semigroup is the associated Arveson **tensor** product/intertwiner system of its free endomorphism flow. Its multiplication is pulled back from operator composition. The title and word free do not give a theorem about all pointed free-product systems in Skeide's later category. Its Fock/free-flow results remain classical background.
