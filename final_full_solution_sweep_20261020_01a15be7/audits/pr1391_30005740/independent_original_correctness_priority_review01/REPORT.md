## 2. Actual original and success criterion

Primary original: Samuel A. Lopes, joint work with Andrea Solotar, Gerstenhaber algebra structure on Hochschild cohomology, OWR46/2023, printed2702–2703, Question(2); workshop15–20October2023, publisher23July2024. DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/OWR/2023/46 . Actual publisher PDF https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/48175 was retrieved; printed2702/PDF22 was visually inspected as well as read as text, and the continuation/references on2703 were read.

The original considers the subalgebra of the first Weyl algebra over a characteristic-zero field generated by x and h(x)y for an arbitrary polynomial h, and asks whether ordinary HH*(A_h) admits a BV structure when its Nakayama automorphism is not semisimple. The preceding definition requires a degree-minus-one square-zero operator generating the existing cup/bracket. It does not ask about finite-dimensional Frobenius algebras, replace the bracket by zero, ask only for a particular Connes-transfer construction, or demand a nonzero Delta. Question(1), concerning arbitrary associative algebras, and Question(3), concerning Poisson analogues, are separate and are not claimed resolved by this classification.

The source's generator hy and the structural paper's generator yh differ by h' in k[x]; they generate the same embedded algebra. Each satisfies [Y,x]=h. The explicit b used below is defined on the chosen presentation; no unstated ambient-inner formula is used.

For h!=0 the presentation is the specified embedded family. The structural source excludes h=0 explicitly. The original's literal arbitrary-polynomial language needs to remain visible: at h=0 its embedded algebra is k[x], not the abstract polynomial plane obtained by setting h=0 in a two-generator presentation. It has ordinary HH^0=k[x], HH^1=k[x]partial_x, higher zero; Delta(f partial_x)=-f' and Delta(HH^0)=0 gives the usual divergence generator in the original signs. This boundary has an affirmative BV answer, but does not lie in the nonsemisimple branch being classified. The abstract plane also has its familiar divergence generator and must not be identified with that embedded specialization.

For h nonconstant in characteristic zero, nu(x)=x and nu(Y)=Y+h'. On the invariant span of Y and h', nu-id is nonzero square-zero, so nu is not semisimple. For nonzero constant h it is identity. Thus the theorem answers the full nonsemisimple branch, not just an example or split polynomial subfamily. Squarefree nonconstant h gives positive examples even with nonsemisimple nu; repeated-factor h gives negative ones.

## 3. Structural imports and independent elementary reductions

Primary structural source actually read: Lopes–Solotar, J.Noncommut.Geom.15(2021),1373–1407, DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/JNCG/439 , publisher PDF https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/30909 . Section3 constructs a length-two free bimodule resolution; its differential, cochain complex, Proposition3.9 proof, Corollary3.11 and Nakayama paragraph were inspected. Section6.1 gives the derivation spanning and innerness statements, and Theorem6.2 gives the Lie center/decomposition. These are established imports, not inferred from an abstract, a DOI title, or a previous acceptance. The deeper original derivation-classification paper of Benkart–Lopes–Ondrus2015 was not independently retrieved/read in this audit: the published Lopes–Solotar explicit recollection is the primary source for that imported spanning theorem. This is an access/read-scope limit, not an unsupported new lemma.

Write d=monic gcd(h,h') and pi=h/d. The structural conclusions needed are HH^0=k, HH^2=A/dA, HH^n=0 for n>=3. In degree1 use D_f(x)=0,D_f(Y)=f for f in k[x]. The source states D_f is inner iff h|f; HH^1 is spanned by their classes and the classes of ad_{g a_n}, with a_n=pi h^{n-1}y^n for n>=1 in the ambient differential-operator algebra, and a_0=1/d in its localization (monic normalization). It also states ad_{g a_n} is inner whenever d|g.

Here are independent checks of the elementary content, avoiding reliance on a full unpublished cup presentation:

* PBW/Ore basis gives A=direct sum k[x]Y^j. The highest-Y coefficient of [t,x] for Y-degree j>0 is jh times the leading coefficient, with Y-degree j-1. Hence C_A(x)=k[x] in characteristic zero. Commuting also with Y forces a polynomial to have derivative0; HH^0=Z(A)=k.
* If D_f=ad_t, its x component forces t in k[x], whence ad_t(Y)=-ht'. Conversely polynomial antidifferentiation in characteristic zero realizes every h-multiple. Thus D_f inner iff h|f, without an algebraically closed field or finite-dimensional assumption.
* On the resolution, the degree1-to2 map sends (alpha,beta) to [beta,x]+[Y,alpha]-F_alpha(h), where F_alpha(x^s)=sum_{i=0}^{s-1}x^i alpha x^{s-1-i}. The triangular [A,x] map has image hA; [Y,A]=hA follows by integrating coefficients. Modulo hA, F_alpha(h)=h'alpha. Therefore the image is hA+h'A=dA, verifying the actual quotient convention used by the cup calculation. The same resolution gives higher vanishing. Since d|h, dA is a two-sided ideal: [Y,d]=hd' is divisible by d.
* For d=1 all normalizer terms in the spanning theorem are inner, so HH^1 is represented by commuting D_f. This also follows from Theorem6.2(c), whose range deg g<deg d is empty. There is no hidden requirement d!=1 in the positive direction. For constant h even every D_f is inner, recovering Weyl HH*=k.

The unresolved central difficulty is not being passed into a new structural assertion: the all-derivation spanning is explicitly an established published import. The BV obstruction and required cup comparison below are checked directly here.

## 4. Centrality rederived from the published spanning

In addition to checking Theorem6.2(b), centrality of c=[D_d] can be derived from that spanning. Assume h monic, which is harmless under the algebra isomorphism rescaling Y by the nonzero leading coefficient. In the localized ambient Weyl algebra with Y=yh, extend D_d by D_d(x)=0,D_d(y)=1/pi. This respects [y,x]=1 because 1/pi commutes with x. For f=1/pi,

D_d(y^n)=sum_{j=0}^{n-1} binom(n,j+1) f^(j) y^(n-1-j).

This follows by the derivation rule, commuting y past f, and the finite binomial sum. Set P_0=1 and P_{j+1}=pi P_j'-(j+1)pi'P_j. Induction gives f^(j)=P_j/pi^(j+1), with P_j polynomial. Consequently

D_d(g pi h^(n-1)y^n)
 = sum_{j=0}^{n-1} binom(n,j+1) g d^j P_j h^(n-1-j)y^(n-1-j),

which lies in A: the structural paper's basis is {h^m y^m}. Thus [D_d,ad_{g a_n}]=ad_{D_d(g a_n)} is inner. For n=0 the coefficient g/d is killed by D_d, and all [D_d,D_f] vanish. The spanning theorem therefore proves c central in all HH^1. This is not an assertion that D_d is central as an actual derivation; its brackets with the normalizer derivations are inner, which is exactly what is needed.

If d!=1, choose any irreducible nonconstant u|d. Both d and d/u have degrees strictly below deg h and are linearly independent over k (a nontrivial relation would make the nonconstant u a scalar). Thus c!=0 and dim HH^1>1. This elementary pair suffices; no inference from infinite cardinality or Witt-module dimension is needed. Nonmonic h follows by the rescaling isomorphism, or directly from Theorem6.2 and the remaining explicit computations.

## 5. All-derivation cup comparison, with orders and normalization

Let r denote the small-resolution generator for Yx-xY-h. The degree1 comparison sends x,Y to their normalized bars. The proposed degree2 image is

F_2(r)=[Y|x]-[x|Y]-sum_{s>=2} h_s sum_{i=1}^{s-1}[x^i|x]x^(s-1-i),   h=sum h_s x^s.

This is an A-bimodule comparison lifting the identity, not just an antisymmetrization of the relation. Its bar boundary is checked using b[a|b]=a[b]-[ab]+[a]b. For each s>=2,

b sum_{i=1}^{s-1}[x^i|x]x^(s-1-i)
 = sum_{i=0}^{s-1}x^i[x]x^(s-1-i)-[x^s].

The first two terms contribute the four Y/x outer terms and -[Yx]+[xY]=-[h]. The polynomial correction contributes +sum_{s>=2}h_s[x^s]. Constants disappear in normalized bars; the remaining -h_1[x] is exactly the linear term in the small differential. Thus bF_2=F_1 partial_2, including both linear and constant h. The degree1 condition is immediate. Since the small complex is a free resolution, this computes the canonical cup product, not a different model's unrelated operation.

For arbitrary derivations D,E the evaluation is

D(Y)E(x)-D(x)E(Y)-sum_{s>=2}h_s sum_{i=1}^{s-1}D(x^i)E(x)x^(s-1-i).

The right polynomial factor stays on the right: E(x) need not commute with x, so silently moving it would be unjustified. For D=D_f all correction terms and D(x) vanish literally, yielding the exact class

[D_f cup E]=[f E(x)] in A/dA.

This holds for every representative derivation E, with no restriction on E(x), Y-degree, outer/inner type or splitting field. In particular c cup z=0 for every z in HH^1. We neither assume nor need all HH^1 products to vanish. The universal telescope above, rather than a finite polynomial test, proves this all-degree statement.

## 6. All possible operators, and a cup-zero noncommuting pair

Let Delta be any k-linear degree-minus-one bracket generator in the original convention. Negative cohomology vanishes, so Delta(HH^0)=0 and Delta(1)=0. Its restriction to HH^1 is a scalar-valued functional lambda:HH^1->k. Values on HH^2 may be arbitrary for now. The degree1 identity is

[a,b]=-Delta(a cup b)+lambda(a)b-lambda(b)a.

Centrality and c cup z=0 force lambda(c)z=lambda(z)c for all z. Choosing z independent of c gives lambda(c)=0, and then c!=0 forces lambda(z)=0 for every z. Hence any such generator must vanish on all HH^1. In particular it would force every cup-zero degree1 pair to commute, regardless of Delta on HH^2 or whether Delta^2=0. This addresses every linear generator, not just a particular construction. Flipping a global convention for the Gerstenhaber bracket changes signs but not this nonexistence conclusion.

Choose irreducible u|d and put g=d/u, a=[D_g]. In characteristic zero, factorization into irreducibles shows d has exponents e_i-1 when h has exponents e_i; hence pi is a nonzero scalar times the product of the distinct irreducibles and u|pi. Define an actual derivation by

b(x)=pi,    b(Y)=L Y,    L=pi d'/d.

The logarithmic derivative d'/d has only simple irreducible denominators, canceled by pi, so L is polynomial over the original field. Applying b to the relation gives

Lh+h pi'-h'pi=0,

because h=d pi. The calculation uses b(h)=pi h' and [Y,pi]=h pi'; no commuting of Y through L is assumed. The cup formula gives a cup b=[g pi]=[d(pi/u)]=0. The derivation commutator has x component0 and Y component

Lg-pi g' = pi d u'/u^2 = h u'/u^2 = q.

Here u^2|h and u'!=0, so q is a nonzero polynomial. Its degree is deg h-2deg u+deg u'=deg h-deg u-1<deg h. Thus h does not divide q, and the elementary innerness criterion proves [a,b]=[D_q]!=0. This contradicts the necessary condition. The construction treats nonsplit irreducible factors directly; no descent from a splitting field, extension-of-scalars theorem for infinite cochains, or inseparability assumption is smuggled in. Characteristic zero is essential to u'!=0, polynomial integration and HH^0=k.

For h=x^n, n>=2, d=x^(n-1), g=x^(n-2), b(x)=x, b(Y)=(n-1)Y, and q=x^(n-2). In particular the Jordan plane is covered. For h=(x^2+1)^2 over Q the pair has g=1, b(x)=x^2+1, b(Y)=2xY, q=2x; no root of x^2+1 is required.

An ambient ad(pi y) may have a polynomial correction when Y=yh. Its extra term is pi h''-Lh'=h(L'+pi'') and can be nonzero. This discrepancy is in fact an inner D_{h times polynomial} class in characteristic zero, but the proof never needs that repair: b is directly defined and relation-checked. The literal equality of derivations should not be asserted.

## 7. Positive direction and scope closure

If d=1, HH^2 and all higher cohomology vanish and HH^1 is abelian, as verified above. Its bracket with HH^0=k is zero because k-linear derivations kill scalars. These exhaust all degrees, so the entire canonical bracket is zero. Delta=0 generates it and has square0. No unknown cup products or higher-bracket identities remain. All nonzero constants are units and squarefree in this convention; their Weyl algebras also satisfy the theorem.

This is full ordinary-cohomology classification on the intended nonsemisimple branch h nonconstant. It does not resolve all twisted Calabi–Yau algebras, finite-dimensional Frobenius algebras, positive characteristic, a modified zero bracket, twisted/invariant Hochschild substitutes or the separate original Poisson question. These are scope boundaries, not gaps in the stated theorem. There is no remaining mathematical gap found after the independent derivation; the remaining research uncertainty is full historical priority and coverage beyond the inspected literature.
