## Independent finite recognition proof

For n>=1, every reduced n-tuple (w_1,\ldots,w_n) defines an endomorphism. Empty basis images are rejected. Make a based bouquet of n subdivided labelled circles spelling the words, with an inverse orientation for each edge. Its connected graph rank is n; its based loop-label subgroup is (H=\langle w_1,\ldots,w_n\rangle\).

Fold any pair of distinct oriented edges with the same origin and letter, identifying their terminal vertices and inverse edges as well. Every fold removes one geometric edge, so the process terminates without a promise of being an automorphism. It preserves H: forward images preserve labels, while a quotient path lifts piecewise after inserting paths (e_1^{-1}e_2\) between vertices being identified; those paths have freely trivial labels. Such adjustments also track the basepoint. Thus this is a based subgroup test.

In the final immersed labelled graph, cancelling inverse letters in a path cancels an edge and its actual inverse, by uniqueness of outgoing edges with a specified label. Therefore a based loop with reduced label (x_i\) reduces to a single-edge loop labelled (x_i\) at the basepoint. H is the whole free group iff every positive basis letter has such a loop. These loops exhaust all directions at the basepoint, so connectedness implies the entire final graph is the n-petal rose; no unexplained core-trimming or infinite membership search is needed.

This surjectivity criterion detects automorphisms without leaving injectivity as an unsupported assertion. A fold of edges with distinct endpoints lowers E and V by one and preserves rank; equal endpoints lower rank. Rank never increases. An accepted sequence starts and ends at rank n, hence all its folds preserve rank. Such a fold is a homotopy equivalence: choose one terminal vertex as representative (choose the common origin if it is an endpoint), retain one folded edge, and use (e_1^{-1}e_2\) and its inverse to adjust the endpoints of all other edges in a reverse map. The composites are homotopic to identity; this also covers a loop folded with a nonloop edge. The induced based map from the original bouquet to the final rose is exactly the input endomorphism, and is an isomorphism. Conversely every automorphism has H=Fn and passes. This is a uniform finite decision algorithm on every finite tuple, not a recognizer that might wait forever on nonautomorphisms.

## Exact maximum and termination

Fix n>=1,L>=0. Enumerate all reduced words of length<=L over the finite alphabet (X_n^{\pm1}\), and all n-tuples of these words. The number of words is

\[
 1+2n\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(2n-1)^j,
\]

with empty sum at L=0. This enumeration is finite, complete and uniform in n,L. Apply the terminating folding test to every tuple. Each accepted tuple is an actual automorphism of the desired norm, and all such automorphisms occur, since reduced basis images determine an endomorphism uniquely.

Only now invoke the imported algorithm A on accepted tuples. Every invocation halts by its actual hypothesis. Freely reduce all returned basis words. An empty basis means a trivial fixed subgroup and contributes zero. A one-element basis consists of a nontrivial generator of the actual cyclic fixed subgroup; its length is the required based length. A basis of cardinality>=2 defines a noncyclic free group and is omitted. This classification needs a free basis, not just a redundant generating list; A's explicit contract supplies one. In a nontrivial infinite cyclic group the only generators are g and g^-1, so their reduced lengths agree and no generator-choice minimization is hidden.

Return the maximum of those lengths and zero. The resulting program has finitely many invocations, all on valid inputs, and therefore halts. Its output is precisely

\[
 B(n,L)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{|g|:\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(F_n),\max_i|\alpha(x_i)|\le L,
                  \operatorname{Fix}(\alpha)=\langle g\rangle\ne1\}\bigr).
\]

Hence B is total recursive, exact and nondecreasing in L. Mere finiteness of the input class would not have proved computability; the recognition and halting/output guarantees are indispensable. Enumerating fixed words until one appears cannot certify triviality or completeness of a basis and is not a substitute for A. A recursive runtime formula is unnecessary for this consequence, although the v6 states more effectiveness.

Boundary cases are correct: B(0,L)=0 for the trivial group; B(n,0)=0 for n>=1 because no automorphism kills all basis generators; B(1,L)=1 for L>=1 because the two automorphisms of Z are identity and inversion, whose fixed subgroups are respectively Z and trivial. Treating the trivial group as cyclic contributes only zero. Ranks0/1 may be handled directly without imposing extra hypotheses on the deep algorithm.

The imported A is for arbitrary finite-rank input, with the finite sequence of basis images supplied, so the construction is uniform in n, not an uncomputable choice of a separate program for each rank. It computes a bound rather than a closed-form rate. This review does not implement A, compute general B values, or claim a smaller complexity class.

## Based versus outer data

For (F_2=\langle a,b\rangle\), let (g_k=b^kab^{-k}\) and let alpha_k conjugate by g_k. Its fixed subgroup is exactly \(\langle g_k\rangle\). To verify the centralizer claim directly, a reduced word not a power of a can be written (a^rv a^s\), with nonempty v beginning and ending in b-letters. Its conjugation of a reduces to (a^rvav^{-1}a^{-r}\), still containing b-letters, so cannot equal a. Thus the centralizer of a is precisely its cyclic subgroup; conjugating by b^k proves the assertion for g_k.

All these automorphisms are in the identity outer class, yet their based fixed generators have length2k+1, while their cyclic reductions have length1. The only cyclic generators are g_k and its inverse. The actual forward-image norm is3 for k=0 and4k+3 for k>=1, so this does not contradict the proved norm bound.
