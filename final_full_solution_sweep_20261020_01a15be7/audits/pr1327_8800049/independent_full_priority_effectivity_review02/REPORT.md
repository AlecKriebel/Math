## Aggregate algorithm: exact count and termination

The first central attack was whether an arithmetic count and a geometric enumeration count the **same unweighted finite set**. A mass weighted by automorphism orders would not suffice. The text explicitly counts polarized k-isomorphism classes.

For every overorder S of R=Z[pi,q/pi], it enumerates weak ideal classes using the finite conductor quotient, checks `(I:I)=S`, and then uses Pic(S) on precisely that stratum. Marseglia's ideal-monoid Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 4.6, independently read with proofs, justify coverage and the free action there. This includes noninvertible ideals over non-Gorenstein/non-Bass orders; replacing them by invertible ideals alone would lose classes. No such replacement occurs.

For a principal polarization, the scalar condition must actually give `aI=bar(I)^t`. A principal scalar-colon ideal by itself is insufficient for arbitrary noninvertible ideals. The proof adds the decisive equality check after choosing a scalar generator. This check is sound; completeness follows because an actual a gives colon ideal aS, and any other generator differs by a unit of S. Imaginary/CM-type positivity filters and the finite unit/norm quotient then cover all polarization orbits. Conjugation stability of the relevant order follows when a polarization exists. Lenstra's unconditional unit/class-group construction supports finite termination; no GRH-dependent shortcut is assumed.

On the geometric side the proof enumerates finite presentations of A in P^26 together with group laws, a principal theta divisor and line-bundle witnesses for the |3Theta| embedding. All equations and chart witnesses are finite exact data. Smoothness, properness, connectedness via H0=k, the Hilbert polynomial 27t^3, and the group/Cartier identities can be checked by the specified effective scheme machinery. Amplitude and Euler characteristic one give a principal polarization. Finite-field Lang descent provides a k-rational theta representative for each k-principal polarization.

The Frobenius filter does not incorrectly apply a genus-three curve point-count formula to a threefold. It takes prime-to-p torsion at a modulus larger than the coefficient-ambiguity bound, enumerates until all torsion splits, computes the original q-Frobenius matrix and recovers the integer characteristic polynomial from its residues and Weil bounds. All these steps have a finite termination argument.

Deduplication is finite in PGL27(k), with theta-translation witnesses. If two objects are principally polarized group-isomorphic, their theta representatives differ by a k-rational translation: the principal map A→Adual identifies the needed Pic0 difference with a k-point. The |3Theta| embeddings then differ by a projective linear map. Conversely accepted witnesses identify the objects. This argument uses the actual theta divisor; it does not infer equality of polarizations by cancelling 3 in characteristic 3.

Consequently every retained presentation injects into the finite arithmetic set of size N and every one of its classes eventually appears. Stop after N distinct classes. This supplies a real NO stopping rule, including the case N=0. The abstract lemma “enumerate a complete decidable finite set until its known cardinality is reached” is elementary; any novelty lies in establishing these particular matching contracts, not in that lemma.

The fixed convention matters: matching only a CM type or a characteristic polynomial would not identify the particular integral ideal.

D2 searches jointly over a k-model A, an abelian scheme B over a locally unramified number-field DVR, a special-fiber identification over k', a polarization/theta package, a lifted endomorphism F_B, finite semialgebraic topology witnesses, and an integral unimodular comparison U. Crucially F_B reduces to the base change of **the original q-Frobenius** and satisfies h(F_B)=0. It is a k'-linear map, not the absolute q-power operation on k' coefficients. D3's canonicity test is stronger than merely requiring extra CM endomorphisms. The ordinary polynomial factors over Z_p into relatively prime unit-root and positive-slope factors. The two lifted factors are uniquely determined, so their split lift is canonical. Serre–Tate equivalence and full faithfulness identify B and its marked maps with the canonical lift. ## Recognition, characteristic two and original-field descent

The relevant [Beauville–Ritzenthaler intrinsic construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3683v1) was independently checked against its stated hypotheses. A singular theta supplies the hyperelliptic case and trivial obstruction. In the smooth case the theta intersection at a good point is a genus-seven curve with free constant C2 action; the quotient has genus four. In characteristic two this is an etale constant group, not mu2. Invariant differentials are computed as a kernel, with no division by two.

The canonical genus-four quadric carries the obstruction in its ruling character. A rational **line**, rather than a rational point, decides splitting. Direct line enumeration works in every characteristic; the optional characteristic-two scalar criterion uses the Artin–Schreier trace. Many nonsplit quadrics have rational points, and our finite negative controls explicitly confirm why a point test would fail.

Recognition may find its good point over an odd extension. If the original obstruction on Frobenius is s in {+1,-1}, restriction gives s^n; odd n preserves s, including characteristic two where these symbols represent a character rather than distinct ground-field scalars. Thus it decides the original field. An even extension would erase a nontrivial character and would be unsound. The proof requires and uses oddness.

## Quantitative bad-locus bound

The strongest new quantitative formula in the packet is

`B(Q)=2Q^2+18Q^(3/2)+2321Q+24sqrt(Q)+2270`.

I checked its delicate rational-image issue independently. A rational image of [2] on a theta surface need not have a rational preimage. The proof does not simply compare their rational-point counts.

For a twist S of Sym^2(C), graded Kunneth invariants with Q_l coefficients give Betti numbers `(1,6,16,6,1)`, including when the ground characteristic is two. Weights give `#S(F_Q) ≤ Q^2+6Q^(3/2)+16Q+6sqrt(Q)+1`; the twist need not have the same Frobenius operator as the untwisted surface.

Distinct geometric preimages of [2]|D differ by a nonzero geometric two-torsion point t. The collision curve is D∩(D+t). Its degree after pushforward by [2] in the |3Theta| embedding is 72 by the projection formula. Translation by t acts freely on that curve and fixes its [2]-image. Whether it preserves a component or exchanges components, the total generic geometric multiplicity is at least two; inseparability only increases cycle multiplicity. Therefore each reduced image has degree at most 36. There are at most 63 nonzero geometric two-torsion points, even in characteristic two, giving total reduced collision degree at most 2268. Lachaud–Rolland Theorem 2.1 applies to geometric projective sets not necessarily defined over F_Q, so these images contribute at most `2268(Q+1)` rational points. Outside them a singleton geometric fiber is Frobenius fixed and therefore rational over the perfect finite field, even if the scheme fiber is nonreduced. This establishes the corrected image bound.

For the difference locus, the absence of a degree-two pencil makes the nonzero ordered-pair representation unique. The split/nonsplit twist counts are `N1^2-N1+1` and `N2-N1+1`. Weil bounds give the common upper bound `Q^2+12Q^(3/2)+37Q+18sqrt(Q)+1`. Adding the three contributions gives exactly B(Q), including its linear and constant terms. Thus no rational-preimage assumption was smuggled into the final coefficients.

Let `L(Q)=(sqrt(Q)-1)^6`. At Q=81, L=262144 and B=216731. Setting sqrt(Q)=9+s, our independent integer-polynomial calculation gives ascending coefficient lists

- L−B: `[45413,144600,57661,10150,958,48,1]`;
- 5L−6B: `[10334,670992,284526,50660,4788,240,5]`.

All are positive for s≥0. Therefore B/L<5/6 and more than one sixth of the rational points are good whenever Q≥81. The least sufficient odd extension has degree seven for q=2, five for q=3 or 4, three for 5≤q<81, and one for q≥81. These statements concern the proof's sufficient threshold, not an optimal bound.
