## Deliberate boundary and falsification checks

- **Reducedness cannot be discarded.** In F2[z_i]/(z_i^2), finite-support permutations move two nonzero polynomials to disjoint variable supports, so their product stays nonzero and zero is G-prime. Yet every prime contains every z_i, making a zero prime-core impossible. Unique squarefree supports justify the product claim. A finite dual-number computation separately confirms that a nonzero squarefree prefix can have a zero power; the finite model itself is not asserted G-prime.
- **Algebraic closedness matters to the ascent lemma.** F4 tensor_F2 F4 has two nonzero linear factors whose product vanishes, despite each original factor being a field. The checker verifies exact F4 arithmetic. This falsifies dropping the hypothesis from the imported ascent assertion, not the main theorem’s behavior over every nonclosed field.
- **Flatness alone is insufficient.** Over Z, the intersection of all(2^m) is zero; after extending to Q, each individual ideal becomes Q and their intersection is Q. The submitted basis-coordinate proof avoids this failed generalization. Finite tests cannot establish this infinite limit or the theorem’s arbitrary-family case; the direct proof does.
- **Rational-point replacement fails.** The stable diagonal, with all first-column variables equal and all other columns zero, is a fixed copy of Spec(K[t]). Its nonclosed generic point exists. The rational diagonal alone has an infinite cofinite topology; its whole space is irreducible without a generic point. Thus replacing all primes by rational points would remove the required witness.
- **Invariant-ring replacement fails.** A nonconstant polynomial has finite row support. A finite permutation moves that support to disjoint rows, where equality to the original polynomial forces both to be constant. Hence S^G=K. Yet the all-zero and all-one fixed points have distinct closed orbit images in Q. Spec(S^G) erases the target.
- **KQ is needed.** The binary sequences i mod2 and the indicator of i mod4>=2 differ infinitely often, so their finite-support permutation orbits differ. Both contain infinitely many zeros and ones. Every finite pattern is obtainable from either by finitely many disjoint swaps with unused tail entries. Consequently a finite-support polynomial vanishes on one orbit exactly when it vanishes on the other, so their orbit closures coincide. The orbit quotient need not be T0; KQ identifies these points. Finite prefixes of the swap construction are tested, while the infinite orbit distinction is a deduction.
- **Finite width and ordinary finite generation are distinct.** The ideal generated by all first-column variables has no finite ordinary generator list, since a missing row survives when every used row is set to zero. With infinitely many columns, the stable ideals generated by the first m columns form a strict chain. The countable-core argument alone therefore cannot supply the missing Noetherian conclusion for that enlargement. No such enlargement is claimed.
