## Exact original scope and convention issue

Put t=pi/n, omega=pi−2t, xi=1, eta=exp(i omega), S=sin(2t), lambda=2cos(t), lambda_k=sin((k+1)t)/sin(t). The side-one polygon has clockwise vertices X0=O=0, X1=eta,...,X_(n−1)=xi and lies above the horizontal axis. A reachable point is the first subsequent vertex of the unfolded trajectory starting at that same O, in the closed sector0<=arg<=omega. Reflection at a vertex is not extended through to another later vertex.

Fuchs's reachable polygons are **linear** SL(2,R) images of this initial polygon, with O fixed and all other vertices reachable. Orientation and area are preserved. No arbitrary translation, movable origin or unconstrained affine image is included. Conjecture2.3 explicitly quantifies every n>=5 and every such polygon; its clockwise nonzero vertex types must be A0,A1,...,A_(n−3),A0. Conjecture2.4 asks that every reachable point lie on a reachable polygon and parenthetically that infinitely many such polygons exist. The surrounding section treats the nontrivial n>=5 setting. D answers the full2.3 range; F refutes both readings of2.4 for every n>=5. Even the independent n6 counterexample below suffices to refute either universal assertion. No claim that D/F resolve n3/n4 is made here.

N means number of trajectory segments, not number of reflections. With Fuchs Fig8 angles, alpha in[0,omega], beta in[2t,pi], the preceding parity sentence selects the branch:

* N odd: alpha+beta=2jt,1<=j<=n−1, type k=n−1−j modulo n−2.
* N even: beta−alpha=2jt,−(n−4)<=2j<=n, type k=j−1 modulo n−2.

Printed cutoffs are just the beta endpoint bounds. For odd n=2m+1, the extra printed first-branch indices m,m+1 impose a negative upper bound on alpha, hence contribute nothing. They need not be silently edited to validate a theorem.

There is a real inconsistency in the isolated sentence saying A0 is always characterized by beta−alpha=2t. Every one-segment diagonal OX_(k+1) has alpha=(n−2−k)t,beta=(n−k)t, so every diagonal satisfies that equation. Fuchs expressly labels them Ak in text and Fig9; parity selects the sum branch and reproduces those labels. Both sum and difference are multiples of2t only when alpha is a multiple of t; then the first encountered vertex lies in the initial polygon and N=1. Thus the ambiguity is confined exactly to the sides/diagonals, for which the explicit labels resolve it. D/F use that coherent full definition, not the false unconditional shortcut. The eta coordinate's extra minus sign in the later unitary-pair paragraph is likewise contradicted by the initial geometry and n6 basis; no different polygon is substituted.

## Adversarial mathematical audit

The following checks were made against the actual publication, not inferred from the PR's acceptance flag. Ferudun PDFpp9–16 supplies the required geometric framework, pp17–19 all five D steps and the unitary-pair converse, pp20–22 Lemma6.1/F. The unrelated B/C/E/G/H claims were not promoted by association.

**Gluing and angular invariant.** A side reflection of a regular polygon has the same image set as its point reflection through the side midpoint; the perpendicular-bisector polygon symmetry proves this. Developed copies are c+epsilon P, and crossing side s changes c by epsilon(Xs+X_(s+1)). Glue P to−P using translations w↦w−Xs−X_(s+1). The actual corner successor is(epsilon,s,omega)=(−epsilon,s+1,0), giving one cone vertex for odd n and two for even n. B=s omega+phi modulo(n−2)pi is continuous around each vertex and agrees with physical ray direction modulo pi. For odd n it winds twice; no injectivity is assumed. K=(B(reversed terminal ray)−B(initial ray))/pi modulo n−2 matches the parity-selected type, including sides and diagonals.

An arbitrary affine automorphism is not assumed to preserve K. The specified polygon rotations, central symmetry and cylinder twists have a common B-offset at every cone vertex; this is essential when there are two vertices. For the direction lift F, F(x+pi)=F(x)+pi. The common offset cancels at the endpoints, giving K invariance; the bisector reflection changes its sign. This avoids incorrectly identifying cone-sheet rays merely because their planar holonomies agree.

**Cylinders, full twists and descent.** A strip between XaX_(a+j) and X_(a+1)X_(a+j−1),2<=j<=n−1, doubled through a side, is a cylinder of height sin((j−1)t) and circumference lambda lambda_(j−2). The parallelogram construction identifies both endpoint families on each boundary modulo that circumference. The triangular j2 and side-boundary j=n−1 degeneracies remain valid half-disk charts; a collapsed endpoint does not create another family. All moduli equal2cot(t), so one full shear glues identically along every cylinder boundary and fixes the appropriate base rays, with B-offset zero.

A ray turned through an angle strictly less than pi toward a boundary cylinder enters its specific half-disk. Its straight lift cannot hit a vertex in the open strip. A vertex-ending connection must cross a full height; exact height forces an opposite-boundary vertex lift. Conversely any straight segment between opposite-boundary lifts is unobstructed in the open strip. These statements concern actual initial germs, not only vector directions.

The bounded-length finiteness argument is valid. Normalize a middle crossed side to[(0,0),(1,0)]. If two adjacent crossings turn around the same endpoint, the first/third ray coordinates have horizontal component<=0 because omega>=pi/2; their chord cannot cross the positive interior of that middle side. If the turns use different endpoints, first/third side segments have horizontal separation at least1. A nonadjacent-side pair has a positive compact minimum distance. Thus a length bound bounds edge crossings, hence finitely many words/endpoints. A non-base vector in the kt-to-(k+1)t wedge has y>0,x>y cot(t); the inverse twist replaces x by x−2y cot(t), strictly reducing length. Finiteness makes descent terminate. A terminal base-direction ray meets an initial polygon vertex; rotations/central symmetry move its actual corner to the distinguished initial corner. The two side representatives belong to the same orbit. This proves the portion of TheoremA needed for D/F without a circular appeal to D or F.

**D: excluding the wrong side type.** Let u=gxi,v=geta. Both rays are in the distinguished sector and det(u,v)=S, so their actual clockwise separation is in(0,pi), preserved by every orientation-preserving affine pullback. Choose a type-preserving map taking delta_k to the connection for v. The pulled-back u must enter the right cylinder, whose height is sin((k+2)t), with transverse displacement S/lambda_k. The resulting inequality is cos((2k+3)t)>=cos(3t); on3t<=(2k+3)t<=2pi−3t it leaves only k0 or n−3, with exact-height equality.

For k=n−3, the first cylinder is the triangle between O,X_(n−2),xi, height sin(t). The additional required point gX_(n−2)=v+(lambda²−1)u pulls back into that same actual half-disk with total transverse displacement(lambda²−1)sin(t). At its first boundary crossing, a vertex would be an illegal intermediate vertex. Otherwise it crosses the interior of the negative long diagonal into the second cylinder, of height sin(3t). The remaining displacement is(lambda²−2)sin(t)=sin(3t)−sin(t), positive and strictly smaller than sin(3t) for every n>=5, so it cannot terminate at a vertex. This excludes that type, including the n5 boundary degeneration. Applying the sector reflection on both sides of g repeats the argument for u using gX2. Thus both side vertices are A0, using exactly the four stated hypotheses.

The k0 side cylinder has height S and circumference lambda². Opposite-boundary lifts are xi+t'eta with t' in lambda²Z or lambda²Z−1. These come from the side and delta1 by full twists and have distinct types0 and1. The A0 side selects the first family. One integer twist therefore aligns both actual side connections, yielding an affine automorphism with derivative g. The interval between their initial rays maps into the original corner; every diagonal image starts there and is reachable, with the required K/type. This proves full reachability and the clockwise type order. The same exact-height argument with two A0 points proves the unitary-pair converse. It does not infer reachable polygons from arbitrary determinant-one holonomies.

**F(a): infinitely many A0 polygons.** A map taking eta to an A0 connection w yields side connections u_i=Axi+i lambda²w, with det(u_i,w)=S. This handles both boundary rays; a planar vector in the sector alone was not used as a reachability criterion.

**F(b): the common integer and endpoint cases.** For1<=k<=n−3, write X_(k+1)=a eta+b xi with a=lambda_k sin((k+2)t)/S,b=lambda_k sin(kt)/S>0. D fixes w's vertex index in every reachable polygon. Thus any second polygon has v'=v+i c1 w,u'=u−i' c2 w, where c1=lambda lambda_(k−1)/lambda_k,c2=lambda lambda_(k+1)/lambda_k and i,i' are integers. ## Independent n6 obstruction without D/F or angle conventions

Fuchs's own n6 basis is xi=(1,0),eta=(−1/2,sqrt(3)/2), with initial hexagon coordinate vertices(0,0),(0,1),(1,2),(2,2),(2,1),(1,0). Tile vertices have integer p,q>=0,p+q!=2mod3. Reachability additionally requires gcd1, or gcd2 and p+q=1mod3. Thus w=(5,4) is reachable.

A candidate reachable hexagon has side vectors v=v1,u=v5, det(u,v)=1, and other vertices2v+u,2v+2u,v+2u. The reachable even middle vertex forces sum(u)+sum(v)=2mod3; neither side sum can be2, so both are1mod3. Therefore w, whose sum is0, cannot be either side. Its odd first coordinate excludes the even middle vertex. If w=v+2u, positivity bounds u=(a,b) to0<=a,b<=2 and determinant requires4a−5b=1, with no solution in that box. If w=2v+u, the only nonnegative determinant-one candidate is v=(1,1),u=(3,2); neither is a tile vertex. This exhausts all five positions without using D, F, type labels, an angular convention or a numerical cutoff. The mirrored point(4,5) similarly belongs to none, and(1,2) belongs only to the initial polygon.

The source's n6 proof of2.3 is sound and remains credited. The paragraph headed as proofs of2.4/2.5 starts with an A0 unitary pair and classifies its line; it contains no implication placing every other reachable point on a polygon. Its arithmetic step3/offset2 equal lambda²/lambda²−1 at n6, not the printed lambda+1/lambda. The exact counterexample explains the heading's failure. No acceptance of the full TheoremE follows from identifying this local mismatch.
