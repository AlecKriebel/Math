# Independent reconstruction before earlier opinions

Established 2026-10-21 UTC, after binding the final six publication files and reading the full original two-page problem and the final manuscript TeX, before opening earlier review conclusions.

## Exact claim and scope

The fixed rationally invertible polynomial map is L(u,v)=(u,(u²+1)v), over Q, and its inverse is (u,v/(u²+1)). Its rational invariant is u. Generic invariant fibers are affine lines and have genus zero. The coefficient (u²+1)^m is nonconstant for m>0, proving infinite order in characteristic zero. This is not a polynomial automorphism over C.

The original source fixes the extension degree and then explicitly restricts the displayed statistic to q=p. It counts points of F_p², divided by p², with period threshold px. Its first conjecture has a universal birational existence assertion; the subsequent integrable conclusion is a proposed reciprocal-integer step law. Its genus-one translation explanation is a later heuristic, not a stated restriction of that universal assertion. Rational forward formulas are treated projectively; this example has a polynomial forward formula and a total affine inverse at every selected prime. The admissible primes p=3 mod 4 have density 1/2 among primes. The literal general existence assertion therefore includes this affine example. This says nothing about an intended narrower elliptic or polynomial-automorphism problem. The separate second conjecture uses prime averaging and p² scaling and is outside the claim.

## Reconstruction and attempted falsification

1. For p=3 mod 4, u²+1 never vanishes. Every fiber is multiplication by a nonzero scalar, so the full affine map is a permutation. A point with v=0 has period one; each of the p−1 remaining points on the u fiber has period ord(u²+1), which divides N=p−1. This is point weighting, not cycle weighting.
2. Every proper divisor of N is at most N/2<p/2. For fixed 1/2≤x<1 and p>1/(1−x), N>px. Hence the only excluded points are the p−1 nonzero points on each of the P_p primitive fibers: D_p(x)=1−(p−1)P_p/p². At x=(p−1)/p this formula is false, as all points are included. At p=3,x=3/4 the eventual condition also fails. The manuscript explicitly limits the claim accordingly.
3. The primitive-element character indicator follows by factoring the squarefree-divisor sum prime by prime. Its principal contribution here is pθ_p, since every u²+1 is nonzero. For nonprincipal χ, the sum over u equals η(−1)J(η,χ). If χ≠η, both characters and their product are nonprincipal and the Gauss-sum identity gives magnitude √p. The exceptional χ=η has J(η,η)=−η(−1), so its magnitude is 1, still at most √p. Thus |P_p/p−θ_p|≤θ_p(2^ω(N)−1)/√p. There is no missing zero or unhandled principal product.
4. Splitting the six prime factors below 16 from factors at least 17 gives 2^ω(N)≤64N^(1/4). Substitution into the exact CDF gives an error at most 1/p+64p^(−1/4). This tends to zero for every fixed x in the claimed interval.
5. The shifted-prime mean follows from θ_p=Σ_(d|p−1)μ(d)/d. For K=(log X)^6, the tail is at most XΣ_(d>K)d^(−2)=O(X/K). The small moduli use Siegel–Walfisz with fixed logarithmic range and arbitrary logarithmic error; the weights 1/d give only O(log K) loss. The absolutely convergent coefficient series follows from φ(d)≥√(d/2). The product is the classical Artin constant A, not an unproved fixed-base Artin conjecture.
6. Its factor at 2 is 1/2, and bounding the odd-prime reciprocal sum by all n≥3 except n=4 gives A≥(1/2)(1−5/12)=7/24>13/48. CRT and Dirichlet let p=3 mod 4 and p=1 modulo an increasing product of odd primes; this forces θ_p→0. Conversely, if only finitely many admissible primes had θ_p≥1/24, the two equal-density odd classes and θ_p≤1/2 would force the global mean to be at most 13/48. The two subsequences establish the displayed limsup/liminf bound, but do not establish the arbitrary further-selection conclusion.
7. The stronger conclusion requires the old atomless limiting law over all primes. If θ_p→c on a positive-upper-density subset, arbitrarily small closed intervals about c would all have empirical mass at least its upper density. Atomlessness contradicts continuity from above. A subset of the admissible density-1/2 class with positive upper relative density has positive upper density among all primes, so no conditional scalar law restricted to the class is required.
8. CDF failure at one point alone would not exclude weak convergence at an atom. The manuscript avoids that gap: h(t)=max(0,min(1,2t−1)) is bounded continuous on [0,∞). Proper-divisor periods contribute zero; maximal periods contribute 1−2/p. Its integral is exactly (1−2/p)(p−1)P_p/p²=θ_p+o(1). Weak convergence would imply scalar convergence and is therefore impossible on the stated subsets.

## Provisional independent result

No mathematical counterexample or circular step has been found in the full written theorem. The primary-body check of the continuous scalar law remains necessary. This reconstruction neither certifies historical firstness nor claims any conclusion for moving x→1, arbitrary zero-density selections, higher extension degrees, projective singularities, elliptic families, polynomial automorphisms, or the prime-average conjecture.
