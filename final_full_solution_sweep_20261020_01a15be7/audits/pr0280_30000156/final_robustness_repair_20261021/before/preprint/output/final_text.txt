                                                 Abstract
          For the ﬁxed birational map L(u, v) = (u, (u2 + 1)v), reduction at every prime p ≡ 3 (mod 4)
      permutes all of F2p . Its point-weighted distribution of periods divided by p has no limit. At
      every ﬁxed 1/2 ≤ x < 1, the distribution function is 1 − φ(p − 1)/(p − 1) + o(1), with limsup 1
      and liminf at most 23/24. Moreover, neither these distribution-function values nor the period
      measures converge on any further prime subset of positive upper relative density. The latter
      statement uses the classical continuous empirical law for φ(p − 1)/(p − 1). The contribution is a
      precisely delimited application to the broad existence assertion in the 2004 Oberwolfach problem
      on ﬁnite-ﬁeld maps. The triangular family, primitive-value estimates, totient mean and scalar
      distribution law are established ingredients; no new arithmetic theorem or historical ﬁrstness is
      claimed.

Lemma 3. If a bounded real sequence bp , indexed by primes, has an atomless limiting empirical
law, it cannot converge along a prime subset of positive upper density relative to the primes.

Proof. Suppose bp → c on a set A with upper density δ > 0. For each ε > 0, all but ﬁnitely many
members of A have bp ∈ [c − ε, c + ε]. Choose endpoints Xj → ∞ realizing the upper density. The
limiting empirical measure assigns the closed interval at least δ: its endpoints have measure zero,
so its frequencies converge. Letting ε ↓ 0 and using continuity from above forces an atom of mass
at least δ at c, a contradiction.

   If Dp (x) converged along a set satisfying (3), then (11) would force θp to converge there. The
density of S and Lemma 3, applied to (14), exclude that possibility.

5    Attribution, historical boundary, and reproducibility
The mechanism is a short application of established results. The speciﬁc assertion documented here
combines the exact point-period identity (6) with classical arithmetic inputs for this ﬁxed map and
the statistic in (2). A bounded search of the closest inspected ﬁnite-ﬁeld dynamics sources did
not authenticate an earlier statement of this exact application. This does not certify worldwide
absence, historical ﬁrstness, or present open-problem status.
    A speciﬁc unresolved bibliographic lead must be retained. Roberts [7, p.220, reference 9] cites
N. Neumärker, J. A. G. Roberts, C.-M. Viallet and F. Vivaldi, The dynamics of reversible bi-
rational maps over ﬁnite ﬁelds, UNSW Preprint (2011). The body and its version relationships
were not recovered in the bounded audit. Citation contexts cannot determine whether that unseen
manuscript contains the present application. If an exact prior statement is later authenticated, this
note’s historical description requires reconciliation; none of the mathematical deductions depend
on assuming its absence.
