Abstract
For the ﬁxed birational map L(u, v) = (u, (u2 + 1)v), reduction at every prime p ≡ 3 (mod 4)
permutes all of F2p . Its point-weighted distribution of periods divided by p has no limit. At every
ﬁxed 1/2 ≤ x < 1, the distribution function is 1−φ(p−1)/(p−1)+o(1), with limsup 1 and liminf
at most 23/24. Moreover, neither these distribution-function values nor the period measures
converge on any further prime subset of positive upper relative density. The latter statement uses
the classical continuous empirical law for φ(p − 1)/(p − 1). A supplementary proposition treats
all odd primes on the invariant regular domain, and under uniform-point deletion or arbitrary
exceptional-period completion. These conventions cannot restore convergence on any positiveupper-density prime selection. The contribution is a precisely delimited application to the broad
existence assertion in the 2004 Oberwolfach problem on ﬁnite-ﬁeld maps. The triangular family,
primitive-value estimates, totient mean and scalar distribution law are established ingredients;
no new arithmetic theorem or historical ﬁrstness is claimed.

Lemma 3. If a bounded real sequence bp , indexed by primes, has an atomless limiting empirical
law, it cannot converge along a prime subset of positive upper density relative to the primes.
Proof. Suppose bp → c on a set A with upper density δ > 0. For each ε > 0, all but ﬁnitely many
members of A have bp ∈ [c − ε, c + ε]. Choose endpoints Xj → ∞ realizing the upper density. The
limiting empirical measure assigns the closed interval at least δ: its endpoints have measure zero,
so its frequencies converge. Letting ε ↓ 0 and using continuity from above forces an atom of mass
at least δ at c, a contradiction.
If Dp (x) converged along a set satisfying (3), then (11) would force θp to converge there. The

Attribution, historical boundary, and reproducibility

The mechanism is a short application of established results. The speciﬁc assertion documented here
combines the exact point-period identity (6) with classical arithmetic inputs for this ﬁxed map and
the statistic in (2). A bounded search of the closest inspected ﬁnite-ﬁeld dynamics sources did
not authenticate an earlier statement of this exact application. This does not certify worldwide
absence, historical ﬁrstness, or present open-problem status.
A speciﬁc unresolved bibliographic lead must be retained. Roberts [7, p.220, reference 9] cites
N. Neumärker, J. A. G. Roberts, C.-M. Viallet and F. Vivaldi, The dynamics of reversible birational maps over ﬁnite ﬁelds, UNSW Preprint (2011). The body and its version relationships
were not recovered in the bounded audit. Citation contexts cannot determine whether that unseen
manuscript contains the present application. If an exact prior statement is later authenticated, this
note’s historical description requires reconciliation; none of the mathematical deductions depend
on assuming its absence.
The accompanying source and veriﬁcation archive contains the TeX source, a dependency-free
exact ﬁnite checker, source pointers, and byte-preserved original research ﬁles with manifests. The
new checker passes 147,619 exact ﬁnite checks over the 87 admissible primes through 1000, including
eight complete aﬃne orbit graphs with 6,088 points and six bad-reduction controls. It veriﬁes ﬁber
orders, divisor ﬁxed-point counts, the point-weighted CDF identity and the continuous moment,
and tests the endpoint and bad-reduction controls. A separate all-odd-prime checker veriﬁes the
regular-domain formulas and completion/deletion bounds through 1000, including assignments with
normalized periods exceeding one. Its exact assertion count and prime range are recorded separately;
it does not replace or inﬂate the original 147,619-check receipt. Each run receipt speciﬁes the actual
range and assertion count. Historical AI audit receipts also record 8,347,742 ﬁnite assertions and
230,780 analytic controls; they are preserved as earlier runs, not attributed to the new checker. The
original SymPy-dependent scripts are retained; this preparation does not claim a successful replay
of those scripts in an environment without SymPy. Finite computations check ﬁnite identities and
implementation consistency. The inﬁnite-prime conclusions follow from the proofs and the explicitly
cited classical inputs, not from numerical extrapolation.
