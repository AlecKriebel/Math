## Original question and strongest verified theorem

[AIM question 8, page 48](https://aimath.org/WWN/qptsurface2/qptsurface2.pdf), fixes an Enriques surface X over a number field, a K3 double cover Y→X, and a Brauer-orthogonal adelic m. It already discusses an adelic lift, then asks for assumptions permitting full or at least algebraic Brauer orthogonality upstairs. Its nonabelian-torsor remark and dated 2004 negative update form part of the historical statement. An algorithm and automatic universal lifting are not requirements stated there.

The PR proves that, once all local fibers of this same fixed cover split, the evaluations of every adelic lift are exactly

    {c + Σ_{v∈S} ε_v d_v : ε_v∈{0,1}}

in the dual of the finite group B=A/(Br₀(Y)+(A∩p*Br(X))), for A=Br(Y) or Br₁(Y). The existence of an A-orthogonal lift is equivalent to the occurrence of zero. This is a real finite reduction, including one common support set for all local choices. It is not a computational proof of an actual surface's Brauer group or a lifting-existence assertion.

Independent derivation in DERIVATION.md checked the subgroup and quotient; representative independence of global sums and local differences; the distinction from individual local evaluations; K3 finiteness; properness for infinitely many switches; Kummer spreading and Br(O_v)=0; the uniform finite S; both directions of binary attainability; arbitrary changes of base lifts; and the corestriction/complement identity Σd_v=−2c. No substantive mathematical defect was found. The intersection in the algebraic case is necessary; full and algebraic orthogonality remain distinct.

[Skorobogatov–Zarhin, theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0605351v4), page 2, supplies exactly the required finiteness over a number field. It needs no rational point or unproved Tate hypothesis. Their corollary 1.4 already draws a finite-evaluation consequence. The PR proof otherwise works for any proper smooth double cover with finite relevant Brauer quotient, rather than depending on a new Enriques-specific arithmetic mechanism.
