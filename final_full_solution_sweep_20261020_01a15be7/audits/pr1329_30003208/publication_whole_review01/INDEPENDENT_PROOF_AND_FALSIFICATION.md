# Independent proof reconstruction and attempted falsification

This is the fresh reviewer's reasoning, written after reading the actual original sources and the full prepared paper. It is not a transcription of an earlier acceptance. Symbols refer to the paper's concrete original GNS representation.

## Exact target and hypotheses

For every unital C*-algebra A, with no separability assumption, and every state psi on the countable universal unital free product invariant under finite permutations, put C_N=C*(pi_j(A):j>N), T=intersection C_N'', and let F be the particular DKW shift-limit expectation on Q=C*(pi(free product),T). D is the smallest unital C*-subalgebra of T closed under all F-valued words with inserted D elements and unrestricted, possibly repeated, copy indices. The target is D''=T in B(H), not merely in a changed representation. The stronger input/output norm-bounded WOT density is also checked. DKW Question5.3.4 concerning D subset pi(free product) is a different target.

The downloaded current DKW body actually gives the needed general-symmetric-state facts: the permutation implementation, isometric shift on Q, contractive F with WOT shift formula and range T, and original recursive definition. These do not require quantum symmetry or faithful state. Replacing j>=N by j>N does not change the decreasing intersection. The OWR2015 and2016 formulations use the same original representation and explicitly separate the two questions.

## Direct proof independently reconstructed

Let B_N be generated by the first N copies. If x is a future polynomial supported on S subset {N+1,...}, the partial map s->s+k has equal-size domain and image. Its support union is finite, and any matching of its unused domain and range completes a permutation of that union. Alternatively its directed chains can each be closed into cycles. Both work even when S intersects S+k. Every occurrence of a repeated index is transported together. This permutation fixes B_N pointwise. On any pair y xi,z xi with y,z in B_N, invariance of the vector state therefore makes the matrix coefficients of alpha^k(x) and x identical, for every positive k. The WOT shift limit for the fixed x makes the coefficients of F(x) and x identical. Norm approximation, contractivity of F, and density of B_N xi extend this to P_N F(x) P_N=P_N x P_N for every x in C_N. It is not a global equality F(x)=x.

The increasing H_N=closure(B_N xi) have dense union: a finite sum of copywords lies in some H_N, and all such sums are cyclically dense. This remains true when individual H_N and H are nonseparable; no countable dense subset of A is used. Thus P_N converges strongly to I on every individual vector.

Given nonzero t in T, r=||t||, epsilon and finitely many arbitrary vector pairs, choose N to make the paper's two projection-error terms, multiplied by2r, less than epsilon/2. As t belongs to C_N'', the closed norm-r ball of C_N gives x with ||x||<=r and the desired compressed coefficients within epsilon/2. This closed-ball assertion is valid for nonselfadjoint t: embed it as the upper-right corner of the selfadjoint matrix [[0,t],[t*,0]], use the selfadjoint Kaplansky theorem in M_2(C_N), and take upper-right corners. The corner norm is at most the block norm and strong convergence on (0,eta) gives strong convergence of the corner. There is no factor2 loss. The matrix-algebra bicommutant is M_2(C_N''), as seen by entrywise strong density. This is already the Halmos matrix device in Kaplansky's primary proof.

Set d=F(x). Then ||d||<=r and ||d-t||<=2r. The compressed coefficient of d-t equals that of x-t. For any eta,zeta, use exactly

    <(d-t)eta,zeta>-<(d-t)P_N eta,P_N zeta>
      = <(d-t)(eta-P_N eta),zeta>
        + <(d-t)P_N eta,zeta-P_N zeta>.

The displayed bound now gives less than epsilon. Directing neighborhoods yields the required bounded net; r=0 uses zero. C_N lies in pi(free product) because a C*-homomorphic image is norm closed. No norm-attaining lift through the original abstract quotient is assumed. No limit is passed through F. Finally every ordinary F(copyword) is in the original D by inserting units, so linearity, norm density and closedness give F(pi(free product)) subset D subset T. WOT density and the bicommutant theorem give T subset D'' subset T.

I find no unsupported central claim or missing hypothesis in this deduction. It addresses the full stated generality, not only a special case. The proof supplies the mathematical verification; finite checks below are diagnostics.

## Bilateral route and the dangerous quotient step

Pullback along a bijection Z->N defines a positive state on the bilateral universal algebra. Finite-word invariance shows both equality of the positive joint law and shift invariance; density extends this to the C*-algebra. A spatial unitary shift exists in its GNS representation without faithful state. In the bilateral future V_k, a finite-word test pair has a largest index n. Once a shift carries the whole future after n, all later-shift coefficients are equal by the same finite permutation construction. For any a in V_k, fixed-shift normality and norm-ball density extend this identity from future polynomials. The stabilization threshold depends on k and test indices, not on a. Uniform norm bounds extend from dense test vectors to every vector pair and yield E_k(a).

Tail elements are fixed by the shift by applying the coefficient identity in V_n to shifts0 and1, for each finite test family. Thus E_k is a unital CP projection onto the positive tail; a norm-one projection onto a C*-subalgebra is a conditional expectation. A bounded convergent net a_lambda in fixed V_k is handled by one fixed normal shift on each finite test pair, then by the uniform bound on all pairs. This proves normality on each fixed V_k, including V_0, using nets. It does not assert normality on the entire bilateral algebra. This is the actual Liu method, extended to blocks of elements of A and nets rather than merely renamed.

H_+=closure(C_0^Z Omega) reduces M_+=V_0; its projection commutes with C_0^Z and hence its bicommutant. Restriction is therefore a normal *-homomorphism rho. Its image is a von Neumann algebra, by the central-kernel decomposition of a normal homomorphism, and normal bounded density identifies the image with the original M. The same reasoning restricted to V_N gives rho(V_N)=C_N_original''. Surjectivity alone would not justify commuting rho with the tail intersection.

For t in the original tail, let r=||t|| and K_N={s in V_N:||s||<=r,rho(s)=t}. Each restricted normal surjection has a complementary central summand isomorphic isometrically to its image. Hence exact norm-r lifts exist in every V_N. K_N are nested ultraweakly closed subsets of the ultraweakly compact norm-r ball of M_+. The finite-intersection property supplies a tail lift. Thus rho(T_Z)=T_original, with the exact bound. Normality of E_0 gives bounded E_0(C_0^Z) density in T_Z. Only on C*-inputs is rho(E_0(x))=F(rho(x)) needed; spatial shift covariance and bounded normality of rho justify that equation. This proves the same conclusion.

## Explicit failed shortcuts and boundary cases

1. Nonnormal F is real, not a hypothetical caveat. In DKW/Liu's C^3 example a_j=e_vj+e_jv on vacuum plus countably many coordinates. The represented C*-algebra is compact-plus-scalars. Its tail is span{e_vv,I-e_vv}; F(lambda I+K)=lambda I+K_vv e_vv. Finite-rank projections q_m grow strongly to I, but F(q_m)=e_vv whereas F(I)=I. Therefore continuity through F cannot justify the theorem. The actual proof never does so. F(a_j^2)=e_vv and F(I)=I give its predicted density directly.
2. The same model, indexed bilaterally, explicitly disproves unrestricted normal descent. Its M_+ on the full bilateral Hilbert space is B(H_+) direct-sum C I_{j<=0}. The central q_- projecting on all j<=0 lies in ker rho. Its shifted projections alpha^l(q_-) project on indices j<=l and converge strongly to I-e_vv. Thus rho(E_0(q_-))=I_H+-e_vv is nonzero, although rho(q_-)=0. The support correctly avoids asserting descent of E_0 on all M_+.
3. The coefficient against xi alone misses e_11 in the unilateral model. Testing only moments at the cyclic vector cannot prove WOT equality. The actual argument tests arbitrary vector pairs through P_N.
4. Dense-vector convergence without a common norm bound is insufficient: n e_0n vanishes eventually on each finitely supported vector, yet eta_n=1/n belongs to l2 and gives coefficient1 against e_0. The2r bound is indispensable and supplied.
5. Nonfaithful pi causes no failure of permutation action: a preserved state preserves its GNS nullspace and all inner products; the spatial action conjugates represented copies. No inverse to pi is used.
6. T is not replaced by the finite-permutation fixed-point algebra; DKW's nonfaithful free-product example already distinguishes these.
