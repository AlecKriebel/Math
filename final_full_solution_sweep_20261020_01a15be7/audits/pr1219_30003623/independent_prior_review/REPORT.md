## 2. Independent central proof reconstruction and falsification

Write c=Cap(E), [A,B]=conv(E), L=B−A, μ=μ_E, λ=dx/L and use nonnegative relative entropy H(ρ|τ)=∫log(dρ/dτ)dρ, infinite without absolute continuity. The paper uses D=−H; every inequality below has been checked in that convention.

**Quantile entropy, including singular μ.** Finite logarithmic equilibrium energy excludes atoms. For a regular real compact set the equilibrium support is E. The continuous distribution F(x)=μ([A,x]) maps onto [0,1]. Each nontrivial fiber is a closed bounded gap of E. Its right-endpoint inverse q is increasing/Borel. Taking q0=A, qN=B and qk=q(k/N), the first cell is closed on the left and every remaining cell open on the left. These cells have mass 1/N and positive containing length ℓk=qk−qk−1. On dyadic frequency cells Jk, F*μ has mass 1/N and F*λ has mass ℓk/L, including any gap atom lying exactly at a cell endpoint. Thus H_N=(1/N)Σlog[L/(Nℓk)] is precisely the partition entropy.

A potential failure would be that collapsing gaps destroys a singular part. It does not. Remove G, the countable union of closed gap fibers; μ(G)=0. Off G, q∘F is identity. If μ≪λ, its density h can be chosen 0 on G, so h=(h∘q)∘F; pushforward integration identifies h∘q as the pushed density and preserves its log integral. If μ is not absolutely continuous, take a λ-null Borel Z⊂[A,B]\G of positive μ mass. Its singleton fibers imply F(Z)=q^{-1}(Z) is Borel and F^{-1}(F(Z))=Z, so the pushed measures also fail absolute continuity. Hence H(F*μ|F*λ)=H(μ|λ), including the infinite case. Collapsed gaps remain atoms of the reference measure and carry no equilibrium mass.

Dyadic partitions refine with half-open cells. Log-sum/Jensen gives H_{2^m}≤H. For any positive continuous f, step approximants converge uniformly, also after log. The standard entropy variational functional Φ(f)=∫f dβ−∫(1+logf)dα has infimum −H. On each finite cell its minimizer is αcell/βcell and value −αcell log(αcell/βcell). Consequently H_{2^m} increases to full H, without assuming μ has a density. All finite cell β masses are positive.

**Determinant, exact constants, and integrability.** For monic L2 norms t_n and N×N moment matrix G_N, independent Gram–Schmidt and Vandermonde expansion yield detG_N=Π_(n=0)^(N−1)t_n²=(1/N!)∫Δ²dμ^N. Restricting to one sample in each cell has N! disjoint labeled orders. Symmetry cancels 1/N! exactly; normalized cell probabilities νk=Nμ|Ck supply N^(−N). Let Iij=∫Ci∫Cj log|x−y|dμdμ. Jensen gives logdetG_N≥−NlogN+2N²Σi<jIij. Finite equilibrium energy controls the negative log part, while the compact diameter bounds the positive part; νk≤Nμ allows Jensen and Fubini. The nonatomic ordered cells also exclude diagonal mass.

Total energy is log c, so 2Σi<jIij=logc−ΣIkk. Because νk is a probability on an interval of length ℓk, its logarithmic energy is at most log(ℓk/4). This is an UPPER self-energy bound, and subtracting it gives the needed LOWER determinant bound. Normalizing t_n=c^nW2,n subtracts N(N−1) powers of c from N²; exactly N log c remains. Division by N gives

    (2/N) Σ_(n=1)^(N−1) log W2,n ≥ log(4c/L)+H_N.                 (A)

Missing N!, missing N^(−N), the opposite self-energy sign, or replacing the capacity exponent by N−1 changes this inequality. Those alternatives were challenged explicitly; no such defect occurs in the source or the authored reconstruction.

**Finiteness and positive length.** If all W2,n≤D, (A) and dyadic convergence imply H(μ|λ)≤2logD−log(4c/L)<∞. For finite limsup M, all sufficiently late terms≤M+ε and every fixed initial log contributes o(1) to the average; hence replace D by M after ε↓0. Positivity is independent of PW: factor any monic polynomial, use the equilibrium potential ∫log|z−x|dμ=logc+g_E(z)≥logc at each root and Jensen to get W2,n≥1. This excludes circular reliance on a new PW theorem.

Finite H implies μ≪λ. A probability supported on a Lebesgue-null E cannot have that property. The zero-length negative answer therefore follows DIRECTLY from the determinant/entropy argument, without separately auditing the cited PW-positive-measure Appendix A. Using only a bounded polynomial subsequence would fail to bound the full average of degrees 1..N−1; the dyadic subsequence is of partition sizes, not selected polynomial norms.

**All gap heights.** Enumerate bounded gaps (a_j,b_j) and define finite-gap outer sets E_m=[A,B]\∪_(j≤m)(a_j,b_j). Regularity excludes isolated E endpoints, so these are ordinary nondegenerate finite interval unions. Their equilibrium density is

    dμ_m/dx = Π_(j≤m)|x−z_j,m| / [π sqrt(|(x−A)(x−B)Π_(j≤m)(x−a_j)(x−b_j)|)],

where z_j,m is the unique Green maximum in gap j. For finite H, μ≪λ and hence μ≪μ_m because that density is positive a.e. on E_m. Every denominator endpoint is a regular point of E, so its integrated log is the finite number log c. Each numerator root lies strictly outside E, so its integrated log is log c+g_E(z_j,m), also finite. Therefore

    ∫log(dμ_m/dλ)dμ = log[L/(πc)] + Σ_(j≤m)g_E(z_j,m).

The coefficient of log c is m−(m+1)=−1, with no infinity-minus-infinity subtraction. H(μ|μ_m)≥0 then gives H(μ|λ)≥log[L/(πc)]+Σ_(j≤m)g_E(z_j,m).

For each fixed gap, standard decreasing-compact capacity/equilibrium convergence gives g_E_m↑g_E locally uniformly there. Regularity forces g_E=0 at both endpoints. Its positive maximum is unique since g_E''(x)=−∫(x−y)^(−2)dμ(y)<0. As g_E_m≤g_E and values at the limiting maximizer converge, z_j,m stay in a compact interior subinterval and tend to z_j. Retain FIRST only j≤r, then let m→∞, then r→∞. Every discarded Green height is nonnegative. This proves

    H(μ|λ) ≥ log[L/(πc)] + PW(E).                               (B)

The finite-gap case takes all gaps directly, including zero gaps for an interval. There is no unsupported interchange of an entire varying infinite sum. Combining (A),(B) yields PW(E)≤2logM−log(4/π)<∞. Since μ is a probability, each monic L2 norm is at most its supremum norm, so bounded Chebyshev factors supply the premise. In the real setting Green critical points occur precisely once in each bounded gap: strict concavity gives the real zeros; the Cauchy-transform derivative has nonzero imaginary part off the real axis. This identifies the sum used here with the PW condition.
