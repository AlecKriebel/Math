## Adversarial proof reconstruction

**Small targets and all positive targets.** Choose a prime q not dividing ab with a/(bq)<=1/400. This keeps the reduced denominator squarefree. The small-target theorem holds for every sufficiently large integer y, so take q successive scales with y_(i+1)^8>y_i^9. Every denominator has a unique larger prime u in that scale's large interval. Across scales, an earlier u may later become a small-side factor, but the larger factor of each product remains unique. Equality of products would force equality of their larger factors, contradicting disjoint intervals. The q sums therefore give a/b with global distinctness. This explicitly covers integer targets and arbitrarily small positive targets.

**Tuning and denominator divisibility.** For fixed reduced tau=a/b<=1/400, use B=primes dividing b, W=primes in (y^2,2y^2], V={2} union B union W, U=primes in (y^8,Y]. V is a set, so the prime 2 is not accidentally counted twice. Let H=sum_(v in V)1/v and H0=3/2+sum_(p in B)1/p. For large y, |W|>=y^2/(8 log y), product V<=2b*16^(y^2), and 1/2<=H<=H0. H0 is independent of y.

The full large interval has reciprocal prime mass at least 1/50, hence mu=(H/2)sum_U1/u reaches at least 1/200>=2tau. Individual increments are less than H/(2y^8)<tau/2.
