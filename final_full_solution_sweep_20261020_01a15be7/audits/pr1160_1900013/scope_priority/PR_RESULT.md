# Partial result for the fixed cubic field classification

For the historical eight-sail Klein model, every totally real cubic field has countably infinitely many integer-linearly inequivalent continued fractions.

The proof extracts the three eigen-hyperplanes from the rays missing the sail union, then extracts a multiplier order from their rational stabilizer algebra and the integer lattice. Its trace discriminant is a geometric invariant. The lattices

$$
\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K,\qquad f=1,2,\ldots,
$$

give pairwise inequivalent continued fractions with invariant

$$
f^4\operatorname{disc}(K).
$$

Every lattice is realized by an irreducible operator in $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)$ with three positive eigenvalues. There are finitely many classes at each fixed multiplier order, and finitely many below each invariant-discriminant bound. A conductor sandwich reduces each order stratum to finitely many subgroups of finite abelian groups, followed by unit and field-automorphism identifications.

The exact arithmetic equivalence relation is $J=a\tau(I)$, where $a\in K^\times$ and $\tau$ is a field automorphism. This includes all orders and all full lattices, not just invertible ideals of a selected order.

An explicit pair of inequivalent examples has the same characteristic polynomial $t^3-57t^2+570t-1$, but recovered-order discriminants $81$ and $6561$. Independent exact implementations verify this pair and the general displayed matrix identities. These checks support the calculations; the geometric recovery and general finiteness/infinitude assertions are proved in the accompanying proof.

This is a partial result for the historical formulation in Karpenkov's [2004 Problem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411054), also appearing as [2017 Problem 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01450). It is not a classification of the possible face data or decorated torus decompositions, not a claim about all generalized sail models, and not a literature-priority claim.
