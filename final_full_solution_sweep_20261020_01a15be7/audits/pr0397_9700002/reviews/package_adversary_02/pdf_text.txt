                                                   Abstract
         We construct a bounded-degree physical tree model in which the normalized marginal admitted de-
     mand drops from one to zero while the spare-capacity graph changes from the entire tree to isolated
     vertices. Capacities arise from the unique cheapest ﬁxed-topology enlargement supporting doubled ini-
     tial traﬃc. Each of the k2 cross-root streams has an independent, ﬁxed-law exponential growth rate.
     An√all-cuts estimate conﬁnes both changes, with probability tending to one, to an interval of width
     16 log k/k around mean doubled demand. Both outer-phase conclusions hold for every optimizer. This
     is a specialized existence response to Aldous’s conceptual congestion toy-model request. The width is an
     upper bound; no critical proﬁle or universal traﬃc law is asserted.


1    The request and the model
Aldous’s problem page [1] asks for a tractable model connecting a rapid decline in the marginal amount
of extra traﬃc admitted with fragmentation of the graph of edges having spare capacity. One suggested
design mechanism minimizes the cost of increasing capacities to support doubled current demand. The
accompanying index classiﬁes the request as conceptual, rather than as one uniquely formulated conjecture.
We implement that mechanism in an explicit family. Its topology, traﬃc pattern and growth process are
part of the model.

2.4    Limits
We have E(Sk /k 2 ) = 1 and Var(Sk /k 2 ) = k −2 . The ﬁxed-t conclusions away from two follow from (i)–(ii),
ϵk → 0, and δk → 0. At two, on the same good event,

                                k 2 + (1 − δk )Sk = Fk (2 − δk ) ≤ Fk (2) ≤ 2k 2 .

This gives the remaining throughput limit without exchanging limits and derivatives. The failure bounds
are summable along binary k; hence the outer-phase events also hold eventually almost surely under any
common coupling, by the ﬁrst Borel–Cantelli lemma.


3     Context, scope and reproducibility
Capacitated random transportation and ﬂow-cut analysis are classical. Hassin–Zemel [3] and Karp–Motwani–
Nisan [4, 5] are essential antecedents, alongside max-ﬂow/min-cut and elementary concentration. The highly
optimized tolerance paradigm is prior work [6], as are congestion-induced fragmentation and shortest-path
percolation [7]. The latter sequentially consumes edges for individual requests, rather than optimizing
simultaneous admitted demand under ﬁxed capacities. The contribution here is the explicit cheapest-design
tree construction and its simultaneous normalized-marginal and every-optimizer spare-connectivity theorem,
not a new general transport-feasibility principle.
    The suﬃcient exponent separation in [3, Theorem 1] does not cover the present balanced near-capacity
regime. The directed theorem in the actual 1988 report [5, Section 4] ﬁxes bounded integer capacities and
a positive constant mean surplus; Section 5 separately proposes a vanishing-surplus extension. That report
has not been equated with an inspected 1993 ﬁnal edition: the ﬁnal journal body [4] was unavailable. These
comparisons do not exclude every possible derivation from prior work.
    The physical graph is sparse but traﬃc is dense, capacities increase with size, and internal constraints
reduce to terminal constraints. Only the ﬁxed-topology design alternative is implemented. No adaptive
capacity growth, generic cyclic-network theorem, linear-variance process, critical proﬁle or universal critical
law is supplied. Throughput saturates; the marginal drops. The width is a suﬃcient bound without a
matching lower bound. Aldous’s 2005 discussion [8, slide 5] already contrasts easy but unrealistic tree
analysis with preferred mean-ﬁeld models. Our tree has no rerouting and does not remove that explanatory
limitation. The suitability of one example as the uniquely right conceptual model is not a mathematical
conclusion.
    A dated bounded audit inspected 16 primary source bodies and recorded 52 literature searches, locating
no earlier exact construction and theorem in that corpus. The ﬁnal-edition gap above, unpublished work
and incomplete indexing remain limitations; no ﬁrst-discovery or global novelty certiﬁcate is asserted. The
source and theorem comparisons are in the supplement.
    The proof is analytic; ﬁnite controls do not establish rare-event asymptotics. The original 24-ﬁle two-turn
packet is preserved unchanged. The supplement’s new replay derivative veriﬁes those frozen ﬁles and requires
exact active second-turn output bytes. It admits bounded absolute drift only at 40 speciﬁed older ﬂoating
diagnostics, together with explicit runtime and public-input projection rules; all other structure and controls
remain exact. Python 3.9.6, NumPy 2.0.2 and SciPy 1.13.1 were tested. The strict historical wrapper fails
at roundoﬀ in that environment and is not advertised as portable. Exact policies, guard tests and limitations
are provided; other platform/library combinations are not guaranteed.
