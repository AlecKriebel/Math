# Original request and independent priority challenge

The actual [congestion page](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/congestion.html) defines maximum admitted simultaneous demand, then asks for a tractable toy model coupling an abrupt marginal-admission decline with fragmentation of spare edges. It suggests minimum-cost enlargement for doubled demand and an adaptive alternative. Linear mean/variance growth is illustrative. Its stated search for the right model remains a modeling judgment. The [index](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/index.html) places this question at0.7 on the conceptual spectrum and cautions that updates are incomplete. Neither the dated2018 status sentence nor continued listing certifies current openness.

Success criteria used here: supply a precisely defined family, a genuine admitted-demand derivative, actual optimized physical edge loads, a shrinking interval containing both outer-phase changes, and a proof under explicitly stated assumptions. The bound paper meets these existence criteria. This does not prove explanatory suitability for cyclic rerouting networks, linear-variance traffic, generic demands, SOC growth or a canonical resolution of the broader modeling program.

I read the candidate paper before the primary retrieval during the byte-binding step; I did not preserve source-only blindness to its formulation. I then read the original page and index independently and used their actual definitions as the acceptance baseline. I reconstructed the mathematics before reading the earlier substantive review reports. Independence here is a fresh adversarial assessment, not a claim of an uninformed human peer review.
