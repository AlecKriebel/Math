                                                Abstract
          We prove positive recurrence at every ﬁxed positive arrival rate for the four-population
      deterministic-ﬁrst-chunk chain described by Norros, Reittu and Eirola. This answers the stabil-
      ity conjecture in Norros’s 2010 Oberwolfach contribution and the retained 2011 author proof,
      for the exact six-rate model with a permanent seed and invisible empty waiting rooms. A
      single coercive Foster function combines remaining workload, rounded cohort imbalance, and
      two bounded ﬁnite birth–death Poisson correctors. Monotone ratio cutoﬀs make every ﬁrst-
      download interface error nonpositive. The other interface errors are controlled pointwise. The
      proof requires no stationary averaging, moment assumption, or uniformity in the arrival rate.
      Exact ﬁnite controls supplement the analytic proof. The contribution is the all-load extension
      of the stated historical conjecture; classical methods and source-version limits are explicit.


1    The model and the question
An arriving peer chooses, independently and uniformly, which of two chunks to download ﬁrst.
Let a, b count empty peers assigned to the two choices, and let x, y count peers possessing just
the respective chunks. A completed peer departs immediately. There is one permanent seed.
The empty waiting rooms are invisible to the sampling denominator. Precisely, for λ > 0 and
D = x + y + 1, the chain on N40 has transitions

                                 increment in (a, b, x, y)     rate
                                        (1, 0, 0, 0)           λ/2
                                        (0, 1, 0, 0)           λ/2
                                       (−1, 0, 1, 0)       a(x + 1)/D                                   (1)
                                       (0, −1, 0, 1)       b(y + 1)/D
                                       (0, 0, −1, 0)       x(y + 1)/D
                                       (0, 0, 0, −1)       y(x + 1)/D.

A decrement at a zero coordinate has rate zero. These are the rates of Figure 2 in [2] and the
checked 2011 author proof of [3]; the seed produces the +1 terms.
    Norros [1, pp. 2782–2783] conjectured stochastic stability despite instability of the corresponding
large-system diﬀerential equations. The retained January 5, 2011 author proof states the stability
conjecture in Section 3.2 as Conjecture 3.6. The earlier 2009 preprint [2, Conjecture 3.4] conjec-
tured high-load instability instead. These editions must be distinguished. Stability here means
irreducibility and positive recurrence in continuous time, as in those sources.

5    The continuous-time recurrence conclusion
We give the recurrence step to make its clock and integrability requirements explicit. Arrivals form
a rate-λ Poisson process. Every internal event decreases W by one and every arrival increases it by
two. Thus the total number of jumps up to time t is at most W (0) + 3Pλ (t), proving nonexplosion.
Every state has a positive-rate ﬁnite path to zero, because the seed makes all required downloads
and departures possible. Conversely, arrivals followed by prescribed ﬁrst downloads build any ﬁnite
state, proving irreducibility.
    Since |Z| ≤ W and hm (z) ≤ |z| + m/2, we have

                                W ≤ V ≤ (1 + c)W + cm/2 + 2G.                                  (17)

Every jump of V is bounded in absolute value by a ﬁnite constant depending on λ. Localize
Dynkin’s identity at the ﬁrst hit τF of F , time t, and the ﬁrst exit from a ﬁnite population set.
Equation (16) and V ≥ 0 bound the expected stopped time by V (s). Nonexplosion and Fatou
remove the population cutoﬀ; monotone convergence as t → ∞ yields

                                     Es τF ≤ V (s),     s∈
                                                         / F.                                  (18)

No invariant law or stationary moment was used.
    For completeness, select from every state of ﬁnite F a ﬁnite positive-rate path to zero. These
ﬁnitely many paths have a common time horizon T and a positive lower success probability. Starting
in F , the bound W (T ) ≤ W (0)+2Pλ (T ) and (17) give a uniform ﬁnite bound on EV (ST ). Equation
(18) therefore bounds uniformly the expected duration of a trial of length T followed by return to
F . The strong Markov property and the uniform positive success probability give ﬁnite expected
hitting time of zero by repeated trials. The mean holding time at zero is 1/λ; each of its two exit
neighbors has ﬁnite expected hitting time back to zero. The resulting regeneration cycle has ﬁnite
mean. Its normalized occupation measure is stationary, and irreducibility gives uniqueness. This
proves Theorem 1.


6    Credit, veriﬁcation, and disclosure
The model, its conjecture, the distinction between stochastic and large-system stability, and the
standard Foster principle are prior work [1, 2, 3, 4]. The ﬁnite geometric birth–death Poisson
equation and Lipschitz rounding estimate are standard tools. The certiﬁcate above combines them
with cutoﬀs whose ﬁrst-download interface terms have a useful sign.
    Oguz, Anantharam and Norros [5] prove stability of their Common Chunk protocol using sam-
ples of three from the entire population and a rule protecting departure of rare chunks. That
protocol is diﬀerent from (1). For example, with just the seed and one peer possessing chunk
one, its departure probability per unit contact time is 7/8, whereas (1) has departure rate 1/2.
