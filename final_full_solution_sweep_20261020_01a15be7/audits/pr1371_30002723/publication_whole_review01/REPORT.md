## Attempts to invalidate the compression proof

**Finite grid versus real identity.** This is the central analytic hazard. On a coarse additive two-face, intersect with every relative refined cell, including artificial open edges and singletons. Interpolation therefore gives zero h-slack on the whole relative refined cell. Their union is the entire real coarse relative interior. Only after obtaining this real identity may bounded interval additivity be invoked. A periodic finite PL h is bounded even with point spikes. Small closed rectangles inside the open face meet Lemma1.7's actual hypotheses. Finite overlapping chains inside the convex face patch slopes; every projected interior point is attained by an interior point. Thus common slopes and J=P=0 on projected interiors follow. The proof does not infer a real Cauchy equation from isolated finite samples.

**Actual/limiting support and point values.** I reconstructed the endpoint-variable geometry separately. In a translated actual edge expression, the crossing jump and point-deviation equations give positive identifications for both J and P. On a reflected actual edge, crossing the second argument reverses its traversal: jumps match positively, whereas spikes oppose. A slope cell maps i to Nt-i-1; a point maps k to Nt-k. At a reflection fixed point the positive jump relation is harmless and the negative spike relation correctly forces that spike to zero. A geometric reflection alone is not a negative character on slopes or jumps.

A limiting zero boundary edge is stronger than an actual additive edge. For a vertical approach from the right, three inward trace relations imply Y-=Y+ and Z-=Z+; the two inward rays with y fixed or x+y fixed then imply equality with the actual Y0,Z0 values.
