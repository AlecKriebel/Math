# Independent audit: holonomy example and periodic-graph lemma

Checked 2026-10-21. This is authored mathematical analysis, not a claim that the historical open question has been resolved or that the results are new. No repository or queue edits are part of this audit.

## Verdict

The positive-quadrant diagonal-holonomy example is correct under the explicit convention that an affine vertex is a noninflectional sextactic point. Its log-coordinate complex structure need not be preserved by its flat connection. The following alternate example removes that compatibility caveat. Both examples remain incomplete and non-equiaffine. Neither contradicts the classical six-vertex theorem for simple closed convex plane curves.

## Stronger compatible example

Let U=C minus {0}, let q=exp(1+i), and let G be the infinite cyclic group generated by z -> qz. Let M=U/G. For every nonzero integer n, |q^n|=exp(n) is not 1, so the action is free. If K is a compact subset of U and r<=|z|<=R on K, q^n K can intersect K only when exp(n) lies in [r/R,R/r]; thus the action is properly discontinuous. The compact annulus 1<=|z|<=e meets every orbit, so the quotient is compact. Equivalently, the exponential universal covering identifies M with C/(Z(1+i)+Z(2pi i)), a complex torus.

The standard real affine connection on U is torsion-free and flat and is preserved by every multiplication z -> q^n z. It descends to a projectively flat connection on M. This connection preserves the standard complex structure: in complex affine charts its transitions are holomorphic linear maps. Thus the example is also valid if complex-structure compatibility means that the connection preserves J or is a holomorphic affine connection.

Consider c(t)=exp((1+i)t), and let gamma be its projection to M. Since c(t+1)=q c(t), gamma defines a smooth map R/Z -> M. If c(t)=q^n c(s), comparison of absolute values gives t-s=n, so gamma is injective. Its derivative is nowhere zero. A smooth injective immersion of a compact circle into a Hausdorff surface is an embedding. In the logarithmic universal covering, gamma is the primitive lattice direction 1+i, so it is essential, not contractible.

Write c(t)=(x(t),y(t))=(e^t cos t,e^t sin t). Direct calculation gives det(c',c'')=2e^(2t)>0. Consequently there are no inflections, and the isolated-inflection hypothesis is satisfied vacuously.

For an entirely real contact calculation, set m=(1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2). The 6 by 6 matrix with entries d^j m_k/dt^j at t=0, for j=0,...,5, has determinant 2560. At general t it has determinant 2560e^(8t), hence is invertible. One way to verify this is to complexify its function span to the exponential basis with distinct exponents 0, 1+i, 1-i, 2, 2+2i, 2-2i; the derivative matrix is a Vandermonde matrix times nonzero exponential factors. It follows that no nonzero quadratic polynomial can vanish to order at least six anywhere on c. The first five derivative rows have rank five, so there is a unique projective conic with contact at least five, and its contact is exactly five.

Nonsingularity can also be checked explicitly. At t=0 the osculating conic is

13x^2 - 2xy + 7y^2 - 8x - 16y - 5 = 0.

Its homogeneous symmetric matrix has determinant -1458, so it is a nonsingular conic. Its restriction to c has derivatives of orders 0 through 4 equal to zero, and fifth derivative equal to 160. Thus its contact multiplicity is exactly five. The continuous similarity z -> exp((1+i)t0)z sends c(t) to c(t+t0); it transports this nonsingular osculating conic to the unique one at any other parameter. Therefore gamma has zero noninflectional sextactic points.

Contact multiplicity with a conic and noninflectionality are preserved by projective coordinate changes. Hence this conclusion is well-defined on M, regardless of which descended affine chart is used. We explicitly adopt the sextactic/contact definition; the historical problem statement does not define its affine vertices, so its intended convention remains a separate source question.

## Important limitations

The flat connection is geodesically incomplete: the lifted affine geodesic z(s)=1-s reaches the omitted origin at s=1. An extension on M would lift through that finite time to U by covering-space path lifting, contradicting the missing endpoint. Compactness of M does not imply affine completeness.

It is not equiaffine in the global parallel-volume sense. Every parallel two-form on connected U is a constant multiple of dx wedge dy, while multiplication by q scales that form by e^2. Thus no nonzero parallel area form descends. Linear and projective holonomy are nontrivial.

The developed curve c is not closed in the plane or the projective plane: it closes only modulo holonomy. It is therefore outside the hypotheses of classical closed-plane or closed-projective-plane vertex theorems. Local strict convexity does not supply a globally closed convex planar oval.

The example proves that zero is the sharp universal nonnegative lower bound for the vertex count in the broad class just specified, even with an embedded curve and complex-compatible connection. It does not settle a general relation between V and I: one example with (V,I)=(0,0) does not even refute a proposed relation depending solely on I unless that relation excludes this pair. It also does not settle variants adding completeness, equiaffinity, contractibility, global convexity, or a different vertex convention.

## Audit of the original quadrant example

For generators A(x,y)=(ex,e^3y), B(x,y)=(x,ey), logarithmic coordinates convert the action into the lattice generated by (1,3),(0,1). The quotient is a compact affine torus. The curve (e^t,e^(3t)) projects to an embedded primitive closed curve and has det(c',c'')=6e^(4t). Restricting conics to y=x^3 gives the exponent set {0,1,2,3,4,6}; its derivative-order 0,...,5 Wronskian is nonzero for x>0. At x=1, the conic y^2-15xy+40y-45x^2+24x-5 restricts to (x-1)^5(x+5), giving exactly order-five contact. Its conic matrix determinant is 59049/4, so it is nonsingular. The example passes, with its independent complex structure and non-equiaffine/incomplete limitations stated explicitly.

## Audit of periodic-graph Proposition B

The independent check of RESULTS.md Approach 4 passes. On a zero-free inter-inflection graph interval, h=|f''|^(-2/3) tends to positive infinity at both endpoints and k=-h''/2. If h'' had an upper bound near either endpoint, Taylor's integral remainder, with its nonnegative integration kernel also when integrating backwards, would bound h above there, a contradiction. Thus there are points on both sides of any interior x0 at which h'' exceeds h''(x0). Its minimum between such points is attained in the interior, where h'''=0 and hence k'=0. No finite-order-zero assumption is used.

Isolated zeros of the continuous periodic f'' form a finite closed discrete subset of the parameter circle. Since its integral over a period is zero, nonzero f'' must take both signs and has at least two zeros. Applying the lemma to disjoint consecutive intervals yields V>=I. The claimed sine family has the stated numerator -epsilon^3 n^9 cos(nt)(36 sin^2(nt)+40 cos^2(nt)), so V=I=2n and the bound is sharp for each positive even I. No stronger assertion about all closed curves or arbitrary projective surfaces follows. No novelty claim is supported without inspecting earlier full-text work.

## Subsequent audit: same-surface finite-count Proposition C

The support-function construction in RESULTS.md also passes. With N=(cos theta,sin theta), T=(-sin theta,cos theta), p=1+epsilon cos(n theta), and rho=p+p'', differentiation gives r'=rho T and det(r',r'')=rho^2. Positivity of rho follows from |epsilon|<1/(n^2-1). The supporting projection in direction N(theta0) has derivative rho sin(theta0-theta), with a unique maximum at theta0; thus r is embedded and strictly convex.

Direct differentiation using ds=rho^(2/3)dtheta yields r_sss=-k r_s and exactly the curvature formula printed in Proposition C. Writing a=1-n^2, its first-order expansion is k=1+epsilon a(n^2-4)cos(n theta)/3+O(epsilon^2), uniformly with all needed derivatives. Hence k_theta/epsilon converges in C1 to n(n^2-1)(n^2-4)sin(n theta)/3. For n>=3 all its 2n zeros are simple. Small disjoint neighborhoods of those zeros have a fixed derivative sign and opposite endpoint signs, while the complementary compact set is bounded away from zero; C1 convergence gives exactly one zero in each neighborhood and none outside. Thus V=2n and I=0 for sufficiently small nonzero epsilon. Affine similarities preserve these counts and allow placement in a single fixed affine chart of one torus.

Taking n=3 and n=4 therefore gives different finite vertex counts at the same inflection count, in the same ambient surface and the same contractible homotopy class. This strengthens the isolated zero-count example by actually disproving determination of unweighted V or V-I by those data alone. It does not disprove inequalities, weighted or signed formulas, or relations with additional geometric data. This is a scoped obstruction, not a resolution of the entire historical question.
