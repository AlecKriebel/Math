# Source gate: affine vertices and inflection points

Checked 2026-10-21. Problem ID 6000013; AMR-059-0013. No new mathematical solution is claimed.

## Original question and hypotheses

Furuhata, Matsuzoe, Urakawa, *Open Problems in Affine Differential Geometry and Related Topics*, Interdisciplinary Information Sciences 4(2) (1998), 125–127, item 4(b), printed page 126 / PDF page 2. Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/iis/4/2/4_2_125/_pdf/-char/en

The question asks for an estimate of affine-vertex counts in the setting inherited from 4(a): a closed curve on a Riemann surface carrying a projectively flat connection. It additionally asks how the affine-vertex count relates to the inflection count when the inflections are isolated. The list does not specify a differentiability class, simplicity, convexity, genus, homotopy class, completeness, nondegeneracy, or a planar/global-chart assumption. It gives no explicit definitions of affine vertex or inflection. Smooth regular curves are a natural working convention, not an explicit recovered hypothesis. Isolated is not synonymous with simple, finite-order, or nondegenerate. The curve is closed, not an arc with endpoints. The 1998 list records questions from a December 1996 workshop.

Its reference [16] points to Sasaki's flat-torus paper. The printed bibliography incorrectly gives 1985 and adds an extra “affine” in the title. Publisher metadata verifies 1995 instead.

## Reference [16]: verified scope, access limit

Takeshi Sasaki, *Inflection points and affine vertices of closed curves on 2-dimensional affine flat tori*, Results in Mathematics 27 (1995), 129–140, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03322277 . Publisher issue date is March 1995; the 2013 webpage publication date is not the original issue year.

The publisher abstract concerns affine flat tori, including non-nullhomotopic closed curves and strictly convex closed curves. It reports differing inflection-count behavior according to whether the affine torus is Euclidean, and an example with six affine vertices. This is earlier special-case work, not evidence that the later general question was solved. Full text is subscription restricted and was not inspected. The abstract's unqualified inflection-count wording should not be converted into an exact theorem without the paper's hypotheses, especially for geodesic or degenerate curves. No purchase or authentication was attempted.

## Checked later special case and definitions

Thorbergsson–Umehara, *Sextactic points on a simple closed curve*, Nagoya Mathematical Journal 167 (2002), 55–94, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0027763000025435 ; author preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0008137 .

Their curves are smooth regular simple closed curves in the real projective plane. At a noninflection, a sextactic point has osculating-conic intersection multiplicity at least six, equivalently a critical point of affine curvature in an affine chart. An inflection has tangent-line intersection multiplicity at least three, equivalently vanishing homogeneous determinant det(X,X',X''). An inflection need not cross its tangent. Their introduction proves optimal lower bounds: non-nullhomotopic curves have at least three sextactics; nullhomotopic strictly convex curves have at least six; nonconvex ones at least three; convex ones at least two. Nullhomotopic curves have at least four inflections and sextactics combined. They discuss a C4 extension requiring an adjusted definition. These are projective-plane, simplicity-dependent results, not a theorem for arbitrary projectively flat surfaces. The contact definition is the appropriate projectively invariant interpretation to test, but the problem list itself does not explicitly endorse it.

## Confusions rejected

No inspected source supports a universal V−I topological identity. Do not replace the verified restricted V+I inequality with a difference identity. Do not infer a sharp universal bound of four or six from classical theorems with extra hypotheses. Do not identify an affine vertex with vanishing affine normal; affine-curvature stationarity is a different condition. Do not silently strengthen isolated inflections to nondegenerate inflections.

Ghomi's *Vertices of closed curves in Riemannian surfaces*, Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici 88 (2013), 427–448, DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/CMH/290 , explicitly studies critical points of geodesic curvature. Its surface classification does not establish the affine-vertex question. Checked abstract/introduction: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43318 .

Kobayashi–Sasaki's *General-Affine Invariants of Plane Curves and Space Curves*, Czechoslovak Mathematical Journal 70 (2020), 67–104, DOI https://doi.org/10.21136/CMJ.2019.0165-18 , studies general-affine invariants and variational problems. Its introductory scope is not a general closed-surface vertex-counting resolution. Checked university manuscript: https://hdl.handle.net/2115/80516 .

## Status decision

SOURCE RECOVERED; SPECIAL-CASE PROGRESS VERIFIED; GENERAL RESOLUTION NOT VERIFIED.

Searches of the exact title, affine vertices with projectively flat/Riemann surface qualifiers, Sasaki's cited title, and sextactic-point literature produced no inspected primary theorem resolving the arbitrary-surface question as of the check date. This is a bounded negative search result, not proof that the problem remains open. Retaining a partial-progress classification is defensible only with the scope restrictions above and deletion of unsupported index-formula claims. A solved/accepted gate is not justified. A sharper original interpretation still requires inspection of Sasaki 1995 and potentially its referenced projective-curve conventions.
