## Scope and article classification

- bioRxiv accepts unpublished preprints across the life sciences. Mathematics
  and physical-science articles are in scope only when they have direct
  relevance to the life sciences. This paper's application is the
  identifiability of evolutionary histories from nucleotide-pattern
  distributions, so the final title, abstract, and introduction should make
  that biological relevance immediately visible.
- The public subject areas include **Evolutionary Biology**, **Bioinformatics**,
  and **Genetics**. Evolutionary Biology is the best initial fit for this paper;
  the author makes the final selection in the portal.
- bioRxiv labels articles as New Results, Confirmatory Results, or Contradictory
  Results. Although this manuscript corrects a claimed theorem, it contributes
  new exact constructions and an answer to an open problem rather than largely
  repeating an experimental approach. **New Results** is therefore the best
  initial fit, subject to author confirmation.
- Screening checks whether the submission is a biological research manuscript
  and an appropriate article type. Narrative reviews, commentary, opinion, and
  stand-alone protocols are not accepted; methods or tools must be presented in
  the context of a research article with results/data. Exact constructions,
  certificates, and reproducible computational outputs should be presented as
  research results, not as a software announcement.

## Manuscript and files

- bioRxiv posts the author-created PDF and does not require a particular article
  style. The canonical PDF should therefore be complete and reader-ready, with
  figures and essential computer-assisted-proof information incorporated or
  permanently linked.
- The official public guidance consulted here does not state a universal page
  limit, template, or bibliography style. It also does not expose all current
  upload-size or extension limits. Confirm those limits in the authenticated
  portal rather than relying on an old third-party checklist.
- The upload process collects title, abstract, author names, affiliations, and a
  subject category. Enter special characters deliberately and ensure the portal
  rendering matches the PDF. The system also provides dedicated funder and
  grant-number fields.
- Data/code can be linked from the submission. A versioned GitHub URL and a
  permanent reproducibility-package DOI are preferable to an unversioned project
  homepage. Do not upload the historical parent ZIP.

## Distribution license: author action required

- Authors retain copyright and select a distribution/reuse option: CC BY,
  CC BY-NC, CC BY-ND, CC BY-NC-ND, CC0, or no reuse.
- This repository does **not** choose that option. Before approval, the author
  must check applicable funder and intended-journal policies and record the
  selected option in `FINAL_CHECKLIST.md`.
- bioRxiv's current license-update mechanism can change a recent posted preprint
  from a more restrictive option to a less restrictive one, but not in the
  opposite direction. This makes the initial choice consequential.
- The MIT license in `../../LICENSE-CODE` covers verifier/build code only; it is
  not the manuscript's bioRxiv license.

## Approval, permanence, and timing

- An article may be posted before or concurrently with journal submission, but
  not after journal acceptance.
- Once posted, a preprint is citable and ordinarily cannot be removed. Revised
  versions may be posted before formal journal acceptance and retain the same
  DOI.
- A DOI is generated when the author finally approves the submission. The DOI
  must remain a placeholder in repository metadata until that occurs.
- Screening typically takes 24--48 hours, but may take longer. Authors cannot
  request an embargo or an exact posting date/time.
- Screening includes completeness, scope, scientific-content, potential-harm,
  plagiarism, and prior-online-appearance checks. Because this manuscript and
  earlier PDF versions are already in a public GitHub history, the versioned
  repository should be disclosed accurately rather than treated as unpublished
  private material.
