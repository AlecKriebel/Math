**Lemma.** If a finite two-dimensional simplicial complex is generated by triangular facets that intersect pairwise in at most one vertex, its first barycentric subdivision embeds linearly in R^3. Isolated vertices may also be included.

### Positions and combinatorics

Take the bipartite incidence graph with one node for every original vertex v and one node f for every triangular facet. Place all these graph nodes at distinct points of the three-dimensional moment curve `(t,t^2,t^3)`. Any four are affinely independent by the Vandermonde determinant. Thus disjoint graph edges cannot intersect: such an intersection would give an affine dependence on at most four nodes. Edges sharing a node meet only there, by independence of the three nodes. Hence this is an embedded straight incidence graph, including any cycles.

Map every original vertex of `sd K` to its graph position v, and every triangular-face barycenter to its graph node f. For a facet `{v1,v2,v3}`, map the barycenter of edge `{vi,vj}` to

`w_ij = f + epsilon*((vi-f)+(vj-f))`, with `0<epsilon<1/2`.

An original edge belongs to exactly one triangular facet, because the facet family is linear. Therefore these edge-barycenter positions are private to that facet and define one global map without ambiguity. There are exactly six maximal simplices over that facet, with images `conv(f,vi,w_ij)` for the six ordered pairs `i != j`.

These are freely assigned images of the vertices of the **abstract** barycentric subdivision. They are not required to be arithmetic barycenters of their image vertices in Euclidean space. The resulting map is affine on each subdivision simplex, as the PL claim requires.

### Exact intersections within one facet

The three vectors `ai=vi-f` form a basis of R^3. In these coordinates, f is 0, vi is ei, and w_ij is `epsilon*(ei+ej)`.

The two triangles in coordinate plane (i,j) lie on opposite sides of the ray through `ei+ej`; each is nondegenerate and they meet exactly in `[0,epsilon*(ei+ej)]`. Distinct coordinate planes (i,j) and (i,k) meet on axis i. A triangle containing ei meets that axis in `[0,ei]`; a triangle that does not contain ei meets that axis only at 0. These are exactly the intersections of the corresponding barycentric simplices. No other intersections are possible. The six triangles form the cone from 0 over the polygonal hexagon

`e1, epsilon*(e1+e2), e2, epsilon*(e2+e3), e3, epsilon*(e3+e1)`.

This proves both simplex nondegeneracy and all face compatibility within the original facet, including mixed-dimensional faces by restriction.

### Finite separation between different facets

Let `S_f` denote the incidence star formed by the three segments `[f,vi]`. Let L be the maximum incidence-edge length and ell their positive minimum length. A point in `conv(f,vi,w_ij)` has the form

`x=f+(lambda+mu*epsilon)*ai+mu*epsilon*aj`, where `lambda,mu>=0` and `lambda+mu<=1`.

Its distance to `[f,vi]` is at most `epsilon*L`, since `0<=lambda+mu*epsilon<=1`. Thus every facet image lies in an `epsilon*L` neighborhood of its incidence star.

Choose a common radius r>0 for pairwise disjoint closed balls around the original vertices, avoiding all incidence graph edges not incident to that vertex and all facet-center nodes. Make r smaller if necessary so that each such nonincident edge has distance more than 3r from the corresponding original vertex. There are finitely many positive distances, so this is possible.

Remove the open balls of radius r/2 from all stars. The remaining compact sets for distinct facets are disjoint, because the graph embedding permits only shared original vertices as intersections. They have a positive minimum pairwise distance delta (omit empty sets). Choose `epsilon*L<min(r/2,delta/3)`. If a point outside all radius-r balls belonged to two facet images, each of its two nearby star points would be outside all radius-r/2 balls, contradicting their distance delta.

Inside a radius-r ball about original vertex v, the neighborhood estimate excludes every triangle whose associated incidence segment is not incident to v. Therefore only the two triangles of each incident facet that contain v need comparison there.

For v=vi, the same barycentric point satisfies

`x-vi=alpha*(f-vi)+mu*epsilon*aj`, where

`alpha=1-lambda-mu*epsilon >= mu*(1-epsilon)`.

If x is not vi, alpha is positive. Its direction lies within angle `arcsin(q)` of the ray from vi to f, provided `q=epsilon*L/((1-epsilon)*ell)<1`. This follows by dividing by alpha and bounding the error ball about the vector `f-vi` relative to that vector's length.

Among all distinct facet rays at a shared original vertex, the minimum angle theta is positive: three moment-curve nodes are never collinear. When there are no shared vertices, no angular condition is needed. Otherwise choose epsilon still smaller so that `arcsin(q)<theta/3`. The corresponding angular cones are disjoint away from their apex v. Hence different incident facet images intersect in that ball only at v.

All required inequalities hold for one positive epsilon because the graph is finite. Combined with the within-facet argument, the map is a straight simplicial embedding of `sd K`, and therefore a PL embedding of K. Isolated original vertices are excluded from every unrelated facet image by the same finite-distance condition. No manifold or local-star regularity is assumed, and there are no wild maps because the construction is finite and affine on a finite subdivision.
