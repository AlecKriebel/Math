# Sources and precise scope

Checked 3 October 2026. Search coverage is bounded; an open classification is not proof that no later or unindexed result exists.

1. E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov, editors, *The Kourovka Notebook*, 21st issue, October 2026 revision, printed p196, Problem 21.128, attributed to I. Soroko.
   - Primary PDF: https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/21tkt.pdf
   - Update notice: https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/2026/09/30/october-2026-update-for-the-21st-edition/
   - Read in the locally downloaded primary PDF and visually checked. The exact F4/H4 pair is unmarked in this revision. The PDF SHA256 is 31baec1b36ec3a956e787355eccfffa2e89df5b1fe31b22a3123377d8103baab.

2. M. Cumplido and L. Paris, *Commensurability in Artin groups of spherical type*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 38 (2022), 503–526; published online 2021. Proposition 3.1 gives the centre/pure-group reduction used here.
   - Publisher PDF: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/39000?nt=1
   - The introduction leaves the pair F4/H4 open. It does not identify its abstract commensurator.

3. I. Soroko, *Artin groups of types F4 and H4 are not commensurable with that of type D4*, Topology Appl. 300 (2021), 107770.
   - Author PDF, reference-correction version dated 12 June 2024: https://sites.math.unt.edu/~soroko/ArtinFHD4.pdf
   - arXiv record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10471
   - Proposition 3, Theorem 4, Theorem 7 and Table 1 are the credited inputs for pure central quotients, commensurator injection, basic periodic representatives and torsion orders. This paper separates each of F4 and H4 from D4; it explicitly does not separate F4 from H4.
   - The author's World of GroupCraft slides at https://sites.math.unt.edu/~soroko/presentation-WOG-handout.pdf are dated 25–26 August 2021 on their title slide. A recent crawl/publication label must not be mistaken for new research.

4. F. Gavazzi, I. Haladjian and L. Paris, *Smallest quotients and profinite rigidity of irreducible spherical type Artin groups*, arXiv:2609.25940v1, 22 September 2026.
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25940v1
   - Theorem 1.1 compares full profinite completions of spherical Artin groups, with one irreducible input. This is not a theorem about arbitrary isomorphic finite-index subgroups.

5. S. Hughes, T. Ng, K. Ragosta, N. Scherich and Y. Verberne, *Finite quotients of spherical Artin groups*, arXiv:2609.27171v1, 23 September 2026.
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27171v1
   - Theorem A and Sections 5.A/5.C give the distinct smallest nonabelian quotients relevant here. Theorem C is full profinite rigidity within the irreducible spherical class. The arXiv record calls this a preliminary preprint. No commensurability resolution is inferred.

6. M. W. Davis, T. Januszkiewicz and I. J. Leary, *The l2-cohomology of hyperplane complements*, Groups Geom. Dyn. 1 (2007), 301–309.
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612404
   - Theorem A / 6.2 concentrates L2 homology in arrangement rank. The downloaded record is v2; a PDF rebuild date is not a new theorem date. Apply it to the aspherical decones, not to arbitrary non-aspherical spaces as though they were group classifying spaces.

7. Reflection-arrangement input and Coxeter numerical data.
   - T. Hoge and G. Röhrle, *Reflection arrangements are hereditarily free*, Tohoku Math. J. 65 (2013), 313–319, Section 2 recalls the free-arrangement factorization theorem and reflection-arrangement freeness: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj/65/3/65_313/_pdf/-char/en
   - N. Bredon and R. Kellerhals, *Hyperbolic Coxeter groups and minimal growth rates in dimensions four and five*, Table 1 (p9), gives the F4 and H4 exponent lists: https://homeweb.unifr.ch/kellerha/pub/Naomi_Ruth-arxiv.pdf
   - The classical Brieskorn–Orlik–Solomon identification with complement cohomology and Terao factorization are also documented in the mathematical software's official reference: https://www.macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2-1.25.11/share/doc/Macaulay2/HyperplaneArrangements/html/_poincare_lp__Arrangement_rp.html
   - Coxeter group orders are products of degrees, giving 1152 and 14400. These are external standard inputs; the scripts verify subsequent arithmetic.
