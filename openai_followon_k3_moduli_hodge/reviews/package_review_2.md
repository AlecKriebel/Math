## The exact theorem and the self-contained transfer

The target is ordinary rational HC in every codimension on every finite
product of eligible smooth projective stable-sheaf or twisted/generic
Bridgeland-stable moduli spaces on arbitrary complex projective K3 bases.
The paper retains actual smoothness and projectivity, fixes numerical data
and the appropriate Gieseker polarization or generic stability condition,
and restricts eligibility to Bülles's exact theorem. It does not infer that
an open stable locus in a singular projective semistable space is itself
eligible, or add an unnecessary fine-moduli or primitive-vector hypothesis.

For a homogeneous cycle `A_j in CH^{a_j}(X x K_j)`, where `D=dim X` and
`N_j=dim K_j`, pullback/product/pushforward sends `(p,p)` to
`(p+a_j-D,p+a_j-D)`. With

    A_j in CH^{a_j}(X x K_j), B_j in CH^{b_j}(K_j x X),
    a_j+b_j=D+N_j, sum_j B_j o A_j=Delta_X,

put `q_j=p+a_j-D`. A source Hodge class is algebraic by HC on `K_j` when
`0<=q_j<=N_j`, and is zero otherwise. Its return cycle has codimension

    q_j+b_j-N_j=p.

Compatibility of cycle classes with these operations and the Chow diagonal
identity give the original Hodge class. This is an actual proof of the
transfer, with no assumption about a Tate-twist naming convention.

Exterior products of the split maps compose to exterior products of their
composites. All cycle classes have even degree. Expanding the product of
the diagonal identities supplies the diagonal of the mixed moduli product,
and the source varieties are precisely `product_i S_i^{k_ij}`. The exact
mixed-K3 theorem covers these different or repeated bases. Separate
self-power statements would not suffice. In the reverse direction taking
every factor `M_i=S_i^[1]=S_i` recovers mixed-K3 HC, so the universal target
contains the entire central base difficulty; the note does not bypass it.

Empty factors have zero target; the empty product is a point; a smooth
projective dimension-zero factor over C is a finite disjoint union of
points and reduces componentwise. Out-of-range codimensions have zero
cohomology target. `S^[0]` is a point, `S^[1]=S`, and positive Hilbert
lengths are the rank-one ideal-sheaf moduli with vector `(1,0,1-n)`.
The dimension-two isotropic-vector case is not lost. No integral/generalized
HC, arbitrary K3^[n] deformation, singular moduli, abelian-surface moduli,
or finite-dimensional motive conclusion is asserted.

The new scope reviewer read [Bülles v1, Theorem 0.1 and Section 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08284v1),
[Markman v4, Section 3 and Lemma 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0009109v4),

For a positive-rank locally free twisted `A`, the algebraic character

has `q_A(E)^dual q_A(F)=ch(E^dual tensor F)` by cancellation. The
quasi-universal root correction pulls back on the Brauer–Severi cover to
the actual character times a rational divisor exponential. Markman's

twists give the diagonal; `p_*(h^{rho-1})=1` makes rational Chow pullback

of the base with `k<=dim M`. No transcendental B-field or false integral
gerbe pushforward is needed. This closes the non-fine/twisted premise as
