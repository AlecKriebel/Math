## Exact proposition under audit

The input consists of `D = Y × T_g`, where `Y` is a smooth symplectic quartic K3 or a point, `g` is even, and `2n = dim_R D` with `n ≥ 12`. The fixed branes are quartic sphere tests and ordered torus graphs. For a rational middle class `a ∈ H^n(D,Q)` annihilated by `ω`, let `Z` be its degree-two annihilator. The additional hypothesis is injectivity of `H^1(D,Q) ⊗ Z → H^3(D,Q)` under cup product.

`prop:realization` in setup.tex:66–88 asserts, after extension of the coefficient field and an abstract complex embedding, an object `E ∈ Perf(B × E^g)` with `dim Ext^2(E,E) ≤ b_2(D) − dim Z`, whose Euler pairings with every prescribed product test equal the pairings with `a` times one common nonzero rational scalar. Its geometric mirror test system is fixed before `a`. This proposition does not itself assert that arbitrary input classes are Hodge classes or algebraic classes on the original symplectic space.

## Topological route and tests of hidden assumptions

1. **Degree-two lift.** In topology.tex:66–132 the torus bundle kills exactly `Z`. In the degree-two model the potential extra pieces are `H^1(D) ⊗ <u_j>` and `Λ^2<u_j>`. The former is killed by the stated injectivity hypothesis; the latter differential is injective because the `z_j` are independent. Their different vertical degrees prevent cross-cancellation. The spin complexification pullback has two-connected fiber; its rational odd-Chern generators have zero differential for the tangent bundle in question. I found no additional degree-two class omitted by this calculation.

2. **Tangential bordism and surgeries.** topology.tex:134–224 uses the Thom spectrum of a prescribed spin stable tangent bundle rather than ordinary oriented bordism. This is the relevant distinction: ordinary homological representability alone would not supply the formal Lagrangian tangent monomorphism. The low-degree surgeries are carried out over the tangential pullback. Polycyclicity makes the group ring Noetherian, providing the finiteness needed for the `π_2` kernel surgeries. A map inducing isomorphisms on `π_1` and `π_2` determines the same two-type and consequently the same degree-two cohomology. Later connected sums add the null-image circles and Euler characteristic without affecting `H^2` or the stable tangent prescription. The pushforward injection then follows directly from the projection formula; this part is an independently checkable deduction.

3. **Area ambiguity in the K3 factor.** topology.tex:250–299 and 404–451 deliberately use chosen filling classes, not a unique area assigned to a contractible loop. The two extra null-image circles provide dense area adjustments; small exact local perturbations then make the finitely many chosen areas exactly zero. The argument for moving filling disks outside the zero/one-handlebody retains their relative classes using the high-index dual handles. The prequantization lift needs zero area, and the text supplies precisely that, avoiding an otherwise material `π_2(D)` ambiguity. General-position inequalities `2+2<2n` and `2+n<2n` are satisfied throughout the permitted dimensions.

4. **Double-point cancellation.** The local source after puncturing is `S^{n−1} × R`, hence exactness follows from vanishing first cohomology. The target is simply connected. The loose negative link and zero signed double count are explicitly arranged. This matches the hypotheses of [Eliashberg–Murphy, Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0586v1#S2). Its extra width hypothesis occurs only in source dimension three, excluded here. The forward/reverse trace signs in topology.tex:502–530 agree with the signs of `G_1,G_2` in [Ekholm–Eliashberg–Murphy–Smith](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0588v1#S3). The resulting sign `(-1)^{k−1}` gives odd ordered branch degrees, and reversal preserves parity because `n=2k` is even.

No low-dimensional conclusion follows from this proof: evenness and `n ≥ 12` are used, not cosmetic. The zero class is not automatically an eligible input, since its annihilator must still satisfy the injectivity hypothesis.

## Categorical completion: independently verified implication

Given `prop:curvature` and `prop:directed-comparison` with the stipulated tests, realization.tex:18–210 supplies the required conclusion without using the desired Hodge conjecture.

* Three copies suffice to prove copy-arrow invertibility: if `vu=0`, composing with a third-copy arrow contradicts the nonzero product for a strictly ordered triple. One-dimensional degree-zero self-Hom and cohomological units then give two-sided inverse arrows. Ordering all permutations concerns the same finite ambient cyclic category, as the comparison proposition explicitly stipulates.
* An ultraproduct of the finite minimal categories is legitimate at fixed labels and arities. Each equation is finite, Hom ranks are uniformly bounded, and the directed comparison fixes the receiving mirror models. Pairing nondegeneracy and copy invertibility can be expressed using inverse matrices. A countable product of fields of cardinality at most the continuum still has cardinality at most the continuum; characteristic-zero fields of that size admit abstract embeddings into `C`. This need not preserve a pre-existing complex embedding of all coefficients, and the manuscript correctly uses abstract scalar extension rather than analytic specialization.
* Cofinal later copies recover restricted full representables by the directed Yoneda telescope. At each argument label, all sufficiently late arrows are strict and the directed-to-full comparison is a quasi-isomorphism. Maps from the telescope into another restricted representable form a homotopy limit of equivalences. Thus this restriction is fully faithful on the copy category. This is why directed comparisons suffice here.
* The generated mirror subcategory is smooth and proper. Its restriction of `Hom(-,L)` is represented by an object `E`. The complement `R` is right orthogonal; the ambient `n`-Calabi–Yau duality makes it left orthogonal too. The triangle therefore splits. In particular, `Ext^2(E,E)` is a direct block of `Hom^2(L,L)`, yielding the bound.
* The transverse Floer Euler characteristic is unchanged by differentials and positive bounding-cochain insertions. It equals oriented intersection with `νa` up to one common orientation sign. Since `ν` is a positive integer, the required common scalar is nonzero and rational. A test can individually have zero pairing; that does not violate the common-scalar statement.

### 1. The relevant tree invariant

For a decorated disk tree with distinguished interior mark, let `v_*` be its vertex. Every non-root vertex `v` has the unique output flag on the edge along the path from `v` to `v_*`. Every other boundary flag at `v` is an input, ordered by the ribbon structure starting at that output. This rule has two consequences that can be checked independently of the analysis:

* Cutting several edges in different orders always gives the same vertex types and the same output flag on each ordinary child. Thus each fully normalized corner uses exactly one interior-rooted factor and the same ordinary factors, with the same linear input orders.
* A cyclic relabeling of external marks fixes the distinguished interior mark and does not change paths to `v_*`. It therefore never asks for a new output mark on an ordinary child. Likewise, a decorated-tree automorphism that permutes identical child subtrees carries each child's output to the output of its image subtree.

This answers the precise noncyclic-child concern: external cyclic equivariance of the new root construction does **not** require a previously fixed ordinary child's perturbation to be equivariant under moving its output. The ordinary data are only transported along output-preserving isomorphisms, already part of their chart construction. In a three-vertex tree `A ← B ← C` rooted at `A`, either cutting order yields `(root(A), ordinary(B with output toward A), ordinary(C with output toward B))`. In a branching tree `A ← {B,C}`, exchanging `B,C` permutes factors, not the output within either factor. These are the local cases from which arbitrary trees follow.

Stabilization does not invalidate this invariant. Contracting an unmarked constant bivalent ordinary disk concatenates the same path toward `v_*`. The constant component carrying the distinguished interior mark and an attaching boundary node is stable and stays. A zero-input constant root is excluded. An unstable two-switch strip carries reciprocal cap lines and contracts by their normalized identity. Hence the same rule also passes through the admissible forgetting maps used by the cited charts.

### 3. Reciprocal switching faces do not add an unproved matching submersion

At a transverse branch-switch node, the parent flag and child flag have the prescribed reciprocal ordered labels `(p,q)` and `(q,p)`. The matching target is the point corresponding to that double value, rather than a positive-dimensional source diagonal. Hence the normalized stratum there is a direct product of the two fixed-jump problems. The direct sum of their surjective augmented operators is surjective; there is no evaluation equation whose transversality remains to be proved.

This observation does not itself prove the immersed gluing theorem. The local fixed-jump gluing charts are supplied by the cited immersed construction, and curvature.tex:342–354 invokes exactly those asymptotic problems. What it proves for this second pass is that adding an interior root and assembling the reciprocal-switch faces requires no additional matching condition beyond that local input. It also avoids discarding switches too early: they remain present in the normalized corners until the curvature output is later ruled out by its degree.

### 4. The outer-collar and relative CF-extension hypotheses

Let `M` be one compact interior-rooted problem in the finite induction. On a normalized boundary, let `S_parent` be its already constructed lower-cost root CF-perturbation and `S_child` the fixed ordinary child's perturbation. On a diagonal face their fiber product is defined because the child output is strongly submersive. On a switch face it is a direct product. Each product is transverse.

At a codimension-two corner both cutting orders give the same product of the same factors. Its CF parameter space is the product of the factor parameter spaces, and its parameter form is their exterior product. Associativity supplies compatible representatives on all iterated corners, rather than merely asserting that separately chosen CF equivalence classes happen to agree. The determinant and factor permutations carry the prescribed signs; perturbation sections themselves are transported by the same products.

Now apply outer collaring. In a chart with normalized corner coordinates `(t_1,…,t_s)`, pull the corner perturbation back along the collar projection that sends the outer-collar coordinates to their corner values. This is precisely the construction used in the proof of AFOOO Proposition 14.8. Because the products agree after every further normalization, the pullbacks agree on overlap collars. They therefore define a CF-perturbation on a boundary neighborhood. This step is essential: Proposition 14.5 requires a perturbation on a neighborhood of the prescribed compact set, not merely a perturbation on the boundary itself.

Choose a closed smaller collar `K` inside that neighborhood. It is compact, since `M` is compact. Apply AFOOO Proposition 14.5 with `f:M→{point}`. The map is strongly smooth and weakly submersive automatically. The existing collar CF-perturbation is transverse, so it is strongly submersive to the point. The theorem gives a compatible thickening and a transverse extension, retaining the prescribed perturbation near `K`. Its compatibility is as CF-perturbation representatives under coordinate changes, exactly the notion needed for composition and Stokes. It consequently does not require changes to the fixed ordinary-child integrations.

The induction is well founded. If a root with `k` external inputs splits into a root with `r+1` inputs and an ordinary child with `s` inputs, where `r+s=k`, then

`[E_parent + ε(r+1)] + [E_child + ε(s−1)] = E_total + εk`.

Every genuine factor has positive cost: nonconstant disk energy is at least `δ>ε`; a stable zero-energy ordinary vertex has at least two inputs; the one-input constant root has cost `ε`; the zero-input constant root and zero-energy unary strip are excluded from this induction. Both factors therefore have smaller cost. Finite arity and the locally finite energies give the finite-order extension the text invokes.

### 5. Why finite equivariance does not force equivariant single-valued transversality

The correct CF object includes a finite-dimensional group representation as its auxiliary parameter space. For a finite group `H`, an explicit local extension can add parameters `w_(h,j)` and perturbation directions

`sum_(h,j) w_(h,j) · h η_j(h^(-1)v)`

with `H` acting by translation of the group index. Choose the local sections `η_j` to span the remaining obstruction directions and multiply the new summand by an invariant cutoff vanishing on the retained smaller collar. The formula is `H`-equivariant as a family. Away from that collar its derivative in the auxiliary parameters spans the required directions, so parametric transversality is available even at fixed points of `H`. An invariant cutoff can be obtained from any cutoff by finite averaging. Doubling real representations if necessary gives an orientation-preserving parameter representation and an invariant normalized compactly supported top-degree form. Adding unused parameter factors is permitted by CF equivalence and leaves the old collar counts unchanged.

This is an explanation of the finite-group freedom used at curvature.tex:429–435. It is **not** the invalid procedure of averaging already transverse single-valued sections and expecting them to stay transverse. The compatible-good-coordinate-system and thickening part of the extension is furnished by the cited CF theorem; the formula shows that the finite symmetry requirement introduces no local transversality obstruction. Charts and obstruction choices are saturated over finite orbits before applying that extension, so the same construction applies to their coordinate changes.

External cyclic relabelings are kept as symmetries of the labeled spaces, not quotiented out in the Stokes count. Their orientation character is handled by the cap-line/suspended-degree signs. Intrinsic finite stabilizers still have their ordinary weights. Thus external cyclic symmetry does not require an oriented quotient of all external rotations, nor does it change the fixed ordinary child's rooted convention.
