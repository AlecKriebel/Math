\section{Cap-construction audit}
\label{app:cap-audit}

We collect the edge cases in \Cref{thm:polytope-monotonicity}.

\paragraph{Several apices.}
A point of $Q'$ may use many apices.  The implication $z_s\ge h_s\Rightarrow b_s\ge\lambda_s>1/2$ is derived separately for each cap.  It follows that two cap inequalities cannot hold simultaneously, independent of all smaller apex coefficients.

\paragraph{Threshold equality.}
At $z_s=h_s$, the preliminary projection is still in the open maximal atom.  Cut neurons are absent because the point lies on the boundary of their final polytope; uncut active neurons remain interior.  Hence the equality layer carries $\sigma_s$ rather than a third word.

\paragraph{Two requested words under one maximal word.}
Their caps use independent height coordinates, even when $m_s=m_t$.  Pairwise disjointness follows from the apex coefficients, not from disjointness of the source balls.

\paragraph{Nested requested words.}
No inclusion assumption among the $\sigma_s$ is used.  A point lies in at most one cap, so nested cuts do not interact.

\paragraph{Lower-dimensional old atoms.}
Only maximal atoms are used for cap projection.  These are open in the ambient.  Existing lower-dimensional atoms are preserved at height zero and are not used to obtain source balls.

\paragraph{Empty and full neurons.}
An always-inactive neuron is removed and restored by a lower-dimensional polytope.  An always-active neuron is represented by the ambient polytope and is cut in a cap exactly when it is absent from the requested word.

\paragraph{Empty atom.}
The chosen empty-word witness remains at height zero.  No cap reaches it because every cap projects to a nonempty maximal atom.

\paragraph{Finiteness.}
The construction introduces exactly one coordinate and at most one new halfspace in each affected neuron for every added word.  All polytopes have finitely many facets.
