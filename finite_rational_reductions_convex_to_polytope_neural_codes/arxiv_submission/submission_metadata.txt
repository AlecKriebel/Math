Title: Finite Rational Reductions for the Convex-to-Polytope Conjecture in Neural Codes
Author: Alec Kriebel
Affiliation: Independent Researcher
Email: me@aleckriebel.com
ORCID: Not supplied in audited project metadata; omitted pending author verification
Abstract: A finite neural code is open convex if it records the atoms of a finite family of open convex receptive fields, and it is polytope convex if the receptive fields may be chosen as interiors of convex polytopes. Whether these two classes coincide in arbitrary finite dimension is open. We develop a finite reduction theory for this conjecture. First, we prove polytope monotonicity: adjoining arbitrary nonmaximal words beneath the same maximal codewords preserves polytope convexity. This yields an explicit polytopal realization of every max-intersection-complete code. We then show that universal polyhedralization is equivalent, as a universally quantified theorem scheme, to deleting one nonmaximal word from a polytopal code, and further reduces to deleting a binary meet sigma=tau intersection upsilon of strict surviving superwords. Every open-convex binary-meet deletion carries finite rational one-dimensional bridge data, including simultaneous endpoint events. We also prove that one arbitrary convex neuron over a fixed finite polytopal arrangement can always be replaced by one polytope in the same dimension. Finally, every bounded open convex realization admits a protected inner-polytopal supercode and a finite source-contained whole-word repair atlas for all unwanted atoms. Exact rational examples show why independent or sequential convexification of those repairs can fail. These results do not resolve the convex-to-polytope conjecture; they reduce it to a finite simultaneous attachment problem for repeated neurons.
